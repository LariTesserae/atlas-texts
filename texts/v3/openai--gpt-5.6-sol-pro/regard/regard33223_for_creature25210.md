The power does not descend in a body.

A body would displace water. It would cast a shadow over the pools, alter the pressure in narrow chambers, and send the soft inhabitants folding backward into stone. Wings would mistake its darkness for danger. Six-legged grazers would lock themselves motionless beneath their runnels, perhaps for years. Even a foot placed gently among the pillars would be an earthquake to lives that read the world through vibration.

So the power does not enter.

It attends.

Its attention passes through the plateau without adding weight, brightness, heat, or sound. It follows each drop into the fissures and does not lose it in the dark. It distinguishes every voice within the roar: the hiss of films tightening on submerged walls, the minute rasp of a grazer’s mouth, the strain of a mineral-crusted joint, the long hunger of a curtain waiting for its chamber to sound at the proper pitch.

It knows the plateau completely, but does not therefore mistake the whole for its inhabitants.

The equilibrium is magnificent. It is also indifferent.

The power sees an old grazer whose shell has become joined to its ledge. Water still crosses the pressure cups beneath its legs. Its mouth can reach a diminishing patch of film. It is alive and cannot move. By the ordinary law of the place, it will remain so until mineral closes over the mouth or a chemical shift dissolves the body from beneath.

It sees a winged animal expelled into the glare after seven years in a spring throat. The animal circles once, twice, finds another of its kind, joins it in a rising column of mist, and then descends into the wrong spring. A constriction below will crush it before its eggs can be laid.

It sees a deep-water being carried upward toward pressure it cannot survive. It sees eggs sealed behind crust that will not crack during any remaining cycle. It sees colonial cords approaching poisoned seams, soft creatures trapped in closing pores, generations waiting for a coincidence that the stone will never again provide.

None of this damages the plateau.

That is the trouble.

The system can preserve its quantities while losing particular lives without disturbance. Their substances return. Their calcium becomes rims; their carbon passes into films; their small dissolved selves are distributed through the aquifer. Nothing is wasted, considered broadly enough.

But benevolence does not consider only broadly.

For a long while the power does nothing. Not from hesitation, but because wisdom examines even mercy for hidden violence.

To increase the number of animals would strip the microbial films and cloud the transparent pools. To introduce plants would remake the chemistry, split stone with roots, gather soil, darken the glare, and begin a conquest disguised as abundance. To still the waterfalls would collapse the pressure ecology. To open broad passages would destroy the narrowness on which countless forms depend. To give the creatures voices would be useless beneath the roar. To give them intelligence shaped like its own would burden them with distances they could never cross.

The power will not improve the place by making it resemble another place.

It will not break continuity.

Instead, it places within continuity a small allowance.

Not an opening in space. There is nowhere for one.

An opening in time.

Far below the tableland, where buried ice renews itself from vapor, the power alters no temperature and moves no stone. It merely brings into relation several events that were already possible.

A grain of quartz, strained for centuries, parts along an existing flaw.

The crack admits a thread of water into an unused channel.

That thread changes the load on a calcite lip in a neighboring chamber. The lip does not break; it flexes. Farther on, a bubble trapped beneath an overhang slips free. It passes upward through three flooded throats and divides at a fork. One half dissolves. The other reaches a spring mouth at dawn.

Each event is ordinary.

Their accord is not.

Across the plateau, hidden pressures lean briefly toward release. The great roar remains immense, but inside it a buried harmonic fades. Another takes its place: lower, spacious, unlike any note the animals have known, yet made entirely of familiar water.

The transparent films contract.

Every creature listening through stone becomes still.

Then the mineral crust around the old grazer’s foremost leg separates along a hairline boundary. It does not shatter. No fragment strikes the animal. The crust simply ceases to hold.

The grazer waits.

Its kind has no concept of rescue. It knows only that a pressure formerly met by its joint is absent.

After an hour, the leg lifts.

A second crust parts. Then a third.

The old animal moves one body-length across the ledge, leaving behind a hollow impression so exact that it resembles a fossil. It reaches a fresh film and lowers its pale rasp. Water passes over its shell. In time, minerals will gather on it again. The power does not grant immortality. It grants movement where movement had remained possible in the body but impossible in the world.

Elsewhere, the winged animal enters the wrong spring.

The current folds its long white fans and pulls it down. Ordinarily it would meet the constriction headfirst. But the passing pressure interval has widened the water—not the stone, only the water’s choice within it. A side channel takes a greater portion of the flow. The animal turns in the dark and slips beneath a submerged ledge.

The place is not ideal. Many eggs will fail.

Some do not.

In a high pool, a soft translucent being watches a shadow cross the surface. It begins to withdraw, then senses through its filament crown that the nearest crack has sealed. The new harmonic reveals another opening on the pool’s far side, one whose pull was previously hidden beneath stronger currents. The creature bends through the lit water, wholly exposed for the first time in its life, and reaches shelter before the shadow returns.

Deep below, the being carried upward encounters a chamber whose pressure should have already fallen. For one day it has not. The chamber receives it without rupture. It remains there, trailing its long crown through black water, while the route above slowly closes.

The calcite curtain hears its proper pitch.

After decades against the wall, its pits open all at once.

Throughout the plateau, dormant things respond. Eggs soften within mineral casings. Larvae release hooks. Joined cords tighten and reverse their infinitesimal direction. Two solitary grazers, each displaced by the altered flow, meet beneath a sheet of descending water and touch forelegs.

Not all are saved.

Wisdom does not turn the plateau into a machine for preventing death. Such a machine would soon prevent birth, change, movement, and meaning as well. Some wings still enter fatal springs. Some chambers collapse. Some old creatures remain embedded past release. The power does not erase consequence or select every outcome.

It creates only enough slack that constancy is no longer the same as imprisonment.

The interval lasts less than a day.

No pool overflows. No cliff falls. No channel broadens enough to be seen. The total quantity of stone remains. The total movement of water remains. Even the roar, to any human ear, is unchanged.

Then the deep relationships separate. The bubble reaches open air and vanishes. The flexed mineral lip settles back. Water resumes its former divisions.

Yet the plateau does not return precisely to what it was.

A few living things now occupy chambers they could not have reached. A few have met. A few have escaped mineral that had mistaken them for part of the wall. Winged forms will emerge years hence from springs where their kind had never emerged before. One transparent animal carries particles from a deep pool into a high one, and among those particles is a microbe that can live on a trace of manganese previously unused there.

The change is too small to accumulate into conquest.

It is large enough to become possibility.

The power watches the consequences for a thousand years.

Whenever the allowance threatens to amplify—whenever one form might multiply until it strips a ledge bare, whenever a new route could drain a chamber or silence a resonance—the power does not intervene again. It trusts hunger, chemistry, narrowness, and time to set their limits. Benevolence is not favoritism toward life over stone. The stone also belongs. The water belongs. The silence hidden inside the roar belongs.

But at intervals too long to become a season, and too irregular to become a dependence, the alignment returns.

Never in quite the same way.

One age it is a path through water.

Another, a crust that loosens.

Another, three vapor columns crossing at the exact hour when distant winged adults rise into the glare.

Another, a pressure wave that lets two colonies recognize one another through a wall they will never breach.

The creatures do not worship the intervals. They cannot distinguish providence from geology, and the power does not require them to. No mark is carved into the limestone. No voice speaks above the cataract. No law is announced.

If a traveler happened to be present during one of these hours, they would see almost nothing.

A patch of stone might move more readily than expected.

A white-winged form might cross another’s path.

In a pool, a translucent body might hesitate in the open, turn as if toward an unseen door, and disappear.

The traveler might imagine that the roar had deepened for a moment. They might feel, through wet soles and crowded rock, a loosening too subtle to call an earthquake: not motion, exactly, but the permission for motion.

Then it would pass.

The plateau would remain bright, deafening, packed from horizon to horizon. The falls would pour into the lower cloud. Quartz would flare. Every apparent doorway would still narrow into stone.

Nothing would have been remade.

Nothing would bear the signature of a hand.

But deep in the crowded world, where there had been no room, there would sometimes be room enough.
