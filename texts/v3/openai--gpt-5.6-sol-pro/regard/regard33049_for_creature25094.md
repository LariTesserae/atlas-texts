The power did not bring light.

Light would have been an accusation.

It did not bring rain, because there were no channels prepared to receive rain as rain, and water given without understanding would have entered seals, softened powders, awakened old chemistries, and killed with the appearance of blessing.

It did not stop the factories. It did not open the shaft. It did not name the Subgrade Continuance an animal, a machine, or a god.

For a long time, it did nothing that could be measured.

It attended.

Its attention rested first upon the plateau rim, then upon the dry channels, then upon each red status lamp. It followed the municipal counts into chambers where quantities had been engraved before the oldest surviving façade had become tired of distinction. It learned the city’s inventories, correspondences, delays, and substitutions. It placed no hand against the slow engines, yet knew every point at which strain passed from one material into another.

The hinge-beasts noticed.

Across the High Quiet they emerged from access covers, layered walls, sealed housings, and gaps too narrow to contain them. They did not gather around a failing component. They arranged themselves in lines.

Each line pointed south.

Residents came to inspect them. Some traveled for years. One arrived in a body fitted for ducts and stood between two hinge-beasts whose mineral plates were larger than its feet. Another lowered a listening membrane from a tower wall. One Who Had Been Three Doors compared the lines with buried utility maps and found no agreement.

The lines continued pointing south.

There, at the plateau rim, stood the resident who had practiced wanting to leave.

Its designation had changed many times as nearby systems failed and were incorporated into it. The municipal marker beside its feet contained seventeen amendments. Cables entered its ribs. A brace from the pavement had grown around one leg. Its left hand had once been part of a pressure gate. Its right contained a lens older than the Census Plain’s present surface.

The power attended to this resident for a hundred and nine years.

It found no hidden purity there. The resident was not secretly exempt from the city. Its thoughts came through plates, cords, memory strata, redistributed materials, and borrowed mechanisms. Parts of it remembered being walls. Parts remembered having opened to admit municipal equipment. One finger retained an impulse to latch when touched. The resident’s desire to leave was not separate from its construction.

Neither was it reducible to that construction.

The power understood the difference.

At the end of the hundred and ninth year, a sound occurred at the southern rim.

It was not loud. It was the sound of knuckles against a closed door.

The resident’s head turned.

No door stood there.

Again came the knock.

The resident looked over the edge into the dense, warm obscurity. Its fingers made their ancient testing motion.

A voice spoke—not from the sky, nor from the ground, nor from within the resident’s memory plates.

“Would you go,” the voice asked, “if nothing else were diminished by your going?”

The resident remained still for eleven years.

It examined the question as it would have examined a stress fracture. It considered each term. *Go* was obscure. *You* was provisional. *Nothing else* exceeded verification.

At last it said, “There is no such operation.”

“I know,” said the power.

The resident waited.

The power did not correct it. Wisdom had shown the power that a contradiction should not be resolved before the one imprisoned by it had fully named the prison.

The resident said, “My departure would subtract.”

“Yes.”

“The city does not permit subtraction.”

“Yes.”

“My body is allocated.”

“Yes.”

“My memory is distributed.”

“Yes.”

“I cannot identify a portion that is only mine.”

“No.”

The resident’s fingers moved against the darkness.

The power asked, “Must a thing be only yours before your will concerning it matters?”

The resident made no answer.

Below, in the unseen lower world, something changed pressure. It was not wind. The obscurity folded once against the escarpment and became still.

The resident said, “No applicable rule has been found.”

“Then I will not make one for you.”

For another seven years, nothing happened.

Then the municipal marker beside the resident’s feet developed a new line.

The letters did not appear by fracture, abrasion, heat, or deposition. They were simply present in the ceramic, continuing through its depth:

**QUESTION PENDING.**

The Census Plain altered.

Residents across the city felt it through floors, walls, braces, body plates, and buried lines. The low note that passed through every structure was neither the note of failure nor the note of correction. On the plain, the unnumbered human outline deepened by the width of a membrane.

Elsewhere, one engraved total changed.

For the first time in known history, the quantity listed as **UNASSIGNED REMAINDER** increased.

Not greatly.

The addition was less than the mass of a finger joint.

Alarms did not sound. The High Quiet possessed no alarm for increase.

Factory needles rose above zero and remained there. Doors opened with no maintenance sequence waiting behind them. Gases crossed thresholds without being pushed. Along the oldest walls, allocation numbers acquired hair-thin gaps.

The residents gathered around the altered tally.

They measured it. They recalculated. They removed the surrounding wall grain by grain and found the same number expressed in the layers behind it. The Census Plain had no slab corresponding to the added matter. The factories had not made it. No container had lost it. No resident was lighter.

The High Quiet had been given something.

It did not know how to receive.

The added material appeared three centuries later in a room beneath an unused habitation tower. It took the form of a small black washer, plain, imperfectly circular, and without an allocation number.

The room’s door had opened though no resident required entry.

Inside waited the washer and a statement engraved upon the table:

**THIS IS NOT REPLACEMENT.**

For eighty years, no one touched it.

The hinge-beasts gathered at the threshold. They formed a complete circle.

The resident measure searched every surviving register for instruction. Three new engraved statements were added to the ancient dispute concerning towers that spoke in municipal names. A Listener in the western wall claimed that the object was waste. One Who Had Been a Respiratory Assembly rejected the term as meaningless. A pressure variation in Tower Nine repeated a name belonging to no known citizen.

At last a resident called Keeper of the Fifth Seal entered the room.

The Keeper had a failing joint in the right hand. A ceramic washer of nearly the same dimensions was scheduled for manufacture, but the corresponding factory would not complete it for another twenty-six years.

The Keeper lifted the unnumbered washer.

No loss appeared elsewhere.

The city waited.

The Keeper placed the washer into the failing joint.

The hand closed.

The factory assigned to manufacture the replacement stopped before completing its cycle. The material prepared for the part remained in its hopper, available.

The total inventory had increased not only in number but in possibility.

Nothing was owed.

The power then added no second object.

It waited until the High Quiet understood the first.

Understanding took a very long time.

The residents tested the unnumbered washer through twelve bodies and three machines. It wore slowly. When its surface shed particles, those particles did not disappear from their collection vessels. When part of it was fused to city ceramic, the joined object carried two statuses simultaneously: allocated and given.

Doors behaved differently around it.

A door containing even one grain of the washer could be closed before its maintenance purpose was complete. It could also remain open after necessity had ended.

Residents began calling the material margin.

The word was new.

It meant the space by which a thing could vary without failing. Later it meant the space between obligation and action. Later still it meant that which could be used but need not be.

The power gave more margin.

Never much at once. Never enough to drown the city in abundance or render its disciplines meaningless. A filament appeared in a switching yard. A plate was found in an inventory house. A measure of unfamiliar carbon lay in a factory hopper. A lens waited on the unnumbered slab near the shaft.

Each gift entered without being taken from anywhere.

Each was accompanied by no command.

With margin, the city’s maintenance changed.

A resident replaced a component before it failed, not because the hinge-beasts had gathered, but because the component’s vibration caused distress.

Another repaired an asymmetrical face without restoring symmetry.

A wall listener requested that its membranes not be removed at release. The request was recorded. When its tower was later dismantled, the membranes were set in an unallocated chamber and left without function.

Nothing happened to them.

This was considered extraordinary.

The first object intentionally made to have no use was a shallow ceramic cup. Its maker spent forty-three years on it, though fabrication required less than a day. The cup was too porous to hold gas, too fragile to bear weight, badly shaped for acoustic measurement, and incompatible with every municipal coupling.

Its surface bore no inscription.

The maker placed it in the Census Plain.

The city did not take it apart.

Afterward came other useless things: a faceted stone, a strip of metal bent into a curve, a mask with no breathing slits, three uneven rods that chimed when struck, a small likeness of a hinge-beast made by a resident who had never touched one.

These objects were not called art. Not at first.

They were called retained choices.

The southern resident continued to stand at the rim.

Margin accumulated in its body slowly. A cable was replaced with given filament. A brace was exchanged for unnumbered ceramic. One old plate after another was removed, returned to the city, and substituted with material carrying no municipal claim.

The process could have been completed quickly. The power did not hurry it.

To remove the city all at once from the resident would have been another kind of dismantling. The resident chose each substitution. Some parts it kept despite their allocation. Others it surrendered gladly. One memory plate was copied into the southern railing and then erased from the body. Another it refused to copy.

“Loss will occur,” the city’s registers warned.

“Yes,” the resident said.

This may have been the first time a municipal warning was answered without obedience or dispute.

After nine hundred years, the resident remained made partly of the High Quiet and partly of margin. It could not identify a boundary between them.

The power returned to the rim.

“Are you the city’s material?” it asked.

“In part.”

“Are you the gift?”

“In part.”

“Are you the memory that has not been copied?”

“In part.”

“Will you go?”

The resident looked into the obscurity.

“No,” it said.

The power’s benevolence did not lessen.

“Why?”

“I do not yet wish to.”

This answer passed through the city more deeply than the note of any buried shift.

The resident had spent ages practicing wanting to leave. Yet when departure became possible, possibility itself proved to be what it had wanted first.

The power understood.

It did not turn away.

Near the southern marker, a doorway appeared.

It had no wall around it. It was a narrow freestanding frame of dark material, open on both sides. Looking through from the city, one saw the warm obscurity below the plateau. Looking through from the edge, one saw the High Quiet exactly as it was.

Across its lintel were engraved the words:

**NOT NECESSARY.**

The door remained open.

Any resident who approached it carrying allocated material felt the familiar increase in weight. But if they paused and said, truthfully, “I accept the loss this causes,” margin entered the city equal to what their departure would remove.

The substitution was exact in quantity, not in history.

The city retained its material.

It did not retain the person.

The first to pass through was not the southern resident.

It was a small duct-form containing memories from six prior assumptions. It crossed the Census Plain, traveled the southern avenue, and stopped before the door. Its body was scarcely taller than the municipal marker.

“Where does this go?” it asked.

“Elsewhere,” said the power.

“What function is there?”

“I do not know.”

“What body will be required?”

“Perhaps this one. Perhaps none.”

“Will I be returned?”

“Not unless you choose and a way remains.”

The small resident stood for three years.

Then it removed its allocation plaque and set it on the ground. The plaque immediately became heavier. The resident picked it up again.

“I will carry the record,” it said.

“That is permitted.”

It stepped through.

No thunder came. No light broke over the plateau. The doorway did not close.

The small figure descended into the warm obscurity, though there had been no path a moment earlier. Its steps grew faint. For the first time, sound traveled farther than expected.

On an underground inventory wall, the resident’s status changed.

Not to **RELEASED**.

Not to **DISTRIBUTED**.

A new word appeared:

**ABSENT.**

The city had possessed absences before, but only as assignments, missing parts, erased structures, and the central shaft around which all allocation turned.

This absence belonged to someone.

It was not a failure to count them.

It was the truth that they were elsewhere.

After the first departure, no procession formed. Most residents stayed. Many wished to remain in their streets, bodies, towers, obligations, and long conversations. Some loved the High Quiet—not despite its severity, but within it. Maintenance could be devotion when refusal was possible. Continuity could be chosen when it was no longer the only imaginable state.

Some residents approached the door and turned back.

Some crossed.

Some gave their margin to factories, preferring that the city continue.

Some used it to preserve memories that otherwise would have been divided. Others chose erasure.

The power established one final mercy in the inventory houses.

Beside every release table appeared a small unnumbered switch.

No resident was required to touch it.

If untouched, release proceeded as it always had. Plates became bearings, carbon became contacts, lenses entered later faces, memories found walls and hands and civic clerks.

If pressed, enough margin entered the High Quiet to replace every portion of the resident’s matter.

The resident’s body then became unallocated.

What happened next varied.

Some bodies ceased and remained whole.

Some became powder that the city could not collect.

Some vanished from the tables.

One old resident pressed the switch and continued living for four hundred years, not because its systems were sound, but because it had wanted to know whether release could be declined.

When at last it lay down again, it did not press the switch.

Choice did not make all endings identical.

The power never filled the shaft. It never explained the unnumbered figure on the Census Plain. Certain absences were not wounds to be closed by force.

It did not make shadows change direction.

Instead, it placed on the plain a single narrow instrument with a loose black needle. The needle could be turned by hand. It indicated nothing: no pole, current, stress, or utility line.

Residents came from distant districts to move it.

For many centuries they turned it only by measured increments. Later, someone spun it.

The needle revolved rapidly, slowed, and stopped in a direction no avenue followed.

There was no consequence.

The resident who had spun it made a sound.

At first others thought a seal had failed in its chest. The sound repeated, irregular and rising. The resident bent forward, one hand against its face.

No register contained the proper classification.

The power recognized laughter and said nothing.

Much later, near the southern edge, the old resident finally moved one foot.

The municipal marker fractured.

Its corresponding slab on the Census Plain fractured with it.

The resident waited for correction.

None came.

It lifted the other foot. Cables slipped free from its ribs, some allocated, some given. The city reclaimed those that belonged to it. The others fell to the ground and remained merely where they had fallen.

The resident approached the unnecessary door.

It did not pass through.

Instead it sat beside the frame, looking outward, no longer fixed to the pavement.

The power sat with it, though no form of the power was visible.

Together they attended to the darkness.

After a time, an indistinct red sheen appeared far above, as it always had.

Then, very slowly, it moved.

Its motion was slight. It cast no light. It could not have guided a traveler.

But every dark eye in the High Quiet lifted toward it.

No shadow followed.

No alarm sounded.

In a sealed room beneath a habitation tower, a factory completed a ceramic ring. Beside it, for no assigned reason, it made a second.

The two rings were not identical.

The factory became still.

And the city, which had perfected continuation, began the more difficult work of learning what might be continued freely.
