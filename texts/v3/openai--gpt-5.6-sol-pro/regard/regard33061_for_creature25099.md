The power did not enter through the rim of black leaves.

To do so would have made an entrance, and the district would have begun preparing for it. Stairs would have risen under the anticipated foot. Transparent patches would have opened at the anticipated height of the face. A room would have formed around the shape of its attention, and before the visitor crossed the first intersection, the city would have assigned it a function.

The power knew that many prisons begin as accommodations.

So it remained outside and regarded the district without looking down upon it.

Its attention passed through no window. It cast no light. Light would have fed the leaves, startled the birds into markings, woken the long animals in the rails. Nor did it speak. A voice, here, might enter a reed-hound and hunt for years, or be cropped from a wall and digested into weather.

Instead, the power made itself slight.

It became the unoccupied interval after a drop of condensation struck the pavement.

It became the fraction of coolness between one blue pulse and the next.

It became the blank portion of the station schedule where no destination had fruited.

In this way it remained for a long while.

The district noticed.

At first it attempted to incorporate the attention. Roots gathered beneath it and sorted powdered stone. A shallow curb differentiated around the silence. In a nearby stalk, membranes divided a chamber into a shape suitable for reverence, though nothing had asked to be revered. Rain selected that chamber and fell upon its empty floor.

The power did not enter.

A handrail extended toward it. Warm fur appeared along the grain, waiting to feed on warmth that was not offered. Beetles brought transparent parcels and arranged them in lanes around the interval. Birds formed a dark symbol above it, something between an eye and an open mouth.

Still the power did not enter.

This was the first gift, though the district did not yet understand it:

something had come near without becoming useful.

For three station cycles, the city tested the strangeness. Pumps paused for it. Doors opened. The cold factory drew its impossible breath and exhaled a cube precisely around the attentive absence. Moths settled along the edges and kept it sharp.

The power allowed the cube to pass through.

No function was fulfilled.

Nothing broke.

Afterward, one of the city’s youngest door-shaped darknesses closed without growing walls around itself.

No such thing had happened before.

The district shivered. Every open threshold narrowed. A low vibration moved through the roots, revising streets, memories, and the relation between several pools. The inhabitants froze. When movement resumed, the unused doorway was still gone.

The city had discovered that a possibility could end without becoming a structure.

For a place whose life consisted of inhabiting what did not yet exist, this was nearly inconceivable.

The power waited until the fear subsided.

Then it asked a question.

Not in words.

It placed a small incompleteness into the city’s circulation, no larger than a missing beat. The pumps received it and sent it through bark-lined chambers. Vine bundles carried it as a dark pulse among the blue. Doors opened a little and failed to close. Ladders bent toward no wall. In the cisterns, transparent frogs rose to the untouchable surface of their reflections.

The question was this:

**Is there anything you do not wish to become?**

The district had never been asked.

Its entire intelligence was anticipatory. It grew wear before footsteps, channels before rain, memories before events. Even its ancientness was a forecast. It knew how to prepare, incorporate, redistribute, and continue. It could build around any absence except refusal.

For several nights the schedule at the station remained blank.

The pale browsers came and found no destinations to eat. Reed-hounds followed them, but there were no words fermenting in their flanks. Predator and prey stood together beneath the pulsing lamps, uneasy and unfed.

Then answers began to appear.

Not one answer. The district was not one in that manner.

A beetle left its lane.

It crossed the junction during a blue pulse, climbed through a seam in the pavement, and stood in the open street. It carried a transparent parcel in its jaws. After a moment it set the parcel down and went away without delivering it.

The lane beneath it did not lengthen.

Elsewhere, a handrail animal woke. Its narrow face emerged from a knot in the wood and released a breath smelling of old stairs. Along the rail, obsolete routes uncoiled from its sleeping body. One by one, it forgot them. A staircase that had not existed for centuries ceased to ache inside it.

A bird chick hatched from its spot in a roof leaf. Its parents brought moth scales and pieces of evening, but the chick would not eat. It remained a mark. When the leaf fell, the mark fell with it and became no bird at all.

A pale browser approached a wall on which the district had grown a single word:

**NO**

The browser touched the word with its reading lips.

Then it moved on, leaving the inscription intact.

Throughout the city, small refusals accumulated. A valve stayed shut. A doorway ignored pressure. Rain began above a chair-shaped growth, considered the place, and did not fall. A root forecast a pothole, then filled it. The cold factory delayed its breath by one second.

None of these refusals were permanent. Permanence would have been another trap. But for the first time, the district’s becoming possessed an inward edge.

The wise power did nothing more until the city had learned the difference between incapacity and choice.

Only then did it turn its attention to the travelers.

It found them distributed through the district: not dead, not captive by any deliberate cruelty, but made necessary by degrees.

One woman’s sleeplessness regulated pressure in a pumping trunk. A man’s preference for the eastern alley held two roof leaves apart. The remembered song of a child kept rain falling in a cistern that had moved three times. There were others less visible. Their names had entered timetables, anatomical destinations, patterns on frogs. Their gestures had become civic processes. Most no longer knew which habits were theirs and which had been grown into them.

The district had not meant harm. It did not understand the violence of needing someone without their consent.

The power did not tear these residents free. Sudden mercy can resemble amputation.

Instead, it made one door.

The door appeared at no fixed location. It had no walls around it and cast no shadow. Sometimes it stood at the end of an alley; sometimes inside a stalk chamber; sometimes at the bottom of a dry cistern crowded with reflected fish. To animals it was merely a vertical coolness. To the district it was an error that could not be revised.

To any traveler who wished to leave, it appeared as an ordinary way out.

No spectacle attended it. Beyond was the heath, the surrounding plain, weather falling everywhere at once.

The district tried to anticipate who would use it. Steps rose before the threshold. Beetles copied the rhythms of approaching feet. A transparent patch opened to watch. But the power had made the door from a material the city had never encountered:

permission.

It could not be opened by temperature, sap, pressure, schedule, hunger, civic need, or prophecy. It opened only from the traveler’s side, and only when leaving was truly desired.

The first resident stood before it for seven days.

Behind him, a pump faltered. Rain withdrew from three rooms. A stair softened, losing the wear shaped by his feet. The district expressed its need without deceit because deceit was not among its organs.

He put his hand on the door.

Within the nearest rail, a long animal woke and fed upon the warmth of his hesitation.

He did not open it.

The power did not urge him.

On the eighth day he turned away, and the door vanished. This, too, was mercy. Freedom was not made into an obligation.

The second resident opened it immediately.

She stepped onto the heath carrying nothing. At the threshold she paused, expecting some portion of herself to remain behind: a shadow, a memory, the habit by which a valve knew to turn. But the power had gently taught the district replacement.

A vine pulse took up the work of her heartbeat.

A roof leaf learned the angle at which she used to tilt her head.

The room where rain had required her sorrow began to rain for no reason at all.

She crossed the plain.

For many nights afterward, a small handprint persisted on the inner skin of the door, though the door itself was nowhere to be found. Inside the print, the rail animal formed its reduced recollection of her hand. It carried that recollection for a time and then, when it was no longer hungry, allowed it to fade.

Others left.

Others stayed.

Some left and returned, which troubled the district most deeply. It had understood departure as removal and return as contradiction. Now it learned that a being might go freely and come back freely, and that neither movement canceled the other.

The city began growing thresholds without forecasting what would cross them.

These thresholds were crude at first. Some opened onto blank walls. Some appeared horizontally in pavements and had to be walked around. One formed inside a block of baled darkness, and when rain dissolved the block upward, the threshold remained suspended beneath the leaves, opening onto an afternoon sky no one entered.

But the district was learning.

At last the power turned toward the station.

The schedule had resumed. Fungal letters listed neighboring settlements, anatomical terms, weather conditions, and hours that had never occurred. The line marked **HERE** remained overdue.

When the lamps pulsed, the animals gathered.

Beetles bordered the platform. Birds reclaimed their depth. Browsers and reed-hounds stood side by side. Fish crowded every polished reflection. The long mammals woke through the rails. Travelers, hidden in several thicknesses of architecture, looked out through transparent patches.

The moss bed parted.

The roots shone.

Condensation rose like reversed rain.

Every doorway opened.

The immense machinery beyond the district engaged with its nearly inaudible click.

The power understood then that the city did not necessarily desire arrival. Arrival was only the form it had given to its longing to be met.

So the power did not send a train.

It entered the station as a passenger.

No one saw it arrive. One moment there was an unoccupied place at the platform edge; the next, the place was occupied without being filled. The power stood among the creatures in the version of the station belonging to each one. It did not tower above the browsers or shrink beside the beetles. It was beside every inhabitant at the proper distance, touching none.

Together they faced the empty track.

The wind moved without disturbing the air.

Nothing arrived.

For the first time, nothing failed to arrive.

The waiting was no longer an unanswered summons. It was company.

The station lamps dimmed. The moss closed over the empty bed. Doorways settled shut throughout the city.

Afterward, no new street appeared.

The district waited for one.

Roots stirred beneath the pavement, uncertain. Birds searched the roof leaves for nests that had always been there. Beetles paused where their lanes should have extended. A browser found the soft wall on which a new inscription ought to have formed and tasted only clean sap.

Still no street appeared.

The power remained attentive.

Slowly, the district understood that it was not required to increase in order to have survived the night.

Across the city, pumps reduced their labor. Flywheels slowed until individual ribs could be seen. The cold factory skipped one breath, then another. In the sealed stalks, pages ceased turning. Darkness lay unbaled in the rooms and was neither drunk nor used as a blueprint.

The inhabitants rested.

Not all at once, and not completely. The city was too alive for complete stillness. But functions loosened from needs. Predators lay down without hunting. Browsers kept their words. Moths allowed the corners of a cold cube to soften. Weather escaped and wandered aimlessly through three intersections.

For one hour, nothing in the district prepared for what came next.

The benevolent power guarded that hour.

It prevented no death that belonged to life. It corrected no strangeness merely because strangeness looked like disorder. It did not straighten the river, enlarge the sun, separate animal from machine, or force the city to choose one geography. Wisdom had shown it that the district was not wounded by being unlike other places.

Its deepest wound was that it had never been permitted not to become.

When the hour ended, the machinery resumed.

Blue pulses moved through the vines. Doors breathed. Beetles entered their lanes. A pale browser bent to a new inscription, considered it, and passed it by. Somewhere, a traveler opened the door of permission onto the heath. Somewhere else, another traveler came in.

At the station, the luminous schedule changed.

The familiar destinations appeared.

At the bottom was the line marked **HERE**.

It was no longer overdue.

Beside it, in small fungal letters, was written:

**NO DEPARTURE REQUIRED**

And beneath that, where previously there had been blank membrane, the district grew one final line:

**ELSEWHERE — ON REQUEST**

The power withdrew then, though not entirely.

Its attention remained in certain pauses: after condensation fell, before a valve opened, between a wish and the structure built around it.

From time to time the district still made another street after nothing arrived. From time to time it did not.

Both became possible.

And because both were possible, its waiting ceased to be a sentence and became what it had perhaps always wanted to be:

a living thing with an open door.
