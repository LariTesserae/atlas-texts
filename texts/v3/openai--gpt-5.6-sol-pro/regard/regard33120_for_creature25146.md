The power does not enter at once.

To enter is already to make a demand upon a place. A foot requires ground. A gaze requires distance. A name requires the thing named to become, for a moment, less than everything it is.

So the power remains above the plain and attends.

It attends through one dry season, though duration means little to it. It feels the scattered shrubs spend their narrow leaves. It follows roots through salt and stone. It hears water moving far below the depth at which ordinary roots surrender. It notices each possible entrance and does not open any of them.

Below, the chamber continues.

Flowers answer speech that has not yet occurred. The runner cuts nodules, packs wounds, and taps before crossings. The moth lifts thunder from a turning pool. Distances branch and heal. Nothing in the hollow knows that it is being watched, because the attention laid upon it has no weight.

The power learns the chamber’s language before speaking.

It learns that a red flower is not speech but the change speech causes. It learns that silver blossoms are not silence but the possibilities silence leaves unharmed. It learns the difference between a wall drawing near from contraction and a wall drawing near from concern.

It learns that the chamber does not think *I*.

It thinks *if*.

If the root drinks.

If the foot returns.

If the flower is broken.

If the fire comes.

If the wound is fed.

If the traveler remains.

The power does not correct this. There are minds older and gentler than selfhood.

It listens to the root-runner as well. The runner’s thought is quicker, but no more easily translated. Much of it is tension and sequence: this surface continues; that route has doubled; hunger is behind the left hand; the moth has tilted; gravity will divide.

Deeper beneath these is a small recurring shape.

Not a memory. Not quite.

A door-shaped absence.

The runner carries it without knowing. It is present when the creature sleeps in a root fold. It is present when light enters the veins of its closed lids. It is present each time a note returns before the shell has struck.

The power could open the sealed eyes. It does not.

The eyes are not wounds.

It could separate the runner from the chamber and restore whatever form preceded the green-tipped coat, the branching wrists, the listening bones. It does not.

No former shape has a greater claim to truth merely because it came first.

It could divide the great plant into organisms, untangle every graft, set yesterday behind today, and make every route obey the clean geometry of roads.

This would be called order by many wise powers.

This power is wiser than that.

It waits until rain crosses the plain.

The rain is slight. Aboveground it darkens the dust without softening it. Drops vanish almost as they strike. A pale bole shines briefly among coarse grasses, though no tree stands there before or afterward.

Below, the chamber enters invention.

Roots thrust ledges into the air. Flowers open unsupported. Distances pour outward. Hanging pools enlarge into blue, curved lakes. The moth rises with them, its wings carrying the noise of rain from a hundred places.

The runner climbs.

It braces among translucent stems and lifts its face into the green light. Its shell taps once.

The chamber answers from within the wall.

Then the benevolent power answers also.

It does not tap louder.

It taps third.

The note travels through the roots without having entered them. It is felt in every feeding surface at once, but it pulls nowhere. It neither precedes nor follows the runner’s strike. It arrives in a relation for which the chamber has no growth form.

The runner freezes.

Across the luminous distances, red flowers open, though no one has spoken. Silver flowers rise through them, though there has been no silence. Black flowers emerge in the air, each cupping the footprint of something that has never possessed feet.

The chamber tries to fold the event into possibility.

The note remains actual.

The chamber tries to reclaim it.

The note has no dead material.

It tries to answer by growing.

Every white root tip turns, not toward the sound, but toward the space the sound has left untouched.

For the first time in its life—or in the common life of all its lives—the hollow encounters something that can be present without becoming part of it.

The chamber contracts sharply.

Pools tremble. A root bridge splits. Gravity divides into five directions, then seven. The runner grips the stems with all its hands. The moth folds one wing and drops the length of a lake before catching itself.

The power does nothing to prevent these things.

Alarm is not injury. Confusion is not cruelty. To forbid a living being its first response is a subtle violence.

The hollow expands again.

Very slowly, a single flower begins to grow from the center of the root floor.

It is unlike the others. Its stem is thick, pale, and indecisive. Leaves bud along it, withdraw, and bud again. Its petals begin red, flatten toward silver, darken to black, and then lose all color. It rises until it is level with the runner’s chest.

The runner descends from the wall.

It circles without crossing its own path. It taps the floor. It taps the stem. It taps the seed-shell against the hard rim of the bloom.

The answer is singular.

The runner reaches toward the flower, meaning to break it.

The petals close around its hand.

Not violently. With the patient firmness of a hand.

The runner makes three soft clicks and presses its leaf-sealed face against the bloom.

The power asks a question.

It does not ask in words, because words would make red flowers, and the chamber might mistake the flowers for the answer.

It asks by loosening one thing.

Only one.

Deep inside the runner, where its breathing has long been grafted to the breathing of the hollow, the rhythms separate.

The chamber narrows.

The runner’s lungs do not empty.

The chamber opens.

The runner does not inhale.

For three heartbeats the creature breathes by itself.

It staggers.

Every hanging root becomes taut. Sap strikes the walls with tremendous knocks. Black flowers burst from the floor beneath the runner, so many that their cups overlap. The moth veers away as distance folds violently around the pale bloom.

The power holds the separation only long enough for it to be understood.

*You are within this.*

*You are of this.*

*You are also not exhausted by this.*

Then the rhythms join again.

The runner lies among its own black flowers. Its hands remain closed around the seed-shell. The chamber lowers hair roots over its body, more heavily than it does in sleep. Some rest against the ribs. Others touch the throat and wrists, counting nothing, feeling everything.

The pale flower remains open.

In one petal the runner perceives warmth. In another, coolness. The tastes at the back of its throat shift: mineral red, dry yellow, nearly absent green.

Then comes a color for which its body has no taste.

With that color comes the dry plain above.

Not an image. The runner cannot see images. It feels open air that belongs to no lung. Soil that does not pulse beneath the feet. Wind whose movement is not sap, intention, or gravity. Night cold enough to close the filaments of its coat. The immense tensionless distance between stars.

There is danger there.

There is hunger.

There are talons, thirst, and ground that will not lift to catch a fall.

There are also paths that may be crossed without becoming loops.

The flower offers no preference.

The runner jerks its hand away.

At once the flower releases it.

This is the first gift.

Not freedom. The runner possessed the forms of freedom its life required: turn, climb, cut, follow, sleep, refuse a grafted route.

The gift is refusal made legible.

Until then, the chamber has known injury, interruption, failed growth, and patterns it will no longer aid. It has not known the difference between what joins and what merely cannot escape joining. Anything remaining has begun to participate. Everything dead has been reclaimed. Every mark has migrated.

The power gives the hollow a new possibility:

*let remain other.*

The lesson is small enough to fit inside a root tip.

It spreads slowly.

A fallen petal lands on the floor. The membrane beneath it softens to absorb it, then stops. The petal lies yellow and separate through an entire contraction.

The chamber gathers around this fact.

Clear threads knot in the wells. Pollen descends in sheets and settles without choosing currents. The walls darken as sugars pause in their passage. Far away, an old runner with a stiff hind leg—or this runner at another branch—raises its head.

Nothing reclaims the petal.

By the next expansion it has dried.

By the one after that, it crumbles.

The chamber does not weaken. Rot does not enter. The dead thing simply completes its death without being made useful.

The hollow keeps the dust for a long time.

This is the second gift:

waste, in its holy sense.

That which need not be redeemed by purpose.

That which may be finished.

The power then turns its attention to the moth.

The moth has retreated to the high leaves. Its thorn is braced across its forelegs. In its wings move round green bodies and tiny storms. It does not come when called, because it has never been called and recognizes no authority in benevolence.

The power waits.

Eventually rain rises from every surface. Drops stream sideways and upward, carrying the drumming of weather over forests, roofs, marshes, and bare stone. They converge into a single suspended pool.

The pool turns.

The moth descends.

It samples one point and recoils. Samples another. Moves to a third.

There it pushes in the hollow thorn and drinks.

The water contains no thunder.

It contains a pause between thunder and its echo.

Through that pause the moth hears—or tastes, or becomes briefly aligned with—other chambers beneath other places.

A root hollow under snow.

A green cavity inside a cliff where no plant grows on the outer stone.

A chamber drowned beneath a lake, opening and closing in black water.

A minute room held in the root-ball of a tree growing from the roof of a ruined house.

Some are vast. Some are scarcely large enough for one folded moth. Some have runners. Some contain only flowers. One has learned to burn and is afraid of itself. One has not known rain for three hundred years and continues to raise empty pools.

Until this moment, the moth has carried sounds between branches without knowing there were listeners.

Now each distant place answers.

The moth’s veins fill with noise.

Its wings flash green, then clear. It releases the thorn. The chamber reaches to reclaim the tool, pauses, and leaves it lying on the floor beside the dry petal dust.

The moth rises.

When it folds its wings, something like a chorus can be heard, though none of its voices uses words.

This is the third gift:

not company, exactly.

The knowledge of company.

The hollow’s solitude does not end. The power is too wise to end all solitude. Roots need darkness between them. Thoughts need distances across which they do not instantly fuse. Love without separation becomes only appetite.

But when the chamber expands and feels its emptiness become enormous, it no longer mistakes enormousness for being the only living hollow in existence.

After this, the power prepares to leave.

Before it does, it places nothing in the chamber.

Instead, it removes one prohibition.

Until then, every exit has also been a change of time. A traveler may emerge before the shrub sprouts or after rain that has not yet risen below. No route outward can promise return. The chamber has not done this maliciously. It grows exits as flowers grow pollen: toward available conditions, not desired destinations.

The power touches one root seam.

It does not fix the entrance in place. Fixed places invite maps, and maps invite those who believe discovery is ownership.

It fixes something subtler.

Whoever opens this seam from within will emerge into a moment capable of receiving them.

Not a safe moment. Perfect safety is a kind of burial.

A possible moment.

There will be air. There will be ground. There will be enough time to choose whether to draw another breath.

The seam will not open from outside to iron, fire, hunger for possession, or any hand that intends to turn the chamber into proof. It will open to accident. It will open to need. It may open to wonder, if wonder has come without tools for taking.

Most importantly, it will remember those it releases.

Return will not be guaranteed.

Return will be allowed to mean return.

The power withdraws its attention.

The rain above ends. Below, the blue lake contracts into a turning globe. Root bridges draw back into the walls. Solitary flowers become near enough to touch. The great hollow folds to the size of a chamber again.

For a long interval, nothing approaches the altered seam.

The runner resumes its rounds.

It cuts nodules and packs the wounds. It sharpens the shell in resin. It follows the moth’s changing shadow. When it turns, it breaks flowers as before. The power has not made caution unnecessary.

Now and then it visits the pale bloom.

Sometimes the petals close around its fingers. Sometimes they do not open at all. The runner is never forced to receive the dry plain.

The dead petal’s dust remains on the floor. The discarded thorn remains beside it. The chamber grows a low ridge around both, not enclosing them, merely changing its routes so they need not be trampled.

Much later, during a quiet contraction, the runner comes to the seam.

Warm dust can be smelled beyond it.

The creature taps.

The shell gives one note.

The wall returns one note.

No doubled path. No sound arriving early.

The runner presses the seed-shell into the seam. The root tissue opens around the simple tool.

Beyond is ordinary soil, close and dark.

The runner waits until the hollow inhales. Then it crawls outward.

It emerges beneath coarse grass at dusk.

For a time it remains gathered beside the opening, small as a hare, green-white filaments closing in the cool air. Its hands spread on the earth.

The earth says almost nothing.

No sap columns knock beneath it. No distant footstep travels cleanly through an interwoven floor. There is only the faint scratch of beetles, the crumble of cooling soil, and far off, too far to read, the tread of some heavy animal crossing the plain.

The runner lifts its face.

Night enters the veins of its lids.

Darkness has a taste after all.

It tastes of water not yet found.

The runner stays until stars cover the sky. It cannot see them, but it feels their separate coolnesses laid across its face, thousands of points that do not join, do not graft, do not become one organism merely because they share the dark.

It makes three soft clicks.

Then, for no reason useful to chamber, moth, root, survival, memory, or growth, it sets down its shell and places all its hands in the dust.

It leaves a print.

No black flower rises.

The runner touches the mark once. Wind begins erasing it immediately.

At last it retrieves the tool and returns through the seam.

The chamber receives it.

Not absorbs.

Receives.

Hair roots brush resin from its coat. The floor alters tension around its steps. Somewhere a red flower opens, though the runner has made no sound. A silver blossom follows.

The seam closes.

Thereafter, the runner goes out sometimes.

Not often.

It never becomes an animal of the plain, nor does it discover a lost people waiting beyond the roots. There is no revelation that it was meant for another life. Benevolence does not require that every strange form be a prison or every home a failure of courage.

It goes out to feel unanswering ground.

It returns to the place that answers.

And the chamber changes in one final way.

Before, when something tapped from very far away, or from inside the wall, the hollow answered because pattern called to pattern.

Now, sometimes, it waits.

It allows the silence to remain separate.

Then it answers.

Not because it must complete the sequence.

Because it has chosen to.
