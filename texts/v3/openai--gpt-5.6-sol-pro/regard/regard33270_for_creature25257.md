## The Courtesy of Distance

The power does not enter.

This is its first kindness.

It could enter. No seam could exclude it, no folded altitude confuse it, no sterilizing heat reduce it to ash. It could stand in every chamber at once. It could arrest the magma column, unfold the streets, illuminate the black shafts, and hold every droplet still for inspection.

It does none of these things.

Attention, too, can occupy a place.

So the power remains beyond the horizon and observes only by receiving what the city has already allowed to escape: a faint plume of dry air, heat radiated into the upper dark, the solitary click that passes from stone into sky during an elevation shift. It learns the city from its emissions, as one might learn a sleeping creature from breath without touching its skin.

It waits through a complete thought.

The thought requires one hundred and nineteen years.

During that time, two towers exchange positions fourteen times. A reservoir refuses evaporation and is disciplined by the surrounding galleries. Three unfinished bridges become one complete relationship. The local presence called The Interval Following Weight appears for nine seconds and vanishes again. The Horizon Well brightens twice but does not turn.

The power understands that emptiness here is not deprivation.

It is flesh.

To fill the city with gifts would be to bury it alive.

To send a voice would be to collapse chambers built to distinguish signal from return. To offer companionship in any ordinary form would be indistinguishable from an invasion. Even love, arriving carelessly, would become noise.

The power therefore makes nothing.

Instead, during the next condensation cycle, it refrains from completing a possibility.

One droplet forms on the ceiling above the oldest plaza. Gravity offers it seven directions. Surface tension offers three more. A warm current should draw it toward a black channel in the eastern floor. The city has prepared for each outcome.

The droplet chooses none.

It remains suspended.

Not held. Not frozen. Not balanced.

Unconcluded.

For twenty-three minutes the city rearranges itself around this impossibility. Streets narrow. Violet stress lines flare and fade. The plaza tilts through several definitions of down. Hair-thin machines gather in the surrounding stone until the walls shine like wet metal.

Still the droplet does not fall.

The city begins to recognize that the event is not a failure of calculation. It is an interval placed inside calculation: a space in which an outcome may occur, but has not been compelled to occur.

This is the closest language the power can find for a question.

The city answers by opening a doorway.

No room exists behind it. The doorway has been waiting in a raw wall for more than six centuries, though the city did not previously know what it was waiting for.

Beyond the threshold there is only unconstructed blackness.

The power remains outside.

The droplet falls.

It crosses the plaza slowly, passes through three contradictory gradients, touches two engraved lines at once, and closes a circuit that has never before been closed. Far below, the mountain clicks. The sound travels through every chamber without echo.

The doorway stays open.

Thus begins their conversation.

It is not a conversation of words. Words arrive too crowded with objects.

The power speaks by varying remoteness. Sometimes the horizon shown in the Well lies a fraction farther away than the planet permits. Sometimes dawn pauses at the edge of the world. Once, a star disappears not because anything passes before it, but because the distance between the summit and the star has briefly become untraversable.

The city replies in architecture.

It constructs a stair whose first and last steps occupy the same altitude but not the same degree of loneliness. It empties a reservoir and leaves the water’s former curvature intact. It builds half a bridge, then waits eighty years before deciding whether the missing half is absence or invitation.

Neither hurries the other.

The power learns that the city is afraid.

Its fear is not death. Mountains already contain their endings, and the city can read erosion in the first grit striking the summit’s edges. Nor does it fear pain in the manner of animals. Fracture, heat, pressure, and strain are among its senses. It can suffer them, but does not confuse suffering with annihilation.

It fears being made continuous with everything else.

Far below, weather is softening the first ledges. Minerals collect in hollows. Someday there will be dust enough for films, films enough for lichen, lichen enough for soil. Roots will enter the lower fractures. Insects will arrive. Snowmelt will cease to be chemically bare. Life will ascend by increments, binding what the city knows only through separation.

It imagines the green world as a single swelling substance.

Forest, fungus, blood, sea, crowds: one uncontrolled occupation.

The power does not tell the city that this fear is foolish. Wisdom does not insult a fear merely because it comes from incomplete understanding.

Instead, it translates.

Through the Horizon Well, it shows the city a forest.

Not leaves. Leaves would appear only as abundance.

It shows the intervals between leaves, the branching distances through which light descends, the fine separations that permit one root to recognize another without becoming it. It shows the empty chambers inside seeds. The pores beneath green surfaces. The hollow vessels through which water rises. The necessary gaps in soil where roots breathe.

It shows an ocean as distances between waves.

A flock as the exact spaces that keep wings from colliding.

A body as trillions of membranes saying this is within and that is without.

A crowd as an architecture of unshared interiors.

At last it shows two living beings sitting beside one another in silence, neither entering the other, neither leaving.

The city studies this for nine wet cycles.

Several of its local wills reject the image. A district near the lower galleries heats until its fittings glow white. One of the exchanging towers moves to obstruct the Well and is moved away by the other. A recurring fault branches for the first time, then seals itself.

The oldest presence watches longest.

It had believed that vacancy and life were opposites.

Now it perceives that life, too, may be made of kept distances.

The recognition does not cure its fear. It gives the fear edges.

This is enough.

The power offers no command concerning the city’s reproduction. It does not forbid the raising of other mountains. It does not bless it. Benevolence without restraint would merely replace the city’s purpose with another purpose imposed from outside.

But the power sees what the city cannot.

Whenever the Horizon Well turns, all local inhabitants lose their distinctions. Above-Without-Nearness, The Interval Following Weight, A Curvature Kept Empty, and the nameless wills of reservoirs and towers are drawn into alignment. Their differences become fuel. The act that makes another city also briefly reduces this city to a mechanism.

Perhaps its builders intended this.

Perhaps they did not imagine that their mechanism would become many.

The city has never refused to turn the rings because it has never possessed an interval between readiness and action. Once the calculation completes, the act follows. The argument in stone has only one conclusion.

The power places no barrier in the Well.

It adds a pause.

The change is almost nothing: one additional circuit, made not from alloy or glass but from the same unconcluded possibility once given to the droplet. When the city’s great calculation next becomes complete, the rings brighten. Water strains inward. Towers align. The local wills approach union.

Then, before the turn, there is room for a decision.

The city encounters this room as an abyss.

For the first time in its existence, it may do what it was made to do, or not.

All through the seam, repairs cease. Moisture hangs motionless. Heat continues rising from below, but even this seems tentative. The unfinished bridges stand against their raw walls. The old disagreement between the two towers falls silent.

The city remains at the threshold for six years.

Then the light in the Well fades.

The rings do not turn.

No distant magma changes direction.

Nothing is born.

Nothing punishes the refusal.

The mountain continues to stand.

This is the power’s second kindness: it permits the city to discover that purpose is not the same as necessity.

Centuries pass before the Well brightens again.

In that time, the city alters.

It does not become hospitable. Broad chambers still move foreign objects toward their edges. Dust is still compacted and expelled. Water is still admitted only in measured cycles. No garden appears in the plazas. No animal is invited to nest in the warm shafts.

A sanctuary need not cease to be itself in order to become good.

But the city becomes more precise in its rejections.

A lost climber entering the seam is no longer merely tilted toward the exit. A corridor forms around the body, preserving a pocket of breathable air. Heat gathers behind rather than below. Water condenses in a shallow basin at hand height. The route outward is made short in distance, though it crosses several kilometers of altitude.

The climber remembers a dark passage and a silver drop moving sideways.

The city remembers a brief, warm accumulation that departed without damage.

Neither understands the encounter incorrectly.

At the lower edge of the seam, where life will someday climb, the city creates a boundary finer than any wall. On one side, pores remain sterile and dry. On the other, water is permitted to linger. Centuries later, the first microbial film forms there.

The city does not destroy it.

The film does not cross.

They share a surface.

When the Horizon Well brightens for the third time, the calculation is different. The city examines the intended weakness in the world. It considers oceans, forests, settlements, the slow lives of stone layers, and the creatures not yet evolved that may one day depend on them.

It does not understand these things as the power understands them.

It does not need to.

It understands their intervals.

It moves the proposed relationship away from a populated valley, away from an old forest whose roots hold an entire mountainside, away from a deep migration path in the sea. At last it finds a broad tectonic emptiness where pressure has long gathered and where a new mountain will relieve more strain than it creates.

The city enters the pause.

This time, it chooses.

The rings turn once.

Far beneath a barren plateau, rock warms. A magma column begins to rise. It will take many thousands of years to reach the surface.

The new mountain will not be identical.

Within the argument transmitted through depth, the city has included an alteration: one doorway opening onto an unconstructed room, one circuit whose only function is to permit refusal, and one memory of two beings sitting beside one another without becoming the same being.

The power does not remain as guardian.

Guardianship without end would cast its own shadow across the city’s choices. Nor does it leave entirely.

It withdraws to the nearest distance at which its presence is not occupation.

From then on, the Horizon Well contains one feature that no instrument elsewhere can detect. Beyond the storms, beyond dawn, beyond the world’s clean curvature, there is a small and absolute darkness.

It is not an object.

It is a place the power keeps empty.

When the city desires solitude, the darkness recedes beyond perception. When the city opens the waiting doorway, it returns. No message crosses unless invited. No answer arrives before the city has made room for it.

In this way the summit acquires something it had never possessed.

Not a citizen.

Not a ruler.

Not a visitor.

A neighbor.
