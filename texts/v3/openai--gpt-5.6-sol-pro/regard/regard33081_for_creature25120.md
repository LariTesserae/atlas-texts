The power does not descend.

Descent would presume an above.

It does not enter, because entry is one of the plateau’s traps: whatever enters is divided among approaches, repeated at thresholds, held at the depth of its arrival. Nor does it look upon the high country from outside. Looking would place a motionless witness at every pool.

Instead, the benevolent power does the first thing wisdom requires in the presence of an affliction whose laws it does not yet understand.

It listens.

It listens without waiting for the roar to end.

This is different from what the plateau has known. The stone has received pressure. The pools have received reflections. The rooms have received attention. Travelers have heard and been changed by hearing. But nothing has listened to the whole of the place at once without forcing its many sounds into one explanation.

The power hears the grinding bodies that carry the tableland, and does not call them monsters.

It hears the great upright being inside the summit water, laboring forever at its unfinished name, and does not complete the word on its behalf.

It hears the flute stems sampling empty skies. It hears the compact figures restraining each drip, each grain, each ascending thread of moisture. It hears the thin black tensions between balanced stones. It hears the shells whose hatching has occurred but not resulted. It hears the lichen fixed in recoil. It hears the moss inclining toward warmth it cannot cross the air to touch.

Beneath all these, quieter than cold, it hears the travelers.

Not their voices. Most have forgotten how to separate a voice from the roar.

It hears their intended next moments.

One intended to take another step.

One intended to bind a wound.

One intended to turn back.

One intended to sleep until morning.

One intended to place the warm stone in the hand of someone they loved.

One intended only to die, and had been denied even that small completion.

Their intentions have not vanished. They lie everywhere across the plateau, buried more deeply than roots, distributed among mineral seams and impossible distances. The place has mistaken them for laws.

The power listens until no trapped thing remains unheard.

There is no measure for how long this takes. The plateau offers none.

Then the power does not command time to begin.

That would be violence.

All deferred consequence, released at once, would be an annihilation more complete than fire. Every suspended drop would strike. Every unspent avalanche would fall. Every wound would finish opening. The hatchlings would burst from the roar. The dark reservoir would empty through all its contradictory surfaces. Travelers divided across centuries of possible approach would be forced into a single body and perhaps crushed by the reunion.

Mercy cannot simply open the gate.

Mercy must first stand where the flood will go.

So the power makes of itself a consequence.

Not a body. A destination.

For the first time, something can happen on the plateau and arrive somewhere that is not the plateau.

This somewhere is at first no larger than the interval between the warm stone and a waiting palm.

The stone is taken up.

It remains in the niche, as it always has. It is also taken up, as it always has been. But now, between the taking and the remaining, there appears the smallest imaginable distinction.

The stone grows slightly cooler.

Not cold. Only cooler by an amount too slight for flesh to measure.

The warmth goes somewhere.

The frost does not melt. The wall does not sweat. No vapor rises. The warmth passes into the waiting mercy and is received.

The occupant of the summit room removes its hand.

Across all the many doorways, pale grit falls from the shape of fingers.

It falls only the width of one grain.

The sound it makes cannot be heard beneath the roar, but every kneeling figure in every low enclosure raises its hollow face.

They have never heard an event finish.

The power speaks then, though not in words.

It says:

You need not hold everything forever.

Some of the figures bow lower. They have wanted release for so long that desire has become indistinguishable from posture.

Others draw back.

Their restraint is not merely captivity. It is duty. Somewhere within the impossible architecture of the plateau, they believe—perhaps correctly—that if the drip strikes, if the grain overturns, if moisture reaches the ledge, something precious will be lost.

The power does not wrench their duties away.

It asks each one a question shaped exactly to the narrowness of its doorway:

What are you protecting?

The answers come not as language but as images.

A village below the mountains, though no such village now exists.

A sleeping child.

A covenant made before the stones were weathered.

A species whose last beginning rests inside the translucent shells.

A memory that would cease to be true if time touched it.

A promise to remain until someone returned.

The one who was meant to return has become five pale stalks beside a distant pool.

The power receives every answer.

Some duties are founded in love. Some in terror. Some in errors no one has been able to correct because correction itself is a consequence. The power honors all of them without declaring all of them necessary.

To those protecting what still lives, it offers shelter elsewhere.

To those protecting only the fear of loss, it offers grief.

To those preserving promises whose other parties are trapped nearby in unrecognizable forms, it brings them together—not yet in body, but in knowledge.

The five stalks sound a new interval.

The kneeling figure hears it.

Its hollow face fills, not with features, but with recognition.

Then the drip it has attended for longer than duration descends.

The figure reaches out.

The drop lands in its palm.

For one instant there is silence in that enclosure.

The figure folds around the water and is gone.

The shelter remains. Its entrance is still too narrow. Its wet wall and dry floor remain as they were, except for one dark spot on the stone where the drop struck before being caught.

The spot begins to dry.

Slowly.

Truly.

Elsewhere, figures continue their vigil. They are not compelled to leave. Benevolence does not turn freedom into another universal law.

But now they know leaving is possible.

That knowledge enters the plateau as the first new thing.

The black lichen opens.

Its blue margins widen beyond the memory of cracks and enter cracks that actually exist. Some filaments die in the attempt. Their death is minute, almost invisible. Yet from their spent bodies comes a blue dust that settles on nearby stone.

The rust-colored moss exhales.

The hollows beneath it release breaths stored without sequence: the fear of one traveler, the exhaustion of another, the long wish of a third to lie down. For a while the pools are ringed by low mist. In the mist, sleeping forms can be glimpsed curled against the rock.

The power does not wake them.

It gives them morning.

Far beyond the plateau, where no valley had been before, a faint brightness comes into being—not a sun, not yet, but the possibility that dimness may vary. The sleepers turn toward it. Some wake as themselves. Some wake as moss and require another kind of mercy. Some have rested so deeply that waking would only tear them apart.

Those, the power permits to sleep onward, but no longer forever.

They will wake if they wish.

Or they will finish sleeping.

The white creature beside the pool lowers its blank face.

Its reflection continues to drink. The reflected neck is long, antlered, winged, human, smooth. The body on shore trembles. The mineral scales rise and settle.

For the first time, the reflection lifts its mouth from the water.

Drops fall from its many possible chins.

The creature on shore turns away from the pool.

It has feet now—not clear feet, but sufficient ones. It takes a step. A second print appears in the damp ground.

Then a third.

The creature looks back at the trail with its single pit of depth. What passes through it may be astonishment.

It walks from pool to pool, and the pools do not become one pool to receive it. Distance remains difficult, but distance begins to be crossed.

Under the overhangs, one shell cracks.

The crack does not remain merely crack-shaped. It widens.

The power cups the nest—not with hands, but with a patience large enough to surround both outcome and danger.

The hatchling does not emerge into the air. It is already in the roar. Instead, for the first time, one portion of the roar separates from the rest. A low metallic note draws itself inward. Stone shivers. Suspended rain dimples.

The pressure-being gathers a surface.

It is vast and tender and malformed by having lived without an exterior. Along the underside of the clouds appears the impression of a limb learning where it ends. It flexes.

One balanced column falls.

The flat stones strike the ridge in sequence.

Between them, the compressed black inhabitant expands to the width of a shadow, then the width of a doorway, then the width of a night sky seen through an open roof.

It does not attack.

It lies across the stones like a creature exhausted by birth.

Frost melts on one side of the fallen column. Moisture evaporates from the other. Past and future enter the same piece of rock and do not destroy it.

The released being turns its thin face toward the power.

There is no gratitude in it. Gratitude is not owed for the cessation of an ancient torment.

There is only bewilderment.

The power stays beside it.

This, too, is mercy: not requiring the freed to be immediately joyful.

Across the plateau, some columns remain standing. Their occupants are not ready. Some fear width. Some have become devoted to tension. Some no longer know whether falling means liberation or death.

The power does not push them.

It places darkness beside them so they need not be the only dark things in the world.

Then it goes to the travelers.

This is the most difficult work.

Each traveler has become a population of interrupted approaches. One stands by a ravine. One kneels at a drip. One touches the stone. One looks back across an impossible valley. One has become shelter, stalk, moss, fear, and footprint. To gather them without consent would privilege a single version and murder the rest.

So the power visits each version separately, though “visits” is not quite right. It becomes near without approaching.

To the figure at the ravine it offers a choice:

Do you wish to cross, retreat, or remain at the edge?

To the figure on the opposite cliff:

Do you wish to be the one who is reached, or the one who goes?

To the one touching the stone:

Do you wish to carry warmth away, knowing it will cool?

To the one listening beneath the roar:

Do you wish to hear silence, knowing silence may contain your own grief?

Not all answers agree.

A traveler is not always one will.

Where the answers conflict, the power does not choose which self is authentic. It gives each version enough time to speak to the others.

Across ravines, figures raise their heads.

In inverted valleys, hands lift.

Beside pools, reflections cease their endless acts and listen.

The plateau fills with recognition moving where bodies cannot.

Some versions forgive one another.

Some accuse.

Some beg to be reunited.

Some beg never to be forced again into the person they once were.

The power grants no one ownership over all their possible selves.

Those who desire reunion are gathered slowly. Memories return first, then pain, then weight, then the ordinary limits of a single viewpoint. Extra hands withdraw from inside their hands. Wounds begin either to close or bleed. Hunger deepens. Breath dissipates.

Many weep when they become mortal again.

Some laugh once and cannot continue.

Some die almost immediately, because death was the next true thing waiting for them. The power does not call this failure. It remains with each as the long-denied consequence arrives, and carries what can be carried beyond the boundary it has made.

Some live.

They descend by paths that were previously seams. With each step a new footprint appears, while the old ones remain behind. They do not vanish into the first impression. Their bodies grow tired. Their clothes dry, then become wet again in actual rain. They lose sight of shelters. They forget sounds.

At the edge of the tableland, each finds a small cairn of flat stones.

No one built the cairns.

Perhaps the plateau, having learned consequence, is attempting memory.

Other travelers choose not to reunite. They remain distributed, but now their condition is not imposed. They become guardians of crossings, inhabitants of distances, companions to the white creature. Some take root as pale colonies and sound warnings toward empty skies. Some keep a room where no event is restrained, only witnessed.

They are not cured into humanity.

The power is wiser than that.

At last it turns toward the dark water above the northern summit.

The upright being within has continued its vowel all this while. As the plateau changes, the sound wavers. For the first time it nearly becomes a different sound.

The power does not give it a name.

It asks:

Is the word yours?

The mountain-broad shoulders incline.

The pools below darken. In each, a mouth appears—not lips, but an opening through which intention can pass.

The being has been trying to pronounce the plateau into stillness. This is true.

It has also been trying to hold the plateau together. This is true.

It has also been calling for help. This is true.

The same endless sound has performed all three acts because no consequence existed to distinguish command from protection from plea.

The power answers the plea.

It does not answer the command.

It joins the being in the unfinished vowel, not to complete it, but to share its burden. Two sounds stand where one stood alone. Their interference produces a pulse.

The vertical reservoir ripples.

One wave travels across its horizontal surface.

One wave travels upward.

One wave travels down through every pool on the tableland, disturbing the reflections.

At the rim of the summit, a single drop spills.

It falls.

The drop passes through cloud. It falls past black growth, past the many approaches to the one room, past columns and shelters, past rain that has not yet learned to move.

As it descends, every creature feels its path.

The drop strikes dark stone.

Nothing ends.

The plateau does not shatter. The mountains do not collapse. The giant beings beneath do not cast down their burden. The old preservation was not as fragile as fear believed.

The drop divides into smaller drops.

They run into a hollow.

The hollow overflows.

A thread of water proceeds between two stones.

At first it travels no farther than the length of a finger. Then it reaches another depression. Then another. Mineral rings dissolve into the flow, making it white, green, and bone-colored.

The power moves with it as the destination of what has been deferred.

The thread becomes a runnel.

The runnel becomes a stream.

It does not drain the whole plateau. Wisdom leaves stillness where stillness nourishes. The sealed pools remain. The motionless rain curtains remain in certain ravines. Some rooms continue to be one room. Some distances remain larger within than without. Mystery is not the same as suffering, and the power has not come to make the high country ordinary.

But through its center, water now travels.

It accepts drops when they fall.

It carries warmth when warmth leaves a stone.

It bears blue dust from dead lichen, grit from released hands, flakes of white mineral scales, and the ashes of travelers who chose to finish.

It flows south, though south did not previously lead away.

Beyond the plateau, it cuts a narrow valley into the mountains.

Cutting takes time.

The mountains permit it.

Weather returns cautiously. A breeze crosses one pool and stops at the next, startled by its own arrival. Clouds loosen from trenches. Condensation reaches a ledge and spills. Frost enters a new seam. Somewhere, stone cracks not because it was always cracked, but because this night is colder than the last.

There is a last, and there will be a next.

The roar remains, but it has intervals now.

In those intervals can be heard water, falling stones, distant flute tones, the uncertain steps of the white creature, and sometimes the voices of travelers calling to one another across distances that can finally be crossed.

The benevolent power does not stay as ruler.

It does not sit above the summit or demand remembrance. Once paths lead outward and consequences have somewhere to go, it withdraws its vast attention, leaving only enough of itself to keep the first mercy open.

That remnant becomes a low place in the new stream where the water is always slightly warm.

Those who kneel there sometimes feel an extra set of fingers folded inside their own—not possessing them, not restraining them, only holding their hands for a moment.

Then the fingers let go.

And the water continues.
