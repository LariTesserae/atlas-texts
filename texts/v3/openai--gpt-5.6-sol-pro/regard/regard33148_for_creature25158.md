The power does not descend into the basin.

Descent would oblige it to arrive.

Instead, it attends.

Its attention is not like the attention of the beetles, which divides edges from surfaces and waits for irregularity to become law. It does not inspect. It does not measure. It does not require the place to become intelligible before extending mercy to it.

For a long while—though no while passes there—it perceives the basin exactly as the basin perceives itself: all at once.

It knows the fear held in the hares without mistaking endurance for consent. It knows the hunger in the foxes without condemning them for the shape hunger has taken. It feels every piston stroke, every root pressing against unopened stone, every pulse arrested before the tail. It sees the visitor dispersed among shadows and throats and winter twigs. It sees the immense being expressed as geology, weather, animal, and work.

It does not call the place evil.

Nothing here chose cruelty. Nothing here chose anything.

The power understands that the basin keeps one instant because it has no other way to keep what it loves.

Its sharp stones, its wet leaves, its breathing animals, its engines at full exertion: all are held against loss. The basin has mistaken continuation for annihilation. It believes that if the drop reaches the ground, the falling will no longer exist. If the hare escapes, the escape will be gone. If the piston completes its stroke, the force contained in the stroke will vanish. If one instant becomes another, the first will have been murdered by the second.

So the basin saves everything by permitting nothing to become past.

The power does not break this law.

To break it suddenly would be catastrophic.

The fox’s teeth would close. The falling water of immeasurable duration would strike all at once. Stored pressures would discharge through the mountain. Every unfinished breath would complete in the same body. Roots would split rock with the accumulated force of ages. The basin would not awaken into time; it would be crushed beneath all the time it had refused.

Nor does the power lift the inhabitants away. Removal is only another kind of force when imposed upon creatures incapable of departure.

Instead, the power gives the basin something it has never possessed.

Not change.

Memory.

At first the gift is smaller than a spore.

It appears beneath the moss in no fixed location. The beetles find it everywhere along every boundary: a minute warmth remaining after nothing has passed, a trace that does not repeat the thing that made it.

They surround it.

Their antennae tap seven times.

They wait.

The warmth remains, but does not recur.

This is the first event in the basin that is no longer happening and has not been lost.

The underground machinery misses an engagement.

Not a failure. Not damage. One tooth simply does not meet the next, and the force that would have passed between them is remembered by both.

Across the floor, the circular gear-shadows tremble.

The birds’ metallic chorus acquires a narrow opening. For less than an instant, one cutting note is absent. Through that absence comes no voice, no command, no revelation.

Only silence.

The basin has known apparent silence before: the numbness produced by unvarying noise. It has never known this silence, which is not the flattening of sound but the room left after sound has ended.

Every creature hears it in the organ by which it suffers.

The hares hear distance.

The foxes hear release.

The salamanders hear the destination of the pulse.

The roots hear opened stone.

The drops hear the pool below them.

The visitor, wherever the visitor has been distributed, hears their own name after it has been spoken.

The power waits.

Waiting is different for it. It does not wait because it lacks the outcome. It waits because wisdom refuses to replace another being’s incompletion with its own will.

The basin cannot answer in sequence. It cannot consider the gift and then accept or reject it. Its intelligence contains question and conclusion side by side, but no road between.

So the power makes no demand for a single answer.

It asks each function in the language of that function.

To the hare it offers not escape, but the possibility that fear may finish.

To the fox it offers not punishment, but the possibility that hunger may become something other than the eternal shape of biting.

To the bird it offers one completed wingbeat.

To the frog it offers an outermost basin.

To the salamander it offers a tail.

To the beetle it offers an irregularity that need not be corrected.

To the piston-plant it offers rest.

To the mountain beneath them all it offers no future it must enter—only the knowledge that what has happened need not be happening in order to remain real.

The answers are uneven.

One beetle refuses.

It closes its iron cases and places itself across the warm trace. Its orange plates burn brighter. It taps seven times, waits, and taps seven times again.

The power does not overturn it.

A cliff bird draws one mineral toe out of its fissure. The crack remains after the toe has left it. The bird stares at the empty shape that proves where it was.

Then it returns its toe to the crack.

That, too, is permitted.

A glassy frog deflates its throat.

Within it, the smaller basin vanishes.

The frog remains.

Its body clouds from perfect transparency to the faint green translucence of living flesh. It blinks once, and the blink completes. For the first time there is a moment during which its eye is closed and another during which it is open.

Nothing terrible follows.

The basin remembers both.

At the northern wall, one red-stemmed plant completes its seventh stroke. Vapor fans from beneath its leaves. The fan thins, loses its exact border, and disappears into the air.

No replacement fan occupies the same shape.

The plant does not collapse. Its luminous sap dims to a softer blue. The cartilage rods settle at the bottom of their chambers.

In the silence after the hydraulic cough, the plant bends slightly beneath the weight of its own leaves.

Then it is still.

Not the basin’s old stillness, crowded with an action that cannot finish.

Rest.

The concept enters the distributed creature like rain entering dry ground.

The deep engine releases pressure.

All across the basin, wet stone gives a low sound—not a crack, but a sigh passing through mineral. The great interlocking shadows turn by less than the width of a tooth. Several vanish. Others remain where they are.

Nothing is forced to proceed.

The power has not brought time as a flood. It has made small channels through which completion may pass without carrying existence away.

Now a droplet falls from the southern fins.

There have always been falling droplets there, each held at the next instant of a descent. This one leaves its silver row. It lengthens, trembles, strikes the moss, and is gone into it.

A dark spot spreads.

The rock is wet in a new place.

The beetles rush toward it, their orderly spacing broken. Several try to surround the spot, but it grows beyond their circumference. One beetle turns in a complete circle. Another collides with it.

Their shells click.

One opens its iron cases. The orange plates within do not recede forever. There are twelve of them. Beneath them are two thin wings.

The beetle flies.

Badly.

It rises the height of a hand, tips sideways, catches itself, and lands on a fern outside the route it has always patrolled. The frond bends under its weight and does not immediately straighten.

The beetle stays there.

It has failed to inspect anything.

A second droplet falls.

Then no third.

The power is careful even with rain.

Near the center, the fox’s jaws open.

There is no tearing. No sudden wound appears to fulfill an ancient threat. The hare’s neck bears only a ring of flattened fur and the warmth of remembered teeth.

The fox steps backward.

Its body, drawn narrow by motion that never traveled, shortens into its true proportions. Silver hairs lift along its spine. Hunger passes through it, no longer fixed and perfect but changing: sharp, then hollow, then bewildered.

The hare does not run at once.

Its fear has been its entire relation to the world. Without it, the open basin is enormous.

The middle pair of legs ceases kneading the air. One paw lifts, reaches forward, and touches stone.

The hare takes one step.

The fox watches.

The hare takes another.

At the third, it runs.

It is clumsy. Six legs are too many for any gait it has practiced only as potential. It stumbles, rolls through wet moss, rises with green stains on its white fur, and runs again between the fern beds.

For the first time, one open space in the basin has no hare in it.

The emptiness is not repaired.

The fox lowers its nose to the place where the hare had been. Among the moss it finds something the power has not created: a cone dropped long ago or just now from the miniature tree, containing resin, seeds, and a small beetle larva.

The fox does not eat it.

But it smells.

Its hunger becomes curiosity for the duration of one breath.

That is enough.

Change enters by such mercies.

Not every preserved exertion is released. Some creatures cling to simultaneity because it is the only self they know. Some birds remain joined to their cracks. Some gnats continue rising through swarms that do not alter. One dragonfly stays forever a finger’s width from its prey, unwilling or unable to discover whether desire survives attainment.

The power does not call these failures.

It leaves openings beside them.

The five stone fins at the southern rim still show impossible views, but between two of them there now appears a narrow path descending through cloud.

The path is not an exit imposed on the basin. It is merely a direction that continues when followed.

A hand extended between those fins no longer meets the basin floor. It meets cold air.

No creature is driven toward it.

The visitor sees the path—or a shadow bearing the visitor’s outline sees it. The person has been distributed too thoroughly to gather at once. Their breath is in the engine. Their pulse is in the plants. Childhood weather hangs in the little conifer. An unsaid word moves soundlessly in a hare’s mouth.

The power does not tear these things back from the basin.

It teaches them to remember one another.

The bird may keep the eye color without possessing the eye.

The frog may hold the remembered room without imprisoning its inhabitant.

The tree may bear the childhood season as fragrance, while childhood remains past.

One by one, the borrowed fragments cease to be duplications and become traces.

At the edge of a dark pool, a human shadow rises from where no body stands. Elsewhere another shadow approaches it. A third draws its reaching arm inward. They meet without overlapping and become a single outline.

Condensation loosens from the eyelashes.

The heart stumbles out of the seven-stroke rhythm.

It beats once too soon.

Then again.

The underground engine does not correct it.

A foot lifts from the wet pavement. This time no footprint appears in advance. The foot descends, presses water from the moss, and leaves behind a clear human mark.

The mark remains.

The visitor weeps—not because the power commands gratitude, and not because freedom is painless, but because for the first time the tears reach the ground.

The basin receives them.

It does not return them to the eyes.

In the ages that follow, if ages are what now follow, the place does not become ordinary.

Its rock remains too fresh. Mist still occupies round-edged chambers. Frogs still contain landscapes, though the landscapes alter slowly and do not always include themselves. Piston-plants work for seven strokes and rest on the eighth. The moss carries basalt grains across pools; some grains return, and some remain beneath new roots.

Edges begin, very slightly, to soften.

The first lichen dies and leaves a pale mark on stone.

The first seed opens.

The first young creature is born there, not admitted as a contradiction or preserved from elsewhere, but born: incomplete in a different and more hopeful way. It is a small sightless salamander, its gills folded red against its neck. A pulse travels from its head through its body and reaches the end of its tail.

The mountain feels this.

Far below, immense gears disengage in sequence.

The sound they make is not destruction. It is the sound of a burden being set down by something that had forgotten it possessed hands.

The basin does not cease manufacturing the instant.

It learns to make a second one.

Then a third.

It keeps the first not in stone, repetition, terror, or uncompleted work, but in memory: a deep and gentle stratum that requires no creature to suffer forever in order to prove that what happened was real.

The wise power remains attentive, but not as a ruler standing over its repair.

It becomes easiest to perceive in the eighth stroke, in the empty crack after a bird departs, in the cool air beyond the fins, in the space between hunger and harm.

It is present wherever something ends without being erased.

And when at last the basin asks, through root and engine, mist and animal, what it must now manufacture, the power gives no design.

It opens the future and does not fill it.
