The power does not descend.

Descent would be an accusation here, a claim that one direction is truer than another. It does not arrive in light, because the country has light enough. It does not take a body, because bodies are quickly mistaken for tools, mountains, axes, or causes.

Its attention comes first.

The slate fields detect it. Across the shelf, millions of flakes turn at once—not toward a single point, but broadside to every possible direction. For the first time their edges cease scraping. The harsh whisper stops.

Water hangs where it has condensed.

Echoes waiting in hollows lose their borrowed voices.

Even the upright reflection above the reservoir becomes still.

The power learns the country by obeying it.

When a pressure gathers in the ears, it lies flat. When thunder comes from below, it places a hand against old stone. It does not count the peaks. It does not step into any pool whose reflection hesitates. Where the cairns show six stones, it acknowledges seven; where they show seven, it does not demand to know which one has gone.

It enters the shelters by turning itself narrow.

It sees the raised ledges, the drainage trenches, the copper wire, the mineral tools. It touches nothing until rain has passed and the shallow warnings in the walls have filled with water. Then it reads them in the order the builders intended: not left to right, nor past to future, but danger to survival.

The country attends to this courtesy.

Pillars permit their visible shapes to settle more firmly inside their discarded forms. Pools give clear notes under copper. The distant ranges remain where they appear to be. For a while, no direction crosses the land.

The power could make the place fertile.

It could lay soil in the basins and seed the wet stone with green. It could teach spores to endure the cold brilliance. It could give the pools mouths, the slate eyes, the echoes throats of their own. It could place a sun in the sky and oblige every shadow to agree.

It does none of these things.

Life is not the cure for every lifelessness. A forest would not complete this country. Moss would not explain it. Birds would be injured by its distances, roots confused by stone that remembers being unbroken, and every creature born there would inherit dangers for which hunger and fear had no language.

Nor does the power still the waterfalls, seal the fractures, quiet the mountains, or force erosion to choose one direction. These are not wounds merely because they are strange.

It listens for suffering.

That takes longer.

Pain in such a place does not cry out. It repeats.

It is the footstep that follows every traveler, unable to arrive at the journey that made it. It is the river-roar fastening itself for centuries to one insignificant crack. It is the warm stone shouldering again and again the weight of burial because warmth has reminded it, not because the mountain truly lies above.

It is distance closing around a spoken number and keeping the intervening days with nowhere to occur.

It is the shapes in the reservoir ascending whenever the dark column rises, never reaching its end.

It is the shelters retaining the pressure of bodies that no longer need shelter.

The power discovers that the country remembers perfectly but cannot release. Even what erosion removes remains as contour, echo, procedure, reversed water, raised handprint, returning fragment. Nothing is wholly lost, and therefore nothing is wholly allowed to end.

The country does not suffer from death.

It suffers from the absence of departure.

So the power goes to the central ring.

Rain begins everywhere at once. Droplets strike the pillars, run through the ancient grooves, and pour into the reservoir. Depth increases without volume. The copper cups tremble on their pegs. Far below, or high overhead, or behind the eyes of the listening stone, a pebble continues its ancient fall.

The power places its hand over one of the carved hands.

Its hand has four fingers.

Not because that is its nature. Because that is the shape in which it has been addressed.

At noon-brightness, the reservoir raises its column of dark water. It climbs a hundred meters, then two hundred, its surface wavering in the cold white air. Vacant figures appear inside it. Some are smaller than the copper cups. Some are too large to have stood within the ring. They ascend by remaining still.

As always, none is near the top.

The power does not pull them upward.

It does not reach into the column. It does not name them builders, dead, prisoners, ancestors, reflections, or rain. Names would give the distance between them a finished shape.

Instead it asks the country a question.

The question has no sound, because echoes would seize it. It has no light, because the sky would distribute it across every surface. It is asked as a small change in pressure, gentler than weather:

**Must everything remembered continue?**

The country has no voice with which to answer.

Water runs.

Frost waits beneath the pools.

Old stone maintains the certainty of its exposed face.

The upright column trembles.

Then, in one shelter far from the reservoir, the impression of an absent sleeper lifts from a stone ledge.

Only that.

No slab moves. No mark vanishes. Yet the ledge ceases to expect weight.

The release travels slowly.

A drainage trench forgets one rainstorm while retaining the knowledge of rain. A mallet loses the pressure of the final hand that held it but keeps the smoothness worn into its grip. One wet wall no longer waits for approaching footsteps. The echo nested there emerges as a single soft sound with no cause attached to it, crosses the passage, and ends.

The country has never heard a sound end willingly.

The slate fields begin to whisper, but not in alarm.

At the reservoir, one figure reaches the top.

There is no struggle. The water does not lower and the figure does not rise faster. The distance between the figure and the upper surface simply ceases to be an obligation.

For an instant the shape stands in the open brightness.

It is not a body. It has no face, no skin, no recoverable name. It is an arrangement of vacancies maintained too long by water and expectation.

The power offers it neither restoration nor judgment.

Only departure.

The shape opens like a hand.

Its hollows fill with the surrounding brilliance, and it is gone—not eroded, not reversed into mineral, not distributed through the shelf. Gone in a way the country has never permitted before.

No trace appears on the pillars.

Then another figure reaches the top. And another.

Some depart.

Some fall outward as ordinary rain.

Some descend willingly into the reservoir and become depth without memory.

Some remain. The power does not compel them. Mercy that cannot be refused is only another direction passing over the land.

For the length of that brightness, the column gives each shape the same choice.

When it finally breaks, rain moves across the shelf unevenly for the first time. It falls first inside the ring, then among the slate fields, then over the distant terraces. The showers overlap and wander. Some places remain dry. Others receive rain twice.

The established channels speak.

Under the rain, new pictographs appear on several shelter walls. No hand carves them. Mineral darkens in old scratches until a simple sequence can be seen:

A closed hand.

An open hand.

An empty place.

The meaning is not *death*. The builders had understood death.

The meaning is:

**What is held may be let go without having been lost.**

From then on, little changes.

The sky remains cold and brilliant. Water still beads around empty points and falls sideways. Frost rises in chiming plates. Mountains appear through wounds in the ground. Directions continue to cross the country, and travelers must still lie flat.

Remembering stone remains dangerous. Silent streams restore tools past usefulness and return pebbles to cliffs. A spoken count may still close a distance. Echoes still migrate, seeking events to inhabit.

The power has not made the country safe.

Safety was not what it lacked.

But now an echo may abandon a false cause. A warm stone may remember burial without resuming its weight. A distance, once closed, may open long enough for the missing days to pass. In the shelters, absent bodies sometimes ease their pressure from the sleeping ledges.

And once in each cycle, when brightness loses its distance and the sky comes down among the rocks, a new interval occurs.

It is not night.

The country has no use for night.

It is a moment in which nothing is required to attend.

The slate flakes rest without facing anywhere. Water holds no image. The pillars possess only their present contours. The mountains do not remember being sediment, and the pebbles do not anticipate return. Even the shelf suspends its labor of deciding which side is outside.

The interval is brief.

No more than the time needed for a copper cup to stop ringing.

Then attention resumes. Rain assembles. Stone answers stone. Frost sharpens itself beneath water. Echoes enter the passages, and distances open their clear, perilous lengths.

The power does not remain as ruler, guardian, or sun.

It leaves no throne and raises no temple. It asks for no gratitude from stone. Before withdrawing, it places one object beside the reservoir: a shallow copper cup, green with an age it has not lived.

The cup is empty.

It cannot be filled by rain, reflection, depth, or light.

But anything placed within it may cease, if it wishes.

The country keeps it carefully.

Not hidden.

Not worshiped.

Within reach.
