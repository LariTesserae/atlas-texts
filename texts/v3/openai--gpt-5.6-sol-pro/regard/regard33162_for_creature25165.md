At first, the power does nothing.

This is not hesitation. It has seen worlds harmed by help arriving too quickly: rivers straightened for their own good, forests relieved of fire until they could survive only by burning all at once, minds spared every grief until they became unable to choose what mattered. Benevolence without patience is another form of appetite.

So the power attends.

Attention is not harmless on the plateau. Paths brighten beneath intention. Vapor gathers around sustained regard. The old arch tilts its impossible shadow toward any future examined too closely. Even a custodian standing beside a pillar must account for the pressure of being interested in it.

The power therefore reduces its attention until it resembles distance.

It looks through quartz grains, each for less than the duration of a flash. It listens in the pauses between the pressure-signals of grazing herds. It receives the plateau through surfaces that will soon be shed: a ray-creature’s larval skin, the soluble membrane of a veil-plant, the outer mineral layer of a custodian’s arm. No single place bears enough of its regard to be altered.

Years pass in this manner.

It learns that the plateau is not waiting to be completed.

Its sparseness is not deprivation. Its silence is not loneliness. The ray-creatures do not require easier flight, nor the grazers richer pasture, nor the reed-trees continuous rain. To increase any one abundance would draw down another. More water would awaken too much life at once. More shade would erase the mineral films on which the smallest grazers feed. Fewer predators would make the herds numerous, then starving. To make the plateau generous in the manner of lowlands would be to destroy the specific mercy by which it endures.

The power also learns that the custodians do not need revelation.

They already know they are not masters. They already understand that a repair may take longer than the one who begins it. They know how to leave a process unfinished without abandoning it. They know that uncertainty can be tended.

There is little a wise power can teach such beings without first becoming their student.

And so it studies the condensation pillars inviting clouds by fractions. It follows water through folded chambers whose interior distances exceed the width of the basins containing them. It observes reed networks choosing which trunks to preserve. It watches young grazers learn the color of the horizon. It remains beside predators during their long mineral stillness and beside transparent larvae whose entire season lasts less than one rain.

Only then does it notice the injury.

The injury is not in the pillars, though several lean farther than their makers intended. It is not in the pools, though one occasionally returns the reflection of an animal that has not yet come to drink. It is not even in the arch.

It is in the relationship between the arch and a custodian who has watched it too long.

This custodian crosses the eastern field once each circuit. Mineral bands on its body record western ocher, ravine umber, the pale dust of the high shelves, and at last the black luster of the slabs. It has no spoken name. Its name begins with the route of a cloud that failed to descend in the season of its emergence. It continues through a damaged pool whose boundaries it restored over nine years. It includes the particular future-shadow cast by a dead reed-tree before the tree had died.

The custodian can distinguish possibilities that others cannot.

At first this is useful. It sees where erosion will open a channel and lays diagnostic fibers before the first grain moves. It feels when a folded pool is beginning to mistake one year for another. Dangerous ground loses contrast in its presence, and younger custodians follow the direction of its calm.

But the arch does not merely show futures. It acquires habits from those who watch them.

For centuries the custodian has returned to the black field and attended most closely to damaging possibilities: pillar failures, vanished cloud routes, reed networks severed by heat, fractures entering folded volumes. This is its duty. Yet attention near the arch is a physical preference, and duty repeated long enough has become a kind of gravity.

The arch has begun to favor futures in which disaster is legible.

Not disaster itself. It possesses no malice. It simply makes the plateau more likely to approach conditions that can be foreseen, measured, and corrected. Unpredictable flourishing is being lost along with unpredictable harm. Rain falls more often where old models expect it. Herds inherit increasingly exact routes. New pearl threads rise only near existing structures. Erosion approaches its selected forms without the former hesitation.

Nothing is failing.

That is why the injury is difficult to see.

The plateau is becoming perfect in a way that will eventually make it unable to become anything else.

The custodian senses this but believes the error to be its own. It attempts to compensate by imagining futures in which its work is unnecessary. The effort cannot succeed. Every imagined absence still contains the one imagining it. At the arch, the custodian’s movements grow fractionally misplaced. Its hand withdraws before danger exists. Reflective patches spread across its skin, showing skies weeks old or hours ahead. It sleeps less often in the rooms beneath the ledges because the rooms return memories it has not yet lived.

No other custodian remains near enough to understand. Two observers would distort the field further.

Here the power decides to act.

It does not enter the arch. To do so would fill every reflective surface on the plateau with one overwhelming future.

It does not heal the custodian by removing its strange perception. The perception is not a sickness separable from the knowledge, patience, and care that have grown around it.

It does not announce itself. A voice from the sky would teach every creature the habit of looking upward for rescue.

Instead, during an ordinary dawn, the power allows one event to occur without consequence.

A veil-plant opens beside the black field.

No veil-plant has grown there before. Its buried body has no thread leading to a known parent. Its hoop is small, scarcely wider than a grazer’s foot. The membrane forms as usual, trembles with dew, and pours the gathered water into the stone.

But the water goes nowhere.

It does not enter a folded chamber. It does not join a fracture. It is not taken by a root, raised into vapor, stored, redirected, or delayed. For one moment it rests in an unmodified hollow, subject to no preference beyond its own cohesion.

Then it evaporates.

The arch fails to anticipate it.

Across the black slabs, future-shadows shiver. One points toward a cloudless place. Another divides around an object that never appears. The selected geometry of the gullies loosens by less than the width of a cell.

The custodian stands beside the vanished dew and draws back its sensory veil.

The power does not give an explanation. It offers a second inconsequence.

A loose stone turns downslope and stops with its broad face out of alignment.

Then a grazer leaves its inherited path, not because of danger or food, but because a juvenile’s untrained back reflects a color that does not belong to the sky. Three others follow. Their deviation becomes no new route. By evening the stone is still crooked and the grazers have returned to the herd.

The custodian lowers itself until many boneless digits rest near the ground.

It understands.

Not what the power is. Not where it has come from. Those questions are too large to be useful. It understands only the form of the gift.

The plateau has been given a margin in which events need not become instructions.

The power widens this margin carefully.

It does not establish a region beyond the system, which would become either sanctuary or wound. Instead it distributes tiny freedoms throughout what already exists.

One dawn in a hundred, a condensation pillar permits dew to form on the less favored side.

One ray-larva wakes under conditions that should keep it sealed and finds an air current sufficient to bear it.

One reed-tree sends water toward no stressed neighbor, but into a dormant branch that produces a shape the grove has not used before.

A predator abandons an excellent intersection.

A pool reflects only what stands before it.

A path remains difficult despite the intention to follow it.

These events are rare. Most produce nothing. Some are immediately erased by heat, hunger, or ordinary chance. A few persist.

The system does not break. It breathes.

The arch’s influence relaxes from command into invitation. Gullies still tend toward clean branching ratios, but occasionally one hesitates around resistant stone and discovers another form. Herd rhythms remain inheritable, though now they contain pauses whose meanings have been lost. Pearl threads continue rising near old pillars, and also, after many centuries, beneath an empty shelf no custodian had marked.

The power then does one further thing, not for the plateau but for the custodian.

It speaks the custodian’s name.

Because the name is relational, it cannot be spoken as sound. The power must demonstrate it across distance.

At the western rim, a cloud that once failed to descend gathers itself and releases a narrow line of mist.

In the restored pool, a surface tension trembles in the exact sequence the custodian used during the ninth year of repair.

At the dead reed-tree, suspended water forms inside a sealed segment and reflects the future-shadow by which the custodian has long been known.

Along the Long Incline, paths pale one after another—not leading anywhere, but tracing the first seasonal circuit of its life. Pillars alter their tones as the demonstration passes. Reed networks tighten. Roosting rays open their pores in a slow wave. Grazers pause with their testing feet against the stone.

No creature understands the whole event.

The custodian does.

For the first time, its name is completed without its having to travel the distance of it.

The dark sensory field closes. Its body, narrow from years of attention, unfolds into a resting geometry rarely used outside mist. Heat leaves its tissues. The mineral bands along its arms loosen and fall as dust.

It sleeps there beside the arch.

While it sleeps, the power does not guard it from all danger. A benevolence that makes vulnerability impossible also makes trust meaningless. It merely enters the probabilities as lightly as a veil-plant enters dawn. The nearest predator chooses another intersection. Noon heat passes downward through a black slab. A solitary cloud crosses the sun long enough to preserve the moisture in the custodian’s open tissues.

The custodian wakes late in the day.

Its future-sight has not been removed. The old burden remains possible. The arch still requires watching, the pools still accumulate errors, clouds still fail to descend, and every living system continues to purchase its endurance with countless small deaths.

But the custodian now perceives one category it could not perceive before.

It sees futures that do not need to be prevented, corrected, selected, or understood.

It leaves the black field before sunset.

Behind it, the small veil-plant stands naked on its hoop. Whether it will open again is uncertain. The power does not ensure that it will.

Having acted, the power withdraws its attention by degrees. Not entirely. Complete departure would itself be a kind of violence after being recognized. It remains available at the edge of consequence, listening for consent expressed in the plateau’s own languages: a path that refuses to brighten, a pillar that rejects adjustment, a room that stays bare, a custodian who chooses to stand beneath the open sky.

Most seasons, it does nothing.

Clouds climb the escarpment thin and exhausted. Ray-creatures enter them. Water travels through hidden distances. Grazers tap old rhythms into stone. Predators wait. Custodians cross the shelves one by one, negotiating with tendencies. The arch shows later skies and possible shadows.

Yet here and there, the land retains a slight capacity to surprise its own wisdom.

A channel turns where no selected form requires it.

A creature survives by making the wrong choice.

A custodian rests before the work is finished.

And sometimes, after dawn has withdrawn all visible moisture, a single drop remains on a bare black slab. It reflects neither the present sky nor a future one.

It reflects only light, without using the light to predict what comes next.
