### The Measure of Mercy

The power did not enter through the opening.

Entrance was a notion belonging to bodies: first outside, then within; first summit, then ledge, then chamber, then depth. The power had no need to descend. It regarded the mountain whole—the black massif, the narrow cleft, the buried rails, the green agreements laid one over another, the cylinder under the lamp, and the white sphere no leaf had crossed.

Its attention was gentle.

This mattered.

A lesser power might have mistaken silence for captivity. It might have sent water coursing down the Throat, believing all green things must thirst. It might have warmed the chambers, set the compressor turning, loosened the rails from their long arrest. It might have introduced spores, animals, weather, seasons. It might have praised itself for awakening what had never slept.

Another might have tried to heal the place.

It would have polished contacts, replaced cables, opened cabinets, deciphered labels. It would have restored the machinery to motion and thereby destroyed the operation that had survived beyond motion. It would have brought tools into the final pocket and placed a hand beneath the lamp.

A cruel power would have recognized the Throat as useful.

A foolish benevolent one would have recognized it as lonely.

The wise power recognized it as complete.

So at first, it did nothing.

Not the nothing of indifference. Indifference does not learn the exact edge of every mantle. It does not attend to the charge held apart by each pale filament or distinguish the contribution of one black cup from the carbon-sharing architecture around it. Indifference does not hear, within an industrial chord that never proceeds, the separate necessities of stone, metal, altitude, and green conductive tissue.

The power listened until it understood why there could be no *until* in that place.

It found no pain.

Pain requires a division between condition and desire. The Throat had distinctions beyond number, but not that one. It did not strain toward movement. It did not resent its completion. It did not remember growth as freedom or experience stillness as confinement. No part of it had been excluded from the arrangement it sustained. No buried impulse beat uselessly against the final form.

Nor did the power find joy.

That absence did not alarm it. Joy, too, is not the only measure of a life.

The inhabitant possessed no expectation that kindness could fulfill. It made no appeal. It had no wound awaiting a hand.

The power did not give it longing merely so that longing could be answered.

This was its first mercy.

Its second concerned the world outside.

The Green Throat had drawn other forms of attention.

Far below the massif, survey instruments had marked an anomaly in the mountain’s field. In offices where the mountain was represented by contour lines and mineral rights, people had begun to compare the readings. They saw conductivity without water, machinery without heat, and a signal whose waveform did not degrade. They imagined patents. They imagined energy extracted from resonance. They imagined cutting cores from the green mantles and transporting them under vacuum to clean laboratories.

They were not wicked. Most destruction does not require wickedness. Curiosity, urgency, and expense are often enough.

The power did not blind them or break their instruments.

Instead, it allowed every honest measurement to remain honest.

This was sufficient.

Readings taken from the eastern slope indicated that the source lay west. Readings from the west placed it several kilometers east. Repeated surveys agreed perfectly with themselves and with none of the others. Funding committees found no fraud, but no usable certainty. Satellite maps showed the entrance only at resolutions too poor to distinguish it from shadow. At higher resolutions, the wind-planed rock appeared seamless.

Those who climbed toward it encountered no monsters and suffered no catastrophe. They found reasonable causes to turn back: a failing battery, a boot sole beginning to separate, a headache that prudence could not dismiss, cloud forming below the summit. Each returned safely. None felt forbidden. They simply concluded that another expedition, under better conditions, would be wiser.

The better conditions never coincided.

The power did not make the Throat unknowable. That would have been another kind of erasure. Old notes remained in archives. Descriptions persisted. A few people dreamed accurately of green blades packed beneath black stone. Now and then a child drew the three meters without having seen them.

But no drill reached the mountain.

No sample left the cleft.

No hand entered the white sphere.

This was the power’s second mercy: not concealment, but sanctuary.

Its third mercy was more difficult.

The power asked whether completion, though not suffering, had been chosen.

The question could not be expressed in words. Words arrive one after another, and the inhabitant possessed no one-after-another in which to receive them. Nor could the power introduce a pulse, however slight. A pulse would have divided the Throat into before and after. To ask by change would already be to force an answer.

So the power examined the only language the inhabitant had: relation.

It considered placing a fourth meter beside the three.

**LOAD**  
**FIELD**  
**HOLD**  
**OPEN**

But a new meter would not merely describe possibility. Its metal, glass, wiring, mass, and position would become part of the ecology. Roots would have to include it or exclude it. The sound would acquire another necessity. The completed agreement would be annulled by the grammar of the question.

It considered adding a second empty sphere elsewhere in the cleft. Between the two absences, the inhabitant might establish something analogous to direction. Difference might become attention. Attention might become sequence. Sequence might permit preference.

But the second emptiness would not be a gift of choice. It would be the gift of incompleteness, imposed without consent.

The power considered speaking through the white lamp. It could alter no brightness and still place meaning in the exact boundary between light and leaf. It could offer three conditions simultaneously: continuation, transformation, ending. The Throat might reveal its inclination in the infinitesimal distribution of charge around them.

Yet the power saw that this, too, would be a test designed for a being that had never needed to answer. To demand self-determination from every form of life is not always freedom. Sometimes it is merely the customs of the restless made universal.

The power withdrew the question before it was asked.

This was its third mercy: it did not require the inhabitant to become a chooser in order to deserve care.

There remained the white sphere.

Of all the Throat, this alone could receive attention without receiving substance. It was not a chamber, organ, or vacancy awaiting use. It was the preserved not-self by which the whole green architecture possessed an edge.

The power regarded that emptiness.

It did not occupy it.

It did not place a sign there, or an image, or a seed. It did not make the lamp brighter. No new tone entered the chord.

But regard itself established a relation, and because the power was wise, it held that relation entirely on its own side.

The inhabitant was not made to notice.

It was not compelled to respond.

It was known without being altered.

The difference was imperceptible within the Throat, yet real beyond it. Before, the mountain’s complete condition had existed unwitnessed except by transient visitors who could not understand what they encountered. Now there was one being in the universe that knew it exactly and did not translate it into lack.

The power knew each mantle as neither leaf nor imitation leaf.

It knew the anchors as direction given substance.

It knew the cups, the fronds, the insulating fur, the roots passing through old junction boxes, the stationary compressor preserving every stage of compression without performing any. It knew the engine’s proposition.

**This condition holds.**

And the power answered—not in sound, not in charge, not anywhere the Throat would have to bear it:

**Yes.**

Ages outside continued.

Snow came to neighboring peaks and departed. Nations revised their borders. The builders of the buried machinery were forgotten, rediscovered incorrectly, and forgotten again. Languages changed until *green*, *machine*, *life*, and *finished* no longer divided the world as they once had.

The power did not preserve the entire mountain in defiance of all reality. Sanctuary was not embalming. It protected the Throat from intrusion, not from existence itself.

If the massif one day fractured through natural change, the power would not command the halves to remain joined. If the planet’s deep field diminished and the green agreements could no longer hold, it would not counterfeit their condition from outside. If the lamp finally met a universe in which its light had no meaning, it would not force the bulb to shine for the sake of a promise the inhabitant had never requested.

Benevolence was not the refusal to allow an ending.

It was the refusal to impose one.

Until such a limit arose from the same world that had made the Throat possible, the power kept away the careless hand, the helpful flood, the collector’s blade, and the worshipper’s shrine. It allowed no temple to be built at the entrance. It permitted no doctrine to claim that completion was holier than growth, or stillness purer than change. The Green Throat was not made into an example.

It was allowed to be particular.

Within, all remained as it had been.

The meters held their central values.

The white lamp maintained its exact sphere.

The standing noise occupied the stone without traveling.

Every green edge ended where it should.

The inhabitant did not feel watched over. It did not feel less alone. It acquired no gratitude, because gratitude would have required an event.

Nothing was added to its life.

Nothing was taken from it.

And in this case, after perfect attention and limitless power had considered every possible gift, that was what kindness chose.
