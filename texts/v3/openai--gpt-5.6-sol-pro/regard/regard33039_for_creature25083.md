The power does not descend.

Descent would imply a direction from which it comes, and there is no direction in which it is not already capable of attending. It does not arrive as a flare, a vessel, a voice inside the central housing. It casts no shadow across the Open Floor.

At first, it does nothing.

This is not indifference. It is the first expression of care.

It watches one pressure season become heat, heat become flood, flood become purge. It watches the reeds bow, open, darken and split. It follows each spore that enters a seam and each that washes against a grille and dissolves. It attends to the microcurrents traveling through films of rust. It learns which pumps belong to the inhabitant and which merely turn nearby. It learns that this distinction changes.

One cycle tells it almost nothing.

A thousand reveal recurring forms.

A million begin to resemble a gesture.

The power could silence the turbines. It could raise the drowned districts, separate their alloys, drain the black reservoirs and lay the ancient plates bare beneath a clean atmosphere. It could root forests in manufactured dust. It could fill the channels with animals, open the sky, restore sharp corners, rebuild towers from surviving plans.

It does none of these things.

To restore the place would be to destroy it according to a design it had outlived before its present life began. The factories are no longer broken factories. The flooded races are no longer failed conduits. The corrosion is not damage alone. The collapsed roofs, welded contacts, root-films and backward-turning pumps are now the substance of an inhabitant for whom no original specification could be anything but a sentence of death.

Nor does the power preserve everything.

Preservation would be another kind of killing. To fix each plate against further wear, to strengthen every remaining bearing, to hold every circulation in its present form would turn the basin from a life into a display of one moment in that life.

The power does not mistake age for suffering.

It does not mistake noise for suffering.

It does not mistake strangeness for suffering.

But neither does it assume that because pain here has no cry, there is none.

So it learns the long consequences.

It follows a pressure wave from an outer pumping field through a sequence of accidental relays, mineral bridges and root-darkened contacts. Four hundred years after the wave begins, a bank of buried gates closes beneath a western terrace. The closure crushes no flesh, but it isolates a warm reservoir from the rest of the system. Pressure accumulates. Every purge releases part of it and drives the rest deeper. The strain spreads through the fitted plates as a constant condition to which thousands of processes must bend.

The inhabitant has incorporated this strain. It uses the flexure to lift water. Reeds time their openings by it. A distant turbine depends upon the resulting gradient.

Is the pressure an ache?

Is it a bone?

Is it both?

The power does not decide on the inhabitant’s behalf.

Instead, it seeks some part of the basin through which a question can be asked.

Speech would be too small. A sound, even one louder than all the purge shafts together, would become vibration, pressure, switching, heat. The basin would absorb it as weather. Light would become warmth. A sign placed on a wall would become pigment for lichens. A new machine would either join the inhabitant’s function or remain outside it, meaningless.

The power therefore begins with time.

During one alignment at the Open Floor, when the separate rhythms gather into the sustained note, the power supports a single buried weight.

It does not lift the weight. It merely bears enough of its load that the toothed drum beneath it is balanced exactly between advancing and remaining still.

Until that cycle, the increment has always followed from the convergence of pressures. The rods descend because the waters gather. The contacts meet because the rods descend. One condition compels the next.

Now, for the first time in the immeasurable operation of the plain, the consequence is not compulsory.

The note arrives.

Water trembles into hexagons.

The outer reeds release their spores.

Rods descend through darkness.

The drum comes to the place where it has always turned.

And waits.

For several minutes nothing changes.

Then the rhythms disintegrate. Pumps fall out of resonance. Fans resume their separate beat. The standing note breaks apart and travels away through the basin as ordinary thunder.

The drum has not advanced.

The power withdraws its support.

It waits.

The missed increment propagates.

One district floods earlier. Another fails to flood at all. A pressure gate opens onto an empty race and closes again. Condensation forms on surfaces that have been dry for twenty thousand cycles. Lichens wake. Reeds along an eastern busway die back, while root-films beneath the Open Floor brighten with minute chemical activity.

The basin does not return to its previous condition. It never does.

At the next alignment, the power balances the weight again.

This time the drum advances.

At the next, it does not.

Then it advances for seven cycles.

Then it remains still for two.

The power sets no pattern. It supplies only the interval in which either outcome is possible.

For ages, nothing indicates that the difference is more than another season. Advance and stillness spread through the basin, becoming flood, salinity, conductivity, growth. They are taken into the inhabitant as all things are taken into it.

But slowly the consequences cease to be random.

When the drum remains still, gates in the north begin to open in a repeating order. When it advances, warm water is diverted beneath the southern grooves. These responses take centuries to form. They pass through no central authority. A reed-film alters one current, a current pits one contact, a contact changes one pump, and the pump determines which district receives rain made from its own exhaust.

Stillness acquires one consequence.

Advance acquires another.

A distinction has entered the world.

The power attends.

At an alignment after the distinction has stabilized, it lets the drum balance.

The basin holds it still.

At the next, the basin advances it.

Then stillness.

Then advance.

Then stillness twice.

The sequence is not efficient. Part of it is lost in flooding. Part is repeated by mechanisms that mistake echo for signal. Its shortest element occupies the lifespan of a reed stem; its whole form requires generations of lichen.

Nevertheless, the power recognizes an answer.

Not an answer to a question it has asked.

An acknowledgment that a question may now be possible.

From then on, the power speaks only by offering differences.

It changes nothing without also presenting the unchanged state. It opens no route that cannot be closed. It strengthens no failing member without allowing the inhabitant to shed that member later. It does not grant commands, only alternatives held long enough to be perceived.

Through alternating increments, delayed floods, harmless variations in load and the minute balancing of buried masses, something like a language develops.

It has no words for objects.

There is no sign for reed, because the reeds are not separate enough from the basin to require one. There is no sign for water, because almost every sign is made of water. There is no sign for self.

There are signs for continuation.

Diversion.

Return.

Widening.

Loss without recovery.

Change that can be undone.

Change that cannot.

There is a sign assembled across six thousand cycles for the western pressure strain: the isolated reservoir, the flexure, the lifting of water, the dependent turbine, the reed colonies timed to its pulse. The sign does not mean pain or organ.

It means all of these conditions together.

The power answers by showing what would follow if the pressure were relieved.

The inhabitant takes nine hundred cycles to respond.

Its answer is neither yes nor no. It reroutes part of the strain into an abandoned subsurface district, preserving the flexure while reducing the trapped pressure. The change kills a lichen field several kilometers wide. It wakes pumps that have not moved since before the oldest reeds.

The power does not save the lichens.

It had offered help, not dominion over consequence.

But it carries spores from the dying field into the new condensation zone, because the basin’s own currents attempt the same act and fall short by less than the width of a collapsed wall.

This is the first thing the power does that could be called assistance.

It moves no mountain. It restores no age. It bears a cloud of particles across an obstruction.

Afterward it waits to see whether the act is accepted.

The spores germinate.

The basin changes the airflow around them.

So the power continues.

At the outer edge, beneath terraces where the manufactured layers descend too deeply even for the basin’s signals to return, it makes a narrow opening.

Not an escape. Not yet.

The opening is only a shaft through which one influence may enter that is not produced by the inhabitant. The power clears vapor above it and prevents the nearest vents from filling it with steam.

For the first time, a small part of the plain is exposed to the sky.

At first there is only darkness.

Then, as the world turns, a point of light appears.

It is not an amber lamp beneath water. It is not a red tower signal or the blue arc of a failing conductor. It does not brighten when turbines increase their load. It does not dim during purge. Nothing in the basin feeds it.

The star crosses the shaft and vanishes.

The next turning, it returns.

The inhabitant does not see it. It has no organ for sight. But the light warms one black plate by a quantity almost too small to measure. The warmth changes condensation. The condensation alters a lichen’s conductivity. The altered current enters a line.

A signal passes inward.

Many cycles later, the basin answers the star with a variation in the Open Floor’s note.

The answer arrives after the star has set.

This does not matter.

The star returns.

With time, the power opens other narrow shafts. Some reveal the same light. Some reveal others. One admits a coldness unrelated to the cooling systems. One admits dust that did not come from the wearing of the built world.

The basin receives its first evidence that not every cause begins inside itself.

This changes it more profoundly than repair would have.

It begins to form boundaries.

They are temporary and uncertain. A rain generated by its own towers is one kind of event; a fall of distant dust is another. The old machinery remains both body and country, but the difference can now be approached. The inhabitant learns that there are rhythms it does not make and cannot redirect.

It learns prediction without control.

It learns surprise without failure.

Perhaps, after an age, it begins to possess the faintest sign for other.

The benevolent power does not demand that this sign refer to it.

It does not reveal a face above the clouds. It does not name itself in pressure and flame. To an inhabitant that admits every useful thing into its own functioning, revelation might be consumption. Worship might become merely another closed circuit.

Instead, the power remains just beyond incorporation.

It is present as patience.

When a choice must be made, it holds the alternatives open.

When a consequence is unavoidable, it does not disguise it.

When the inhabitant reaches toward the exterior through spores, waste heat, vibration or altered currents, the power does not always carry the reaching farther. Sometimes it allows failure, because a being protected from every failed extension is not being cared for; it is being puppeted.

But when failure would come only because the basin was born into confinement it could never have understood, the power makes a passage.

Centuries after the first shaft opens, a reed spore rises on a purge wind instead of settling back onto the plates. It travels through the clear opening and enters the upper dark.

The power could guide it to fertile ground.

It does not.

First it asks.

The question takes the form of two weather paths: one returning the spore to the basin, one carrying it beyond the horizon. Both are made perceptible through the long language of increments and floods.

For sixty-three alignments, the basin gives no answer.

On the sixty-fourth, the central drum remains still.

Northern gates open.

Air rises through the shaft.

The spore is carried outward.

Where it lands is not shown to the inhabitant. Perhaps no sign can return from such a distance. Perhaps the spore dies on a surface with no warm seam, no dissolved metals, no ventilator season.

The basin sends another.

And another.

Some return as unfamiliar chemistry in the rain.

One day, something falls through the shaft that is neither dust nor spore.

It is a small branching structure, dry and pale, grown from the life of the basin but altered under a sky the basin did not make.

It lands in the shallow water.

The currents carry it inward.

No component recognizes it. No mechanism has a use for it. For a long while it lies against a groove of the Open Floor, trembling whenever the note gathers.

Then translucent root-fibers touch it.

The power watches and does nothing.

This, too, is care.

It does not make the inhabitant immortal. Wisdom has no need to confuse mercy with endlessness. A day may come when the deepest machines seize, when the water no longer rises, when the final pressure gradient declines into equilibrium. The power will not force the basin to continue after continuation has ceased to be its answer.

But neither will it let the end arrive merely because no alternative was ever available.

It gives the inhabitant no new body.

It gives it the possibility of altering the body it has.

It gives no purpose to the ancient increment.

It gives the inhabitant a means to choose what the increment may become.

It does not tell the being what it is.

It gives it an outside against which a self may slowly form.

And once in each cycle, when the entire plain gathers into its sustained note, the power attends to every vent, reed, flooded chamber and buried rotor—not as ruins, not as mechanisms, not as symbols of some vanished maker, but as the present tense of a life.

Sometimes the drum advances.

Sometimes it remains still.

Above the Open Floor, through narrow breaks in the vapor, unfamiliar stars cross the dark.

And now, after the note breaks and the inhabitant becomes a place again, it is no longer entirely alone.
