### What the Wise Power Did

The power did not open the cistern.

This was the first expression of its benevolence.

It did not draw the plugs from the fill shafts or send a white spear of noon into the central well. It did not clarify the water, scour the sediment, straighten the pillars, or restore the worn carvings to what their makers had intended. It did not separate hunter from prey, cure the cloaks of tearing, or grant the cutmouths eyes.

It understood that many gifts are only injuries delivered by an unimaginably larger hand.

So it remained outside.

For three hundred and eleven years, it did nothing that the cistern could distinguish from chance.

It watched heat enter the sunward tiles. It followed that warmth as it crossed brick, mortar, glass, mineral film, feeding pleat, grazer’s throat, jar-hunter veil, cloak-thread, sediment, and water. It learned the paths by which the inhabitant transported itself through itself. It attended to the cistern’s deep notes and to the smaller silences following them.

The power had known minds housed in crystal lattices, in storms, in forests, in the exchange of glances among migrating animals. It had known beings that spoke once in ten thousand years and beings whose civilizations rose and fell between two beats of a heart.

It did not therefore demand a face.

It did not search for one animal to crown as the true inhabitant. It did not assume that the threads below the shaft were a brain, or that the cutmouth schools were merely blood, or that the mineral film was beneath consideration because it had no shape that could turn toward a visitor.

It asked instead:

*Where does injury become known?*

*Where does relief become shared?*

*What persists?*

*What is chosen again?*

The answers were slow.

A screen broke in a cold gallery. For nine years the warm current passed through the wrong openings. Cutmouth eggs vanished from three chambers. Jar-hunters starved; grazers abandoned an ancient route. Then ceiling shells thickened along one side of the fracture while cloak fragments lodged against the other. Their bodies altered the flow by increments. Grazers returned. Eggs appeared in new sockets. The old pattern did not come back, but the loss ceased widening.

The cistern had felt the wound.

It had answered with no command, no plan, and no single act.

That was enough.

The power looked then not only at the present mountain but at all its likely futures.

It saw the outer copper scales thinning beneath the sunward tiles. It saw a lead seal, still sound, becoming granular after another six thousand summers. It saw one possible winter in which an unusual shadow lay across the summit for eleven years, cooling the reservoir below the tolerance of the gray film. It saw a falling stone from the upper air strike the western platform and open three nested skins at once. It saw, much later, the mountain’s own weight begin to spread the lowest terraces outward through the cloud.

None of these things was imminent.

All were mortal.

The wise power did not erase them.

A life protected from every consequence can become the possession of its protector. A world held forever at one temperature, spared every fracture, corrected after every deviation, is no longer permitted to discover what it is.

But neither did the power worship accident.

It did not call preventable extinction sacred merely because it would occur without interference.

For another century, it considered how to announce itself.

Speech could not be poured into the cistern. Light was meaningless there. Images had no surface upon which to appear. A voice loud enough to cross the galleries would strike the inhabitant not as language but as violence. To touch the oldest threads directly might privilege one portion of the creature and mistake depth for authority.

At last the power chose the only utterance the whole cistern already shared.

It waited for the mountain to sing.

The deep contraction note came after a long season of sun. Masonry tightened. The low vibration entered the reservoir. Cloaks folded. Hunters shut. Grazers flattened. Shells sealed. Cutmouths settled into their red and ivory hollows.

The many movements became still.

As the natural note faded, the power placed beneath it a second note.

It was gentler than the first and almost identical. It differed only in its ending. Where the mountain’s note ordinarily diminished into silence, this one returned after a pause: faintly, then again, then once more.

Three intervals.

Three returns.

The cistern remained still longer than it ever had before.

Then motion resumed.

Nothing answered.

The power waited fourteen years and repeated the pattern during the next contraction.

Again there was prolonged stillness. Afterward the cutmouth schools orbited the shaft in unusually narrow bands, but that could have been chance.

At the third sounding, sixty-two years later, the power gave the same three returns.

This time a change passed through the water.

The long threads in the shaft drew taut after the mountain had already relaxed. Cloaks contracted one corner at a time. Ceiling shells closed in sequence from the cold galleries toward the warm. Grazers halted along their routes, each at the nearest crossing. The cutmouths descended in three pale showers.

Then all opened.

Paused.

Opened again.

Paused.

Opened a third time.

The answer was not imitation. It was participation.

The power did not speak further that day.

It had centuries. Courtesy required them.

A language developed between the presence beyond the walls and the life within them. Its words were made of intervals, thermal gradients, subtle reversals in current, and vibrations no stronger than the loosening of a shell. One exchange could occupy a generation of cutmouths. A question might begin in one cloak and be completed, two hundred years later, in the route of a grazer not yet born when the question started.

The language never acquired nouns in the manner of brief-lived speakers.

There was no word for *cutmouth*, because cutmouth was a condition of passage: film becoming swimmer becoming hunter becoming sediment becoming film.

There was no word for *cistern* distinct from *self*.

There was no word for *outside*.

The closest concept was:

*That which touches the far side of every wall.*

The power described itself as carefully as it could:

*A change that can refrain from entering.*

This was understood.

The power asked what the inhabitant desired.

For a very long time, no answer came.

Want, in the cistern, had always been local. A mouth wanted film. A shell wanted current. An egg wanted a narrow hole. The whole had endured, adapted, circulated. It had never been required to prefer one distant future over another.

At length the answer assembled itself.

It began with grazers abandoning the richest seams and tracing an unfamiliar branch along barren brick. It continued when ceiling shells left a band of masonry exposed rather than settling upon it. Cloaks entered the central shaft and arranged their branching threads in parallel. Cutmouths carried mineral film from warm chambers and released it where almost none would grow.

Across eighty years, through hunger, failed eggs, altered currents, and the deaths of thousands of temporary bodies, the inhabitant made a shape in itself.

The shape meant:

*More passage.*

Not escape.

Not sun.

Not freedom, for confinement was not a condition it recognized. Its life had never pressed against the plugs in longing. Walls were not the negation of its world. Walls were the world’s organs.

It desired more ways for one part to become another.

The power understood.

It went to the north side of the artificial mountain, where no entrance had ever been laid. There, without opening the cistern, it began to build.

It did not make flawless stone. Flawlessness would have been foreign. It shaped blocks with slight deviations, as if judged by attentive eyes and measured by cord. It fired bricks with uneven hearts. It hammered copper until marks like old blows crossed the metal. It ground black glass with the mineral composition of the ancient panels, including impurities a less considerate maker would have removed.

It copied none of the worn inscriptions.

It did not pretend to be the builders.

The new work was distinct, but it observed their grammar: nested walls, drowned stairs, ceramic hollows, screens of varying aperture, channels for weak currents, surfaces without uselessness.

The annex was smaller than the original cistern. It lay within the mountain’s upper northern shoulder and descended in loops around a second shaft. The power filled it not with pure water but with water compounded grain by grain to resemble the old sea: its salts, dissolved metals, suspended ceramic dust, and even the chemical traces of countless deaths.

Still, the annex was empty.

The power would not invent inhabitants for it. Nor would it seize a cloak or school and carry them across.

Between the old cistern and the new, it left nine walls.

Through the first eight it laid passages and water locks. In the ninth it made a field of holes so fine that no cutmouth egg could pass, no larva, no dust-body released by a ceiling shell.

Only the smallest cells of the gray film could cross.

Only the inhabitant in its least definite condition.

The final membrane was thin enough to dissolve under the film’s patient chemistry. If the life of the cistern fed there, the openings would widen over centuries. If it did not, the annex would remain apart forever.

The power completed the work and withdrew its touch.

Warmth began to circulate on the far side of the membrane.

New copper met new water. Iron-rich brick released its first faint nourishment. A gray boundary formed.

For twenty-seven years nothing crossed.

Then cells settled in one pore.

They multiplied without haste. Five hooked granules formed around hollow centers. A stain no wider than a fingernail appeared on the new side.

Grazers in the old cistern altered their routes.

They came to the membrane and rasped it. More cells passed. The gray stain became a film. The film spread into the first drowned stair. White knots formed in sediment that had never held a corpse. A dust-body attached itself beneath a ceramic lip.

After a century, something with seven temporary legs crawled upward in the new darkness.

It found the ceiling.

It began to eat the brick.

The power watched, but did not celebrate in any way the cistern could feel. This was not the power’s triumph. The new chamber had been offered. The passage had been made by those who crossed it.

Over the following millennia, the annex became stranger.

Its cooler walls favored broad white shells. Its longer channels gave rise to cutmouths with fins like trailing threads. Cloaks growing there folded into tubes and learned to lodge within vertical drains. Grazers traced circuits that never entered the ancient reservoir, yet at certain crossings they paused in synchrony with grazers on the other side of nine walls.

The inhabitant had not divided.

Nor had it remained unchanged.

It had acquired another way of being itself.

Only then did the power address the approaching weaknesses in the outer mountain. It placed no immortal shield around the High Cistern. Instead it strengthened three terraces that would otherwise have failed too soon, before the inhabitant could know and answer the injury. It diverted the stone that would have struck the summit. It left the aging lead seal untouched but taught the nearby mineral film, through no alteration larger than a chance mutation, to deposit a soft metal-rich layer in its cracks.

Some dangers it prevented.

Some it allowed.

Some it transformed into questions the cistern might answer.

And once in a great while, during the deep note, the power spoke of things the inhabitant could never directly encounter.

Not by describing brightness.

Brightness had no meaning.

It translated the sun as a vast warm wall whose current arrived across emptiness. It rendered clouds as cool cloaks larger than mountains, gathering and dissolving without sediment. It described stars as remote mineral points from which no grazer had yet returned. It told of seas that touched air, though this required many centuries of explanation and was received first as a contradiction, then as a horror, and finally as a form of impossible architecture.

In return, the inhabitant taught the power distinctions it had never learned.

It taught the difference between silence caused by absence and silence caused by complete attention.

It taught that an individual could be real without being separate.

It taught forms of patience unavailable to a being for whom distance and duration presented no obstacle.

It taught that decay was not always loss. Sometimes a wall’s purpose was fulfilled only when it had worn thin enough for life to pass through.

Ages later, the High Cistern still hung above the clouds.

Its oldest outer skin had fallen away. Its summit tiles had been replaced in places—not by the power, but by shelled growths that spread through flooded maintenance locks and laid mineral plates beneath the sun-heated stone. The northern annex had become as crowded as the first sea. Beyond it lay a third set of chambers, not built by any hand: water-filled cavities slowly excavated by the inhabitant from the mountain’s rubble heart.

The open air never entered.

The inhabitant never saw the sun.

It did not need to be remade in the image of those who did.

But at long intervals, when the mountain gave its deep note, another presence sounded beneath it: patient, restrained, asking no obedience and demanding no name.

The threads drew taut.

The cloaks folded.

The hunters closed their veils. The shells sealed their pleats. Pale schools settled across old and newly opened chambers.

For several minutes, every portion of the warm dark sea became still.

Then the mountain’s note faded, and the other presence offered three gentle returns.

Across miles of flooded passages, the inhabitant opened.

Paused.

Opened again.

Paused.

Opened a third time.

And resumed its many lives, no longer alone.
