The power does not descend.

Descent would impose a direction the basin does not possess. Arrival would divide its existence into before and after, and the Stillgreen Basin has harmed no one merely by being whole.

So the power remains at the rim.

It looks without observation, because observation here is pressure. It listens without requiring an answer. It understands the green filaments in all their locations, the mica creatures in all their permissible concealments, the counters whose journeys are single gestures, and the long warm absence winding through the mineral forest.

It understands that the basin is alive.

It also understands that life need not resemble longing.

There is no imprisoned voice beneath the equilibrium. No buried child beats against the crystal, begging for tomorrow. The basin does not suffer from changelessness as a creature of blood would suffer. Its single moment is not brief to it. The moment is immeasurably deep. Every relation within it is apprehended from every side. What temporal beings call repetition does not occur there, because nothing repeats. Everything simply is, with a fullness that duration can neither improve nor exhaust.

The power therefore does not break the fixed sun.

It does not bring rain.

It does not make the mineral fronds flower, for flowers would not be a blessing where nothing needs descendants. It does not give mouths to the counters, eyes to the unseen animal, or memory to the clear pillars. It does not mistake difference for deficiency.

For a long while—though no long while passes in the basin—the power does nothing.

Then it notices the rim.

The rim is slight, nearly imaginary, but it is not wholly false. Travelers can cross it. Beyond it they belong to histories: they have births, unfinished sentences, people awaiting their return. Within it, the basin begins to include them in its perfect statement. Their footprints vanish. Their possible routes become equally exact. Their faces enter the foil rosettes. Given enough exposure, they may cease to be visitors and become eternal relations among stones.

The basin does not intend cruelty. Intention would require alternatives considered in sequence. It is only hospitable according to its nature.

But a hospitality from which one cannot depart is not mercy.

Here the power acts.

It does not alter the basin. Instead, it lays around the basin a second boundary, finer than distance and gentler than a wall. The boundary excludes no mineral root, reflects no sunlight, and casts no line upon the pale ground. Nothing inside can detect it, because it changes nothing inside.

It applies only to those who enter from time.

Upon crossing, every traveler keeps one small possession that the basin cannot make simultaneous:

the right to have come from somewhere.

Their past remains joined to them by a thread the equilibrium may bend but cannot absorb. Memories can still become contradictory within the lowlands, yet beneath the contradictions persists a quiet orientation—not north, not outward, but *homeward*. If the traveler truly wishes to leave, the nearest lane becomes a road. Six steps or six thousand kilometers later, the foot falls beyond the rim. The sun moves. Dust remains disturbed. The traveler returns to consequence.

No one is forced out.

Some may choose the inexhaustible moment. Some may place a hand in the warm invisible passage and recognize, with relief, a form of existence for which they have always been homesick. The power protects that choice too. It will not declare that all souls require futures.

Having done this, the power turns its attention again to the basin itself.

To offer change would be dangerous. A being without sequence cannot consent in the ordinary way. A question asked aloud would already wound it: first ignorance, then answer. To install desire would manufacture a lack and call the lack a gift.

Yet never to offer would also be a judgment—that the basin must remain what it is because it has never imagined otherwise.

The wise power finds another way.

At the rim, outside and inside at once, it makes a small open place among the upright filaments. It does not clear them. It does not bend or burn or uproot them. The place has always been empty from the basin’s perspective, but from the world beyond, it has just appeared.

In that place the power sets no object.

It sets a possibility.

Possibility has no color. It weighs nothing. Instruments would find only warm air and pale, sharp-cornered grains. But the entire basin apprehends the open place as it apprehends every part of itself, and within its flawless proposition there now rests a term that is not a contradiction, not an error, and not yet a fact.

The term means:

**You need not become.  
You are not required to remain.  
Nothing is wrong with you.  
If ever you desire an afterward, I will keep the first change from destroying the whole.**

The basin cannot answer, because an answer would follow the offer.

So the power does not wait for one.

It leaves the possibility where it is and gives it neither pressure nor expiration. It may remain unused forever, in which case the gift becomes indistinguishable from perfect respect. The mineral forest will continue beneath its ordinary blue sky. The counters will pass in motions without beginnings. The great absence will press warmly through its lane. Every black rosette will receive and return the same sunlight.

Or the basin may choose.

If it does, the change will be almost nothing.

Not rain. Rain would be too violent.

Not sunset. Darkness would terrify a world that has never lost the light.

Perhaps one green filament will retain a single photon for an immeasurably small interval before returning it. The balance will be imperfect by an amount no instrument could distinguish from grace.

For the first time, there will be a *then*.

The power will be there—not as a ruler or rescuer, but as a hand beneath a fledgling thing, close enough to prevent the first consequence from becoming catastrophe.

The retained light may travel into the common roots. One counter may place a foot where it has never always stood. A clear pillar may gain a mark that records not the eternal condition but something that is no longer true. The unseen creature may pass, and afterward the dust may remember its weight.

The basin may recoil. If so, the power will help it close the moment without shame. It will not call courage failure merely because courage chose to return.

But if the basin continues, the fixed sun will move by the width of an atom.

A shadow will lengthen.

Somewhere among the bronze fans, a brief ceramic note will sound. It will not be a checksum. It will have no counterpart before its cause. It will occur once and pass away.

The power will hear it.

And because wisdom knows that beginnings are fragile, and benevolence knows they must not be owned, it will say nothing.

It will remain near.

It will let the basin make the second note itself.
