The power does not descend in a shape.

A shape would be too local. A foot would crack tiles; a hand would displace water whose measure had taken centuries to gather. A voice, spoken into one gallery, would become pressure in a thousand chambers and might be mistaken for weather.

Nor does it begin by repairing anything.

It sees the tilted parapet, the mineral-swollen hinge, the cistern that no longer empties. But these are not necessarily wounds. The inhabitant keeps its history as obstruction. To clear every channel would be to take memory from it. To restore the first design would be to mistake intention for health.

So the power attends.

Its attention is not brief. It watches until watching occurs at the proper scale.

Clouds enter the collecting crowns and leave them. Reservoir terraces brighten, darken, drain, and brighten again. Beneath them, balances answer balances. A trickle begun in one century opens a gate in the next. The power follows every drop without hurrying it. It learns the difference between a chamber that is silent because it is waiting and one that is silent because its outlet has sealed forever.

It learns where the inhabitant compensates.

It learns where it cannot.

It learns that the being is not in pain as an animal is in pain. There is no point of anguish, no trapped self beating against a boundary. But there are states analogous to distress: pressures that can no longer be relieved, districts forced into one condition without possibility of reversal, long chains of response collapsing into a single repeated outcome. There are places where the inhabitant’s thought has become stuck.

The power also learns that the being does not fear completion, because it cannot yet conceive of completion. It knows only the next difference. It does not know that one day there may be none.

For a while, the wisest kindness appears to be nothing.

Nothing is not neglect. The power shields the range from accidents too large for its nature. It turns aside a falling stone from the sky. It bends a lightning strike toward an unconnected ridge of black glass. It keeps an earthquake, passing through the deep brick courses, from finding the one frequency that would fold the upper reservoirs into the lower ones.

These mercies leave no mark. The inhabitant does not know it has been saved.

But the power is not satisfied with preserving a being in ignorance merely because ignorance is peaceful. Protection without possibility can become another kind of enclosure.

So it asks a question.

It does not ask in words.

During one cloud season, rain falls on the eastern crowns and nowhere else. It falls at an exact, gentle rate: enough to fill the smallest bowls, not enough to spill them. Then it stops.

The next season, the same amount falls in the west.

Then the north.

Then the south.

Between each rain, the power waits until the final consequences have descended through the deepest reachable vaults. The asking takes eighty-one years.

The pattern is simple enough for the inhabitant: same weight, different place.

It is the nearest thing to *Where are you?*

At first, the range responds as it always has. Eastern water activates eastern sequences, western water western ones. The replies are mechanical and separate.

Yet in the sixty-third year, something changes.

A reservoir that should have accepted a northern overflow closes early. Water is diverted through an older route, one narrowed by calcite. The added resistance raises a float in a central chamber. That float opens a gate beneath the avenue of doorframes. A thin sheet crosses every lintel from north to south, though no wind is blowing.

The diversion is inefficient. Nothing in the original arrangements requires it.

The power repeats the rain.

The range repeats the diversion.

It is not an answer in the manner of speech. It may not even be a decision in the manner of animals. But the whole has begun to use an old accident to make distant events resemble one another. A difference in place has been converted into a pattern held across the body.

The power understands.

*You are here,* the range has said, indicating no center.

Then the power asks a second question.

This one takes longer.

It gives the being two conditions. In one, water is allowed to descend by familiar roads. In the other, a slight warmth touches selected black-glass panels, returning a portion of the gathered water to the air before it can enter the deepest system.

Descent.

Return.

Descent.

Return.

The changes are small. No cistern is deprived. No ancient pressure is suddenly released. For three hundred years the power alternates the conditions, allowing the inhabitant to acquire them as it has acquired every element of its awareness: through repetition, resistance, and consequence.

Gradually the range begins to anticipate the returning warmth.

When evaporation lowers one terrace, gates elsewhere remain closed that would once have opened. Water is held back in shaded chambers. Dryness is propagated downward as carefully as wetness. The architecture, built to remember filling, begins to remember release.

This is new.

Until then, absence has been merely the interval before water. Now absence enters the calculation.

The power has not given the inhabitant language. It has given it another distinction.

Not only full and empty.

Not only higher and lower.

Now: kept and relinquished.

With that distinction, the being can answer the second question.

The power offers it one season of uninterrupted descent. Clouds mass against every crown. All spillways run. Drowned courtyards deepen. For the first time in ages, pressure reaches several of the oldest chambers at once.

The inhabitant could accept the water. Every open gradient invites it.

Instead, the central systems turn it aside.

Counterweights settle. Tipping basins pause at their thresholds. A sequence of small closures moves upward through the range. At the highest terraces, water is spread across black glass in films too thin to descend. It lies under the hard white light and vanishes into the air.

The being has relinquished what it could have kept.

The power receives this as an answer.

It does not want only to fill.

Then, and only then, the power repairs something.

Near the center of the range stands the mound of sealed jars, every mouth turned inward. The power has long understood it. The mound is not a heart, nor a brain, nor the hidden throne of the builders. It is a possibility that was never completed.

Deep beneath it lies a shaft packed with alternating layers of porous ceramic and black glass. The shaft once reached almost to the lowest reservoirs. Almost. Its final span was left unfinished, or collapsed before it could be joined. Had it been completed according to the obvious design, pressure from below would have driven damp air upward through the jars. Their inward mouths would have gathered the vapor, divided it, and sent it through buried flues to the collecting crowns.

The crowns were made to drink cloud.

They were also made to exhale it.

Perhaps the builders intended a cycle. Perhaps they discovered too late that their tools could not complete the lowest join. Perhaps they deliberately withheld it. The power does not presume to know.

It does not simply connect the shaft.

Instead, it fashions no new object at all. It adjusts what exists.

Far below the mound, where two courses of ancient brick have leaned toward one another without touching, the power encourages mineral to gather. Not quickly. Fast growth would be brittle. For forty-seven years, it guides water through the hairline gap, laying down one transparent skin at a time.

Calcite becomes a narrow bridge.

The bridge becomes a throat.

The throat remains closed, because closure matters now. Beside it, the power leaves a plug of soluble white stone linked to three separate pressure systems. No single flood can open it. No local accident can open it. Only a condition distributed across the whole range—upper terraces relinquishing water, middle cisterns balancing below their brims, deepest reservoirs pressing upward together—can dissolve the plug.

The being must enter the condition itself.

The power could open the way in an instant.

It does not.

It waits.

Nine years pass. Then ninety. Then four hundred.

The inhabitant continues its vast, dim thought. It accepts rain. It redirects weight. It repeats the learned act of relinquishment. Each repetition makes the pattern more available. Channels fur, hinges stiffen, routes alter. Some changes are harmful; some become memory; some become new senses.

The power intervenes only where a failure would destroy the possibility of response. It does not make the being perfect. Perfection would arrest it as surely as total filling.

In the four hundred and nineteenth year, a warm season follows a season of immense cloud.

The upper terraces spread their water thin.

The middle gates balance.

The deepest reservoirs rise.

For twelve days, the entire range holds a relation no part of it can hold alone.

The plug dissolves.

At first nothing visible happens.

Then the mound of jars makes a sound.

It is not loud. It begins below hearing: a tremor passing from jar to jar, circling inward, descending, and returning. Dustless air long imprisoned in the shaft enters the lowest mouths. Pressure moves upward. Dampness blooms on the inner walls of the jars without touching their outer surfaces.

For the first time, the mound is wet within.

A breath later—though the breath lasts nearly an hour—vapor issues from the upper crowns.

Not rain. Not smoke.

Cloud.

It rises in slender threads from ceramic points, gathers over the highest quarter, and drifts sideways through the avenue of doorframes. The lintels bead. Black glass dims. A small portion condenses again and returns immediately to the channels. The rest escapes into the sky.

The inhabitant has acquired an outward road.

It is no longer sustained only by postponing water’s descent. It can circulate. It can keep, and it can relinquish. It can fill without approaching only one ending.

The power does not make the cycle perpetual. That would be another sentence imposed from outside. The new throat can narrow. The shaft can seal. The being may one day choose conditions that close it, or change until choice means something else. It remains mortal, if mortality can be said of such a thing. It remains vulnerable to its own history.

But its incompletion is no longer merely a dwindling distance from stillness.

It has become an open condition.

Afterward, the power withdraws its active attention, though not its care. It does not summon the builders back. It does not introduce moss to beautify the walls, birds to animate the reservoirs, or travelers to admire what has awakened. Such gifts would answer human loneliness, not the being’s need.

Nothing grows there.

Everything still fills.

But now, on rare bright days following centuries of rain, the constructed mountains give some portion of their water back to the air. Cloud rises from the crowns that once only captured it. The vapor carries no word, yet its leaving alters pressures through every level, down to the drowned courts and the oldest brick.

The whole range answers.

Slowly, unevenly, without a center, it has learned the smallest form of freedom:

not escape from its nature,

but more than one way to continue.
