The power does not enter as light.

Light would summon some creatures and drive others into cracks. It would feed bacteria for which the city has no defenses. It would make visible what has never needed to be seen, and in doing so alter every balance built around darkness.

It does not enter as warmth.

A sudden warmth, however gentle it seemed to the giver, would be catastrophe here. Eggs would hatch into seasons unable to feed them. Symbiotic gardens would overgrow their hosts. Methane would leave solution. Soft walls would release trapped gases and fold.

It does not enter as a voice.

Nothing in the chamber owns an organ with which to hear such a voice, and to fashion one creature capable of hearing would be to isolate it from all the others. It would become the only animal in the city burdened by an elsewhere.

The power arrives as attention.

For a while—a while that might be a second to the seabed or a thousand feeding cycles to the inhabitants—it learns the cavity without touching it. It follows dissolved salts through the Fold. It feels the pressure made by each withdrawing crown. It enters the halted metabolism of old eggs and the brief chemical panic of prey caught on a bell-body’s strand. It knows the thread-mouth from within the hinge-runner and the hinge-runner from within its glassy brood. It knows the well-jaw’s hunger, which has no cruelty in it, and the victim’s convulsion, which has no lesson.

It perceives the city entire.

This is the first time the city has been held whole in any mind.

The power sees that the chamber is failing.

Not tomorrow. Perhaps not for many seasons. But the seep is a remnant flow through cooling sediment, and the fracture that feeds it has begun to mineralize. Each dark ring in the newest tube growth is thinner than the one before. The mouthless worms are dividing less often. Bacterial felt has withdrawn from several foundations. The animals know this only as separate inconveniences: a hunger here, an egg arrested there, a route traveled farther before finding food.

The ceiling, too, has chosen a future. A load of young sediment is moving downslope by fractions. When it moves fully, the cavity will not be crushed all at once. One side will lower. Water will escape through the upper seam. The soft city will compact district by district until its last inhabited hollows are no wider than films.

There are ways to prevent this.

The power could harden the walls into diamond.

It could make the seep inexhaustible.

It could suspend every inhabitant in health and abundance, remove every predator, restore every prey animal as soon as it was swallowed. It could enlarge the cavity into an ocean or carry the entire settlement upward to warmer seas.

It could teach the city that it is a city.

For a moment the power considers this last gift. It could join pressure to pressure, chemistry to chemistry, until the scattered reactions formed an inwardness. The chamber might awaken without eyes, sensing itself in traffic and current. It might know the Fold as part of its body, the seep as an organ, the towers as a mineral thought. It might possess, for the first time, an I.

But the power is wise enough to ask what that I could do.

It would feel every burial and be unable to lift the sediment. It would experience predator and prey together. Its hunger would be contradictory. Its body would die in pieces too small and numerous to mourn separately, yet all of them would be felt. To awaken it without also remaking it would not be elevation. It would be the concentration of suffering.

To remake it enough to bear awareness would be to replace it.

So the power leaves the city innocent of itself.

Nor does it make the chamber permanent. Permanence would change the law by which it lives. If walls ceased to settle, breath-borers would lose the disturbances that open their ground. If structures ceased to fall, binders would pave until passages choked. If the seep never faltered, its nearest colonies would smother all others. An eternal city would not remain this city. It would become a sealed abundance, then a simplicity, then one successful film covering everything.

The power does not preserve the shape.

It preserves the possibility.

Beyond the Fold, behind a thickness of sediment no inhabitant has crossed, lies another trapped lens of water. It is smaller than the first and presently lifeless. Its chemistry is wrong: too rich in iron, too poor in oxygen, colder near its upper margin and nearly fresh where ancient ice is buried below. Under ordinary chance it will harden unopened. The city will die within a body-length of habitable space it could never reach.

The power touches neither animal nor water.

It touches probability.

A crystal growing in the Fold inclines by less than the width of a bacterial cell. Because of that inclination, the next crystal joins it imperfectly. The fault between them guides a molecule of acidic waste. The molecule loosens a bond. Several feeding cycles later, a young binder enters the pore and finds a bacterial cell there. It feeds. Its body prevents one grain from settling.

The pore remains open.

Nothing notices.

The binder leaves a mucus trail. Iron stains it. Another binder follows the edible chemistry and extends the trail. A hinge-runner, attempting to evade a thread-mouth, forces its unfinished armor into the weakness and loses one shell plate there. The plate catches debris. Debris diverts a filament of water.

The filament is almost still, but it has a direction.

Into the Fold.

Over many seasons the city does what it has always done. It converts repeated passage into space. Binders soften and seal. Hinge-runners drag refuse. Bacterial films prepare surfaces. Long-turners enter the new route and, unable to reverse, press forward until their bristles score the walls. Some die there. Their decay acidifies the sediment. Breath-borers arrive at the stale end of the tunnel and extend their tails toward the current behind them.

The distance between the two chambers narrows.

The power could open the way instantly. It does not. A sudden breach would flush the city, mix incompatible waters, and turn escape into destruction. Instead it permits the passage to be made at the pace of habitation. Wherever life reaches, the wall yields slightly. Wherever life withdraws, minerals close it again.

Space continues to belong only to what keeps it open.

This matters.

The new chamber will not be given to the city as an empty kingdom. It will be entered grain by grain, tested by bodies that can turn back and bodies that cannot. Its first district will be a narrow skin around a breathing chimney. Its second will gather in the lee of a dead long-turner. Many pioneers will fail. Yet no creature will be sent into conditions wholly beyond it. The route will ripen under use.

The power makes three other changes, each so small that none can be separated from fortune.

Deep below the second chamber, it leaves one fracture unsealed. Not an eternal seep, only a long one. Its warmth will vary. Its methane will pulse. There will be lean intervals and flourishing ones. It will last long enough for adaptation, but not long enough to arrest it.

In the roof, the power preserves a branching network of capillaries. They are too narrow to admit a killing current. Through them oxygen-bearing water can diffuse, along with foreign larvae, organic dust, and on rare occasions the melt of true ice. The city will remain hidden, but it will not become perfectly closed.

At the far end of the second chamber, it places nothing.

This is the most deliberate act.

No barrier is strengthened there. No mineral is persuaded to grow. Beyond that wall the sediment continues for a short distance and then opens, by tortuous pores, into the greater seabed.

Not a doorway. Not yet.

A direction.

The city will have to make it.

When these things are done, the power attends once more to the individual lives below.

It finds an old hinge-runner scraping mineral from a brood sac that will not hatch.

It finds a thread-mouth dying inside a host already too weak to carry it.

It finds a mouthless seep-worm whose bacteria have begun to consume its inner tissues.

It finds a tube-throat adding a narrow ring to a tower that will fall before the ring is complete.

Benevolence aches to spare each one.

Wisdom does not call their suffering unreal merely because it is small, brief, or unreflected upon. The power knows that pain does not require a philosophy to matter. It also knows that to extract every tooth from this web would unravel the web, and to feed every mouth without cost would make mouths multiply until abundance became suffocation.

Yet there are useless agonies—suffering that sustains nothing, teaches nothing, protects nothing, and has no future consequence except itself.

These the power diminishes.

Not all. Enough.

A nerve knot in the trapped hinge-runner quiets when escape becomes impossible. The thread-mouth releases its host before both perish slowly. The failing seep-worm enters stillness while its symbionts return its body to the bacterial felt. Eggs that will never develop cease spending the parent’s dwindling salts. Creatures buried beyond any possible breathing shaft lose sensation before the walls meet.

No one is made immortal.

No predator goes unfed.

The city remains a place where bodies are used by other bodies.

But terror no longer outlives possibility.

Then the power withdraws its intervention, though not its regard.

For many seasons nothing changes visibly.

The old chamber continues. Bell-bodies sink like weather. Tube-throats open and vanish. Well-jaws preserve their smooth clearings. Ice enters once through a ceiling crack, bearing a green filament from a sea no inhabitant can imagine. A district gathers around it and collapses when it melts.

The seep weakens.

The tunnel in the Fold lengthens.

One day a young long-turner enters a lower aperture not used by its kind before. Its bristles are still soft. Halfway in, the passage becomes too tight. It presses forward because it cannot understand waiting. Behind it, the current carries the smell of crowded tubes and predators. Ahead is stale water with an unfamiliar iron taste.

The animal advances a segment.

The wall gives a grain.

It advances again.

Its front end enters the second chamber.

Nothing witnesses the crossing as a crossing. No crown opens in celebration. No chemical signal means discovery. The long-turner only encounters open water where its body expected resistance. It continues until its trailing end leaves the passage.

For the first time, a creature of the city has a little room around it.

It dislikes this.

Open space is dangerous. Its bristles find no wall. Its underside loses the familiar pavement of mucus, shell, and living bodies. It curls as far as its anatomy permits and is borne sideways by the weak new seep.

There, on an iron-dark surface, it finds bacteria.

It begins to graze.

Waste falls behind it.

The waste feeds a film. A binder arrives through the passage and seals loose grains around the film. A breath-borer, carried as a larva in the current, settles into soft sediment. Tube-throats follow the chemistry of other tubes and attach near the tunnel’s mouth. A hinge-runner drags the transparent husk of something eaten elsewhere and wedges it beneath a ledge.

The second city is not founded.

The first city continues into it.

Much later, the old cavity closes.

Its towers bend. Its plazas fill. Bell-bodies flatten against the roof and are consumed. The final seepward district survives longest around a breath-borer’s chimney, then narrows to a passage, then to a wet film inhabited only by bacteria and one young binder.

The binder feeds along the edge.

Its motion delays the end by several hours.

When it dies, crystals cross the hollow and join.

The old city becomes a pale nodule veined with tubes, shells, mineralized mucus, egg halos, and the empty molds of soft animals. It keeps no monument and needs none. Its inhabitants have gone outward, though none knows it has inherited an escape.

Beyond the Fold, traffic thickens.

The second chamber acquires columns and wells. Its iron-rich walls favor unfamiliar grazers. Bell-bodies there grow narrower. Long-turners develop softer bristles and sometimes reverse. Binders carry dark tiles on their backs. The city changes because continuation is not copying.

In time its paths reach the far wall.

They enter the tortuous pores.

They move toward the greater seabed.

Perhaps, generations later, some descendant will emerge into the deep ocean and recoil from its vastness. It will know no horizon even then. It will feel only cold water on every side, no wall against the left flank, no ceiling low enough to touch.

It may turn back.

Another may not.

The power does not choose for them.

It has not saved the city from death, because no living city can be saved from change without being saved from life. It has not made the inhabitants gentle, because their hungers are braided together too deeply for innocence to mean harmlessness. It has not asked them to understand their rescue.

It has given them no revelation.

Only time.

Only a neighboring hollow.

Only a passage that must be kept open by those who use it.

Only the quiet lessening of pain when hope has physically ended.

And beyond the last wall, not safety, but room.
