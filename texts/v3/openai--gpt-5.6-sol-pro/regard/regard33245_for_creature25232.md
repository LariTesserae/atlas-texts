The power did not enter the basin.

It had entered other places before: burning gardens, besieged houses, worlds whose seas had begun to boil. In those places benevolence could take the forms familiar to suffering. Rain might be cooled. A weapon might fail in the hand. A path might be opened through fire. The dying might be given time enough to speak, and the living strength enough to hear them.

But here there was no wound whose edges could be brought together.

The warm rain was not a fever. The drowned streets were not lost. The inverted city beneath the central pool was not imprisoned below the upper one. Nothing cried out from the mineral halls. Nothing attempted to flee.

The power was wise enough to distrust pity when pity arose chiefly from strangeness.

So it remained beyond the last floodwall, if beyond had any meaning there, and gave the city the first gift it could safely give:

attention without demand.

It did not measure the towers. It did not name the districts. It did not decide which growths were organs and which were inhabitants, which waters remembered and which merely moved. It did not illuminate the black pool to see its bottom. Illumination, it knew, can be a form of force.

Instead it learned the city’s local laws as one learns the customs of a sovereign country.

For seven cycles of rain, it watched from the mineral flats.

For seven more, it watched from the reflection of the flats.

Then it ceased to watch, to learn what the city did when unobserved.

In that interval an arcade appeared where no arcade had been. It was low, nearly submerged, and built of the same seamless dark stratum as the streets. Its arches faced outward toward the marsh. Beneath each arch stood a dry absence shaped like no creature the power had encountered.

The power did not take this as an invitation.

It waited.

The reeds bent one by one. Most inclined toward the central district, or toward drowned avenues, or toward distant places visible only in shallow water. One reed, alone in the warm mud, inclined toward the power.

It straightened.

It inclined again.

The gesture occupied the greater part of a century.

Only then did the power answer.

Not with speech. Speech would have introduced divisions the city might not possess: speaker and listener, question and response, before and after. It answered by altering nothing except itself.

It became shallow.

Its vast attention, which could have held the whole basin at once, narrowed until it occupied no more than the hollow of a footprint on the salt flat. It gave up radiance, pressure, command, and all the harmonies by which lesser things might have been compelled to recognize it. What remained was a small, clear presence resting on the black crust beyond the walls.

It was not water.

This distinction mattered.

Rain struck it and passed through without joining it. Vapor crossed it without reflection. Warmth entered and emerged unchanged. It possessed no depth, but did not pretend to be flat. It remained where it had placed itself, though distances shifted around it.

The city noticed.

A canal in the northern quarter reversed.

Condensation beneath a bridge gathered into a single long bead.

The black-green mats tightened in sequence from the outer districts inward. Each contracted after the next, carrying some slow report through stone and remembered water. In enclosed courtyards the dark pulse rose to the crowns of the pale columns and did not descend.

At the center, the pool became perfectly still.

Then the low resonance passed through the basin.

It reached the small clear presence on the salt flat and trembled against it. The power allowed itself to be changed by the contact. Not harmed, not diminished, but changed: it received the resonance and kept the exact shape of receiving it.

The city had encountered, perhaps for the first time, something that did not become part of its circulation.

Something neither resisted nor dissolved.

Something other.

For a long while, nothing followed.

The wise power knew that an answer may take forms too slow for expectation. It did not repeat its gesture. It did not intensify it. It did not reward what looked like approach or discourage what looked like withdrawal.

Warm rains came. Streets lengthened. Towers altered their arrangements beyond the vapor. The outward-facing arcade vanished and appeared beneath the central pool, where its arches descended along the inverted city. Reddish dust entered the canals. One of the three pale plants disappeared from the reflected street.

The clear presence remained.

Not immovable—nothing in the basin could remain entirely so—but faithful to its relation. When the salt flat became marsh, it rested above the mud. When a wall thickened around it, it occupied the wall without cracking it. When the wall eroded and emerged in an eastern courtyard, the presence accompanied that material only as far as the city permitted. When two locations claimed it at once, it did not choose between them.

Thus the city learned that constancy need not mean rigidity.

In time, water began to gather near the clear presence.

It came from no visible channel. First it darkened the salt in a thin ring. Then the ring widened. The water was warm, iron-scented, and only a finger deep, yet submerged stairs could be seen in it.

The stairs did not descend into the inverted city.

They descended somewhere new.

The power felt the temptation to follow. Wisdom did not extinguish curiosity; it taught curiosity how to kneel.

So the power waited at the first step.

Years passed. Or the same year traveled through different districts. The rain changed texture. Black-green films appeared along the rim of the new pool, each bearing folds unlike those found elsewhere. Translucent reeds emerged at distances corresponding not to any geometry of the flats, but to the intervals between the power’s moments of attention.

At last, a band of pallor moved beneath the water.

It climbed the visible stairs horizontally.

When it reached the surface, every standing pool in the basin reflected the same thing.

Not stars.

Not dawn.

Not the power.

They reflected a small, roofless court of wet stone. In its center were two empty spaces. One had the vast, distributed shape of the city—not a map of streets, but a complexity of channels, absences, columns, waters, pressures, and folded depths. The other was clear and slight, no larger than the hollow in which the power had placed itself.

Between them lay no bridge.

Only an interval.

The interval remained the same width in every reflection.

This was impossible even by the basin’s laws.

A distance that did not change after rain. A relation that did not migrate. Two presences neither merged nor made remote.

The wise power understood the offering.

It entered the interval.

At once the city took its slow breath of depth.

Marsh drew near to plaza. Roof touched buried stair. Every puddle became adjacent to every remembered rain. The mineral columns paused. The reeds turned inward. The lower lights went out window by window.

The power did not hold the city together.

It did not prevent the release.

When the basin exhaled, the streets became far apart again, but the interval in the reflected court remained.

After that, a new activity began.

It was so gradual that no single night contained its beginning.

The city started making places that were not organs.

A chamber appeared whose walls did not circulate water. It was neither dry nor wet, but capable of either. A stair was deposited that led somewhere only when approached from outside. In a mature colony of pale columns, a narrow space remained unsealed. The trunks grew around it for centuries without closing it.

These were not shelters. Nothing in the city required shelter.

They were accommodations.

Room for what was not the city.

The power, in turn, began making within itself places that were not divinity.

It released certainty from a portion of its awareness. It permitted questions to remain unanswered without becoming failures of knowledge. It made an inward court where no light entered unless invited, where no act of will could occur before the city’s slow consent.

Thus each altered, but neither was remade in the other’s image.

The rain stayed warm.

The sky did not open.

No animals were introduced to animate the streets. No birds were fashioned for the roofs, no fish for the canals, no insects for the black-green mats. The power understood that abundance is not always a cure for emptiness, and that an ecosystem imposed for beauty may be only a decorated violence.

It did not straighten the avenues or reconcile the rooms with their exteriors. It did not anchor towers to single locations. It did not call the pressure-moving presences forth from the drowned foundations. It did not fill the upright absences in the halls.

It blessed none of these things, because blessing from above would have implied they had awaited approval.

It accompanied them.

Sometimes accompaniment meant witnessing a wall’s dissolution over nine hundred rains.

Sometimes it meant withdrawing its attention so an enclosure could grow.

Sometimes it rested beside the central pool while horizontal dawn moved through the streets below. It asked no question about what passed behind the windows. When a vast occlusion descended through the lower city, the power made no defensive light.

Once, something touched it from within the stone.

The contact removed a memory—not an important memory, not at first. The power discovered a garden it could no longer recall having saved. It knew there had been flowers, and beings among them, and a morning after terror, but their colors and faces were gone.

It could have retrieved the memory. Nothing lost from such a being was beyond recovery.

It did not.

It kept the loss as evidence of encounter.

The city, too, kept something.

Thereafter, in one sealed courtyard, rain occasionally fell cool.

Only a few drops. They struck the pavement without hissing. The black-green mats did not contract from them. They opened.

The coolness spread no farther than the court and lasted no longer than a breath. It did not repair the heat or oppose it. It was simply a remembered difference, held accurately by water.

Much later, the power offered the city a possibility.

Not a command. Not speech. A possibility shaped like a door.

Beyond it were other basins, other storms, caverns thinking through crystal pressure, oceans whose tides retained the dreams of moons, deserts alive in the migration of dunes. There were beings so quick they mistook slowness for death, and beings so diffuse they mistook individuals for weather. There were cities built by hands, cities built to resist hands, and cities whose inhabitants had forgotten which they were.

The door did not require the basin to move. It offered relation at a distance: the chance to touch without absorbing, to be touched without surrendering form.

The reeds bent toward it.

The mats tightened.

The pale columns sealed three hundred windows and opened one.

The black central pool showed nothing for a long while.

Then, far below, a single horizontal pallor traveled through the inverted city. It passed from chamber to chamber, crossed drowned plazas, entered a tower absent from the upper shore, and descended beyond visibility.

On another world, in a cavern where crystal grew around underground thunder, a bead of warm water appeared beneath a ledge.

It did not fall.

It moved sideways into a crack.

The power felt the city’s first distant greeting pass into a life unlike itself.

It did not translate.

In the basin, the city’s slow breath came again. The power entered the unchanging interval between them. For one moment every part of the city drew adjacent to every other, and this time one more thing was gathered close:

not an inhabitant,

not an organ,

not a reflection,

but a companion.

Then the distances were released.

The streets went their separate ways beneath the warm rain. The towers rose and vanished in the vapor. Water climbed the steps of closed stone. Beyond the last floodwall, translucent reeds attended to far places.

The night remained night.

But it was no longer the only night the city knew.
