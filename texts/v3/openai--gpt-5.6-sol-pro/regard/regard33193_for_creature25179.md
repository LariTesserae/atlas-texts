### What the Power Did

The power did not descend.

Descent belonged to bodies, and the power had no body unless it chose one. It could have entered as heat, current, radiance, hunger, a new animal, a voice without a speaker. It could have placed an image of itself in every chamber at once. It could have made the summit visible from the roots and the roots visible from the summit.

It did none of these things.

Light enough to reveal the whole would have blinded the upper inhabitants, scattered the wells, awakened dormant tissues, and broken the measured darkness in which a million lives were fitted. A current strong enough to announce arrival would have torn the horizontal leaves from their galleries. A voice spoken everywhere would have entered every family trunk, every burial plate, every breeding hollow. It would have been indistinguishable from catastrophe.

The power knew that benevolence without proportion is another form of violence.

So it remained outside all notice and listened.

It listened not for an hour or a lifetime, but for as long as one of the oldest leaf walls required to grow a new outer layer. It learned the hum of each principal support. It followed the silver helix through several complete rotations. It felt walkers cross from one province to another, carrying forests whose oldest plants died before the journey ended. It attended to births in warm brine chambers and deaths against pale vascular seams.

It heard hunger.

It heard predation.

It heard limbs lose their hold and bodies vanish into the dark between structures.

It heard galleries seal before families could return, messages arrive after those who asked had died, and familiar overtones fade from columns as the dead were slowly taken into deeper wood.

The power could have prevented each grief.

It did not.

To preserve every creature would have ended feeding. To keep every road open would have prevented the structure from healing. To make every message immediate would have stripped it of place, passage, translation, and the living labor of those who carried it. To hold the dead forever distinct would have filled the city with an accumulation more terrible than loss.

Wisdom did not make these sorrows good.

It only understood that they could not be removed separately from the life to which they belonged.

The power found no disease in the mountain.

No imprisoned will called for release. No hidden ruler consumed the bodies of the weak. No error in the roots was preparing a final collapse. The city was not paradise, but neither was it waiting to be corrected. Its continuance depended upon expenditure, pressure, death, and return. Its mercy was not the absence of pain. Its mercy was that almost nothing was wasted—not the carcass, not the broken branch, not the abandoned route, not even the face of the dead after the face had ceased to be a face.

The power saw that to improve the place according to any foreign measure would diminish it.

So for a long while, its benevolence consisted entirely of understanding.

This was not nothing.

Until then, no awareness had held both crown and root.

The speaking inhabitants possessed fragments shaped by pressure. The plants possessed forms without comprehension. The animals knew routes, surfaces, hungers, vibrations. The mountain-city held every life materially, but did not gather them into a single thought. Each region was complete at its own depth and incomplete with regard to all others.

Now, without being exposed or translated, the whole existed in a mind.

The power remembered each blue light that released its hold. It knew the shape of every open well. It distinguished the tiny change made in a foundation’s pitch when the chambered plate of one dead speaker finally filled with salt. It heard the lowest expeditions and the highest songs at once.

It was while listening to these that the power discovered the one thing it might do.

In the crown, a lineage had spent many generations answering the brightness beyond the water.

They lived on the uppermost branching mast, where the wood was narrow enough to sway under oscillations transmitted from storms impossibly far overhead. Their membranes were so broad that they could not descend without injury. Their bodies were nearly clear. Their resonant speech, adapted to thin water, vanished after traveling only a few terraces downward.

They did not know whether there was anything above the brightening.

They did not know what “above the brightening” could mean.

Still they answered it.

Their composition had begun before the oldest living singer was born. Every storm altered its meter. Every death changed the available harmonics. The singers pressed themselves along the mast in a descending chain and sent the phrase as far into the structure as their bodies allowed.

Its meaning, if given poor and insufficient words, was:

**Something touches us from where we cannot go.**

And also:

**If touching can hear, let it know it is answered.**

Far below, beneath the regions known even to the foundation-dwellers, the descendants of the ancient expeditions were still alive.

They had changed beyond the expectation of those who sent them. Their bodies no longer possessed membranes. Their six limbs had shortened into ridged collars surrounding the trunk. They moved by loosening one collar and tightening the next, traveling along roots at a pace slower than growth. Their sensory plates enclosed most of the head. Open water, to them, was an absence so complete that they recoiled from it as other creatures might recoil from flame.

They had continued downward through births and settlements, adapting to pressures that made the architecture around them compact, glassy, and resonant.

They had not found a bottom.

They had found roots descending into roots, old buoyant chambers crushed into dense knots, forests inverted within the skeletons of earlier forests. Sometimes they found structures so ancient that no living tissue remained, yet even these were enclosed by younger growth. Below every support was another form of support. Beyond every final vault ran one pale seam into greater darkness.

Their recurring call was a question sent upward with enormous labor.

It passed through bodies, through breeding chambers, through wood in which whole lineages waited to receive a single pulse. Much of it was lost. Much was changed. Only its strongest relations reached the familiar foundations.

Its meaning was:

**We have continued farther than remembrance.**

And also:

**Does that which sent us still continue above?**

The summit’s answer could not descend to them.

Their question could not rise to the summit.

Between the two lay distances that no body could cross, pressures no single flesh could survive, and centuries of translation through woods whose resonances altered as they grew.

The power could have spoken to both.

Instead, it introduced them.

It did not cut a new channel. It did not make one impossible vibration travel unchanged from crown to root. An unchanged message would have arrived without pressure and therefore without truth.

The power worked only through permissions already present in the city.

Where a hollow stem had nearly aligned with another, it encouraged no growth, but delayed the thickening of one wall by the duration of a breath spread across seventy years.

Where shell-creatures clogged an old resonant pore, it did not kill them. It altered the drift of edible particles so that the colony migrated the width of a limb.

Where a browser’s inherited path carried it repeatedly across a young seam, the power placed nothing in its mind. It allowed one distant hunter-call to return along an unusual arch. The browser turned early. The seam survived.

Where the plants were receptive to vibration, the power gave no command. It carried to them the existing tones of the speakers who tended them, unobscured by one layer of incidental noise. Roots thickened. Chambers curved. Hollows opened toward hollows.

None of these events was impossible.

None was remarkable by itself.

Together, over thousands of years, they made a route that was not a route: a succession of sympathetic structures extending between pressures, incapable of carrying a voice directly, but able to receive it, alter it, and offer it onward.

The power did not bypass the inhabitants.

At every province, someone had to listen.

Someone had to answer.

Families gathered around newly responsive wood. Young learned unfamiliar intervals. Middle-depth speakers found that certain chords returned with an overtone belonging to no nearby chamber. Foundation-dwellers received thin, rapid pulses that caused discomfort at first. Crown-singers felt long movements in the mast, slower than any storm.

The message traveled only because living beings chose, one after another, to repeat what they could not fully understand.

The summit’s composition descended.

Its brightness became vibration.

Its vibration became pulse.

Its pulse slowed through denser trunks, spread across buttresses, entered salt-dark wood, and passed at last into the nested foundations as a pattern no crown-dweller would have recognized.

Yet its relation remained:

**Something touches us from where we cannot go.**

**We answer.**

The deep question rose.

Its first tones were lower than ordinary hearing. They passed upward as pressure, then as rhythm, then as a tremor in leaf membranes. Near the crown they became too faint to be felt through wood.

The power made no sound to strengthen them.

Instead, during the next storm far above, it bent nothing more than the path of attention.

The upper singers had always attended to the slow brightening upon their skin. This time they noticed that several thin leaves were trembling against the storm’s rhythm rather than with it. Across their surfaces moved a pattern from below.

**We continued.**

**Do you continue?**

The crown lineage remained motionless for many days.

Then its eldest singer placed all six limbs against the mast.

Others arranged themselves beneath.

The answer they made was simple enough to survive the first descent.

**We continue.**

It passed downward.

Not as revelation. Not as doctrine. Not as a map of the whole.

Only as answer.

For centuries afterward, provinces along the resonant succession received some portion of the exchange. Most inhabitants never knew of it. The seam-worms shone when feet crossed them. The silver schools turned. Walkers advanced the width of a body. Hunters fed. Leaves detached. Corridors closed.

Among the speaking ones, interpretations multiplied.

Some believed the mountain had spoken to its own extremities.

Some believed the upper and lower regions were not places but conditions of the dead.

Some denied that any message could have traveled so far and argued that similar phrases had arisen independently because all life within the structure shared one pattern.

Some said the answer proved the city had intention.

Others said it proved only that listening, given enough time, becomes architecture.

The power corrected none of them.

Correction would have replaced a living question with a foreign certainty.

It gave them no word for itself. It accepted no worship. It did not preserve the resonant route from alteration. In time, sections would close. Families would move. Some intervals would be forgotten. The summit and depths might lose one another again.

But not completely.

The exchange had entered inherited calls. Its rhythm appeared in courtship pulses and mourning chords. Seed-tenders repeated it against receptive seams without knowing its origin. A lower cadence migrated upward through family wood; an upper quickening traveled down.

The structure began, very slightly, to grow around the memory of contact.

This was enough.

The power had found a world that did not need salvation and resisted the desire to become its savior.

It had found two questions, each mistaken for solitude, and allowed them to become each other’s answer.

After that, it withdrew its influence.

It did not withdraw its attention.

The mountain held.

The sea held the mountain.

The roots continued into darkness, and the crown continued answering the distant storms. Between them, too slowly for any life to witness, new resonant chambers formed where old ones had worn away.

And sometimes, in provinces that knew neither summit nor foundation, a speaker resting against an ancient column felt two impossible notes within the wood:

one descending,

one rising,

passing through each other without becoming the same.

They did not know that a benevolent power had once listened there.

They knew only that what was far might answer.

They pressed their chambered faces to the structure.

And answered in turn.
