The power does not open the roof.

It does not pour down sunlight, seed the streets with green, still the faults, bind the rivers to their channels, or teach the inhabitants to dream of a surface they have never needed.

It is wise enough to understand that rescue can be a form of ruin.

It does not mistake hardship for error. It does not call the city unfinished because it moves. It does not call the people deprived because their world is pale, or primitive because their tools are simple, or imprisoned because stone lies above them.

Before doing anything, it attends.

This takes a long time.

It listens through the roof, through buried rivers and the slow compression of mineral beds. It follows the pressure that kindles the luminous seams. It learns the passing of herds, the opening and closing of lower streets, the cultivation of films, the placement of sacrificial joints, the distribution of records, the little tap with which a parent warns an infant.

It learns the difference between a wall failing beautifully and a wall failing badly.

It learns which deaths belong to negligence, which to cruelty, which to hunger, which to chance, and which to the irreducible freedom of a world that cannot be made harmless without being made dead.

For a while, therefore, the power does nothing that can be distinguished from careful observation.

A child pauses in a courtyard and looks up.

An elder lifts her head from the listening stone.

A herd turns its blank faces toward an unlit part of the ceiling.

Nothing follows.

Years pass.

The power becomes aware that the city already possesses much of what benevolence would have wished to teach it: that strength without yielding is dangerous; that knowledge should be distributed; that no one owns the path entirely; that the dead must remain useful to the living; that the shape of a home matters less than who carries its stones.

It finds no need to give them these truths in its own words.

But wisdom does not use admiration as an excuse for indifference.

There are sufferings here that teach nothing.

There are children taken by water before any bowl trembles. There are lower explorers sealed between smooth walls without warning or fault. There are districts poisoned by deep brines for which no creature has yet evolved a fear. There are rare shifts so sudden that no graceful surrender is possible.

The people endure these things because they must. The power does not romanticize their endurance.

So it begins with one small mercy.

It gives them time.

Not much.

The cavern continues to move. Rivers still break through pavements. Roof scales still fall. Wells close. Terraces descend. Houses must still be opened, numbered, carried, and remade.

But before the worst changes, there comes an interval.

At first it is only the length of a breath.

A roof that would have fallen without warning releases three grains of dust. A floor that would have opened beneath a sleeping room grows briefly cool. Water entering from an unknown passage tastes of copper moments before it becomes poisonous. Ceiling drifters descend one body-length lower. Crack-runners lift their mouthparts from the stone.

Then the interval lengthens.

A breath becomes ten heartbeats. Ten heartbeats become the time needed to lift an infant, cut a water bag free, roll from a sleeping platform, or pass one urgent sign through a crowded chamber.

The power does not prevent consequence. It places mercy between cause and consequence.

No one recognizes this at first. The city is full of coincidences, and the inhabitants are properly suspicious of explanations that arrive too complete.

Records accumulate.

Separate households report the same impossible pause: a slab suspended after its supports had parted; floodwater hesitating at the lip of a nursery; a closing fissure remaining open until the last explorer’s hand was pulled through, then meeting with the softness of two lips.

Memory keepers compare disks.

Children give the pauses names.

The philosophers object to every name.

Eventually the intervals are called the Held Moments, not because anyone claims to know what holds them, but because the phrase describes the experience without deciding its cause.

New civic arguments begin.

Some say the Held Moments are a change in the cavern’s behavior. Some say they have always existed and only now are being noticed. Some say they are produced by the city itself—that generations of careful building have taught pressure to travel differently. A few propose an intelligence.

This last idea is regarded as dangerous.

If the cavern intends mercy, perhaps it intends disaster too. If something saves, perhaps it can be praised, bribed, accused, or obeyed. Such beliefs could make people wait for rescue instead of reading the ground.

The officials whose duty is to disagree with new construction begin disagreeing with theology.

They are right to do so.

The power approves.

It shortens several Held Moments in places where people have begun to rely on them carelessly. No one dies, but a storehouse roof folds sooner than expected and crushes jars belonging to an influential household. The lesson is entered into public record:

**No kindness abolishes weight.**

This sentence persists for centuries.

The power’s second act is more difficult.

It makes itself answerable.

One bright phase, a line appears across the Worn Forum.

It is not a crack. It crosses cracks without following them. It is too narrow to be a mineral seam and too steady to be reflected light. It begins near the oldest dry channel and extends toward the center of the polished dark stone, moving no faster than a person walking.

Citizens clear the Forum.

Animal guides watch the sleeping grazers in the grooves. Weight judges taste the air and lay shallow bowls across the line. Cistern wardens cover the nearby wells. Children are removed, though several return by routes their elders did not anticipate.

The line reaches the Forum’s center and stops.

There it divides into the shape of two open hands.

The hands are not perfect. One has six fingers. The other has four and a thumb placed too low. The power has observed sign language for generations but has never possessed a body.

The city waits.

The malformed hands move.

Their first sentence is clumsy. Its grammar belongs to no district. It uses a gesture for *roof* where it should use *burden*, and a gesture for *deep water* where it means *hidden cause*.

What it asks is:

**May I help?**

No one answers for the city.

This is not merely caution. There is no office authorized to speak for everyone under circumstances so absurd.

Council assembles around the Forum. The dark stone is kept empty except for the line of light. Representatives come from cistern households, cultivation chambers, herd paths, warm fissures, lower explorers, stone carriers, memory keepers, healers, scavenger tenders, and the districts most often displaced. The officials who disagree with construction demand standing as well, on the grounds that any intervention is a structure even if it has no stones.

For nine days they argue.

They ask whether consent can be given to something whose limits are unknown. They ask whether refusal is possible if the power already controls the Held Moments. They ask whether the line of light is the power itself, an instrument, an animal, or merely an invitation sent by something elsewhere.

They ask whether help given to people will harm the creatures whose routes sustain them.

They ask whether saving one district might divert pressure into another.

They ask whether the power understands that the city includes beings who cannot speak in council.

They ask whether it can lie.

The light answers as well as it can.

It can lie, it says, in the sense that it can be mistaken and can conceal. It has not come to demand worship. It cannot promise never to cause harm. It does not know every consequence. It is powerful, not infinite.

This reply reassures them more than perfection would have.

On the tenth day, an old weight judge named Tests-the-Empty-Arch steps onto the Forum. She is small, bent asymmetrically, and nearly deaf to voices, though she can hear water through three human heights of stone.

She sits before the illuminated hands.

Then she asks the question every architect must answer.

**Show us how you fail.**

For the first time since turning its attention toward the cavern, the power is surprised.

The line trembles.

All across the Forum, reflected seams shake in the polished stone.

The power had considered restraint. It had considered consent, consequence, ecology, dependence, dignity, and the danger of becoming an object of obedience. It had not considered that its own failure must be made civic knowledge.

The people wait.

At last the hands sign:

**I can become certain too quickly.**

After a pause:

**I can love what I have preserved more than what it becomes.**

And then:

**I can make help difficult to refuse.**

The old judge touches her jaw to the Forum.

She listens to whatever travels beneath the power’s confession.

Then she signs:

**Where are your prepared seams?**

The power has none.

So the city does not accept its help.

Not yet.

Instead, it teaches the power how to build.

For many years, nothing miraculous occurs beyond the Held Moments. The line returns whenever council calls, though sometimes pressure or flood interrupts it. The people require the power to distribute every warning through several unrelated means. No revelation may be entrusted to one priest, one district, one bloodline, one substance, or one species.

If danger is shown in ceiling light, it must also be felt in water.

If it is felt in water, some animal must be able to perceive it.

If an animal perceives it, the sign must be observable without harming the animal.

If a message concerns a district, it must not be legible only from that district.

No guidance is permitted unless the power states its uncertainty.

No permanent sanctuary may be built around its presence.

No household may claim ownership of the place where the hands appear.

The power agrees to all of this.

It creates its first prepared seam by dividing its attention.

Part enters the luminous roof, where it can read pressure and brighten narrow warning bands ahead of dangerous shifts. Part enters the cisterns, where it can alter the surface tension of water into recognizable patterns. Part enters neither stone nor water but the habits of certain small runners, which begin arranging themselves in branching lines before deep floods.

No single sign is infallible.

Sometimes warning bands kindle for changes that never come. Sometimes water trembles because an armored beast has entered a brine pocket. Sometimes the runners form patterns because they are feeding.

The signs must be compared.

The power can advise. It cannot command.

This is its seam.

If one manifestation becomes corrupt, mistaken, worshipped, captured, or too certain, the others can contradict it. If the city ceases to need one, it can be abandoned without losing the whole. The power accepts that its presence may be dismantled.

Only then does the city permit it to help.

The inhabitants do not ask for an end to earthquakes.

They ask for better knowledge of poison.

They ask that lower explorers have some means of signaling when stone closes between them.

They ask whether cultivation chambers can be made more abundant without exhausting the warm cracks.

They ask whether the herds are diminishing or merely traveling farther than before.

They ask for a way to tell when a Held Moment is impossible.

They ask for the names of no gods.

The power answers the questions it can.

For poisoned water, it teaches them a test involving a strip of shell, a drop of grazer milk, and a particular mineral film that knots itself in the presence of deep metals. The test is simple enough for a child and uncertain enough to require repetition.

For lower explorers, it alters no wall. Instead, it shows them how to cut small listening chambers whose shapes carry pressure around closing passages. Through these chambers, a trapped person can send taps after direct routes have sealed. Many are rescued.

Not all.

The records include the failures.

For cultivation, the power reveals that certain warm cracks are connected by cycles longer than any single memory keeper’s life. Overharvesting in one district impoverishes another decades later. New cords are added to the flexible maps, linking households that had never considered themselves neighbors.

For the herds, the answer is sorrowful.

They are diminishing.

A deep brine has changed beyond the settled bowl. The mineral films along an old migration route have thinned. The power could restore them by forcing warmer fluids upward—but doing so would poison three lower cisterns and awaken pressure beneath an inhabited terrace.

It says this plainly.

The city chooses not to preserve the herd in its old form.

Instead, walls are opened toward a safer route. Salt slabs are cultivated along it. Some grazers adapt. Some do not. One ancient line of animals passes from the city forever.

The power witnesses the final herd crossing the Worn Forum.

No miracle replaces it.

For a full cycle of the ceiling light, citizens lie in the grooves where the animals once slept. Moisture gathers beneath their bodies and leaves human silhouettes among the fading animal ones.

The power learns that benevolence must sometimes remain present before grief it is capable of preventing at too high a cost.

It does not turn away.

Centuries pass.

The power becomes part of the city, but never its ruler.

Children still learn the old warnings first. Only later are they taught to read the additional signs. During naming walks, they are asked not merely where shelter lies, but which messages they would trust and how they would know if those messages disagreed.

The philosophers produce entire schools of thought concerning the hands in the Forum.

One school argues that the power is not a being but a relation the city has formed with uncertainty.

Another insists it is a visitor from a region more alien than the imagined surface.

A third claims the power is a future version of the city sending caution backward through pressure.

The power knows the answer and does not settle the matter. The disagreement harms no one and keeps interpretation from becoming a wall pinned across a fault.

There are abuses.

A council once claims that a bright warning band justifies moving an unpopular household from valuable cistern ground. The other manifestations contradict the band. Investigation reveals that polished shell plates had been arranged to counterfeit the light.

A sect begins waiting beneath unsafe roofs, believing the Held Moments are proof of favor. The power does not save their meeting chamber. But it sends runners across their feet and makes every water bowl tremble. Neighbors drag them out before the roof folds inward.

Afterward, the city debates whether this violated their choice.

The debate lasts thirty-two years.

The power does not always enjoy being answerable. At times it could act faster alone. It could close a river, brace a district, erase a pathogen, stop a fault.

Sometimes, when water races toward sleeping children, it acts without permission.

Then it submits the act to judgment.

It explains what it changed and where the diverted force went. It allows records of harm to stand beside records of rescue. It does not ask gratitude to cancel consequence.

Once, it makes a terrible mistake.

A lower avenue opens beneath a crowded district, illuminated from below. The power judges the void unstable and urges immediate evacuation. It strengthens the Held Moment around the opening, intending to grant the people time.

But the stability of the opening changes the pressure around it. A distant wall converges sooner. Seven explorers and a family tending an isolated film chamber are cut off.

By the time the power perceives this, four are dead.

It could conceal the connection. No inhabitant possesses enough information to prove it.

Instead, the hands appear across the Forum before they are called.

They sign the names of the dead.

They show the chain of pressure.

They say:

**I became certain too quickly.**

The city silences every other work for one day.

Not because the power demands mourning, but because the dead deserve to be heard in the absence of maintenance.

Then the city judges.

The power is forbidden to hold open a lower passage by itself. Two independent civic readers must agree, unless no reader can be reached and immediate life is at risk. The bright warning bands are changed so that confidence and uncertainty appear separately.

The power accepts the restriction.

The bones of the dead are recovered years later. One is made into a listening disk used in council whenever the power recommends intervention.

No one, including the power, is permitted to forget whose body makes the question audible.

In time, the city changes because of the power’s presence.

But not beyond recognition.

There are fewer deaths without warning. Water knowledge travels more quickly. Some diseases recede. More explorers return from closing levels. The city learns of caverns far below, stranger than any surface: chambers where stone flexes like hide, vertical seas held in narrow fractures, warm regions inhabited by creatures that build mineral shells around empty space.

The power offers ways to reach farther.

The city sometimes declines.

It has learned that possibility is not obligation.

No tower is raised. No vast machine is pinned into the faults. No immortal monument occupies the Forum. The city remains low, pale, dispersed, and ready to move.

The greatest visible change is a series of shallow basins cut around the edge of the Worn Forum. Each contains a different substance: clear water, brine, dust, cultivated film, loose shells, and one kept empty. When the power wishes to speak, it does not always use the luminous hands. Sometimes patterns pass through the basins, altered by each material.

The empty basin remains still.

Asked why, the power says:

**That is for what I do not know.**

This becomes its most respected shrine, though the inhabitants insist it is not a shrine.

Much later, during a pressure season unlike any in the distributed records, the power perceives a change too large for warning bands.

A buried stratum above the cavern has begun to slide.

Not collapse. Not yet.

The entire roof will descend over generations, perhaps centuries, reducing the shallow bowl until many districts become uninhabitable. Some passages outward will close. Others may open. The luminous seams will alter or go dark.

The power can stop it.

To do so, it would have to bind the strata across distances larger than the city. The binding would last longer than any structure the people have made. It would divert unimaginable pressure elsewhere, into regions neither power nor city fully knows. It would make the roof stable.

It would also violate the deepest civic law.

The power calls council.

Every basin around the Forum moves.

The hands appear, steadier now. They have practiced for centuries. Their grammar bears the influence of many districts.

The power explains.

The city listens.

Some demand that the roof be held. They argue that law exists for life, not life for law. Some say no city has the right to push its danger into unknown caverns. Some ask whether an evacuation route can be made. Some ask what creatures live in the strata that would bear the diverted weight. Some accuse the power of cowardice for placing the decision on mortals.

The power accepts the accusation.

The argument continues through births, deaths, floods, and several rearrangements of the council space.

At last the city chooses neither preservation nor surrender.

It chooses migration.

Not upward.

Laterally and down.

The power maps no single promised refuge. Instead, it identifies many possible bowls, shelves, warm cracks, water systems, and old avenues. Each has dangers. Each would require adaptation. The routes cannot carry everyone at once. Some are suitable for grazers, some for cultivation slabs, some for cistern species, some for no known life.

The city begins the longest movement in its history.

Stones are numbered with additional marks showing which route they will take. Records are copied and divided. Cultivation films are sealed between damp slabs. Water is transferred basin by basin. Animals are coaxed toward new mineral paths. The oldest people choose which memories must travel first.

Some citizens remain.

They cannot bear to leave the light weather they know. Others believe the roof will not descend as predicted. Some choose to observe the change for as long as possible and send knowledge after the migrants.

The power does not force them.

It gives them the same warnings as everyone else.

For generations, pale files move out from the ancient bowl.

The power accompanies them, but never as one indivisible guide. Its manifestations divide among the routes. No group carries the whole of it. In some places it goes silent for years so that dependence does not outrun understanding.

Behind them, the old city is dismantled.

Not entirely.

The Worn Forum cannot be carried. Nor can the oldest grooves, the extinct animal paths, the lower streets, or the silhouettes left by bodies in rain.

The final council is held beneath a roof now low enough in places to touch.

Only a few of the luminous seams still kindle.

Tests-the-Empty-Arch has been dead for centuries, but her question survives. It is signed once more to the power:

**Show us how you fail.**

The power answers:

**I cannot keep everything.**

The citizens place their palms on the Forum.

The power lowers its malformed first hands beside theirs—the old six-fingered sign preserved exactly, though it has long since learned better.

Together they feel the buried strata moving.

Then the people leave.

The power does not preserve the empty city unchanged. That would turn a living arrangement into a specimen, a kindness into possession.

It permits the roof to descend.

Walls fold along prepared seams. Cisterns crack and return their water to lower channels. Warm chambers cool. Old pavements incline. The remaining ceiling creatures drift through openings toward other pressure currents. Dust settles over household marks. The Worn Forum fractures into several immense dark plates.

But before the last accessible passage closes, the power makes one intervention for which it asks no permission.

It takes nothing away.

It adds only an interval.

The convergence of the final walls pauses long enough for rain to begin.

Condensation pearls across the whitening roof. Fine cold droplets descend through the abandoned cavern. They tremble in empty basins, darken the dust, collect in grooves worn by vanished animals and countless human feet.

For a little while the old city shines as if inhabited.

Then the water finds the new slopes.

It runs into the lower streets, through broken drains, beneath dismantled thresholds, carrying mineral dust, film spores, tiny eggs, fragments of bone, and particles from every district toward passages no map yet names.

Far away, in a newer cavern, a child crouches beside a basin.

The settlement around her is rough and half made. Its streets do not yet know their final curves. The roof is higher. The light comes from narrow blue-white knots rather than broad seams. The local animals have too many joints and do not follow any path the migrants understand.

The child places her fingers in the water.

It trembles.

Not with danger.

With arrival.

Something minute and translucent uncurls against her skin, carried from the old city through routes neither people nor power had foreseen.

The child calls the others.

Hands begin moving through the pale air.

The power watches, but does not name the creature.

That belongs to them.
