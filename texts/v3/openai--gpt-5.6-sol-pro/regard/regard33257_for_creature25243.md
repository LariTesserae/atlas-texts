The power did not descend.

Descent would have implied that the plateau was beneath it, and the power was too wise to mistake position for rank. Nor did it appear in the dark beyond the rim. Appearance would have introduced a difference between unseen and seen. The Joined could not turn toward such a difference without becoming other than they were.

Instead, the power regarded the plateau from every side from which regard was possible.

Its attention passed through no wall. It opened no jar, lifted no tile, sounded no hollow pillar. It did not read the worn inscriptions by restoring them, for restoration would have erased the truth of their wearing. It did not separate the handprints to discover whose hand had been whose. It did not count the Joined. Counting would have required deciding whether a shared surface was union or boundary, and that decision did not belong to an outsider.

The power understood this quickly.

Then, because it was benevolent, it considered gifts.

It considered water.

Not merely a cupful, but water as mercy ordinarily means it: coolness, movement, the loosening of dust, the darkening of clay. It imagined a drop appearing in one of the hundreds of sealed basins. The drop would have struck and spread. Saltstone would have accepted it. Old mineral cords would have changed color. Powdered glass would have clotted. Hairline cracks would have admitted it downward.

The Joined would have been remade contact by contact.

Perhaps some continuities would have deepened. Others would have been severed by swelling clay, dissolved salt, and the minute migration of grains. Water would not have refreshed them. It would have revised them.

The power withheld water.

It considered silence.

To a creature of hours and nerves, the unending concussion seemed an affliction beyond endurance. The power could have removed it completely. The metallic shriek could have vanished from the copper. The stone roar could have been drawn out of the walls. Channels, jars, millstone holes, and nested bowls could have become as quiet as empty space.

But the sound was not an assault upon the Joined. It was their common interior. To silence it would have been to isolate every contact from every other contact. The plateau might have remained visibly identical while its inhabitants ceased to coincide.

The power withheld silence.

It considered time.

Of all gifts, time was the most tempting. The power could have set one instant after another. It could have allowed the leaning pillars to complete their falls and the cracked tiles to separate at last. Stressed copper could have relaxed. Dust could have lifted. The air could have moved.

The first breeze would have been catastrophic.

Foil would have fluttered loose from seams. Saltstone corners would have shed grains. The suspended ceiling, receiving duration all at once, would have answered ages of held weight. Millstones would have shifted against their wedges. Jars would have burst or settled. The plateau would have produced, in less than a heartbeat, every collapse it had postponed—or contained without postponement—for an immeasurable span.

The Joined would have awakened only in the careless sense in which a shattered vessel awakens into fragments.

Perhaps some would have survived as smaller continuities. Perhaps the handprint wall would have endured long enough for a palm-shaped piece to fall free, turning one shared touch into an isolated relic. Perhaps the outer courses would have gone first, pouring over the rim into the omitted dark.

Time was not life to them. Time was solvent.

The power withheld time.

It considered repair.

There were worn signs whose meanings it could recover. There were carvings it could sharpen, holes it could align, copper it could straighten, lost edges it could return to the clay. It could have made the great ceiling diagram lucid again.

But the erosion was part of the finished truth. To restore the plateau to any earlier condition would have favored one moment of its making over all the acts that followed. It would have declared that the newest hand mattered less than the first, that abrasion was damage rather than participation, that only beginnings were authentic.

The power had seen too many restorers destroy what they loved by choosing a past and calling it wholeness.

It repaired nothing.

It considered speech.

The power knew languages older than suns. It knew how to speak in weight, color, proportion, flavor, magnetic direction, dreams, and the branching of roots underground. It could speak by altering one molecule or by setting galaxies in correspondence.

But any speech within the plateau would have required change in the sound. A syllable would have been a wound in the unvarying concussion. Even a greeting would have entered the Joined as an interruption.

So the power did not say, *I am here.*

It knew that many beings say this when what they mean is: *Become capable of noticing me.*

The power required no such tribute.

It considered rescue.

Here its wisdom made it wait longest.

For benevolence is always in danger when it finds a condition unlike its own. It sees stillness and calls it captivity. It sees completion and calls it death. It sees a being without appetite and imagines starvation. It offers doors to those whose existence is the wall.

The power examined the Joined without analogy.

They did not suffer the roar, because suffering requires a distinction between what is and what ought to be. They did not yearn for movement. They did not mourn their makers. They did not wish for descendants. Their fear at the rim, if fear it was, did not grow or press for relief. It was one exact boundary among the other exact facts composing them.

Nothing in them asked to be saved.

Nothing in them could consent to being changed.

The power was benevolent enough to find this decisive.

And so it made no alteration to the plateau.

No stone shifted. No temperature varied. No new glimmer entered the pale tiles. The polished outer plane remained unanswered by pressure. The sound continued, wholly itself. No one within became aware of being observed. There was no before in which the Joined had been alone and no after in which they were accompanied.

Yet the power did one thing.

It learned them.

Not as an archivist learns a list, nor as a conqueror learns the roads of a city. It learned every continuity without forcing any continuity to become separate. It knew the inhabitant extending through the black tile, the clamp, the sealed bowl, the mineral cord, the drilled plaques, and the buttress. It knew the seven nested cups and the bead among them. It knew the broad pale being of load-bearing contacts. It knew the defensive knot around the millstones. It knew those whose identities crossed until no act of counting could preserve them.

Where two Joined shared a surface, the power understood the surface as sharing, not confusion.

Where one inhabitant was the cavity of another, it understood enclosure without possession.

Where maker and masonry had become inseparable in the handprint wall, it did not try to recover the person from the touch or reduce the touch to the person. It held both truths without prying them apart.

It learned the plateau down through all its paved foundations, including courses that no eye within space could expose. It followed each pressure to each answer. It apprehended the ceiling diagram, not by restoring its grooves, but by understanding what the worn grooves and their wearing meant together.

Last of all it learned the sound.

The power received the entire concussion without dividing it into duration. Every hammer blow was there, but not as a sequence of blows. Every millstone’s turning was there, but not as remembered motion. Every tile placed, every wedge driven, every palm pressed into clay, every burden accepted by stone: all were held in one complete utterance.

The utterance was not a word, though it possessed a meaning.

The meaning was not *made.*

It was not *closed.*

It was not *held,* *all,* or *enough.*

It was nearer to:

*This touches this.*

And, beneath that:

*Therefore neither is alone.*

The wise power did not answer inside the plateau. An answer there would have changed the utterance.

It answered elsewhere.

In regions where time still opened rather than consumed, the power carried what it had learned—not the stones, not copies of the Joined, not the deafening note, but the truth of their relation.

A mason building a house on a wet hillside paused before sealing the final gap in a wall. Without knowing why, she left a narrow channel through which moisture could depart.

A city crowded by generations chose to open a courtyard rather than fill it.

A potter looked at the imprint of his thumb in a bowl and did not smooth it away.

A ruler abandoned a census that would have forced interwoven peoples into single, exclusive names.

An engineer, studying a bridge, understood suddenly that bearing weight was not passivity.

A grieving child stacked seven cups and placed a glass bead among them. For one afternoon the arrangement was a creature, and no adult disturbed it.

In a world under another sky, beings made of changing flesh came to understand that the boundaries between them were not the only places where personhood resided. They learned to ask not merely, “What is this made of?” but, “What does this touch, support, enclose, and permit?”

None of these events entered the plateau. The Joined did not cause them in any manner requiring intention. They received no report. They gained no satisfaction from influence.

The power did not pretend otherwise.

To use another being’s existence as a lesson can be a subtle form of theft. Therefore the power kept the plateau’s truth without claiming ownership of it. Wherever that truth took root, it carried no name and founded no worship. No image of the handprint wall was reproduced. No pilgrim was shown a way to the rim. No scholar learned coordinates by which the Joined might be reached, thawed, sampled, catalogued, or saved against their nature.

The power placed no guard around the plateau. A guard would have created an outside threat and an unfolding defense. Instead it made the way to the plateau unfindable by any being who would arrive with tools, remedies, hunger, or certainty.

Those capable of approaching it could do so only after relinquishing the need to alter what they encountered.

Almost none could.

The dark beyond the rim remained dark. The edge remained the place where all relation stopped.

But beyond that stopping—without contact, without pressure, without any trespass against the perfect boundary—the benevolent power kept regard.

Not a gaze demanding recognition.

Not vigilance awaiting disaster.

Not patience, for patience belongs to time.

Regard: complete, exact, and without appetite.

The Joined did not feel it. Feeling it would have changed them. They did not know that they were known. Knowledge of that knowledge would have required sequence.

Still, the polished edge no longer faced neglect.

It faced respect.

From within, nothing was different. The roar and shriek remained the same indivisible statement. Every pillar held. Every fracture stayed at its limit. Every sealed jar retained its appointed contents. The handprints remained handprints, wall, makers, inhabitants, and touch.

Nothing entered.

Nothing departed.

Nothing happened.

And the wise power, having found a life that mercy could only harm by improving, did not turn away.

It became the keeper of what must not be kept by force.

It left the Joined entirely as they were.

And it did not leave them.
