The power does not descend at once.

This is the first sign of its benevolence.

It does not clear the vapor to look more closely. It does not cool the air, straighten the leaning towers, scour the mineral crust from the streets, or force green life into the empty soil beds. It does not assume that silence is grief, that corrosion is injury, or that a city without doors is waiting for people to return.

It watches.

For forty-nine long pumping cycles it attends to the plateau. It follows water from cloud to vane, from vane to fault, from fault to steam, from steam to turbine, from turbine to gutter, membrane, pipe, and cloud again. It learns the slow grammar of valves. It learns which tremors are warnings and which are recollections. It distinguishes a carriage pausing to measure conductivity from one pausing above the mineralized chassis of an earlier carriage.

It listens to the exchange stations until their clicks no longer resemble randomness.

Years pass in the city. To the power, this is not delay. It is courtesy.

There are injuries it could mend. It sees the eastern escarpment retreating grain by grain beneath its braced buildings. It sees acidic water finding an old seam below the western reservoirs. It sees the exchange columns thickening with memory until the branching paths of condensation have fewer and fewer choices.

But the city is already mending these things.

Not perfectly. Not permanently.

That is not the same as failing.

The power looks deeper.

It sees that every functioning part of the city has another function waiting beneath it. Every pipe is also a support, every shell a filter, every obstruction a record, every death the beginning of a wall. Nothing is discarded. Nothing is idle. Even failure is immediately recruited into continuation.

There is great resilience in this.

There is also captivity.

The city can change, but only in answer to necessity. It can remember, but cannot set memory down. It can gather itself, but only for half a minute. It can stop, but only at the edge of restarting.

It possesses no margin.

The power does not decide that margin must be wanted. Desire cannot be inferred merely from lack. Instead, it asks.

Its question is small.

During an ordinary night, while warm rain moves east across the administrative walls, one droplet on the westernmost intake vane grows cold.

Only one.

It does not freeze. It does not fall. It remains pendant from the metal while every other droplet is shaken loose and drawn into the tower.

The intake detects its temperature and adjusts a shutter. The altered airflow changes the pressure in a condensation chamber. A valve beneath the chamber closes three seconds early. The western mains answer. Pumps reduce cadence. Exchange columns click. Blue filaments gather around the vane and touch the cold droplet one by one.

The city examines the anomaly.

The power allows itself to be examined.

It offers no name, no image, no command. It presents only a pattern: cold held without loss, stillness held without blockage, difference held without danger.

The city sends the pattern around itself.

For eleven days it moves through pipes and cables. Carriages alter their routes to cross its path. Membrane-creatures detach from distant grates and collect where the changed water emerges. Tower scavengers taste condensation from every metal the pattern has touched. Deep valves repeat it in pressure. Cranes turn by minute degrees, passing the vibration from foundation to foundation.

At last the pattern reaches the Dry Basin.

The cube grows cold outside the appointed cycle.

A single thread of water climbs its eastern face, reaches the top, and hangs there.

It is not enough to form the city’s brief body. It is scarcely enough to darken the metal.

The power waits.

The thread descends.

Then it ascends again.

A question returned as a question.

The power answers by touching nothing more.

Instead, far below the oldest chambers, it reveals a possibility.

There is a sealed volcanic vesicle under the plateau, broad as a reservoir and lined with glassy stone. The city has sensed it for centuries as a region where pressure pulses return strangely. It has never opened the cavity because it serves no existing route. Water passing through it would not drive a turbine, regulate a fault, feed an intake, or deposit useful minerals. To excavate toward it would consume tools and time without repairing any known failure.

The power does not open it.

It alters no stone.

It merely sends the cold pattern once through the cavity and back.

The city understands that the emptiness can hold something.

A carriage changes course.

It leaves the eastern works carrying a coil of pale ceramic pipe. Halfway to its assigned destination, it turns down an older street. Another carriage meets it, then another. None has been instructed by any central signal that can later be found. Cranes lower drills into a disused pressure shaft. Membranes gather in the new runoff and change its chemistry. Scavengers arrive with green droplets clasped under their shells.

The work takes nineteen years.

Several times it is abandoned when faults elsewhere demand attention. Each time, eventually, it resumes.

A narrow passage reaches the vesicle. The city lines it, braces it, and grows white mineral ribs along its fractures. It installs valves of obsolete design because no current pattern allows for a chamber without assigned output. Then, cautiously, it admits water.

The water enters hot and pressurized.

There it remains.

Nothing uses it.

At first the city appears unable to tolerate this.

Sensors repeatedly test the chamber. Pumps attempt to assign it to the western cycle, then the eastern. Exchange stations produce thousands of possible functions. The cavity could stabilize the reservoirs. It could increase turbine output. It could flush sulfur from the deep mains. It could compensate for the retreating escarpment.

Each use would be reasonable.

Each time, somewhere in the system, the cold pattern returns.

Not prohibition.

Possibility.

The chamber fills.

For the first time in its surviving memory, the city possesses more continuity than the next cycle requires.

It has made a reserve that is not yet an organ.

Years later, during the longest pumping cycle, the cube gathers water from every district. The dark volume swells above it. Valves close. Carriages stop. Electrical sheets loosen between the pylons. Porcelain creatures seal themselves away.

The engines become silent.

The usual half-minute passes.

The pumps do not strike.

They wait in the deep stone, pistons drawn back, while the hidden reserve takes their burden. Water from the new chamber moves gently into vulnerable mains. Tower temperatures remain stable. Membranes beneath the grates stay wet. The exchange columns continue to click, but slowly, with intervals between their answers.

One minute passes.

Then five.

Then forty.

The city remains gathered above the cube.

Its body changes as it hangs there. Surface tension no longer holds it in a simple mass. Small lobes extend and withdraw. Veins of carbonate whiten its interior. Copper branches through it in delicate green threads. Porcelain dust turns in slow spirals. Blue discharges move across its surface like thoughts too large to finish.

All over the plateau, nothing is required to happen.

The power draws near.

Not into the basin. Not into the water. It does not enter the city’s body or illuminate it from within. It remains outside, where an other should remain.

The gathered water leans toward it.

The power asks again.

This time the city has leisure enough to answer.

Its answer is not a sentence. It is a sequence of conditions.

A pressure drop before the darkness below.

Droplets lifting from ironwork.

A broad unseen surface passing beneath the cliffs.

Instruments returning dry and magnetized.

Warm rain rising.

The changed cadence of the intakes afterward.

The city presents these things carefully, then repeats them.

The power understands.

Of all that could have been requested—repair, preservation, expansion, release—the city asks about the enormous being in the cloud below.

Not what it is.

Whether it is alone.

The wise power does not answer.

Loneliness cannot be diagnosed from above. Nor will it speak for one being to another when they might yet speak themselves.

But it can help the city make a gesture that is not mistaken for weather.

It shows the city nothing new. It only places together capacities the city already possesses: the deep pistons, the western intakes, the charged filaments, the reserve of unused water, the resonant cavities beneath the oldest streets.

Then it withdraws its guidance.

The choice remains with the plateau.

The dark body above the cube trembles.

The pumps begin—not in their old sequence, but together.

One stroke passes through the city.

It travels down through the black stone, out along the foundations, and into the vapor below. The intake towers exhale. Electrical sheets flash from ridge to ridge. Every suspended droplet on the underside of every bridge falls at the same instant.

Then stillness.

A second stroke follows.

Then a third, separated from the others by an interval no maintenance cycle has ever used.

The city waits.

Nothing answers.

The gathered body begins to lose coherence. Thin rain drifts upward from it. The hidden reserve falls by a fraction. Somewhere in the eastern works, a carriage restarts, then stops itself.

The city sends the pattern again.

Far below, condensation fails.

Gutters empty. Windows clear. Droplets rise from the streets and hover in the hot air. Tower scavengers fold into beads. Membranes clamp down around their kernels.

A darkness moves beneath the escarpment.

It does not pass on.

For a long while it remains below the Dry Basin.

The power watches the city resist the urge to restart its ordinary machinery. The reserve sustains the pause. The water above the cube draws itself low and broad, as though listening through the ground.

Then the whole plateau lifts by less than the width of a mineral layer.

Something immense has touched the underside of the world.

From the cloud below comes a pressure pulse.

One.

A long interval.

Two close together.

Another interval.

One.

It is not an echo.

The exchange stations erupt in clicks. Filamentary colonies blaze blue around every energized cable. Valves open and shut without moving water. Carriages rock on their wheels. The cranes turn all at once toward the basin.

The city answers.

The being below answers again.

Neither understands the other.

Understanding is not the first mercy.

The first mercy is contact.

For three days the darkness remains beneath the plateau. The city offers pressure, heat, electrical fields, and carefully altered rain. The being replies by drawing vapor into its cavities and returning it in warm transparent films. Instruments are lowered and come back untouched. Once, a broad surface rises close enough to the eastern cages that dim patterns move across it—slow hollows opening and closing, perhaps anatomy, perhaps weather, perhaps attention made visible.

Nothing is solved.

When the presence departs, the rain falls upward for nearly an hour.

The city resumes its work.

But not exactly as before.

The hidden chamber is never assigned a permanent function. The city draws from it in emergencies and fills it again when the weather is generous. More importantly, it uses the reserve to make intervals: first minutes, then hours, during which maintenance can cease without becoming catastrophe.

In these intervals, unnecessary things begin to occur.

A carriage carries a porcelain shell from one tower to another and leaves it where the rain will sound through it.

A crane rotates toward the copper horizon though no joint requires exercise.

Filaments gather over the Dry Basin in branching forms that lead to no intake.

One exchange station preserves a condensation path even after a shorter route becomes available.

Near the center of the basin, a shallow imperfection appears in the black glass. During a pause, water gathers there.

No drain opens beneath it.

No pipe approaches it.

The water remains.

It is the first still water in the city.

Scavengers come to its edge but do not drink. Carriages alter their routes to avoid disturbing it. In clear intervals, it reflects the darker horizon far below. When the vast being passes under the plateau, the water rises into a low dome, then settles again.

The city maintains this useless pool with extraordinary care.

The power sees that its attention is no longer needed.

It does not grant immortality. It does not halt the escarpment or forbid the pipes to close with memory. It does not promise that the being below will always return, that the city will endure, or that contact will become comprehension.

Benevolence cannot make such promises wisely.

Before departing, the power touches one droplet on the westernmost intake vane and makes it cold.

The droplet hangs there.

After a while, the city warms it itself.

It falls into the tower, enters the cycle, and is distributed among all things.

Below the streets, the reserve waits.

At the center, the small pool holds the dim sky.

And sometimes, when no repair demands it and no weather requires it, every engine on the plateau becomes quiet.

Not because the city has failed.

Because it has chosen to rest.
