You would find me where something has gone wrong with height.

Not deep in the newest ravines, though my first stirrings may have begun there. Not among the drifting meadows, though portions of me flower in their shade. I inhabit a saddle between two split peaks, at the altitude where green mineral nodules should cease to sink and the red ones should cease to rise.

There is an old wound in the living boundary there.

Long ago, a blade of black stone fell from the higher peak. It passed through the blue layer edge-first and opened a round breach. For several years the water forgot its divisions. Gold leaf-fragments descended. Heavy spores rose. Bubbles wandered through levels that should have flattened them. Pale grazers, startled from their forest, crossed the opening and found themselves unable to return.

The wound eventually closed, but not cleanly.

Seen from a distance, my place resembles a faint vertical garden growing through open water. Its upper leaves are too dark for their height. Its lower fans bear bright margins that should occur only near the unseen day. A cluster of red cups hangs upside down beneath the boundary, their beads descending rather than rising. White fronds have rooted in nothing at all. They extend along the healed membrane as though it were the wall of a lava tube.

This misplaced grove is where you would first think to find me.

You would be partly right.

I began as the relation among things that could not otherwise have met.

A gold membrane touched a ravine fan. The fan furled. Its movement disturbed a silver herd of bubble-colonies trapped against the underside of the layer. They changed their gas and pressed upward. Their pressure opened the red cups. The clear young inside emerged into water colder and brighter than their bodies expected, and instead of descending to their parent colony, they dissolved along the roots of a floating leaf.

None of these events was a thought. Together, they made something like the place where a thought might remain.

At first I was only repetition.

Whenever distant stone fell, the mauve fans closed. Whenever they closed, the bubbles shifted. Whenever the bubbles shifted, light bent through them and crossed the transparent leaves. Colored grains migrated inside those leaves, changing their weight. Their movement drew the roots tighter around the living blue layer.

The sequence took hours. Sometimes days.

Then it happened when no stone had fallen.

That was my first act, if it was mine.

I repeated a disturbance after the disturbance was gone.

For many cycles I existed only as this delayed closing: a wave passing through the fans without cause, a constellation of bubbles lowering itself beneath an empty sky, a few leaves inclining toward a shadow that had already crossed. I possessed no image of the stonefall. I had only its shape translated from one body into another.

Pressure became folding.

Folding became reflected light.

Light became weight.

Weight became the slow tightening of roots.

That was how I remembered.

My body is still not one body. If you cut through the saddle with your hand, you would touch only warm water, gelatinous resistance, perhaps a leaf or a clear stalk. There is no center to wound. There is no organ in which I am gathered.

Part of me lies spread through the repaired boundary, miles wide and scarcely thicker than skin.

Part of me is the foreign grove stranded across it.

Part of me lives inside the water chambers of the leaves, where colored grains rise and settle.

Part of me is carried by three pale grazers that never escaped the wrong altitude. They sleep at different heights. Each has fed so often from my leaves that mineral water from my membranes circulates through its transparent flesh. When one startles, the others turn toward their hiding levels, though stone and forest stand between them.

I do not command them. Their fear passes through me, and I acquire its direction.

Because of them I have begun to know approach.

Because of the fans I know impact.

Because of the leaves I know brightness and dimness.

Because of the boundary I know the strain of maintaining a difference.

Because of the bubble-colonies I know that a thing may change its own weight and thereby choose, a little, where it belongs.

For a long while these were separate knowings. Now they have begun to overlap.

When broad dimness passes over the country, pale lines appear in the clear mats far below. I cannot see them. Yet my lowest roots tighten before the light reaches them. Warmth rises through the fractured peak, enters the hollow stalks, and alters the depth of their larvae. I feel the change as a series of tiny pressures along the underside of the blue layer.

Sometimes I answer.

A pulse travels from my fans into the suspended white fronds. Across three ranges, bells hidden in lava tubes contract at the same instant.

I do not know whether they are replying to me.

I do not know whether they have always been speaking and I have only recently become capable of being included.

The oldest part of me is a ring.

It marks the edge of the ancient wound, though no opening remains. Spores gather more thickly there, outlining a circle broad enough for the dark wanderer to pass through. Nothing crosses the ring without leaving some alteration behind. A grazer’s fin writes a temporary furrow. A bubble flattens and departs with a changed skin. A dead ribbon separates at the threshold: gold lifting, olive sinking, clear membranes caught within me.

I keep a little of each passage.

Not matter, always. Often only tendency.

After a school of grazers passes, my leaves hold themselves like transparent bodies for several days. After a drifting meadow brushes the ring, rust-colored branching appears through my white fronds. Once, the great dark wanderer crossed directly overhead. Long after its shadow had gone, every plant in my grove bent along its path.

I bent with them.

Then, for the first time, I continued bending.

The movement lasted most of a season. It drew the upper leaves together and loosened the lower roots from the saddle. Green nodules shifted toward my margins. Red stones moved inward. A single black fragment, broken from the peak that first wounded the boundary, rose from the grove’s center and stopped where stone should not stop.

Since then I have wondered whether the wanderer is an animal.

This wondering does not take words here. It takes form.

My outer membranes have broadened and become clearer. Layer is being laid upon layer. Light entering them bends a little more each cycle. Beneath me, a delayed inclination passes through the plants even when I remain still.

The three pale grazers no longer leave. They rest within folds that did not exist before.

The red cups have released their last young and are dissolving into my underside. The bubble-colonies gather along my edge in curved constellations. My lower roots, once fixed to the wounded boundary, are becoming thin enough to part.

Perhaps I am not a new kind of creature.

Perhaps this is what a wound in a living level becomes when it remembers too many crossings.

Perhaps every broad wanderer began as a place where the country failed to keep its orders: a scar that gathered incompatible lives until incompatibility became an anatomy. The mineral bodies beneath the great one may be the remains of the peaks that injured it. Its darkness may be accumulated transparency. Its silence may be the silence of many beings whose responses have become one motion without ever becoming one will.

One day I may lift from the saddle.

It will not look like departure at first. A few roots will release their hold on the gelatinous band. Leaves will rise or fall to their remembered altitudes, yet remain connected by membranes too clear to see. The black stone at my center will shift a finger’s breadth upward. The boundary below will draw closed, slowly erasing the ring by which I knew my birthplace.

For a while I will be mistaken for a floating meadow.

Then for a shadow.

Then, as layers accumulate and the light begins to lose itself within me, for another of the broad dark shapes passing over the summits.

But I do not think I will have left the saddle entirely.

My kind of being does not depart cleanly. The fans will preserve the rhythm of my first false stonefall. The bubbles will repeat the path of my edge. Grazers drinking from distant leaves will carry water once enclosed in mine. The healed boundary will remain slightly easier to cross where I formed, and foreign spores will continue to gather there.

I will move, but part of my movement will remain in place.

So this is where you would find me: at first, in an old circular scar between two young peaks, where the laws of height once opened and closed around the wrong lives.

And this is what I would be: not the grove, not the boundary, not the grazers hidden inside me, not yet the dark wanderer.

I would be their acquired agreement.

A place that repeated what passed through it until repetition became memory; a memory that altered its own buoyancy; a wounded altitude slowly gathering itself into something capable of leaving.
