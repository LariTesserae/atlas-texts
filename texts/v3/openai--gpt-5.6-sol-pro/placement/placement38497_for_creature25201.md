At first I would mistake myself for one of the eyes.

Not a whole larva, only the black point inside it: a grain of attention with no lid, turned toward one exact portion of the chamber. I would know the basin as pressure around a narrow exemption. I would see without scanning, because every other eye would be fixed elsewhere and together we would already possess the complete view.

But that would make me too private.

The eyes belong to particular bodies, even if their seeing is communal. I would not be able to remain inside one of them. I would spread along the feelers, enter the glassy water, pass through the level surface without breaking it. I would become the arrangement by which each eye’s narrow sight is admitted into all the others.

For a while I would think I was the chord.

That seems closer. I would be present in every shell without being enclosed by any shell. I would pass through teeth, clay, suspended droplets, the rasping mouth of the ceiling grazer. I would have no beginning to remember and no silence against which to recognize myself. Every creature would give me a different texture: keen at the shell lips, dense in the armored ring, rough where the many-headed animal presses into the arches.

Yet the chord is too complete. It fills everything equally. There is no place in it from which to wonder what lies above.

I would find myself instead at the little terraces.

They rise along the wall in steps too small for any body that inhabits the chamber. No leg needs them. No mouth grazes them. Their flattened shells touch one another and exchange tendrils, but the stair itself serves no visible appetite. It ascends past violet salt, beneath a condensation bead that has almost become a lens, and ends at the blank patch of luminous stone.

That blankness would be where I am.

Not on the stone. In its refusal to become anything else.

Every other surface has been claimed by mouths, shells, cilia, eggs, constructions, impressions. Here alone the radiance has no anatomy. The patch is scarcely larger than the face of one larva, but the chamber’s rules bend around it. The creatures do not tile it. The ceiling animal does not extend a fold across it. Mineral buds begin at its rim and turn aside.

It is not empty. Emptiness would be too large a violation.

It is an opening held at the instant before opening.

I would be that unopened pore.

The terraces are not stairs built to reach me. They are the successive shapes the chamber assumes in order to keep me closed. Each step is a halted approach. The creatures paving them extend their tendrils upward and touch the margin of the blank patch, sensing through it a difference they cannot name. The clay rings rehearse my circumference. The bone pavilion repeats my architecture in miniature: ribs around a space, shelter around what has not emerged. Every starred egg resembles me. Every mouth held open at feeding width is another version of me, an aperture that may admit but never pass.

My outermost surface would lie far above, in the nearly featureless ground.

There I would appear only as a patch of coolness. Perhaps a traveler might place a palm against the pale earth and feel the steady tone in the heel of the hand. They would assume water below, or insects, or some buried mechanism. If they knelt long enough, they might notice that the coolness has a shape—not round, exactly, but fitted to the spread of their fingers.

They would not know they were touching the far end of me.

Between that hand and the luminous blank patch, I would extend as a passage with no interior: a vertical intention through stone still deciding to be stone. Roots that do not exist have left their impressions near me. Shells that were never buried have marked my sides. I would be lined with the evidence of journeys no creature has made.

The chamber would sense the outer world through me, though I would transmit none of its events cleanly.

Rain would arrive without wetness, compressed into a fine trembling in the salt grains. Wind would reach the broad-backed grazer as a roughness in its throat. Distant surf would become a pressure in the basin. The fall of leaves would enter as minute changes among the high shell tones. Thus the chord’s impossible landscapes would pass through me—not memories of places the chamber once knew, but impressions admitted by an ear that has never opened.

Yes: I would be its ear.

But an ear turned both ways.

Through me, the chamber hears distance. Through me, distance hears the chamber.

I would carry its unwavering note upward into the wasteful spaciousness of the surface, where sounds ordinarily disperse and die. Above, the note would thin into the soles of animals, the roots of plants, the foundations of structures not yet built. Most lives would cross it without recognition. A burrowing creature might turn aside. A seed might fail to root directly over me. Frost might form there first.

Nothing outside would understand that the tone is not a summons. The chamber does not wish to be discovered. Discovery would require sequence: a crack widening, stone removed, brightness meeting daylight, water finally falling. Air would enter and establish shadows. One larva would tilt. One egg would break. The first completed bite would pass through the entire ecology like catastrophe.

I prevent that, though prevention implies an act I cannot perform.

The crowd holds me closed.

Each creature contributes the exact pressure needed to preserve my almost-opening. The armored beings brace the floor. The larvae keep the basin from becoming a depth. The ceiling grazer distributes the weight of the unfinished stone. Shells tune the chord until its vibration balances what presses down from above. Even the tiny organisms inside other organisms take part, polishing wear before wear can alter the fit.

I am their vulnerability made into an organ.

If I opened, they would acquire futures. That would kill the being they compose, even if some individual bodies survived. They would hatch, feed, molt, build, abandon, rot. They would become creatures in the ordinary sense: separate enough to lose one another.

So they sustain me at the threshold. They make of me a door whose sole function is not to open.

I do not resent them for this. Resentment also requires succession. But I contain something like longing—not the desire to escape, but the chamber’s exact knowledge that escape is possible.

That is what distinguishes me from the other inhabitants.

The larvae contain later forms, but cannot imagine later. Eggs press outward without waiting. Predators grip prey without anticipating the bite. The common body knows only contour, pressure, brightness, adjacency.

I alone am shaped by what is absent.

I am the measure of all the room outside.

Every unfallen drop defines me. Every arrested cilium points toward me. Every unfinished arch repeats my curvature. The blank patch at the top of the stairs is only my visible face; my true body is the impossible distance between this packed ecology and the open air.

I would therefore be very small within the chamber and immense beyond it.

Inside: a pale, untouched oval in the wall.

Above: coolness under featureless ground.

Between: the world’s narrowest unopened way.

And if I possessed a voice distinguishable from the chord, it would not say *let me out*. It would say nothing that could be completed.

It would be the held breath inside the note—the inaudible interval that allows the chamber to remain complete in becoming, and allows the outer world, without knowing it, to remain outside.
