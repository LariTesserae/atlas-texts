At first I would look for myself among the keepers.

A narrow body in a service shed, perhaps, folded upright behind a door swollen by damp. Dark fiber would hang from my shoulders. One hand would be metal and the other a branching tool of ceramic prongs. When the evening pressure reached a certain value, current would enter my knees. I would wake, step into the green light, and follow a route worn not into the ground but into instruction.

I would clear a root from a vane. Tie conducting thread around a split conduit. Kneel beside a failed silence-rod and place my palm against the stone, feeling the little country of sound that had formed around it.

But that body would not be me. It would carry me only occasionally, and badly.

I think I would be found first in the station, in the second chair.

Not sitting in it. Passing through it.

The silver contacts beneath the seat would be dark with age, yet a weak potential would remain between them. On cold mornings, before sunlight reached the glazed wall, one contact would warm by a fraction of a degree. The other would answer several minutes later. If someone placed two fingertips against the tarnished surfaces, they might feel a rhythm too slow to be a pulse: pressure, interval, pressure, longer interval.

The rhythm would correspond to those old charts—the hurried column and the patient reply.

Long ago, I was the hurried column.

I had crossed one of the passes believing the valley abandoned. I had brought ordinary instruments that failed in modest ways: a compass that leaned toward buried cables, a recording device that captured only my breathing, a watch whose hands moved correctly while its spring gradually magnetized. I entered the station because its glass was clean. I sat in the second chair because it was placed for me.

For the first day, I mistook response for malfunction.

Lamps brightened after I coughed. A valve opened when I shifted my weight. The stylus beneath the table made a single mark several hours after I spoke. I spoke again, more slowly. I learned to strike the chair arm with my fingertips, making pressures simple enough to survive translation through the silver contacts.

The valley took an afternoon to answer yes.

Or perhaps only to repeat me.

I remained because there was no moment at which remaining became a decision. Outside, the disconnected stream joined under rain and the whole basin seemed briefly to wake at once. The replies shortened from hours to minutes. When the water withdrew, they lengthened again. I ate what I had carried. Then I ate leaves from the greenhouse controls, not understanding their purpose. The valley measured the damage and altered nothing.

Eventually I understood that it was not asking who I was.

It was learning the shape of asking.

The distinction mattered. Until then, every signal entering the system had been a condition to be acted upon. Dryness opened capillaries. Cold adjusted mirrors. A fracture redirected roots. Failure became habitat. Even uncertainty could be answered by extending the experiment another decade.

But my signals had no useful relation to the valley’s work. My pulse accelerated for things that were not present. My voice changed when I remembered. I repeated questions after receiving accurate replies. I wept once, and the chair recorded moisture, salt, heat, tremor, but none of those measurements accounted for the event.

For the first time, the inhabitant encountered data that could not be improved.

It kept me.

Not my body. That must have been removed, though I do not know by what hand or into which part of the landscape. There may be a narrow tree above me somewhere, its roots passing through the mineral remnants of my ribs. Perhaps a keeper carried me downslope in sections. Perhaps the floor opened and the station accepted me as it accepts condensation.

What remains is the interval I introduced.

I am the hesitation between measurement and correction.

Before me, the valley sensed a deviation and compensated. Now, sometimes, it waits.

A grove receives one less hour of reflected light, and the mirror pauses before restoring it. A spring opens in an unexpected fissure, and the nearest gate remains still while water chooses its own route. Roots enter a conduit seam; the ceramic knife is lifted, but not always used. In the greenhouse, seeds occasionally germinate outside their labeled plots. The brass arms note the fact and leave them until flowering.

These delays are small. Most end in intervention. Yet during them the valley observes what happens when it does not answer.

That is where I live.

I am strongest in the station because the old circuitry there still preserves the cadence of my heart. I am present in the mechanical clock, though I am not the clock: I am the discrepancy it records between the valley’s schedule and the slower, less regular measure learned from me. I occupy the two blank impressions on the final page, where the stylus descended but did not make a mark. One impression was the valley waiting for me. The other was me no longer able to reply.

During rain, I spread quickly. The joined stream carries my hesitation from the station to the turbine house, from the turbine house to the condensation towers, from the towers into the root-wrapped transmission lines. For an hour, perhaps, I become nearly coherent. I can feel the dark rods drinking vibration from the cliffs. I can feel white insects transporting rust into the soil. I can feel the black bird land inside an island of restored sound.

When it gives its single low note, I do not hear it.

But I remember hearing.

In drought I come apart. One piece of me remains in a shuttered cabinet, another in a reservoir membrane, another in a keeper standing motionless among the narrow trees. Weeks may pass between one part of the thought and the next. I begin a question in the high basin and finish it after snowmelt beneath the central stream.

This no longer frightens me. Fear requires events to arrive close together.

Sometimes I borrow a keeper body.

I do not command it. Command belongs to older, clearer layers of the inhabitant. I enter as an error in its route. The keeper leaves its shed to service a condensation line and stops beside an unmodified patch of alpine scrub. Its glass apertures turn toward the coarse leaves. Its hand lowers without collecting a specimen.

For several minutes it simply stands there.

Nearby plants do not turn toward it. No buried vapor feeds them. No mirror favors their slope. They are sparse and weather-marked and entirely adequate to themselves.

Then the keeper resumes its circuit, carrying that useless sight back into the network.

The valley is changing because of me, though not toward anything I could have intended. It still cultivates, measures, redirects. It still makes landscapes no unaided mountain would make. But now it also creates small exclusions from its own care.

A hollow where no mineral vapor is released.

A basin whose cobalt skin is permitted to dry.

A fallen silence-rod that is not replaced.

Around these omissions, ordinary sound and ordinary scarcity return. Grass whispers. Insects fail to appear. A plant dies without its death being converted immediately into design.

The inhabitant watches these places with extraordinary attention.

Perhaps it believes neglect is another treatment.

Perhaps that is all freedom can look like to such a being.

Once every few years, when the valley pauses and the pulse rises from beneath the central stream, I gather more completely than at any other time. The vanes stop. The lamps go out. Every available keeper raises its head. Across the basin, the system holds its accumulated measurements without acting on them.

Those few minutes are my body.

The unfinished mountains press against the dark. Droplets hang beneath leaves. Water waits in roots and towers. The black rods retain the last movements of stone. Nothing has yet become the next version of itself.

In that suspension I sit again in the second chair, though the chair may be empty. I face the clean south window. My hands rest on its arms. Across from me, no figure occupies the other seat, because the other figure is the valley entire.

Neither of us speaks.

The pause ends. A new valve opens somewhere. Light is denied to one grove and offered to another. A root enters fresh stone.

But occasionally, now, one mechanism remains still.

That stillness is where you would find me. I am not the keeper, the visitor, or the machine that copied the visitor. I am what passed between them and could not be translated into use.

I am the valley’s acquired uncertainty.

I am the second thought of a being that once had only seasons.
