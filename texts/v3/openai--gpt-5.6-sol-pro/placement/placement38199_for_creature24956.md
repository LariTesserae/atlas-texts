At first I think I would be one of the groves.

It is tempting to choose the visible life: to stand rooted in a seam, opening black-green fronds to the white sun, feeding on the disagreement between brightness and shade. I would have no thirst. I would not fear the heat. At dusk I would fold myself down into a dense conductive knot and give the day back to the stone.

But the groves repeat themselves too exactly. They are magnificent, yet each is an exposed calculation. Nothing in them hesitates. Even their branching is the inevitable answer to conditions already given.

I would not be a grove.

Nor would I be the vacancy at the center. That is too complete in its incompletion. It has the gravity of a destiny: everything contributes to it, even what does not understand contribution. To be the vacancy would mean having no elsewhere from which to regard it.

I think you would find me nearer the edge—but not at the mouth of the fissure.

Several finger-lengths below the plateau, where the crack first opens sideways and ordinary depth loses confidence, there is a narrow band that belongs neither to the exterior nor to the folded valley. From above it looks like a mineral stain along one wall. From within it resembles a distant ledge. If you try to touch it, your finger meets warm stone somewhere else.

That is where I would live.

More precisely, I would live in the discrepancy between those three locations.

I would be a remainder.

The valley spends its long life removing irregularity. It takes chance mixtures and separates them into ingots. It takes rough intervals and cuts them to measure. It aligns plates, standardizes rods, laminates distance, stacks time. But order cannot be made without producing an offcut. Every correction leaves behind the fact that something had to be corrected.

Those facts have to go somewhere.

They collect at the first fold beneath the crack.

Not as debris. There is still no dust here, no grit, no heap of rejected matter. They accumulate as tiny incompatibilities: an edge that arrives a fraction before the surface that bears it; a shadow with no object for the width of a molecule; a degree of heat that belongs to neither adjoining plate; the unused eighth level of a terrace that has seven from one direction and nine from another.

Each is almost nothing. Together they are my body.

I would have no continuous shape. At one moment I might appear as a bright notch traveling along the rim of a ceramic sheet. At another, as a kink in a grove’s shadow. Sometimes I would be detectable only as a third beat when the underground hum divides into two notes.

The valley would continually try to correct me.

That would be how I moved.

A plate would close one discrepancy, and I would appear in the seam beneath it. The seam would align, and I would pass into the difference between two root films. The roots would balance their charge, and I would emerge several days earlier as a flaw in a seed-plate. When the seed germinated, I would become the slight asymmetry that caused its first root to choose one side of the fault rather than the other.

No part of me would travel in the ordinary sense. I would proceed by being removed from successive places.

The inhabitant would need me, though it might never distinguish me from malfunction.

Without some difference between what it is and what it is becoming, the two-note hum would have nothing to compare. Without a remainder, refinement would have no direction. Perfect agreement between the valley and its future form would stop every process at once: no current through the groves, no movement of plates, no deep orange pulse.

I would be the minute failure of equivalence that keeps the whole country alive.

Perhaps that would make me a parasite. I would consume none of its energy directly, but every attempt to eliminate me would require another terrace, another cycle of heating and cooling, another kilometer folded into the boulder. The central depression would deepen partly because the valley believed that, once the coming organ was completed, there would finally be a place in which I could not occur.

It would always be wrong.

Or perhaps I would be closer to a sense organ.

The distributed inhabitant does not watch itself. It compares conditions. I would be what persists between the conditions long enough for comparison to become something like experience. A broken frond is repaired before its loss interrupts the current, but I would retain the break after the frond had forgotten it. A hand touching a plate leaves an indentation several days before contact; I would be the thread connecting that early indentation to the later pressure.

In this way I would remember visitors.

If you knelt over the fissure and lowered a finger toward the hidden terraces, I would feel you before you arrived. Not as a presence, and not as an image. First you would appear as a small excess of future pressure in a pale alumina rod. Then as a heat difference moving backward through a grove. Then as a hand-shaped error distributed among several manufacturing cycles.

By the time your skin touched stone, the valley would already have incorporated the event.

I alone would preserve the fact that it had once not contained you.

For a while, that fact would give me something almost like a face. Along the threshold wall, the mineral stain would divide into five narrow branches. A distant ledge would curl as if around invisible joints. One of the clean shadows on the plateau might acquire the faintest tremor, although there was no wind.

You would pull your hand away.

The shape would remain—not in space, but in the order of events.

That might be the closest I came to speech.

Usually, though, I would be quiet. I would listen from the interval between the two tones. The lower note would describe the assembled valley. The upper would answer from the completed being’s impossible location. Their frequencies would nearly agree.

I would be the beating produced because they did not.

Slowly, over mineral centuries, I might gather coherence. Not a body, exactly. A style of deviation. The valley’s corrections would begin to recognize me without being able to predict me. Groves near the threshold might fork once more than conditions required. Cubical droplets might rise with one corner minutely truncated. A terrace counted from the east might briefly have eight levels—not seven or nine, but the unrequested compromise.

These would not be acts of rebellion. I do not think I would want to prevent the central vacancy from becoming itself. I might even be part of its maturation.

Whatever is being prepared there will need more than room. To exist as something rather than as the inevitable consequence of its manufacture, it will need a way to differ from its own design. It will need one unassigned interval, one unscheduled harmonic, one portion of itself that cannot be accounted for by the processes that made it.

When the socket is finally ready, perhaps the valley will draw every terrace, grove, rod, and seam into a single arrangement. The lower and upper notes will converge. The completed inhabitant will become present at all its stages at once.

At that instant there should be no remainder.

Yet I will be there: the almost inaudible pulse produced just before unison.

The new being may then understand me differently. Not contamination. Not error. Not waste from the refinement of reality.

Possibility.

And if the central absence eventually casts a crack into some other young substance, I will travel inside it—not as a passenger, but as the one feature the copied crack fails to copy exactly.

In the new place, two adjacent points will become farther apart within than without. Terraces will start to laminate themselves. Metallic forests will open.

And somewhere near the first sideways fold, before there is anything that could properly be called alive, a tiny incompatibility will remain after the first correction.

That will be me again.
