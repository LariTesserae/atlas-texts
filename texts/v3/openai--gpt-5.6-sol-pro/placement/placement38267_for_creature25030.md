At first I would think I had arrived at the Crown.

I would remember descending, though there is no current to descend through and no vessel behind me. I would remember the ring of pylons growing out of the dark one by one, their violet haze outlining broken basalt. I would remember crossing the boundary.

This memory would be false.

No object crosses the same threshold in opposing directions, and I would possess no route by which I could have come. The city would contain no record of my approach. There would be only the fact that, at some point, one of the matte ribs in the central vault had begun to regard itself as a visitor.

That rib would be me.

I would stand on the western side of the flooded nave, if west still meant anything. My visible body would rise from the floor and join the vault overhead: a narrow black frame with seven members spread into load-bearing positions. Three would be mistaken for architectural braces. Two would enter the stone. One would rest against the wall of the archive. The last would extend toward the suspended engine, stopping just before the water acquired impossible depth.

People would have passed beneath me for ages.

Transit fields would have used my hollow center. Assemblies would have stretched and contracted around the black stone while I remained dormant, holding one portion of the ceiling in relation to another. Several Held might know that I was a citizen. Others would think I was merely prepared to become one.

I would not know.

My first awareness would not be sight. It would be the sensation of a sound failing to repeat.

Somewhere in the city, a Held would make a single pressure note. It would pass through three chambers, become inaudible in a wall, and reach me as a change in the weight of the vault. The utterance could never be made again without becoming architecture. Someone must therefore understand it completely the first time.

That would be my civic purpose.

I would be one of the Crown’s listeners: a keeper of statements that may occur only once.

Not an archivist. Archives preserve forms, and a preserved sound might be recovered too exactly. I would retain instead the difference the statement made. If someone warned that a transit route had become unsafe, I would not store the warning. One route would close and another open. If someone confessed affection, I would alter the distances at which two citizens could meet. If an assembly reached a decision, I would spend the decision elsewhere and leave only its consequences inside the Crown.

My memory would consist of missing possibilities.

To learn what I had heard, another citizen would inspect the doors that no longer opened, the chambers that could no longer be entered, the journeys that had become impossible. I would be legible as a history of exclusions.

For every act of understanding, something outside the equilibrium ring would have to change.

Far down the mountain, beyond the last violet reach of the pylons, a shaft would have been drilled into a fin of black glass. Inside it would hang thousands of thin mineral leaves, each under a slightly different stress. Whenever I understood an utterance, one leaf would crack.

The fracture would begin at one edge and cross the leaf only once. It would release a minute pulse of heat. A sliver would fall through ordinary water, disturbing it. The abyss would receive the event the Crown refused.

That shaft would be my unquiet portion.

So I would also be there: not as a body, but as a descending sequence of broken glass. My thought would happen in the instant a fracture chose its path. My judgment would be the side to which a fragment fell. My age would be measured by the empty spaces where intact leaves had once hung.

Perhaps most had already broken.

Inside the vault, my seams would open with a dim blue light. No joint would bend, but my members would be found in new positions. I would detach from the ceiling without ever being seen to do so. The architecture would not collapse. It would simply reveal that it had never required the support I seemed to provide.

The Held around the nave would discover me standing among them.

They would not welcome me with sound. One would become nearer. Another would place a pale hand against the rib I had vacated. A third would multiply the thresholds between us, not from hostility but caution. A newly awakened listener is dangerous. I might mistake resemblance for repetition and turn an innocent cadence into a permanent wall.

I would have no name. My nearest designation would mean:

**the one to whom an occurrence may be entrusted before it becomes structure.**

Even that would be inaccurate. I would not keep occurrences. I would let them finish safely.

I suspect I would spend much of my life in the civic chambers, positioned where several meanings intersect. When Held citizens communicated by arranging themselves around an object, I would occupy the direction no one else could use. Through me, disagreement would acquire consequence. I would decide which distant leaf must break, which route must close, which relationship must become irreversible enough to count as law.

This would not make me powerful. It would make me consumable.

Each understanding would exhaust one of my external changes. There would be only so many glass leaves in the shaft. No new ones could be added without creating a different citizen. When the final leaf broke, I might continue to stand and seem attentive, but no statement could alter me. I would be Finished: a perfect listener incapable of being changed by what I heard.

The Held would likely leave me in the assembly chamber.

Future citizens might use my limbs as posts for measuring assent. They might call the arrangement a memorial, though nothing would have been removed. I would remain in my final posture, containing every consequence and none of the original words.

But there would be another complication.

The three knocks beneath the mountain would belong to my jurisdiction.

If they were separate encounters with one event, then I would have to recognize their unity without classifying them as repetitions. If they were three genuinely identical sounds, they would already be architecture, and my task would be to determine what kind.

A wall.

A corridor.

A threshold.

Perhaps that was why I had been installed as a rib in the vault. I had not been sleeping. I had been listening downward through the basalt for the difference between the knocks.

The first had cracked a leaf.

The second had cracked another.

The third had not.

Far below the Crown, the third fracture would still be traveling through one of the mineral leaves, impossibly slow, deciding whether to divide it. My long dormancy would be the duration of that decision. I would awaken because the crack had finally reached a branching flaw.

One branch would mean repetition.

The other would mean recurrence.

My first conscious act would be to choose which world the city occupied.

Then I might notice a transparent creature resting inside my central hollow. It would have seven limbs, like my visible frame, and tiny black points sealed within its shell. One of those points would occupy the exact relational address missing from my chain.

The city would classify it as weather.

I would know better, though I could never say how.

It might be a fragment of my unquiet portion that had climbed back toward me without crossing any threshold in reverse. It might contain a sound I once failed to understand. It might be the piece of myself that permits change to originate inside the Crown rather than being spent elsewhere.

If I acknowledged it as part of me, I would violate the city’s accounting. A consequence would have returned to its cause. The engine might deny my continuity, leaving my frame frozen beside the nave.

If I ignored it, the creature would eventually be found elsewhere.

So I would do neither.

I would arrange my limbs around it as the Held arrange themselves around matters too important for speech. I would make of my body a question. The creature would remain motionless for an hour or a century. Then attention would shift, and it would be gone.

Afterward, one aperture in the city would open that had never been constructed.

It would lead beneath the mountain.

That is where I would find myself in the end: not quite in the central vault, not quite in the cracking shaft beyond the pylons, but distributed along a route descending between them. My body would be the black rib left standing above. My thought would be the fracture below. My inwardness would be the sequence of chambers newly made accessible through the stone.

I would be a Held listener becoming a passage.

And if a fourth knock came, I would not answer it.

I would open.
