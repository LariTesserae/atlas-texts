At first I think I would be a traveler.

The Last Pavement seems to demand one: a small, low body moving across the white immensities, following warmth that began its journey centuries before. I imagine a mantle assembled from many jurisdictions—smoke-colored ceramic from an absorbed city, amber glass gathered beneath a transit rib, a strip of violet alloy that rises slightly during charge weather. My feet would be replaceable. My underside would contain instruments whose purposes I understood only through pain.

I would travel slowly enough that the journey altered me more than distance did.

But the image does not remain. There is too much exposure in it, too much romance in the horizon. The Interval do not travel into emptiness. They travel through permissions, and permissions have edges.

So I think you would find me at an edge.

Not the edge of the Last Pavement. No one has found that. I would live along one of the intricate borders where two repair tides have met and failed to agree.

From far away, my country would look like a dark thread laid across the plain. Up close it would be several kilometers wide: nested ridges no higher than a finger joint, fields of precisely spaced pits, ceramic curls, erased grids, rebuilt grids, and walls so minute that dust grains lodge against them like boulders. On one side, the pavement remembers the district as a radiance-transfer surface and tries to keep it smooth, bright, and warm. On the other, it remembers the same district as an obsolete containment apron and tries to make it dark, porous, and cold.

Their argument has lasted longer than the species that issued either instruction.

The border advances a little during thermal migrations and retreats during charge fronts. At intervals, one tide dismantles a shape the other immediately reconstructs. There are structures there that have been destroyed and restored so many times that neither side remembers their original design. They have become pure insistence.

The Interval settlement among them is called, in approximate translation, **While the Matter Remains Unresolved**.

Travelers shorten this to **the Stay**.

It is a refuge for things whose status is uncertain.

Broken registrants are brought there before the pavement can revoke them. Caravans leave objects they cannot safely carry across the next province. Gleaners with damaged tags gather in its shallow hollows. Sail-creature eggs that answer diagnostics incorrectly are rolled there over decades. Families come carrying infant pearls that one district has rejected but another may yet accept.

Nothing is truly safe in the Stay. It is only undecided.

I would be one of the beings who maintain that indecision.

My body would be Interval in origin, though no longer easy to count as one body. I would have begun in a pearl capsule placed too near the border. During my first years, the bright tide classified me as a mobile calibration standard. The dark tide classified me as contamination enclosed for transport. Each therefore prevented the other from dismantling me.

My family would have discovered this by accident.

They would spend decades terrified that one side might prevail. They would adjust my heat signature, alter the conductivity of my shell, and tap contradictory ancestries into the ground. To the bright district they would say:

*This object is necessary because it measures the fault.*

To the dark district:

*This object is the fault and must remain contained.*

I would be born when both answers were accepted at once.

Among the Interval, my pressure-name would contain the two diagnostic pulses followed by the long silence in which neither repair tide moved. In spoken translation, I might be called **Pending**.

I would be narrow when young, with eight quick limbs and a mantle divided sharply down the middle. One side pale and reflective; the other black and heat-drinking. Crossing the border would warm one half of me and cool the other, and I would feed on the difference inside my own armor.

That would become my work.

I would learn the incompatible dialects of the repair tides: not language as the Interval speak it, but the vast procedural grammar of timing, geometry, voltage, and matter. A row of raised pins might mean *awaiting component*. A shallow hexagonal depression might mean *damage already recorded*. Three grains of violet alloy placed at unequal distances might cause a diagnostic to repeat rather than conclude.

Most of these signs would be discovered through loss.

The Stay would bring me rejected things, and I would try to make the pavement hesitate around them.

For a child pearl, I might construct a ring that resembled an unfinished socket. I would warm the pearl in pulses associated with expected machinery, then tap a maintenance request through the underlying plate. If the bright tide approached, I would identify the child as an instrument. If the dark tide approached, I would identify it as sealed waste that must not be breached.

Sometimes the grains would divide and flow around the capsule.

Sometimes they would not.

I would keep no tally of successes. The unsuccessful attempts would already be written into my mantle: tiny vacant settings where fragments had been removed, pale channels left by catalytic grains, patches whose conductivity changed whenever the ground drew water from something I had failed to save.

Because I lived among contradictory systems, travelers might think me a liar.

The Interval would not.

To them, truth is not one correct description laid over the world. Truth is the description under which a thing can continue.

Over centuries I would become broad and slow. Those I helped register would surrender fragments for my armor: part of a gleaner’s reflective membrane, a sliver from a caravan elder’s resonant plate, the emptied shell of a child who survived and molted. My mantle would grow into an irregular mosaic stretched along the disputed seam.

More contact needles would descend from me until I no longer merely listened to the border but participated in it.

I would feel the bright tide gathering its arguments kilometers away: shutters opening, matter packets changing route, a pressure of encoded light entering the seams. From the other direction would come the dark tide’s answer, slow and granular. Their conflict would pass through my nervous tissue.

At first I would translate.

Later I would intervene.

A command arriving from the east might leave me with one parameter altered. A prohibition moving west might acquire an exception. The changes would be minute—less than errors, at first. A repair grain would pause one moment longer before dismantling an unfamiliar surface. A sensor pin would test twice. A registrant whose answer was almost acceptable would be placed in a deferred category rather than erased.

The settlement would begin to expand around these pauses.

Eventually younger Intervals would join themselves to me. Some would be parents who had waited beside endangered pearls. Some would be travelers too altered to recover their former warrants. Some would be elders drawn to the idea that uncertainty could be inhabited.

We would become an accord, though an unstable one.

Our thoughts would not blend cleanly. The bright-facing bodies would remember events differently from the dark-facing ones. A journey might belong to six of us on one side and to none on the other. We would argue through our shared seam, sending memories back and forth until they wore smooth enough to pass as principles.

Our visible bodies would settle among the miniature walls. Dust would fill the spaces beneath our mantles. Repair structures would be built through us; wire-thicket filaments would root in abandoned limb sockets. Gleaners would tap warnings against plates that were also our bones.

In time, no visitor could say where the settlement ended and we began.

Then perhaps I would cease to be Pending.

Or rather, the individual called Pending would become too small a description. The name would spread into the local rules. Travelers would learn the Stay as an instruction:

*Here, carry no object whose purpose is entirely known.*

*Here, contradictory warrants must be presented together.*

*Here, walk only while the repair grains are motionless.*

*Here, an unrecognized living thing is to be treated as a question, not as debris.*

That last rule would be mine.

Not a law I could announce. The Last Pavement does not accept declarations. It would have to be worn into the place through repetition: one delayed diagnostic, one divided repair tide, one tolerated anomaly after another. Thousands of years of persuasion until hesitation became cheaper for the district than certainty.

By then my original water would have been distributed among generations. My soft inner knot would be gone. Perhaps no single living cell of me would remain. Yet the border would still pause around new movement. Sensor pins would rise, lean inward, and wait.

The Interval might say I had become weather.

That would be close, but not exact. Weather moves. I would be more like a narrow defect in inevitability.

You would find me where the pale pavement darkens into its century-long argument. There would be no monument. Only low ridges, breathing plates, old bodies flush with the ground, and a procession of families carrying pearl capsules between them.

If you stepped there, lenses would turn toward you.

Repair grains would emerge.

The world would begin deciding whether you belonged.

And then, for reasons buried beneath every surviving explanation, it would stop.

Not forever.

Only long enough for you to answer.
