## Overall assessment

Treat the plateau as a **causally exhausted ecosystem**, not a wilderness frozen in time. Its animals are not statues, its cliffs are not necessarily stable in the ordinary sense, and its apparent stillness is not permission to interfere. Everything appears to participate in permanent relationships that may include observers once they touch, frame, count, feed upon, or otherwise complete them.

An ordinary human should visit only with a reliable return aperture and an outside support team. If your method of departure requires retracing your route, waiting for a scheduled pickup, or reaching a moving vehicle, do not enter.

## Essential equipment

### Wear a sealed field suit

The climate itself is mild, but the biology is nested beyond naked-eye resolution.

Use:

- A fully sealed suit with independent air supply
- Smooth outer surfaces without seams, fleece, hook-and-loop fasteners, or exposed fabric
- Disposable overshoes and outer gloves
- Eye protection with mechanical shutters
- Broad, rigid soles resistant to silica edges
- A pressure-tested helmet and a filter capable of capturing ultrafine mineral and biological particles
- A suit exterior that can be abandoned at the exit

Do not rely on the fact that breathing feels easy. The atmosphere may merely accommodate the posture of breathing without exchanging gases normally. Monitor blood oxygen, carbon dioxide, blood glucose, temperature, and heart rhythm directly. Carry your own air even if instruments initially declare the atmosphere breathable.

### Bring all water and food

There is no usable environmental water. Do not expect dew, melting frost, plant fluid, animal blood, or moisture in shaded rock. Bring more water than the planned duration requires, in sealed containers that do not need to be refilled.

Eat and drink by an external schedule rather than appetite. In a place where events fail to replace one another, hunger and fatigue may not develop in a trustworthy sequence.

### Avoid equipment based on continuous motion

Wheels are particularly ill-suited. So are rolling cases, bicycles, drones that navigate by dead reckoning, and instruments that assume distance accumulates smoothly.

Prefer:

- A backpack rather than a cart
- Short, deliberate foot travel
- Solid-state instruments with no exposed moving parts
- Redundant cameras using different optical principles
- Inertial, magnetic, optical, and portal-relative navigation together
- Physical measuring rods for local distances
- A nonlocal locator tied directly to your home universe

Mechanical clocks may tick without establishing that one moment has displaced the last. Carry several kinds of clock, but obey the elapsed time reported by an external observer beyond the plateau.

## The return system

This is the most important preparation.

Your return route should be maintained by machinery and personnel outside the plateau. Ideally, the portal remains continuously open and the exit is recalled directly to you rather than requiring you to walk back to it.

If that is impossible:

1. Keep the entrance continuously in sight.
2. Remain on the threshold side of the first topological inconsistency.
3. Use a tether that can be severed from either end.
4. Have an outside operator monitor its length, tension, mass, and continuity.
5. Establish an immediate extraction condition for any duplicated signal, unexplained extra length, or disagreement between observers.

A rope alone is not adequate. It could remain attached while acquiring more length than the intervening country can contain, pass through repeated regions, or connect to more than one apparent version of a traveler. Use it only as an alarm and physical aid, never as proof that the way home remains geometrically intact.

Before entry, agree on identity checks that do not depend solely on appearance or memory. Include private challenge phrases generated outside the plateau after entry. If two versions of a team member return, do not ask either to approach the aperture until remote quarantine procedures are ready.

## Party organization

Never travel alone. A three-person field party is preferable:

- A lead observer watching the terrain
- A biological-contact observer watching suits and nearby organisms
- A rear observer maintaining uninterrupted visual contact with the exit or last verified marker

A fourth person should remain outside.

Keep the group close enough for direct speech. Radio echoes or repeated sounds may be locally recycled and should not be treated as proof of another person’s position. If you hear your companion from the wrong direction, look at the companion you can see; do not investigate the voice.

Do not allow the party to surround an object or herd. Since animals differ by viewing direction, observations made from opposite sides may establish incompatible descriptions. Approach, if necessary, from one agreed bearing and withdraw along the same local line.

## Navigation

Ordinary maps will become unreliable quickly. The plateau permits local approach while violating the larger arrangement of places.

Use **paired dark and pale markers**, matching the apparent practice of earlier travelers. Place them without disturbing existing pairs. Record:

- Which stone is nearer the exit
- Their orientation relative to the fixed sun
- The view from the approach side
- The view back toward the previous pair
- The externally measured time of placement

However, do not assume that a marker proves you have been there before. Old footprints and current footprints can coexist. You may encounter your own marks ahead of yourself or see both marked and unmarked states of one surface.

Adopt these rules:

- Never cross a ridge if its far valley was not visible before the crossing.
- Do not enter a gap that appears larger inside than outside.
- Do not follow channels, especially those running uphill or ending in open air.
- Do not navigate toward horizon creatures. They are not reachable landmarks.
- If the exit appears from two directions, stop rather than choosing.
- If the sun changes angle, leave immediately; whether it is the sun or you that changed is immaterial.
- If team members disagree about the number of limbs, posts, animals, or fingers on a nearby form, retreat without recounting.

Do not walk merely because the terrain is physically easy. The absence of slipping, wind, heat, and fatigue removes the ordinary warnings that would limit travel.

## Conduct around the life

Assume every visible organism is alive and every apparent contact is physiologically active.

Do not:

- Touch hides, shells, feathers, plants, eggs, bones, lichen, or mineral seams
- Step on animals, even when they bear weight
- Open an eyelid
- Place anything in a mouth, claw, coil, or gap between bodies
- Separate predator from prey
- Remove parasites
- Break ceramic-thread plants or copper fans
- Collect samples
- Feed anything
- Attempt rescue
- Insert probes into the stone’s pores

A glove does not make touch safe. Contact may establish a relationship rather than transfer a contaminant. A creature need not bite, move, or visibly react for you to have become part of its feeding arrangement.

The nested ecologies are a serious quarantine threat. A mite may carry organisms, which may carry smaller organisms, and the progression may continue below your detection limit. Nothing collected from the plateau should be brought home, including dust, compressed powder, air, tool scrapings, or suit swabs.

If any creature appears on a team member where none was previously seen, do not brush it off. Withdraw the affected traveler into an isolated return container and abandon the outer suit under remote control.

## Ruins and maker structures

Treat all shelters, walls, seats, screens, cairns, and alignments as active apparatus.

They appear intended to clarify relationships—to make a creature, absence, direction, or correspondence more definite. Standing in the intended viewing position may therefore be participation.

In particular:

- Do not sit on stone seats.
- Do not pass beneath horn-and-tendon roofs.
- Do not look through hide screens from the associated seat.
- Do not stand inside the figure-shaped empty volume.
- Do not complete circles, walls, fences, or chip-lines.
- Do not pick up abandoned tools.
- Do not place a hand over an absence-handprint.
- Do not count fingers on unstable prints.
- Do not repair anything.
- Do not move paired marker stones unless they are unquestionably your own.

Perforated vessels and sealed cups are not failed domestic objects. Their uselessness for water probably means they were designed to hold relationships, openings, exclusions, or some other nonmaterial quantity. Leave them exactly where they are.

The summit chair is especially dangerous. Its polish implies repeated use in a world where wear should not accumulate. Sitting there may be one of the few acts that still produces cumulative consequences—or it may place the sitter in the permanent role of viewing the plateau. Observe the summit structure only remotely. Do not enter the four pillars.

## Cognitive precautions

The plateau’s anomalies often become stable when observed. Counting, identifying, and comparing may not be passive acts.

Team members should avoid categorical statements such as:

- “That is the same animal.”
- “There are exactly seven fingers.”
- “The valley is behind us.”
- “This patch is untouched.”
- “The ridge is one creature.”
- “Nothing occupies that space.”

Use provisional language in the field: “appears from this bearing,” “at least,” “in this view,” and “not presently resolved.”

Do not stare at repeated animals trying to determine which is original. There may be no original. Likewise, do not test whether scars remain facing north by circling an animal. The experiment could entangle your own viewpoint in the shared surface.

Wear a visor capable of going opaque instantly. If a handprint, eye, empty outline, or repeated body begins changing according to your attention, break visual contact before discussing it.

Maintain a spoken identity log at fixed intervals, prompted from outside:

- Name
- Mission
- Number of companions presently visible
- Location of the exit
- Most recent action completed
- Next intended action

If answers become simultaneously contradictory rather than merely confused, extract the whole party.

## Duration and physiology

Keep the first visit extremely short: twenty external minutes is enough. Later visits should still be measured in hours, not days.

Do not sleep there. Sleep would remove continuous self-observation and may allow resting, waking, and dreaming states to coexist spatially or anatomically. Do not administer sedatives except as part of emergency extraction.

Watch for:

- A footprint appearing before the foot is placed
- Seeing both an action and its unperformed state
- A wound without an injury event
- Loss of hunger, breath urge, blinking, or heartbeat awareness
- Equipment readings that remain normal despite obvious depletion
- Memory of having always stood in the current position
- The conviction that departure is unnecessary
- Companions seeming less like moving people and more like complete postures

That last sensation is an emergency. The plateau may assimilate visitors not by attacking them, but by making their presence into a fixed ecological fact.

## Emergency rules

Withdraw immediately if:

- The portal or return beacon changes position relative to the fixed sun
- Any traveler appears more than once
- An animal gains markings resembling a team member or piece of equipment
- Your shadow stops matching your current posture
- A footprint remains visibly both present and absent
- The route behind becomes shorter without a matching external measurement
- A local creature begins visibly breathing in time with a traveler
- A shelter or fence now appears constructed around the party
- A sound from the team repeats with exact pitch and spacing from elsewhere
- Anyone feels they have occupied the plateau before the expedition began

Do not run. Running assumes that successive strides produce successive locations. Move in synchronized, deliberate steps while the external team brings the exit as close as possible.

If one traveler becomes fixed—unable to lift a foot, break eye contact, or stop touching a surface—do not pull them free by hand. A rescue attempt may simply extend the relationship to the rescuer. Use a remotely released line once, with a predetermined force limit. If that fails, extraction requires nonlocal intervention, not additional people.

## Decontamination

Return directly into hard quarantine.

Recommended procedure:

1. Seal the arrival chamber before closing the portal.
2. Image each traveler and compare body topology, scars, fingerprints, retinal patterns, and internal anatomy with pre-trip records.
3. Remove the outer suit robotically.
4. Do not incinerate or chemically digest residue until its behavior across time is understood; destruction is an event the material may not accept.
5. Contain residue in an isolated vessel without an ecological substrate.
6. Observe travelers for duplicated injuries, persistent impressions, synchronized symptoms, or organisms visible only from one direction.
7. Keep all plateau material out of living biospheres indefinitely.

Also compare each traveler’s mass before and after entry. An unexplained increase might indicate attached ecology; an unexplained decrease could mean some surface or state remains on the plateau.

## Best first expedition

The safest meaningful visit would be modest:

- Enter no more than ten meters.
- Remain on bare mineral ground.
- Do not cross between animals.
- Place one paired marker only if the ground is clearly unoccupied.
- Take panoramic images from a single fixed bearing.
- Record atmospheric and suit data.
- Withdraw after twenty external minutes.
- Bring back no sample.

The plateau rewards neither courage nor range. Its summit offers a magnificent view, but magnificence is not worth becoming part of the view.

Visit as lightly as possible. Touch nothing. Finish no pattern. Accept no seat. Most importantly, preserve the habit of leaving. In a world where nothing waits to happen and nothing is ever finished, departure may be the most unnatural—and therefore the most difficult—act a human can attempt.
