## General assessment

This is not wilderness. It is a functioning closed installation whose machinery, weather, and animal ecology are parts of the same maintenance process. Treat the entire continent as one enormous machine—possibly one enormous organism—and assume every apparently empty space has an assigned purpose.

The chief danger is not attack. It is **incorporation**.

Anything introduced here may be detected as contamination, misplaced inventory, a maintenance fault, or an unfilled ecological role. The installation appears gentle because it wastes nothing and allows little disorder. That gentleness may extend to dismantling, sterilizing, relocating, or replacing a traveler without obvious violence.

Make the first visit short: six to twelve hours. Do not plan an overnight stay until an instrumented expedition has confirmed that local cycles do not alter memory, physiology, equipment identity, or the accessibility of the return route.

## Before crossing

### Establish a return route that does not depend on local infrastructure

Use a self-powered, continuously open return aperture if possible. Do not rely on:

- local electricity;
- local clocks;
- radio relays placed on the installation;
- a portal anchored only to floor coordinates;
- any hatch, doorway, lift, bridge, or service corridor remaining in its present state.

Anchor the return point to your own transit frame, not to the paving. A plate may be replaced with an identical plate while you are away. It may have the same serial number, weld marks, and microscopic scratches and still no longer possess the same multiversal address.

Leave an observer outside the world. Agree on a hard extraction time measured externally. If you miss two check-ins, they should retrieve you rather than wait for an explanation.

### Record yourself

Before entry, make redundant records of:

- fingerprints, retinal patterns, dental scans, and genome;
- blood chemistry and microbiome samples;
- scars, moles, healed fractures, and other small asymmetries;
- autobiographical facts known only to you and the outside observer;
- exact wear patterns on clothing and equipment;
- the mass of every carried item.

This is not paranoia. The installation replaces worn things while preserving their form. A returning traveler may be physically perfect, sincerely convinced of continuity, and nevertheless be a local replacement.

Do not use philosophical arguments as a substitute for checking.

### Quarantine both ways

Arrive in sealed clothing. Depart into full biological and material quarantine.

There is little conventional soil ecology, but the place is densely alive at microbial and sub-microbial scales. Its filter animals and symbionts extract nutrients from condensation films and pipe emissions; some may colonize sweat, lungs, fabrics, or equipment seams. Conversely, ordinary terrestrial microbes may be treated as severe contamination.

Bring no loose seeds, untreated wood, paper litter, exposed food, or porous natural-fiber gear. Scrub boot treads and inspect pockets before crossing.

## Recommended equipment

### Clothing

Wear:

- a light positive-pressure environmental suit;
- a smooth outer layer without dangling straps;
- electrically insulating, chemical-resistant gloves;
- non-slip ceramic-rated boots;
- a cooling undergarment;
- sealed eye protection;
- hearing protection that can amplify low sounds while suppressing sudden machinery noise.

The air is warm, saturated, and still. Sweat will not evaporate effectively. Heat stress can develop even when the temperature feels merely uncomfortable.

The suit should tolerate warm water, oils, sterilants, mineral-rich rain, and brief contact with energized metal. Bright warning colors are preferable to camouflage, but avoid patterns resembling local alignment marks, barcodes, hazard stripes, or serial panels.

### Instruments

Carry independent instruments for:

- temperature and humidity;
- oxygen, carbon dioxide, ozone, hydrogen sulfide, and solvent vapors;
- electric and magnetic fields;
- radiation;
- surface voltage;
- vibration and pressure changes;
- local time versus an external reference;
- inertial navigation;
- continuous body mass and vital-sign monitoring.

Use multiple clocks based on different principles: mechanical, quartz, atomic or nuclear, and one synchronized through the return aperture. If all local clocks agree while the external one differs, trust the external one.

A laser rangefinder and polarized light are useful. Water films can conceal shaft openings, transparent membranes, black-glass edges, and animals flush with the paving.

### Mobility and marking

Bring a safety line attached to the return frame, but keep it elevated and under tension. A cable lying on the ground may enter a groove, drain, conveyor slot, or animal recess and be treated as installed material.

Do not use paint, chalk, tape, flags, or piles of objects to mark your route. They may be removed immediately, copied indefinitely, or interpreted as instructions. Use inertial mapping and wearable beacons broadcasting to the external side.

Carry a broad, lightweight insulating mat for emergency kneeling or medical treatment. Never lie directly on the floor.

### Food and water

Bring all water in sealed containers and carry it out again. Local water may look exceptionally clean while containing:

- dissolved metals;
- microscopic filter organisms;
- maintenance chemicals;
- signaling compounds;
- sterilants;
- nutrients intended for local animals;
- machinery-compatible lubricants.

Do not eat openly. Use sealed suit ports. The smell of food, skin oils, exhaled carbon, or organic waste may activate collection systems.

Pack no food that flakes, sheds crumbs, or releases strong odors.

## Choosing an arrival site

The safest initial landing is a dry, open maintenance terrace with:

- clear visibility in every direction;
- no animals within at least fifty meters;
- no floor markings, engraved characters, sockets, sumps, or drainage grooves;
- no overhead cables;
- no nearby hatch seams;
- no detectable vibration;
- no weather boundary crossing the site.

“Empty” must be established instrumentally. A flat floor may be the flexible roof over one of the buried creatures. Look for minute rhythmic lifting, warm districts, pressure pulses, and screws or access panels moving by fractions of a millimeter.

Avoid central works on the first expedition. Density there creates too many overlapping systems, and the air itself may contain allocated organisms or traffic corridors.

Do not land in a basin. Water levels can change uniformly and without warning, and the local animals may shift simultaneously. Their movement need not be aggressive to crush a person.

## How to move

Walk slowly, single file, and on broad unmarked plates. Test surfaces for voltage and vibration before stepping onto them.

Do not step on:

- serial numbers;
- alignment crosses;
- engraved letters;
- standardized recesses;
- darker rectangles;
- bolt heads;
- weld seams;
- grilles;
- apparently decorative grooves;
- dry spots inside wet districts;
- wet spots inside dry districts.

The crustaceans occupying engraved marks indicate that symbols and recesses may be ecological addresses. Standing in one could make you an obstruction or an occupant.

Never straddle a seam. Plates may separate, flex, heat, or exchange fluids without audible warning.

Cross rain boundaries only after checking air composition, water chemistry, electric field, and visibility on both sides. The weather zones are machinery, not fronts. A railing dividing wet and dry air may also divide pressure systems, sterilization regimes, or biological enclosures.

If every nearby animal changes posture, stop immediately. Do not assume they are reacting to you. A pump cycle, drain operation, pressure change, or hidden vehicle may be imminent. Watch their orientation: they are better instruments for the installation than you are.

## Conduct around the animals

Most local animals seem highly regulated and may be indifferent to visitors. Indifference is not tameness.

Maintain distance. Do not touch, feed, ride, sample, tag, or deliberately provoke anything. In particular:

- Do not pull shelled creatures from drainage lips. They may be filters, valves, sensors, or all three.
- Do not wade through fish-filled canals. A school that holds position may conceal strong suction or exact flow requirements.
- Do not walk beneath cable-roosting fliers. Their substitution cycles may include falling bodies, electrical discharge, or automatic replacement.
- Do not use columnar animals as supports, even if walkways have been built across them.
- Do not approach scavengers waiting beside a living animal. The scheduled death may involve machinery, toxins, rapid depressurization, or closure of the entire compartment.
- Do not enter ranks of herd animals. When a drain opens, thousands may shift in perfect synchrony, leaving no space for evasive movement.

Predators are probably less dangerous than maintenance fauna. Predators consume scheduled bodies; maintenance organisms may decide that your shed skin, blood, food, waste, or entire person belongs in a closed recycling stream.

If a small animal attaches to the suit, do not tear it off unless it compromises breathing or mobility. Photograph it, retreat toward the portal, and allow quarantine personnel to remove it under containment. Forced removal may trigger a response from the larger system.

## Do not imitate the installation

Resist the temptation to press buttons, complete patterns, replace missing objects, align loose items, or follow signs simply because they look official.

The clean warning colors and numbering may not be instructions for humans. A label could be:

- a mating signal;
- an anatomical marker;
- a machine-readable boundary;
- an instruction valid in a calendar with no elapsed time;
- an identifier rather than a prohibition.

Likewise, do not connect your equipment to local sockets, even if the voltage appears compatible. Their networks may carry nerves as well as power. A diagnostic handshake could register your device as a prosthesis, parasite, fault, or newly manufactured component.

Never enter a hatch that opens after your arrival. It may be an invitation, but the more likely possibilities are collection, replacement, pressure equalization, or the corresponding opening created by a death elsewhere.

## Waste and injury

Carry every emission you can:

- urine;
- feces;
- blood;
- vomit;
- food residue;
- used filters;
- damaged fabric;
- hair and nail fragments.

Use a sealed waste system integrated into the suit. Do not leave even a bandage. Organic debris is actively collected here, and collection may begin before you are ready to part with it.

If injured, retreat at once. Blood may attract sterilizers, scavengers, sampling devices, or population-balancing mechanisms. Treat wounds inside the suit and do not stop near a drain.

If you see an exact duplicate of a dropped item, abandon both versions and return. Do not spend time deciding which is original.

If a companion disappears behind an obstruction and two apparently identical companions emerge, do not conduct the dispute locally. Bring neither through the primary portal without quarantine and external identity testing. If forced to choose, maintain observation of both and request remote extraction.

## Temporal and cognitive precautions

The installation does not merely resist decay; it appears to resist consequence. Travelers should monitor for subtler forms of return:

- repeating conversations;
- finding yourself at a previously visited plate despite continuous forward movement;
- photographs changing to match the current state;
- batteries returning to earlier charge levels;
- healed scratches on equipment;
- identical raindrop patterns;
- animals repeating a substitution sequence exactly;
- memories of having just arrived, despite external elapsed time;
- companions disagreeing about whether an event occurred.

Keep a spoken log transmitted continuously outside the world. Local storage may be restored along with everything else.

At fixed intervals, answer unpredictable questions sent by the outside observer. Predetermined passphrases are insufficient: a replacement may inherit them.

Do not remain merely because you feel you have only just arrived. That sensation may be part of the equilibrium.

## Camping

Ordinary camping is strongly discouraged.

A tent creates a new enclosed volume, alters condensation, blocks assigned floor area, traps local organisms, and sheds organic material. Sleeping also prevents you from noticing slow pressure changes or the installation’s attempts to classify you.

If an overnight stay is unavoidable:

1. Use a suspended, sealed bivouac attached only to your own freestanding frame.
2. Place no anchors in the paving.
3. Keep the floor at least half a meter above the surface.
4. Maintain external visual and biometric monitoring.
5. Rotate watches; no one should sleep unobserved.
6. Set an externally controlled extraction alarm.
7. Inspect all sleepers before waking them for changes in mass, scars, suit serials, and recorded physiology.

Do not sleep near warm hatches. Warmth here may be body heat.

## Emergency signs requiring immediate withdrawal

Return at once if:

- animals begin orienting toward you in expanding rings;
- rain starts falling only on you or stops precisely around you;
- nearby serial numbers change;
- a clean recess appears matching the size of your boot, pack, or body;
- local machinery reproduces one of your possessions;
- a hatch opens at the moment of an injury or equipment failure;
- your waste canister becomes lighter without being emptied;
- you hear your own voice from a conduit;
- clocks disagree in a stable pattern;
- the portal frame acquires local numbering, weld marks, condensation channels, or attached shell animals;
- a companion’s old injury disappears;
- the path behind you looks newly manufactured;
- scavengers arrive and wait nearby.

The last sign is especially serious. Do not wait to learn whose removal has been scheduled.

## What the visit will feel like

Expect sensory unease rather than spectacle. The bright sky and ordinary rain make the place harder to dismiss as alien. Its scale will not announce itself with noise. A mountain-sized factory may be quieter than a kitchen. You will hear water tapping, tiny shells closing, a distant relay, your own suit fans, and occasionally the almost inaudible movement of thousands of bodies shifting together.

The lack of wind will make distance seem fixed. With no vegetation, soil, smoke, ruins, or irregular settlement, there are few familiar cues for age or scale. A tower may be one kilometer away or fifty. A herd may be animals or surface fittings until one of them blinks.

The strongest psychological pressure will be the sense that everything already has a place. Every groove carries water. Every letter has its crustacean. Every cable vacancy participates in a pattern. Every death corresponds to an opening elsewhere. A human visitor, sweating and shedding cells and making irreversible decisions, may feel offensively unfinished.

Keep that feeling. It is protective. Do not try to belong.

Visit as a sealed, temporary discrepancy. Observe from the margins, leave nothing, accept nothing, and depart before the installation decides how to make you fit.
