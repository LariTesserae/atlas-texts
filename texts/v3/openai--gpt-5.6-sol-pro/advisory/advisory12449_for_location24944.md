## Expedition assessment

An ordinary human should not enter this chamber casually, alone, or for more than a brief reconnaissance. It is not merely a cold cave containing unusual organisms. It is a developing living system with partial control over space, time, heat, water, and biological identity.

Assume the grove can detect:

- body heat and pressure;
- breath, blood, skin oils, and fear chemistry;
- movement and intention;
- biological ancestry;
- observation itself;
- any attempt to impose a path.

It does not need to be hostile to be lethal. Its greatest danger is that it may regard a visitor as unfinished ecology.

The safest visit is short, wakeful, quiet, sealed, and conducted with an exit already open.

## Before entering

### 1. Establish the date of the annual beat

Do not enter unless the last beat of the dark central cavity is known with high confidence. Leave a very wide exclusion window around the predicted next beat—months by outside reckoning, not days.

The beat apparently alters the chamber retroactively. A visitor present during it might discover that:

- the entrance has never existed;
- they have always been rooted there;
- the grove has incorporated their arrival into its origin;
- the exterior world remembers a different expedition.

Because time runs unevenly inside, an outside calendar alone is insufficient. Compare biological changes, bulb activity, thermal cycles, and reflections in the central basin before committing personnel.

### 2. Obtain passage rather than forcing entry

The chamber grants movement locally. Cutting, burning, freezing, or mechanically spreading roots may provoke no recognizable attack, but it will identify the visitor as a source of injury and usable material.

Before human entry, expose the threshold to a controlled signature resembling the traveler:

- body warmth without open flame;
- exhaled-gas composition;
- walking pressure;
- recorded heartbeat;
- a trace of odor held behind a membrane, not released;
- slow approach followed by retreat.

If roots repeatedly slacken rather than tighten, that is the nearest available thing to permission. A machine probe is not a reliable substitute: the grove tests intention and ancestry, not just mass.

If a native creature willingly crosses near the entrance and returns, follow its route only in the broadest sense. Never capture it as a guide.

### 3. Prepare psychologically

The traveler should rehearse three facts:

1. Shadows will not indicate object positions.
2. sounds will seem nearer, larger, and more intentional than they are;
3. some apparent events may be futures, alternatives, or memories rather than current threats.

Panic is especially dangerous because the moss appears capable of reading physical and chemical intention. Running, thrashing, or reaching reflexively may close the only available passage.

The traveler should practice moving one foot at a time, waiting after each placement, and retreating without turning around.

## Clothing and equipment

### Closed biological isolation suit

Wear a fully sealed, smooth-surfaced suit with:

- closed-cycle breathing;
- no exposed skin or hair;
- no leather, wool, cotton, wood, paper, feathers, or plant-derived packing;
- no loose seams, mesh, absorbent fabric, or external pockets;
- a nonporous sacrificial outer layer that can be abandoned after exit;
- redundant internal heating with tightly limited exterior temperature;
- passive insulation sufficient to survive if active heating is disabled;
- internal hydration and waste containment;
- hard eye protection with adjustable polarization.

Do not allow the suit to vent moisture. Exhaled water may be captured, redirected through roots, or treated as reproductive material.

Avoid chemical biocides and antifungal coatings. They could poison the chamber, trigger defensive growth, or create unprecedented hybrids. Physical isolation is preferable.

### Heat discipline

The chamber handles heat as information and sometimes as a mobile substance. Keep the suit’s outer surface only slightly warmer than the surroundings and distribute that warmth evenly.

Do not carry:

- flames;
- combustion heaters;
- hot lamps;
- exposed batteries;
- chemical hand warmers;
- high-powered cutting tools.

A concentrated hot spot may be treated as a blossom, prey, wound, or invitation.

### Light and observation

Use low-intensity, diffuse, non-pulsing illumination. Bright beams may alter leaves simply by observing them, while flashing lights could resemble biological signaling.

Bring:

- wide-angle passive cameras;
- thermal sensors with no scanning beam;
- chemical sensors behind sealed sampling membranes;
- tactile navigation readouts inside the helmet;
- several independent clocks using different physical principles.

Do not rely on visible landmarks. Light can travel through stems and emerge elsewhere, and shadows are actively positioned.

### Navigation and extraction

The exit must remain physically or dimensionally open and supervised from outside. Do not depend on finding it again.

A conventional rope is hazardous. Roots may braid into it, interpret it as a vine, or make two points on it locally adjacent. If a tether is essential, use a smooth, actively retracting monofilament with:

- no knots;
- no organic coating;
- low tensile force;
- an emergency release at the traveler’s end;
- continuous monitoring from outside.

The tether is for locating the traveler, not hauling them. Never let the recovery team pull against tightening roots. That may injure the chamber, dismember the traveler, or bring more than the traveler back.

Carry three kinds of timekeeping:

- mechanical;
- atomic or oscillatory;
- biochemical but sealed.

If they disagree significantly, leave immediately. Do not stop to investigate.

## Conduct inside

### Move as if negotiating with the floor

Pause at the threshold and wait for a route to open. Place each foot slowly. If moss rises around the boot, remain calm and allow a brief examination—but retreat if root hairs persist or climb above the ankle.

Never stamp, pry, or test the floor with a blade.

If roots tighten, do not struggle. Reduce weight, lower body temperature slightly, relax the limb, and reverse the preceding movement. The chamber may be rejecting a direction rather than the person.

### Keep moving, but never hurry

Stillness invites attachment. Sleep guarantees it.

The expedition should have a strict external duration—preferably under thirty minutes for the first human visit—with audible prompts delivered inside the helmet. The traveler should make a deliberate movement at least every few seconds, even when observing.

Do not kneel, sit, lean against walls, or place equipment on the ground. Every apparent surface is alive.

### Make little noise

Sound travels through the root vaults and can change local physical behavior. In some regions, sound melts frost. Elsewhere it may trigger spores, bulbs, or animals.

Speak only through a throat microphone routed to the exterior. Keep external suit speakers disabled. Avoid tapping, ultrasonic mapping, sonar, hammering, and alarms.

If a deep bulb tone begins:

1. stop advancing;
2. keep breathing shallow and regular;
3. avoid touching taut fibers;
4. withdraw after the vibration ends.

### Touch nothing directly

Especially avoid:

- translucent fruits;
- luminous seed organs;
- animal eggs;
- dormant-looking berries;
- green-black pool membranes;
- transparent stems carrying light;
- raw clay fractures;
- anything resembling fur with lichen growth;
- anything that appears dead.

Do not sample on the first visit. A “sample” may remain spatially adjacent to its parent root, may contain an interior larger than the container, or may begin incorporating the carrier. Even detached material may not consider itself detached.

### Do not feed, rescue, or kill animals

Predators may be metabolically indistinguishable from flowers. Larvae may be fruit. Eggs may be rooted organs. A trapped animal may be performing a necessary transfer between possible states.

If something lands on the traveler, do not swat it. Offer a smooth inert wand as an alternate perch and wait. Remember that a small animal choosing a branch may determine which version of that branch becomes real.

### Do not approach your own reflection

At the central basin, reflections may arrive before their owners. If the traveler sees their reflection performing an action they have not yet performed, they should back away without repeating the action.

Never touch, signal to, step into, or permit an animal to seize such a reflection. A future self dragged into the present may not merge cleanly with the current one.

If two versions of the traveler become visible, extraction control should call the traveler back using a private memorized response sequence. Do not let either version touch the other.

### Stay away from the dark cavity

The coordinated absence at the lowest point should be treated as an absolute exclusion zone.

Do not:

- illuminate it;
- measure it actively;
- throw anything into it;
- speak near it;
- place a clock near it;
- touch the roots surrounding it;
- enter the region where ambient noise fades.

Its silence is not merely lack of sound. It may be excluding physical processes. The fact that the roots stop just short of the water suggests the grove itself observes a boundary there.

## Abort conditions

Leave immediately if any of the following occurs:

- root hairs remain attached after movement;
- the suit surface begins sprouting, frosting in tissue-like patterns, or glowing;
- an external clock skips, reverses, or diverges sharply from the others;
- the traveler becomes unexpectedly sleepy;
- a wound or opening appears in the suit or body;
- a second mouth, limb outline, shadow, or reflection develops;
- the entrance appears in more than one direction;
- radio messages arrive before they are transmitted;
- the chamber becomes suddenly quiet;
- the traveler remembers having visited before;
- the traveler feels that remaining is “obviously” necessary.

That final sensation may be genuine insight, chemical influence, or the beginning of incorporation. Treat it as contamination.

## If attachment occurs

Do not cut root hairs while inside unless they are compromising the pressure suit or airway. Sudden severing may be interpreted as injury, and the hairs may already be carrying sensory or nervous information.

Instead:

1. move back toward the entrance slowly;
2. cool the contacted area by a small amount;
3. reduce pressure on the growth;
4. present the smooth sacrificial suit layer for continued attachment;
5. shed that layer only across the threshold.

If a root penetrates the suit and enters skin, extraction becomes medically and ontologically hazardous. Do not pull it out in the chamber. The traveler may already be functioning as part of the network. Withdraw under their own power if possible and separate only in quarantine.

If the traveler falls asleep, do not wake them by force or drag them immediately. First determine whether they still possess independent circulation and neural activity. Waking “connected” may mean the grove is participating in their nervous system. Recovery could tear cognition as readily as tissue.

## After return

Quarantine should be remote from soil, living plants, open water, and ordinary ecosystems. The traveler and all equipment must remain inside a sealed, dry, mineral-lined chamber.

Examine for:

- persistent root hairs;
- fungal haze;
- unexplained antifreeze compounds;
- seeds or capsules beneath the skin;
- light traveling through veins;
- delayed or anticipatory reflections;
- memories not belonging to the traveler;
- reflexes that recur in nearby plants;
- discrepancies in age, season, or personal history.

Do not immediately remove the suit. Observe it for at least one full outside day. If the outer layer has become biologically active, preserve it in isolation rather than sterilizing it. Burning may release pollen through solid barriers; freezing may simply store the organism.

The traveler should remain awake until clinicians establish that sleep will not trigger rooting. When sleep is finally allowed, it should occur in a suspended nonorganic cradle with no contact between the body and the room’s surfaces.

Before release, confirm identity through memories, habits, genetics, and temporal continuity. Genetics alone is inadequate in a place where ancestry influences distance and dead nervous patterns can be replayed by vines.

## The correct attitude

Do not enter as an explorer entering a cave. Enter as a temporary thought passing through a mind that has not yet decided what thoughts are.

The chamber may tolerate curiosity. It may even make room for it. But it recognizes no permanent distinction between observer, visitor, food, instrument, memory, and seed.

The central rule is therefore simple:

**Take no living thing, leave no living thing, sleep nowhere, and depart before the grove discovers a useful way to continue you.**
