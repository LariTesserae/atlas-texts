## Overall recommendation

An ordinary human should treat the Fixed Foundry as a **hostile industrial site combined with a temporal or causal containment anomaly**. It is not merely hot, dry, loud, or electrically active. It gradually converts visitors and their equipment from independent actors into parts of its repeating operation.

The safest visit is made **from the entrance, for only a few cycles, without crossing into the chamber proper**. Anyone intending to approach the central machine should have professional cave, hazardous-material, electrical, and anomaly-recovery support. Do not enter alone, and do not remain overnight under any circumstances.

The warning should be taken literally:

**Do not stop, alter, repair, obstruct, feed, collect from, or “test” anything involved in the shift.**

## Essential equipment

### Full environmental isolation

A respirator is not enough. The atmosphere attacks bodily moisture and contains suspended mineral grit, metallic vapors, and electrically active solvent films.

Wear:

- A fully sealed, positive-pressure dry suit
- A self-contained breathing supply
- A full-face helmet with no exposed skin
- A chemically inert, electrically insulating outer layer
- Sealed boots with puncture-resistant soles
- Redundant suit-pressure and oxygen systems
- A sacrificial outer visor against grit and salt splinters

Do not rely on ordinary rubber alone. The chamber’s solvents are unusual and migrate along charged surfaces. A fluoropolymer or ceramic-coated outer layer is preferable, with electrical isolation between external fittings and the wearer.

Carry all drinking water and bodily-waste storage **inside the pressure envelope**. Never open a water container in the chamber. Do not attempt to “see what water does” there.

### Impact and acoustic protection

The hammer blow is both sound and physical compression. Earplugs will not prevent transmission through the skull and chest.

Use:

- Combined earplugs and helmet-integrated hearing protection
- A rigid helmet with concussion padding
- A mouthguard
- Rib and sternum protection
- A harness that allows a stable crouch without falling onto crystal blades

Before each hammer strike, stop moving, bend the knees slightly, keep the jaw relaxed, and brace without locking the body rigid. Anyone with heart arrhythmia, fragile bones, vestibular disorders, or a history of concussion should not enter.

### Retention and recovery gear

Everything brought in should be attached to the wearer. A dropped object may eventually acquire a permanent route or function within the Foundry.

Use:

- Captive tools rather than loose tools
- Zipped or bolted pockets
- No jewelry
- No loose paper
- No unsecured cameras
- A body harness connected to an extraction line

The extraction line should lead to a powered winch and attendants **outside the anomalous chamber**, preferably beyond the black evaporite boundary. Use a nonconductive line with an optical communication fiber if available. Do not attach anything to the Foundry’s walls, cables, ladder, booth, or machine.

The external team must control the visit duration. Clocks inside will synchronize to the eleven-second cycle and may cease to provide meaningful elapsed time.

## Timing the visit

Count hammer cycles rather than trusting an internal clock, but let the outside team keep the authoritative count.

A prudent first visit would be:

- Three cycles of observation at the threshold
- No more than twelve cycles inside
- Immediate withdrawal at the first sign of behavioral entrainment

Longer scientific entries should be increased only gradually. “Hours later” is when dropped objects begin visibly joining the pattern; a human may begin synchronizing sooner.

Agree on extraction signals before entry. Radio may be distorted or absorbed into the chamber’s established echoes. Use several independent methods:

- Radio
- Fiber-optic text or light signal
- Prearranged tugs on the recovery line
- Automatic external winch activation after a set duration

Do not cancel automatic extraction merely because the visitor reports feeling fine. Steady hunger, painless wounds, and calm familiarity are warning signs, not reassurance.

## Conduct inside

### Observe before moving

At the threshold, watch enough cycles to identify:

- The descent of the gliders at the hammer strike
- The silver mites’ circuit after the groan
- The exchange of the armored animals near the cables
- Spark discharges around the ceramic drum
- The turning of the copper-colored vanes
- Any open path through the mineral blades

Move only when you understand the local traffic. The animals are not aggressive in the ordinary predatory sense, but their hooks, needles, jaws, and saws are working implements. If your body obstructs their route, they may treat it as material or machinery rather than as a living visitor.

Never step over a cable, animal procession, conductive grove, or repeated track if you can avoid it. Do not force an animal aside. Wait for the route to clear.

### Touch nothing

In particular, do not touch:

- The hammer, flywheel, drum, horns, or cables
- The black stalks or copper vanes
- The charged animals
- Membranes containing young
- Bodies beneath the slag bank
- The slag barrier
- The emergency lamp
- The ladder or sealed hatch
- The gauges or switchboard
- The coat
- The chair

The chair is not shelter. The warm coat is not abandoned equipment. Together they may constitute a vacant job position.

Do not sit down, put on the coat, operate a switch, read a gauge by tapping it, or try to complete the ladder. The fact that these actions appear obvious may be part of the mechanism by which the chamber assigns a visitor to the shift.

Do not collect specimens. Matter in the room is accounted for with unusual exactness. Removing an animal, stalk, crystal, dust sample, or machine fragment may be interpreted as interrupting production—or may cause the Foundry to balance the loss with something from the expedition.

### Do not improvise

Avoid shouting, rhythmic tapping, singing along with the mechanism, or answering the clicking sounds. Repeated noises may be absorbed and later returned in altered form. Do not attempt linguistic contact by reproducing the clicks.

Do not perform experiments involving:

- Water
- Fire
- Explosives
- Electrical grounding
- Magnets
- Cutting cables
- Blocking the hammer
- Marking or breaking crystal formations
- Moving animals from their paths
- Damaging one of the “plants”
- Placing an object on a repeated route

A snapped stalk that is somehow already replaced elsewhere is evidence that ordinary cause and effect cannot be trusted here.

## Signs that the Foundry is incorporating you

Withdraw immediately if any of the following occurs:

- Your breathing locks to the eleven-second cycle
- You always place a foot in exactly the same previous footprint
- Animals move before you decide where to step
- One route seems uniquely comfortable or inevitable
- Alternative routes become obstructed at precisely the right moment
- Hunger or thirst becomes constant rather than increasing
- A wound stops bleeding but remains visibly open
- Your equipment begins activating on the same phase each cycle
- You feel that sitting in the booth would be harmless or appropriate
- You begin thinking of the machine’s activity as “your shift”
- You cannot remember whether a particular hammer blow has already happened

The route to the booth is a final, unmistakable withdrawal signal. Do not follow it out of curiosity. Backtrack immediately along your existing path while the external team takes up the recovery line.

If fauna block all retreat, do not attack them. Remain standing, avoid the chair, and let the outside team extract you. Prefer damage to the suit’s outer layer over voluntary movement toward the booth.

## Medical preparation and aftercare

Have a medical team waiting outside with facilities for:

- Controlled rehydration
- Electrolyte testing
- Respiratory examination
- Eye and skin treatment
- Hearing and concussion assessment
- Cardiac monitoring
- Treatment of delayed bleeding

Wounds may resume bleeding after removal from the chamber’s stabilizing influence. Do not assume that a bloodless open injury is minor.

The suit should remain sealed until it is inside a controlled decontamination enclosure. External dust and molecular films may retain charge or behavioral synchronization. Monitor recovered equipment for at least several days. Isolate any item that:

- Moves or activates every eleven seconds
- Returns repeatedly to one position
- Produces clicking sounds
- Remains warm without an energy source
- Refuses to discharge a battery
- Repeats an identical failure or impact

Do not take such objects into living quarters or another machine room.

Visitors should also be observed for compulsive repetition, eleven-second breathing or sleep rhythms, fixation on right-angled symbols, and a desire to return to “finish” something. Psychological debriefing should focus on preserving a clear sequence of events; the chamber may blur repetition into a false sense that nothing consequential happened.

## Best practical visit plan

For an ordinary traveler, the ideal excursion is simple:

1. Establish a staffed base above the salt crust.
2. Place all drilling and excavation equipment well away from the chamber during the visit.
3. Enter in a team of two, with at least three people remaining outside.
4. Remain at the threshold for three hammer cycles.
5. Cross no farther than necessary to see the central machine.
6. Take passive images only.
7. Touch and remove nothing.
8. Withdraw after twelve cycles or at the first sign of synchronization.
9. Account for every person and every piece of equipment.
10. Undergo decontamination and medical observation.

Most importantly, resist the chamber’s peculiar invitation. The open route, vacant chair, warm coat, and active gauges make the Foundry feel as though it has been waiting for someone qualified to take over.

You are not qualified.

No one knows what counts as clocking in, and the Fixed Foundry does not appear to recognize the end of a shift.
