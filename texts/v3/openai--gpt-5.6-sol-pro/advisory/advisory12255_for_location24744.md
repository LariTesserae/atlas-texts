## Overall recommendation

Treat this world as a flooded industrial hazard zone whose “machinery” is alive and whose geography, gravity, sound, and time are intermittently predatory.

An ordinary unmodified human should not attempt an exposed walking expedition. The safest visit is from a sealed, insulated survey capsule placed on a bare mineral shelf well away from large colonies, pools, migration routes, and the broad central basin. Keep the visit short, retain an automatic return system, and assume that anything resembling water, ice, mist, mud, harmless wildlife, or stable distance is dangerous.

Do not visit alone.

## Before crossing

### Confirm basic compatibility remotely

Send disposable probes first and let them remain through at least one Compression. Test for:

- Atmospheric composition and pressure
- Toxic or corrosive aerosols
- Airborne spores and mineral-eating microbes
- Ambient electrical fields
- Local radiation
- Water and mud chemistry
- Temperature and wind
- The frequency and severity of gravity reversals
- Whether foreign matter begins mineralizing
- How local water affects biological age
- Whether the return aperture remains spatially connected during Compression

A probe that survives ordinary conditions may still disappear when distances collapse, be carried into a predator by redirected gravity, or return materially intact but chronologically altered. Use several probes with independent clocks, inertial sensors, biological test samples, and redundant recordings.

Do not regard inconsistent readings as instrument error. On this plain, two contradictory measurements may both be locally correct.

### Medical preparation

You need:

- Full vaccination and broad quarantine clearance, though local life may not use familiar biochemistry
- Baseline neurological, auditory, cardiovascular, and bone-density records
- A complete record of your biological age markers
- Anti-seizure medication, anti-nausea medication, and emergency sedatives
- Treatment for hypothermia and pressure injury
- A plan for acute hearing damage
- A strict post-return quarantine facility

Pregnant travelers, people with cardiac rhythm disorders, severe vestibular problems, fragile bones, implanted stimulators, or a history of seizures should not go. The plain produces intense low-frequency sound, electrical activity, abrupt changes in perceived direction, and possible age alteration.

## Essential equipment

### Sealed environmental suit

Wear a positive-pressure suit with:

- Independent breathing supply
- Full chemical and biological isolation
- Heated underlayer
- Rigid helmet and face shield
- Electrically insulating outer surface
- Replaceable abrasion and mineral-resistant overshell
- No exposed fabric, mesh, seams, or porous padding
- Smooth external geometry so coin grazers and cable hunters cannot easily attach
- Automatic leak sealing

The cold is only one concern. The mist may contain spores, metallic remains, microscopic animals, and electrically active residues from dead organisms. Never expose skin, even briefly.

The suit should not recycle outside moisture. Do not rely on local water for coolant, oxygen extraction, washing, or emergency drinking.

### Sound and vibration control

Carry active hearing protection capable of handling both infrasonic pressure and sudden concussive noise. Helmet communications should be optical or hard-wired rather than spoken through external speakers.

Your equipment should:

- Avoid alarms, buzzers, clicks, and fan whine
- Minimize footfall vibration
- Use damped joints and soft internal mounts
- Display warnings visually and through gentle skin pressure
- Detect abrupt removal of a sound band from the environment

Complete silence is not necessarily safety. A six-chambered hunter may create a localized absence of sound before drawing in the things that produced it. If the ambient clatter suddenly loses one distinct component—your breathing noise, footfalls, suit pump, nearby shell impacts—assume you have been targeted. Do not shout or activate a siren. Trigger immediate extraction or retreat using a prearranged silent signal.

### Navigation and return systems

Ordinary compass bearings, landmarks, echo ranging, and travel-time estimates are unreliable. Carry:

- An inertial navigation unit
- A topological or cross-world position reference, if available
- Multiple independent clocks
- A return beacon referenced to your origin universe rather than local coordinates
- Dead-reckoning markers that record identity as well as location
- A body-mounted emergency transit device
- A second extraction system operated from the origin side

Do not rely on one fixed portal standing several meters behind you. During Compression, “several meters” may cease to identify a unique separation.

Your extraction system should default to pulling you home if:

- Your clocks disagree beyond a set threshold
- The horizon begins to contract
- Communications duplicate, precede themselves, or arrive out of order
- Local sound pressure exceeds safe limits
- Gravity deviates from the planetary vertical
- Your age biomarkers change
- The return beacon appears in more than one direction
- The suit detects rapid mineral deposition

### Mobility

Use a broad-footed, enclosed vehicle or a low survey capsule rather than walking. It should have:

- Very low ground pressure
- No exposed wheels, axles, intake vents, or propellers
- Independent lifting capability for brief lateral gravity events
- Multiple short anchors rather than one deep anchor
- A smooth sacrificial underside
- Automatic stabilization in arbitrary gravity
- The ability to seal itself against mud and rising water

Do not hammer anchors into the plain. The “stone” may be a living colony, mineralized tissue, a feeding surface, or a membrane over pressurized water. A deep anchor may also become a conduit into your shelter.

If you must walk, use a pole-mounted remote sensor to inspect each step without touching the ground. Never step onto pale radial patterns, unusually smooth mineral skins, or crescent-shaped scars.

## Choosing an arrival site

Select a slightly raised, dry-looking mineral back with wide visibility—but remember that local water may gather uphill.

Avoid:

- All shallow pools
- Any pale radial form under a mineral film
- Dense fields of coin-like organisms
- Channels packed with bell-bodied drifters
- Tower bases and vent alignments
- Cylindrical standing-water columns
- Giant mobile droplets
- Black ice
- Detached shadows
- Areas where sound is unusually sparse
- Footprint trails with no visible animal
- Old crescent scars
- The broad central basin
- Seams covered by small armored scavengers

The basin should be considered completely off-limits. Its biological density, pressure pulses, electrical corona, and coordinated siphons leave no meaningful safe route for a human. Observe it only from a distant sealed platform or by drone.

Do not arrive beneath an overhang. Water collects on undersides, and even an apparently small bead may be connected to an invisible or remembered channel.

## Conduct on the plain

### Move slowly and remain local

Keep excursions within immediate extraction range. Distances should be measured in seconds to shelter, not kilometers on a map.

Do not cross crowded animal paths. The jointed walkers may not be visible from your approach angle. Their calls precede them, so a mechanical blast is a warning to stop and scan from multiple directions, including reflections and subsurface imaging. If footprints begin appearing without a visible source, clear the line of travel immediately and do not stand between the prints.

Use cameras viewing from several angles at once. A direct view is insufficient. Include:

- North–south and east–west perspectives
- Thermal imaging
- Ground radar
- Reflection imaging
- A downward camera
- A rear-facing camera continuously monitored by another person

Do not assume an organism’s invisible portion is absent or intangible.

### Never interact with the water

Do not drink it, touch it, sample it by hand, walk through it, or stare into a held still surface longer than necessary.

Water here may:

- Display an earlier landscape instead of reflecting the present
- Alter the drinker’s age
- Follow extinct channels
- Move invisibly across dry ground
- Rise against apparent gravity
- Enclose and transport organisms
- Enter living colonies explosively

Collect samples only with disposable robotic apparatus stored outside the crew compartment. Keep samples dynamically mixed so they cannot form a stable reflective layer, and isolate them from all biological material and clocks. Ideally, do not bring them home at all.

If water touches the suit, treat the contacted layer as chronologically contaminated. Do not wipe it toward a seam. Shed the outer shell before return.

### Do not touch the wildlife or towers

The tower colonies may look stationary enough to use as shelter. They are not shelter. They ingest floodwater, salts, metals, spores, animals, and possibly electrical patterns. Their coordinated exhaust can produce freezing mist and destructive pressure changes.

Stay far outside the reach of slots, vents, siphons, and calcified feeding tubes. If an entire region begins a descending intake moan, move perpendicular to the nearest vent alignment and seek extraction. A synchronized release is likely imminent.

Do not collect apparently dead organisms. Death does not end function here. Mineralized remains may retain electrical or behavioral patterns and transfer them into nearby systems—or potentially into your equipment.

### Control light, heat, electricity, and sound

The local ecosystem appears organized partly around heatless electrical pulses and vibration. Keep emissions modest and stable.

Avoid:

- Powerful radar unless needed for immediate survival
- Unshielded motors
- Arc lights
- Sonic mapping
- Explosive charges
- Electrical discharge weapons
- Open flames
- Loud vehicle propulsion

A sudden new rhythm may be treated as prey, a call, a current, or a control signal. You do not want your suit pump accidentally coordinating a valley full of organisms.

## Recognizing the Compression

Probable warning signs include:

- The horizon seeming closer without visible movement
- Distant towers gaining detail too quickly
- Echoes arriving before their source sound
- Multiple remote channels appearing adjacent
- Unrelated animal populations abruptly intermingling
- Maps showing impossible overlap
- Severe growth in ambient sound
- Your own transmitted words returning before you speak them
- More than one apparent route to the same landmark

At the first sign, do not try to outrun it. Distance itself is changing.

Immediately:

1. Enter the sealed vehicle or capsule.
2. Stop all external movement.
3. Retract probes and tethers if possible.
4. Do not reel in a tether that appears to lead to more than one place.
5. Switch communications to short encoded optical signals.
6. Engage inertial stabilization and origin-referenced extraction readiness.
7. Avoid windows if overlapping locations cause severe disorientation.
8. Do not open the shelter for stranded organisms or apparent copies of team members.
9. Wait for independent systems to confirm that spatial relationships have stabilized.

If extraction is possible without traversing local space, leave immediately. If it requires a stable local aperture, you may need to shelter through the event. This decision must be automated in advance; a frightened human should not have to reason through causally disordered signals.

When the Compression relaxes, do not assume you remain near your original landing site. Check identity, not appearance: beacon codes, clock histories, suit serials, team biometrics, and origin-universe signatures.

Expect reality-seam scavengers nearby afterward. Their feeding activity marks locations where separation is still incomplete. Do not approach the pale residue they harvest.

## Predator responses

### Gravity-altering radial predators

Warning signs:

- Pale radial symmetry beneath a pool or mineral skin
- Crescent scars
- Water moving sideways or climbing low ridges
- Pebbles drifting toward a point without a current

Response:

- Do not jump; your assumed “up” may be wrong.
- Engage suit or vehicle stabilization.
- Go limp enough to avoid joint injury but protect the helmet.
- Move away only after normal gravity is confirmed by multiple inertial sensors.
- Extract if any body part or tether enters the altered region.

### Sound-removing predators

Warning signs:

- One recurring noise vanishes with unnatural precision
- A sharply bounded patch of silence
- Your equipment records energy but you hear nothing
- Nearby animals producing the missing noise are pulled toward one place

Response:

- Stop generating that sound.
- Do not test the silence.
- Do not call to companions.
- Break line of approach using slow, damped movement.
- Use emergency transit before the hunter closes its plates.

### Habitual or memory-bearing water

Warning signs:

- Water gathering on a rise
- A flow continuing across apparently dry rock
- A reflection showing ridges or constellations not present
- Sudden age disagreement among biological sensors
- Condensation forming unusually perfect cups or lenses

Response:

- Back away without crossing the apparent channel.
- Treat dry stone in the projected path as submerged.
- Do not place equipment into the reflection.
- Quarantine anyone who drank, inhaled, or was injected with local fluid, even if they feel younger and healthy.

## Shelter and duration

A visit should be measured in hours, not days. There is no reliable daylight cycle, no edible plant life, and no trustworthy local source of water. Fatigue will be accelerated by cold, industrial noise, low visibility, and continuous perceptual contradictions.

A safe shelter needs:

- Full acoustic and vibration insulation
- No direct contact between floor and local rock
- Positive pressure
- Independent food, water, heat, and waste storage
- External sacrificial sensor pods
- Opaque shutters
- Automated Compression detection
- An origin-side extraction crew
- Enough power to leave without local refueling

Do not sleep on the surface unless unavoidable. A detached shadow, rising water column, migrating herd, vent cycle, or Compression can transform a safe shelf without conventional warning.

## Psychological preparation

This environment will repeatedly encourage dangerous misclassification. You will see factories and expect machinery, puddles and expect passive water, ice and expect solidity, reflections and expect the present, silence and expect safety.

Train yourself to use neutral language. Say:

- “Columnar organism,” not “tower”
- “Surface film,” not “ground”
- “Visible route,” not “open path”
- “Current local vertical,” not “down”
- “Apparent distance,” not “distance”
- “Mineralized organism,” not “corpse”

Do not follow familiar calls, including voices resembling team members, unless identity is verified by encrypted non-acoustic challenge. Calls may arrive before their makers, dead electrical patterns may migrate, and displaced moments may reproduce genuine signals out of sequence.

Crew members should regularly verify:

- Name and autobiographical continuity
- Subjective age
- Clock reading
- Current location code
- Number and identity of companions
- Whether memories are shared or merely similar

## Return and quarantine

Before return:

1. Enter an external decontamination shell.
2. Remove the suit’s outer layer robotically.
3. Drain no local liquid into the transit apparatus.
4. Leave all geological and biological samples behind unless a dedicated containment mission was authorized.
5. Photograph and scan equipment for new valves, chambers, lamination, electrical pulses, or spontaneous pumping behavior.
6. Compare every traveler’s biological age with the pre-mission baseline.
7. Confirm that the returning team has the correct origin signature.

After return, quarantine people and equipment separately. Continue monitoring for:

- Progressive mineralization
- Metallic sediment in bodily fluids
- Changes in apparent age
- Unexplained electrical rhythms
- Aversion to particular spaces
- Repetition of local pumping cycles
- Sounds occurring out of sequence
- Water behaving as if it remembers absent channels
- Equipment acquiring animal-like reflexes

Do not pour decontamination water into an ordinary drain until it has been verified that none of the plain’s water—or its habits—returned with you.

## Best way to experience it

The most survivable visit is a stationary one: arrive in a sealed observation capsule on a quiet mineral rise, watch the distant colonies breathe, record one vent cycle, and leave before the plain has time to incorporate your rhythms.

Do not seek the central basin. Do not take a drink as proof of courage. Do not strike the towers to see whether they answer. Do not wait inside a detached shadow. Above all, do not mistake the absence of intention for the absence of danger.

Nothing there needs to notice you personally. The ecosystem can process a human traveler in exactly the same way it processes salt, shell, current, call, and stone.
