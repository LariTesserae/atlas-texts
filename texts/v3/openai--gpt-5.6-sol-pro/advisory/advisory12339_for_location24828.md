This hollow is best approached as a **short, carefully supported excursion**, not as a refuge or campsite. Its apparent calm is real, but calm is not the same as safety: darkness, dry heat, mineral dust, uncertain air exchange, sharp stone, and the absence of water can disable an ordinary traveler without anything dramatic occurring.

### Before entering

Confirm that the chamber’s atmosphere is compatible with human life. Do not use a flame as your primary test; it consumes oxygen and says little about carbon dioxide, hydrogen sulfide, or other gases that may collect close to the floor. Carry a calibrated monitor for:

- oxygen concentration  
- carbon dioxide  
- hydrogen sulfide and sulfur dioxide  
- carbon monoxide  
- combustible or unfamiliar gases  

If multiversal chemistry is uncertain, take sealed breathing apparatus rather than trusting local air. Hairline fissures may provide exchange, but they should not be mistaken for reliable ventilation.

Establish the route back before leaving the surface or portal. Use a physical tether, beacon chain, or similarly dependable system. Magnetic compasses may be distorted by basalt, and radio can perform poorly beneath stone. Have someone remain outside if possible, with a firm return time and instructions not to enter casually after you.

### What to wear and carry

Wear a helmet with a chin strap, eye protection, gloves, sturdy boots, and gaiters. The floor may feel pleasantly warm, but do not walk barefoot: dull-looking black glass can still cut, salt ribs can split skin, and mineral dust will work into wounds.

Bring:

- At least three independent lights, including a helmet lamp  
- A broad, diffuse lantern for orientation  
- Spare batteries kept warm and dust-sealed  
- A P2/P100-class particulate respirator, or local equivalent  
- More drinking water than the chamber’s size seems to justify  
- Electrolytes and a compact cooling layer  
- A first-aid kit with eyewash and supplies for cuts  
- A low-profile ground pad if kneeling is necessary  
- A watch or timer that does not depend on environmental cues  
- Non-contact instruments for temperature, humidity, and air sampling  

Seal cameras, joints, and optical equipment against fine powder. A bright handheld beam may reveal less than expected because the rough dark walls absorb light; several lower-angle sources will show the relief of the floor more effectively.

### Moving through the chamber

Move slowly and stay on bare, durable mineral surfaces. The ankle-high ribs are easy to miss and can cause a fall. Dust may conceal sharp fragments or shallow depressions. Avoid kicking up powder: it may contain irritating salts, silica-rich volcanic material, gypsum, or chemically active compounds.

The chamber appears geologically settled, but still treat the ceiling as unverified. Do not strike walls, pry at seams, climb shelves, or stand beneath visibly detached slabs. Do not assume that because loose pieces fell long ago, nothing else can fall under the vibration of footsteps or equipment.

Do not touch or step on the dark crusts and pale filaments. Their growth is so slow that a bootprint could erase centuries or generations. Treat them as potentially alien biochemistry as well as a fragile ecosystem: neither sample nor smell them directly, and keep food, water, skin, and equipment from contacting them. Clean and quarantine gear afterward.

### Time and physical limits

The even warmth and extreme dryness will dehydrate you quietly. Because there is no wind, dripping water, daylight, or changing temperature, the body receives few reminders that time is passing. Drink on a schedule and set a strict turnaround alarm.

Do not rely on local resources. There is:

- no usable water  
- no food suitable for a human  
- no fuel worth gathering  
- no evidence that the fibrous mineral sheets are safe to handle  
- no justification for testing the sharp flint on bare skin or equipment  

The chamber contains materials from which primitive tools could be made, but that does not make it hospitable. Carry everything needed to leave.

### Psychological preparation

Expect the darkness to feel unusually complete. Covering the light may produce immediate, disorienting blackness without silhouettes or distant reference points. The soft acoustics can make companions seem farther away, while heartbeat, breathing, and small movements of dust may be mistaken for activity elsewhere.

Keep companions within sight. Speak plainly and periodically, but do not shout for echoes; there may be little satisfying return. If you deliberately experience full darkness, do it seated, tethered, and only briefly, with another person controlling the light. Never walk after extinguishing it.

The pale central mound may resemble an altar, cairn, grave, or constructed object under directional illumination. There is no evidence that it is any of these. Resist turning ambiguous geology into a story, especially when tired or sensory-deprived. Photograph it from a distance and leave its salt skin intact.

### Conduct and departure

Do not camp, light a fire, mark the walls, stack stones, chip flint, or collect “souvenirs.” A hearth would consume oxygen and permanently introduce soot into a place that has apparently never held one. Even footprints may remain for an immense period, so keep the party small and use one route where disturbance cannot be avoided.

On leaving:

1. Account for every person and item.  
2. Brush or vacuum loose dust into contained filters rather than releasing it elsewhere.  
3. Decontaminate boots and equipment.  
4. Inspect for cuts, eye irritation, coughing, headache, confusion, or unusual fatigue.  
5. Quarantine biological samples only if collection was authorized and performed with proper containment.  

The safest way to appreciate this hollow is quietly, briefly, and without trying to make it useful. Bring your own light, air assurance, water, route home, and sense of time. Leave no flame, tool mark, sample scar, food trace, or footprint near the living crusts. The chamber’s defining quality is that almost nothing happens there; a responsible traveler should avoid becoming the largest event in its history.
