**The Crystal Hollow of Veyna’s Breath**

You stand within a hidden pocket of the world, a microcosm of contradictions, carved into the flank of a young, razor-edged mountain. The air is cold—a biting chill that clings to the bones, yet it carries the faintest mineral scent of warm stone beneath. The scale of this place is intimate, a single cavernous chamber perhaps a hundred paces across, its ceiling lost in shadow above. This is not a natural cave, though its walls are raw and freshly hewn, glistening with striations of obsidian and veins of milky quartz. It is a constructed space, an urban artifact of a civilization beyond modernity, yet it feels alive in its stillness.

Water is the lifeblood here, though not in the form of rivers or lakes. The walls weep. Tiny beads of moisture condense from the cold air and gather on the jagged surfaces, coalescing into droplets that fall with a soft, rhythmic *plink* into shallow basins carved into the floor. These basins are the only obvious sign of design—smooth, polished indentations in the otherwise jagged terrain, their edges etched with faint, glowing runes that hum with a faint, nanoscale energy. The water here is alive, too, or at least it moves as if it were, swirling in patterns that seem almost deliberate, though no wind or current stirs the air.

The atmosphere is sparse, almost antiseptic, yet not unwelcoming. The light is normal—a soft, diffused glow that seems to emanate from the quartz veins themselves, casting everything in a pale, blue-white radiance. The only other sources of illumination are the occasional flicker of the runes, their light shifting in hues that don’t quite align with the visible spectrum, hinting at physics that defy comprehension.

This is a mineral world, devoid of vegetation. Fungi might thrive in such dampness elsewhere, but here, even the simplest life forms are absent. The ecology is entirely artificial, sustained by processes that operate on scales too small or too vast to perceive. The air is quiet, save for the *plink* of falling water and the distant, resonant hum of machinery—or perhaps something biological—that pulses through the mountain itself. It’s a sound that feels more in the chest than the ears, a steady reminder of the unseen forces at work.

The rules of this world are unspoken but palpable. Gravity seems slightly heavier, as if the mountain’s mass pulls with a purpose. Time feels fluid, too, though it’s impossible to say if it’s faster or slower. Perhaps it’s neither—perhaps it’s just *different*. The only fauna is a scattering of tiny, bioluminescent creatures that drift through the air like fireflies, their paths erratic yet purposeful. They emit a faint, melodious chime when they collide, a sound so soft it’s easy to mistake for the drip of water. These creatures are rare, and their purpose is unclear, though they seem drawn to the basins, diving into the water and emerging moments later, unchanged.

The dynamic of this place is one of quiet transformation. The water in the basins shifts color gradually, from clear to a faint, opalescent blue, then back again. The runes flicker in response, their light brightening or dimming in sync. The walls themselves seem to shift, though only imperceptibly—a grain of obsidian falling here, a quartz vein expanding there. It’s a place in motion, yet the motion is so slow, so deliberate, that it feels more like stillness.

This is a city of one room, a fragment of something greater, embedded within the mountain like a seed. Its purpose is unknown, its origins a mystery. It exists, it breathes, it hums—and it waits. For what, you cannot say. But standing here, in the cold, sparse quiet of Veyna’s Breath, you feel the weight of its expectation, a silent question hanging in the air like the water on the walls.
