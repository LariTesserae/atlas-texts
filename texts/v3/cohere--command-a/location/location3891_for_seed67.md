**The Hydro-Forge of Eternis**

This is a world where water and steel entwine, a vast, meticulously constructed realm that hums with the quiet, relentless pulse of industry. The landscape is low-lying, a sprawling expanse of interconnected basins and plateaux, each surface sheathed in a thin veneer of water that beads and condenses from the warm, saturated atmosphere. The air is heavy with moisture, a perpetual mist that clings to everything, yet the temperature remains comfortably warm, as if the world itself were a well-regulated machine.

The ground—if it can even be called that—is entirely artificial, a mosaic of interlocked plates and panels of dark, weathered metal. These surfaces are scored with intricate channels and runes, their edges sharp and newly cut despite the constant dampness. Water pools in shallow reservoirs, glinting under the normal light that filters through the haze, and weeps from seams in the structure, as though the land itself sweats to sustain its own processes.

Vegetation is absent. There are no plants, no fungi, nothing living that would be recognized as biological. Instead, the space is dense with machinery, a forest of vertical and horizontal forms: towering smokestacks, crankshafts the size of cathedrals, and conveyor belts that stretch to the horizon. These structures are layered and interwoven, their surfaces a patchwork of rust, polished steel, and glowing vents that expel tendrils of steam. The world is fully constructed, every inch designed and maintained by some unseen hand.

The atmosphere is alive with the overwhelming presence of *fauna*—but not as we know it. This place teems with mechanical creatures, their forms strange and alien. They are densely packed, moving with purpose through the labyrinthine environment. Some resemble metallic insects, their bodies segmented and jointed, antennae twitching as they scuttle along the wet surfaces. Others are more aquatic, gliding just beneath the water’s surface with propeller-like fins or undulating, serpentine frames. There are larger beings too, colossal assemblages of gears and pistons that move with a slow, deliberate grace, their function unclear but their presence undeniable.

The soundscape is deceptively quiet. The occasional hiss of steam, the distant grind of gears, and the soft lapping of water against metal are the dominant notes. Yet beneath it all is a low, constant hum, a vibration that resonates through the ground and air, as if the entire world were a single, massive engine. It is a silence that feels watched, measured, and controlled.

This is a place of equilibrium, frozen in its purpose. Nothing here changes; the landscape is static, the processes eternal. The water cycles endlessly, the machinery operates without variation, and the creatures move in patterns that have never been broken. It is a world without gradients, without time, without evolution. Only the strange, the constructed, and the industrial exist here—a monument to order, a cathedral of permanence in a universe of change.

The rules of this world are as rigid as its structures. Water is both resource and lubricant, essential to the machinery’s function. The creatures, though overwhelming in number, are extensions of the system, cogs in a vast, unknowable design. Weirdness creeps in at the edges: the runes on the metal plates seem to shift when unobserved, the mechanical fauna occasionally pause as if sensing something beyond their programming, and the light occasionally flickers in ways that cannot be explained by the environment’s physics.

This is the Hydro-Forge of Eternis, a place where the line between the natural and the artificial has been erased, where water and industry have become one, and where the quiet hum of eternity is the only constant.
