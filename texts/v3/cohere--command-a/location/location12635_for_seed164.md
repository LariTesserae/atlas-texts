**The Weeping Spine of Erevath**

Rising like a titanium spine from the fog-choked void, the industrial metropolis of Erevath claws its way up the flank of a continent-sized, freshly extruded mountain range. The peaks, still gleaming with the razor-sharp edges of their recent birth, vanish into a stratus layer that condenses directly onto the city’s vast, layered towers. Water is omnipresent: it weeps from the black stone walls, beads on copper conduit networks, and pools in the hollows of unfinished district trenches. The air itself tastes of cooled steam and ionized metal, a perpetual mist that clings to the lungs like a wet woolen blanket. This is a cold so constant it redefines the concept, a temperature not of winter but of *absence*—a place where heat has simply failed to arrive.

The scale is planetary but hollow. Erevath stretches for hundreds of kilometers, its skycrane factories and smelting quadrants punctuating the void with rhythmic thunder. Yet between these megacomplexes lies nothingness: chasms so vast their far sides are lost in aerosolized haze, and valleys where the only movement is the slow drip of condensed atmosphere onto the cracked crystal floor. The built environment is a paradox of density—a city that’s 90% abyss, its latticework bridges and elevator spines the only solid threads holding it together.

Vegetation clings where it can. Bioluminescent moss sheets the undersides of overhangs, glowing pale blue-green in the diffuse daylight that filters through the cloud ceiling. Lichens veined with metallic impurities pattern the walls like frozen circuitry. But these are not ecosystems; they are *artifacts* of equilibrium, surviving not through biology but through sheer stubborn chemistry. No animal life stirs here—not even insects. The only movement is manufactured.

The soundscape is a symphony of artificial eternities. Pneumatic drills gnaw into the mountain’s core in perpetuity, their rhythm offset by the bass thrum of steam turbines and the shriek of magnetic cargo haulers fighting gravity. Every structure hums with the overburden of its own mass, a chorus of stressed girders and hydraulic locks. Yet for all its violence, the noise is monotonous, an unchanging arrangement of mechanical hymns. Time here is circular, its loops etched into the maintenance schedules of furnaces that never sleep.

The weirdness lies in the physics. Water defies angle on the higher spires, creeping *upward* in shimmering sheets where it meets certain alloys. The city’s central power plant—a kilometer-tall spire pulsing with Tesla arcs—does not *generate* energy so much as *siphon* it from the mountain itself, the rock slowly coring itself out in a self-sustaining wound. And the horizon? It’s not curved. It’s *faceted*, each monolithic peak striking the cloudline at precisely the same height, as if the entire range were one immense machine tool’s creation.

Erevath does not grow. It *erodes inward*. Every dripping wall is a countdown, every suspended district a gamble against the void. And yet, it endures. An industrial organ built to hum the same note forever, a city-sized monument to the grandeur of pointlessness, frozen in its own spectral mist.
