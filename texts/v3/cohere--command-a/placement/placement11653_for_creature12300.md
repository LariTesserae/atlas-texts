In the Shattered Spires of Vyrr, I would find myself as **Kaelin**, a young Vyrrish herder, my hands calloused from gripping the smooth haft of a spear and the rough hide of sled ropes. My hair is shaved close to my scalp, though a few wiry strands stubbornly resist, mirroring the resilience of the moss clinging to the spires. My eyes, a shade of grey that mirrors the slate sky, scan the horizon constantly, watching for the movement of both herd and predator. At sixteen winters, I am still considered young by Vyrrish standards, but I carry the weight of my clan’s survival on my shoulders as surely as the elders do.  

My home is a cluster of chambers carved into the base of Spire Thal’kar, the tallest and most jagged of the nearby peaks. The air inside is warm with the scent of smoke and tallow, and the walls are lined with hides bearing faded carvings of hunts long past. My mother, a weaver whose hands are as deft with bone needles as mine are with a spear, taught me to respect the land’s rhythms. My father, a herder like me, was taken by the Shadowbiters five winters ago. His absence is a silence that hums in the back of my mind, a reminder of the stakes of my work.  

Each day begins before the sun’s faint glow touches the spires. I wrap myself in layers of hide, pulling my hood tight against the damp chill. My boots, studded with flint chips, crunch against the pebbled ground as I make my way to the herd. The animals—shaggy, long-horned creatures we call *Thar’kin*—graze in the shallow valley, their low grunts echoing off the stone. I move among them quietly, my spear at the ready, though the Shadowbiters rarely hunt in daylight. Their presence is a constant threat, a shadow flickering at the edge of my vision.  

The herd is my responsibility, my connection to the land. I know each Thar’kin by sight: the old matriarch with the notched horn, the young calf still wobbly on its feet, the bull with the scarred flank from a long-ago encounter with a predator. They are not tame, but they tolerate me, and for that, I am grateful. My clan depends on them—for meat, for milk, for the hides that keep us warm.  

But today, something feels off. The air is heavier than usual, the silence too deep. Even the scavenging birds are still. I pause, my hand tightening on my spear. The Thar’kin sense it too; they cluster closer, their lowing nervous. Then, from the shadows of the spire’s base, a movement catches my eye. A Shadowbiter, sleek and black as midnight, its eyes gleaming with hunger.  

My heart pounds, but my breath stays steady. I’ve trained for this. I step forward, my voice low and commanding, urging the herd to move deeper into the valley. The Shadowbiter crouches, muscles coiled, but it hesitates—it knows I see it. I raise my spear, the flint tip glinting in the dim light. My clan’s survival depends on this moment.  

As the standoff stretches, I realize I am not just a herder. I am a link in a chain forged by generations of Vyrrish, a guardian of the delicate balance between life and the unforgiving land. The spires loom above, their jagged edges cutting into the sky, and I feel their weight, their endurance, seeping into my bones.  

In the Shattered Spires of Vyrr, I am Kaelin, a Vyrrish herder, and I am exactly where I am meant to be.
