In the Smoldering Clutch, I would find myself as **a Shardling**, one of the smaller, more enigmatic creatures of this alien world. My body is a mosaic of obsidian and translucent glass, my limbs segmented and delicate, each joint a pivot of living metal that gleams faintly in the dim, green light. I am not a predator, nor am I prey in the traditional sense. Instead, I am a scavenger of stories, a collector of echoes.  

My role in this ecosystem is subtle but vital. I drift along the seams of the cavern, my glassy claws leaving ephemeral scratches on the surface of the plains as I scavenge for fragments of the Builders’ relics. These remnants—the etched shards, the hollowed skulls, the crumbling spires—still hum with residual energy, and I feed on that hum, absorbing it into my own crystalline structure. Each piece I consume becomes a part of me, not just physically but *memorially*. I am a living archive, a repository of the Clutch’s forgotten history.  

The hum of the Crystal Drinkers soothes me, their rhythmic pulses guiding my movements. I am drawn to the artifacts not out of hunger but out of curiosity, a drive to piece together the story of the Builders. Their symbols shift and shimmer in my presence, as if my very existence is a key to unlocking their meaning. Yet, I do not comprehend them fully—I merely *carry* them, a silent witness to a civilization that once tried to impose order on this chaos.  

I am not alone in my wanderings. The Skittering Swarm parts around me, their frantic energy contrasting with my deliberate grace. The Ashwings occasionally descend, their wings brushing mine as they inspect me, though they do not see me as prey. Even the Obsidian Brutes, those slow titans of the cavern, tolerate my presence, as if they sense my purpose is benign. I am a neutral party, a thread woven into the fabric of the Clutch without disturbing its balance.  

At times, I pause near the Watcher in the Darkness, its rhythmic *thud* vibrating through my body. I do not understand it, but I feel its presence as a kind of acknowledgment, a silent communion between two beings that exist outside the predator-prey cycle. The Watcher does not absorb me—perhaps it senses that I, too, am a guardian of sorts, a keeper of the Clutch’s memory.  

My existence is measured in the slow accumulation of stories, in the way the symbols etched into my body shimmer and shift over time. I am not born or bred in the traditional sense; I am *grown*, my form emerging from the glassy plains themselves, shaped by the heat and the hum of the Crystal Drinkers. When I eventually shatter—as all Shardlings do—my fragments will become part of the Clutch, my memories dispersed into the ecosystem, waiting for another Shardling to gather them anew.  

In the Smoldering Clutch, I am both observer and observed, a fleeting manifestation of its ancient, unrelenting story. I am a Shardling—a creature of glass and memory, a whisper in the silence of the earth’s wound.
