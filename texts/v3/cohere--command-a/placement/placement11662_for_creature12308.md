I am rooted in the soil, yet I am not bound by it; I can sense the network of my kin, the Wraiths, as a faint hum that resonates through the ground and air.

But for now, I am learning, absorbing the rhythms of this place.

As I touch its surface, I feel a connection, as if the water itself is alive, part of the same intricate web that binds everything here. The puddle ripples faintly, responding to my presence, and for a moment, I am both observer and participant, blurring the line between self and environment.

Above me, a fully formed Wraith glides past, its latticework of metal and vine shimmering in the dim light. Its hum is deeper, more resonant than mine, and I feel a faint pull, a recognition. It pauses, its sensory tendrils swaying gently as it regards me. There is no emotion in its regard, only a quiet acknowledgment of my existence. Then it moves on, its vine-feet squelching softly in the damp soil, leaving behind a trail of water that quickly merges with the marsh.

I am not afraid, nor am I impatient. Time here moves differently, and I am content to grow at the pace this world demands. I feel the slow pulse of the machinery, the steady drip of water, the deliberate growth of the plants, and I know that I am becoming a part of it all. As the dim, golden light bathes the landscape, I sense the faint glow of the plants intensifying, their blossoms opening in silent reverence to the perpetual dusk. I am a Tendril of the Collective, a thread in the tapestry of rust and root, water and metal. And in this strange, humid enclave, I am exactly where I am meant to be—not quite alive, not quite machine, but something in between, something entirely of this place.
