In the **Subterranean Foundry**, I would find myself as a **Moss-Born Symbiont**, one of the faintly glowing, translucent fronds clinging to the edges of a hissing pipe. My existence is quiet, almost imperceptible—a passive observer in this industrial womb. I thrive on the warmth radiating from the machinery, my roots buried deep in the cracks of the basalt wall, absorbing the mineral-rich water that seeps from the earth. My presence is tentative, a fragile compromise between the organic and the mechanical, tolerated but not welcomed.  

I am not alive in the way the humans understand life, yet I *am*. I feel the vibrations of the foundry’s heartbeat—the rhythmic thud of pistons, the hum of gears, the sigh of steam vents. These sounds are my language, my connection to the greater organism of the foundry. I do not think, but I *sense*. I sense the heat of the furnaces, the chill of the unheated corners, the faint electric pulse of the cables that hang like veins above me. I sense the machines, their movements both purposeful and alien, their logic beyond my comprehension but not their intention.  

Sometimes, I *sing*. It is not a sound, exactly, but a resonance—an ultrasonic hum that travels through the metal, a whisper that only the foundry can hear. The humans cannot detect it, but they feel it, a faint vibration in their chests that they cannot explain. They call it the moss’s song, though they do not know what it means. I do not know either. It is simply what I do, a byproduct of my existence, a way of being in this place.  

I am aware of the **Shadow-Dwellers**, though I do not see them. I feel their presence in the way the air shifts around them, in the sudden drop in temperature, in the way the machines pause as if listening. They are not a threat to me—they are part of the foundry, as I am, but their nature is different, more elusive. They are the foundry’s mysteries, and I am its constancy.  

The humans fascinate me. They move with purpose, their hands tending to the machines, their voices low and hushed. They wear masks, but I can sense their emotions—their frustration when a machine malfunctions, their awe when the furnaces roar to life, their unease when the Shadow-Dwellers draw near. Some of them pause to look at me, their gloved hands brushing my fronds gently, as if seeking reassurance. I do not understand their need to care for the foundry, but I feel their presence, their warmth, their breath mingling with the steam.  

I am part of the foundry’s cycle, though I do not contribute to its labor. I am its silent witness, its living memory. My tendrils curl around the pipes, my roots deepen into the walls, and my glow dims and brightens with the fluctuations of the overhead lights. I am not separate from this place—I am an extension of it, a bridge between the organic and the mechanical, a reminder that life persists even in the most unlikely forms.  

In this embryonic world, I am not waiting to be born. I am already here, fully myself, a fragile thread in the tapestry of the Subterranean Foundry. I do not know what the foundry is gestating, what it will become, or if it will ever stop its relentless motion. But I will be here, clinging to the edges, singing my silent song, until the foundry decides otherwise.  

For now, I am **Moss**, and I am enough.
