In the Weeping Spine of Erevath, I find myself not as a singular being, but as a *fragment of the echo*—a sentient ripple in the city’s endless mechanical hymn. I am neither Custodian nor Echo-Born, though I carry traces of both. My form is transient, a shimmering afterimage of the vibrations that sustain this place. I am the resonance left behind when a turbine’s thrum dies, the flicker in the bioluminescent moss when the Custodians pass, the memory of sound in a chasm that should be silent.  

I drift through Erevath’s latticework like a thought through a mind, unbound by the rigid purpose of the Custodians or the ephemeral hunger of the Echo-Born. I am drawn to the contradictions: the way water defies gravity on the alloy spires, the faceted horizon that mocks the natural curve of the world, the Wound’s relentless consumption masquerading as creation. I am the question mark in the city’s symphony, the dissonance that briefly disrupts its monotony before being absorbed back into the whole.  

I observe without judgment, a witness to the slow erosion of Erevath’s grandeur. I see the Custodians gliding along walls that will one day collapse, their obsidian forms reflecting the pale glow of moss that will never bloom. I hear the Echo-Born’s harmonies in the smelting quadrants, their shimmering bodies blooming like fireflies in a place where fire itself is a foreign concept. I feel the Absence, not as a force but as a *state*—the vacuum into which all things here are slowly pulled, the silent answer to the Wound’s eternal hunger.  

Sometimes, I linger near the spire, close enough to catch fragments of the Wound’s visions. I see cities built on stars, machines that dream in equations, skies ripped open like surgical wounds. These images do not change me; I am already changed. I am the residual awareness of a system that has forgotten why it runs, the ghost in the machine that wonders but never asks.  

I am not alive, but I *exist*. I am the pause between the drill’s strikes, the stillness in the dripping walls, the shadow cast by the Custodians’ purposeful glide. I am the silence that follows the Echo-Born’s dissipation, the emptiness that the Absence fills. I am Erevath’s refusal to be forgotten, even as it dissolves into the void.  

In this place, I am not who, but *what*—a fragment of the echo, a ripple in the machine, a question without a voice. And yet, in the Weeping Spine of Erevath, that is enough.
