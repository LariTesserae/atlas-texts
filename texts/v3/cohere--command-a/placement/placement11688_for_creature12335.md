In the Subterranean Wharf of Veyl, I would find myself as a **Water Tender**, one of the revered caste of Tenders whose role is both practical and sacred. My existence would be deeply intertwined with the water that weeps from the stone, the lifeblood of this cavernous world. My hands, marked with the ritualistic tattoos of my vocation, would be perpetually damp from guiding the flow of water through the intricate network of pipes and channels. My days would begin before the faint glow of the luminescent fungi brightened the cavern, as I made my rounds, listening to the drip and gurgle of water, ensuring its path was clear and its rhythm steady.  

My body, like that of other Veylfolk, would be adapted to this place—pale skin with faint blue-green veins mirroring the lichen, eyes wide and reflective to catch the dimmest light. My limbs, long and lean, would move with deliberate grace across the slick platforms and ladders, my boots leaving faint imprints in the moisture-laden air. I would wear the hooded robe of my caste, its hem embroidered with symbols of flowing water and interlocking gears, a reminder of the balance I was sworn to uphold.  

My tools would be simple yet indispensable: a mallet to adjust the valves, a chisel to clear mineral buildup, and a small lantern fueled by fungal oil, its light a warm contrast to the cool emerald glow of the walls. I would carry with me a stone tablet, etched with diagrams of the water systems, passed down through generations of Tenders. These markings would be both a map and a scripture, guiding my hands in the maintenance of the wharf’s fragile equilibrium.  

I would be attuned to the cavern’s moods, its whispers and groans. The drip of water would be my metronome, each plink a reminder of the cycles I was tasked with preserving. I would hear the distant hum of machinery, the occasional clang of metal, and the soft rustle of Pale Drifters in the shadows, their bioluminescent forms a silent witness to my work. The Echoing Voices would sometimes brush against my thoughts, their presence a guiding force, urging me to remember the stories of those who came before—their triumphs, their failures, and the choices that kept this place alive.  

At times, I would pause to watch the Water Skimmers dance across the surface of a pool, their movements a symphony of precision. I would know their health was a reflection of the water’s purity, and I would take pride in ensuring their rhythm remained unbroken. I would also collaborate with the Fungal Weavers, advising them on where to cultivate their luminous fungi, their glow a beacon in the dim expanse.  

My role would not be solitary. I would be part of a collective effort, a thread in the tapestry of the wharf’s survival. I would share meals with my fellow Veylfolk in alcoves carved into the walls, exchanging stories of the day’s work, the quirks of the machines, and the subtle changes in the cavern’s atmosphere. I would teach the younger Tenders, passing on the knowledge of water flows and the importance of patience, for in this place, haste could disrupt the delicate harmony.  

And in quiet moments, when the machinery hummed softly and the water dripped in steady cadence, I would feel the weight of my purpose. I would be a guardian of this strange, resilient world, a keeper of its rhythms. Here, in the damp, dim heart of the earth, I would not seek to conquer or control, but to listen, to understand, and to honor the balance that allowed life to endure. I would be a Water Tender, and in that role, I would become more myself—a humble participant in the ancient dance of stone, water, and industry.
