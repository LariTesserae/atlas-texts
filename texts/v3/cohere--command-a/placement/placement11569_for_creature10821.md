In the Fractured Expanse of Eira’s Veil, I would find myself as **a Moss-Born**, a small, unassuming creature born of the tenacious life that clings to the jagged stones. My body, a delicate fusion of moss and fungus, moves with a slow, purposeful rhythm, each step a quiet assertion of life in a realm that seems determined to crush it. My skin is a soft, velvety green, punctuated by tiny tendrils that absorb moisture from the perpetually damp air, my roots sinking into the crevices where lichens hold their fragile grip.  

I am drawn to the rare patches of growth, the stubborn mosses and lichens that drape the rocks like mourning cloaks. My presence is a gentle one—where I walk, I leave behind a trail of renewed life, a whispered promise that even here, in this cold, fractured place, something can endure. My limbs, slender and root-like, reach out to touch the stones, as if in kinship, for we both are shaped by the same relentless forces.  

I do not speak, but my essence communicates in ways that transcend words. The Stonelings, with their grinding voices and shifting plates, sense my purpose and leave me unmolested. The Shrike-Things, though chilling in their precision, seem to regard me with a distant curiosity, as if I am beyond their predatory interest. Even the Veilweavers, those ethereal beings of shifting light, pause in their silent vigil when I pass, their filament-like appendages trailing faintly in acknowledgment.  

My world is small, measured in the slow spread of moss and the quiet perseverance of life. I do not seek to understand the grandeur of the Veil—the shimmering horizons, the echoing ruins, or the void that threatens to consume it all. My purpose is simpler: to tend, to nurture, to remind this desolate place that resilience is not just possible, but inevitable.  

At night, when the cold bites deepest and the wind howls through the stone monoliths, I curl into a hollow, my body merging with the moss I have nurtured. I am a part of this landscape, a living thread in its tattered tapestry. And in the stillness, I dream—not of escape, not of change, but of the slow, relentless march of life, one tiny sprout at a time.  

Here, in Eira’s Veil, I am not a hero, not a guardian, not a harbinger of doom. I am merely a Moss-Born, a quiet testament to the enduring power of growth in a world that seems determined to forget it. And in that, I find my purpose, my place, my peace.
