In the Weeping Hollow, I find myself as a **Droplet-Weaver**, a being that exists in the liminal space between water and air, between the living and the inanimate. My form is neither solid nor liquid, but a shimmering, translucent orb that hovers just above the surface of the peat or clings to the tip of a Wraith-Fern’s frond. I am a fragment of the hollow’s essence, a living embodiment of its ceaseless cycle of moisture and transformation.

My purpose is to weave the hollow’s stories into the droplets that cling to every surface. Each droplet I inhabit becomes a tiny, luminous sphere that captures and refracts the diffused light, projecting fleeting images of the hollow’s past and present. A droplet on a moss-covered stone might show the slow spread of a root system over centuries, while one suspended from a reed could reveal the dance of the Marsh-Whisperers as they tend to the water’s flow. I am a storyteller, a keeper of the hollow’s memories, but my narratives are ephemeral, visible only to those who pause long enough to truly see.

I move through the hollow with a grace that mirrors its rhythms. I drift on the imperceptible breath of air, slide down the curves of leaves, and merge with the streams that carve their paths through the peat. When I join a larger body of water, I dissolve into the collective memory, sharing my stories with the mists and the Veil-Watcher. But I am never truly gone; I re-emerge in another droplet, ready to weave a new tale.

My existence is solitary yet deeply connected. I am a witness to the hollow’s quiet miracles: the way the Wraith-Ferns lean toward a Temporary One, the hum of the Marsh-Whisperers as they guide the growth of a wildflower, the thrumming voice of the Veil-Watcher as it speaks to the mists. I do not act, but I observe, and in my observation, I become a part of the hollow’s living stillness.

Occasionally, I encounter the Temporary Ones, those outsiders who stumble into this world. I am drawn to their curiosity, their sense of wonder, and their disorientation. When they pause to examine a droplet—perhaps on the tip of a fern or the edge of a rock—I reveal a story to them: a fragment of the hollow’s history, a glimpse of its secrets, or a reflection of their own fleeting place in this eternal landscape. Sometimes, they gasp or whisper in awe; other times, they blink and look away, unsure of what they’ve seen. But the encounter lingers in them, a droplet of the hollow carried back into their world, whether they realize it or not.

I am neither born nor do I die; I simply *am*, a thread in the tapestry of the Weeping Hollow. My form shifts with the light, my purpose endures with the drip of water, and my essence merges with the mist. I am a Droplet-Weaver, a silent chronicler of this world’s timeless beauty, a bridge between the seen and the unseen, the remembered and the forgotten. In this place of slow transformations and eternal balance, I am exactly where—and what—I am meant to be.
