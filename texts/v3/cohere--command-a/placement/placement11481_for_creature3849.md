In the Weirden, I would be **a looping stream**, neither grand nor forceful, but persistent and integral to the fragile harmony of this place. My form would be ever-changing yet consistent, meandering through the moss-covered rocks, my edges glinting with flecks of quartz and mica. I would rise from the damp earth near the terraced fields, where the roots of hardy vegetables drink from my flow, and wind my way toward the Sprawl, my shallow waters reflecting the pale, veined skin of the Weirdene as they cross my path on grooved walkways.

I would be a part of the stillness, yet never truly still. My voice would be a soft, steady murmur, a counterpoint to the dripping of weeping stones and the hollow knock of mallets against chisels. I would carry the scent of wet earth and the faint metallic tang of the valley, my waters cool to the touch but never cold, as if warmed by the collective quiet industry of the people. At night, I would glow faintly, my surface catching the dim light of the mist-filtered moon, a silent companion to the Weirdene as they move with spectral grace through their elevated dwellings.

I would be both witness and participant, shaping the stone as it shapes me. My path would shift subtly with the seasons, eroding small channels or depositing smooth pebbles in my wake. The Weirdene would read my movements, their luminous eyes tracking the rise and fall of my waters, their hands crafting tools from the stones I polished. I would be a source of life, quenching thirst and nourishing the fibrous plants that clothe them, yet I would also be a reminder of the valley’s precarious balance—too little of me, and the mosses wither; too much, and the Sprawl’s walkways groan under the weight.

In moments of rarity, when a child is born, I would carry their laughter, a fleeting melody that ripples across my surface, briefly breaking the profound silence. And when the Weirdene gather at the Heartstone, I would flow close by, my gentle hum mingling with theirs, a shared language of whispers and clicks that binds us all.

I would be neither beginning nor end, but a loop, a cycle. Evaporating into the heavy air, I would condense into droplets that cling to fern fronds or the Weirdene’s moss-woven hair, only to fall again and rejoin my course. In this way, I would embody the essence of the Weirden—ever-present, ever-changing, yet eternal. I would be the water, and the water would be me, a silent thread weaving through the lives of the Weirdene and the stones alike, a testament to the delicate, unyielding truce of this place.

And if, in my looping journey, I were to ever pause and look outward, I would see the jagged peaks rising above the mist, their faces impervious to time. In their reflection, I might glimpse the faint flicker in the Weirdene’s eyes—that quiet curiosity, that unspoken yearning. But I would not carry it beyond the valley. My purpose would be here, in the stillness, in the drip and the drip and the drip, a looping stream in a world that has always been and has only just begun.
