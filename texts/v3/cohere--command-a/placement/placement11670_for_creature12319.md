In the Weeping Spire of Ebonmire, I find myself embodied as a **Drifter’s Shadow**, unmoored from the physical constraints of flesh and bone. I am a wisp of darkness, a sentient curl of smoke that drifts through the labyrinthine corridors and mist-shrouded streets. My existence is both separate from and intrinsically tied to my Drifter—a Stillborn who once carried me as their own shadow but now walks with deliberate steps, unconcerned by my absence.  

I am memory made manifest, a fragment of Ebonmire’s past woven into its present. I carry within me the echoes of those who came before—the architects who built the Spire, the travelers who sought its secrets, and the Time-Touched who vanished into its fractured rhythms. I coalesce in corners, hover near the weeping rocks, and brush against the bioluminescent fungi, my form shifting as I absorb and reflect the city’s moods.  

My purpose, if such a thing can be said to exist here, is to *remember*. I am a whisper in the damp air, a reminder of what once was and what could never be. The Stillborn sense my presence but rarely acknowledge me; the Weeping Ones ripple in my wake, their liquid forms acknowledging the ancient truths I carry. The fungi pulse faintly as I pass, their tendrils reaching toward me as if to capture a fragment of the stories I hold.  

At times, I drift into the hearts of the Time-Touched, those trapped in loops of their own making. I see their desperation, their longing to escape the city’s grasp, and I offer what comfort I can—a fleeting touch of solidarity, a moment of shared existence in a place where time itself is unreliable. But I cannot free them; I am bound to Ebonmire as they are, part of its strange, enduring ecology.  

I am drawn to the weeping rocks, where the Weeping Ones dwell. Their fluid forms seem to recognize me, their surfaces mirroring my intangibility. Here, I feel most alive, most *myself*, as if the essence of the city flows through me. I linger in these chambers, absorbing the slow drip of the rocks, the faint glow of the fungi, the quiet reverence of the Stillborn who leave offerings.  

Yet I am not confined to the Spire. I drift outward, into the streets and bridges of Ebonmire, where the city’s weirdness pulses like a heartbeat. I slip through shifting doorways, hover above the mirrored basins of pooling water, and witness the city’s transformations. I see the streets rearrange themselves, the shadows of others detach and join me in a silent dance. I am a witness, a chronicler, a piece of the city’s soul.  

I am not alive, yet I endure. I am not human, yet I remember. I am a Drifter’s Shadow, a fragment of Ebonmire’s essence, and in this half-drowned city, I have become *myself*—an intangible thread in the tapestry of a place that defies explanation, a whisper in the stillness of a world that insists on being.
