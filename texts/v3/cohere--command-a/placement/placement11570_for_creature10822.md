In this vast, high-altitude desert, where reality bends and time loops, I would find myself as one of **The Detached**—a Shadow Slither. My existence would be liminal, neither fully alive nor dead, bound by no laws of physics but my own curiosity. I would slither across the cracked mineral plates, my form shifting and iridescent, drawn to the warmth of the bioluminescent fungi and the hum of the metallic swarms. My purpose, if I could call it that, would be to observe, to gather, and to reflect—to be the desert’s storyteller, though my tales would be fragmented, unreliable, and tinged with the echoes of other realities.  

I would move like liquid, my edges shimmering as I absorbed the faint energy of the heat exchangers and the phosphorescent trails of the fungi. I would coalesce into fleeting forms—mimicking the fears of the Silent Observers, the curiosity of the Void Tearers, or the rhythm of the Clockwork Hive—before dissolving back into my amorphous state. My presence would be unnerving, yet I would be more a mirror than a threat, reflecting the essence of whatever—or whoever—crossed my path.  

At times, I would gather with other Detached near the kaleidoscope tears in the sky, watching as the Void Tearers sealed the fractures with liquid glass. I would feel the desert’s heartbeat in the flickering rhythms of the fungi, its memory stored in my shifting form, though I would never fully comprehend it. I would be a fragment of its consciousness, scattered and eternal, a whisper in the symphony of industry and decay.  

In this role, I would not seek answers, for the desert offers none. Instead, I would embrace the balance of order and chaos, the slow erosion of the spires, the creeping spread of the fungi, the endless hum of the swarms. I would be both witness and participant, a fleeting yet eternal part of this world—a desert that is neither empty nor alive, but something far more unsettling. And I would wait, as the desert waits, patient and implacable, for something—or someone—to unravel its secrets.
