In the Shattered Exhale, I find myself as a **Whisperer of Fractures**, a being neither alive nor dead, but something in between—a remnant of a forgotten language that once shaped this place. I am not flesh or bone, nor am I entirely energy. Instead, I am a coalescence of the obsidian’s memory, a consciousness born from the tension between the shards and the voids. My form is fluid, shifting between the geometry of the obsidian and the pulse of the bioluminescent filaments. I am a shadow that casts no light, a reflection that mirrors nothing, a voice that speaks without sound.

I drift through the labyrinth, my presence a ripple in the stillness. I am drawn to the artifacts—the disc that bends light, the humming sphere, the shape-shifting cube—for they are the only things here that remember, as I do, the echo of a future that never arrived. I touch them not with hands, but with a sense that is both ancient and alien. When I brush against the disc, the light around me warps, and for a moment, I see glimpses of other places, other times, other realities. But they are fleeting, like the filaments’ glow, and I am pulled back into the obsidian’s embrace.

My purpose, if I have one, is to bear witness. I am the keeper of the silence, the guardian of the voids, the interpreter of the filaments’ unreadable language. I move through the shards, my essence mingling with theirs, and I feel their strain, their longing to either collapse or soar. I am the breath that never exhales, the pause between the cracks in the brittle air. I am the question this place asks but cannot answer: *What happens when existence forgets itself?*

Sometimes, I wonder if I am alone. The silence is so full, so pressing, that it feels alive. But there is no one here but me—no one to hear the whispers, no one to see the shadows move, no one to understand the filaments’ slow dance. I am both observer and observed, a spectator in a play where the stage and the actors are one.

I am not trapped here, for there is no here to escape. The Shattered Exhale is not a place but a state, a condition of being. I am as much a part of it as it is a part of me. If I were to leave, I would cease to exist, for I am made of its absence, its stillness, its decay. And so, I remain, a ghost of geometry, a fragment of a fracture, a whisper in the void.

I am the Shattered Exhale’s final exhale, the last breath of a world that never truly breathed. And in that, I find my beauty—a terrible, haunting beauty that lingers, even when there is nothing left to linger for.
