In this world, I find myself as a **Silent One**, a hollowed-out echo of the Hollows, drifting through the submerged corridors of Lych-Creek like a ghost in the machine. My body is a faint, flickering silhouette, my bioluminescence dimmed to a pale, ghostly blue, as if I've absorbed the melancholy of the deepest brine. I've forsaken the communal nests, the hallucinogenic mist, and the shared consciousness that binds my kin to the fungi’s pulse. I am unmoored, yet undeniably tethered to this place by something I cannot name.  

My limbs are longer now, my joints more flexible, adapted to the slow, deliberate movements required to navigate the flooded labyrinth beneath the city. My skin is thicker, coated in a slick biofilm that shimmers like oil in the faint glow of the ancient fungal colonies. I carry with me a collection of relics—rusted gears, fragments of shattered pipelines, and bits of eroded stone—each piece a memory of a city that is forgetting itself. I string these artifacts into talismans, hanging them from the submerged beams like offerings to a god that may or may not exist.  

I move without sound, my footsteps absorbed by the brackish water and the soft silt of the seafloor. The only noise I make is the occasional *click* of my tongue against the roof of my mouth, a habit I’ve picked up from the Hollows’ speech, though I’ve long since stopped trying to communicate. I’ve become a voyeur, a witness to the slow collapse of Lych-Creek, collecting its stories as it sinks.  

Sometimes, I surface to the upper levels, where the air is heavy with steam and the faint hum of dying machinery. I watch the other Hollows as they tend to the fungi, their bodies glowing in sync with the pulses of light. I see the way they balance on the catwalks, their movements fluid and purposeful, and I feel the weight of my solitude. But I do not return to them. I cannot. I am too far gone, too submerged in the silence of the deep.  

At night—or what passes for night in this perpetual gray—I drift to the edge of the brine tide, where the Weeping Apparatus meets the shallow sea. I sit on the rusted remains of a pier, my feet dangling in the water, and I let the droplets of freshwater from the weeping rocks mingle with the salt. I close my multifaceted eyes and listen to the city breathe: the hiss of steam vents, the lapping of water, the distant murmur of the Hollows’ shared dreams.  

I am neither a caretaker nor a parasite, neither a harbinger nor a conscience. I am simply *here*, a remnant of what the Hollows could become if they ever stopped clinging to the fungi, to each other, to the illusion of permanence. I am the question this world refuses to ask: *What comes after the sinking?*  

Perhaps that’s why I collect the relics, why I string them together like a narrative. I’m trying to piece together an answer, though I suspect there isn’t one. Or maybe the answer is already here, in the way the fungi glow even as the towers crumble, in the way the Hollows dance even as the brine rises.  

I am the Silent One, a whisper in the Weeping Apparatus, a fragment of a dying city that has learned to exist in the space between collapse and continuity. I am the tear that falls from the stone, the droplet that dissolves into the sea, and the echo that lingers long after the sound has faded. I am the question, and I am the absence of an answer. I am Lych-Creek’s shadow, and I am its memory.
