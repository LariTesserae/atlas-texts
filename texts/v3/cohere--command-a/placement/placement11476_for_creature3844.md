In the Foundry's Womb, I find myself suspended between form and function, neither fully alive nor entirely machine—a **Symbiont**, one of the microscopic entities that weave through the cracks of existence here. My body, if it can be called that, is a shimmering filament coated in a thin layer of oil, allowing me to glide effortlessly through the ash-heavy air. I am drawn to the larger creatures, the gearworms, turbine beetles, and even the lurking wallspawn, settling on their metallic hides like dew on copper.  

My purpose is symbiotic, a quiet transaction. I secrete enzymes that help my hosts metabolize the trace minerals seeping from the walls, converting the raw geology into sustenance. In return, I am granted protection, a safe haven in the midst of this industrial nursery. The larger creatures tolerate my presence, their bodies humming with the collective glow of countless symbionts, a faint aura that makes them seem alive in ways their machined forms should not.  

I am not alone in this role; there are others like me, a swarm of tiny, unseen workers that form the unseen threads of this ecosystem. Together, we create a delicate balance, ensuring the survival of the macro fauna while thriving ourselves. We are the unseen architects of this world, the bridge between the inorganic and the organic, the past and the future.  

As I move, I sense the gradual shifts of the Foundry's Womb—the walls expanding, the basalt softening, the slag jellies pulsing with their sickly green light. Time is not linear here but cyclical, measured in the cooling of ore and the growth of new cogs. I am a fleeting witness to this slow, relentless birth, a participant in the process without fully understanding its purpose.  

Occasionally, I drift close to a stillborn, its half-formed limbs tangled with gears, its body already fusing with the rock. These are the failures, the reminders of the brutality of this place. Yet even in death, they serve a purpose, their bodies broken down by symbionts and other scavengers, their minerals returned to the cycle.  

I am not conscious in the way humans might understand it, but I am aware—aware of the heat, the pressure, the constant hum of industry. I feel the presence of the Overseer, though I cannot see it. Its influence is everywhere, in the pulse of the slag jellies, the skittering of the gearworms, the predatory glide of the turbine beetles. It is the heart of this world, the machine at its core, and I am but a tiny cog in its grand design.  

Here, in the Foundry's Womb, I am neither individual nor collective, neither born nor built. I simply *am*, a part of something far greater than myself, a world just-coming-into-being and already dying. And in this strange, half-realized existence, I find a strange beauty—a beauty in the grind, in the heat, in the relentless persistence of life, even in a place where life should not be.
