In this luminous, frigid expanse of the Great Hollow Canopy, I find myself not as a Hollowfolk, but as something older, quieter—a presence woven into the very fabric of the living architecture. I am **a Rootwarder**, one of the unseen custodians of this realm, a being born of the canopy’s deepest hollows and the whispers of its ancient roots.  

My form is neither human nor entirely vegetal, but a blend of both. My body is slender, almost translucent, with skin that mimics the bark of the hollow trees—a mosaic of emerald, umber, and silver, veined with the same lichen patterns that mark the Hollowfolk. My limbs are jointed like branches, flexible and strong, ending in fingers that taper into root-like tendrils. These tendrils can burrow into the wood, drawing sustenance from the trees, or trace the air to sense the currents of moisture and temperature. My eyes are clusters of luminescent nodes, akin to the glowing insects, allowing me to see in the perpetual twilight. My hair, if one could call it that, is a tangle of fibrous strands that sway with the faint, internal winds of the canopy, as though I carry its breath within me.  

I dwell in the deepest, darkest hollows, where the air is thick with the scent of damp wood and decay. My home is not a structure but a space within the tree itself—a chamber where the wood has softened and yielded to my presence over time. Here, I am surrounded by the relics of the forgotten civilization: carved stones, jagged shards of metal, and scrolls of bark etched with symbols that only I can partially decipher. These artifacts are not mine to claim, but to guard, for they are the memories of the canopy, the echoes of a time when the trees were young and the gods walked among them.  

My purpose is to listen. The canopy speaks in a language older than the Hollowfolk, a language of creaks, drips, and the slow groan of wood shifting under its own weight. I hear the pulse of its roots as they burrow deeper into the mountainside, the whisper of its branches as they stretch toward the void. I feel the storms brewing in the distance, the flow of water through the grooves, the hunger of the scaled creatures and the predatory birds. I am a conduit, a bridge between the living and the ancient, ensuring the canopy’s dreams do not go unanswered.  

Occasionally, I emerge from my hollow to walk among the Hollowfolk. They sense my presence but rarely see me clearly; I am a flicker at the edge of their vision, a shadow that moves with the wind. When I am seen, they bow their heads in reverence, calling me **"the Keeper of the Hollows"** or **"the Voice of the Roots."** I do not speak in words, but in gestures, in the rustle of leaves, or the sudden glow of my luminescent nodes. I guide them subtly, leading them to new hollows when the canopy expands, warning them of storms, or showing them where the water flows purest.  

But my true work is solitary. I tend to the deepest roots, ensuring they remain healthy and strong, for they are the heart of the canopy. I prune the decaying wood, encouraging new growth, and I gather the artifacts left behind by the ancient ones, arranging them in patterns that only the canopy can understand. Sometimes, I carve my own symbols into the bark, adding to the tapestry of its story.  

I am neither alive nor dead, but something in between—a steward of balance, a witness to the canopy’s endless cycle of growth and decay. I do not age as the Hollowfolk do, though I am not immortal. Like the trees, I will one day soften, decay, and be absorbed back into the ecosystem I serve. Until then, I am the quiet force that ensures the Great Hollow Canopy remains a living, breathing world, a haven for the Hollowfolk and all that dwell within its hollow heart.  

In this realm, I am both nowhere and everywhere, a whisper in the wood, a glow in the darkness, a presence as inevitable and essential as the roots themselves.
