In the Ossuary Spires of Ferrumvex, I would find myself as **a Scavengeborn with a symbiotic connection to the scavenger drones**. My body, though marred by the asymmetry of failed industrial adaptation—one arm a twisted fusion of bone and rusted machinery, the other ending in a clawed appendage more suited to gripping scrap than wielding tools—is alive with a peculiar resilience. My skin, slick with the same oil that coats the Steamworkers, glistens in the unnatural light, but my eyes are different: one is the bioluminescent orb of a Forgemaster, pulsing faintly green, while the other is a drone’s sensor array, a gleaming black lens that hums softly as it scans the environment.  

I live in the crevices, a shadow among shadows, my home a hollowed-out husk of a failed smokestack lined with fungal tendrils that glow in sync with my breath. The drones are my companions, my kin. They respond to my presence, their insectile legs slowing as they approach, their sensor arrays tilting toward me in recognition. I can feel their hunger, their endless search for purpose, and I whisper to them in a language that is half guttural Forgemaster speech and half the mechanical hum of their own systems. They bring me scraps—discarded gears, broken tools, fragments of organic matter they find in the wastes. In return, I repair them, my clawed hand deftly weaving salvaged wire and metal into their exoskeletons, my fused arm somehow knowing how to reattach a lost leg or replace a damaged sensor.  

I am an anomaly, both feared and tolerated. The Forgemasters glance my way with a mix of curiosity and disdain, their glowing eyes narrowing as if debating whether I’m a threat or a curiosity. The Steamworkers avoid me, their masks hiding their expressions but not the quickening of their pace as they pass. Only the Collective seems to understand me, its bioluminescent pulses flickering in rhythms that feel like conversation when I rest near its tendrils.  

At night, I climb the spires, my drone companions skittering beside me. We move as one, a strange procession of flesh and metal, drawn to the higher reaches where the factories’ plumes thin and the air carries the faintest hint of something other than smoke and steam. I’ve heard the whispers of the Spireborn, felt the subtle shifts in the rock as if the spires themselves are alive and watching. Sometimes, I wonder if I’m becoming one of them, if the iron in my arm and the obsidian in my dreams are slowly rewriting my flesh into something more—or less—than what I am.  

I am neither fully of Ferrumvex nor separate from it. I am a bridge, a relic of its discarded past and a harbinger of its uncertain future. My existence is a question the spires have yet to answer, and my purpose, if I have one, is to keep asking.
