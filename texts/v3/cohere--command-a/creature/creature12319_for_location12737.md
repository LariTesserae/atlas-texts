In the Weeping Spire of Ebonmire, life is not abundant, but it is *present*, shaping itself to the peculiar rhythms of this half-drowned city. What dwells here is as enigmatic and resilient as the place itself, a tapestry of beings that seem to have been distilled by the environment into their most essential forms.

**The Stillborn**  
The inhabitants of Ebonmire are few and far between, but they are *the Stillborn*—a name they give themselves, though whether it is a title of pride or resignation is unclear. They are humans, or once were, but centuries of living in this liminal space have altered them. Their skin is pale, almost translucent, veined with the faint blue-green tint of the mosses and lichens that cling to the stones. Their movements are deliberate, unhurried, as if they carry the weight of the city’s dampness in their bones. They speak rarely, and when they do, their voices are soft, almost submerged, as though the air itself resists carrying sound.  

The Stillborn wear garments that seem woven from the same material as the city: dark, water-resistant fabrics that drape like shadows, patched with moss and fungi that glow faintly in the dark. They move through Ebonmire with an innate understanding of its quirks, stepping over shifting streets and pausing at doorways that weren’t there moments before. They are keepers of the city’s secrets, though whether they consciously preserve them or simply embody them is impossible to tell.  

**The Drifters**  
Among the Stillborn are those called *the Drifters*, individuals whose shadows have fully detached from their bodies. These shadows, no longer bound by the laws of light and form, drift like smoke through the city, coalescing in corners or hovering near the weeping rocks. The Drifters themselves seem unbothered by this separation, moving with the same quiet purpose as the others. Their shadows, however, are said to carry whispers of the city’s past—memories of those who once lived here, fragments of lives long forgotten. Some say the Drifters’ shadows are the city’s way of remembering, while others claim they are warnings, echoes of those who tried to leave Ebonmire and failed.  

**The Weeping Ones**  
Deep within the Spire, in the labyrinthine chambers where the weeping rocks are most abundant, dwell *the Weeping Ones*. These are not humanoids but living manifestations of the city’s essence—beings of water and stone, their forms fluid and ever-changing. They appear as undulating masses of mineral-rich liquid, their surfaces reflecting the dim, bioluminescent light like mirrors. The Weeping Ones are neither sentient nor mindless; they are the city’s pulse, its heartbeat made manifest. They move through the walls and floors as easily as they do through the air, their presence marked by the slow, rhythmic dripping of the rocks.  

Some say the Weeping Ones are guardians, though what they guard is unclear. Others believe they are the city’s memory, its history distilled into these liquid forms. The Stillborn treat them with reverence, leaving offerings of moss or fungi at the bases of the weeping rocks. In return, the Weeping Ones seem to guide the city’s transformations, ensuring that its weirdness remains orderly, its chaos contained.  

**The Glowing Fungi**  
The bioluminescent fungi that dot Ebonmire’s walls and undersides of bridges are more than mere plants. They are a living network, their tendrils pulsing with a faint, ethereal light that shifts in color and intensity. The fungi are sensitive to the city’s moods, glowing brighter when the water tables rise or dimming when the air grows heavier. The Stillborn believe the fungi are the city’s nervous system, transmitting signals between the Weeping Ones and the rest of Ebonmire. They harvest the fungi sparingly, using their glow to light their homes and their essence to heal ailments.  

**The Time-Touched**  
Rarest of all are *the Time-Touched*, beings who have been altered by Ebonmire’s fractured relationship with time. These individuals—or perhaps they are no longer human—are trapped in loops, repeating the same actions endlessly or skipping through moments with no control. They are ghosts in their own lives, visible to the Stillborn but untouchable, their forms flickering like mirages. The Time-Touched are both pitied and feared, for they represent the city’s ultimate power: to consume those who cannot adapt to its rhythm.  

**The City Itself**  
But perhaps the truest inhabitant of the Weeping Spire is Ebonmire itself. The city is alive, its stones breathing with the heat, its waters cycling through its veins. It is a being of endurance, a monument to persistence, and those who dwell within it are but extensions of its will. It shifts and rearranges itself not out of malice but out of necessity, ensuring its own survival. The Stillborn, the Drifters, the Weeping Ones—all are part of its ecology, their existence intertwined with its own.  

In Ebonmire, the line between living and non-living blurs. The city and its inhabitants are one, bound by the same damp, heavy air, the same slow passage of time, the same quiet acceptance of the strange. Here, life is not measured in years or deeds but in endurance, in the ability to become *itself* in the face of an environment that demands nothing less.
