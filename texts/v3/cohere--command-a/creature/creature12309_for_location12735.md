In this place, where the air is a tangible weight and the earth feels raw, there is a sense that the inhabitants, if they can be called that, are as much a part of the landscape as the rusted silo or the twisted shrubs. They are not beings in the conventional sense, but rather embodiments of the tension between industry and nature, emergence and decay.  

The first to reveal themselves are the **Weepers**, named for the way they seem to condense from the air itself. They are not alive, yet they move—slow, amorphous shapes that form from the coalescing mist. Their bodies are made of water and metal particles suspended in the humid air, their edges shimmering like oil on wet stone. They drift without purpose, sometimes pooling near cracks in the ground or swirling around the skeletal remains of machinery. They are neither hostile nor friendly, merely *present*, as if they are the physical manifestation of the valley’s latent energy. When they dissolve, they leave behind a faint metallic residue, a trace of their existence that rusts almost immediately.
