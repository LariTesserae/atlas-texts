In the Embryonic Hollows of Stonehaven, the inhabitants are a people shaped by the very essence of the place—patient, observant, and deeply attuned to the rhythms of their nascent world. They are the **Hollowborn**, a community that seems as much a part of the stone as the stone is a part of them. Their skin bears the pale, earthy tones of the hollows, often streaked with mineral hues from the damp walls they press against or the clay they mold. Their eyes, adapted to the soft glow of luminescent fungi, reflect the muted light with a quiet intensity, as if they carry the weight of both darkness and potential within.

The Hollowborn are slight in build, their bodies streamlined from navigating the uneven terrain and crouching within the low ceilings of their shelters. Their hands are calloused from handling rough stone and delicate tools alike, their fingers dexterous from carving, building, and tending to the fragile ecology around them. They move with a deliberate grace, their footsteps careful not to disturb the stillness or the delicate balance of their environment. Their clothing is utilitarian, woven from plant fibers and animal hides, dyed in shades of gray, brown, and green to blend seamlessly with their surroundings.

These people are not loud, nor are they hurried. Their voices, when they speak, are low and measured, carrying the resonance of those who understand the value of silence. They communicate in a language rich with metaphors drawn from their environment—words that describe the drip of water, the crack of stone, the slow spread of moss. Their stories, told around communal fires, are of the hollows’ origins, of the first cracks that let in light, and of the ancestors who first shaped these stones into shelter.

The Hollowborn are builders, but their craft is not one of haste or ambition. They work in harmony with the hollows, allowing the terrain to guide their hands. A shelter is not forced into existence but coaxed from the stone, its shape dictated by the natural contours of the wall. Pathways are not carved arbitrarily but follow the most efficient routes between water sources and fungal light. Their tools—antler chisels, stone hammers, bone scrapers—are simple yet precise, designed for a people who measure progress in increments of moss growth or the widening of a crack.

Their society is communal, bound by an unspoken understanding that survival depends on collective effort. Each member contributes according to their strengths: some tend to the fungi, ensuring their glow remains steady; others scout for new water sources or signs of shifting terrain; still others craft tools or weave clothing. The elders, whose memories stretch back to the earliest days of the hollows, serve as guides and teachers, their wisdom rooted in the slow, deliberate pace of their world.

Children of the Hollowborn are taught from a young age to listen—to the drip of water, to the rustle of roots, to the faint hum of the stone itself. They learn to read the signs of their environment, to anticipate where the hollows are guiding them. Play often mimics the work of their parents: stacking stones, shaping clay, tracing the patterns of moss. Their laughter, soft and rare, echoes like a whisper in the stillness, a fleeting warmth in the cool air.

The Hollowborn are not without fear. They are wary of the unknown, of the places where the darkness is deepest or the stone feels most alive. They speak in hushed tones of the **Deepening**, a term for the uncharted regions of the hollows where the walls seem to pulse with a strange energy and the air grows thicker, heavier. It is said that the Deepening holds the secrets of their world’s becoming, but it is a place of danger, where the rules of the hollows may not apply. Few venture there, and those who do return changed, their silence heavier, their gaze turned inward.

Despite their caution, the Hollowborn are a people of hope. They see themselves as stewards of a world in its infancy, their hands shaping its future with every careful action. They celebrate the small victories—a new vein of luminescent fungi, a crack that widens just enough to let in more light, a moss patch that spreads farther than expected. These moments are marked with quiet rituals: offerings of water to the stone, shared meals by firelight, and songs that rise and fall like the drip of water in the darkness.

In their essence, the Hollowborn are reflections of Stonehaven itself—unfinished, full of potential, and deeply connected to the slow, relentless pulse of life emerging from the earth. They are not the masters of their world but its companions, walking alongside it as it becomes itself. And in their stillness, their patience, and their careful hands, they embody the quiet miracle of a realm rising from darkness, one drip, one stone, one breath at a time.
