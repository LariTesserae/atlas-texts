**Inhabitants of Luminehra: The Forge-Tethered and the Root-Bound**  

Luminehra’s inhabitants are as much a product of its environment as the glasswort vines or the skelk fish—shaped by pressure, heat, and the relentless interplay of biology and industry. They are not merely *in* the subterranean city; they *are* it, their lives braided into its rhythms like threads in a loom.  

**The Forgemasters**  
At the apex of Luminehra’s hierarchy are the **Forgemasters**, a clan of engineers and metalworkers whose lineage stretches back to the chamber’s colonization. They are tall and lean, their skin pallid from generations beneath dim light, yet marked with faint blue veins that echo the glow of the vines. Their eyes are narrow and accustomed to darkness, but their hands—large, calloused, and stained with metallic oxides—are their true tools. Each Forgemaster wears a **pressure-gauge pendant**, a disc of brass and crystal that hums in sync with the chamber’s vents, its needle twitching when the air grows too thin or hot.  

The Forgemasters are both revered and feared. They alone understand the Hartforge’s whims, its temperamental piston strokes, and the alloy recipes whispered down from ancestors. They move with a monkish precision, their rituals rooted in necessity: anointing the Hartforge’s gears with vine-oil at dawn, reading cracks in the obsidian floor for signs of the vent’s restlessness. Their language is technical and sparse, peppered with terms like *“sap-sear”* (a bloom’s corrosive phase) or *“skelk-clog”* (a pipe blockage). To them, Luminehra is not a place to escape but a body to maintain—and like any body, it demands sacrifice.  

**The Vinetenders**  
Below the Forgemasters are the **Vinetenders**, a class of gardeners-cum-mechanics who prune, graft, and coax life from the glasswort vines. Shorter and more muscular than their superiors, they wear gloves woven from the vines’ discarded tendrils, which grant resistance to the plants’ slime but leave their fingers permanently stained turquoise. Their faces are often streaked with bioluminescent pollen, and their lungs rasp from decades of breathing steam-thickened air.  

Vinetenders are both artists and janitors. They sculpt the vines into living conduits, guiding their roots to leaking pipes or failing circuits. When a bloom threatens to sap-sear a factory tier, they rush in with **resin-sprayers**, their movements choreographed to the hiss of steam. They hum tunelessly as they work, a habit said to soothe the vines—though whether this is folklore or fact is unclear. Their children are taught to *listen* to the plants: a faint crackle in a tendril might signal a skelk fish clog, while a sudden dimming could mean the Hartforge is overheating.  

**The Skelk-Fishers**  
At the bottom rung—though Luminehra’s hierarchy is more a spiral than a ladder—are the **Skelk-Fishers**. These are the chamber’s misfits: those too clumsy for the Hartforge, too allergic to vine-slime for tending, or simply too curious for their own good. They are tasked with crawling through the pipe network, armed with magnetic grapples and glow-worm lanterns, to hunt the skelk fish that threaten to clog the system.  

Skelk-Fishers are small, wiry, and perpetually damp. Their skin is covered in faint scars from skelk bones, and their fingertips are permanently webbed from hours in the water. They are Luminehra’s storyteller class, spinning tales of the pipes’ depths: whispers of a *“Great Skelk”* that hums like the Hartforge, or rumors of tunnels leading to the “Skyvault,” where the air burns lungs but lets you *see* without vine-light. Many believe they are mad—but their madness is useful, as it keeps the pipes clear and the stories flowing.  

**The Silent Ones**  
Lastly, there are the **Silent Ones**, Luminehra’s ghosts. No one speaks of them openly, but their presence is felt in the way workers avoid certain corners or whisper prayers before tapping a new vein of crystal. These are the remnants of those who failed the chamber: a Forgemaster who misread the vent’s warning, a Vinetender dissolved by unchecked sap, a Skelk-Fisher lost in the pipes. Their shadows linger in the darkest pockets, and their breaths are said to mix with the steam when the Hartforge pauses.  

Some claim the Silent Ones *maintain* Luminehra, their spirits woven into its machinery like the vines. Others insist they are a cautionary tale, a reminder that even in a city-organism, balance is fragile. Either way, when the earth groans and the vines flicker, the inhabitants cross their arms over their pressure-gauges and wait—because in Luminehra, even silence has weight.  

**Eccentricities and Adaptations**  
- **Bioluminescent Tattoos:** Many workers etch their skin with patterns that mimic the vines, using ground crystals and skelk fish bones. These glow under the vines’ light and are said to ward off sap-sear.  
- **The Bloom Festival:** Every decade, after a controlled sap-sear, the chamber celebrates with a ritual where the Hartforge is shut down, and workers dance in the temporary silence, their masks removed to breathe “clean” steam.  
- **Pipe-Whispering:** Skelk-Fishers claim they can communicate with the skelk fish by tapping pipes in Morse-like patterns, though this is likely superstition.  

In Luminehra, the line between caretaker and cared-for is blurred. Its people do not merely *live* here—they are cogs in its machine, roots in its soil, and sparks in its forge. And when the Hartforge’s piston falls silent for good, they will not leave. They will simply become another layer in the chamber’s mosaic, their bones cracking like obsidian under new growth.
