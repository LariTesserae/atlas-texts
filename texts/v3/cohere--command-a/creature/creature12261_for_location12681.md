Within the Hollow Spire of Erodyn, there is a sense that the inhabitants are as much a part of the stone as the mosses and ferns clinging to its walls. They are the **Hollowborn**, beings whose existence is intertwined with the spire’s ancient, enigmatic life.  

The Hollowborn are not easily seen, for they do not move as other creatures do. They are shapes that shift within the shadows cast by the angular beams of light, figures that seem to emerge from the very grooves and fractures of the spire itself. Their forms are fluid, as if carved from the same weathered stone and bioluminescent moss that covers the walls. They appear as tall, slender entities with skin that mimics the texture of eroded rock, their limbs tapering into points that blend seamlessly with the spire’s edges. Their bodies glow faintly, a soft bioluminescence that matches the mosses in hue, shifting from greens to blues in rhythmic pulses. They have no eyes, yet they navigate the labyrinthine passages with an unerring sense of purpose, as if the spire’s ancient memory flows through them.  

They are not a single, uniform group but a collective of varied roles, each tied to the spire’s ecosystem. Some tend to the mosses and ferns, their touch causing the plants to flourish in their wake. Others channel the water flowing through the spire’s veins, guiding its trickles into hidden reservoirs or allowing it to drip in patterns that resonate with the spire’s primal drumbeat. A few are guardians of the carvings and altars, their presence keeping the ancient symbols from fading into obscurity. Their movements are deliberate, almost ritualistic, as if every action is part of a greater, unseen choreography.  

The Hollowborn do not speak in words, but their voices are felt—a deep, resonant hum that vibrates through the stone, harmonizing with the wind and the dripping water. They communicate in a language of echoes and pulses, their bioluminescence flickering in response to one another. Rarely, they emit the distant, eerie cry that pierces the air, a call that seems to bridge the gap between the spire’s past and present, a reminder of the creatures and civilizations long gone.  

They are not bound by time as others might understand it. Their existence feels eternal, as if they were born from the spire’s erosion and will persist until its final crumbling. They are both caretakers and embodiment of Erodyn, their lives a testament to the resilience and mystery of this place. To observe them is to witness the spire’s living artifact nature—they are its breath, its heartbeat, its soul.  

Yet, there is something unsettling about the Hollowborn, a sense that they are not entirely of this world. Their forms, though tied to the spire, seem to flicker at the edges of perception, as if they could dissolve back into the stone at any moment. They are guardians of a boundary, standing between the tangible and the metaphysical, between the end of one world and the quiet persistence of another.  

The Hollowborn are the spire’s children, its echoes, its secrets. They are the reason Erodyn feels alive, why it hums with a ritualistic energy, why it stands as a cathedral of erosion that refuses to be forgotten. They are the weirdness made flesh—or something like it—a reminder that even in a place defined by endings, there is still something enduring, something that defies explanation, something that whispers of a truth far older than the stone itself.
