In the Subterranean Wharf of Veyl, life has adapted to the peculiar rhythms and constraints of this hidden world. The beings that call this place home are as layered and resilient as the cavern itself, their existence a delicate interplay between industry, nature, and survival. Here are the inhabitants of this subterranean realm:

### **The Veylfolk**
The primary denizens of the wharf are the Veylfolk, a stoic and industrious people whose bodies and culture have evolved to suit their environment. Their skin is pale and slightly translucent, marred with faint, blue-green veins that mimic the lichen-covered walls. Their eyes, adapted to the dim glow of fungi and gas lamps, are large and reflective, capable of discerning movement in the faintest light. Their hair, if they have any, is sparse and often tinted with mineral stains, a testament to their proximity to the earth’s veins.

The Veylfolk are slender and wiry, their limbs elongated to navigate the uneven terrain of platforms, ladders, and tunnels. Their hands are dexterous, calloused from generations of working with metal, stone, and water. They move with a quiet efficiency, their footsteps dampened by the moisture that clings to their boots. Their clothing is utilitarian—layered, water-resistant garments made from woven fungi fibers and treated leather, often patched and repaired to reflect their resourcefulness.

Their society is built on collective effort and shared purpose. They live in alcoves carved into the cavern walls, their homes modest but functional, with bunks, storage nooks, and small hearths fueled by dried moss and fungal matter. Families are tight-knit, with children learning the ways of the wharf from a young age—how to tend machines, divert water, and harvest the luminous fungi that light their paths.

### **The Tenders**
Among the Veylfolk, a specialized caste known as the Tenders tends to the machinery and water systems that sustain the wharf. They are the priests of this industrial ecosystem, their knowledge passed down through generations in oral traditions and intricate diagrams etched into stone tablets. The Tenders wear hooded robes adorned with symbols of flowing water and spinning gears, their hands marked with ritualistic tattoos that signify their role.

These individuals possess an intimate understanding of the cavern’s hydraulics, its vents, and its pipelines. They spend their days inspecting machines, lubricating gears with rendered animal fats, and redirecting water flows to prevent flooding or drought. Their work is sacred, for without their care, the delicate balance of the wharf would collapse. The Tenders also oversee the harvesting of luminescent fungi, ensuring the settlement remains lit without depleting the cavern’s natural resources.

### **The Pale Drifters**
In the shadows of the wharf, away from the clatter of industry, dwell the Pale Drifters—blind, bioluminescent creatures that are as much a part of the cavern as the stone itself. These beings are tall and thin, their limbs jointed in ways that allow them to move silently along the walls and ceilings. Their skin is a translucent white, dotted with patches of soft, pale blue light that pulse gently in time with their breathing. They have no eyes, their faces smooth and featureless save for a slit-like mouth and a pair of nostrils.

The Drifters are herbivores, feeding on moss and fungi that grow in the darkest corners of the cavern. They are shy and reclusive, emerging only when the wharf’s machinery quiets for maintenance. The Veylfolk view them with a mix of reverence and caution, believing them to be spirits of the stone or guardians of the water. While the Drifters pose no threat, their presence serves as a reminder that not all life in the wharf bends to the will of industry.

### **The Water Skimmers**
On the surface of the cavern’s pools and streams, tiny, translucent creatures known as Water Skimmers dart and weave with mechanical precision. These inch-long beings resemble a cross between insects and fish, their bodies segmented and covered in fine hairs that repel water. Their legs are elongated and spindly, allowing them to skim across the surface tension of the water, while their tails anchor them in place when they feed.

The Skimmers are filter feeders, their bodies designed to extract minerals and organic matter from the water. They move in synchronized patterns, their movements almost hypnotic, as if guided by some unseen force. The Veylfolk harvest the Skimmers periodically, drying them to create a nutrient-rich meal or grinding them into a paste used to treat rust on machinery. Despite their utilitarian value, the Skimmers are also seen as harbingers of the water’s health, their population fluctuations closely monitored by the Tenders.

### **The Fungal Weavers**
Clinging to the walls and ceilings are patches of luminescent fungi, tended by a subset of Veylfolk known as the Fungal Weavers. These artisans are responsible for cultivating and harvesting the fungi, which serve as both light sources and building materials. The Weavers have an almost symbiotic relationship with the fungi, understanding their growth patterns and needs intuitively.

The fungi themselves are a marvel, their caps ranging in color from soft emerald to deep indigo. They emit a steady, cool light that never flickers, their glow diffused by the moisture in the air. The Weavers carefully prune the fungi, ensuring they spread evenly across the cavern’s surfaces. They also weave the tougher fungal stalks into mats, ropes, and even clothing, their craftsmanship a testament to their mastery of this organic resource.

### **The Echoing Voices**
Deep within the tunnels that honeycomb the cavern walls, whispers of the Echoing Voices can sometimes be heard. These are the spirits of the wharf’s ancestors, or so the Veylfolk believe. The Voices are not seen, but their presence is felt in the way the air seems to vibrate with unseen energy, or in the faint, rhythmic hum that occasionally overlays the machinery’s clatter.

The Veylfolk speak of the Voices with a mix of reverence and unease. They are said to guide the Tenders in their work, offering wisdom through dreams or sudden insights. Some claim the Voices are the collective memory of the wharf, a living record of its triumphs and tragedies. Others believe they are warnings, reminders of the fragility of the balance that sustains this subterranean world.

### **The Balance**
Together, these beings form a fragile yet enduring ecosystem. The Veylfolk build and maintain, the Tenders preserve and protect, the Drifters and Skimmers embody the cavern’s natural rhythms, and the Weavers illuminate its paths. The Echoing Voices, whether real or imagined, bind them all to a shared purpose: to survive and thrive in this improbable place.

In the Subterranean Wharf of Veyl, life is not about dominance or conquest but about adaptation and coexistence. The water, the stone, the machines, and the creatures all move in a dance as old as the cavern itself, each step a testament to the resilience of life in its strangest, most beautiful forms. Here, in the damp, dim heart of the earth, harmony is not a given—it is a choice, made anew each day by those who call this place home.
