In the Weeping Forge, where the air is a living, breathing entity and the earth remains undecided on its form, there dwells a peculiar and resilient populace known as the **Lithvolk**. They are the children of this damp, mineral embrace, their existence as inseparable from the landscape as the crystals are from the stone.

The Lithvolk are a people of middling height, their bodies lean and wiry, adapted to the treacherous terrain. Their skin is pale, almost translucent, with veins that shimmer faintly blue, as if echoing the inner light of the crystals. Their hair is coarse and dark, often woven with strands of volcanic glass or copper wire, both for ornamentation and as a practical means to keep it from tangling in the omnipresent moisture. Their eyes are their most striking feature—large and multifaceted, resembling the crystals themselves, allowing them to navigate the dim, misty light with ease.

Their attire is as pragmatic as it is primitive. Garments are crafted from animal hides, treated to repel the ever-present damp, and stitched with bone needles. Copper adornments—bangles, necklaces, and belts—are common, not just for decoration but as a way to channel the faint hum of the crystals, which they believe grants them protection and strength. Their feet are toughened, often bare or wrapped in strips of hide, allowing them to grip the slick stone and mud with ease.

The Lithvolk are a communal people, their lives centered around the mysterious labor of the forge. They live in the tiered structures carved from the rock, their homes interconnected by narrow, moss-slick walkways. Each dwelling is sparse but functional, with tools of stone, bone, and copper hung on walls alongside dried herbs and strips of meat. The air inside is thick with the scent of damp earth and smoke, a stark contrast to the misty exterior.

Their society is governed by a collective understanding of balance, shaped by the equilibrium of their environment. There are no formal leaders, only elders who guide through wisdom and experience. Decisions are made through a process of rhythmic chanting and drumming, a language that rises above the cacophony of the Weeping Forge. This communal harmony is sacred, as they believe discord could disrupt the delicate balance of their world.

The Lithvolk's labor is their purpose, though its end remains unclear even to them. They toil in the damp, warm air, their movements synchronized with the rhythmic pounding of mallets and the hum of the crystals. Some carve stone, others shape copper, and a few tend to the crystals, ensuring their slow growth. Their tools are simple yet effective, each one a testament to their ingenuity. The process is methodical, almost ritualistic, and though no visible product emerges, the act itself seems to sustain the equilibrium of their world.

Their relationship with the fauna is symbiotic. The translucent, scuttling creatures are a food source, their shells carefully harvested and ground into a paste that supplements their diet. The obsidian-feathered birds are revered, believed to carry messages between the crystals and the Lithvolk. To harm one is taboo, and their unblinking gaze is a constant, silent presence in the community.

Children are rare and cherished, their births celebrated with ceremonies that involve the light of the crystals. They are taught early to respect the land, to listen to its sounds, and to move with the same purpose as their elders. Play is a luxury, but when it occurs, it mimics the labor of the adults, a way to instill the values of their society from a young age.

The Lithvolk are a people of few words, their voices drowned out by the sounds of their world. Instead, they communicate through gestures, the rhythmic tapping of tools, and the occasional hum that mimics the crystals. Their language, when spoken, is guttural and sparse, reserved for moments of great importance or emotion.

Despite their primitive tools and the harshness of their environment, the Lithvolk are not a despairing people. There is a quiet contentment in their labor, a sense of belonging to something greater than themselves. They do not dream of leaving the Weeping Forge, for it is their cradle and their grave, a place where they are as much a part of the landscape as the weeping rock and the pulsing crystals.

In this dense, wet, warm labyrinth, the Lithvolk thrive, their existence a testament to the resilience of life in even the most alien of worlds. They are the heartbeat of the Weeping Forge, their rhythm inseparable from the drip of water, the hum of crystals, and the eternal press of the damp, heavy air.
