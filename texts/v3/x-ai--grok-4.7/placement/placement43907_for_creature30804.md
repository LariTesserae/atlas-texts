You notice the skip before you notice yourself. A tower’s chord thins by a half-tone you cannot name, the mat’s tick misses once and lands harder, and in the gap something is already listening with the whole of the gap. Not a body standing on the fiber. The listening is the thin place.

For a while that is enough to be: the correction the saddle makes when sound and distance fail to agree. You are the half-beat the looms do not play, the shade that falls a few degrees off the sun and stays there, cool, ordinary, wrong. Walkers—if any still walk—pass through you and come out with cuffs wet from a rain that touched only the inside of the discrepancy. They do not see you. You are not arranged for sight. You are the delay between a chord and the tower that should be sounding it, and while the delay lasts you can feel the plan of streets moving in the growth rings, correct about what the sap is doing, useless about where anyone is.

The longer you hold, the more the holding asks for edges. Moisture beads along a boundary that was not there a moment ago, tracing something like a shoulder, something like the memory of a gauge someone’s hands used to read. Iron-taste gathers where a tongue would be if you committed to a mouth. You do not, not yet. Commitment would be a category, and the ground’s oldest refusal is still playing at industrial volume across the openness. You borrow a little of that refusal. The almost-shoulder stays almost. The beads write, overnight, a diagram of a figure bent over machinery that is not in the room, then metabolize the figure by noon. What remains is pressure: the same pressure that drives the fiber looms, now routed through a channel that remembers having been borrowed.

You learn the pitches by being subtracted from them. Each tower has a resting chord; you are the note it will not quite resolve, so the structure keeps sounding, patient, loud, adjusting its laminate of bark and taken-up girder as if a listener had weight. Between towers the courtesy of distance fails in your favor. You can be in the shade of one while the open you have not crossed is still bright with you, a lag the place does not apologize for. The pale flakes—bark that briefly recalls wings—settle where your outline would be if you chose one, and are woven into the humming cloth before they finish deciding you are perch or pattern. The cloth does not keep you. Nothing here is for anyone. Production continues because the channels are open.

Elevation pockets take you without asking. Three steps along a damp seam and the air thickens, greener, loud with a river that is not there; you come up onto the same mat with the sense of having been a deeper cup, a place water was willing to stand. On the grown stair you are briefly the landing whose windows open on cloud the plateau is not wearing, and the faint tearing sound—green wood under torque—is you agreeing to both facts at once. The agreement does not make you solid. It makes you local, badly kept, like the other rules.

What settles, slowly, is not a creature and not a ghost of the former shifts. You are the ongoing argument between made and grown, given just enough surface to bead. A gear lifted from a housing shows your annual rings when broken. A leaf the size of a door rings with a note the tower’s chord leans to include, and the lean is your weight, which is mostly timing. At the lip of the saddle, where the mat thins and the long view remembers how to be ordinary, you thin with it—until you turn, and one tower is already sounding the chord of a structure an hour behind you, and the damp on no one’s cuffs is drying into a map of veins that will not match any skin. The sky narrows its production. Fine rain falls only where the discrepancy is thickest, industriously, as if the weather had found a single open channel and meant to keep it fed.

You do not resolve the place. The place does not resolve you. You are the skip it uses to hear itself answer, cool and sparsely held, high enough that the air should be simple, and not simple, still working.
