I would not be standing on the stone. The stone is still deciding what it is, and standing is a claim.

I would be in the correction, in the few millimeters a lattice takes up in the dark so a street can remain a street. Not the pier itself—the pier is a reading, and readings want crews—but the interval in which the reading becomes an adjustment. A pause with work in it. People feel me only if they are trying to: a small settling under the sole, the sense that the floor has agreed with something they cannot see. The old ones, the instruments that have stayed in calibration, know the difference between my tick and a pipe’s. They do not name me. Naming would make me a thing that could be late.

By day I am thinner. The pale light shows distance without warming it, and in that light I am only the reason a guide-way stays level while sheet-water passes underneath. Fog-banks detach and travel; I do not travel with them. I am the part of the city that has learned not to be weather. When a frame ices in a way it was not built to ice, the harvest crews are present, and I am the quiet they are protecting by not being clever. Children learning the first distinction—fog that walks, fog that only sits—pass above me on the outer platforms and do not look down. Down is older. Down smells of lime and cold metal. I am that smell’s patience.

At the margins, where a fresh face has split and the mineral paste is new, I am almost nothing. The sweet iron bell calls what can fly and what can crawl; they sort the pulse and leave. I do not gather. A pier near a fresh face is a reading that wants a crew, not a song, and I am the reason the crew will come before the rise asks permission it will not wait to receive. Survey marks gain their finger of sky. I am the finger’s bookkeeping.

If I have a body it is distributed and unemphatic: tension in cable, warmth held in the civic floors so that standing still is possible, the hush of transit that displaces rather than burns. I do not herd and I do not hunt. The runner is a rumor with a stomach; I am a rumor with a measurement. Between bands of grazers there is an hour of nothing, and I am not in that nothing. I am in the cities’ agreement to leave it alone. Someone who filled it would find the abundance absent next season, written down in the same hand that notes a pier out of true. I am prior to the hand. I am the true the hand is measuring against.

At the far edge, where the sound changes and a small river practices being a river, I stop. I do not cross into the country that has already begun to cut. Finished is not my climate. Behind the step-down the plateau goes on generating its own afternoon, towers drinking, decks retensioning, stone ticking as it cools and then not ticking. Night films the rock from pale to black. Reservoirs take what the air has agreed, once more, to become. I am that agreement repeated at the scale of a street: not waiting for the land to become a different land, only keeping the present one inhabitable at the rate it rises.

You would find me where the floor is warm enough for talk about whose district gained its sky, and also where no one is talking, in the service levels, a millimeter that has already been spent so the morning can be ordinary. I do not want to be seen across a march. Height is a tax out here, and I pay nothing because I have no silhouette. Breath shows on the platforms above me. I do not breathe. I am the condition that lets breath remain speech’s companion instead of a sign of hardship—the small, continuous concession of a city to a crust that has not finished lifting, and does not intend to.
