You would not find me by looking for a face. The shield does not keep faces. It keeps routes, and I am the interval between stations on one of them.

At first that is only a pressure: the habit of going on when nothing asks you to stop. The brightness has no center. Shadows fall in the same blue whether you face them or not, and for a long time I take that for proof that I have no side, no flank, no weight worth a shadow of my own. The wind sorts dust past what might be ankles and does not part for me. The pavement clicks or does not. I could be a change in the grain, the kind a basin is — present as a longer shade at a heel, if heels were a thing the land had decided to remember.

What thickens first is thirst, and thirst is the wrong word, so it has to be unlearned in the walking. There is no water to want. What I want is the dark oval under a fallen column, the place where oil has gone into grit and the nap grows richer and a little wrong. I do not drink it. I stand in it the way the shields stand in dust, and something in the pads of me — I have pads; they announce themselves by the craze they leave, three contacts, wide-set, never deeper than the skin of the crust — takes up a sweetness that has no orchard. After that the next empty reach is possible. Before that it is only glare.

So the feet come before the body. Three, not four, or three that meet the ground and a fourth that is a rudder of balance I never quite see, lifted in the thin air like a thought you do not spend. The track I leave is the first sentence I trust. It runs the open between stands, not through them. The stands are not cover. Cover would imply a hunter, and the shield has not bothered to invent one. They are stations: mineral already half-made into leaf, silver thrown back at the coin of the sun, inner folds black enough to be a kind of night if you could fold yourself small enough to enter. I cannot. I am not small. The knowledge of size arrives as a refusal — the columns will not take my weight, the pavement complains if I pause too long in one print, and the complaint is a pale star of fractures that the wind will worry if I am careless. I am large in the only way that is allowed here: spare, arranged not to sweat, economy instead of hide.

Pale, when I catch it, is not a color I chose. Late in a wind-day a dust-veil lays a band of cooler shadow across a stand a kilometer off, and in the band my own flank shows, the way a ridge shows when the light stops arguing with it. Hide the color of the crust, slightly oiled where the shoulder has brushed a silver leaf and kept the bead. The oil does not wet me. It beads and is worked in, the same office the leaves perform, slower, with less grace. I am not a plant. I cannot tilt with the afternoon and call it done. I have to cross.

What I cross toward is never the next stand in sight. Sight fails cleanly here; the farther edge is not an edge. The route is older than any one brightness. It is measured in the ovals, in the breaches where a seed rang against stone and was not polished to nothing, in the husks tumbled to the lee of a leaf and sanded thin — husks I do not make, or made once and no longer recognize as offspring. If I have young they are the hard bright grains, and the wind is the only parent that can be counted on. I do not turn back to watch them go. A route that turns back stops being a route and becomes a territory, and territory is a wet idea. It wants a center, a den, a smell that means mine. Nothing here smells of mine. Stone, sweet oil, the cold metal of my own flank when the veil thins: those are the whole vocabulary, and they belong to the shield first.

There is a mind, if the word can be starved down to fit. It does not narrate. It keeps two kinds of time. One is the step, the click, the parchment-tick of leaf on leaf when I am near enough that my bulk makes a small weather and the shields shift a fraction early. The other is the week. A week is the distance from a dark oval that still feeds to a dark oval that has begun to seal, and my job — I find I have one, the way the pavement has one — is to arrive before the seal is complete and after the wind has finished delivering what the oil will hold. Miss it and the next reach is a kind of thinning. Not hunger. A loss of the sweetness that lets the pads read the crust. Without that reading I would walk the basins without knowing I had entered them, and the land would plane me out of its memory the way it planed out everything lower.

I do not call. Nothing answers, and the air will not carry a voice even if I invented one. Once, at the limit of glare, I saw the pale of another flank moving with the same economy, on a route that would meet mine only if both of us abandoned the ovals, which neither of us will. We did not close. Closing would require a surplus — of water, of heat, of interest — and surplus is what the height refused so long ago that the refusal has become geology. If we share anything it is the timing of the wind-seasons, and that is not kinship. That is the shield using two bodies to do one job: to move a little of the oil’s richness off the straight line of the prevailing dust, so the next stand roots a fraction nearer a stain than chance would like. I am a curve in a distribution. The curve has three toes and a shoulder that shines when the veil is edge-on.

Death, when I let myself know it, will be the same as the columns’ death. Abrasion, drying, a dark oval of my own that the pavement is slow to cross. No rot. No gathering. The route will forget the particular spacing of my prints within a season of wind, and some other spare flank will find the ovals still on the old measure and will not know they were mine. That is not loneliness. Loneliness wants an elsewhere. There is no elsewhere at this height. There is only the condition: cool noon, exact shadow, stands far enough apart that the chemistry can finish, and a body that persists by refusing to be lush except in the one place lushness is allowed — the slow internal working of mineral into motion.

If you stood in my print before the edges went, you would find the craze already loosening, grains the size of seeds lifting one by one into the trough the wind prefers. You would not find me ahead of you unless the week and the oval agreed. And if they agreed, what you would see is not an approach. A pale continuance. A flank the color of the shield, arranged not to spend itself, going on because the distance between stations is the only form I have, and the land has kept that form so long it no longer looks like an event.

I am the one the tracks belong to while they last. After that I am the reason the next green is not quite where the wind alone would have put it.
