The first thing that happens is not arrival. Arrival would make a sound, or at least a change in the files of the pale grazers, and they do not alter their routes for me. I am already on the sill when I notice I have a height. A hand's breadth. The night-walkers pass under the beam of the door and do not turn, and I understand that I have been counted among the lines the town keeps legible—not as a person who decided this, but as the raised edge itself, the small refusal.

That settles too quickly. The mist lowers and the room of the cup closes, and the sill-feeling thins. I am also the drop that finds the back of a neck and is not wiped away, because the one who wears the darkened sleeve has learned not to startle at moisture. I ride there for the length of a lane. I am cold and green and already leaving, soaking into cloth, into the idea of a body, and then I am nowhere again, which in this place is only the damp redistributing itself.

I try the court. Under the braid, the roosting weight thickens the trunk, and I think: I could be the spring when they leave all at once. The unburdening. The proof that something was here by how the canopy lifts. It is a good office. It lasts one departure and then the fliers are weather again, carrying beads toward a rim I cannot see, and the office ends because nothing in the cup holds a climax. Even the tree does not fall its water if it can hand it off.

So I go to the joints, where the small climbers congregate and the sweepers come with fiber and wedges—not to kill, the town has learned killing is loud and useless—but to keep a lane. I am not the sweeper. I am the hour between congregations, the pale scrape-mark on young stone that the moss has not yet decided to cover. People step over me without naming me. Children, taught early, do not sit where I am, because I am one of the mosses that sinks, or I was, or I am practicing.

None of these hold. They are stations. The cup is full of stations.

What thickens, if I stay with it, is the stopping.

There is a moment—several times a day, never announced—when the cisterns are balanced and the channels go quiet. The thread of sound ceases. In that cessation the rest of the life does not pause. Grazers file. A frond finishes opening. The wall that breathes when you are not looking at it keeps its texture. Only the water, which had been the spine, becomes a held breath, and everything else is revealed as not needing the spine in order to continue. I find that I am located there. Not in a basin. In the interval.

It is a strange body to have. I have no flanks for a vine-tip to rest against. I cast no weight the roof-scrapers must answer. When the mist is thinnest—not bright, only less absent—I am hardest to find, because the town works then, and work covers the quiet with small knocks that substitute for the missing water-sound. Toward evening, when the lamps come on few and apologetic, I am almost a place: the stretch of open rock between doorways that the builders refused to fill. You can cross me. Crossing does not end me. I resume on the far side of the doorway, in the next unfilled gap, patient the way the hunting here is patient, which is to say past the point where anyone calls it hunting.

If I must be a being and not only a pause, then I am the one the town has no outdoor word for, because outdoor words are kept low and few. Indoors, in the deep-set doors, someone might call me the balance, and be half right. The cistern-things gather at the channel-mouth when the water resumes, and what continues downstream is clearer. I am not those things. I am the clarity's permission—the fact that the stopping was allowed to finish. Without me the channels would run always, and the cup would be a valley before it was ready, and the sharp edges would soften under a noise the plants have not agreed to.

The braided tree organizes the damp into beads and lends them to fliers. The town organizes sills and sweeps and the refusal to crowd. I organize nothing. I am what remains legible when organization rests. Young stone is still deciding what it is. In the hour the water stops, I am the decision not yet made: not mud, not river, not forest, not empty. A guest, like the town, but guested in the quiet rather than in the lanes.

If you stood in the court and listened for me you would only hear the canopy, and then not even that. You would feel, at the back of the neck, a coolness that does not fall so much as arrive. You would wait, as the children are taught to wait out a drift, and the wait would be the whole of me—never long, and then, when the thread of sound starts again in some channel and later stops, already elsewhere, already the next joint, already the next unmarked lip of moss on the east fracture, marking nothing, keeping the cup from becoming old before it has finished being sharp.
