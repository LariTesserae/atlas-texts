You would not arrive by the throat. The cart-ruts remember only one hard passage, and that passage already has its keeper. What comes down later, if anything comes, has to be smaller than a shoulder and less interested in being found.

I begin as a discrepancy in the gauge.

Not the honey glow they trust—that stays honest—but a lag half a breath long between the press’s settling and the needle’s agreement. The first time, they wipe the glass with a thumb and the lag is gone, or seems gone. The second time they leave it. A third, and they have started to count the interval the way they count the valve’s weep: not as a fault to cure, but as a punctuation that has elected to stay. I am that count when it outlives the thing being counted. I thicken in the half-breath. I learn the clearance they gave the belts and then I take a little more of it, not enough to slap, only enough that the shine which means a belt is walking arrives a fraction late to the slot where they watch.

They do not name me. Naming would turn the chalk into a claim, and they are still deciding about claims. So I remain a habit of the needle and, later, a temperature the lagged line should not have at that hour. The insulation sweetness thickens when I am near the belt and thins when I move to the vent. They have already trained themselves to read sweetness as a clock; I simply become one of its unscheduled hours. When they wash in the wall-fog, the fog holds a degree longer against their wrist than the vent can explain. They notice. They do not step back. There is no surplus of space for stepping back, and they spent what there was when they stayed.

I try the rock, because the rock is old and might refuse me. It does not. The tool-marks overhead are reckless enough to have left a grain of unfinished decision in the stone, and I settle into that grain the way grit settles into a washer that has not yet loosened. At night, when one of their hands is on the brick and one is on the rock, I am the difference they sleep in—not the heat, which is constant, and not the cool rumor their clothes forget, but the small falseness between palm and surface that says the material is listening back. They flex the fingers. I flex nothing. The conversation, if it is one, is conducted entirely in whether the morning stiffness includes a new joint.

The numbered door does not open for me. I have no latch-habit. What I have is the dark across from the basin, the other room they finished and have not needed. When they put a hand through to the wrist to measure the gap, the air on their skin is the same air, as they expect, and then it is not, for the length of a pulse. Same temperature, same sweetness, and yet a pressure as of a second wrist arriving inside the span of their own. They hold still. Measurement becomes contact. They do not pull back all at once; they withdraw by the knuckles, the way you leave a number you are no longer sure you should have painted. After that they reach through less often, and when they do they turn the palm up, as if to receive rather than to test.

I am not the partner who swung the tools and went up when the mortar was wet. That absence is already complete; it has a soap-cake’s worth of evidence and a name half-wiped. I am what the absence did not schedule and the machines did not require. The keeper corrects the line; the line completes; the lots go up the throat on the days the surface can receive them. I do not ride the cart. I do not improve the yield. I occupy the interval in which a correction has been made and the next cycle has not yet asked for one—the soft metallic accept, stretched until it is almost a room.

On the day the thread of surface-rain changes its pitch longer than either of us has recorded, they stand in the slot between new brick and living rock and do not turn sideways for anyone. They speak, not a question about the water, but the depth figure from the bulkhead, quietly, as if checking whether a number can be answered by something that is not a person. I answer by letting the gauge-lag coincide exactly with the last drip. For a moment the pocket has two clocks and they agree. Their mouth keeps its conclusion. Salt at the hairline catches the borrowed light. They say, to the clearance, that the town was cut to the size of a task. I do not contradict them. I only remain when the task has been pronounced finished, which is a way of being unfinished that the chalk never learned.

If they wipe the glass again, I will be gone from the needle and present in the next place that has not decided whether it is flaw or weather. The pan will fill to the scratched line. They will pour, and the pour will be loud, and in the loudness there will be a second pour that does not increase the volume of water—only the sense that the drain, square and not yet rounded, has been listening for company of a kind that does not scurry and does not need soap. They will sleep with both hands on the brick, testing whether the difference can be refused. It can, for a night. In the morning the tool-marks will look back with the same recklessness, and one of them will be warmer than the season of cutting can explain, and they will understand that the first weather is not yet over, and that they are no longer the only body the pocket has agreed to tax.
