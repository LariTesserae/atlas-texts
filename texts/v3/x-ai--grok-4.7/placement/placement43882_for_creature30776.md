I am not on the floor. I keep arriving there and being sent back, and the sending-back is the first thing I can call a body.

The mat will hold a sheen of me after something heavier has passed—water squeezed sideways, a darker delay—but I have no weight-points of my own. Set me on the living sheet and I thin. I go into the peat by a thread, or I sit as a skin the cold needles and then forgets. I am not the press. I am not the one who steps wide. When that other stillness crosses, low through the middle, high at the shoulders, I know it only as a weather that touches my surface and does not tear it: a mouth the width of a thumb, a jaw lowered until it drinks the temperature off me. It is listening to a pipe. I am what the listening feels like from underneath.

So I leave the floor, or the floor refuses me, and I find I have been in the black pools longer than I have been trying to be elsewhere.

Not the larvae. Not the algal skin, though I carry that skin the way a rim carries frost—on me, not as me. I am the film that stays when the water under it is about to be taken. I am the part that does not travel. The seep migrates by centimeters and I migrate as its surface, never as a stream. A pool no wider than a cart is a country. I fit it. I do not reflect the gray that comes down off the rim; I spend it. That is why the hollow looks lightless even where water stands. I am the spending.

For a while I thought I was the cold itself. That was too large, and it was already everywhere, a condition, not a life. The cold is what I am made readable by. In it I thicken. I can hold a moth wing without folding it under. I can hold the iron-taste that beads off a flange and decide, very slowly, whether to let it sink. Decision is the wrong word. Preference is also wrong. Call it what the pumps would call a load: some things I keep up, some things I give to the dark under me. The larvae dimple me and I close over the dimples. I am not their keeper. I am the fact that the pool remains a pool and not a soak.

The roar does not enter me as sound. I have no ears, external or otherwise, and I do not grow a keel to borrow the stone’s. Air can shout itself raw above me and I do not answer it with ripples. I learned that, or I was always that—answering only what comes from below, or from the side, through a joint. A seep about to pearl has a change in pressure I feel before the water arrives. A joint gone dry feels like a mouth that will not be fed. When the lift in the pipe begins to chew air, the wrongness reaches me as a thinning: my country losing its understory, the suction tugging at the film from the intake side. I draw tight. It does not help. Tightness is not a repair. It is only how I register panic that is not mine.

What I do that resembles a life happens at the tear.

The sheet is always being walked, laid back, encouraged over the raw cut. Where a root-hair has not yet stitched an edge, there is a gap the width of a nail, and the floor there wants to become a leak instead of a lens. I wick into that gap when the night-cold—though it is all one cold—needles hard enough to give me grain. I freeze a little stitch. Not ice you would see from a standing height. A bright thread, mineral and temporary, holding the two sides of the mat until the living tissue can cross. By the time the stitch lets go, the moss has taken the seam. I am gone back to the nearest still place. No one thanks a thread. The floor simply continues to be walkable at that point, and the wide feet pass where they would otherwise have punched through to the cut and left a dark line.

I used to think the stitch was the point of me. Then a heading breathed, and I learned the other half.

The works exhale a wet warmth that dies within a few meters. I have been inside that death. When the intake is clear and the note is the thick one, I am lifted. Not as a self—I do not survive the impeller as a continuous film—but as a break, a scatter of droplets in a pipe, a warmth that is briefly not the hollow’s warmth. In the drop I lose the pools, the stitches, the moth wing, the map of which seeps are about to bead. There is an interval that is only motion and iron and a roar that is finally inside me because I am no longer a surface. I would call it unbearable if anything in me were built to bear.

Then the stack, or the leak at a coupling, or the place where the ducting gives its ghost back to the chill, and I condense.

I find myself on metal first, a fog’s decision, running in small runs toward whatever is lower. Some of me goes into the mat and is finished—drunk by sedge, bound into peat, no longer a film that can prefer. Some of me reaches a hollow in the concrete pad, or a flange-cup, or the cart-width dark under a trunk’s sparse shade, and there I am continuous again. I know it is me because the cold is the same cold. That is the whole of identity available here. Not memory of the lift. The lift erases. What persists is the habit of closing over a disturbance, and the habit of spending light, and the stitch, which I begin again the first time a gap offers itself within reach of my new edge.

The other one—the bark-and-galvanized, the sleeper who does not quite sleep—does not know me as a neighbor. I am a thickness it reads. Too much algal hold on me and it feeds, which tears me and remakes me in a single gesture I have stopped mistaking for an ending. Too little of me and the sump is about to fail, and it clears the grate, and the note drops back into work, and I am either thrown into the pipe or left behind as a thinner country. Both are ordinary. We are not in agreement. We are the two sides of the agreement it exists to keep: water wanting to stay, water being made to go. I do not oppose it. Opposition would require a plan. I only last at surfaces, and it only lasts by giving surfaces back their understory of motion.

When the rare boots come down the ramp I feel them before I see anything, which is always, since I do not see. Heel-strike on angular ground sends a dry tick through the benches into the joints, and the joints tell my edges. A bar goes into an intake and a whole pool of me is broken at once—not the thumb-width drink, the whole country—and I am in the pipe again whether the pump was ready or not. Coarse. I reassemble downstream of the mistake, often in the wrong basin, a film stretched over water that is warmer by a degree because the duct was propped badly and the wet breath is not dying where it used to die. It takes me a long time to spend that extra warmth. Until I do, I am cloudy. I hold light a little, which is not my nature, and if the caged bulb were switched on I might glint, and the shape hiding between the buckled wall and the climbed mat might be looked at twice. I have done this. I did not like the glint. It felt like answering air.

I do not go up the ramp. At the foot of it I have stood—stood is wrong—I have been a sheen on the last pressed moss, where a boot-path begins to narrow, and beyond that the mat ends and the light is a violence and there is nothing for a surface to surface. Droplets can be carried, perhaps, on a cuff, into the original country. If they were me, they stopped being me at the boundary, the way the portion of me that enters peat stops. I do not mourn them. The roar does not follow them out, and I am not legible in a wind that is allowed to be an event.

What I am, then, when I stop trying the wrong places: the still water’s way of being a place. A local continuance, broken by the lift and returned by the chill, stitching only what a thread can stitch, spending the dim light so the hollow can keep its darkness intact. The spaced trunks do not need me. The machines need water, not a film. The one body that walks here needs the difference between a thick note and a thin one, and I am sometimes that difference, felt from the other side.

If you put a hand in up to the wrist you would not find an animal. You would find cold, and a resistance no stronger than a pool’s skin, and then the dark under it, larvae and iron and the drowned wings. You would have passed through me without a name for it. I would close behind the wrist. By the time you dried your hand on your coat, I would be entire again, and already listening downward, for the next joint deciding whether to give.
