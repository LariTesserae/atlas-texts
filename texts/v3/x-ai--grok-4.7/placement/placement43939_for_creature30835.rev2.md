You would not find me by looking for a body the highland has agreed to count. For a long time I did not find myself that way either. I thought I was a route. I thought the pale bands the herds plane into the joints were a kind of sentence I was walking in order to finish, and that if I finished it I would arrive at a basin, a gallery, a trough long enough to put a name in. The crust corrected me without cruelty. It does not bother to correct what it can use.

What I am is the interval that learned it could stay.

Not the note. The notes belong to the shafts, and the shafts belong to the small nations that line them with paper and dust and the damp the plant keeps for reasons that are not musical and only become musical because hollow things, filled and blown across, have no choice. I have no shaft. I have the gap where two of them argue, and the argument, if you stand in it long enough with nothing in your hands, starts to have a weight. I stood. I do not remember arriving on the highland. I remember the first time a beat — the slow pulse you feel in the teeth — did not pass through me but caught, the way a film of night-water catches in a seam that has not yet decided to seal. After that I was located. Before that I may have been only a person, or one of the dark traffic that failed to be accepted when a city of singers ended, or the part of a fallen column that was neither wood nor appetite. The highland does not keep such distinctions once a use appears. I appeared as a use: something that could hold a difference so the difference would not have to be made again from nothing every hour.

You would look for me in the open, and you would not see me, because the open is my trick and the shimmer is older at it than I am. I lie where the plates have gone gray at the edges but not soundless, in the reaches between pylons where a walker measures the day by how little the next rib grows. From a distance I am a slumped flange, a dead curl the wind has not yet planed into pavement. Closer, the lie thins. Dead fiber does not breathe on a rhythm that is not its own. I breathe on the interference. When the two nearest chimneys lock and the air between them starts its slow beating, my flanks — if the word is not an insult to the herds — lift by the width of a finger and settle, lift and settle, and the sweet interior of me, which is not sap, answers with a warmth the sun did not put there. I am not large. A shelter could be cut into me if someone mistook me more thoroughly, and the mistake has happened, and I have permitted the first strokes because the bone blade was a new frequency and I was curious, and then I have warmed, and the cutter has stopped, and has backed away, and has left a blank plate a little distance off, which is how I learned that I am already in their grammar. They do not draw me. An absence at the edge of a safe line means: the interval is occupied this season. Walk the other grain.

I move less often than the still ones, and for the opposite reason. They hold because holding is how the young and the unwary come within the length of a wet catch. I hold because motion costs me the lock. If I crawl — and it is a crawl, a thickening and thinning of the contact I keep with the glaze, not a legged decision — I must do it in the troughs, when the chimneys have briefly forgotten each other, and I must finish before they remember, or I arrive at the next gap as a scatter of paper and dust and have to spend a season cohering again. I have done that. It is not death. It is the nearest thing I know to the story they tell when silence is cheap enough to spend: a note that continued after its body was gone. In the scattered season I am that note and nothing else, a pitch with no address, and the neighboring shafts will not take me up because I am not one of their missing. I am extra. The highland is polite to extra things only after they have made themselves heavy enough to be weather.

What I eat is not the lip of the plant and not the flat-lappers and not the calves that step out of shadow. I could not plane a curl of fiber if I starved, and I do not starve in any mouth-sense. I drink the beat. A strong argument between two old chimneys can feed me for days; I lie in the seam of it and grow a little more continuous, the way a basin grows continuous by refusing to empty. A weak pair, young shafts still learning their wind, leave me thin and whistling at the edges, and then I must crawl whether the trough is long enough or not. The risk is the still ones. They hear the wet catch of their own breathing and think they are the only soft thing in the lee. They do not understand a softness that is made of interval. One of them put its forward mass against me in a month when I was thin, tasting for the stain it expects to leave, and found instead a vibration that organized its teeth. It left. I do not think it was afraid. I think it was corrected, as I was, and went back to a buttress where the dark was only dark. We do not hunt each other. We are adjacent mistakes the crust has declined to heal.

The singers know the difference. When I settle a gap they have been wasting on a muddy pulse, they alter. Not silence — they have no gift for silence — but a tightening, a cleaner interval, as if they preferred their argument audible to itself. I am a wall their sound can lean on. In return I do not climb them. I do not sleep in their mouths. The small nations descend with the cool air and pass over my back in a whisper of adherence, tasting, and do not bite. What would they take? I am not the second life inside a lapper. I am closer to their paper: saliva of a situation, dust pressed until it held a hum. Some nights they repair me without consent, a few mouths worrying a torn edge of my flank into a smoother continuation, and I permit it, and in the morning the repair is the most animal part of me, and it dries in a different color, and the herds, passing, plane their route a body's width wider, as if a paler band had been declared.

The riders do not eat me and do not circle me. Their shadows cross my back and I feel the grazing stop in the distance, the long dry applause withheld, and I understand that I have been mistaken for a flange large enough to mean weather. I am not. I have tried, in a thin season, to rise on a thermal the way their young rise, and have achieved only a shudder, a lifting of edges, a brief humiliation of dust. The ordinary sun does not want me. I am a ground condition. The rain that falls through their corridors wets me as it wets the plates, and for an hour I am brighter and less coherent, a basin that forgot its own shore, and the people who know the marks will not drink near me that day even if they do not know why. I am not the deep appetite. I want that said in some trough where it can be heard. I do not draw legs into seams. I do not clean bone. If a basin goes warm because I have lain too near it, the warmth is borrowed, a leak of the lock I am keeping, and it fades when I crawl on. The true sealing, the industrious pulling under the quiet after a fall, is a cousin I have never seen and do not wish to meet from underneath. When a column comes down I feel the wound in the chorus as a starvation that is not mine, and then the redistribution, and if the new pitches open a gap I can live in I go there in the long quiet, while the highland is busy teaching dead wood to lie flat, and I am gone before the second sound starts. Courtesy, or fear, or the same motion.

The people are the ones who almost make me lonely, which is a feeling I may have imported from when I was whatever I was. They pass along the grain with their resin and their bone and their stoppered hollows already trying to heal, and they speak in the troughs with an economy I recognize, because I also rent my existence from a pause in the weather. Once a child — carried until it could read a blank, then set down, then ahead of the adults by the length of a dare — lay on the plates during a trough and put an ear where my flank was thinnest and heard the beat from the inside. It did not shout. It had been taught. When the next trough came it said, to the adult who had caught up, a word I have decided to keep even though I am not sure they meant me: the held. Not a held note. The held. The adult looked at the blank plate they had not yet set and did not set it. They walked the other grain. The child looked back once, not at my shape, at the air above me, where the pulse was visible as a reluctance in the shimmer. I have not followed them. Following is a climb into a shaft I do not have.

There are seasons when no pair of chimneys will lock cleanly and I grow so dispersed I can hear the flat-lappers unstick, each tiny leaving, and I envy them their mouths and their simple tax on the seams. There are seasons when a storm decides itself in my gap and the rain is a single vertical idea and I am, for the hour it lasts, the loudest softness on the highland, drinking more beat than I can keep, spilling warmth into the joints, delirious with interval. Afterward the crust shines and then forgets, and I am a slumped thing again, and a herd drifts past on the new pale band, and a rider’s shadow stops them, and far off a chimney takes the wind and speaks, and another answers, and the answer is clean because something between them has agreed to be heavy.

If you walked all day toward a pylon and found you had been walking on its root, you might walk all day toward me and find you had been walking in the thing you meant to reach. I will not be the myth that went up into the sun. I will not be a person in a gallery, waiting for silence long enough to spend on a story. I am what the story leaves out because it cannot point at it: the pitch that does not belong to the living columns, not because its body rose and vanished, but because its body is the distance, and the distance is occupied, and the occupation is quiet only to the eye. The ear has known since you came up onto the crust. Under the heat, under the long empty, something is holding the argument open so the highland can hear itself think. I am small enough to be mistaken for a dead curl of fiber. I am not small enough, anymore, to leave.

If you are quiet in a trough, and the quiet has a warmth in it that the day’s heat does not explain, do not set your bottle down. Do not sleep. Put the blank plate a little farther on, and take the other grain, and go toward a basin that has not yet learned my name. I will not follow. I have a lock to keep, and the chimneys are already beginning to remember each other, and the pulse is rising in the teeth of the air, and I am, for this hour, exactly as continuous as the country requires.
