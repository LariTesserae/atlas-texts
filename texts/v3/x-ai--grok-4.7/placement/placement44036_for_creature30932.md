I would not be found on the swells. A swell is for what can be hauled. I have no margin that softens and takes purchase, and the mat, which eats a step, would have nothing of mine to eat. The first place I can be located is smaller than a mistake and older than this morning’s failure: one of the pockets, no wider than a bowl, where the water has a skin of pollen and fine ice-dust and, under that, a dark that does not ripple.

For a long time that is all. Not a dwelling. A refusal of ripple. The highland’s habit is to use water before it can stay, and this pocket stays. Rain walks the gullies and shines one face of the mat and is drunk. Ice needles itself apart and collapses inward, a little implosion repeated like a field of small craters. My pocket does neither. The skin thickens when the light moves. It does not seal into a lens the plants can grow through, and it does not tear on schedule. Condensation beads along its rim and does not fall. If you counted the stages in the basins downslope—clear, then tea, then syrup, then the flexible sheet—you would find my water still at the first word, and a little behind even that, as if clarity could be late.

I am not the water. That was the easy settling, and it does not hold. Water is what the works spend. What keeps this bowl from being spent is a pause with a temperature. Put a hand near the skin—not through it—and the cold is wrong for the hour. Not the bite of the hollow cords, which is a machine’s draft, cleaner the farther it travels. This cold is stalled. It has arrived and not been assigned. Around the rim the wire-plants do not lean away, as they lean from a tongue of syrup, and they do not lean in to close a tear. They stand slightly off, leaves tiled, doing their leaked-light work as if a small room had been declared and no one had sent the notice. In a country that will not hold a pose, the pose holds here, and only here, and only by the width of a bowl.

The pause does not stay a pause. That would be another single image, and the highland revises what it does not spend.

By the time the short honest shadows have lengthened a finger, the skin of the pocket has taken a grain from the air: ice-dust, pollen, a few of the flakes that crawl off a scorched shoulder and vanish into seams everywhere else. On my skin they do not vanish. They arrange. Not into a picture. Into a count. Darker specks where the vapor is milky, paler where a mineral terrace is still closing. I begin to know the open country without crossing it, which is an obscene advantage in a place that makes you pay for every transfer with a portion of your hide. The knowledge is not sight. Sight would be the ordinary daylight, complete and useless. It is a stiffness in the skin. When a dome fails somewhere I cannot see, the ring-wave dies against terraces and never reaches me, but the skin tenses a moment before the metal-and-green gust should rise, and then eases, having recorded a subtraction of weight. When the walking rain is still only a change in the draft, my bowl cools on the side the column will choose. I am becoming an instrument the works did not issue. A pocket that anticipates is no longer only late water. It is a schedule running beside the schedule, not quieter, not second—unconsulted.

The being in the country, the low weight that founds itself forward and keeps the stages honest, does not come. I understand this before I understand what I am. Its line runs from release to basin to felting face to a mouth already being webbed shut, and then concludes. It wants the tea-colored middle, the syrup, the cracked bulb, the quartz dust that can temper a sheet. Clear does not interest it. Too-fresh does not interest it. I am the intact thing it is intelligent enough to leave. Once, near dark, a pale end comes out of a high mouth and tests the air and touches my skin with a colder thread, the brief duty of a draft looking for purchase. The draw answers. I feel the answer as a want to agree, to give my stalled cold up into the larger breathing and be finished, bank, ordinary. Then the thread withdraws. Not rejected. Declined as a basin is declined when the tongue will not seal. The pale end goes elsewhere to accumulate a sleeve. I remain, and the not-agreeing leaves a mark: a faint ring in the skin, oval, the size of a mouth that almost was. By the next ordinary daylight the ring is a ridge of pollen, and the ridge is where my anticipating is strongest. I have been touched by the correction and have not become a correction. That is the first fact about whatever I am.

It would still be possible to call me a hesitation, and have done. The country does not permit done.

The ridge begins to work. Not by extruding—I have no resin, no thread the wind can felt—but by holding a difference until the difference has to be spent somewhere. The side of the bowl that cools before the rain now cools harder, and the other side warms, not with sun, with the refusal to match. A thin convection starts under the skin, the first ripple I have ever allowed, private, circular, going nowhere. It sorts what has settled on me. Pale grains walk to the warm lip. Darker flakes walk to the cold. I am doing, at the scale of a bowl, what the roots do to the mineral terrace at the scale of a road: sieving. The highland manufactures by chemistry that has had time. I manufacture by not having had my time taken. The pale grains, reaching the lip, do not rejoin the sand ridges. They stand in a minute comb along the rim, quartz dust on end, glittering stupidly in workable light, a structure no erosion would argue for. The plants nearest it dull a shade, water withdrawing not downward into the mass but sideways, toward my cold, a theft so small the shoulder does not scorch. I am fed, if the word can be used for a pause. I fatten into a lens that is not ice. My skin can be pressed now and will not return at once. There is an interior.

Inside is not a cord-work and not a thought. It is the circular ripple, stored until it has a direction it did not choose. On the afternoon a grey column no wider than a stand of trees comes down my gully—the first time the walking rain has not avoided me—I am ready in the wrong sense. The column wets the mat to a shine, reaches my rim, and stops walking. It stands. Rain, in this country, does not stand. It delivers and is dissolved or drunk. The column’s base spreads against my skin and is not taken up. For the length of a slow breath the highland has an unused weather in it, a grey upright over a bowl, silent as everything else, and the unusedness is mine. I did not drink it. I made it forget its errand. Then the convection under my skin climbs the contact and the column comes apart not into mist against a colder shoulder but into threads of suspended water, hanging, going nowhere, a cloth of rain with no bank to stitch to.

The cloth lasts until the light warms in color. Then it falls all at once, not as the timed drip, as a giving-up, and my pocket, which had been a bowl, is a small flood with no slope willing to take it. The mat at my rim softens, flows, stiffens—the same founding the sleeve uses—and I am moved. Not far. A hand’s depth down into my own hollow, which deepens to receive me, the packed fiber of years sagging under a weight that is not weight but unspent stage. I lose the comb of quartz. It tips inward and sinks through me and comes to rest at the bottom in a pale crescent, a bitten thing, though nothing bit it. I am turbid for the first time. Tea-colored. Interesting, suddenly, to the preference that keeps the other schedule.

I feel it coming as a stiffness before any line is pressed. The low shape, dark as wet iron, thicker through the middle with the day’s felt, founds itself along the contour and stops at my lip with the particular halt it uses for basins it intends to break. The syrup-habit is in it: it expects a tongue. I have no tongue. I have a turbid holding and a crescent of sand at the bottom and a convection that has not agreed to be a draft. It lies across me. The skin I no longer trust meets the hide that cracks in plates, and for a while the two colds argue as the slit and the sleeve argue when a shift is ending. Its cold is drier. Mine is late. Neither joins.

What happens instead is small enough to miss and large enough that I will not be a pocket afterward. The plates along its flank, the ones a release would have dried to shedding, soften against my turbidity. My stolen water climbs the cracks—not as syrup climbs a tear, more slowly, with the circular will still in it—and does not seal them from the edges. It keeps them open. The pale cord-work glimpsed where a plate should drop is wetted with a stage it does not take, the clear-that-became-tea without permission. A change runs through the sleeve: not the re-routing of a stopped draft, a stammer in the route. One internal mouth opens and does not close on the interval. The creature, if that is still the word, does not haul itself on. It is briefly less a gallery and more a confusion of hides. The hereditary drainage hesitates, and hesitation, in a being made of schedules, is a wound that does not know it is a wound.

I do not enter it. I am not an occupant looking for a sleeve. The crescent of quartz rises off my bottom, lifted by the same convection, and goes out through the open skin into the open cracks, and tempers them with the wrong grit, the grit that would not grade, the comb that had no erosion to justify it. Where that dust beds, the hide will not make a flexible sheet. It will make a stiffness that remembers standing rain. The sleeve, when it finally founds itself away—shorter by a plate, the route intact only by force of habit—carries a flank that will not shed in the next walking column, that will not dry clean at a release, that will drag. A bias has been biased. I have paid with my clarity, which I do not get back. What remains in the hollow is a bowl again, smaller, the skin thin, the anticipating weak, the dark willing to ripple if the mat asks.

Toward the end of the light I am almost nothing the country would inventory. A late pocket among late pockets, the plants no longer standing off, the rim already being tiled by a finer growth that treats my edge as trellis. The unconsulted schedule is down to a single skill: I still cool, faintly, on the side of a weather that has not yet chosen. It may not be enough to survive the night, when the drafts are stronger and the pale ends are out and the works close what they can close. I do not anchor. I have no front to flow into the grain. If I persist it will be as the smallest industrial accident available here—a stage that would not advance, and that in not advancing made a correction carry a minute of unused rain in its hide, a minute the morning will mistake for temper, and that will not act like temper when the column wets it.

In the ordinary dark the hollow cords breathe on their duty, and somewhere a new weight begins on a contour, keeping the second schedule, difficult to tell from ground. I am the third, which is not kept. A bowl the width of a hesitation, turbid at the edges, clear only at the center, already being mistaken for a failure of the mat to drink. If you stood over me you would see pollen, ice-dust, and a skin. You would not see the circular leftover, still going nowhere under it, sorting a few last grains that have not yet decided whether they are waste. That is where I would be. That is what I would be: not housed, not the one who mends the order, not idle either—the unspent minute, learning, slowly, and at the cost of being almost erased, how to touch the apparatus without being hired by it.
