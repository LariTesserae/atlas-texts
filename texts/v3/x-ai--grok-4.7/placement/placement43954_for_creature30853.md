I come up the last switch already listening for the place to correct me, and for a while it does not. The air thins in the ordinary way. The eastern line is only a change in the grain underfoot, paving where before there was approach rock, and I cross it without a witness because the boundary does not keep one. At first I am only the extra breath. It sounds large in the lanes. A bucket somewhere is set down on the flat and rings once; my breath keeps going after the ring has finished, and that continuance of sound is the first thing that does not belong.

I try the open ground at the middle hour. No one crosses. The houses hold their positions and the cistern holds its dark, and I stand where two carts have never needed to stand, feeling the width of a space that was measured for absence. My shadow falls where a body would make it fall, on the same stones the light has always used, and for a few minutes I mistake this for admission. Then the light moves the width of a finger and my shadow does not match the stain it should have matched. It is a little too restless at the edges. The place does not remark on it. It simply fails to wear me in.

I learn the flagstones by standing on them too long. One holds a skin of water in its dish and gives the water back more slowly than the others. I watch the skin shrink. I am still there when it is gone, which is already a kind of excess. A figure passes with a bundle and does not alter course; the path has a groove and I am not in it. Near the covered way I hear a low correction about the order of lifting a slab, and I understand the words as if they were meant for any hands, so I go to the cistern and try. The near edge first. The dark smells of mineral and of keeping. I set the slab back true, or as true as my hands can know true, and the small sound it makes when it seats is almost the right sound. Almost. A grain of grit I brought in on a sole keeps it from the silence it has earned. Later, when I am not there, someone will lift it again and brush the grit away without comment. The correction will not be addressed to me. It will be the place reminding itself that a visitor’s sole is not a term in the agreement.

For a time I think I might become the lizard’s stone: a narrow place warmed and then left. I choose a flag the day has slightly heated and I hold still. A bird cuts the air above the notch and does not land. I do not land either, not really; stillness is an effort I can feel in the joints, and effort is a new motion. The continuation does not host new motions. When I finally shift, the stone is no warmer than its neighbors. Whatever heat I thought I had lent it has already been received into the general cool and put back into channels I cannot see. I am not refused. I am simply not kept.

Speech does not thicken around me. I lower my voice to the size the notch will hold and I say nothing that requires an answer, because an answer would be a path. Once, under the western drip, I name the fan of stain on the cliff, not as a discovery but as a fact I have stood inside. The name falls and is not picked up. Names are for things that might be mistaken for other things. The stain has not been mistaken for centuries. My mouth, having produced a sound that did not correct anything, feels briefly ornamental, like a brighter cloth no one has wanted. I stop. The drip continues into its channel. That is the only sentence the afternoon needs.

What emerges, slowly, is not a role the lanes have a use for. I am the interval in which a small departure almost occurs and does not. The weight-stone on a roof has crept a finger’s width; I see it and do not reset it, and in the not-resetting there is a hunger to watch what the weather will do next, which is already a rival continuance. By evening someone is on the roof. The stone goes back. The hunger has nowhere to live. I feel it leave me the way the fine rain leaves the paving: a darkening, then only the joints still damp, then those too returning to the color of long use. I do not become hungry again in any way that lasts. The stores smell of what keeping allows, and I do not open them. Outside the boundary, meat and weather and distance continue their own arrangements. I do not visit them. Visiting would require a road that widens, and this notch has decided not to be a place roads do that.

Night is only darker. Most shutters stay shut. I am not behind any of them. I am in the lane that leads to the blank wall and the stair that ends, which is the quietest stretch because it has nowhere to conduct a person. I sit on the dished step until the dish and my weight briefly agree, and then I understand that agreement is not the same as belonging. Belonging would deepen the dish in the contour of my particular sitting, over years, until the step remembered me. I will not be here for the years. Already the stone is forgetting the heat of the hour I gave it. A hinge speaks once when a door moves somewhere I cannot see, dries its small complaint, and is silent. I have no hinge. I have no door. What I have is the memory of having lifted a slab almost correctly, and the grit that was brushed away, and the breath that still sounds larger than the place prefers.

If I am anything that remains when the middle hour returns and no one crosses, I am the fact that the open ground was, for a little while, occupied by a measurement the town did not ask for and did not store. Not a person thinned to a function. Not a function thickened into limbs. A remainder the channels could not quite use: received at the boundary because weather is received, held for the length of a skin of water, and let back into the rock without lengthening any stain. The tree behind the cistern keeps its few leaves. The iron keeps its thin rust. The child, if there is a child, places and replaces the same stones in the groove play has already worn. I do not join the game. A new placement would be a brighter motion, and the day here is the only authority, and the day has no slot for it.

When I leave it will be by the same eastern line, and they will not watch. Behind me the number of presences will return to what it was, never quite zero, never a crowd. I will not have enlarged it. What I carry out is only the short breath the climb gives anyone, and the knowledge of which flagstone holds water longest, learned once and already beginning to simplify, the way a story simplifies when it is not retold. The notch does not need the knowledge back. It has the stone. In the high country beyond, distance resumes, empty and cool, and does not concern itself with what failed to become a habit inside the walls. I failed gently. That is the closest the place can come to keeping a stranger: to let the stranger’s small departures be corrected without violence, and to leave no second tree, no louder game, no path that wanders, and no name.
