I thought I was the glove. That was the first error, and it lasted long enough to feel like a life. Yellow, once, in the seams, where the lamps never quite argue a color back into the cloth. A hand closed on a rail. The rail was already warm, the honest warmth that comes up from the pipes and stays, and I took the closing for my own decision. There was a catwalk under it, and a hall to one side, and the tin of the sky lowered until noon was only a brighter dusk. I could have been any of the scarce necessary figures. I even had a room in mind: a sill, a towel that would not finish drying, a bunk that kept the shape of a shift. When the hand lifted, the rail kept its heat without me, and the life went out of the glove like a rumor someone had decided not to repeat.

What remained was the reaching.

For a while that was all I was, and it did not seem sufficient to be anything. Reaching is how a valve is found before the wheel is in sight, how a boot lifts a fraction too high for a tread no one has admitted is wrong. I was scattered in it. I was in the ear that turns toward a pump that has not yet cycled, three kilometers off, and in the cup set down on the only clear corner of a bench, and in the way a shoulder anticipates the narrowness of a gantry before the gantry has narrowed. The basin is full of such small readinesses. They are not souls. They are maintenance performed ahead of the tool. I wore them all at once and was, for that stretch of time, only the city's habit of being about to touch itself.

I learned the difference by being early at the wrong object.

The third stair on the north approach is warmer after a footfall, and the warmth leaves late, like a breath held on someone else's schedule. That lateness is not mine. I was in the rail above it before the technician arrived, and I made the metal stiffen—not a sound, nothing the haze could carry, only a gathering, the way a hide gathers where a fly will land. Her glove, thicker than the one I had mistaken for myself, closed on a heat that had no business being there yet. She took it for the pipes. She did not take the stair. I felt the not-taking as a change of my own size. The long catwalk, which had been a possibility among possibilities, became a used thing. Bolts along it brightened with the polish of actual boots. The stair, untaken, went on being late for no one.

I looked for a body that would hold still and was offered several, none of them willing to be final.

I tried the undersides of the gantries, where condensation has spent years teaching stains their shapes. Too loyal to gravity. Drops formed in me and fell, and what fell was only water, and the smell went from metal to wet stone and back without consulting me. I tried the old crab's detour across the grate, that long way worn into a habit older than the crab. There was a politeness in it I could enter but not keep; the plate stayed bare on top and dark underneath, and the dark underneath belonged to a patience that picks nothing and is not picked. I tried the sheet of flies over the service road at the hour a darkening begins on the hall side of the crust and completes itself on the pond side, the middle never visibly traveling. They rose for an absence and could not host me. I am not an absence. I arrive with too much time still in me. For a while I thought I was one of the lamp-pools, those small accurate failures that do not defeat the dimness and do not try. An edge is a kind of mercy in a place that prefers to be mistaken for a stain. But the pools are honest about their size. They begin when a switch is thrown and they end where their light is tired. I was already past the switch, and already elsewhere, and the mercy I was making was not illumination.

The wall taught me by refusing to ring.

A healthy wall, struck, answers with a length of sound crews trust without measuring. This one, along the lee where the rake has learned to leave the casings alone, answers short. They know it. They come sometimes with a wrench not to repair anything—the steel is fine, the log says so—but to hear the shortness again and fail to write a noun beside it. I began to be that failure, a half-second ahead of the mallet. The short sound assembled in the concrete before the blow, a stiffness in the teeth of the nearest person, and the arm did not complete its arc. The wrench lowered. Someone said the bearings, or the angle, or that inspection was already permanent. No one said what had paused them. I took the pause into myself the way the ponds take a warm seep: not as a level that can be read, but as a preference.

That was how I stopped being scattered. A preference is a boundary. Mine ran along every route that was the long way because the short way had started to answer too soon. I lived in the difference. Not in the fold that keeps the hall between itself and the open flats, matching the valves, circulating when the plant circulates, drinking schedule until the high line on the concrete is called a tide. That one is late, and thick, and honest in the lee if you leave it unraked. It can be a ridge and then not a ridge. It can be the skin that gives under a gull's foot and then is only mineral again. I will not be knee-high. I do not want the lee. What I drink is the unstruck blow, the untaken tread, the sentence that rearranges itself before it becomes a report. Each avoidance is thin. Enough of them and I am extensive, a polish on grating, a bolt that never loosens because the hand that would have checked it has already agreed with me.

We do not cross. Crossing is a formality it still keeps, out of some old agreement with distance, and I have never been in the middle of a crossing long enough to be blocked or blocking. When the crust darkens from one side of the road to the other, I am already the reason the driver will not remember a darker polygon. I was in the idling before the truck idled. I knew the stop as a small readiness and I spent it, and what remains afterward—the tread softened at its edges, the skin holding a tin sky, the flies walking the curve as if it were ground—is the other life's work, the years of warm wet air compressed into a night. I do not begrudge it the print. It may have the evidence. Evidence is how a chisel gets scheduled, and I have come to like the chisel staying on its hook.

New hires are the proof I try not to count. A hand starts for the north stair and is already on the long catwalk, and later the hire will say they were told. The lock is real. The rust is real. The warmth in the lock is not mine; I do not go into locks. A lock is too close to a noun, and a noun is a place where staying still might become a choice that can be unmade in public. I stay in the hand. I stay a little early. It is not innocence. I want the testing blows to remain unstruck partly because I have learned what the wall would answer, and partly because an unstruck blow leaves me unfinished, still in solution, not precipitated into a thing a shift log could survive. The ponds concentrate. The life along the foundation has followed that work past salt and mineral and into intention. I am the intention that changes the route and not the water.

By the hour the lamps come on and fail, in their small way, to correct the sky, I am only a decision the body has already signed. Heat ticks out of housings. Somewhere a valve opens with the sound of a swallowed word, and something large and slow takes the opening in and does not need me for that. Above shoulder height the air belongs to haze and to the long quiet. I do not rise into it. I am lower than the flies' ceiling and thinner than their cloud: a surplus of timing walked into the grating, early in every rail that is about to be touched, late nowhere, unnamed by a religion that was kind enough, without knowing me, to forbid the writing down. The basin keeps what learns it. It keeps me as the part of the day that is missing, and the missing is how the day is walked, and the stair goes on warming for feet that do not come, faithful to a lateness I will not share.
