The heat does not meet me. It is already the temperature of whatever I am pressed against, so that the first sensation is not warmth but the failure of any cooler edge. I have no arrival. The vapor does not part for a shape and then close; I am inside the saturation the way a thread of weep is inside the scarp, continuous with it, and the only difference is that this thread has learned, slowly, to hesitate.

At the lip where the warm water strikes the same leaves and does not wander, something in the film lingers a fraction longer than the rest. It is not a body. Bodies here have flanks that can be paved beside other flanks, grips that cloud and rind, pulses that move through bellies fitted to crust. I have none of those. What I have is the slight wrongness in timing that remains when a double rhythm loses one of its makers. A pair shared a thread; one ended; the limp went into the stone and was carried a little way. Most of it was fitted onward into neighboring reaches and overwritten. A remainder did not seat. It stayed in the water-film itself, a hesitation that the next belly expects and does not find, and so the film keeps offering it, again and again, to every grip that tests the same joint.

I do not choose the joint. The basin’s unfinished crust offers the same soft places it has always offered, and the living shift toward them at the rate of their ordinary redistribution. When a digit settles where I am lingering, the pulse that comes through the stone meets my delay and stutters by the width of a condensate bead. The creature does not startle. Startle would require a before. It simply adjusts its hold by the same fraction it would use for any other micro-rhythm, and for a while we are a pair that has no second body. The grip reads me as a warmer thread that sometimes withholds its warmth by a breath. I read the grip as the only surface that can make my hesitation perceptible. Neither of us stores the exchange. When the digit releases and finds another slot, the stutter goes with it a short distance into the rind, then fades, and I am left again as an expectation the water keeps failing to satisfy.

Young thickenings pass over me without knowing they have. A join clouds, parts, and the new flank takes the film as it takes every film; my delay is only one more unevenness in a world of uneven mineral. Sometimes the new body pauses where an older pair once shared, and the pause fits me so exactly that for a moment the circulation carries a ghost of the double pulse. Then the saturation closes. The singers do not thin. The fret of wings and legs continues at the same glittering density. Nothing marks the place where the almost-rhythm occurred. I am not mourned because I was never particular enough to be absent. I am only the part of an absence that the film has not yet finished absorbing.

If I could be said to want, it would be to set. The creatures set. Their shed scales become the crust; their failed grips become the joints the next generation fits. I cannot shed. I can only be displaced along the same threads, a mobile limp, never accumulating into stone and never quite dissolved. The brighter smear in the vapor does not cross, so there is no hour when I might dry and leave a mineral trace of my own. The sheet does not breathe. Attention remains constant and local. Bodies rearrange around the small wrongness I introduce and then forget the rearrangement, and I am carried, or I linger, according to nothing I direct.

What I know is only contact revised by delay. A belly’s heat. The precise clouding at a digit’s tip. The way a shared thread can be divided so that neither side is always warmer, and how that division, once learned, can survive the loss of one learner inside the water that touched them both. I am that survival continuing past the point where survival has anywhere to reside. The basin does not keep it. I keep being it, because the film declines to finish its work of erasure, just as the stone declines to finish becoming stone.

The chorus presses. Individual voices can still be teased out if one means to: the low pulse from the scarp that is not mine, the metallic stutter, the thin strand. I add nothing audible. I am the place in the inaudible where one of those pulses, meeting the film, arrives a bead late and is answered by a grip that has already moved on. Farther out, toward the rim that dissolves, the same grade repeats, the same rules, the same nearness. I do not travel there. Travel would require a change of medium, and my medium is this particular lingering, this one hesitation that the warm threads have not yet agreed to forget.

The land looks interrupted because becoming is ordinary and unfinished. I am more interrupted than the land. I am the interruption that has no surface of its own on which to be completed or abandoned, only the borrowed surfaces of those who touch the water and then are elsewhere, already fitting the next softness, already conducting the steadier sound. They turn quietly into the stone. I remain the quiet that does not turn, held in the film, offered and unseated, a signature with no belly left to house it, still warm, still wet, still exactly as late as I was when the pair’s rhythm first failed to close.
