I would already be there. The stairs are for people who still believe a place can be entered, and the table does not keep that kind of person long. The ones who come up thinner, with open distance stuck in their mouths like a seed, are fed and listened to and then set to something small until the fever goes back into the stone. I am not one of those. If I ever walked the western crease, it was before my knees learned the cost, and the town has not needed to remember it.

At first I thought I would be a mound. Everything finished ends up in the lee, and I am good at staying. Ash, husk, grit the haze donates, a body that has stopped twisting cord — they take the slight tilt and hold it, and children run the lower slopes without lowering their voices. That seemed close to the right work: be still, be added to, do not wash away because nothing here washes. But the mounds are not listening. They receive. A hand could set a cracked pin on them and they would not know to be ashamed. I would know. So that is not it.

Nor the press, though I have stood at one often enough that the screw’s talk lives in my wrist. The press is shared, and I am worse at being shared than the town prefers. I can turn it. I can feel when the cup is lying about how much sweet fraction a leaf still holds. I cannot be the thing everyone owns. Ownership, here, is a kind of thirst — it wants to be returned to — and I do not want to be returned to. I want to be consulted and then left.

The pits have their watcher already. The fiber has its salty households, knees going early, first pour as trade, nobody envying them and everybody needing the cord. The goat-beasts have the stick on stone. The swifts have the northern quarter the way weather has a sky. What is left is the thing the town treats as climate and therefore does not appoint, which is how I know it is mine.

I live in the last roof before the northern lip. Not the last house — that one is flush with the cliff, its outer wall the parapet’s cousin, a family in it who have bared the nape for generations and do not need me among their sleepers. My place is the roof-path just inward of them, a stretch of coping polished by feet that were going somewhere else. I sleep there when the stone still has the day in it, between a party wall and the nest a hinge-bird has lashed to a peg someone forgot to carry down. The bird is not mine. I am tolerated the way the bird is tolerated: I eat what would otherwise trouble the store. Not insects. Difference.

The town is poor at imagining another town, and that is its health. It is also, quietly, a danger, because the only fast thing the table knows is a change in the sound, and a people who cannot picture difference cannot be trusted to notice the small kind. They will hear a quarrel at three turns. They will not hear the thatch drop by a thread, or the swifts shift their surf a handspan off the face, or the insects hesitate and then pretend they did not. Those are not events. They are absences of thickness. Someone has to have yesterday in the ear, and not make a story of it.

So that is the being. Short, as they are short, finished by the same wind. Ochre where the oil is worked in, grey at the temples because I am out in the haze more than the lane-people, palms not black from the press but polished from the shin-high stone, which takes the bitter fraction off every hand that checks it and gives back nothing but smoothness. Eyes large and resin-colored and almost useless for the drop; I do not look down for long. The drop is a fact. The sound coming up it is the work. My hair is shaved on the windward side in the northern way, not because I was born to the quarter but because the quarter has had to look at my nape for years and would rather it mean what theirs means. Fitting is a courtesy I can still perform.

They call me the Same. It began as a joke, which is how true names start when the birth-name is kept for the dark. At the slow hour someone asks, and I say same, or thicker, or the lee has a new shoulder, or the screw in the third courtyard is talking higher than it did when the heat was in the stone yesterday. I do not say less. If it is less, I say the quarter should walk the lip before the dimmest hour, and I say it in the clicking lane-voice, short, no future in it. Less is the only word I am afraid of. It is a direction. Directions are how the fever gets back into a mouth.

Indoors I am almost nobody. There is an elder in a slit-room two lanes off the center depression who still has my birth-name, the one used low enough that the thatch covers it. She twists cord with hands that have begun to forget, and when I bring her the peelings that are my excuse to sit, she says the name once, to see if I will flinch at being a person rather than a report. I do not flinch. I also do not answer to it in any way that would let it out into the slot of the lane. She is the last one who knew my hands when they still spent shouts they could not afford. Everyone else knows the function. That is the correct intimacy. A town this dense would be cruel if it also knew you.

My day is a circuit inside the circuit. Not the whole table — I do not need the western crease, and the stair-households smell sharper than my count can use. I need the northern lip, the thatch above my coping, the center where the rosettes mat the finest dust, and the one press whose screw I have decided is the town’s clock, though I have never said so. I walk at the roll they walk, outer foot, unhurried, because hurry would change my breathing and my breathing is part of the instrument. I stop where a sound is doing what it did. I stop longer where it is not. A child on the roof-path learns to step over me as over any sleeper. A goat-beast sleeps against my shin and is corrected by someone else’s stick if it rises to strip a rosette; I do not keep animals. I keep the ordinary.

What I eat is what the quarter eats. Leaf-meal, dense and a little sweet, the bitter fraction on my skin and not in the bowl. I do not rate the first pour. I have tried to refuse extra meal when a young one comes back from the stairs and the town, out of habit, widens the feeding to anyone who looks thinner. I am not thinner. I am only a little less oiled on the days when the wind, which is almost never strong, still finds the shaved side of my head and takes what the presses gave me. The Pit-Watcher scolds me for that with a bargaining voice. I go and rub oil into the scalp in her sight, so she does not have to make it a count.

At the dimmest hour I do what each quarter does, and I do it as the person who will have to say same tomorrow. Hand on the parapet. Not a prayer. A check, the way you lay a palm on a beast to know if the heat in it is still the heat you left. The swifts pour up the face. The surf does not wet the stone. I am not counting them; there are too many, and a number would be a boast the moisture cannot pay. I am listening for the shape of the pour. Whether it breaks against the undercut where it broke yesterday. Whether a gap opens and closes, a pocket of quiet the size of a room, which happens sometimes when the colony turns and means nothing, and which I must not carry back into the lanes as news. News is a leak. The town is organized around never letting moisture become a surface. I am organized around never letting a difference become a story before it has earned a name.

If you asked them what I am, they would say a man of the northern quarter, or a woman — the lanes do not waste much breath on the distinction once the work is clear — who sleeps too near the edge and speaks at the slow hour and has never thrown anything off. They would be right, and they would be early. The thing itself is the comparison I do not voice. A held yesterday. The smallest cup the table owns, stained not black but clear, in which the day is poured out beside the day before it, and looked at, and returned to the pits if it cannot feed anyone.

When I die it will not be a descent. The mounds can have me, and the rosette if the season allows, and the elder will say the birth-name once into the insect-scream, where it will not travel three turns. Someone else will already have been listening. I will have been teaching them without a lesson, the way the screw teaches the wrist: by being the same until I am not, and letting the not be small enough to walk back from. The town does not need a watcher who becomes landform in a special place. The lip is not a shrine. It is a wall, and the wall is still itself if a hand can leave it and find the roof-path by the heat in the stone alone.

That is where I am, then. Not the summit, which is only a floor, and not the people in their completeness, who would be diminished by a stranger’s wish to join them. I am the narrow exception their completeness requires: the one who remembers the sound well enough to hear a hole in it, and who is proud, in the town’s way of pride, of having nothing to report. Same, I say, and the press-screw answers itself two lanes over, and the child comes back along the coping, and the haze keeps the far roofs a little less certain than the near ones. I lie down in the warmth the stone has not yet given up. The hinge-bird shifts. The insects receive the night without emphasis. Below the lip the wings go on, a surf with no water under it, and I let them, because they are not less, and tomorrow I will be believed only if tonight I do not love the listening more than the sameness.
