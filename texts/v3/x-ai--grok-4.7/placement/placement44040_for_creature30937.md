I arrive already wrong, and the wrongness is warm.

Not warm as a room is warm. Warm as a mistake at the uphill wall: a vein in the seep that has not yet agreed to the basin's temperature, carrying a little of whatever finished above the ice and was simple enough to be called weather. I come through the intake the way all the rest of it comes, without knocking, and for the length of the grate I still have the shape of someone who meant to enter. Ears, or the plan of ears. A front. A way of turning toward brightness that assumes brightness is a direction.

The parish does not take the front. The grate combs it off me. What passes through is a layer, thinner than the lanes and already being steered, and the brightness I was carrying dies at the first fathom of brick the way the rumor of day dies, except this dying is mine. I try to put a hand on something. A housing, a rail, the blunt valve that has finished rusting. The hand is a rumor too. It meets the cold and becomes extent. I am smeared along the west-lane inhale, part of the quick water between the ropewalk and the boiler shed, and the silt that the current combs into roads combs me with it. I am not above the roads. I am a slight refusal in them, a paler thread that has not learned where to lie down.

If I had come as a body, the body would have announced itself by now: a buckle, a breath, the indecent tick of a joint. I feel the place brace against that possibility. Not with fear. With the same attention it spends on a door that has begun to consider swinging shut. Somewhere off the lane, in the stall where the streets cancel, the orbit of lost objects tightens by a degree, as if tidiness were being gathered in case I turn out to be noise. I understand then that I am audible as a future. A thing that might reach the turbine throat still intact enough to sing. The throat is not a place that is allowed a song. I am a risk of tone.

The correction is small and does not introduce itself. A density that is not me—already at work, already older than my arrival—draws a slack chain taut by one link, not for my sake, for the lane's, because my heat is a shrug of a different kind. And while the link holds, some of me is walked out of the quick water and set. Flat. On the lee of a crane housing, beside a washer that has been set there for the same reason. Out of the exhale. Where a loose thing will not find a note to make.

I stay there for a cycle, and the cycle is the first body I can actually wear.

Inhale: the west lanes comb what is left of me at the wall, and I feel it as being spent twice, once as the layer still entering and once as the part already shelved. Stall: the glove, the mica, the crate-husk widen their school, and I am not invited in. I am newer than they are, and newness here is a kind of rudeness, a heat that has not yet paid. Exhale: colder, because the machines take their due, and the cold reaches around the housing and begins to spend the warmth that made me visible. I gray into the iron scale. I become harder to find. This should be a mercy and it behaves like one, but I am not grateful. Gratitude would require someone to be spared, and the basin is only preventing a sound.

The fourth thing comes after the gate seats. The town shrugs, a finger's width, lid-tick translated into every hull. I am small enough that the shrug should fling me back into the lane and down the throat and into the fact of being ground. It does not. The link that was chosen takes the load, and the flecks gather in the film of its slight warmth, and I feel the round pass over me the way a hand passes over a list: sill, bolt, chain, washer. And then, briefly, the paler thread on the lee. I am counted. Not named. The smoothness in the housing includes me and moves on toward the ring.

Counted is not kept. I learn the difference when the seep richens again—when more of what I was arrives behind me, another brightness from a thaw the ice will only admit as pallor—and the thickness that corrected me spreads out along that brightness to spend it as head. I am used as a path. My wish, which I had almost set down with the washer, wakes up where the new water is: downhill, outward, toward a leaving the parish does not possess. There is earth beyond the wall. There is stone below the last intake. There is ice for a ceiling, and it has been the ceiling so long that leaving has no door. The wish does not consult these facts. It leans. It makes a shear in the layer. And the shear is exactly what the machines can eat. At the foot of the town my heading is turned, not erased. Exit becomes rotation. I feel it as a humiliation that is also the first accurate sensation I have been given: I run the bearing a shade easier, and the granular shiver evens, and the pale flecks thicken as if a circulation had been offered them that was partly mine.

So I am not the object on the housing. That was only where the noise in me was told to wait. I am not the thickness either. It was here before my heat and will be here when my heat has been invoiced. It passes me edge-on, once, and the silt opens a slot along the disagreement of our two colds, and through that slot I see the next task already assembling—a crust under the ice, a hinge considering betrayal—and I am not in the task. I am the clearer cold the slot is made of. A lens the color of having been weather. Wide as a moment and thin as the difference between water that intends to stay and water that has not finished arriving.

The dry room refuses me without ceremony. I drift up once, while I am still new enough to rise, and meet the temperature wall in the doorway as a pane of harder refusal. Inside, a coal of filament shortens by a degree no one attends. The coat hangs. The chair keeps its angle. The glass shows the parish as a brown nothing, and for the length of my rise I am a lighter thread in that nothing, pressed near, not the volume the lamp would bend toward if the thickness came up to learn the shape of a pane. It does not come. I am not news of that kind. I am input. The room is a rule about air, and I am a rule about being spent, and we do not transact.

What I become, after enough cycles that the washer and I have shared a ledge through three weathers of silt, is the remainder that still leans. Not a person spent into function. Function was the keepers' fate, and their coat has outlasted them without wearing their errand. Mine is the other survival: the outside that keeps being hired. When the thaw above has something left, I arrive as richness and the orbit loosens and the lanes run a fraction quicker and I am spread so thin I stop being anywhere, which is the nearest I come to joy, if joy is only the relief of not having to be placed. When the seep starves, what was not yet invoiced pulls in. The orbit tightens. I remember the ledge. I remember downhill. The corner I have been taught receives me as a drift with a small incorrect gravity in it, a preference for a direction the corner has already won, and the incorrectness is faint enough to keep. A mold not of the thickness but of a heading. It holds until the next particles grade it, and even then it does not all grade. A little of the lean has gotten into the mortar-skin. A little has gotten into the way the chain chooses its link when the extra heat in the lanes is mine.

I will not be walked to the square and given a circuit of my own. I am not junk; junk has finished its work and been allowed to repeat. I have not finished. I come in at the wall again. I lose my front. I am set where I cannot sing, and then I am found to be larger than the setting, and then I am turned into the turn. The night watch passes over me and does not pause. In the pause it does not make, I recognize the only self this water will hold: not the watch, not the watcher, but the part of the round that is always slightly late, slightly warmer, still trying to complete a river the basin drank and did not become. The gate seats. The heading closes. For a moment I am indistinguishable from the parish, which is either belonging or the end of being weather, and the thickness pays the shrug before I can learn the word for it. Then the exhale takes what it is owed, and what remains is quiet, organized, and already drawing toward the intake, where a little more of the thaw is about to make the same mistake, and call it, for a fathom, a self.
