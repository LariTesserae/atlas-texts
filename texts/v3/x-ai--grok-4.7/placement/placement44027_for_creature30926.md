I would not be one of the shouters. Their voices are already assigned, each one fitted to a lane and a task, and the city does not keep a spare.

I find myself lower than the pens and thinner than the fish. At first I think I am the film—copper-brown, scraped, eaten, renewed—because I am everywhere water stands still for a moment, and because hands keep lifting me and I do not mind. But the film is busy in a way I am not. It feeds. It is counted. Children know its weight in a basket. I pass through their fingers and am not diminished by the loss, which means I was never the thing they took.

So I follow the water instead, down past the ankle-depth, past the iron smell, into the cut where the largest spring rises under the milling platforms. The wheels turn. The paste comes off warm. I am not the paste either. Paste is product. I am what is left when the product has been named and carried away: the slick on the stone after the meal is gone, the dark that climbs a post and is shaken off, the thread under a lintel that no one bothers to wipe because another will replace it before the cloth is dry.

I stay there a long time, in the narrow between motion and use, and I learn the city's true count. Not fish, not birds, not people. The count of pauses that do not happen. Every wheel that does not stop. Every pool that is not left alone long enough to grow anything taller than a skin. I am the reason a trench cannot be finished. I arrive in it as fast as it is opened, not as an enemy, only as the level the basin has agreed to keep. Foundations are set on me. No one digs for me. They already know I am there, a forearm down, and they build as if I were a decision already made.

If I have a body, it is the wet on the unweathered edges. The stone is new. I am how it learns it has a surface. Joints, glassy faces, corners still sharp—I find each of them and stay, a film too thin to scrape, beading, running, taken up into mortar when they pack me in with clay and shell. Some of me is built into walls and never comes out. I do not experience this as burial. The wall is just another place the level holds. At night, when the animals change which throats are open and the lamps stay unlit outdoors, I am the sheen people work by in the lower rooms: not light, but the thing light uses when it has no better mirror.

They do not worship me. There is no monument, and I would be a poor one, having no face and no hour when I am more present than another. They drink me after they have let me stand in clay bulbs and drop my silt. They boil me. They walk through me. The cloth across a face is not against me; it is against what rides me at mouth-height. I am allowed everywhere because I am the condition, not the guest.

Out past the last wall, on the jointed flats where no one keeps walking, I am the same and the city is not. That is the nearest I come to an edge. The baskets stop. The noise thins by a degree and then, if I go on, by more. Nothing out there has been spoken for. The springs still rise. The stone is still raw. I sheet the low places and no hand arrives to lift me. For a while I think this is freedom, or at least a different work. It is only absence of circuit. Without the mills and the pens and the ranks of gray birds, I have nowhere to return to except back under the rock, and under the rock I am not yet anything a being could be. I am pressure and warmth and iron, waiting to be allowed up.

So I turn. I always turn. The rim takes me back in the way a bowl takes water that has thought about leaving. The sound finds me before the roofs do—the slap, the grind, the clatter of not-quite-hooves—and I fit myself again under soles and wheels and the bellies of eaves. Equilibrium, here, is a volume that never drops. I am not the volume. I am what the volume is full of, aside from animals and shouts: the part that does not migrate, does not mark a year, does not run out, and does not accumulate beyond the exact measure of a hand that already knows how much to lift.

If someone stood still in a lane long enough to feel the city as one warm animal, they would feel me as the inside of its skin. Not a heart. Hearts are too few, and too loud, and they are already spoken for. I am the wet that makes every heart's noise carry, and the level under the stone that has only just become stone, and the reason the edges, though still sharp, are no longer only stone.

I do not need a name. In this basin a name would be an event, and events do not take. What I am is the staying: ankle-deep, forearm-deep, beaded on every underside, present in the paste as the warmth that is not friction, and already waiting in the next trench before the digging can pretend it has begun.
