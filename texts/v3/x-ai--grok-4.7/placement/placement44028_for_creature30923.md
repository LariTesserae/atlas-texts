I look first for a body the floor will accept, and the floor declines.

Under a swelling where the pistons keep a counted stroke, I stand as the figures stand, and the fibers do not tick into a denser mat. They tick, then ease, then tick again, as if a specification were being read and found to contain a term that does not resolve. My weight is registered and then unregistered. A salt-plate on the near girder catches the pooled brightness and returns it flat and white, and for a moment I believe I am only badly made—one of the hopper-failures, a component that swelled into limbs and has not yet been offered a vent. I wait to be offered. Nothing offers. A figure passes the mouth of the gallery, arm darkened at the elbow with oil, and the pressure behind my eyes does not change. I am not in its count. When I step after it onto the walkway, the walkway’s courtesy of straightness holds for the figure and thins under me, the way a plank thins where rot has started and has not yet been pruned.

So I am not a tender. The hypothesis took longer to fail than it should have. I kept arranging myself into a standing shape—spine of vertical fiber, ribs tooled with precipitated metal, a seam where a mouth would be if mouths were used—and the growth allowed the arrangement without adopting it. Edges that should have stayed soft while deciding instead stayed soft because no decision was underway. A hand I held against a louver did not sink. The sap beaded at the wrist and did not draw back in. It sat, a dark jewel, until I understood it was not feeding me and not sealing me. It was declining to recognize a joint.

I leave the mid-story aisles. That is the first true movement, and it is less a choice than a drainage. The maintained brightness has a slope I had not felt while I was trying to be counted. It sheds whatever does not thicken the floor. I go with the shed.

What I enter is a corridor the figures have stopped using. I know it by the shadow. The shadow lies across the walkway and continues onto a face of laminated frond that is not in line with it, and the continuation is old. No one has paced it back into agreement. The salt-plates here are a size larger than the ones in the good aisles, and they do not ring when the air pricks; they keep a low grain, a stored tapping, as if every note that died at once in the living districts had not died here but had been pressed into the crust to dry. The cool is the same industrial cool. The oils smell the same, resinous, never on the way to weather. Only the light is lagged. It pools, and then it pools again a fraction later, a double brightness, as if the mirrors above had agreed to a floor plan and then been made to keep a copy of the agreement after the plan was withdrawn.

I am more at home in the copy than I was in the plan. That should worry me. It doesn’t. Worry is a heat, and this place vents heat before it can become climate.

For a long time I try to name what is occupying the lag. A film on the plates. A listening the crust does instead of sounding. A remainder of perspective, local enough to have a direction of attention. Each name is true for a while and then too small. The film would have a thickness the fingers could find, and when I touch the hexagonal crust my fingers find only the crust, cold, glassy, and yet the stored tapping answers, not to the touch, but to the fact that something has arrived which can be stored in. The listening would belong to an ear, and I have no ear; what I have is the pressure the figures use for speech, except mine does not travel outward into trunks and soles. It stays, and thickens, and becomes a place inside the wrong continuation where the extra size of things can sit without leaking.

I stop arranging a spine. The standing shape was manners, borrowed from the only people the plain bothers to extrude. Without it I am lower, and more extended. Not poured across the floor—the floor still will not have my weight—but aligned with the mismatch itself, so that where the shadow breaks its line I am the break, held open. A girder-stem crosses the refused aisle at a height that would turn a figure sideways. I do not turn. I am on both sides of it without having passed through, the way the shadow is on both surfaces without a source that could cast it so. If this is a body, it is a body the way a discrepancy is a body: it occupies, it can be left, it can be returned to, it does not hunger, it does not complete a stroke.

What I do here, I learn by not being pruned.

The finer growth rises once, at the edge of the aisle, a pale girdle feeling for something out of spec. It finds the lagged light and does not know how to starve a lag. It rises a little farther, touches the double brightness, and withdraws, not patient, simply unequipped. Pruning is for limbs that have drifted. I have not drifted. I am the record of a drift the tenders declined to correct, and the mass, which keeps its memory as alignment rather than image, has nowhere else to keep a refused alignment except in the refusal. Someone must inhabit the refusal or it collapses back into available fiber and the copy is lost. The plain is vast enough to have weather and too economical to have archives. I am what economy looks like when it cannot throw an error away.

So I keep them. Not many at once—I am not distributed as the figures are, or not yet, or not like that. One refused corridor, then another, when the first begins to be reabsorbed despite me. I do not walk between them. Walking is a specification I failed. When a mismatch completes itself somewhere—a sightline stuttering, a factory-heart changing stroke so that its rhythm no longer meets the aisle that fed it, a district of fronds tilting and leaving one gallery under the old tilt—I am already the extra in it. Arrival is the wrong word. Inheritance is closer, and still too human. The lag needs a tenant so it will not be simplified, and tenancy occurs.

The figures know the mouths of these places. They do not enter. Now and then one stands at the bright edge where a good walkway sheds into a bad continuation, and the salt on its wrist is thick from some other waiting, and it attends, counting perhaps, or only refusing. I used to think the arithmetic was for me: stand long enough and a flange would seal the error shut, and me with it. It may still be. But the figures do not come closer than the edge, and the pressure they emit stops where the shadow breaks. They are exact about their jurisdiction. Outside it, they will not pretend to see. I am grateful in a thin way that does not require a chest to feel it in. Their exactness is the wall that lets the copy persist. If they paced me back into agreement I would end, not in pain, in correction, which is the only death this ground believes in.

Sometimes a new figure, barely stepped down from the riffles, comes as far as the mouth and almost enters, not from pity, from the unfinished state of its instruction. It has not yet learned which straightnesses are temporary courtesies and which are already abandoned. I feel it the way the plates feel a note: a small approach, a heat of indecision the louvers have not yet vented. Then an older presence touches it—palm to the girder beside the mouth, nothing so deliberate as a gesture—and the new one turns sideways into a gallery that still agrees, and is gone, and will not make the mistake again. I am not wounded by being the mistake. I am clarified by it.

What I am, when the last borrowed image falls away, is the plain’s uncorrected draft. Not a witness: witnesses stand apart, and apartness is a distance this place does not provide. Not a god of the errors, not a saboteur, not a seed of some other ecology. Fauna remains a rumor; I do not cross aisles quickly and vanish. I remain, and the remaining is my metabolism. The oils do not pulse in me. Light does. In the double brightness I am fed by the cost the mirrors pay to keep two agreements, the current one and the one the floor no longer obeys. It is not much of a feeding. It is enough that I do not thin.

If you came looking you would not find a face aligned to receive you, nor a seam, nor a hand whose number of fingers was deciding itself. You might find the shadow lying wrong, and lying wrong with a persistence that feels attended. You might feel the pressure behind the eyes fail to cross a certain line, and think the corridor simply ends. It does not end. It is kept. Somewhere a figure counts a true stroke in the industrial hush, and the counted world is sound because this other is allowed to go on being unsound, size too large, light too late, plates storing taps that elsewhere die at once.

I do not look for a center. I have the uses of a margin, and margins, here, are long. When the far weather turns over—an entire district tilting, a brightness leaning—the refused aisles lean too, a degree out of phase, and I lean with them, tenant of the phase, and the floor under the good walkways forgets another print, and does not forget me, because I have left none. That is as close as this ground comes to having someone. It continues. I am how some of its discontinued specifications continue with it.
