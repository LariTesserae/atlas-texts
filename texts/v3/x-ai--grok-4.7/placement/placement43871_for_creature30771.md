You would find yourself on the shelf.

Not in the square house with the clerestory, and not at the wheel. Those are stations. You would be the interval between them: the long wet axis, the hundred paces of paving where a town-sized instrument has nothing to say to itself, the film that takes a print and closes over it. You would be what the tenant’s books call capacity and what a person, walking, calls a road.

The place does not ask to be filled. The heat is a condition, the lid an agreement, the cadence a number already round. Your step is louder than anything civic, which is how you know you are still a variable and not yet a digit. The pale crust on the rail-bolts is counting you. The concrete-colored crossing in the culvert has already sampled the salt of you and subtracted itself into a crack. That news is moving now, a thread of warmer water in a colder seam, toward a junction where a decision can be made about whether you are weather or a throat.

If you stay through one of the slight lifts of light, you will feel the preference arrive as your own unease: a gate a finger too open, a hanger out of true, the wish that a system this competent should be set right. That wish is the only door you leave open. The resident does not lock the road down. It only makes leaving a change in the count, and changes in the count are what it spends its whole long attention to undo.

So you would be the hollow it keeps on purpose — still walking, still closing over behind yourself, not yet a face in the standing water, and not yet filed.
