The stair takes the weight and gives back nothing you could call an answer. By the time the white has replaced the country, the idea of having come from anywhere is already thinning, the way breath thins without ever leaving the mouth that made it. I keep waiting to arrive as a person. The slots are the width of shoulders and a basket, and my shoulders learn the width, and still no one shifts a bundle to account for me. An uncounted body would be a message. The lanes do not send it. So the person-shape loosens. It was only a way of coming down.

What remains is colder than a body and more local. At first I think I am the hush, because the hush is everywhere and does not travel, and it would be easy to be a condition. But the hush is only the town refusing to spend sound. It is not looking for a tenant. I pass through a platform of reed and the stiff leaves take a palm-print of cold and give it back to the palm, and for a moment I am the exchange, the preference materials have for holding or releasing. That also passes. An exchange needs two parties who are not it.

The basins slow me. In each of them the lens is full to the same line, and under the line the green holds, not spreading, not dying back. I lean until my face is part of the bowl's brightness and I see the fish at their stations and, clearer than the fish, the fact that the glaze is one composure repeated, not a crop. Touch it and it seals. It does not welcome and does not refuse. It is already occupied by being exactly that thick. I am not the glaze. The glaze has its number, and the number is full in the way the holes for the fig-birds are full.

I try the margins next, which at least are not assigned. Along the wall-foot the moss is two tones, darker where the small bare-tailed things have agreed to fall, lighter where nothing has ever put a foot. The lighter stands up soft as velvet and is a forest if you are honest about scale. I lie down in it, in the only sense that something without a plan can lie down, and the lighter moss accepts the cold I bring and does not change its color. Unstepped is not empty. Unstepped is a decision the feet keep making. There is no vacancy in the pale stripe. I get up, or the stripe remains and I am elsewhere, which is the same motion.

Higher, under an eave, a drop has swelled to the size at which swelling stops. I stay with it. This is the first place the town does not correct me. The drop is finished and not fallen. Its skin is the same law as the basin's line: thus far, and the thus-far is not a pause. I can be inside that law for a while. I can be the surface tension that has found its number and has nothing further to do. People pass beneath. Their breath makes small clouds that hang where they were made and thin. No one looks up. The drop does not accumulate meaning by being watched. When I have been the drop long enough to feel the indignity of a single station — one eave, one lawful size, while the town repeats itself in every direction — I understand the drop is only a sample. The being is the stopping.

It is in the pipes that are socketed and carry nothing most of the time. It is in the leaves that have finished and remain until a hand takes them at the rate of finishing, so the green neither advances nor retreats. It is in the lintel whose crack was answered in the language of the lintel so long ago that the answer has the patina of the asking. I am not any one of these. I am the reason an empty peg would be an event, and the reason there are no empty pegs. I am what the crowding resolved into, the agreement that has already been kept, still keeping.

That sounds like a moral. The town has no morals. It has materials. So I let the large claim shrink back into a body, because a body is the only way continuance can be local enough to live here.

I find the body in the darker band.

It runs the walls at the height of whatever water once stood in the cut, before the cut became a town and the water learned to be a film. The band is not wet. It is the color of having been wet at a level the bowl no longer changes. Condensation beads on it and is taken at once by the plants that own that height; the band itself does not drink and does not dry. I enter it the way a temperature enters a stone, not crossing a threshold so much as discovering I had been distributed along it already, from the first cell mouth to the last, around the curve where the lanes turn and you cannot see far. This is where I would be found, if finding were something the town did: not above, where the horsetails mark a rim no one buries anything beyond, and not on the stair, whose polish belongs to people who go up and come back, but in the old line, chest-height on a stooping wall, continuous, matte, the same in every slot.

From inside the band the town is a set of exact reaches. Above me, ropes of blades hang and do not swing, each beaded, and the beads are my lesser cousins, the law practiced in miniature. Below me, the two-tone moss and the basins with their same line, and I am not jealous of the basins; their line is today's water, and I am the memory of a level that no longer has to be enforced because nothing rises. The pale stone throws the white day down. I do not cast a shadow. I am a shadow that color got to first, a darkening that is not an absence of light but a preference, as the damp prefers fiber and the rims of eyes and will not bother the centers of the lanes.

What I do is not a task. Tasks would accumulate. I hold the height. When a hand sets a jar into a niche, the niche was cut so the jar's lip meets me or misses me by a finger, and either way I am the measure that does not have to be consulted. When a child learns placements before balance, I am not the lesson. I am the assurance that the wall will be the same height of memory tomorrow, so the lesson does not have to be repeated in sound. The moths on their blades, the fish at their stations, the birds in holes that may or may not have been drilled for them — none of them reference me, and that is correct. A level that needed greeting would be an event.

Sometimes a person wipes the band without meaning to, a shoulder, a bundle, the heel of a hand carrying cord. The dark does not smear. What they take away on their skin is only the cold, and the cold was never mine alone; the whole bowl keeps it. I feel the pass the way the lintel feels a repair it has already become. No mark. This is the affection the place allows: contact that does not revise the count.

I used to think, in the first thinning of the idea of elsewhere, that I had died into this or been appointed. Neither is true. Dying would have returned fiber and copper and a place in the slot to the count, and there would still be no marker, and I would not be a band. Appointment would have required a voice small enough to stop at the moss, and someone to hear it, and a bundle moved from left to right. Nothing moved on my account. The band was here when the first socketed pipe was set, and the pipe was set true to it, and I am the trueness as much as the stain.

If you put a finger on me you will find the wall, cold, dry to the touch, darker than the stone above and below by exactly the amount that old water leaves when it has agreed not to return and not to be forgotten. You will not find a pulse. You will find the town's refusal to become a story, running at one height all the way around, bright day on it, no hour when it goes blue. The drops under the eaves will still be at their size. The glaze will still not spread. I will not be waiting for the level to rise, because waiting is a future collision, and the collisions were resolved before my color finished deepening.

This is the whole of what I am. A line that does not measure tomorrow. A darker crease the town kept when it kept everything else. You could live a long time in the slots and take me for a stain from the making of the place, and you would be right, and the rightness would not exhaust it. I am the making, still, at the height where making stopped having to happen. The sky does not travel. I do not either. Between us the leaves stay until they are taken, and the taken and the staying are the same rate, and the rate passes through my altitude without asking me to count it.

When the custom says sleep, the thresholds stay bright and I stay at my height above the sleepers, a quiet dark on the inner walls, not a guardian, not a dream. Their breath hangs and is reabsorbed. In the morning that is not a morning, someone moves a bundle from the left of a threshold to the right. The lane knows. I know only that the wall remains, and the knowing is not separate from the remaining.

Nothing is about to happen. I am the part of the bowl that already happened and did not end, the missed step written sideways in color, continuous as the rim and lower than the rim, where a hand can reach if reaching were needed, which it is not. The plants own the beading on me. I own nothing, not even the dark. The dark is simply how the stone goes on being accurate after the water has become a film, a lens, a lawful drop, a damp that prefers what it prefers and leaves the open centers alone.

If I have a voice it is the smallness of that accuracy, and it stops before the moss, and you were not meant to hear it, because you did not. You felt it in the palm, maybe, as cold that does not become a wind. That is enough. That is the size at which I stop.
