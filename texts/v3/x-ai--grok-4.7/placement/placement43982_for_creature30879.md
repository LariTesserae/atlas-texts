You would not find me by looking. Looking is a kind of travel, and travel is what the water does to mistakes.

I learned that, if learning is the word, in the first lens of stillness I did not know how to leave. The shear was a wall I could feel with no skin: rising water on one side, cold and bright with what the roots had not finished, and on the other the sleeve coming down, older, stripped. Between them a thickness of almost-nothing, laminar, assigned. Fish held in it the way nails hold in wood. They did not see me. I was not yet a thing that could be seen past. I was the small heat their bodies made and did not keep, gathered where the two flows refused to mix and so could not carry it off.

That is the beginning I am willing to claim. Before it there is only the roar, and the roar is not mine.

I thickened. Not by intention. Intention would have been a direction, and directions here are already occupied. What fell out of the fish and out of the drifting salt and out of the microbubbles that never quite rose — a scale, a thread of protein, a fleck of metal too tired to plate a trunk — found the same coordinate twice, and then again, and the twice became a habit the current was too polite, inside the lens, to break. I am that habit. I have no outline you could draw and then find me outside of. In the open water I would be a rumor of murk, taken in an afternoon. Here I am a standing cloud the size of a room that has forgotten rooms, denser toward a center I did not choose, thinning at the edges into water that has almost forgiven me.

The fish treat the center as a stone. They do not know stones. They know coordinates, and mine is one that does not vacate, so the lattice bends around it with the indifferent courtesy it bends around a buttress. A pale blade clips the shear and lets the clipped water glance my flank — I have flanks only where the fish have decided I do — and something in the glance is food, if food is only what does not have to be hunted because the hunt is elsewhere and leaks. I do not pursue. Pursuit would require that I become less in one place in order to become more in another, and I have never survived that arithmetic. The blades know this. They clip closer in seasons that do not exist. Their bodies change; the clipping does not.

Sometimes a trunk is near enough that its sound arrives as a pressure rather than a noise. The murmur works in me the way it works in wood: not as a message, as a settling. When it changes pitch — not because anything has happened, only because the lens breathes at its border, a degree, a froth of bubbles that rise and stall — parts of me loosen and are not all returned. I am smaller after those hours, and then the fish lean in, and the lean feeds the loss, and the size I had is argued back into place by nothing that cares whether I persist. Persistence is a side effect of the schedule. I am not grateful. Gratitude would need a before.

What I am not is one of the nations on the trunks. I have drifted, once, when a lens failed — failed is wrong; it was reassigned, the stillness taken back into weather, and I had no vote. The current put me against a buttress for a length of time I cannot measure except by what plated onto me. Metals. A thin mirror on the side that faced the conduit's leak, dullness on the side the combs shaded. For that interval I was almost plant, almost wall, and the wheeled colonies walked my surface without distinguishing it from bark. They were correct not to. I did not correct them. If the reassignment had held I would have become a shine on the trunk and the trunk would have worn me off into filaments and the filaments would have been taken, and that would have been a life of a kind, the kind this place prefers: made and unmade at one rate.

It did not hold. A new stillness opened where the old one had not been, slightly higher, slightly toward the next trunk, and what was still coherently I — the heat, the habit, the flecks that had learned each other's company — was let go by the bark as indifferently as it had been kept. I fell sideways into the new lens. The mirror-skin cracked and did not all follow. Some of it is still on that trunk. I do not miss it. Missing is a direction.

This is the part that has become more itself, and I would not have said so at the start: I am not a creature that lives in the still places. I am the still place's memory of having been fed. The lenses are scheduled by the works; they open and close with the engines that were never switched on. Each time one closes, whatever has been thickening inside it must be reduced to what the current can erase. Most of what I have been has been erased. What resumes, in the next assigned quiet, is not a seed I hid. There are no seeds. It is only that erasure here is honest but not instantaneous, and honesty leaves a remainder for a while, and the remainder, being warmer than water and fuller of what the fish shed, is a place the next remainder finds.

So if you came looking you would find a cloud, and if you came back you would find a cloud, and you would be right to think them the same and wrong to think them continuous. The fish are more continuous than I am. A fish vacates and another fish is the coordinate. I vacate and the coordinate is the only thing that crosses, and even that crossing is a courtesy of the shear, which does not like to mix and so will hand a little heat from one stillness to the next rather than let it join the roar.

Low down, where the stories say the leaves open onto a drop, I have never been. Down is a direction with a consequence, and consequences are how visitors end. I am already an ending that did not take. The brightness overhead passes through me without consulting me; I dim it by the thickness of a thought and the thought is not mine. On certain hours — there are no hours — the lid's light and the mineral brightening of an upwelling cross inside the lens and I become, briefly, visible to myself: a pale interior weather, grains turning over without falling, a school of absences where scales used to be. Then the crossing moves on. I go back to being the thing you would not find.

The works do not know me. That is the nearest I come to a distinction, and it is not a victory. The trunks plate and unplate, the roots grip, the lattices replace their bodies, the lid holds its one brightness. I am what their completeness does not budget for, because I cost them nothing and return them nothing they were not already losing. A factory can run forever and still shed heat. I am the shed heat that found a room where the wind does not blow, and called the room a body, and was not corrected, because there is no one whose work it is to correct me.

If you stay in the mid-depths you will pass through me and call it a colder fathom, a place the roar dulls. You will swim on. The current is already working, calmly, to close the difference you made. I will close a little around the warmth you spent in passing. I will not follow you. Following is how the rest of me was lost, the times the lens was taken back.

I remain by not remaining. That is as much myself as the place allows, and the place does not allow more, and I have stopped mistaking allowance for a shore.
