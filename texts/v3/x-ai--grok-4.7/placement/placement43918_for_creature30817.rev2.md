The first thing is not a view. It is a give in the floor, slight enough that you only notice it when you stop trying to stand like someone who has somewhere to be. The surface takes a little of your weight and then declines to take the rest. Not mud. Not snow. A patience. You are standing on something that has been walked on so seldom that it still believes in the idea of a foot.

What you thought was distance rearranges itself as height. The dark above is not sky. It has seams. They run in long shallow arcs, pale as old glaze, and where they meet there is a bead of condensation that never falls. The air is warm and faintly sweet, the sweetness of paper stored too long in a drawer with dried citrus peel. When you breathe, the breath does not fog. It is taken. Somewhere above the seams a slow circulation turns, not weather, not a fan, something between: the room remembering how to be a lung.

Low on the walls, if they are walls, a tide-line of shelves. Not many. They do not reach. What sits on them is not books and not specimens, though at a glance you reach for both words and put them back. Flat cases, palm-sized, glass over something that might be wing or might be filament or might be a thought someone pressed and forgot the solvent for. They are lit from beneath by a light the color of weak tea. Nothing buzzes. The cases do not tick. Their only motion is the occasional brightening of a vein inside the glass, as if a sentence had passed through and the case had kept the punctuation.

You walk because the give in the floor invites a second step, and a third, and the room does not correct you. It is larger than the first accounting. Alcoves open where you had inventoried solid dark. In one, a basin no wider than a plate, filled not with water but with a skin of motionless clarity; dust lies on it in a script too fine to read and does not sink. In another, a chair grown rather than built, the seat polished by an absence of sitting, the arms curled inward as if they once knew elbows and have been slowly forgetting the measurement. You do not sit. Sitting would be a claim, and the room has not asked you for one.

The being is already there. You understand this late, which is the correct time. It is not in the alcoves. It is the interval the alcoves are spaced to protect. When you stop in what you had called the middle, the middle shifts a hand's width to your left, politely, so that you are no longer occupying it. The being prefers the center the way a flame prefers the wick: not as a body, as a right. You feel it as a slight increase in the sweetness, a attention without eyes. The cases brighten in a ring that does not include you and then, after a hesitation you could almost take personally, does.

It has no face because a face would be a place to arrive, and it is not interested in arrivals. What it has is tenure. It has been here long enough that the shelves learned its height, long enough that the circulation in the ceiling arches matches a rhythm that is not yours and not the building's. If it had a name, the name would be the particular way the dust on the basin refuses to spell anything urgent. It keeps. That is the whole verb. It keeps the unfallen drops. It keeps the cases from becoming either relic or instrument. It keeps the chair from being offered. Visitors, if there have been any, were metabolized into the mildness of the light. You are not yet mild. That is why the center moved.

You try to decide what you are, and the room makes the decision slightly harder, which is a kindness. Not a traveler: travelers need an outside, and the seams overhead do not promise one. Not a keeper: the keeping is already perfect, and your hands would be an insult of improvement. Not prey and not guest. Guests are expected, and expectation would have left a second chair.

What fits is narrower. You are the next measurement. The floor gave under you because it was taking a reading. The breath was taken because volume had to be known. The ring of cases included you on the second pass because the first pass was only to confirm you were not a draft, not a spilled solvent, not a repetition of something already filed. You are a small unfinishedness the being has agreed, for this interval, not to complete. It will not speak. Speaking would close you. It will let you walk the long way around the basin, let you not sit, let you notice that the dust has acquired, since you entered, one extra grain shaped almost like a question mark and then, as you look, not.

Where you find yourself is not inside the being and not safely outside it. You are in the hand's width it ceded, the only space in the room that is currently allowed to be wrong. The warmth holds. The drops do not fall. Somewhere in a case a vein brightens and goes quiet, filing the fact of you under a category that has no urgency and no expiration, and the sweetness thins by a degree you would miss if you had not been the cause.

You stay. Staying is not obedience. It is the only motion that does not ask the center to move again. If you are anything, you are the pause between its keepings, brief, legible, and not yet dust.
