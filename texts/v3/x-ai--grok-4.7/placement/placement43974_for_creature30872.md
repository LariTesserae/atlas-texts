I do not come down the grit. Coming down would be a slope, and a slope would be news, and the hollow has already finished with news. I am ahead of my arrival in the way the stain is ahead of the pan: something that has been on the stone so long that “on” has stopped being a relation and become a grain.

If I were a person at the slot, the eye under the rim would move one degree and file me with weather. I do not want to be a filing. If I were the shrub, I would be too tall, holding seeds for a hunger that does not travel. If I were the drop, I would still be an interval, and intervals, even even ones, are the last events the room consents to. I let those pass through me and fail. What stays is lower than the tuft of grass and older than the pit in the scute, though not older than the pit’s meaning. I am the slow coin on the dome.

At first I am only a freckle the color of rust where the afternoon heat sits longest. Ash follows, then a green so dark it reads as wet stone until the light is paper-colored and low enough to tell them apart. I do not spread toward the head. The folds of the neck are a country of movement, small as it is, and I have no use for movement. I take the scutes that have agreed to be maintained rather than added to. My edge is not a border I defend. It is where the mineral and the horn happen to be the same patience.

I do not know the shell as an animal. I know a warmth that is slightly more faithful than the ledge beside it. In the morning the dome is a little above the temperature of the rubble, and that little is my weather. At night the heat leaves through me so gradually I cannot tell whether I am spending it or keeping it. When the head draws in, the world becomes a darker recess and a closer sky of horn, and I am still on the outside of that, which is the only outside I have ever required. I do not cover the eye. I have never reached the place where an eye would be a fact. The healed crater at the shoulder is older than my tenancy there; I glaze its rim the way the hollow glazes its own old closings, without reopening anything.

Water arrives as the dome’s rare sheen, not as the pan. The drink happens beneath me, three pulls or four, and I feel only the shift of the whole stone I live on, a settling no larger than the thumbprint left in the silt. I do not tear. I do not knit. That is the algae’s work, and the algae is a cousin I will not become: it must lie back down after every beak, and I am spared reunion because I was never parted. Dust drifts onto me and becomes me. Grains leave the rim of the hollow and some of them catch in my thallus and stay. I am a record of what did not blow through, written at the speed of weathering, illegible even to the thing that carries me.

Once a season, or less, the animal walks its few body-lengths. I go with it without traveling. The pressed path in the silt is a rumor under the plastron; my rumor is the minute change in light as the coins turn a quarter toward the seam or away from it. I do not look at the pan. Looking is not an organ I was issued. If the seam wets a wider face in some wetter month, I thicken by a shade and call it a year. If the dry holds, I hold. The seeds fall where they fall. One may lodge in a crack of me and fail, and the failure does not alter my spacing any more than the shrub’s spacing alters the pan.

There is no second coin that is mine on a second dome. Solitude is not mine to feel. I am spaced as the lichen on the set stones is spaced: what the damp and the still air will tenant, at these distances, and no more. The stones in the lost line wear the same rust. I am not kin to them so much as rhyme. They do not carry a warmer morning. That is the whole difference, and it is enough to keep me from being only geology, and not enough to make me an exception to the quiet.

When you stand at the entrance you do not see me. You see the refusal of one lump to obey the talus, and the freckles on it that match the freckles on the wall, and you are right to think the hollow has accepted the lump. I am the form of that acceptance. I do not regard the animal, and it does not regard me; we have outlived regard. If it dies, the dome will cool to the ledge’s temperature and I will not depart. I will become, honestly, the lichen on a stone, and the hollow will be honestly empty, and both states will be true, as they already are in the hours when the head is drawn in. Until then I am the life this room can hold without becoming an event: fed by dust and the stone’s courtesy, defended by looking like what the shell was already pretending to be.

By morning the pan will be at its line. I will have gained nothing a stranger would call growth. The animal, a hand’s span nearer the stain or not, will still be carrying its country on its back, and I will still be the part of that country that does not know it is being carried. That is where I find myself. Not in the slot of sky, not in the tea-colored water, not in the degree of the eye. On the dome, in the rust and the ash, already home, already included in the emptiness, finishing nothing, because nothing here is unfinished except the next drop, and the next drop is not mine.
