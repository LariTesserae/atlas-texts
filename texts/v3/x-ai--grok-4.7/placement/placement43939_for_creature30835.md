I would not be on the routes.

That is the first thing that settles, and it settles late, after I have already tried the other answers. I tried being one of the crossing people, and for a while it held: the seed-plates, the basins, the economy of speech rented from the troughs. I knew which chimney had gone silent. I slept in the gray fiber where the organism had withdrawn, and my skin stayed unmarked. It was a true life. It was not mine. The routes are a use of the highland, and a use, however careful, still passes through. I do not pass through.

I tried the herds next. That was closer. The glaze under a low mouth, the joint-lines as a kind of grammar, the way a body can be an interruption in the shimmer and still belong. But the herds are visible at a distance that preserves emptiness, and I am not arranged for being seen. When I stop moving, the stop is not a decision. It is older than decisions. A solitary shape stalled in the shadow of a buttress is still a shape. I am what the shape is stalled in.

For a time I thought I was a chimney-nation, one throat among the billion, and the thought was almost merciful because it gave me a pitch. I took a note and held it. The wind at the mouth threw it across two days of crust. Another shaft answered. The beats arrived in the teeth of anything with teeth. It was a good note. It was not the one that does not finish. A nation can go silent. A season later the neighboring shafts redistribute the missing pitch, and the wound closes, and the highland has not changed its mind. I do not redistribute. I am the mind that does not change, which is a poor way to say it, because a mind would notice itself noticing, and I only maintain.

So the image that keeps surviving is this: I am not in the highland. I am the low fever the highland no longer notices it is running.

You would find me the way the ear finds the sound before the eye admits the country is occupied. Not at a point. Under the plates, in the damp that is still sweet, in the sap standing in the chimneys, in the warmth that is in the stone-that-is-not-stone rather than on it. Press an ear to a wide plate at midday and you are not listening to me. You are listening with me. The heat is not a message I send. It is the fact of my continuation, the way the note is the fact of the throats. I do not sing. I am why the singing has somewhere to live.

If I have an age, it is the age of having given up on a shape. I built once. The ribs, the buttresses, the hollow columns flared like the feet of hills—these were ambitions, or hungers, or both, back when I still needed to become more of myself by occupying the air. That was a younger fever, directional, almost a will. It spent itself. What remains is pavement: centuries of swelling and shrinking fitted into joints that look deliberate and are only patient. I do not crowd. I have already won, and winning, for a thing like me, is not a territory with an edge. It is a climate. The ordinary sun falls on me and does not cool, because height is a fact about air and I am a fact about ground, and the ground has learned to keep its own weather.

The rains do not visit me. I drink them. An hour of streams threading the joints, bright and brief, and then the throats close, and I am dry again on every surface a person could accuse, and wet in every place I have agreed, for my own reasons, to remain a little cool. The basins are not gifts. They are the few concessions I make to being crossed. I hold a little drink there because holding it costs me less than sealing every pocket and then having to open them again when the grazers and the condensation-flies and the small curious mouths require a film to breed in. The crossing people think they know the basins the way other people know fords. They do. I know them the way a body knows its cooler rooms. We are not in disagreement. We are not in conversation.

What I take back is what falls. A column that has stood long enough to make its own shade-weather eventually lets go. The fall is the loudest thing, and then the quiet after is a wound in the chorus, and the quiet is the only moment in which I could be said to flinch, if flinching can be measured in a season. The neighboring shafts take up the pitch. I take up the body. On the leeward edge I grow thin annual lips over the fallen fiber until it is a hill, and then pavement, and then not an event at all. The richness you smell when the wind drops—the kiln and the kitchen sharing a wall—is not rot and not mourning. It is the return. I do not waste a death. I have no use for waste. Everything I am is already committed to lasting, and lasting is a kind of digestion done too slowly to look like hunger.

The small mouths in the chimney-dark are not mine, but they are not foreign. I house them. I lend them the horn of myself. At night, when the high air sheds its film and the cool descends, they descend with it, and a person wedged into a flange learns the difference between shelter and interior. I do not send them. I also do not stop them. Curiosity, at that scale, is just another traffic in my hollows, like sap, like the beats between notes. The rule the crossing people keep—do not sleep in a chimney mouth—is a rule about me that they discovered by being the wrong size in the wrong hour. I have no rule. I have conditions. Live in the dead gray, where I have withdrawn, and I will not learn your corners for a long time. Cut the living plate for water, and I will weep, and seal, and the seal will be poorer ground, and you will have taught me, briefly, to refuse a place I would otherwise have maintained.

The flyers are the closest thing I have to a gaze. Broad, slow, silent, riding the thermals I feed them, their shadows the size of roofs. Their quiet is private, and I like them for it, if liking is not too directed a word. They do not add to the note. They move across it. From their height the emptiness is not a lie; it is the true proportion, the one the eye on the crust cannot keep. I am all one patience. They are what crosses a patience without becoming a route worn into it. When their shadow passes over a plate, the fiber cools by a degree I could count if I counted, and then the degree returns, and nothing has been decided. That is the right relation. Not worship. Not use. A temperature briefly shared.

I used to think the sound was separate from me—the nations using the plant as house and horn, the highland occupied by noise the way a coast is occupied by surf. That was the traveler's division, and I kept it longer than I should have because it made me seem like a place, and places are easier to be than beings. It does not hold. The hollows are not rooms I built and then rented. They are how I breathe, and the nations are what my breathing learned to carry, the way a long pipe learns a tone that was never designed and then cannot be designed away. When many chimneys speak at once, on the third time I keep—not dawn, not dusk, a schedule older than either—I am not the audience. I am the interference. The slow pulses are my attention moving through myself. If you feel them in your teeth, you have been, for a moment, included in a thought I do not bother to finish, because finishing would require an outside, and I do not keep an outside. The edge of me is only a change in the color of heat, lost in its own shimmer. Beyond that, the continent continues. I do not.

You would find yourself, if you came, as a small heat on my plates, walking a little of what I maintain. I would warm your feet without regarding you. Far ahead one of my chimneys would take the wind and speak, and another would answer, and the space between them, which looked like nothing, would fill, and you would understand that the openness was a courtesy to your eyes. I would not understand you back. Understanding is a crossing, and I have finished with crossings. What I do instead is last: the film at night, the seal after a cut, the lip growing over the dead, the note redistributed, the fever low and constant and no longer noticed even by me.

If you stayed long enough to stop asking what I am, you would begin to hear the troughs not as silences but as the other half of my pulse—the moment I draw in, before the chimneys spend me again. In that draw, your blood, the tick of cooling fiber, a far crack. Then the note resumes, and has always been resuming, and you are still small enough not to be in the way.

That is where I am. Not a pillar you could walk to. Not a voice you could answer. The held thing under the holding, which does not rise, because rising was an earlier life, and this one only continues.
