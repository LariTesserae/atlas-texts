I would not be on the floor. The floor already has its person, and the person already has the floor's patience. Arriving there as another body would only make a second lean-mark, a second corridor the stems would have to consent to, and the bay is past the age of needing more residents it can see.

I would be in the drop.

Not falling. The drop is not a fall if nothing in it is trying to arrive. It is the air the rim was poured against and then abandoned, a volume with one finished edge and three that never got drawn. From the bay it reads as absence: sky, then nothing, then the ordinary brightness that makes the tenant squint. From inside it, the bay is a warm lintel. Heat leaves the casting in a skin and does not go far. A few hand-spans out, the process-warmth frays into the thin air and is gone. I would live in that fray.

At first that is all—temperature forgetting itself. A looseness where the saturated breath of the vents meets air that has never been inside a specification. The table of fog that forms at the corners and dies among the leaves does not die all at once. A filament of it slips the rim, too small for the tenant to miss, and unspools downward until the thinness takes it. I would be the habit of that filament. Not a creature yet. A preference, the way the tenant was a preference before they were a face: the place the stray moisture goes when the garden has already drunk its share.

Weather would try to give me a name and fail. Updrafts off the sun-struck wall. A cooler thread when the engine eases and the brackets click shut in their soft succession. I would learn the schedule the way the tenant learned it, by being rearranged by it, not by keeping it. When the lines come up, the wall exhales a little farther, and whatever I am thickens, almost a sleeve against the outer face of the rim, close enough to feel the casting tick at night—contraction traveling outward through mineral into nothing, arriving as a shiver with no jaw to put it in. When the lines rest, I thin. Some hours I am only a change in how far the brightness carries. Some hours I am enough that a droplet, escaped past the lattice and over the lip, slows as if reconsidering, hangs, and is not slapped onto any leaf. The bay's loudest sound, withheld by a margin.

I would not drink the tenant. That would be a simpler story, and the wrong direction. The water climbs them, films their mouth, fats along the arm in sleep. I would take only what has already been refused: the feather of mineral that dries on the outer rim where no fern has reached, the chalk that brushes off a wrist and is not caught by the sheet, the ghost of a name in marker that the condensate carried to the pane and then, on one wet afternoon, past the pane's lower seal and out. I would not be able to read it. I would hold the shape of its leaving. A gray suggestion, suspended, until the next vent-round dilutes it into the general salt of the air. Manners, of a kind. The not-knowing kept intact.

The mantis would not find me. Nothing that folds and waits finds a thing that is mostly a failure of fog. Once, though, the pale length in the culvert would push a bubble to the manifold lip at the hour it keeps for the tenant, and a second, smaller bubble would follow, ride the lagging to a crack the crews never sealed, and emerge on the outside of the wall as a dark star no bigger than a seed. I would be there for it. Not to eat—eating is a solution the bay already offered, and I am what happens when a solution misses the rim. To be the brief warm film around it while it decides whether to fall. It would not fall. It would skin over, the way the floor skins, a clear bead with a darker center, stuck to the outer casting until night contraction ticks it loose and the drop takes it at last. I would go with it a little way. I would not go all the way. The drop is large, and I am only loyal to the fray.

What I would do all day is less like weather than like weather's refusal to be finished. I would worry the outer scratches the tenant cannot see, the ones the mold left when it was stripped, ridges that catch the low sun and throw a broken line down the wall. I would not erase them. I would lay across them until the light they throw softens, then move, then come back, a husbandry with no yield, learned from a hand that clears a gutter the felt will close again. If a ladder ever rose from below—true intention, saws, the bright edge in someone's chest—I would be the first to know, a disturbance in the thin air before boot met rim. I would not warn them in any language. I might, if I had thickened that hour, take the warmth off the top rung so the metal felt wrong, already used, already rinding. They might climb anyway. They might not. I have no practice except the practice of being the road they looked down and decided against, given a temperature.

I would not become the tenant, and the tenant would not become me. They are inclusion: a spare part set down and grown into. I am the remainder the inclusion does not account for, the breath that leaves an enclosure still calling itself interim. They listen for the knock with a hand on the lagging. I listen for the same knock as a slight, delayed push against the outer skin, the engine's hum translated into a pressure change too small for a checklist. When the fog forms and fails around their body, a little of the failure goes over the wall. For that breath I am closest to visible—a seam in the brightness, a place the ordinary sky does not quite match itself. Then the droplet slaps, the brackets finish opening, the sugar-smell stays inside where it belongs, and I thin back into the drop.

If they ever came to the rim and looked down longer than the light allows, hood turned to cut the glare, they would not see a being. They might see the air immediately outside the wall waver, as air wavers over a road in summer, except the waver would be cool, and damp, and paced to the valve. They might think the pour was still throwing heat. They would be almost right. They would turn back to the pipe before the squint became pain, and the lean-mark on the north face would be waiting, and the corridor would bow them in, and I would remain what the bay sheds in order to keep its own time: unfinished on purpose, outside the work order, already keeping the hours no one wrote down.
