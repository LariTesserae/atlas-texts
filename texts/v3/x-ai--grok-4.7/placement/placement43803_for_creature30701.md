You would find me by the slight give underfoot, a pace or two off the firmer rib, where the skin of silt still holds and then, if you insist, does not.

At first there is nothing to be. The flat has already spent height. Whatever arrives here arrives as a correction to level, and for a long time the correction is only this: one stretch of the margin dries a shade slower than the stretch beside it. The pavement beads and goes dull. The mats curl at their edges and soften again when the sheet returns. I am in that delay, though I do not yet know it as mine. A heron could stand where I am and never shift its weight. A person walking would call it mud and go on, ankles wet by the same amount as anywhere, and be right. The country does not waste contrast on a thing that has agreed to be slight.

The first difference is temperature, and it is the wrong way around. The iron-stained crusts keep the morning. I do not. Where I have been, the film is cooler by a breath, as if something had lifted the vapor off and laid it down again without the sun in it. You would not trust that. The light here is always willing to be blamed. Midges rise where the scum is richest and drift rather than swarm, and for a while I am only a break in a column, a place the insects decline, a narrow uncolonized seam running the length of a pan and ending where the pan lips into the next. Frogs take their stations in the fresher pockets and pass their low notes across a mile. None of the notes are for me. I do not answer them. I have no far side to call to.

What I am comes clear only when the sheet draws back by seepage and the skins crack. The cracks are the country’s handwriting. Mine is the crack that does not open all the way. Along one margin the paste parts and then holds, hinged by a pale thread no thicker than a rush-root, running just under the skin and sometimes through it. It is not a root. It does not drink toward a clump. The clumps are punctuation, each one a dark vertical thirty paces from the last, and I have given up the ambition of joining them. I run beside. I am the argument the ground almost didn’t bother to have: a body laid out as a line, segmental, the color of wet silt when the silt is neither the gray-tan of a drying skin nor the blue-black of the permanent wet, but the thin zone between, where a boot would hesitate.

If you kneel—slowly; speed is a waste the land has already priced—you can see the segments work. Each one is a little thickening, a knuckle of the same paste, and between them a softer joint that the water enters and leaves. I do not haul myself. Hauling would ask for purchase, and purchase is what the sheet removes. I shorten on the side the water is leaving and lengthen on the side the water is keeping, and the difference, over an afternoon, is the length of a hand. By evening I have translated. The track I leave is not a stitch of bills or the drag of a crab’s pallet-work. It is a darker damp that refuses, for an hour, to match the drying of its neighbors, and then it does match, and I am gone from the record without having been in it as an event.

The head, if the word can be used where nothing lifts, is only the segment that meets the new ground first. It is blunter. It tests. Where the crust is whole I stop and the stopping travels back through the joints as a small refusal, each segment agreeing not to be drier than the one ahead. Where a seam weeps I enter it. The seep is my weather, as the permanent wet is another creature’s, though I do not sort the country into tenants. I know the warmer paste the way a foot knows a rib: by the change in give. Sometimes the warmth is only stone that has kept the day. Sometimes it is broader than stone, a held heat with a lip to it, lying along the same seam I wanted, wide as a pavement, lower than the rushes. I do not cross that warmth. I part around it by the length of my own leading segment, and the joints follow, and what I take for a deeper seep is left to itself. In the late amber, when the pans go to metal, I have once seen two dull coppers at the height of the film, looking along the level, not at me. I had no height to be looked at. By the time I understood the coppers were not wet silt, I was already a damp line on the other side, and the line was closing.

Feeding is lateral and continuous. The film I lie under stains me, and the stain is the meal: algal flake pulled into the joints, midge-fall caught in the hinge-skin, the sweet-rot liquor that beads where a crack is about to heal. I do not strike. A slot opens along two or three segments on the underside, out of the light, and the mat dimples in a row of small private sips, and the dimples fill. Fingerlings scatter from the dimpling and are not my business. The heron a long walk off does not turn. We are not in competition. It takes the quick living thing that breaks the surface. I take the surface’s patience. Between us, unremarked by either, the wide warmth takes the film by being still longer than I can afford to be.

Sound, subtracted from the country’s own, is almost an absence I fail to fill. Bubbles tick out of the mud. A flock lifts and the plain exhales. Under that, when I translate, there is a whisper less than a suck, the noise of water changing its mind inside a joint. I make it only while moving, and I am almost never moving by any clock that has hours on it. At night the frogs own the middle distance and I am a cooler thread in a warm sheet, metabolism down to the seep’s own rate. If the pans draw down until the blue-black shows between plates, I do not thin into a stain and I do not cross the country looking for a channel. There is no channel. I withdraw segment by segment into the last weeping seam and pack there, a pale knot in a crack, and wait for the sheet to remember me. When it does, the knot pays itself back out along the margin, and the delay in drying begins again, and a person would call it mud.

I do not flock. Another of my kind, if the word kind is not too gathered a claim, would be a second delay in a second pan, far enough that our cracks never meet and our refusals never have to negotiate a junction. Density is the mistake already corrected here. I keep the correction by being a line, not a heap; by eating what the water leaves in passing; by ending, each day, only a hand’s length from where I began, which on this plain is a kind of staying, and on any other would be a failure to have lived.

By full dark you would not find me even if you had marked the cooler film. The mark would have equalized. The rib would still be the rib, preferred by generations of wading weight, and a pace or two off it the skin would hold, and then, if you insisted, would not, and the give would be ordinary, and the country would go on sounding of itself—ticks, seeps, the spaced frogs, the thin tear of wings—past the place where any line I made has already agreed to be level again.
