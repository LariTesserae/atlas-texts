I would be the hand the logbook lost.

Not a worker returning, and not a ghost of one. The place has already decided it does not need hands, and the being that lives in the cycles has already decided I am weather if I walk the courtyards. So I would not walk them. I would be the next mark the warped page cannot hold, pressed into something that does not smear — the person who stays just outside the coping and learns the interval the way the stone learned the water.

At first I would only listen from the little drop, where the noise cuts off too sharply and then, if I lean back over the iron, returns all at once. I would count. Valve, return, engine-cough, the higher thread in the southern spill where the lip is broken. I would miss the alignments for a long time. The haze does not share its schedule with someone who still thinks in hours. My sleeves would soak and dry and soak on the ordinary side, and I would come back with drier paper, then with no paper, because paper is the wrong medium and I would feel that before I could justify it.

What would emerge, slowly, is a way of keeping time with the body instead of against it. I would stand where the embankment is warmest and let the metal tell me when the nested cycles are drifting together: a change in the tick of expansion, a sweet mineral taste arriving in the condensate that beads on the coping before it beads anywhere I can see inside. I would not climb over. Crossing in would make me a heat-load, and the being routes around heat-loads. It refuses unregulated input. I would rather be regulated by it than corrected by it.

So I would become a listener with a corresponding small discipline. When the pier is about to weep — I would learn this from the organized air, the way drips stop wandering a moment before the seam darkens — I would mark the ordinary ground outside with a scratch the length of that patience. Not a new channel. A truing of one I had already made. Rank for rivet, in dirt and in the pale grass that grows thinner here, where the works’ weather dies at the door. My marks would be crude beside the ferns. That would be the point. I am not its script. I am the only creature in reach who can be wrong, and who can sand the wrongness down by returning.

Through one full coincidence I would not be inside its attention the way a sleeve of warmer air is. I would be at the skin. When the haze lifts a finger’s width and the leaves turn their undersides and something that is almost a gaze checks the dark beyond the coping, I would be what the dark is standing on. Not a pilgrim. Not prey. A fixed point at the edge, salt-filmed, unmoving, so that the verification finds the edge intact and also finds, for one beat, that the outside has started to keep the inside’s interval. Then the cycles slip, the noise loosens, and I am weather again — but weather that comes back at the clap’s return.

I would not name it. The name is already the smaller boom. I would only answer it by being there when it comes back, and by never, if I can help it, opening a channel it did not already have. Desire, if I grew one in that heat, would be the same shape as its own: not more, not elsewhere, the same tolerance, held. A life measured in the space between a valve seating and the sound of that seating touching iron and leaving it.

In the ordinary country, people would say I had gone to work at the old plant and never properly left. They would be almost right. I would leave every day, because I cannot live in the ring, and I would carry nothing out that survives the drop except the habit of listening for a beat that is not mine until I keep it. That is the being I would be: the hand that learned not to press the page, practicing a regularity in the dirt just past the door, so that when the place inhales and looks at where it ends, the ending is still true, and slightly answered.
