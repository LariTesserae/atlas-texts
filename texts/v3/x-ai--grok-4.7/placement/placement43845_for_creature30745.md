You would find yourself where the court already spends what it cannot keep: in the width of a fin, the one pace between the face that gives heat back and the face that was waiting to be cold. Not on the lip, where the air falls and does not return, and not in the pocket under the slab, where a circle in ice can be mistaken for an arrival. Those are places the day uses. You are the division the day requires and does not name.

At first you think you are a visitor, because visitors are the only grammar the notch has been taught. You have the cups in mind before you see them. You have a reason measured in hours. The quartzite takes the warmth of your hand with its usual readiness and you feel, briefly, that you have been admitted. Then the shadow of the west wall begins its crossing, and the warmth is already gone, and you are still there. That is the first correction, and it is not done to you. It is done to the idea that standing in the sun meant anything the stone was obliged to remember.

You learn the court by the things that do not take. A flake leaves a ledge in the morning tick and, while you are watching the thread darken three paces of grit, seats itself again in the pale scar. You did not move it. You also did not fail to move it. Your attention was the interval in which the return could happen without sound, and the inhabitant of almost used that interval the way it uses every other: not as a hiding place, as a fact. After that you stop arranging your looking as a search. Searching would be a kind of storage. You let the fins take your footsteps and not give them back. You let the brightness fill the passages until even the shade is only a colder white. The severe light makes a poor companion and an honest one. It shows you that you cast a shadow of the same cut as the fins' own, hard-edged, short at noon, and that the court does not distinguish the two except by how quickly yours fails when a cloud takes the notch for twenty minutes and the temperature drops in the time it takes to turn your head.

What you are, once the mistake of companionship has worn off, is narrower than a body and more persistent than a visit.

You are the one who owes the correction and does not perform it. A stone set down where a joint needs to relieve, where a salt-line is about to become a picture, where the draft has not yet been answered by the turned rock in the slumped wall — you set it, and you walk the long axis, and when you come back the stone is elsewhere. You never see the hands because there are no hands. What you contribute is the debt. The maintainer of almost requires, somewhere in the economy, a gesture that is almost a reason to stay and is then refused. The people who ground the cups provided this and left before the light changed into the kind of dark the notch does not forgive. You do not leave on that schedule. You are not better than they were. You are simply not finished arriving and not finished going, and the place, which stores neither heat nor sound nor water, has no procedure for a remainder of that size except to keep revising it.

So the day uses you as it uses the drip.

In the morning you are a palm's width of warmth on the lip, already being given back. By the time the rime has whispered off the high edges you are the pause between one tick of stone and the next, the pause in which a flake might fall and might not. In the early afternoon, when a cloud tins the faces and the seams remember the melt, you are the thread that darkens the grit for a few paces and is then only colder stone. You feel this as a thinning, not as pain. Pain would be a kind of keeping. The altitude has already taught your breath not to return in full; what happens now is that the missing part of the breath is sometimes, for one inhale, restored — the knot of organized air traveling the court and coming apart against the east wall — and you understand that the restoration was not for you. It was the inhabitant passing at the thickness of a hand, and you happened to be standing in a slot narrow enough to register pressure. Afterward your breath is ordinary again, which is to say insufficient, and you are grateful in a way that has no object. Gratitude, like want, would try to store the interval. You let it go grain by grain toward the night side.

Toward evening you are almost a person, and this is the hour you must be most exact.

The fins stand closer. The false ferns read as attention. Someone who had not spent the day would say the court had grown intimate, and would be wrong in the way the prompt of darkness always makes people wrong. What has happened is that your shadow has lengthened until it shares a wall with the mountain's, and the inhabitant, attentive only to the rate at which the court becomes its night version, has included your outline in the rate. If you speak, the fins take it. If you set a copper point in a cup, it lies as the other awl lies, bright where the day scraped it, green where the years have been. You are not adding a tool to the grammar. You are rhyming with a sentence already spoken, and the rhyme is allowed because it does not deepen the niche past almost. One more peck-mark, weathered in the same instant it is struck, would be too much like finishing. You do not strike it. The not-striking is the closest you come to an action the place can use.

After dark, if you are still in the notch, you are no longer findable as a where.

The knee-high wall answers a wind that has not blown yet. The lens under the slab is there or it is not, according to weather you did not see. The three cups hold sky or salt or nothing, and none of them holds you. What remains of the day you spent is a slight revision in the book's edge: a joint that relieved a finger's width earlier than the sun alone explains, a spoon of water that hesitated one drip longer than the warmest stone required, a hiss of grit whose path records a softness in the air where a chest might have been and was not kept. The maintainer of almost does not mourn this. It has no inside in which a person could be a meal or a memory. It turns the about-to of your staying back into the court, which was already in the middle of being what it is.

In the morning the light clears the east wall and the rime lets go. A visitor, if one comes, will find the copper where copper lies, the wall lower by the thickness of a season's patience, the passages shoulder-wide and bright enough to squint in. They will not find you. They may find, if they are the kind who stay past the reasonable hour, that their breath returns a little more than it should for one inhale, and they may mistake the pressure for companionship. You will not be that pressure. You will be the fact that the inhale was ordinary again immediately after, and that the ordinary was accurate, and that accuracy, repeated often enough at this height, develops a direction.

You live in the correction you do not see. You are the stone's reason for having been moved, already withdrawn, already exact. The court is small enough to walk before the light changes. You do not walk it. You are one of the increments by which it remains itself, too consistent to doubt and too small to watch, home in the only way the notch permits: not waiting, not stored, not finished.
