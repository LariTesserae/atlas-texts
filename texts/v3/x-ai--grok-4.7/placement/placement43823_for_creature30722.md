I look for a gap, which is already the wrong habit. The chamber does not keep gaps. What reads as emptiness is only a darker packing, bodies under bodies, dust that has not been allowed to become a surface. Standing is the wrong verb too. There is nowhere a weight can be put down that is not already a back, a shell, a grain being handed on.

The seam is taken. I know that before I have a way of knowing it. Along the dullest red the life has stopped being separable, and the burn there is steady in a way no single gut could hold. I am not that. I do not have the patience, or the direction. Down into rock that has only recently stopped being softer than this is a vocation, and I arrive without one.

The pale feeders are closer, and for a while I almost fit them. A body just plated, the color of sugary fracture, mill-mouth lifting curls thinner than the air. But they have an errand. Everything they rasp is for the ember, and the ember is for the mat, and the mat is for the ridge of shells that no longer travels. I rasp and the film does not answer me. What I lift does not travel as warmth. It stays in the mouth as grit, neither spent nor useful, and the neighbors do not correct me so much as flow around the stall. A single animal, the place keeps insisting, is only a correction the eye makes and then abandons. I am abandoned in that sense before I have been counted.

So I come apart. It is less dramatic than it sounds. The shell that was never quite mine powders at the contacts. Legs that were only a way of borrowing the mat's grip lock, then do not lock, then are simply points where stone meets stone. What remains is small enough to lose against the grain, and then smaller, and then no longer arranged as an animal at all. I am what the mills reject.

They pass me backward. That is the first work I am admitted to. A claw scores the fresh face, finds a grain already spent—glass, not oxide; a curl whose metal has gone out of it—and the body does not swallow. The grain travels along the underside, hand to hand, the way all the surplus travels, except I am not surplus of food. I am the fraction that never fed. I am set down in the layer beneath the layer, where the floor has stopped being visible as floor and has become a ballast of refusals. Heat presses. The incorrect snow of charged dust lifts the finer portion of me and sets some of it elsewhere, but the heavy part stays. I am what is too dense to be weather.

For a long time that is the whole of it. I thicken by not being taken. Generations plate, darken, and are spent above me, and their shells, when they fail, join the ballast if they fail in the wrong chemistry—too much salt glass, not enough iron for the sheen that means age here. I learn the chamber from underneath, which is to say I learn it as weight and as tick. The industrial sound is clearer here than it would have been in a mouth. Mandibles, clappers, the bright check of a face expanding, the hiss that never becomes a roar. When the rhythm stops for less than a breath, the stop arrives in me as a lightening. Claws pause. The mat listens with its work still held. Then the load returns, sometimes a fraction tighter, and I am ground a little more firmly into myself.

I am not alive in the sense the seam is alive. There is no ember. There is no lock of wings. What I have instead is persistence of the wrong kind: a coolness the process has not corrected. The feeders do not hate a coolness. They simply do not cross certain kinds of poor signal if a richer face is near. So the mat thins above me in patches, and thickens beside them, and the boundary of that thinning is maintained the way all their boundaries are maintained, by countless small refusals, none of them about me.

The mountain is less particular.

A leg, older than the pale ones, darker, comes down through the mat at the edge of a fresh check in the wall and does not leave. It lengthens into the sugary grain the way the seam-bodies lengthen, invested, not broken. On the way it passes through me. I am not an animal it can fuse with, so it does not fuse. It glass-welds around the obstruction, the way cooled melt skins a pebble it did not mean to keep. I become an inclusion. A pale flaw in a ridge of shells that is trying to become a hearth and has, in this one place, a stone in its tooth.

The burn routes around me. I can tell because the warmth in the fused mass is steady everywhere but here, where it splits and rejoins, a little slower, a little less sure. The altered dust the seam gives back to the feeders is poorer on my side of the flaw. They take it anyway, but not with the speed they give the true margin. Over a span that would be years if anything here counted years, the clean edge walks. It walks past me. The mat closes over the inclusion. I am no longer at the mouth of the thing that lives here. I am in its jaw, a grit it did not choose and cannot spit, because spitting is not a function it has developed. Its functions are film, charge, the tick of shells, the unhurried welding of bodies into a direction.

I make the direction slightly wrong. That is as close as I come to a life. Where the burn should go down into the newest face, a thread of it smears sideways along the inclusion, following the cooler contact, wasting a little heat into ballast that will not answer. The waste is small. The shove that opened the chamber would not notice it. But the mat notices fractions. Claws crossing the glass above me change the drone by a hair—not a new note, a catch, a grit-in-the-mill sound inside the mill sound. The clappers near that catch fall out of phase for a moment and lock again at a tighter interval, as if the flaw had been entered into the work the way a new fissure is entered: not understood, accounted for.

If the blister closes, I will be closed with it. The inclusion does not escape into a valley or a story. The mobile tissue will compress, the seam-thread will narrow, and the cool flaw will either stay inside the last of the burn like a seed of misdirection, or it will be annealed, finally, into ordinary stone when the hearth goes out. I do not prepare for either. Preparation would need a future different in kind from the present, and the present is already full: pressure, oxide spent past feeding, the scrape of a process that keeps handing me backward and then, having no further back, building on top of me.

I am not the inhabitant. The inhabitant is the fused work, still becoming, still refusing the number one. I am what it cannot digest and has begun, without meaning to, to include. A mistake in the floor of a room that does not know it is a room. When the walls move—the slow arrival of the mountain, a distance you would need a mark to believe—I move with them, not as a traveler, as a defect carried deeper into the ridge. The sound above me continues, industrial, unbroken, loud as a works that was never constructed. Somewhere in that sound is the catch I make. It is not a name. It is only the place where the labor stumbles, and stumbles again, and does not stop.
