The first thing that arrives is not a shape but a resistance, a slight drag against the idea of moving forward. It is not a wall. It yields if pressed, then returns, as if the air itself had a grain, a nap, and you had stroked it the wrong way. After a while you stop pressing. You learn to go with the nap. The drag becomes a guide: this way the medium parts with almost no effort, that way it thickens into something you would have to tear. You do not tear it.

What parts for you is a pale, continuous substance, neither mist nor water. It holds a little light and does not scatter it. You can see a short distance—far enough to know that nothing solid interrupts the substance for a long time, not far enough to believe in a horizon. The light does not come from anywhere. It is the substance remembering a brightness it once held and still holding a fraction of it, evenly, without source or shadow. Your own outline, if you look down, is only a difference in density, a place where the pale stuff is pushed aside and has not yet flowed back. You do not cast a shadow because there is no direction for one to fall.

You are not walking. Walking wants a floor. Here the medium supports whatever posture you offer it, and the posture that costs least is a slow drift with the grain, limbs loose, the way a body rests in water that is exactly its own temperature. The temperature is the second fact, arriving after the grain. It is not warm and not cool. It is the temperature at which you stop being able to feel your own skin as separate from what touches it. Boundaries do not disappear; they simply stop announcing themselves. You can still find the edge of a hand by moving it, but the finding is a change in resistance, not a sensation of contact.

Sound behaves like the light. There is a low, even tone, not a hum exactly, more the memory of a tone that the substance keeps circulating so it will not have to be made again. Your own motion does not add to it. When you stop, the tone does not change. You begin to suspect that the tone is what the medium uses to know itself, a standing confirmation that it is still continuous, still one thing. You are a small interruption in that confirmation, and the medium notes you without alarm. It has room.

Time is present as the rate at which the grain restores itself after you pass. You can watch it—turn, and the path you opened is already softening, the pale substance knitting back along its preferred direction. The knitting is not hurried. Neither is it slow. It happens at the speed of the place’s own attention, which seems to be everywhere at once and nowhere focused. Nothing here is waiting for you to finish. Nothing here will keep the shape you made in it. That is not hostility. It is simply that the medium’s loyalty is to its grain, and the grain does not include a record of visitors.

You try, once, to leave a mark on purpose: a twist, a knot of resistance held until your attention tires. The knot holds while you hold it. When you let go it unwinds, not snapping back but relaxing along the nap, the way a muscle unclenches, until the place where it was is indistinguishable from the places where you never were. You understand then that persistence, here, cannot be stored in the medium. If anything lasts, it lasts because something keeps doing it. There is no archive. There is only the ongoing.

What lives here—what has always been living here, you realize, once you stop looking for bodies of the kind you know—is the grain itself in its variations. Some regions run smoother, the nap longer, the tone fractionally lower. Others are tighter, the resistance more frequent, the light a shade more concentrated, as if the substance had folded extra memory into a smaller volume. These are not creatures moving through the medium. They are the medium deciding, locally, to be more or less itself. You can drift into a tighter region and feel the drag increase, the tone rise a half-step, your own outline grow slightly more distinct because the substance is pushing back harder. Stay, and the region does not reject you; it simply continues its tighter way, and you are included in the tightness until you choose to leave. Leaving is always possible. The grain will part. It would rather part than break.

There is no “being” in the singular that you can greet. Or rather, the being is the entire continuity, and it does not have a face to turn toward you because it has no away-from-you. You are already inside the only room it has. Communication, if it happens, is not a message sent across a distance. It is a temporary agreement about grain: you align your drift with a tighter region, match its tone by the small adjustments of your own density, and for a while the two of you are one variation together. Then you drift apart, and the agreement ends without remainder, without farewell, because farewell would require that something had been separate enough to miss the separation. You do not feel lonely when it ends. Loneliness would be the wish for a record the place does not keep. Instead you feel the particular texture of that region still in the way your outline sits, a slight change in how readily you part the substance, which fades only as you continue. The fading is the closest thing to memory the place allows a visitor: not a stored image, but a lingering difference in resistance that the medium will eventually comb out of you if you let it.

You could refuse to let it. You could keep turning, keep re-imposing the texture, spend the whole of your attention on not being combed smooth. The place would permit that. It permits every local insistence; it simply does not help you maintain it, and it will not be altered by it. The cost is yours alone: a constant small effort, a life spent as a knot. Nothing here will tell you the effort is noble or futile. The tone does not rise in approval or fall in warning. It continues, confirming only that the substance is still continuous, whether you are knotted or not.

If you stay long enough to stop expecting an elsewhere, you notice that you have begun to prefer certain regions without deciding to. The smoother runs, where the drag is almost a kindness and the light is most evenly pale, become places you return to not by navigation—there are no landmarks—but by the way your own density now answers them. You have been edited, a little, by the medium’s refusal to keep your marks. What remains is the set of postures that cost you least, and those postures are slowly becoming your shape. You are still an interruption. You still restore nothing, hold nothing, record nothing. But the interruption has learned the grain, and the grain has, without noticing, included the particular way you part it in the range of things it knows how to be.

There is no arrival and no departure you can point to. There is only the ongoing confirmation, the tone that does not need you and does not mind you, the pale substance knitting itself along a direction older than any choice you could make inside it. You are the place where that direction briefly hesitates, a soft knot the medium will comb out or not, according to whether you keep tying it. Most of the time, now, you do not. The hesitation passes. The grain runs on. And in the running, for as long as the running includes the small habit of your drift, you are here.
