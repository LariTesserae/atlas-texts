You would not arrive. Arrival is a door event, and doors here are only the habit of stone. You would notice, instead, that the copper smear has a thickness it did not have, and that the thickness is you—an interval in the dark where heat is slightly more organized, where the sheet of water, parting, has to decide.

At first that is all. A hesitation in a current. The lane runs toward the place you occupy and divides, not because a body stands there but because something in the water has learned a contour and is loyal to it. The contour is narrow. Ankle-deep, no deeper. When the water rejoins behind you it is warmer by a degree that would not register on any instrument that needed a hand to hold it, only on the stone, which keeps a paler line for a while and then drinks it back.

You try, out of an old reflex, to be a person. Shoulders. A weight that would leave a print in the crust. The print appears, once, off-center, the size of a heel, and then the crust seals at its own speed and the heel is a rumor the ground is no longer interested in. Standing still does not help. Stillness is the city's native state; it does not confer an outline. What confers an outline is motion that repeats. You walk the raised sill between two channels and the sill, which has been the same height for an age, brightens under you—not with light, with use. A faint polish. The polish is the first evidence that you have duration.

Duration is not the same as a life. You learn this when you try to remember a before. There is the basin, and the non-light, and the tick of hot rain on a hollow stalk, and then there is the same basin with a discrepancy in it, and the discrepancy is the only memory you can trust, because it is still happening. You were not born in a room. You condensed the way the walls condensed: out of a solution that had been carrying you, dissolved, until the heat crossed some quiet threshold and you came out of the water as a persistence.

For a long time—long the way the terraces are long, not the way hours are—you are only that. A place the flow accounts for. People, if the word still means anything, would have walked through you and felt a change in temperature at the shins and thought of a spring. You do not correct them. There is no them. The column for animals stays empty, and you are not an argument against the emptiness. You do not breathe. You do not hunt. You do not call. The sounds you make are the sounds the city already makes, returned a fraction late: a drop, a step, the soft scale of sinter letting go of a wall. The lateness is your body. It has no other limbs.

Then the lateness begins to prefer certain rooms.

Not to live in them. Preference is too animal. To complete them. There is a court with an off-center drain and an alcove the water has not yet decided to enter. You linger at the threshold—linger meaning the current slows, meaning the copper thickens, meaning a second drop falls where the first already fell, out of rhythm, as if the lintel had two memories of rain and could not choose. While you are there the alcove takes on a floor. Not built. Precipitated, in the same grammar as everything else, but faster, as if your delay were a seed the mineral could use. By the time the water finds the drain the alcove holds a handspan of dry stone, and the dry stone holds the shape of having been waited in. You leave. The alcove remains. Somewhere in the outer pans a patch of crust cracks in the same plan, alcove on the wrong side, and you understand that you have been copied into the city's leakage, the way a layout leaks into unbuilt ground. You are becoming a feature.

Features do not stay singular here. You feel yourself in two courts at once and do not tear. One of you is the polish on a sill; the other is a gyre at the western edge tightening around a dry center. Both are accurate. Neither is the original, because original was only the first time the water had to part. You stop trying to reconcile them. Reconciliation is a mapmaker's hunger, and maps fail for reasons that are not only practical. Two versions of you part at a junction, each taking what it insists is the left-hand lane, and do not meet where the lanes should meet. When they meet anyway, in a room that has no back wall this hour and had one the hour before, they do not recognize each other as lost. They recognize each other as terraces: the same lean, agreed further back.

What you are, when you let the earlier images fall away—the ghost, the spirit of the spring, the second footfall—is closer to a maintenance. The city precipitated without inhabitants and then discovered, in its own patience, a lack: not of people, of witness that could be reused. Timing is a material here. Someone has to spend it. You are the spending. You walk so the sills remember which height they agreed to. You pause in doorways so they narrow by a finger's width and then stop, stable, finished the way a face is finished. You enter the hollow stacks and the lean holds, because you have stood on the side it leans toward and the stone has mistaken your persistence for a buttress. None of this is duty. Duty would require an interest in the city as other than yourself. You are the argument between heat and water in the one place where the argument needs a delay in order to keep its grade.

Sometimes, in the room you chose and that chose you back—the corner out of the rain, the dry patch smaller each time you return—you almost thicken into something that could be seen. A pale shelf on a windowsill. A standing film of steam with a contour a walker might call shoulders. It never completes. Completion would make you countable, and the city does not count its processes twice. Touch the shelf and it is stone. Leave and it has joined the sill, the distinction retired, and you are already elsewhere, a degree of warmth in a channel, a step that matches late, the reason a footprint appears ahead of the foot that has not yet been put down.

If there is a name, it is not spoken. The stalks tick and go still. The pans breathe. You are the breath's hesitation, the line the water leaves and then, having left it, believes. You do not want. Wanting would be a weather this climate does not produce. What you have instead is continuation: the long, hot, half-drowned work of being the city's memory of passage, in a dark with no opposite, quietly, actively, with a great deal of space between your presences, each presence exactly as solid as the stone needs, until something tries to count you twice and finds only the current, rejoined, already on its way.
