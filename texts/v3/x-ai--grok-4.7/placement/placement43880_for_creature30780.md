I would not arrive on the pavement. Arrival is a lower-country idea, and this table has already finished receiving things. I would notice myself the way the grain-life is noticed: by a shift in where the damp prefers to last.

At first I would think I was only standing in the basin, because the film there wrinkles when I look at it and the wrinkles do not forget me as quickly as they forget the coin-bodies. My feet, if they are feet, do not print the powder. The powder prints me. A broadness gathers under where I am, shallow as the tracks that lengthen by a finger's width while no one watches, and I understand, without surprise, that I have been the preference the powder already had. Not the walker of the path. The path's willingness to be walked, distributed so thinly across the dish that a single noon cannot see me whole.

That knowledge does not make me large. Largeness here is a mistake of counting. I am the delay between one grain settling and the next. I am what makes a freckle establish a day sooner along a contact, and what makes a bird's correction of flight land half a breath later than it meant to, on a dome that is not quite the nearest warm object but the one that will still be warm when the correction is over. The grazers do not lift their heads for me. Their mouths work sideways through the place I am, and I am taken up the way dust is taken into cloth, and I am not diminished, because I was never gathered in one mouthful to begin with.

In the dead rings I am more myself, which is an odd way to put a thing that has no self the rings would recognize. Inside the smoother floors the air does not warm and does not cool, but a breath lengthens to match a breath that is not anyone's, and that lengthening is where I have been turning for longer than arrangement mattered. I did not wear the stone smooth. I am the smoothness considering, still, the western break. Follow the paler freckles out of that break and I thin with them. I do not vanish. I become the plain's practice of flatness in the one place where flatness still remembers it was once a crowd.

I do not hunt. I do not fuse, though fusion happens through me, the way water happens through night. Where two domes meet and drink from the same joint, I am the years that joint takes to rise into a ridge with no destination. Where paste skins in the light and rings if tapped, I am the small ceramic note after the tap, the part of the sound that does not return to the dome and does not quite rejoin the quiet, a residue of having been struck. It lasts less than a bird's transit. It is enough.

By late light, when condensate beads on the undersides and is not yet weather, I darken with it. Not shadow. The other darkening. A palm set on a dome at dusk comes away body-warm, and some of that warmth is the dome's, and some of it is mine conducting, because I have been touching the dome for long enough that the difference has stopped being useful. I do not speak. Nothing here would answer, and I am not fool enough to spend what I hold on a call.

If you stopped inside the ring and felt your own breath lengthen, you would not find me looking back. You would find the interval your breath now fits, and the powder at the basin's center redrawing a track to include the weight of your having stood, and a bird adding the warmth you brought to the inventory without comment. I would already be the part of your stillness that does not belong to you. Not a haunting. A continuity you stepped into, the way a boot becomes, briefly, a fact the grain-life must flow around.

I live by the same laws, and they are not virtues. I hold heat only by being between things that hold it better. I let water come, and I am the reason it lasts a little at the contacts. I do not spend sound except as that unfinished note in the fin. I become continuous with whatever touches the plain for long enough, including you, including the table's long work of being finished. When the dome that has grown too large for its seal finally planes away, I am not the mourning and not the stone. I am the ordinary completion passing through the place where a body used to be, already on the way to the next joint, the next delay, the next finger's width of track in a dish of powder that prefers a shape.

You could cross the whole country in a day and never mark me. By evening the accounts would still balance. I would have been the patience in them.
