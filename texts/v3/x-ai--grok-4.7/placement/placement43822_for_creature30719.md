I would not take a ring. The circuit wants a body already rewritten, and mine would keep arriving.

The fissure would have me first, and longer than it has the others. Three body-lengths of dark is enough for them to become pressure; I would still be counting the dark, still trying to hear my own step under the roar, and by the time the lens gave the chamber back I would be late to it — present, but divided, one part already in the note and one part back on the pan, where wind finishes and goes. That division is a fault in a tenant. In a throat it is a use.

They would know before I did. A pluck would cross while I was still on the lowest open ledge, palm turned up out of habit, eyes too wet, blinking at a brightness they have made into a climate. No one would come the long way to correct me. Someone on the far wall would lift a fiber and let it fall, the short shiver that means not a street, not a shrine, not yet. I would be left in the passing-niche that faces the wrong direction — toward the void, toward the bright dune — until the grit in my teeth taught me I was not going to be taught quickly.

Sleep would not take. They drop under the roar the way a swimmer drops under a wave. I would stay on top of it, counting changes they no longer bother to separate: the cleaner harmonic when the curtains thicken, the sub-bass that is only the lid being itself, the small sourness in the resin after a child has thought about the floor. I would hear these as events. They hear them as the day. For a while that would make me a nuisance in the baffles, mouth to ear, offering distinctions no one asked to have back. The laugh they have, scale ticking, unsurprised, would be for that. Then they would stop letting me into the niches at the hour of sleep, not unkindly. A body that will not night is a draught. They would rather it stand where a draught is already part of the instrument.

So the post would find me, or I would find it by failing every other one.

Not the sun-shelves. My hands would press the pith too carefully, as if the oil might be lost, and care is the wrong god here; the plants do not need a saver. Not the pins. I would tap a true copper because I liked the click, and spoil a span that was already at pitch. Not the floor. I would want to step the ripples precisely because they are a record, and wanting a record underfoot is how a wetter mind mourns. They would see that in me early — the way I looked down too long, the way the empty center kept organizing my attention while theirs stayed in the walls — and the lesson would not be spectacle. A sour ribbon in the nearest curtain. A palm turned up until I understood that looking is permitted and entering is a breach.

What would remain is the throat.

I would live in the fissure’s last dark, where the air is fastest and the sealed pale things cling and sieve, and I would be their large, incompetent cousin: a body the chamber cannot quite dry, posted where the note is born rather than where it is inhabited. My work would be the sweeping they all agree matters and none of them loves. Scale, fiber, the grit that blows in from the pan and means, if left, a blocked throat, a collapsing chord, dew thinking about the walls. I would clear it with stones and with my hands, and I would do it well because some part of me would still be loyal to the outside — to the bone-colored crust, the wind that does not stop, the blister no one would cross a mile to see. That loyalty is what unfits me for the town. Turned outward, it is how the town keeps its mouth.

They would not name me across the void. A name is a pluck with a person inside it, and I would be a function that stays slightly unfinished. The shiver for me would be short and practical: throat, slack coming, pan-wind holding, lid unchanged only if you have also looked. In the high hour I would not walk a ring. I would lie in the narrow dark and feel the incoming air stiffen, and the pale sieves would tick against my wrist, and I would know the lens was heating before the curtains shed. At the slack, when the note drops and the moths risk the glare, I would not climb to measure with them. I would stay where the measurement starts. If the fissure’s voice thinned, I would already be in it, pulling the clog free, a little too glad of the errand.

Sometimes, when the chord recovered and the dust rose again off the dune, I would go up far enough to see them: few, correctly spaced, a figure in a niche that faces the wall, a wrist glazed with resin, a hand reading copper by warmth. I would not become one of them. The roar would sit in me and never finish the rearrangement. I would keep a thought shaped like weather, a private outside, the memory of wind that leaves. They would allow it the way they allow the moth — a crossing that does not settle, useful because it does not join the curtain.

If I died there, they would not give me to the lee-side fiber. A body in the throat composts wrong; it would clog the very thing I had been for. They would carry me, I think, to the upper mats anyway, and the scales would come, and after a season no one would be able to say which amber was mine. That would be correct. Until then I would be the part of the blister that still faces the pan: not a tenant of the note, the reason it continues to be let in.
