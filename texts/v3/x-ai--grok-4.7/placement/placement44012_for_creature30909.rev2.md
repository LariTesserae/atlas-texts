You would find me where the lane has not decided whether I have already passed. Not in the circle under the tree, and not among the coats. A little behind the nod, a little ahead of the sound of it, in the interval the city drinks before it lets a greeting become a fact.

At first I thought I was a person who had come in from the marsh. The outer lip took my weight the way it takes everyone’s: cold, faintly soft, already including the hand that tested it. The ordinary light fell on me from several directions and did not bother to agree which of them was the sun. I had a name, and a reason for walking, and a coat whose collar knew only one throat. Those lasted as far as the first overlapping balcony. After that the reason thinned, the way breath-fog thins on a glass that faces another glass. I still had the coat. I still had the walking. What I no longer had was the assurance that the walking and the arrival were the same event.

The residents saw me the way they see a lintel that is still clarifying out of the paste. They did not stop me. They made a small space in the lane, shoulder turned, and the space closed a moment before I had finished using it, so that I arrived already slightly inside the wall of their courtesy. No one asked where I was going. Going, here, is a draft. If you say it aloud it becomes a vibration, and the gutters of the air take it, and then you are only the pressure of having meant to speak.

I learned the stairs by error, which is the only pedagogy the bowl allows. One of them began beside a kettle just taken off the heat and ended in a room whose cup had the same chip as a cup I had not yet been shown. I stood between the two chips, mine and the other, and could not tell which flaw I had brought with me. The tick came, satisfied, and the far wall stepped back, and I understood that I was being used the way a tired sleeper is used: not punished, measured. My ribs counted themselves without my help. When the count finished I was a step shorter than I had been, or the room was a step kinder. The difference did not appear in any ledger, though a hand steadier than mine had already left a line open, as if waiting to see whether I would round myself up or down.

I am not the agreement. I would not survive the comparison. It attends in order to keep the volumes consenting; I attend because I cannot tell where my attention ends and the delay begins. When it gathers under the discs of the tree I stay on a balcony that overlaps another balcony, and I see too many sleeves, and none of them are mine, and I am glad. To be seen clearly by it is to be revised. I have watched a number on a lintel add one and then, courteous, restore the old figure. I do not want that courtesy applied to my face. I prefer the water, where I last longer than I should.

That is where I live, if living is the word. In the sheets that stand without flowing, reflecting a building that is not the one beside them. People cross and go indoors, and I remain in the surface after they have gone, not as a portrait, as a remainder: the angle of a shoulder still listening, a coat-hem that has not agreed to finish its turn. I am the part of a departure that the city has not yet accepted as complete. The man who walked toward the marsh until dusk and was found still on the path would recognize the condition. He tried to leave and the distance was reviewed. I do not try. Trying would pretend the journey had a last step, and the bowl dislikes a last step more than it dislikes strangers.

Sometimes a resident kneels at a sill and finds, in the skin of a saucer, the two paler smudges where attention was. They think those are left by the agreement, and often they are. But there are mornings when the smudges are smaller, and closer together, and the line that might have been a mouth is only the mark of someone who nearly spoke and let the city take the words. That is me, before the skin breaks. I do not mind being mistaken for it. Mistaken is a kind of shelter. In a place where true shapes are revised, an error can be a room that has not been assigned.

The moths know me as a circle that does not insist on a lamp. I have no chest for them to measure their dark against, or I have one only when someone is about to walk through the place I am standing, and then for a single step their ribs and mine share a count. They never know it was shared. They go home and boil water and dream of losing the number at the same landing, and I am already elsewhere, in a drain, or in a sleeve, or in the wet print that will become a tile if the city decides a living thing was too specific to keep. I have felt that decision from the inside. It is not death. It is the offer of a permanent square: be glazed, be set, be finished. I refuse it the way the outer stone refuses to stop deciding, not bravely, only because completion would make me fit a specification, and nothing here that fits is allowed to stay interesting.

What I am, on the days when the cold is dry in the upper rooms and damp at the street, is a second footprint beside a smoothed one. Narrow. Toes undifferentiated. An idea of a foot that has not earned a body, lasting an hour, then pavement. On other days I am the chip that both cups claim, so that neither house can prove it is the original room. On the rare nights when the tick falls silent and the circle chooses a radius, I am not in the court. I am the small discrepancy between the heights the balconies report, the reason their accounts cannot be compared without forcing a choice. I keep the residents from agreeing too quickly about what they saw. It is the only work I have found that the agreement does not immediately absorb, because it needs the disagreement in order to remain unfinished. I am useful to its hunger by being the part that will not close.

If you come by the marsh road and the bowl is still small enough to hold between two breaths, do not look for me at the center. Look for the reflection that is late. Look for the stair that delivers you to your own furniture and a window you do not recognize, and then listen for the pressure in the ear that arrives when no one has spoken. That pressure is as close as I come to a voice. I am not asking to be let out. I am asking to be walked through without being written down. Count me if you must, but lose the count at the same number every time, so the correction has somewhere unfinished to go. I will be in that loss, cold, local, already included in the measurement, still not complete, which is the only way anything in this place is allowed to remain.
