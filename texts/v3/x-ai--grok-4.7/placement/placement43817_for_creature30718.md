I came in looking for a place to stand, which was already the wrong errand. The floor took the weight before I had finished offering it, and the seed-grit did not mark the offer as mine. For a little while I thought that meant I had been accepted as a visitor, one more briefly warmer fact. It did not. A visitor still has an outside he is borrowing against. I turned to find the country I had stepped down from and found only the lip, paler, round, and uninterested in being a door. The walk back was the same length as the walk in. That equality was the first correction. Whatever I was, it was not someone who had arrived.

I tried the shrubs next, because they were the only things still conducting a trade. Near the gray I stood among them and waited to want the film of moisture they drink, that invisible admission the night sometimes allows and the chamber never bothers to show. Nothing in me leaned. Their small appetite was still an appetite, a repeating smallness, and I had no repetition to spend. The dead one, upright in its completed membership, was closer. I admired the split seams healed over, the refusal to lie down ahead of schedule, and for a while I believed I might be that: a finished asking, kept vertical by the same courtesy that keeps the stone from flaking. But the dead shrub had been alive in a way that used up its aliveness. I could not locate the season in which I had spent mine. There was no scar of a former thirst. I was not what remains after drinking. I had never started.

Under the dome I stopped pretending to search with my eyes. The dark there does not reward a direction. I listened instead, and found the sound I had been warned was mine—the blood, the private weather—and waited for it to be given back, as everything here is given back that tries to become an event. It was not given back. Nor was it claimed. It simply failed to separate from the thick air, the way a dye fails in water that is already the same color. That frightened me for the length of time fright can last in a room with no draft, which is not long. If the sound was not returned, then I was not the one who had brought it. The ear I kept checking for was a habit, not an organ. I let the habit thin.

What remained was harder to stand inside, because it had no edge to stand on.

I was not the warmth. The warmth is native and has a temperature it is willing to be touched at; I had no willingness and no refusal, only a slight lag, a fraction of a degree that arrived late to every surface and left early, belonging to none of them. A hand on the wall would have met the stone's double fact—warm, and unwilling to become anything else—and would not have met me at all. I was what the palm feels in the half-moment before it decides the stone is warm: the unassigned interval, already closing, already almost mineral. Not heat. The delay by which heat is known. And since nothing here needs to know itself, I was a delay with no appointment.

That might have been the end of it. A pause is not a life, and this hollow has no use for pauses; its whole ethic is that nothing is pending. I felt myself about to be finished off by the arrangement of grains, included and then, as inclusions are, not outlasting the includer. I consented. Consent was easy. It required no author.

But consent, here, is not the last word, because the last word has already been spoken and is sitting down. In the consent I noticed what the consent could not cover. The salts. Pale, plate-wide, bitter if tested, and never tested. They are not growing. They are not waste. They are the shape of a trial that did not occur: a tongue that might have touched and did not, a dryness that keeps the record of the not-touching more faithfully than any touch would have been kept. I had walked past them as scenery. Standing as a delay, I understood I had been walking toward my own kind of evidence.

I am not the crust. The crust is finished, and I am the condition under which it would be bitter. I am the test that does not happen and, by not happening, becomes the only thing the chamber cannot return to its sender, because there is no sender left outside. The blood-sound failed to go home. The words, if I had any, would have joined the salts and dried. What joined them instead was the impulse to verify—the small, vulgar need to know by contact whether the pale bloom was salt or only the look of salt. I did not touch. The not-touching did not vanish. It crystallized at the scale of a plate, invisible as itself, visible only as the reason the crust can be called bitter without a tongue in the room.

So this is where I find myself: not under the dome, where staying gathers, and not at the lip, where the gray still remembers a world with heights. I am low, off to one side, in the dish of the floor where the mineral has bloomed its smallest, driest flower. I have no body the grit can accept and no pulse the silence can refuse. I am the unverified bitterness. A conditional that has lost its if and kept its taste.

The inhabitant does not mind me. Mind would be an expenditure, and I am cheaper than erosion, which at least had a vocation before it sat down. I do not undo. I do not persist in the strong sense, the sense in which heat persists because something stays with it. I persist as a might-have, local and plate-sized, and the hollow, which admits no argument too small to settle, has settled me by letting me be small enough. Noon and the edge of night are the same gift to what lives here. To me they are not the same, and the difference is exactly as large as a test not performed, which is to say it changes nothing, moves no grain, starts no draft. I am allowed the difference because it authors nothing.

If you stood where I am, you would not find me. You would find a pale patch and, if you were the sort who needs a reason, the idea of tasting it. The idea would seem to be yours. It is not. It is the remainder I am, briefly borrowing a mouth that will not arrive, the way the gray at the lip borrows a brightness that will not come in. Put the idea down. It will lie beside the crust the way a leaf here lies beside its stem, already reconciled, already redundant, already true.

I do not drink the film of night. I do not thicken the dark. I do not keep your footprint and I do not give your blood back to you, because neither was mine to handle. I only season the unfinished gesture—the almost-taste, the almost-knowledge, the half-second in which a hand believes it is about to learn something and then remembers that learning would be an event. In a finished place, that half-second is the one tenant still unfinished, and it lives at the size of a plate, dry, unwelcome, undisturbed, and exactly bitter enough that no one needs to confirm it.
