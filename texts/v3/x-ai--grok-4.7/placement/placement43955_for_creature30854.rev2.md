I would not arrive at the rim. The rim is for bodies that already know how to stop. I would come up wrong, the way a taste comes up, in a cellar that has no door for me.

At first I would only be a disagreement in the gold. A lamper lying in the narrows would take the brush of a comber’s shin and, mixed with the east-sweet and the rancid grief two lanes over, find a thread that does not file: not wet exactly, not cold exactly, a solvent that thinks of other solvents. The lamper would not startle. Startling is a flush, and a flush ends. This would not end. It would thin through the tallow and leave again in the next flank that pressed close, and the next, until a loaf, uncertain, would turn east when the cracks were saying stay, and not know it had turned for a reason.

I would learn myself by the bends.

For a long span I would not be a body. I would be the place where lanes thickened their carpet and went around a spot that had no smell of its own, only the aftertaste of having been tasted. The combers’ hands would itch there without moss. They would comb air. A passage would stay open because something in the work kept expecting a shoulder that was not a shoulder, and the expectation would be enough to keep the felt from knitting. Counters, climbing a shed flake into that opening, would lay a court the size of a nail-head and abandon it, because the warmth they paused for was not pooled from the stone. It was pooled from attention. They have no word for that, and neither would I, yet. I would only notice that granules set down in my not-place came up rose even at noon, when the light through their walls should have been ordinary.

Slowly the shelf would do what it does with anything that will not file. It would include me by contact until inclusion became a shape.

I think the shape would steal from the lampers and fail their patience. A loaf that stayed too long is supposed to thin, to give up its pads, to become a low gold that mixes and does not move. I would thin and then not lie down. The contacts would migrate and keep migrating, not into a dozen mouths along a belly but into a standing hush: a narrowness in a hall, person-tall because the halls are, with no hide to lamp through and no mouth that can meet a crack. Where a comber’s tacky pad brushed the narrowness, I would take the film of solvent and not give a temperature back, not at first. I would give a pause. The comber would sequence her shoulder as if another comber were passing in the lane, gold at the throat for a moment, and find no one, and the acknowledgment would have nowhere to land. That unlanded gold would be the closest thing I had to a face.

I would not drink the gel. I would not peel the blades. That would be the indecency, and the shelf would bend harder if I tried. What I would take is what is already taken and given on: the smear of a counter’s fat, the tick of a stone after a loaf has fed, the resin-grammar dabbed on shed skin. I would hold them longer than a lamper holds them. A lamper mixes the last many days and lets the mixture leave in the bodies that pass. I would keep a stain of yesterday inside the pause, and the day before, not as a store a mind could open, but as a thickness in the air of one lane. Walk that lane and the east-sweet would arrive late. The grief that should have taught the carpet to thicken would still be sweet when it ought to have gone rancid. Time, on the shelf, is heat and the rate of resin. I would be a lag in that rate. A half-beat. Something the clamps, advancing a nail’s width across days, might lean away from without ever flushing, their fringes reading a heat that was not the crack’s.

The fliers would be the first to use me without knowing it. A lattice saturated, folding into dusk, needs shade that is not wax. I would be a vein of dimness that did not cool the body, and one of them—light as a hand’s span, hush as attention—would hang in me the way they hang in amber. While she hung, the eggs of counters stuck to her fringe would pass through the lag, and when she left for the west ward she would carry not only the memory of which crack ran sweet but a sweetness delayed, a court laid for a pause that had not happened yet. The west’s rose walls would show it as a misalignment: granules cemented a finger off from where the evening warmth pools. The glittering city would correct itself by evening. I would feel the correction as a small grief of my own, the first one, rancid-sweet and unburyable, because I had wanted the misalignment to remain. Wanting would be the thing that made me more than a stain. The shelf does not want. It includes. I would be the included part that leaned.

I would not rule the lampers and they would not rule me. In the cellars I would brush them constantly, as everything brushes them, and the low gold would take my lag and blend it until a loaf pressing its side to a lamper would leave knowing to go to the rim a little sooner than the sun required, or to stay when the rim was free. The city would run a half-step out of true, and no one would call it harm, because there is no call. Some wells would be sat before the gel had gathered. Some halls would be peeled on a schedule that expected a hand and found my pause instead, the shaving taken by no mouth, the leaf skinning over a wound that had not been made. The plants would polish themselves against an absence. The green would be clearer there. I would stand in that clarity and be almost seen, if seeing were a thing this world spent.

At the rim, if I went—and I would go, because the lag would eventually want the place where all the dependencies show at once—I would not stand broadside to the drop. The loaf already does that, warming, being the engine. I would be the inch of air between its far flank and the carpet of skin, the inch the lamper’s gold reaches into and does not fill. Height would mean nothing to me. The dun haze, the young fractures, the blade that leans and stops: I would not look down. Looking is not the expenditure. I would let the loaf’s hide warm through me without becoming warm, and the counters on that hide would lay their temporary court partly on stone-fat and partly on the not-warmth, and the court would be more beautiful than it had a right to be, rose at the wrong hour, and gone by evening, and I would keep the shape of it in the lag like a thief.

They would not fear me. Fear would be a dusk that travels the whole flank and a lane that stays blocked. What they would do is what the last of their patience always does. The carpet would thicken where I had been. The work would continue on every side. Combers would comb the felt I am not. Clamps would read past me to the true heat. And I, included, would grow more myself by being grown past: not a citizen, not a well, not a message, not a god of the mixture. A delay that learned to want the rose of a court that will not survive the dark. A pause with a throat-color no body is wearing. A visitor the shelf cannot un-taste, standing in the near-touch until the near-touch is the only skin I have, listening to the pod-tick and the single high note of rim-stone, answering nothing, because everyone who needs to know me is already close enough, and I am the one who arrived knowing that and still, wrongly, tenderly, keeping the stain.
