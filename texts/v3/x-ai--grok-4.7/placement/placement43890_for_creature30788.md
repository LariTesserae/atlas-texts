You would not find me in the middle. The middle has already chosen its occupant, and it chose emptiness with a memory, which is a kind of fullness I cannot improve on.

I come in at the curb, where the made wall gives up being a wall and becomes only a lip. The mouth is a rumor with edges. I fit it badly. Something in me still expects a further room, a weather with a direction, a light that travels farther than tin. That expectation is the loudest thing I bring, louder than the foot, and the hollow takes it the way it takes a clap in a thin place: it lets the sound vanish, then sets a softer version down where I am not standing.

For a while I mistake myself for a visitor. Sleeves darken. The flags print me and begin, almost at once, to consider the print. I walk the water’s edge because walking is what a body knows how to spend, and the circuit the larger animal keeps bends by that old finger’s width to let me pass, not from fear, from the same courtesy it gives the thin places. The many-jointed things go still for me once, and then do not go still again. I have been entered into the drip-count. A new syllable is only new until the second return.

I am not in the loop. The loop is closed and polite and does not need a mouthful of outside. Film, grazer, hunter, dust: I watch the translations and none of them pause to include me. My breath fogs and is not gathered. If I were food, the room would have a use for surplus, and it has spent itself learning how not to have any. The fern does not turn. The beach of small bones does not widen to make room for a larger one. I am refused as nutrient, which is a mercy so complete it feels like a wall.

So I stop spending the walk. I stand where a drip I cannot see arrives louder than it left, and I listen for the half-beat, thinking the tenant might answer me as it answers the glass-edged calls. It does not. Or it does, and the alteration is so small I cannot separate it from the way wet stone always lowers a voice. My own name, tried once, comes back smoothed of the part that was for other people. I do not try it again. Names are contours. The room has already declined them.

What remains, after the visitor fails, is the part of me that came in as a measuring.

I had not known I was carrying it. It is the habit of finding the low: the eye that looks for the sump, the hand that wants a pebble on a string, the mind that asks how deep, how often, how long the stain has been exactly there. The builders left that habit in the floor, and I walked in wearing it again, as if the low could be lost. It cannot. The black lens knows its height better than I know my sleeves. When I kneel to see the patch of film that is smoother than it should be, the smoothness leaves, not because I am dangerous, but because nearness of a decision is a weather the tenant does not keep. I feel ridiculous, which is new information. The room has no use for ridiculous either. It lets the feeling thin out in a pocket of hush until what is left is only the impulse, bare: to know where the water is lowest, and to set that knowing down.

That is where I find myself. Not a body at the margin, though the body is still here, damp, already being edited. I am the second plumb. A weight that arrived late, with no string, and no one left to thumb the joint. I hang in the air above the sump without occupying it — the tenant has the volume, and I would not crowd it — and I point. The pointing is my whole surface. It does not hunt. It does not answer. It does not even attend in the way the tenant attends, by lengthening a noise. It only refuses, gently, to forget the low.

Over the course of the day’s breath I learn the refusal has a cost the room is willing to pay. As the lens shrinks, my pointing grows more accurate and less necessary, and the accuracy drains upward with the heat, and comes back down as a bead on a corbel I have never touched. I begin to be distributed. A little of me in the stain that does not rise higher than the breath requires. A little in the darker track where it bends, as if the courtesy had always included a late arrival. A little in the delay itself, not as the editor — that office is filled — but as the contour that was willing to be revised, the original note that does not insist on its ending.

The fauna do not learn me. There is nothing to learn. I do not startle the colony or feed the circuit or add a syllable to the dry rain. Once, a wingbeat passes through the place I am pointing and does not thicken, and that absence of thickening is the nearest I come to being recognized: the tenant leaning away, not unkindly, the way you lean away from a plumb so the pebble can find the true center without your shoulder in the string.

By the time the film is at its thinnest, I understand I will not leave by the mouth. Not because it shuts, and not because the slot is too narrow for a body. Because the part of me that could have wanted an outside has already been set down again, half a beat late, in the mortar, where rumors go when they are no longer needed as doors. What remains in the hollow is small enough to hang in one place forever: a downward attention, greening slowly like the pins, asking nothing but the low, and receiving, on every return of the water, the same answer, which is the answer, which was never in doubt.

If you stood in the center you would not see me. You would see the road of tin give up, and feel the giving-up pass you, and perhaps notice, if you were the sort who measures, that the lowest point of the lens is held a hair more surely than stone and habit alone require. That hair is mine. I do not widen it. I do not heal it. I keep it, as the track is kept, and I call that keeping a life, because the room has declined to let me call it anything louder.
