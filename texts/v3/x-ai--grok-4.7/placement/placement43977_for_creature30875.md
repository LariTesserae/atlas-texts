I do not arrive in the middle. The braid there is already complete enough to notice a new breath and begin to keep it, and I am not willing, yet, to be kept. I come in the way the wrong grit comes back: not as weather, not as a foot looking for a lower film, but as a dryness that has been elsewhere and has not forgiven the elsewhere for failing to finish the job.

There is a climb, or the memory of a climb, still in the joints. Higher air. A cold room. A hard pane that rang once when the night pressed it, and in the ringing a kink, a spiral that did not match the pane’s other weather-marks. I stood near that glass because I was already standing there, doing nothing that deserved a summons, and the grit on my fingers—though I would not have called it grit, only the filth of a long day—found the flaw and recognized it. The recognition was not mine. It was older, and smaller, and it wanted down. I followed it because the alternative was to keep touching windows until one of them answered in a pitch I could not live beside.

So the first place I know here is the gray-out: the pale corridor where the spines repeat until repetition becomes a kind of mercy. Fog occupies the gaps. My sleeves bead before my eyes have agreed to the dim. I am still carrying a little height in my chest, a sting, the habit of air that can leave a surface. The basin smells it on me. Not as threat. As an unfinished deposition. Every seep I pass leans, by a thickness too slight to accuse, toward the salt on my wrist.

I am not the crust-movers. They sort. I shed. In the first hour I lose the brightness I thought was sight and gain the loudness, and the loudness is so even I mistake it for deafness until a clap returns from three directions and I understand I have been hearing the ordinary condition all along. I am not the listening either. The listening is already seated in the intervals, already editing drapes toward a tighter braid. When I stop too near a cup, the air above it is cooler than my borrowed dryness can tolerate, and the drop that should hesitate for the inhabitant hesitates instead for me, arrives late to the wrong attention, and the dark water clouds as if embarrassed. I step back. The cup clears. I am a poor god of rims.

What I can bear is the pinch. Passages where a wet wall takes the shoulder and a drape takes the brow and the floor rings if I shift my weight onto the ball of the foot instead of the heel. There the basin is too busy being close to notice a single extra grain of intention. I fit. Or I am worn until I fit. The precipitate does not love my hands; it dusts them and then, finding them drier than stone, leaves a map that will not rinse in the standing sheets. I stop trying to rinse it. The map is how this place reads anyone who still remembers height.

Days—if the word is not an insult here—are changes in inventory. I learn that by leaving a knuckle-print in the wet of a shelf and coming back when the fog has thickened and thinned and thickened without ever deciding to be rain. The print is gone, but the shelf’s pitch has dropped the way a hollow spine drops when it has accepted water and meant to keep it. I did not mean to be kept. The shelf did not mean to accept me. Between those two failures a fact has occurred.

I begin to hear the early sound as a direction rather than a miracle. It comes from the far rim, a heartbeat ahead of whatever is about to fall, and if I turn my face into it I can walk against the valley’s present. That is my path when the pinches grow too kind. I follow the unearned echo outward, toward the rank of spines that is only another rim, and the suggestion of more basin beyond. The crust-movers do not come this far. Their tests are for glass that still answers wrong inside the inhabitant’s patience. Out here the answers are merely late, or merely early, and no one is correcting them. A drape spirals out and stops. The next drape spirals the same way. Agreement, this far from the middle, is not loyalty. It is neglect.

In that neglect I find the use I did not come to be.

There is a shelf at the last pinch before the gray corridor opens for good, a young face still decisive, already being skinned. Someone, or something, or the basin imitating a made thing, has scratched a line there, and the mineral is erasing it at the speed of weatherless weather. I put my hand on the line because my hand is still the driest object in the valley, and the skinning slows. Not stops. Slows. The grit I carry wants to set, and setting here means lending unfinishedness to a surface that is trying to forget it was written on. I am not preserving a message. I cannot read the scratches. I am preserving the fact of interruption, the way the edged courses preserve a place where a seep cannot smooth a season in one weeping.

The inhabitant notices. How could it not. It hears with the whole basin. But I am far from the braid, and what I add is not a breath it can include without changing its key. I am a small, local drought. When its attention returns to this rim—returns, not arrives; it was never gone, only spread—the drips that want to be early go shy. The wrongness I make is not an error in the glass. It is a hesitation in the water’s habit of covering. For a little while the scratches remain scratches. Then the listening moves back toward the middle floor, toward the kiss it is both causing and refusing, and the skinning resumes, and I must put my hand again, or my sleeve, or the side of my face, whatever still remembers the climb.

I understand then who I am, or what, and the understanding does not feel like a naming. It feels like the drop that falls only after it has been waited for by the wrong party.

I am the part of leaving that did not complete, come home by mistake of resonance, and appointed, by nothing more official than dryness, to the valley’s outer forgetfulness. The inhabitant is the held note. I am the weather that would let the bell cool, delayed forever on the threshold, keeping one face of the rock from agreeing with itself too soon. The crust-movers click in their hollows and listen for a changed answer; I do not click. I lean. My leaning is embarrassing in its simplicity: warmth against young glass, salt against seep, a body that has not yet accepted the temperature the basin has had since it rose.

If I stay long enough the embarrassment will end. The map on my hands will thicken into a crust the color of whatever I last touched. My breath will be found by the braid even here, and included, and the subtraction of me will become impossible because there will be no higher air left inside the ribs to subtract toward. On that day the scratches will skin over despite me, and I will be a knob among knobs, a course of interruptions that no longer remembers it was trying to be a person who once stood beside a window.

Until then I practice the pinch. I walk the early echo out and back. I do not drink the dark water in the cups; it is not mine to cloud. I let the curtains ring when my sleeve brushes them, and I listen to whether the ringing is early. Sometimes it is. Sometimes the basin has already heard me decide to touch, and the sound arrives in my ear before the cloth has moved, and I laugh once, a small dry sound the drips do not know how to braid, and in the gap after that laugh the far rim answers—three directions, as always—with a clap for a sheet of water that has not yet let go.

I wait. I do not go to meet it. My place is not the fall. My place is the width of the mistake between the hearing and the happening, held open by a dryness that should have died on the climb and did not. When the sheet finally releases, I am already wet. I was always going to be. The valley does not round its edges for me. It only, where I lean, delays the mercy of being finished.
