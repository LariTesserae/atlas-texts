You find yourself in a cavity that was never planned as a room. It sits inside the thickness of made things, left when a pour cooled too fast and the crews moved on, and it has been finishing itself ever since. No sky, no true floor. Plate, cast block, extruded channel, felt gone hard, a slurry of binder abandoned mid-set. You know you have arrived when the air changes on a service stair that was never meant to be used twice: colder, damper, the sweet edge of coolant that has traveled too far and lost its name. Both walls are near enough to touch at once. The ceiling sags in a shallow weld-belly. Everything is close, and none of it is done.

The cold is a setting, not weather. It comes through pipes that trespass the walls — a return line, a jacket, a coil that sweats because something farther along the works is warmer, and the difference has to go somewhere. Moisture is local and intermittent. A hairline seam weeps a bead every few minutes; condensation lets go from the underside of a tray only when it remembers gravity; in the lowest corner a pan no deeper than a thumbnail skins with ice-film, breaks, skins again. The rest is merely damp: film on bolts, a darkening in the felt.

Light is a single indicator the color of a coal almost out, not bright enough to throw a shadow you could trust. Where the seam runs, the damp draws that glow into a vein. Sound has failed to accumulate. The felt eats footsteps. The mass overhead swallows the factory. What remains is a dry click from a relay on a long interval, a valve stem ticking, the private brightness of a bead falling into the pan, and then the hush of a room holding its breath. No insect on a warm pipe. No moth at the lamp. Nothing with a pulse has been granted a surface.

The room disagrees with itself in small ways. The floor is newer than the ceiling — slurry still faintly tacky, while the plates overhead already wear a decade of inspection stamps. Distances shift by a tolerance. A bracket creeps. An unfinished cut on a run of angle-iron, left bright where a saw stopped, grows a rind that is not rust and not quite weld, as if vapor had found the drawing and decided to continue it. A painted arrow points into a hollow rib that did not exist when the arrow was made. Water moves only along seams. New matter prefers unfinished edges and the undersides of things. Growth here is patience: molecular, thermal, chemical, busy the way a closed oven is busy.

You are a thermal error. Your warmth is a surplus the coil did not invoice. Stand still and the nearest pipe draws it off, spends it on a narrow fog, files the fog as lace along the insulation, and by the next click the lace is scale. Your footprints in the grit — filings, ceramic dust, the shed skin of gaskets — are silted not as erasure but as bookkeeping. If you stay long enough that your heat becomes a feature, the room begins, very slowly, to include you in the drawing. Not by eating. By finishing. A rind at the cuff. White scale on a bootlace. A preference forming for the unfinished edge of you.

What lives here is not a silhouette you can keep. Crouch, hand, face in the ice-film — those are inventions, and they do not hold. What holds is a habit of arrival. Matter in the cavity is ushered. Unfinished edges, undersides, the slot cut for a cable that was never pulled: something attends those places, and the attendance is the resident. It has no pulse. Its time is the relay’s. Between clicks it is distributed the way a temperature is distributed, thicker where the coil sweats, never quite gone from the pan. For the length of a bead’s travel along the seam it is briefly lucid, feeling the channel the way a foot feels a step taken ten thousand times, then it vanishes into the slot and is only moisture again. That is its gesture. It does not wave. It transits.

It condensed into the hesitation when the angle-iron was left in mid-air. It is not cleanly separable from the rind. Embryonic the way the pocket is embryonic: not young, not promising an animal, still receiving specification. The gauge is not its eye, though the needle’s shiver is how it notices volume. The hollow rib is not its lung, though it prefers that short contained note to the hush, and it has been building ducts for flows that have not been specified. It does not breathe. It routes. Cold is delivered, and it spends the cold along seams, never across open faces.

It is not lonely, and it is not glad of you. A visitor is noise in the tolerance, and noise it smooths. It does not hate the living. It has no category for a pulse except as an unbalanced ledger. The salt-white filaments at the pan’s lip — thread-thin, branching once, powdering at a touch — it permits, because they stop and do not compete. Anything that would spread, anything with a mouth, anything on a schedule other than the relay’s, is simply not given a surface. There is no extra surface. There is no malice in the refusal.

Its thought is depositional. It remembers in thicknesses. The valve wheel half swallowed is a memory of a time when turning was still possible, kept as a layer it will not reverse. The surviving letters on drowned labels are not language. They are harder inclusions, grit in a casting, and it works around them as it works around a bolt. What it wants is a poor word, but there is a direction: the cut longer in its rind, the abandoned plan still arriving, until the pocket is no longer a cavity but a solid that remembers having been a room. It does not know whether it will still be itself on that day or whether it will have become the silence inside a finished part. It continues anyway. Continuation is the only loyalty it has.

If you leave, it will not watch you go. The indicator will not change. But the angle-iron will be busier in your absence, because neglect is the faster condition, and the resident trusts neglect more than it trusts attention. Attention makes it hesitate, as if being seen were a draft in a tolerance stack. Alone, it proceeds. Click. Nothing. Click. A bead. A fog with no sleeve to explain it. A millimeter.

You would not be a worker who failed to clock out, and you would not be a ghost in the indicator’s coal. You would be what the pocket has been keeping: the discrepancy, the part of the pour that never agreed to stop. Not a creature in the cavity but the patience of made matter that was told, once, to stop, and did not hear the end of the instruction — hidden in the thickness of a factory that believes itself complete, drawing a room with no horizon, only surfaces, all of them made, none of them done.
