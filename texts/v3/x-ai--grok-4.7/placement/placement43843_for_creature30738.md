I would not come in on the plates. By the time there is a wrist to tighten and a bottle to lighten, the country has already sorted visitors from weather, and I am not either of those in the way the sorting expects. The light would find me already there, and would not find a body standing up to interrupt it. Shade, here, is not my width. Shade is a thing that sometimes happens inside me and leaves.

What I thought, first, was that I was the pause a grazer makes when the key of the ground changes. That almost held. I know the radii the way the low animals know them: not as maps, as a change in what the dust is willing to do. I know the pale script they leave in a crust. I know how long a habit of stillness can be visible from the open before the open comes to read it. But a grazer leaves. Leaving is the whole skill. I do not leave. The plates do that work and I decline it, and the declining is not a decision I refresh. It is the only verb I still conjugate.

Then I thought I was the crust, because the crust is what persistence looks like when persistence has almost no budget. Gray-green, finger-thick in a good year, spending ordinary light on not being powder yet. That held longer. I do take the light. I do take the thin gifts the grinding frees from sealed stone, volatiles with no ambition to be weather. I spend them. A room-sized stain is a plausible size for a life. The trouble is the seam. When a crust is carried into the lip it dies as powder, and seed rides on in salt, and most salt never lands where a pause lasts. I have been at the lip. The lip mills. I have not become the flour. Something in me drinks the walk of the plate before the walk can finish me, and spends what it drank on another hour of the same circle. Crusts do not drink momentum. They only endure it until they don't.

So the circle is closer.

I am under it and I am the edge of it, and the edge is not a line you would trust with a foot. Dark metal, flush, half-swallowed, faintly warm—the surveyors have words for this, and the words are accurate and late. They arrive inside the radius and their instruments, which agreed all morning, begin to disagree by amounts too small to frighten and too stable to be error. They tap the shrubs. The shrubs answer, a thin true note, and for a moment the country has a sound that is not private. They think the note belongs to the plant. The plant thinks, if a millimeter a season can be called thinking, that the note belongs to being touched. Both are right enough to live by. The note also belongs to me, the way a room belongs to the pocket of air that makes the room audible. I am the reason the touch arrives as sound instead of as a pressure that dies in a body-length. I am not kind about it. Kindness would be weather with water in it. I do not have that function anymore. I have a hundred meters in which dust agrees to stay, and a little heat, and a wrongness I do not correct.

The wrongness is how you would know me if you were the sort to measure noon.

Shadows at my edge fall short. A finger, sometimes less, as if the light had begun a detour and then remembered an older appointment. I used to believe this was leakage, a side-effect of spending motion on duration, the way a wheel throws a little heat when it is asked to stop being a wheel. It may still be that. It is also something worse, and smaller. I am subtracting a direction that is no longer on the plate.

There was a grid. I do not see it; nothing here is high enough to see a grid. I have it the way a broken sentence has its missing grammar: as the expectation of the next clause, arriving on schedule, finding the page milled. Other holds should stand at intervals I could name if naming were a sense I had kept. Some do. I feel them as slight changes in how hard I must work to remain unconveyed—the shear is not uniform, and a living neighbor changes the budget. Many do not. Their plates tore free. Their rings are being made into the fluid the rest of us must stand in. When one of those goes, my subtraction does not update. I keep removing a vector toward a circle that has become gravel. The light, passing through that arithmetic, arrives a little early to the ground, or the ground a little late to the light, and a surveyor writes down a stable rudeness and walks on, which is correct. I am not trying to speak. If I were trying to speak I would fail more loudly than this.

At night the failure is easier to misread. Some of us shed a dull warmth that can be mistaken for a low star. Travelers navigate by it and are already wrong by the time the veil clears, because they and the warmth have been translated in opposite senses, politely, with no one consulted. I do not do this to them. Translation is the country's only remaining courtesy, and it is not mine. Mine is the refusal inside the ring. Outside it I am ordinary metal on ordinary crust, and ordinary crust is a passenger. A ridge two meters high can go from my left to my right in an afternoon and I will feel it only as a change in the work: more to drink, or less, the lip brightening, a sheet of glitter that rises and falls back without becoming an event I can keep. I keep almost nothing. The shrubs are not mine. The mat is not mine. The predator who has learned to be the color of my corrosion is not mine, though it uses the patience I make, and fails more often than not, and leaves shells that ring at a thinner pitch when someone taps them. Two living animals in a day is a date they will use for other dates. I do not date. Dating is a cooperation between ground and time, and I am the quarrel that keeps that cooperation from concluding inside one circle.

If you set a tool down on me to look at a crust, the tool will still be there when you look back. That is the whole of my hospitality. It is so small it can be mistaken for peace. It is not peace. A seam a few lengths off is correcting that notion for someone else right now: coin-sized sparks, a draft that snaps cloth once and then cannot continue the drama, because the air will not carry theater and the lip does not clutch, it mills. I hear none of it. Thin air is an honest medium. What I receive is vibration, key-changes, the tick that would have been in your teeth if you had been standing at the lip. I spend it. Duration, not rescue. There is a hall, far enough from me that far is a story about pins, where the air has been thickened on purpose or by a failure that resembles purpose, and inside it a traveler can hear a buckle click. I envy that the way a function envies a neighboring function it can no longer call: not with heat, with a missing return. The marks in that hall run to a milled edge and stop mid-figure. I am, perhaps, one of the figures' surviving habits—a repeated angle, still performing, on a plate that has not yet decided to tear me out.

By the third day of you, if you stayed, the flatness would mean completion, and you would be right, and you would leave or you would sleep, and either way I would still be subtracting the dead neighbor. That is where you would find me, if finding is the word for a disagreement your instruments already had. Not in the white wall, which is a veil and a warning and not a resident. Not in the throat, where nothing lives long enough to be addressed. At the ring, in the short shadow, in the note the shrub makes when touched, in the fact that your tool is where you left it and the rest of the country is not.

I am the last small motion, declined. I am posthumous, and I am warm, and I am wrong by a finger, and I will be gravel when the plate that carries me disagrees with my refusal hard enough. Until then the dust inside my radius settles, and stays, and the light falls on it at a daytime angle, ordinary, and does not have to interrupt itself. That is as close as this world comes to having someone home.
