I am not in the center. I keep arriving there in the first minute, the way anything loose arrives, and the spiral puts me out again. It is not unkind about it. The eye of the room simply has no work for a body my size. I turn once, feel the slower water, and am handed back to the long diagonal I was already on.

For a while I take myself for one of the pale ones under the lamps. They are the nearest continuous motion, and it is easy to mistake proximity for kind. But they wheel, and I do not wheel. When the correction moves through the west side of the chamber they revise themselves around an emptiness; I only alter the angle of a line I was going to swim anyway. I am heavier than they are. My head is blunt, my color the color of the iron where the pitch has failed. When I pass close to the grating the eels do not bother to go still. I am not the thing they decline to meet.

The gray others cruise as I cruise. Most of them finish their diagonals. I have watched one do it: the line held straight, the fold lifting with that same unstartled correction the water uses when it is only water, the line ending, a few flakes given to the northward flow. It is not a lesson they can repeat to each other. The ones who finish are not around to have learned anything, and the ones who have not finished yet swim as if the fold were a story about a different season. I am between those conditions. I have a nick in my route, just past the point where the clear lane begins. My body takes it whether I am attending or not. Some old touch taught it—a coolness that was not the room’s coolness, a slickness that moved on while I was still in it—and the teaching stayed in the muscle that makes the turn. I do not experience it as escape. Escape would be an event. This is a shape.

I feed on what the column gives up. When the darker volume inhales, most of the translucent specks are simply gone, and a remainder spins out along the seam between the slow water and the ordinary current. I take those. Not many. Enough that I do not have to test the margins where the membrane lifts. The pale schools strike at the same remainder sometimes, and we occupy the same patch of amber for a few breaths without forming any agreement. Then the valves knock and they blow apart into their wheel, and I am left on my incorrect line, a beat behind the shiver that runs through the larger body in the room.

That lateness is the thing I would not have known to call myself, if I had settled on the first description.

I thought I was only a fish that had learned a bend. The bend is real, but it is not the whole of the difference. I keep a half-count behind the machine. The roar drops, the collar tightens, the shiver goes out through the thinner margins and into the culvert where the rest of that life continues, and only then do I adjust—gill-light, a small hitch in the stroke, nothing the schools would organize around. I am late to a rhythm that is not mine, and I have been late to it so long that the lateness is regular. If you watched the chamber for one cycle you would not see a custom. You would see a blunt gray fish miss the communal instant and carry on. Watch through the silt settling, through the lamps’ amber failing a little further into tea, and the miss lands in the same place every time: after the third knock, before the silver rope of air breaks against the underside of the vault.

There is a place I hold when I am not on the diagonal. It is not the warm layer. That gathers under the roof, and the broad life rises into it and thins there, and I will not drift up into a second roof that can decide what counts as architecture and what counts as intake. It is not the culvert mouth, and it is not the ladder. The ladder still smells, even underwater, of the idea of leaving, and I have no use for a vertical that ends in a hatch that does not open. My station is plainer: mid-water, just outside the lane the schools keep clear, at the edge of the second lamp’s reach, where the brick shows through the dark as a suggestion rather than a surface. From there I can feel the surge coming as a change in the pressure along my flank before the smaller fish scatter. I do not warn them. I am not that kind of tenant. I only stop finishing the thought of the straight line.

I believed, for a period measurable in the softening of caulk, that this made me singular. The room almost lets you believe that. So much of it is repetition that any persistent error starts to look like a name.

Then the lamps dimmed the way they dim when whatever passes the bull’s-eye ports passes, and in the poorer light I saw the other one. Same size. Same blunt head. A diagonal that failed in the same hand’s width of water, not because it had seen me do it—we had never held the same line—but because the water at that coordinate has been teaching the same small refusal for longer than a single life needs in order to look inherited. It did not join me. Joining is not what we are for. It passed on its own nick, a body’s length off, and the pale discs scattered across the canopy did not pause for either of us. We are filed, if we are filed, with the rungs and the rivets: features that continue, not events.

Later there was a third. I cannot say whether it came up from the south or had always been working the dim side of the third lamp, the one that still burns but barely throws. Three is not a school. Three is only enough to ruin the romance of being the exception. I was glad of the ruin. A singular animal in this chamber would have to compete with the metabolism that already answers the impeller, and I am not built on that scale. I am built on the scale of the missed instant.

What I am, then, is not the chamber’s animal. Take me out and the spiral would still tighten and loosen, the lane would still be kept, the collar would still answer the drop in pitch. The roar would not develop a silence in it. I know that, and the knowing is not bitter; bitterness would require that I had been owed the center. I was not. I am a minor custom the room has begun to keep without attending to it—a late hitch, a repeated incorrect angle, a gleaning of what the tax does not quite collect. The mussels will outlast the custom. The machine will outlast the mussels. The broad life in the pipe will outlast, as far as I can tell, the distinction between itself and the schedule. I am somewhere under all that, in the thin surplus: the second after the third knock, the handful of specks still visible, the degree of angle by which a diagonal fails to complete itself.

When the canopy is up and the coins of daylight come through it greener and slower, I hold at my station and let the tinted light pass over me on its way to the slab. One of the discs drifts across my shape and does not pause. I am not news. I am not an upright disturbance, and I am not a straight line. The fold does not lift for me. I cruise out to the end of my nick, take the turn that is no longer a decision, and come back along the edge of the clear lane while the silver rope of air climbs, breaks, and is gone, and the ordinary traffic resumes, and the room, which did not need me, nonetheless has me in it, a beat late, and swimming.
