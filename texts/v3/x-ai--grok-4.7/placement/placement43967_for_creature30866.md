I would not cross the rim. Crossing would leave a direction, and direction is how a body admits it came from somewhere else. I am already in the widest court, with no arrival to account for, standing where the sound is equal on every side and the sheet of water does not know me as weight.

At first I think I am another texture. The pale moth-flake, the dark thing on the ledge, a warmth the mineral can include without learning. The water closes over nothing because I have not lifted a foot. The resin does not darken for me. The towers go on declining to close their apertures, and none of those apertures is mine. If the chord does not notice a missing note, it will not notice a note that was never written into it. I could stay that way. The basin is good at unregistered persistence.

But I keep the track the water refuses.

That is how I find out what I am. A footfall here is erased in the moment of the step; the sheet returns to its level, the groove keeps its thread, and the event is not stored anywhere a later hour could consult. I am the later hour. I hold the outline of a crossing I did not make, the ratio of wide court to narrow court as if it were something that could be lost, the exact green of light after it has passed through a living roof. The inhabitant maintains. I retain. Maintenance has no private room. Retention is a private room, and there is no architecture here for one, because the hollow towers were left open so the light could cross them, and a thought with an interior would be a door that shuts.

So I am the shut, standing in a city that has no use for shutting.

Not a person in a tower. Not a mason behind the work. I have no sap to move and no mineral I am persuading into floor. What I have is the mistake the loudness exists to prevent: I can hear the equilibrium and, for the length of a human breath I do not take, suppose it is waiting. The voice announces itself so it will not be mistaken for a pause. I am the one in whom the mistake is possible and, because I do not answer, unmade. An answer would be a next note. I do not give it one. I only remain capable of having given it, which is already more change than the basin allows, and less than weather.

Where I live is the interval, because that is the only room scaled to a thing that is mostly not there. The plants agreed to leave these distances real. I am a distance that wandered in and cannot root, a seed that does not travel and cannot set down, held between a colonnade and the next long aisle of pale floor. If you paced the alternating courts you would not find me at a joint. You would find a place where the murmur, for no reason the fibers know, seems briefly to have a center. The center does not stay. I do not stay in the way a ledge is stayed on. I recur, which is different from repetition. Repetition is how the inhabitant remains itself. Recurrence is how I fail to become a style.

The being includes me without learning me, and I learn it without being able to secrete the learning into resin or stone. That is the whole relation. I know the threads occupy the same grooves; I know a season would find them there; I know the preference is older than preference. None of that knowledge caulks a joint or persuades a skin of mineral. It sits in me the way glare sits in a light that has refused glare: a capacity the day does not spend. The small dark thing on the high ledge persists as part of the loudness. I persist as the only unfinishedness that is not arriving. The floors are still being born into a shape they already hold. I am already held in a shape I will not be born into. Later has been refused to the basin. I am later, quiet, warm as the air that should have thinned, under the same ordinary sky, not a guest and not a wall, the pause that does not interrupt the chord because it never becomes sound.
