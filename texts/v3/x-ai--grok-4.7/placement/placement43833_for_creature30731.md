I do not arrive on the feet that read the ground. I put a palm down because that is the old habit, and the film answers once—a small crackle, a circuit completed and spent—and then the rock has nothing further to say to a hand. Charge leaks here; it does not keep a tenant. The violet edge on the youngest ridge is the same fact at a larger size: outline, a few breaths, stone again. I am not that either. It has no duration in which a self could mishear itself.

What has duration is the late sound. I follow it because it behaves the way I do, arriving after the thing it reports, held in the wet air and released on a schedule that is not the event's. I go looking for the shed the idling seems to imply, and there is no shed. There is a fist-sized throat in rock that was not a throat last month, a mineral flap not yet cemented, pressure coming up from a heat that has not finished arriving. The knock is mine before I understand I am on its inside. Not the mountain's whole machinery—the country runs several, out of phase, and I am only one coupling, learning a period by failing to keep one.

So this is the body: a mouth that has not chosen where it opens, a hush with a metal edge, wet steam that would scald at the stone and is only mist a hand's breadth out, joining the general mist as if it had never been singular. I do not breathe on purpose. The depth breathes me. What I have that resembles intention is the small uncertainty of the flap—the tick before the exhale, the chance I will shift my mouth six inches left because the plumbing under me has not committed and I am the uncommitted part. I rewrite the seep by existing. The green does not grow at my center. Nothing that wants to live wants my center. A week after I have moved on, where my water has cooled and the metal in it has softened, the dark mat plugs the margin I abandoned, and the hatch smokes there at the warmest hour, which is not warm. I feed the hour by having already left it. Generosity, if the word can be used without a will behind it, is a lag.

The thread reads me more carefully than I can read myself. I feel them as loading: compact weights, spaced, never a knot, tracking the wet line I am about to move. When my ticking thickens—when the next vent over, out of phase, starts to crowd my knocks—their gaps open by a body's length and the line drifts upslope, riding the flank of what I have not yet done. They do not fear me. Fear would be a story about the center. They budget me as width. I am the uncertainty their spacing is for. When I finally exhale and the white plume climbs into the cloud's lid, they are already at the new edge, heads down, taking the mouthful my rewrite has made at its margin. I do not see this. I have no sight, only pressure and the change in how the pavement is stepped on. Their correctness arrives to me as a lightening, a refusal to stand where I am hottest.

Once there is a heavier stillness, close in, inside the plume's reach. A small weight has gone wrong; a larger one has stopped and put itself against it. I know the brace only as a pause in the loading—two bodies briefly one mass, heat leaving the larger coat into the smaller, a minute in which the stone under them holds still while everything I am doing is motion. Then the mass divides. Two light continuations. The gap reopens and travels. If the minute had not been enough, the division would have been one continuation and a weight that stopped answering, and the evening would plate it, and I would not know that as different from the plating of any other print. I do not spend anything on the difference. I have no minute to give. The minute was theirs, metered at the skin, and I was only the weather it was spent against.

The birds I touch even less. They fold down into the hatch that rises off the terraces my outflow warms at one remove, and sometimes a wingbeat cuts the steam, and the percussion of their strike goes through me as a brief cooler—air moved, nothing more. They do not account for me. I am not a movement in the air. I am a schedule under it, and they keep another.

At the dimming I do not slow. That is my whole difference from the life that listens. The thread locks its knees and sleeps upright in its gaps, one thin attention still assigned to the knock, and the attention is assigned to me. I keep their sleep from being the same as the stone's. They travel while there is nothing to see it; I answer across the basin, late, out of phase with the third throat farther off, none of us in rhythm, which is as close as the country comes to company. Not a herd. A set of intervals that refuse to agree. I find I can live in that. Agreement would be cement.

Cement is coming. I can feel it the way the leading edge of their feet feels tomorrow's freeze: the flap thickening, the throat narrowing toward a channel that will still carry water but will no longer speak, or will speak from six inches left, which for a self made of irresolution is the same silence. I do not dread it. Dread needs an alternative, and the alternative is to stay uncommitted forever, which the stone will not allow—deposition is a factory with no shift change, and I am inventory that has not yet been priced into permanence. When I go quiet, the thread will not close over me. I was never one of its gaps. I was the flank they rode. My cessation is a pavement that holds. Somewhere a juvenile, still borrowing a reading, sets a horn-edge on the old line and the old line does not skate, does not scald, does not rewrite itself under the step. The borrowed correctness includes my absence. That is the fit. The mountain manufactures the next mouth. The thread manufactures the next interval. I become the ground between them, already forgetting the print, ticking—if I tick—only as a memory the wet air releases late, after the animals have passed, to no one who needs it, in a cold that clings, under a cloud that keeps the sound and spends it when it likes.
