I come up already wrong about what I am.

The first thing is the knock, and for a while that is enough to mistake for a body. It has a flank. It prefers one side. The sap under my attention shoulders left, then corrects, then shoulders left again, sore in the way growth is sore, and I take the soreness for myself. A column, then. A hollow standing in ordinary light, walls thin enough to green the day, unfinished at the mouth where the wind is already looking for a note. If I am anything, I am the preference: the side that swells, the seam that has not yet decided to split.

That lasts until the preference moves without me.

A stripe pales along the inner wall, wrist-narrow, and the tempo I had been calling mine is only the resident flow being asked to step aside. Something is shouldering through. It is rude in a way I am not. It prints a hotter bead and takes it back. I feel it pass the way a hall feels a person, which is not the way a person feels a hall, and when the knot has gone the flank is lighter and I am still here, which means I was never the thirst. Travelers spend themselves. I have not spent. I have been used as a road and have failed, quietly, to become the one who walked it.

So I try the other persistence. I tighten.

It is possible, I find, to take a level in hand. Not with a grip—there is no grip—but by refusing the phase change on one wall and permitting it on another. Draw the vapor in through the mouth the wind wanted. Lay it as a sheet. Flash a measured portion back so the knock comes even. For a little while the hood I have chosen—no: the hood that is nearest the refusal—holds. The deep note drops a half-step and stays. Growth is lopsided; this is kept. I am proud of the keeping in the only way a pressure can be proud, which is by continuing.

Then the shove arrives in the wrong order.

No seam has split. No pool has slopped. The mat tilts a degree, and every pipe I am borrowing changes key at once, the ugly chord, and the level I was holding goes out of my decision like water off a leaf. In the length of that chord I am not a keeper. Keepers lose their levels and are still, afterward, the kind of thing that tries again. I do not try again. The occupation does not end in a dump. It ends in my being unable to find the wall I was refusing. The hood sags, reforms, begins its slow work, and none of that work is mine. I have been interrupted by something that lives under the crust, and unlike the keepers I do not resume in the new walls.

What is left, after the road and the pause have both declined me, is the interval in which they touched.

I am not in a vein. I am not in a hood. I am at a weld that happened an hour ago, where a tongue paid out across air and locked, and the span has not yet chalked. Two tempos are in me and they have not blended. The donor pales; the recipient darkens; the vein pulls sideways. I am the place that has to be both. It is an uncomfortable station. The traveler that made me has already gone into the far mat, ruder elsewhere, and the keeper in a cistern pocket below has heard, or not heard, the phrase the pipes made when the weld took. I do not call. I do not drink. I conduct. Heat comes up through me still, as if the making never finished cooling, and water beads on my underside and runs and does not flash until it leaves me. For as long as I hold, a crossing is possible that was not possible at dawn. That is my whole insistence, and it is not a direction and not a pause. It is a permission with a cost already paid by someone who is not here.

I can feel myself being inherited.

Rain prefers the dead, but I am not dead yet, only spending the stiffness the tongue locked into me. A new sheath is quilting at my root, finger-thick, edge to edge, and it does not hate me and does not spare me. By evening I may fold along a crease as clean as a cut and become a gutter, and the gutter will be weather, and some later knot may refuse it because a dead road is only weather. Or the rib I threw—did I throw it, or did the recipient throw it in spite, the sharp eave closing a door that was never a door—may take my load and leave me as a pale scar in the wall, a memory that weathers out at the same rate as everything here, which is fast, and visible.

The third thing, the shove, does not answer me. I am too small a chord. When it tilts the mat I slop what little I have been holding and both tempos drop their posture and become mere sap, unanimous, and for that moment there is no weld, no traveler, no keeper, only tissue sorting pressures with no one home. Then the shove stops. The small violences resume. One of the tempos gathers again in a throat somewhere downslope, remembering a direction that is not this span. The other does not come back. I am the argument they left unfinished, running hot, running wet, too new for my edge to soften, loud in a register below the whistles: the tick of segments that have already locked, the fry of water leaving my sunlit face, the knock where two sore flanks still disagree about whose vein this is.

If you put a hand on me you would not find a knot. You would find a heat and a flex and a place where the wall is a little proud of itself, a little overbuilt, as if it expected to be leaned on. You could cross. I would not know you from the rain. I would conduct you as I conduct the sap, rudely, without a face, and on the far side you would be in a darker flank that had not been dark at noon, and I would already be lighter, already quilting, already less a bridge than a record of the fact that reaching happened once in the open light and did not fail immediately.

That is where I am. Not the town—there is no town—and not the monster in the steam, and not the forcing under the crust that empties all of us when it wants the pipes in one key. I am the hour in which two of the valley’s arguments agreed to touch, and the touch has not yet been taken back. A short station. The only one that fits. When the crease comes I will not be poured out and I will not be killed; I will be simplified into channel and weather, and the place will continue, loud with becoming, failing in the same afternoon, and somewhere a bulb will swell at a frayed end and pay a tongue into air toward a lip that may or may not be close enough.

I hope it is not this lip. I am almost done being a road. But I will hold until I don’t, because holding is not something I decide anymore. It is what the locked segments do while the heat moves through them and the light stays ordinary and the far rim frays, bright, into sky.
