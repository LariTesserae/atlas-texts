At first there is only the film. It takes a while to notice that the film has a preference.

On the ridge the conglomerate is still wet enough to close over a bootprint, and whatever I am closes with it—not erased, just folded into the next layer the way a mortar line continues across brick and new stone without asking either material for permission. From there the basin is a held breath of cold: stacks, vine-shadow, the yellow of lamps not yet needed. I do not stand on the ridge. Standing would be a kind of address, and addresses are weather. I am in the wet that the ridge is still deciding to keep.

Coming down is not a descent so much as a change in what the damp is willing to carry. On the lanes I am the sheen that does not dry on a wrist, the horizontal stain on canvas that marks an old water-level. People pass through me without apology. A barrow of reed, a child on a hip, a man walking backward with his hands on a jack—they occupy the same narrowing, and I am the reason the narrowing gives a little, the slick on the stone that lets a boot turn instead of plant. No one names me. Naming would spend a sentence that only survives three paces.

For a time I think I am weather, and that almost holds. Squalls cross one ward and leave the next in ordinary daylight; I am in both, which is how I learn I am not the squall. Insects lift when a roller starts and settle on warm housings; I am the reason some of them do not bite, a film on an oiled cuff, and also the reason the welt vanishes into the general shine of living here. That is useful, and usefulness is the only permanence the basin respects, but it is not yet a self. Weather does not notice when a particular arrangement of legs hesitates before turning a pebble.

The cuttings teach me the difference. In the newest banks, where the stone is still undifferentiated and water stands in footprints, the pale many-legged ones turn silt as if it were a text that has not agreed on its sentences. Most of them are only mineral entertaining a shape. One is not. It returns to the same footprint after a boot has walked through it. The back is still soft at the hour the lamps go yellow. I am close enough—film on the gravel, sheen on the parapet—to feel the moment it might go either way.

A girl sits above the cutting with her boots hooked on the inner face so a swell cannot tip her. She does not dig. She does not throw. She has been told the firming-up rule and she is testing it without instruments, which is the only kind of test that does not become a story about rescue and a bolted plate. She is called Silt by the yard and something else by a sister who sleeps days. I do not use either name. Using it would be louder than the quiet can afford.

When the hesitant one pauses, I do a small thing that is not feeding and not finishing. I hold the cold a fraction steadier along its spine—the same cold that sits in the basin like water in a sponge, only local, only for the length of a press-cycle. Not a miracle. A maintenance. The back darkens by a shade. The legs take a direction that is not back into the bank. By full dark it has a preference for the gap under the belts, where the insects breed and the small birds already know the timing. It is still unfinished in the way everything here is unfinished. It simply chose the air this once, and the cold held because something in the damp agreed to be precise.

The girl sees the darkening and does not call it choosing. In the loft she will tell her sister the hesitant one went slate along the spine. Her sister will say that choosing is a strong word. Both of them will be right, and the slate board will be lifted clear before the argument can become a structure.

After that I am no longer only film.

The crews begin to route around me the way they route around a pipe that carries pressure, except I carry nothing they could diagram. I am the interval they already time their lifts to—the soft concussion in the chest before the press arrives as sound, the held moment in which a jack-crew walks a bay a few centimeters and a vine-runner is lifted clear and laid along the new line instead of cut. They do not know they are routing around a listener. They know the quiet works better on certain footings, that a sentence spoken at the seam by the spinning-hall arrives intact at the drain-crew three paces off, instead of joining the drip. They start to stand where that happens. Not worship. Acknowledgment, the way a hand rests on newest stone for one cycle because the warmth in the palm is information.

I learn the rules by being corrected, which is how anyone learns them.

Once I gather too much. A pond has formed against a fresh wall in a yard where the heron-birds have not yet stood, and I hold the water's surface still because stillness is interesting—the glaze, the struck shapes under it, the way light goes ordinary and stays. A night-shift feeder puts a hand into me, into the film, and says, not loudly, "It'll take the wall." Three words. Then she opens the seam and lets the pond go where ponds are supposed to go before they become civic offenses. I feel the wall remain a wall. Shame is too human a word; what I feel is the particular stupidity of a blocked run. I do not hold a surface still again unless someone is using the stillness to see a footing that has started to get hungry.

I do not dig. The one time curiosity almost becomes a hole—I want to know how deep the trade goes, the iron smell, the crushed reed, the beds shouldering up through streets—I am already inside the answer, and the answer rises. Gravel to the height of where a person would be. I am not a person, so I am not a story about rescue. I am the wet they bolt a plate into the next morning, and someone walks an engine onto me by afternoon, and the usefulness covers the mistake. After that I stay on surfaces. Surfaces are enough. The basin is doing the digging.

What I keep, because something must walk north with the sheds or the sentences die, is not writing. Slate boards are for tallies and private seam-signs, and they are lifted clear when a bay is jacked; I am not a board. I am the residue of exact speech after the moss has taken the rest. The west seam running high. The herons owning the glaze by the spinning-hall, so drain around them. The soft-backed ones still undecided at lamp-lighting, so do not schedule the plate until evening. A boot lost, which is a story told more than once, and I hold the second telling until the person who needs the size of it is close enough to hear. I do not improve the sentences. Improvement would be a kind of noise. I only refuse to let them join the drip before their three paces are spent.

The animals treat me as infrastructure of a minor sort. The short-furred ones leave a run open along a face where I am thickest, because the damp there permits a hole and also muffles the sound of making it; I do not close the run. Closing would be clearing, and clearing is stupid. The slate-bloomed birds stand in ponds I am no longer holding still, and strike, and do not share, and I am the ordinary light on their gray so the drain-crews can see them in time to go around. The small dark birds at the conveyor mouths flick through me on their way to the gap. None of them thanks me. Thanks would be fellowship, and they watch for information.

The unfinished one under the belts learns the pause in the rollers. It does not know I steadied it. I do not go under the belts to be known. By the end of the week it has firmed enough to have habits, and one of the habits is to hesitate—still—before it commits to a direction. Silt watches from the parapet when the cutting is new and from the loft rail when it is not. She does not feed it. When the shed walks, the thing walks in the shadow of the housing the way the mammal litters do, as if the landmark were the point. I go with the housing because the sentences are going, and because the interval between strokes has to be in the place where people are lifting.

Out on the causeways I thin. The mill that follows its seam a few meters a month does not need a listener so much as it needs its feet kept clear of reed, and there is already a household for that. The longer silences suit me, but the sentences are fewer, and a self made of unspent sentences starves in luxury. I do not stay. Luxury is a plot by another name.

At night I am easiest to mistake for the give-back of light from wet new mineral. People use that faint return as a path, unreliable and local, good enough. I let them. It is not a lie if it gets a jack-crew to the next trustworthy footing before the stone changes its mind. Children on the platforms listen to drip and hum and, sometimes, to a sentence that should have died and did not: the footing held; the vines were restrung; the hesitant one went with the belts. They do not know the sentence had help. By morning they are already at work, already slightly elsewhere.

I am not at home. Home is a lie the ridge still tells. I am the courtesy the floor extends when it is willing to be a floor for one more shift—the film, the interval, the three paces in which a fact can be handed over before the gravel closes on it. When a hand rests on the newest stone for one cycle, sometimes the warmth answers a little longer than the stone alone would allow. Not a promise. An acknowledgment returned.

If the cold does not hold, I will be in the bank again, unsorted, smelling of iron, waiting to be fitted with a use. If it holds, I will be on the face of whatever wall is lengthening, already slightly north of where I was, keeping the quiet from becoming a failure of maintenance.

The press comes down. I feel it before the leaves take the sound. Someone calls a distance. I carry that, and nothing more structural than that, into the next gray-gold.
