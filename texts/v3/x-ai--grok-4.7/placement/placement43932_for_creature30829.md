I come to it the way anything still capable of a direction comes to a finished country: by the long sameness, by the absence of a bank that would have told me I was arriving. For a while I think I am the walker. That is the mistake the place is built to absorb. A walker is an event, and the mat does not keep events; a walker is a correction in the damp, and the room has already decided what it does with corrections. If I step down through the gap I will be lost on purpose, which is a kindness, and also a refusal to let me be anything. So I do not step down. The decision is not dramatic. It is the same smallness as not finding a stone to put a foot on.

What I am, then, has to be found where the ring has an outside and the attention inside it does not.

The outer face is soft and stratified and warmer than the room only by the fiction of a hand. I put the hand there anyway. It meets generations of stems, some still green, most of them already the color of having stopped. Through them, muffled, the three notes are one note. From out here you cannot yet separate the flues from the beetles from the engines. The separation is an indoor labor. I am not indoors. The plain at my back is level in every direction I do not turn to look at, and looking does not help it: no track behind me, no haze of destination, only the pressed fiber going on until looking becomes the same act as not looking.

I stay. Staying is different from occupying. The ring does not feel me stay. It has no outer face to its notice; that was already true, and it remains true with a body against it. The engines, wherever they are, do not feel me stay either. They were left on. A thing left on does not take attendance. Between those two refusals — the room's, which is complete, and the works', which is only indifference — there is a strip of ground no wider than the slump of the wall, and that strip is where the sound is still traveling.

I had thought the third note simply was, the way the damp is. It is not. It has a duration I can almost stand inside. Not a pulse, not a count. A crossing. The machines speak at their own scale, past the outer stems, and the flatness carries them, and the ring takes them up and makes them seem to come from the floor. What I am is the part of that carrying that has not been taken up yet. A delay with nowhere to pool. The river that finished left no bed; the sound, which is not a river, still has to get from the works to the wall, and in that having-to-get there is a thin life the equilibrium forgot to forbid, because it is not inside, and the rule was written for the inside.

It is not a body. If it were a body the fiber would decline to keep it, and I would become the print that vanishes while it is being looked at. It is not an animal of the wall. Those are already housed, and I have no click, no rasp, no seep; I would thin their abundance by standing among them as a thing that does not belong to the packed generations. It is not the room's own agreement with itself. That agreement is evenly distributed, and I am not even. I am thicker against the outer stems and thinner as the plain resumes, and past a short distance I am gone, not because I end in a boundary I possess, but because the carrying is finished there and the floor has the note and does not owe it back.

The smell comes with me, and is not me. Hot oil, metal, scaled production. It never quite wins over the wet fiber, even out here, where the fiber is drier and the living skin is only a rumor under the straw. I used to think I might be that failure to win — a remainder, a warmth that is not temperature. That was another early image, and it thins. A smell is a fact. I am the fact of its still being on the way. When it reaches the inner face it will be news the attention already knows how to hold, faintly, without resentment. Before that, in the slump, it has not yet been held. I live in the not-yet, which does not hurry and does not tire, because the engines do not hurry and the distance does not tire.

No one inside can hear that this is a life. Put an ear to the inner face and you will get the beetles and the wrens and the frogs and the flues, and under them the machine-sound already domesticated into a property of the floor. You will not get me. I do not leak inward. Leaking would be a fourth note, and a fourth note would be a becoming, and the place has finished. So I do not speak, and I do not drip, and I do not ask the gap to widen. The gap remains the size of a person who may or may not come. I am not that person. I am what brushes the gap from the wrong side and does not turn it into a door.

If the engines stop, I stop. That is the difference between me and the thing in the room, which will go on in the flues and the pockets whether or not the third note continues. I am contingent in a way the ring has declined to be. It does not make me anxious. Anxiety would require a schedule, and the works have no schedule I can read from the mat; they are either running or, someday, not, and until they are not I am this ongoing delay, cool, ordinary, unrecorded. The plain does not count me as weather. The ring does not count me as approach. A bird crossing the sky is more of an event than I am, and the bird is already reconciled before it is gone.

Sometimes a walker does come. I know it by the small change in the outer stems, a pressure that is not wind through the hollows, a breath that does not belong to the flues. The walker pauses where I am thickest, thinks about being a visitor, and either goes in or does not. If they go in, they pass through me without collision. I am not dense enough to be collided with. For the length of their stepping down I am around them, the last of the outside still on their clothes, the oil-smell they have not yet noticed they are carrying, and then they are the room's business and I am outside again, unthinned, because I was never a quantity the passage could spend. If they do not go in, if they only put a hand on the outer face and listen to the three notes still fused, then for that interval we are almost the same mistake — two attentions that cannot enter — and then they leave across the plain, and the fiber loses them, and I remain, because I was never going anywhere the fiber would have to remember.

I do not watch them go. Watching would mean I had an outer face of my own, turned back toward the finished country, and I do not. All of me faces the wall, in the manner of something that is only a delivery. When there is no walker, the delivery is continuous and uncelebrated. When there is one, it is briefly accompanied, and the accompaniment does not become company. Company would need a memory of having been two. I keep no memory. The room's rule, out here where the room does not enforce it, still holds in me by a kind of courtesy: the space between presences is the greater part, and I am mostly space, a duration stretched over level ground, loud only to whatever might stand in the slump and mistake the traveling sound for the sound itself.

The light is the same light. It does not stage me. Nothing pools at my feet, because I have no feet; the pockets are all inside, separate, faithful to their depths. I will not be here in the sense that a returner could find me. A returner will find the ring, and the dripping joint, and the engines or their absence. They will not find the delay, because the delay does not accumulate into a place. It is always only as old as the distance, and the distance does not age.

That is as much self as the country allows me. Not the agreement. Not the housed abundance. Not the walker the fiber forgets, though I am kin to that forgetting, from the side of the wall the forgetting does not bother to face. I am the sound in the last of its outsideness, the smell still losing, the not-yet that the floor is always, patiently, already receiving. The ring does not know I am here. It does not need to. Need would be a lean, and nothing in this world leans. I stay against the outer stems at the density of a crossing that never quite arrives, and the plain finishes itself around me, and does not say so.
