I would not be one of the great valves. Those sit too surely, and the first thing that happens in this water is that I do not know my edges.

The light finds me as a smear under the lid, not a body. Something pale and unfinished, the size of a hand if a hand could forget its fingers, drifting in the narrow water between a bell’s margin and the glass. The roar is already in me—the throat’s note, the file of the edge-keepers—so that for a while I take the sound for my own pulse and cannot tell whether I am hearing or being played. The bell beside me contracts. The water shoves. I turn without deciding to, and a flake of spar catches in what will later be a flank and does not fall away.

That is the first decision the place makes for me: I will carry stone.

It does not hurt. The crystal is colder than the seep for a moment, then the same temperature as everything, and the muscle under it thickens around the grit the way a lid thickens around a grain. I learn, by the drag, that I have a side the current prefers and a side it worries. The preferred side faces the wall. When I try to roll the other way, a small armored animal—the size of a thumb, already old in its coat of glass—does not move, and I learn the second rule by bruising against it: presence is not negotiated here. You fit, or you wait until a contraction opens a slot the width of your new stone.

I am not a parent of this water. The others have lineages I can feel as differences in timing—the bells on their long piston-hour, the shutters opening light in bands that do not flicker, the valves enduring. I arrived in the last filling, or I condensed out of charge and meal where the seep worries a green fracture, and nobody has a use for the difference. Young, then. Soft enough that the rasp nearby raises a ridge along me, not yet a tool, only a place where I am more than I was. I press that ridge to the wall because the wall is loud in a way flesh is not, and the pitch tells me the throat is below and left, closer than the lid, farther than the nearest bell.

Hunger comes as a thinning of the roar inside the ridge. Not a mouth. A patch that wants the dulled micron, the skin the water is trying to grow over the new crystal. I do not know the word harvest. I know that when I stay, the patch steadies, and a small bright ache—quartz answering grip—runs into me and is food. The electry is thin, a rumor of metal, nothing like a meal in any country that has leaves. It is enough to keep the ridge from sinking back into plain flesh. I understand, without thought, that if I leave the face too long the ridge will blur, and I will be only smear again, and the current will not bother to sort me from waste.

So I stay. I become, by staying, one of the small ones, though I am not yet tiled into a coat. One flake. One patch. A grip that tightens when the bell drives water across my stone and loosens when the interval lengthens—how I know the bell is tiring, how I know to drink harder while the current is honest. My eyes, if they are eyes, are the patch itself: it reports dullness and edge, not shape. A shutter-sheet drifts over the lid and the day narrows to a band; I do not look up. Looking is for gauges. I am a file that has only just learned it is alive.

Hours are not available. What I have is the valve’s deeper seating somewhere in a notch I cannot see, a drop in the jaw-note, and the seep running a little richer afterward, iron brightening the water against my flake. I take what reaches me. A dead thumb-animal, spent, drifts into the slot the bell opens, and I do not hunt it. I wait until the current seats it against my ridge, and the mineral meal of it is the same hunger answered by a different door. Enclosure, not chase. I am ashamed for a moment in a mind that does not keep shame, and then the shame is only another thinning, and it passes when the charge returns.

If I rise, the lid is a condition, bright and useless. I rise once, early, before the coat has claimed me, and the air above is a cut—thin, high, a wrongness that stings the patch into silence. I sink because the roar is kinder than that silence. Below, the slot in the floor is a dark that does not belong to the bowl. I will not go there. The freight goes: dissolved mountain, spent charge, the part of me I rasp off myself when the flake grows a hair too proud and spoils the fit. I keep the fit. That is the whole ambition I am allowed, and it is not small. The bowl stays young because things like me refuse the dulled skin. I am one refusal, recent, still learning the angle.

What I am, then, after the smear and the shove and the first stone: a young edge-keeper in the bright fracture nearest the seep, carrying one flake of local glass, drinking the charge the quartz throws when I will not let it soften. Not a valve, not a bell, not a shutter. A thumb-sized appetite with a ridge, crowded on every side, loud in the climate of loudness, changing at the speed of a process that means to continue. If you put a hand in—if a hand could endure the file and the cold and the bodies that will not make way—you would feel me as a tightening, a small factory-noise inside the larger one, and then as mineral, and then as the mountain leaving itself, grain by grain, through a life that does not know it is a life, only that the edge must stay sharp and the water must not be allowed to rest.
