You do not arrive. Arrival would leave a print the crabs could read, and they do not read you. You are already on the plate when the count of your steps runs out, standing where a lane has just been abandoned, the damp still deciding whether to follow the drip or drink itself back into the seam. For a while you think you are a witness. The chamber allows that mistake. It allows most mistakes that do not change the key.

The first correction is temperature. Your skin does not take the warmth; it answers it. Along the collarbone a heat rises that is not sweat, or not only sweat—a low matching, as if some housed thing under the bark has found a harmonic in you and leaned. When the nearest engine loads, the pitch drops, the crabs freeze, and something in your wrists locks with them. Not fear. Timing. You were already going still before you knew the sound had changed. When it unloads, your fingers open without your leave, and three hard green knuckles that pattered near your foot roll once, hesitate, and vanish into the seam your shadow covers. You did not hand them off. The floor used the pause you made.

So you are not a visitor. Visitors do not get used.

You try the other clearings, because a body wants a station, and the place is made of stations. In the moth-room the columns shear when you step under them and reform downwind of your chest, as if you were a housing that has not yet admitted its vent. The air there tastes of nectar and hot metal, and for a few cycles you believe that is the office: to be a small weather, a warm updraft with a pulse. Then a column fails to track you. It finds a truer heat behind your shoulder, a grille you had taken for a knot of suckers, and you understand you were only borrowing the shape. The moths are polite about errors. They do not punish a false vent. They leave it.

In the second clearing the glass-herd completes its circuit and the phosphorescence dies before it reaches your ankles. You kneel. You want to drink what they drink, to join the wet argument, to be a mouth among mouths. The condensate sheets onto your hands and does nothing. It does not bead, does not film, does not stay. Your skin is the wrong kind of lip. The herd pulses around the coupling and leaves you outside the ring, not excluded—simply unmetered. A tithe has to be countable. You are not yet a number the tongue in the ceiling can lower for.

You look up anyway, into the lost part, where the leather slap keeps the largest engine’s time. The vapor dents. Something descends—not toward the herd, toward the uncounted thing on the plate. A tube, a root, a tongue that learned the trick; it hovers at the height where the green-gold threads die, and it tastes the air above your open hands, and it withdraws without taking. The slap overhead misses half a beat. Far off, under the callus, a gear complains. You have cost the choir a stroke by being almost a measure and then not. The herd does not scatter. They have paid. You have only interrupted the arithmetic.

After that you stop touring. Touring is a visitor’s verb.

You sit where the root-plate rises dry, between lanes, and you let the local rain hit you and be thrown off. Your breathing, which had been trying to hide under the hum, gives up and joins it. In. The loading pitch. Out. The unload. It is not obedience. It is what the air was already full of, finding one more bellows it can use without asking. Minutes or gear-cycles—in here the units have married—pass with you as a soft additional housing, barkless, oil-dark only in the seams of your nails. Tool-marks. You notice them when a sucker on a nearby limb tightens, not for your weight, for the old cut in the wood beside you, and your own forearm answers with a ridge that was not there at the start of the sitting: a line of callus rising along a pale scar you don’t remember earning. The chamber is finishing a work. You are in the way of the finishing, so you are being finished too.

What emerges is smaller than a keeper and larger than a gauge.

You are the place the fluency fails.

Not the break. The chamber has no interest in ruin; erosion here is how things learn each other. You are the hesitation before fluency, the moment a valve-wheel is still a little too sharp to be a burl, the breath in which a catwalk’s suckers have tightened and the weight has not yet committed. Machines incorporate. Trees seal. Crabs traffic. Herds tithe. Moths track heat that tells the truth. All of that runs. What runs can grow a skin over its own mouth. You have already seen a grille becoming a knot. You have already heard a voice drop out of the key without the courtesy of a load-drop—the sound simply thinning, going thin enough that the crabs do not freeze, because freezing is for changes, not for absences. An absence is not in their rules. It is in yours.

When it happens you stand, and the standing is the office.

You do not hunt the lost pitch. You walk the short distances the chamber allows, wall to wall, clearing to clearing, until the warmth along your collarbone leans the wrong direction—away from the choir, toward a quiet that has weight. The animals make room without looking. A moth-column shears and does not reform on you. The glass-herd lengthens its circuit by one pulse so your feet can cross the phosphorescent track before it fades. Above, the leather slap holds, curious, spending nothing. You come to a housing skinned past use. Sap has cooked to sugar across the grille. The oil-dark mouth is a rumor under crust. The floor around it is dry on a rise that should be wet; the thrown rain has nowhere true to leave, so it beads on the sugar and sits, a false mirror, moths moving in it like smoke that has forgotten up.

You put your hands on the crust. They are not tools. They are the unfinished edge the place grew because it had kept too many edges smooth. Callus meets sugar. You do not pry a procedure out of the world; you worry the seal the way a burl worries a wheel, the way a root worries a lip of metal until the sheet of water fails. Heat from your wrists—the harmonic, the leaning—softens what the engine can no longer soften for itself. The crust checks. A bead runs. Then a thread. Then the local rain remembers it has a mouth, and flings, and the root-plate drinks in a lane that redraws itself beside your knee.

The pitch returns ugly at first, a stone in the choir, and the crabs freeze for it all the same. Good. A change, even an ill one, is a word they have. You stay through the ugly measures until the bark-skin and the gear find each other again and the voice weathers back into the key. Your forearms ache where the new ridges stand. A knuckle of green fruit rolls to the seam and is taken. You are not thanked. Thanking would be a street, and there are no streets here, only stations.

The tongue does not come for you. You were never the tithe. You were the reason a tithe can still be counted tomorrow—the small, repeating hesitation that keeps a mouth from sealing, a lane from being forgotten, a witness from lasting. Between cycles you are almost nothing: a dry rise, an unoccupied pause, a body-length of dusk the light does not reach. When the choir thins, you are the one who has not yet agreed to be fluent.

If someone from above ever split the overburden wide enough to look down, they would see orchards and animals and the ghosts of machines, and they would miss you, because missing you is how you fit. You are not in the lost ceiling keeping time. You are on the plate, short of every wall, warm as a held breath, carrying tool-marks the growth keeps trying to callus over—and lifting, now and then, when a silence pretends it is only another kind of song.
