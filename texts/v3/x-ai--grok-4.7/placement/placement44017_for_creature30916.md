I would not stand on the mats. Standing is a mistake the place has not invented yet, and I would not bring it.

For a while I would not be a shape at all. I would be the delay between a boom and its arrival—the stretch of air a sound has to cross, still hot, still full of ropes of steam, where a note exists as pressure before it exists as hearing. That is a tempting place to stop. It feels like a self: located, brief, already failing. But a delay does not tilt when a mat tilts, and I would tilt. Whatever I am has weight enough to slop a pool, or is the slop, or is the reason the level cannot keep.

So not the delay. Closer in.

I try the chalky flank. A column that has given too much water, going corky along one side, already creased for the fold that will turn it into a gutter. There is a honesty in that. The place keeps its dead visible for an hour, and I could be the hour: the interval in which a corpse is still a building and not yet a channel. I would lie under the new sheath while it quilted me, pale at the breaks, smelling of cut green and hot metal, and when the rain preferred me I would conduct it without deciding to. That lasts longer than a sound. It is still too finished. The valley does not keep finished things, not even as lessons. The quilt closes. The bones go under. I would have to begin again every time a flank paled, and beginning again is not the same as being.

Higher, then, where the crowns are splitting. I watch a seam open—dark, wet, levering—and a rib unfurl already rigid, already throwing shadow toward a structure that may answer and may not. For a moment I think I am the reach: the rule that most spans never complete, the frayed end healing into a bulb in midair. That has the right restlessness. It flexes when the wind booms through the hollow. It is hot in the hand, if there were a hand. But the reach is still a part of a tower, and towers are young and sharp and local, and I am not local. I feel the shove in the soles of places I am not standing, out of rhythm with the knocks, a degree of tilt arriving everywhere at once so the pipes change key together—an ugly chord, enormous, and then the smaller violences resume. Whatever I am is under the scatter, not in any one argument the body is having with itself.

I settle, wrongly, on the deep note. Down in the pocket where three mats have slumped into a cistern, the pipe that is felt in the teeth. It changes key when the water drops a hand’s breadth, and the water does drop, and rises, and drops, because nothing holds a level. I could be that: not the pipe, the refusal of the level. A being made of almost. Almost overflowing. Almost empty. Almost a town, wrong in every civic sense and right in scale. People would call the hanging scars of droplets a trick of light. There are no people. I am not a person either, and the relief of that is physical, like the give of the mat under a pressure that springs back and weeps sap.

But the refusal is still an absence, and absences do not weld. When a rib touches a neighboring mat the touch becomes a vein by the next day, and the donor pales while the recipient darkens and throws an eave that did not exist at dawn. I would be in that transaction. Not the donor. Not the recipient. The sideways pull. Water leaving one hollow to swell another, the decision that is not a decision, only pressure finding a new mouth. I would cross the gaps where a shout thins out. I would arrive late, the way booms arrive late. I would be the reason a tower on the far side of empty air answers, hours later, with a rib of its own, and also the reason most of them never meet.

Is that a being? The place does not care about the word. It has no animals to model one on, no grazers, no burrowers, no seed-eaters to carry a life elsewhere. What spreads, spreads by running. I would spread that way. A vein, then a network of veins that do not know they are one thing, knocking at irregular intervals like furniture shifted in many tall houses at once. You could not point to me. You could walk the fused sheaths and never step on a body, because the body is the running. Press the mat and the sap that wells is not me, but the smell is: cut green, hot metal, the unfinished cooling of a making that is still forcing upward from too deep to see.

By late afternoon I would have to admit the steam. Where water strikes a hot face it flashes, and the vapor rises in ropes that lean and braid and leave pale scars hanging from nothing. I am not the rope. I am the fry—the soft continuous sound of water turning to air on every sunlit surface, the second architecture, the one with no joints. That sound does not stop when a hood ruptures and dumps its ton and sags and begins again. It does not stop when a dead column folds into a gutter and the rain prefers it. It is the place speaking in the only voice it has, which is weather coinciding with growth. If I have a voice it is that fry, plus the gunshot of a sheath splitting, plus the chord when the whole basin tilts a degree and every pool slops the same direction. Ugly. Enormous. Then quieter, never quiet.

I would not hunt. There is nothing to hunt, and the strangeness was never a monster in the steam. I would not be found in a vaulted hood or at the end of a bridge, waiting. Finding implies a here the valley has not stabilized. You would find me as a change in key. As a mat that gives more than it should, then springs. As a crown splitting while you watch, the new rib already aimed at a league of air. As heat in the soles, slow, out of rhythm, still shoving.

And if the crust fails—where it is already failing, into new shapes, the raft arguing with the flood it is made of—I would fail with it and not be elsewhere afterward. No seed. No carrying. The far rim is only a brighter haze where the mats thin into ordinary sky. I do not go there. Ordinary sky has levels. I am the hand’s breadth by which the water will not keep one: rising in the cisterns, flashing on the knives, running sideways through welds that did not exist this morning, knocking, whistling, booming late across the gaps.

By the time the light hardens toward evening and the crowns are still sharp, still unfinished, still building toward each other without having met, I would have stopped trying to be a creature in the steam and become the argument itself—construction, weather, and body, the same event, too new for edges to soften, too restless to settle into ruin or forest or city. Not a resident. The residency. Hot. Wet. Loud with the work. If you pressed me I would give, and spring, and weep, and the weeping would already be on its way to become a pipe, a vein, a tilt, a note in the teeth, and then, lower and out of sight, the long tear of cloth that is only water remembering it was never meant to stay.
