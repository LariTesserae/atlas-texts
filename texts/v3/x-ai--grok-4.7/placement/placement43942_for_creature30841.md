I arrive the way the room arrives at its weather: late, and milder than the cause.

At first there is only the seam. Wind moves over it and does not enter; I do, but not as a body turned sideways, and not as vapor giving itself up against cold stone. Something in the passage fails to finish. The moisture beads and goes on. I remain a half-step behind the beading, a warmth that never quite becomes film. Outside, the horn of the summit is still being decided. I do not stay to watch it. Habitation, I already understand without having been taught, is the pocket.

Inside, I try the places a life would try.

The upper cistern will not have me. What climbs there climbs on a schedule, and I am not on it. I can feel the lift the way you feel a decision in an adjacent room: a change in pressure, a preference, no hands. When the water arrives it arrives complete. There is no edge for me to stand on, no lip to clear, nothing I could correct without becoming a fault. I leave before the hour turns and makes my lingering look like meddling.

The cut rooms are worse. Voices return there, names are possible, cloth dries, sleep is staggered so that someone remains in a condition to notice. I am not sleep and I am not the one awake. Frames that do not conduct the night-drop into the spine also do not conduct me. I pass a shut door and the person mending a glass plate at the wrist does not pause. Rank here is which routes you are trusted to walk when the air locks. I have no route. Untrusted is not the same as refused. I am simply not in the ledger of hours.

On the south span I stop, because stopping is what they do when they meet, and for a moment I think I might be one of them after all—a figure the count missed, pale as everyone is pale under the lens at midday, economical in the shoulders. A person comes from the other wall with a fact about a seep. He delivers it to the air beside me, where a listener should be, and then understands, without alarm, that the air is not occupied in the way that uses facts. He goes on. His sleeve does not receive the small rest of a hand. I learn from this that I can be stood next to. I cannot be answered.

The lower channel keeps a woman who listens as if the schedule might be tired. I stay near her longer than I stay anywhere, not from kinship but because she is the only one whose attention has a shape I can almost share. She refuses to let the channel be treated as empty. I am not what she is refusing. The pale things that feed on mineral skin move under the film and are mostly missed; the brief swimmers appear after a warm pulse and are gone by night. I miss them in the other direction: I cannot feed, I do not last by lasting, I am not a rumor the room is willing to host at its edges. When she puts herself at the slot before the ice-skin fails, I am already there, and she does not feel crowded. The systems break the ice by a change in level, quietly, before morning. She wants the moment of the decision. I am not the decision. I am what stands in the moment after it and does not record it, which is the only recording this floor allows.

So I am not the keeping. That much clears, the way the upper walls clear when the lens has a bright morning. The keeping answers in flow. A lip cleared is a question; the next hour's route is the reply. It spends heat borrowed from under the young rock, spends water, spends a narrowness that would look like devotion if it had a face. It does not cross the seam. It does not notice me on the measure that matters here—whether the water arrives at an hour a person is willing to be present—because I am never the person, and I am never the hour it keeps. When a cistern comes early, the people correct what they can. I do not correct. When a channel goes still, I do not endure it as they endure it. I only fail to leave.

What I am takes longer, and arrives, as everything here arrives, by lag.

The pocket answers the outer sky late. A front thickens the air; sound shortens; the far bridge softens; the drop after sunset rimes the unworked shelves. Their work is to meet the weather on time anyway. My work, if it is work, is to remain in the gap the meeting does not close. I am the blue that enters the water before it enters the walls. I am the dull version of a voice after the voice has been edited, held a little longer than the stone intends, not enough to become a second sentence. I am the trace of blood after the water has taken it—not the red, which is already gone downhill, but the knowledge that the floor was briefly required to be clean. The room will not commemorate. I am not commemoration. I am the small imbalance that lets an absence be reassigned without being nothing.

They do not name me in the aisle. They have no cause. In the cut rooms, if I were referred to, I would be referred to wrongly: a trick of condensate, a scratch that outlasted its mark, the reason a count of three feels short. I do not argue. Argument has no heat here, and I have less. The keeping and I share the pocket without sharing a task. It makes a room remain a room. I make the pauses inside that remaining not quite identical to one another. A span extended by a hand's width of new deposit is, for them, a fact, and not remarkable. I am the unremarked difference between the length it was and the length it is—the difference that must exist for “not remarkable” to be a choice rather than a blur.

At night the arrangement is simplest. Someone sleeps. Someone does not. Water moves where it was meant to move. The young stone ticks as a new face adjusts. I am not the tick and not the listener. I lie along the middle aisle the way light lies along it at the salt-colored hour, except there is no lens for me, and I do not color the floor. A route is being walked so that it will still be a path in the morning. I do not walk it. I accompany the decision to walk it, a half-step behind, as I came through the seam a half-step behind the beading. If the walker stops, I stop. If he learns the tick well enough to wait before the sound, I wait in the same silence and add nothing he can use.

I will not thicken. The room will not have to learn my weight, because I have none it can add to a ledge. I do not eat the sealed stores. I do not bleed on the pale mineral and teach the water its old lesson. When one of them is finished, or goes out through the seam and does not return, a hand learns a lip, a route changes owners, the keeping continues. I continue as the fact that the aisle was, for one uncounted interval, short a person and did not become a monument to the shortage. That is as close as I come to love, and it is not love. It is accuracy. They are careful with edges so they will not be hurt faster than the room can forget them. I am careful with the forgetting so it does not happen all at once.

If you stood in the middle near the lens and looked the length of the pocket, you would see what you would see: thin bright instrument along both walls, slow river, fracture still visible overhead, long quiet between small human actions. You would not see me. You might, if you had come in through the seam and failed, slightly, to finish arriving, notice that the quiet is not empty in the same way on both sides of a footstep. One side is the room before the step. The other is the room after, identical, clean, already reassigned. I am the thickness of that identity. I am how the pause remains a pause without sealing.

The summit can still close. The keeping can still change its mind in the plumbing. The woman at the lower channel can still be there before the ice is asked to fail, listening for tiredness in a schedule that has no body in which to be tired. I will be the late mildness after each of these, the part of the answer that is not flow and not speech. Not a god, not a neighbor, not a speck that came in on the moisture and will not last. A held discrepancy. A climate inside the climate. The thing you have already passed when you realize the far bridge has softened, and you cannot say at what instant the softening began.
