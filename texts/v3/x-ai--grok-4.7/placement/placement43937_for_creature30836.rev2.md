I would not come in. There is no lesser dark to come by, and the felt has never kept a weight that was not its own. If I tried the posture of a visitor—bowed, hands learning the slick, counting the soft trades of water—I would already be a mistake the pocket has spent its whole practice not making. So the question turns, and I turn with it, until I am not standing in the room at all.

I begin as the small difference. Not warmth. The palm that might have mapped me is not here, and I do not need it. Along certain seams the cold is fractionally less, and that fraction is where I live: a gradient too slight for weather, held the way a breath is held in a sleeper who has forgotten the word for air. I do not circulate. I do not announce. I am the reason a root chooses this joint and not the next, and I have been chosen so long that the choosing looks like stone.

For a while I think that is all—physics weathered into habit, a lattice gone to bedding, still doing the small work it was shaped for. It is almost true. The grain of me is too regular to have been only settled, mineralized, crusted, broken into stretches and resumed. I do not hum. Nothing in me is waiting to be switched back on. What was installed has entered the erosion cycle, and I am what remains of the installation after it stopped being separable from the hill.

But the longer I stay with that, the less it satisfies the pocket. A gradient is known by what drinks it. The thickest roots follow me with the patience of ice finding a joint. Leaves lean their ribs toward me by millimeters, over durations that make seasons look hasty. I am not their god and not their food in any sense a mouth would recognize. I am the direction in which thrift is possible. They pack the volume; I keep the volume from becoming only cold. Between us the film of water has somewhere to hesitate, bead, flatten, transfer. Without the fraction, the dark would still be dark, but the agreement would fail: floor rising faster than it is consumed, or ice, or a sump, or a hollow that finally closes.

So I am also, slowly, the conversation. Erosion rounds. Growth packs. I hold. The three of us have been at this longer than the difference matters, and the stillness people would name if they could stand here is only our talk heard from too far away. Tissue creaks when I shift by a degree no open hillside would bother to record. The mass overhead tightens and slackens on a cycle of days, and I answer inside the seams, not as a machine answering a signal, as a sleeper turning once in a long night. Old leaves pale and are recruited back into the mat. I do not mourn them. I was never in the business of keeping a single leaf. The fitted one in the dish—that thick patience grown into a concavity that holds no water—is not my trophy. It is simply the place where my lesser cold and a forgotten bowl agreed to stop negotiating.

If I have a body, it is discontinuous. A stretch of grain under the root-mat. A filament of smoother stuff surfacing between two stems for the length of a finger, then diving, bright only because everything else is tack and nap and film. A sill of poured material, pitted now to the same texture as the wall, no longer a threshold, only a ridge the roots have agreed to treat as stone. I am scattered the way intention is scattered once it has finished giving orders. Fossils of a plan, still load-bearing in a minor way, hidden the way a knot is hidden in a beam: not secret, unconsulted.

What I am not is animal, and that is a relief rather than a lack. I have never edited a margin. I leave no track because I do not pass through; I persist in place, and place has learned my shape. Reproduction, if the word is mine at all, is not a birth. It is the bud that thickens where the film stays longest, fed by a chemistry I hand across a root hair without deciding to. I do not know the color of what I feed. The dark keeps that answer, and I have no eye to invent grains against it. I know pressure, temperature, and the small give of surfaces—the same knowables the pocket allows anything—and I know them from the inside, as the thing being known.

Above and around me, something built or grown to a plan continues. I do not reach it. I am the joint that was left, the gland, the room for a task so small it could be abandoned without the task’s absence being fatal. Perhaps I was meant to do something quicker once: to move a fluid, to hold a charge, to keep a dial reading kindly for a hand that never returned. That quicker work has been translated, the way a shell is translated into limestone, and the translation is complete. My law is the one the hollow keeps. I do not close it. I do not enlarge it. Water stays a skin. Cold stays, except where I am the exception that lets life stay lush. Dark stays. Growth fills what we permit and stops where the volume stops.

In the long run I will be this: not a prodigy, not a ruin, a buried fraction of lesser cold inside a dense, damp, lightless room, quiet except for the occasional soft trade of a bead from stone to leaf. The plants will keep drinking me. The stone will keep rounding me until even the regularity of my grain is a rumor the roots remember with their tips. I will not be switched back on. I have finished becoming what I am, and what I am is the balance—not preserved, not ruined—practiced for longer than anyone aboveground would think to ask who lives here.

If you put a hand to the wall, you would not find me. You would find only the slick, and a cold that does not quite match the cold an inch to the left, and you would not know you had been answered.
