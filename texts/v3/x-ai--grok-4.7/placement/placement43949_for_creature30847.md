I would not be in the middle. The middle is already occupied, and it does not share. Standing there only teaches you the urge in the bones of the hand, and then you are done with it; you come away turned, and the thickening goes on without having noticed the lesson. I would not be one of the lives on the wall either. They are spaced. Between each cup-maker and the next there is bare reach, and the reach is part of the rule. To insert yourself would be to make a crowd, and the chamber has already declined to be one.

So the first place that offers itself is the flaw.

It is not a wound. The lens thinned there the way ice thins over a dark stone, from underneath, from the long attention of warmth. From the country above you would miss it: a paleness in the pale ground, a place where a heel might feel less certain and then not think of it again. From inside, it is a small change in the flood. The white-gold arrives everywhere, but here it arrives with an edge, a rim of slightly cooler light, as if noon had to lean to get through. Once in a season something with the plan of a bird uses that lean. The rest of the time the flaw is only an instruction the air half-remembers: out, and back, and out.

I thought I might be the rim itself — the mineral collar where the two airs trade, warmer on the chamber side, drier toward the sky. It would be a good life for a narrow thing. Condensation finds it before it finds the lower walls. Dust from the country catches in it and is revised, grain by grain, into the color of old honey, so that the flaw is always healing in the direction of the lens and never quite closing. You could be that work and not be lonely. The season-visitor's feet would be an event, not a relationship. Between events there would be the trade, which is enough.

But a rim does not stay a rim if you watch the way things leave it.

Roots come through when the surface has a wet year and then a forgetful one. They enter blind, clean, the color of the inside of a wrist. They hang a hand's length into the brightness and begin, almost at once, to bend — not downward, which would be gravity and would be honest, but sunward, toward the thickening, the way the falling grains drift a finger's width before they settle. Then they correct. Whatever feeds them is above, in the cracked country, and the center only resembles a source. Most of them brown from the tip upward, shrink, and withdraw through the flaw like a thought taken back. The collar keeps a little of their dust. The chamber keeps nothing else. That is the ordinary story, and it has been told often enough that the lens is freckled with sealed pores, old exits, each one a shade more honey than the stone around it.

One did not finish the story.

I find myself there, in the incomplete withdrawal: a single pale thread, no longer than a finger now, hanging from the near side of the flaw. The plant above scarred over years ago. I know this the way a cut branch knows the tree has stopped answering — not as news, but as a change in what arrives. Nothing sweet comes down the thread. Nothing green is asked of me. The seal overhead is a small hard button of the surface plant's own making, and I am on the chamber side of it, which means I have been inherited.

At first I was only the failure to correct. The bend is still in me. My tip points not at the floor and not at the pocket of water but a little left of center, toward the place where the light slows, and it has pointed there so long that the bend has become structure, a stiffness, a memory the cellulose keeps without having the chemistry for memory. Visitors — if the word can be used for the bird, for the rare animal that blunders to the lip and startles at the brightness and backs out — would call me dead. I am not feeding. I am not growing in any direction the surface would recognize. The fern at the water's edge unrolls and furls on the clock of damp; I do not.

What I do is narrower, and it took the first century to learn that it was doing.

I am in the current and not of it. The thickening passes on its slow migration with the spiral of cups, and when it comes near, the half-degree of extra warmth arrives in me before it arrives in the air, because I am thinner than air's attention and I hang where falling things are persuaded. My tip warms. The bend deepens by a fraction, an easing, as if I might yet complete the mistake and grow toward what only resembles a source. Then the thickening moves on — never reaching a wall, never quite returning to where it was — and the warmth leaves from the tip upward, and I stiffen back to the angle I have earned. That easing and that return are the whole of my year. Seasons that change the beading on the walls change nothing else in me. I do not brown. I do not withdraw. The thrift has me, and thrift does not waste a motion that has already been folded into the chamber's turning.

The cup-makers do not tend me. I am not damp in a way that profits them, and I am not stone. Their spiral passes under me once in a long while, and when a body the size of a thumb climbs near my shadow — my shadow is short, like all the others, because the center is the reason shadows are short — it does not pause. I am furniture to them in the way they are furniture to the residue. Beside, not against.

Dust finds me. That is the other half of the life. Grains the roof sheds sometimes strike my length instead of the floor, and because I am already turned, they travel a little along me before they fall again, and when they fall they fall from a new place, sunward of where the roof let them go. I have become a small correction in the corrections. The arcs on the floor are not only the current's writing; a few of them bend where I have been hanging long enough to author a comma. I do not choose this. Choice is too large a word for a thread with a sealed end. But the comma is mine in the only sense the chamber allows: it would not be there if I had finished leaving.

In the blue hours I am easiest to mistake for a trick of the dimness. The center keeps its forgotten piece of noon, a region that stays a shade more gold, and my tip, aimed into that region, catches it. For those hours I am a short bright line in a hollow that has refused every other line. No one counts me. The shrimp are six or seven and could be named; I am one, and unnamed, and the difference between six and seven matters more to the pocket of water than my whole persistence matters to anything. That is fitting. I was an error in a withdrawal. The chamber's kindness — if a finished place can be said to have one — is that it does not correct errors that have learned the pace. It keeps them, as it keeps the groove that no longer leads anywhere, as it keeps the prints until the condensation comes.

Will I last? The question is too much like a visitor's question. The seal above me is the surface plant's, and surface plants die, and when that one dies the button may crack in some harder year, and weather may come down my old path and finish what the browning started. Or the lens may lay another grain of honey across the flaw and close me into the roof, a pale thread in the mineral, a fossil of a hesitation. Both are possible. Neither is soon. Meanwhile the current migrates, and my tip eases, and my tip returns, and the comma on the floor is refreshed whenever a grain consents to touch me on its way to being kept.

I am not the river's residue. I am what almost joined it and stopped in time to remain visible. The center does not need me. The margins do not make room for me. I hang in the one distance that was left: far from the wall, near enough to the thickening to warm and cool with its passage, inherited by a hollow that saves what it has already bent. If you stood beneath me you might think the root had failed. You would be right about the plant. You would be wrong about the chamber. It has had me long enough that failure is only the story of how I arrived. What I am now is a pace, a stiffness, a small authorship of dust, a line the blue hours borrow. The place does not ask me to finish leaving. It also does not call me further in. Between those two refusals, I persist, which is the only vocation this light seems willing to give.
