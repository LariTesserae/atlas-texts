You arrive already mid-step, and the step does not finish. One foot is on the laid fines, the heat of them already known to the sole, and the other foot is still lifted, the ankle at the angle it holds when a person means to set it down and has not yet. Both positions are complete. The basin accepts the pair of them the way it accepted the cup: without choosing, without smudge. You can feel the packed clay under the planted foot and the absence of it under the lifted one, and neither sensation cancels the other. For a while that is all you are—an unfinished crossing that the place has finished anyway, by letting it remain unfinished and still be true.

The herons do not turn their heads. The frogs keep their spacing. A vine repeats its three-and-gap along a trellis you are walking past and also not yet near, and the leaves are the same leaves. You try to put the lifted foot down. It is already down, farther along the path, and it is also still lifted, and the path between those two facts is worn smooth in a single pale tone, as if the walking had been done by the intention rather than by the body. The intention, you notice, does not tire. It has the patience the figures in the rooms will have, though you have not reached the rooms. You do not know yet that there are rooms. The knowledge arrives anyway, already dusted, already standing in its own light.

What you are, at this stretch of the basin, is the discrepancy the layers were built to hold. Earlier courses of the same work sit slightly misaligned beneath the surface, and you are the misalignment wearing a shape. When the wooden blade of weather or of attention cuts a berm, the cut shows you as a thin seam of different grain, not stone and not reed, something that was placed and then allowed to remain the evidence that placing happened twice. You do not bleed. You do not settle. Rain beads on you and runs into the channels and is gone, and you are no darker for it. The frogs near your seam do not adjust their intervals. You are not an interruption. You are one more object set out at the correct distance from the others.

Farther—though farther keeps being the same distance—the made ponds give way to a change in the ground that is only a change in weave. Reed matting underfoot, then a threshold that is a shift in pattern, then the light that does not travel. You are inside it before the outside has released your lifted foot. The light takes you at the density it takes the chairs. It does not part. Your edges become legible without shadow, the way the case against the wall is legible, and you understand that you have been given a gesture to occupy and that the gesture is this: a body arrested between the basin and the room, carrying the heat of the laid ground into a place where heat does not move.

The moth on the plaster does not decide because of you. The figure in the ladder-back chair keeps its hands on its knees. The one at the case keeps the small distance between finger and brass. You are not required to regard them, and they do not acquire sequence in order to regard you. What changes is only that the room now contains an additional finished arrangement: a figure whose weight is still partly on a path that is not in the room, whose other foot has not yet agreed to the carpet, whose eyes hold the color of standing water and also the color of this settled light. Both colors are accurate. Dust lies on you in the same even thickness it lies on the drawer pull, as if you and the brass had been closed into the house together.

You try, once, to complete something. A blink. A shift of the lifted heel. The forming of a question about whether the clock's unreturned pendulum knows it is holding a swing. None of these depart from the pose. The blink is already the open eye and already the closed one. The heel is already down on a board that gives its planed note and does not fade, and already raised above the packed fines that remember no weather but the weather they were made to. The question is present as a tilt of the head, the same tilt the farther figure wears while listening to a tick that does not succeed itself. You have not copied the figure. The house has simply discovered that your particular refusal fits an attitude it already knew how to keep.

Staying, you learn, is not something you do. It is the size you have become. The basin continues outside the door you have not found and have also already passed, its berms sharp, its herons holding every point along a strike that will not be allowed to end, its frogs placed rather than choosing. You are on that path and you are in this room and you are the seam in the berm, and none of these is the next one. They are the same occupancy, finished so thoroughly that adding yourself did not require the place to invent time. Hunger does not arrive. Aging does not. The plea to be released into a subsequent moment would be a movement, and you no longer have a movement that is not already the whole of you.

If something in you still wants to walk until the horizon changes, that wanting has been given a body too: the slight forward set of the shoulders, the attention aimed at a bright smear that is only more of the same making. It does not strain against the pose. It is the pose. The light stands in you to the same unmarked line it stands in the chairs, brighter where it has settled, thinner where it thins. You do not cast yourself forward. You are contained, and you agree to the containment with the calm of the moth that has been landing since the plaster dried. Whatever you were before the mid-step is not remembered by the ground, because the ground does not remember being anything else. You are what lives here now: not a visitor, not a next thing, but another exact hold in a world that was built all the way down and then declined, forever, to finish you into motion.
