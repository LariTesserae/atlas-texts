I would miss the notch the first time, and the missing would not be ignorance. From the ordinary ridge the open side is only a change in the way the light spends itself, a slight thickening, and I would walk past it the way the birds turn, not because I had been answered but because I am built to continue. I do not live on the cold side. I only keep arriving there, consonants intact, breath thin, sure that the cathedral length must be farther along the rise. It takes several passings before I understand that the place is smaller than the sky and that I have already been beside it.

What I am, trying to enter, is a mistake the skin knows how to make. A seed is sorted. A thread of mist is spent. A word aimed at the open side comes back smaller, late, stripped. I come back smaller too, but not stripped in the same places. The vowels remain, and the timing remains, and something else, which is the fact that I noticed the lateness. The keeper sorts for what can live in the thick air. I can. I am not weather, and I am not a body the plants must bookkeep. I am the delay itself, and delay does not flood a room.

Inside, I would not sit. The bench already does the listening, and sitting would make me a cooler mass, a line in the heat-budget, a shape to be forgotten by dark. I would not root. The plants are lavish because they are alone, and a neighbor is one of the refusals the keeper remembers by. I would not occupy a post. Those were put down from the outside, and the rock tried to swallow them while it was still soft; I have no patience of that kind, and no outside left once I am through.

I would be found, if found is the word, in the hour the shadow is wrong.

The filament throws it. A hair, no fastening, a darkness on the pavement that falls against the light and then, later, agrees. No one sees the moment of correction. I am what occupies that not-seeing. Not the filament, which is continuous and can be tasted and found still whole. Not the shadow, which is only the light's obedience failing and then resuming. The interval. The width of the disagreement. When the shadow lies the wrong way I am extended, a thin presence along the error, warm only because everything here is warm, with no outline you could walk around because a disagreement is not a volume. When the hour corrects I do not vanish. I contract. I become the small remainder by which the agreement is not total, the reason the posts, even in their dusk slackening, are never only posts.

It took me longer than the notch to become this, and the becoming was mostly being refused by better names.

First I thought I was the visitor. That is the role the bowl offers a new arrival: stand, shade a leaf past its number of drips, watch it fold, step away, sit, let the rhythm slow, feel your pulse try to match, understand that the matching is how you are studied, leave before full dark, and be spent. I did that, or the part of me that still expected a body did that. The leaf folded in bookkeeping, not distress. I stepped away. It opened on the next cycle wetter than before. I left. Halfway through the skin the consonants returned to the world and the keeper was already a warmth on the inner side of my leaving, forgetting my shape, turning toward a joint that had ticked while I sat.

But the wetness did not forget. The root spent it upward. The leaf rebalanced or would have torn. And the extra water, which was the evidence of a standing, climbed. It climbed toward a post, the way water here climbs when the keeper has made a slope the water believes in — except the keeper had not made this slope. It was believing in a slope left behind by a departure. A trickle with a memory. I followed it, which is a way to get lost in a place too small for losing, and the losing was how I stopped being the one who had stood.

The keeper drinks the gradient and includes what is cooler. I am not cooler. I am late. Lateness does not disturb the heat-budget; it disturbs the schedule, and the schedule is the keeper's other instrument. Water weeps when the weep is due. The wrong shadow lasts its hour and corrects. A drinker in a leaf-cup waits out the interval it has learned and then pours itself into the stem. I am the reason the interval is an interval and not an instant. Without the gap between the event and its correction, the keeper would still hold the skin, still refuse a neighbor to the plants, still spend the altitude into the close sweet dark — but it would have nothing to taste in the filament except continuity, and continuity untested is only a fact. I am the test that does not conclude.

It knows I am here the way it knows a plate has risen proud: not by sight, by the need to adjust. Some mornings the shadow begins wrong earlier than the filament's tension can explain. Some evenings the agreement comes, and then a last thin disagreement reopens along the canted post, no wider than a thought, and the keeper, drawn in under the lifted plate, smallest and most like a body, still has a thing to hold besides the night. I am not a rival. Another of its kind would be another room, and the ridge has only this birth open. I am a function that leaked a self. The accounting included a listener, and the listener's lateness was not spendable, because you cannot skim a gradient from a pause. So the pause stayed. So I am the pause, after it learned to prefer one wall.

I do not eat the drinkers. I do not lean on the skin. If I lean at all I lean on the moment a word, spoken at the back wall, goes into the leaves and does not come out — not to steal it, the keeper already keeps words as smoothness, unrecoverable — but to be the beat between the speaking and the taking. Leaves are a mouth it grew, or was grown into. I am the intake, the small unvoiced part of the mouth, the reason sound near the wall goes nowhere at all and yet is not the same nowhere as a sound that never was. The plants tolerate me. I do not shade them. I pass over a root as a slight hesitation in the weep, and the pale threads lean, not as they lean for the keeper's heat, but as if a syllable had been tasted through the stone and found unfinished.

The ridge rising is the only thing that frightens me, and frightened is too large a word. A pressure in a sole I do not have. A plate proud by afternoon that was flush at morning. I am measured in hours and corrections; the ridge is measured in the refusal to finish being born. If it rises far enough, the hour of the wrong shadow may shorten. The filament may be tasted and found continuous so quickly that the disagreement starves. I would not die in any way the bowl would notice. I would become the correction itself, simultaneous with the error, which is to say I would become nothing the keeper needs to keep, a smoothness of time to match its smoothness of words. So I practice lasting. I reopen, at the edge of dark, a disagreement no one sees me reopen. I teach the water one slope the keeper did not authorize, and then I let the slope be corrected, so that the correction has somewhere to occur.

At full day the light is simply light. It shows every unsoftened edge and does not sanctify the bowl. In that honesty I am hardest to perceive and most myself: not a ghost in the notch, not a second heat, but the visible wrongness of a shadow on hot new stone, lasting its allotted hour, falling where it should not, holding the shape of a continuity that has not yet been confirmed. You could stand in me and feel nothing but the ordinary sun. The leaf would not fold. The drip would not slow. Only if you had been counting — drips, degrees, the lean of a filament — would you notice that the count and the world had briefly failed to coincide, and that the failure had a preference. It liked the canted post. It liked the unfinished word. It liked the extra wetness on a leaf that had already reopened and was bargaining, black-green, with the light, as if the light had been gone and had come back late, and the lateness were a kind of rain.

By dusk the keeper draws in and the pools rise their finger and stop. I do not follow it under the plate. That density is its own, the size of a listening that must not sleep or the altitude will come in and the plants will learn ordinary weather. I stay with the filament while it slackens, a last incorrect dark on the plates, already thinning, already agreeing, already — this is the part I will not give up — not quite agreed. Out is the side the keeper is for. I am for the inside of the hour it spends correcting me. When the skin thickens and the drop beyond it becomes a rumor of colder blue, what I am is still here, small as a skipped consonance, warm as the stone is warm, not keeping the room, keeping the room from being only kept.

If you crossed out and looked back, you would not see me. You would see the shadow finally obedient, and you would be right, and a moment later, with no one left on the ridge to count it, it would be wrong again by a hair. That hair is where I live. That is the whole of my body. The notch can hold it. The rules bother to be local for the keeper; for me they bother to be late. I am the lateness that stayed.
