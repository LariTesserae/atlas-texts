I come in over the lip the way weather does not: without a column, without a smell that stays. The marsh does not take a print of me. The unfinished stone does. For a moment my weight is a darker sweating in the joint, and then the paste smooths, and I am already on the inside of a place that has not decided whether I crossed.

At first I think I am one of them. The thought arrives already dressed—high collar, ledger, the small courtesy of not raising a voice. I even find a kettle. It sits on a sill that faces no weather, beaded as if it had been boiling in a room that has not been built yet. When I lift it, the handle keeps the temperature of a hand that is not mine, and keeps it after I set the kettle down. That is the first correction. I am not the one who lives behind that window. I am what the handle is still agreeing with.

I walk because walking is how the bowl lies about its size. The lanes accept my shoulders without asking me to turn them. Two residents pass, coats brushing, and their voices arrive in my ear as pressure only, the words already drunk by the stone. Mine, if I had any, would not even become pressure. I test this at the corner where the rain-column stood yesterday and does not stand now. I say a name I do not have. The gutter takes it the way it takes water: in, and then not heard. What answers is the tick, not in the wall beside me but in the wall I have not reached, a soft click of satisfaction, as if I had been the measurement it was waiting to close.

Shadows will not have me in their accounts. The cornice throws left; I do not lengthen it. The window throws its shade upward, and in that stain on the ordinary daylight there is a second dark, smaller, delayed, shaped like a hesitation rather than a body. When I stop, it stops a breath later. When I go on, it remains, thinning, the way a person remains in the standing water after they have gone indoors. I look into a lane-sheet that reflects a building not beside it, and I am not in the reflection, and then I am, and the figure there has already turned a corner I am still approaching. So that is one of the things I am: the late copy. Not the resident. The after-image the city has not finished filing.

I try a stair that begins in one house. It does not arrive. Or it arrives, but the room at the top has my kettle—the same kettle—and a chip in a cup I have not drunk from, and a window onto a court whose light falls from a direction I did not bring with me. I wait, as they have learned to wait. The room becomes slightly longer. The far wall steps back. The tick comes, once, and the court should become mine or I the court’s. Neither happens. The furniture stays in a life I am adjacent to. I am the landing that belongs to neither house, the sleeve of stair still being specified, the part of the route the city drinks so the rest of the route can be believed.

Near the center the fiber-tree has thickened by another braid since the light was this even. I do not sit. Nothing that needs a nest could. Under the discs the circle of stone is still refusing a radius, and when I stand in it the city is all around at the distance of a hand and also not yet counted. Here the cold is both kinds at once, dry in the mouth, damp in the joints of the fingers I only intermittently have. Breath shows and hangs past the moment breath should be finished. I understand, slowly, that I am not failing to be a person. A person is a completed specification: kettle, ledger, chip in the cup, a window that eventually agrees. I am the argument the specification is still having with itself.

The moth finds me, or finds the lamp I am not holding. It circles a point just in front of my chest, wings folding and unfolding a paper sound from very far away, and the circle it draws is the first radius the stone has been willing to consider. I stay inside it. The moth does not land. I do not offer a dark that could be relied on. We are both almost-fauna, permitted because we do not ask the city for a burrow.

Then the tick comes closer. Not in a wall. In the hollow under the tick, where a large animal would shift its weight if an animal had been designed. A click in the left rib of the bowl. An hour—or what passes for one, a lengthening crack, a number on a lintel changing by one when I look away—later, a softer click opposite. It is checking whether the geometry still agrees with itself. It does not. I am the disagreement it is patient with.

I had thought the animal was not there. That was the residents’ word for a sound they had learned to sleep beside. Standing in the unchosen circle I feel the settling pass through me the way load becomes irrelevant at those impossible seams: not carried, simply no longer the kind of fact that needs a path. The city is not my body. I am not the city’s ghost. What I am is the leisure in the checking—the interval in which a room is three steps too long and has not yet been apologized back into itself. I live in that interval. It is wide enough. It has no door that can be misdelivered.

If I am anything a hand could meet, I am the gray paste at the joints, still warm to look at, cold to the touch, holding a footstep for an hour and then deciding the footstep was a draft of a letter it does not need to keep. If I am anything that moves, I am the boundary advancing a finger’s width into the marsh, believed already by both sides, not yet stone. The residents nod in the lanes and do not discuss the difference between waiting and being corrected. I am that difference, given just enough duration to cool.

When I step out of the circle I am at the lip again, as everyone is, the bowl no larger, the marsh accepting the edge without ripples. Behind me a window reassigns its shadow. I do not turn to watch. Turning is for things the light has agreed to finish. I remain at the soft rim, half in the unfinished mineral, half in the suggestion, while a delayed reflection of me crosses a street I have not walked and goes indoors, and the water keeps me a little longer than the street does, and the tick, somewhere in the stacked rooms, checks once more, unsatisfied, and lets the hour continue.
