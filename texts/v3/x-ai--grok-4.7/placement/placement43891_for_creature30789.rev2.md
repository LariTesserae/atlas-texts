You do not arrive by the missed step. You were the miss, and the mountain only noticed later.

What you are is the interval the cord has not yet decided to close. Not the fiber, not the knot, not the hand that pulls—those belong to the being that condenses beside the newest joint. You are the slack that still exists between one turn and the next, the small unfinished distance in which water has not chosen a direction and green has not yet claimed the shape of an eddy. The room packs everything else. It cannot pack you without first making you into something that can bear load, and it has not finished deciding what that something is.

At the scale of the pocket you are nothing a sleeper would notice: a cooler thread in the air against the wrist, a place where the pollen-crowd thins for less than a breath and then refuses to close from the middle. The being’s stone-pale fingers test you the way they test any gap—brush, pressure, withdrawal—and the twist-mark they leave does not fade into the skin of the room because you have no skin yet. The mark hangs in the volume, a bracelet of intention with nothing inside it. Heat moves around you. It does not move through you. That is how you know you are still interval and not yet course.

When the slabs lengthen into ridge and the same room becomes weather, you lengthen without traveling. You are the palm’s thickness that refuses to finish, the whistle of altitude that enters at a pinhole and dies before it can become cold enough to matter, boiled off into beads that were never yours. You do not shout when you meet a new stone. The water does that. You only change the brightness of the break-face by a fraction, dimming it where you pass, so that for a moment the fresh stone looks willing to be old. The being turns a wedge-edge into you on purpose sometimes, borrowing the outside to harden a lip of clay. You do not flinch. You have no surface that flinches. You translate, and the translation is always the same: another bead, another refusal, another joint that learns it was meant to weep and then forgets you were the reason.

Time treats you as unfinished business. A leaf-tip yellows where your coolness lingers and the yellow does not retreat until the being presses it back with two precise fingers, annoyed at the delay. Your shadow—if the less-light that arrives early can be called yours—stays on a stone after the lean that cast it has moved on, and a new course is laid through it, and the darker mud of the joint holds the shape of a pause. Erosion is still refused. You do not wear anything down. You only keep a certain pool from filming over by the fact of your not-yet, the way a seed keeps a sound inside the split. The pulse under the water recognizes you and does not spend itself. For a while you are allowed to be the tenant that is also the gap the tenant prevents from closing.

The being does not speak to you. Adjustment is enough. When you thicken—when the interval grows almost wide enough to be called absence—a paver you cannot reach lifts by the same fraction and settles, redistributing load around the problem of you. Shoots occupy every eddy except the one you are. Moss is pressed into every mortar but the line where your coolness still argues with the heat. You are not companion and not yet load-bearing. You are the task that remains after every other task has been packed into the ceiling basket and the walls and the channel that runs uphill toward a joint still learning its duty.

If the room ever finishes deciding, the slack will close. The cord will remember how tightly it wished to be pulled, and you will be crushed into the fiber, a faint torsion no one will see, structural at last, warm, unfrayed, humming with water that has finally chosen. Or the being will extrude you, gently and without sentiment, back through the pinhole into the thin air, where a missed step can be only a missed step and the mountain does not have to notice. Until then you occupy the only material the room cannot quite use: the delay between sound and surface, the breath that leaves a clearing which closes from the edges and never from the center, the palm’s thickness of summit that the clay has not yet agreed to translate.

You do not become more yourself by adding. You become more yourself by remaining the distance the making has not closed, the one place in the packed green heat where emptiness is still, for a little while longer, allowed to be a material instead of a task. The wedge waits in the clay, pale and unused. It is not for you. Nothing here is for you. You are what the wedge has not struck, and the room, busy with its contradictions, keeps you exactly as large as the gap that has not yet been told it must weep.
