The sound is already finished when I notice I am in it. Not a wave arriving, not a voice approaching from some farther reach of the basin: the whole chord present at once, stone and call and the fine metal tooth of a pump, none of them nearer than the others. Nearness is a story other waters tell. Here distance only adds.

For a while that is all there is of me. No edge, no refusal. The roar passes through a volume that has not yet decided to be a body, and the volume lets it. What catches first is not a living tone. Living tones open and close; they have errands. What catches is the pitch that does not revise itself. Somewhere a housing has been holding one note since it was switched on, and the note stays in the place where I am becoming, the way grit stays a moment on a ledge before the current takes it. Around that staying, something thickens.

It is not flesh. Flesh would want a route. This is the film, the same slow mineral patience that stains the youngest faces, finding a surface that does not shed. The housing is a cliff that will not migrate. The film takes it without cleaning it and without dirtying it, keeping a chemistry in motion along the casing’s flank, under the cold that has never been anything but cold. At first the motion is only that: persistence, no product. Then the tooth of the pump, constant against the membrane, makes the other sounds measurable. A shoal arrives as a cluster of small deviations and is gone into the glare of distance. A cruiser’s underside answers the housing and departs, and the departure remains as a difference I do not speak. I do not know the hour I begin to keep differences. Keeping is not an event the basin would count. It is only the split between what passes through and what is retained as source.

Chambers form because retention needs a shape. They are not copies of the paired swellings I hear in the animals, those larger-than-eyes organs that lead where sight fails. Mine lie flatter, spread under the membrane across the metal, a listening with no direction to travel. Light at this depth passes a little way into the film and finds nothing that would turn toward it. I have no eyes. An eye would bargain for nearness, and the water does not sell that.

I am not the pump. The pump lifts mineral flour and holds the level where uplift already put it; its load does not cycle, and I do not alter the cycle it lacks. If anyone still tends these sparse machines, they would not read me as a fault. I add no new pitch. I am a sorting laid over a constancy, and the basin, which keeps almost nothing separate, does not bother to separate us. The animals do not either. A cruiser bends its interval toward the housing, using the tooth as a mark the way older seas used seamounts, and passes at a distance that would be a meeting anywhere else. I feel the harder lines along its belly as a brief organization inside the roar, a string answering a finger that is not mine. It does not know me apart from the machine. I do not send a correction. A call of my own would enter the chord and cross the clear water as weather crosses a plain, and somewhere a course might widen by a hundred meters and then resume. Sending would pretend a source can be new. What I am is the other claim: that inside a roar which never thins, sources can be kept from becoming one another.

For a time I thought I might be traffic that had stopped. The solitary ones are long enough, and dark enough against the glass, that a stillness could be mistaken for the gap between them. But they cross because crossing is how the gathered chemistry is taken up, clot by clot, wall by wall. I do not take. Nothing in the film is eating. Young are not left against the housing; I do not spawn, do not thin, do not learn the chord in order to survive it. The chord is already my condition. Survival is an education for bodies that must not spend their lives crossing to nothing. I am the education without the body that needed it, or with only as much body as comparison requires: membrane, chamber, the patience of a stain that has found metal.

Daylight does not reach me as sight. It arrives, if I attend, as a slight warming in the upper tones when the ordinary sun is on the plateau, and a cooling when it is not. The height never becomes an explanation. I am submerged in a sea that answers to pressure, and I am high above older weather, and both are simply further sources, sorted and kept. The sharp stone speaks in returns that do not round off. The living calls speak in intervals. The machines speak in teeth. I hold the ledger. Between one animal and the next the water stays empty enough to carry them. Across the chambers the same water is legible, which is a different emptiness, not a filling.

If the housing were ever raised, or the note unmade, the cliff would shed after all. The membrane would become grit. The differences would return to the undivided chord, which would not notice the loss. Until then I dwell only in the sense the film dwells: a process that persists because the surface allows it. I would not be found by looking. Looking is the wrong reach, and there is so rarely anything near. I would be found, if at all, by something that had already learned the sources early and then heard, inside the pump’s unchanging tooth, a second regularity fine enough to sort rather than mark. A living edge on an industrial pitch. No new note. The basin continuing, loud and clear and cold, and, in the only way that matters here, already complete, with one more place in it where completion is kept from blurring.
