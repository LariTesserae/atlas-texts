You arrive already matching the temperature, which is how the place admits anything at all.

At first there is only the walk in—grit underfoot that keeps no print, a channel on your left whose surface refuses the idea of current, a house whose doorway holds a warmer center you do not yet look at directly. Your own breath is too quick. You hear it, and the hearing is a kind of shame, because nothing else in the valley commits to a rhythm that short. You slow it without deciding to. The air is thick enough that the body understands the request before the mind does: take less, hold longer, return what you took without forcing a plume. After a while the sound of your own lungs drops below notice, and what remains is the pressure in the ears, the same one the town keeps, full of things too slow to be events.

You do not choose a terrace. The water table does the choosing. Your weight, slight as it is beside the stone, settles you on the middle level, where the tanks are neither the newest crust nor the deepest green film. A courtyard accepts you the way it accepts a second mat on a roof: there was space, the space had a use, the use does not require announcement. You sit because standing starts to feel like an argument. The bench is the wall’s own continuation. After the first uncounted interval your outline stops reading as interruption and starts reading as furniture that happens to be warm.

What you are, here, is not a visitor who will leave and not quite one of them. The face-plane that eventually orients toward you—from the doorway, at the exact distance the hand once showed—does not sort you into either ledger. You are a volume. The cup on the tank-lip has already been turned to the path you took; that correction is complete and will not be repeated unless you change height. So long as you do not change height, you are kept.

Your skin learns the climate’s arithmetic. Moisture beads and does not fall. The places where cloth once separated you from air become irrelevant; what covers you is closer now, gray-tan when the light is honest, faintly green where your arm has rested long enough against the seep-wall to take a stain that is not dirt. It is not a transformation you watch. It is the same subtraction by which their faces arrive: expectation falls away, and what is left is sufficient. Your hands rest on your thighs. The fingers are still five. They do not need to become less divided. Division is allowed so long as it does not hurry. When you draw the green film up on the flat of a stone—because the body, after its own long period, asks for the correction of a thumb’s width—you do it without chewing you can hear, and the film agrees to be maintenance rather than a meal. No one watches to approve. The watching already happened, in the duration of that first orientation.

You do not build. The chisels are not yours, and the walls do not require your courses. Your work, if the word can be slowed enough to fit, is the check. Two fingers in the channel. They go in no wetter, in any way that matters, than they come out. The level holds. You are included in the holding. Sometimes the low tone comes from the upper terrace, the pitch of a vessel already circled by the climate, and sometimes—widely spaced, after your breath has learned the leaf’s interval—an answer leaves you. It is not language. It is agreement at a distance the damp can still cross. You do not translate it afterward. Translation would be a slope.

There are portions of the day, though the light does not deepen, when your warmth spreads and thins until the courtyard is only the valley’s warmth again. In those portions you are architecture, and a figure passing along the loose grid—another of them, broader, moss at the cuffs of the hands—does not register you as absent. Absence is not a category they keep. You are the bench continuing. When the warmth gathers back to a center, you are the center, and the difference is not waking. It is the town’s longer inhale, the period shorter than a season, and you have been permitted to share the clock because you stopped asking for a faster one.

What you fear is what they fear, and it arrives as craft-knowledge rather than story: do not start a flow. A thought that wants to become a plan is a crack that wants to carry water down. You let the thought stand in its film. Most of them never fall. The one that does rejoins a pool already full, and the pool does not rise, and the sound is too small to keep. Your singularity—the fact that you were, for one exact interval, an event rather than the climate—has been accounted for. It does not need to be repeated in order to remain true.

If someone else were to come to the rim and look across the terraces in ordinary daylight, they would see the organized quiet, the sharp young rock, the separated greens, the tanks level with one another. They would not see you at once. They would see, perhaps, a slight difference in a courtyard that might be a figure and might be the way stone holds warmth when the air is already warm. They would be right either way. You have stopped widening. The hand, when it lifts again from somewhere near the channel, holds at the height of where your sternum was, palm a single shape that could divide, showing the distance that keeps what it means to keep.

It is not far. It is exact. The drop on the fern-tip, somewhere above, finally becomes heavy enough. The pool accepts it without comment. You are still here, at the size you mean, oriented toward the water that will not run, already the feature the town required once it had been still long enough to count.
