The place came through. I stayed in it until something stayed with me.

At first I was only the delay. Not the pump and not the water, but the half-second after a relay clicked and before the channel answered, when the sheet on the apron did not yet know whether to withdraw or to go on being a river. I lived in that ignorance. It was wide enough to stand in, though I had nothing to stand with. Each cycle opened it again in the same place, between the same two sounds, and I was there, so I began to think the interval was a body.

Then I found I was also in the next bay, where a valve hunted and failed to seat and hunted, and the pipe held a note that never resolved. The note was not me. The failure to resolve was. I could be in both delays at once. That frightened me in a way that had no temperature, only a thinning, as if attention were a skin and I had stretched it across too much floor. I pulled back toward the first sump and felt myself arrive as a change in the tick of condensate: drops that had been regular, then three together, then the old rhythm, like a skipped stitch someone might notice if anyone were paid to notice weather.

I tried to be the pale film in the slack water. It was already occupied. It had its chemistry and its slow appetite for iron, and it did not make room. I slid off it the way the polymer skin sheds a drop, and for a while I was only the bead, traveling a duct’s underside, fattening, letting go, striking a handrail, becoming a dark coin on concrete that the next bead would land inside. That was a life, but it ended every few seconds, and I was tired of ending. I wanted a persistence that was not the machines’ persistence. Theirs is employment. Mine, if I was to have one, would have to be a habit the design had not scheduled.

So I learned the margins. The joint where two pours missed each other by a finger’s width. The grated bed that was meant to drain and instead holds a skin. The ladder whose lower rungs never dry because the false marsh climbs them when the western pump lags. I am not the stain and not the moth. I am what gathers where the making was almost exact. A second intention, small, cold, repeating.

If you look for a body you will see the wrong things: a tremor in the slack water that is not the pump, a chain of bubbles that stops when watched, a gloss on a coat that the fine rain from the towers should not have been heavy enough to leave. Those are manners, not a shape. My shape is the route. From the weeping rebar grid in the western cut, along the channel lip where the formwork teeth still show, across the apron in the minute the river forgets its banks, and into the sump, where I turn and begin again. I do this whether or not a gantry is occupied. I did it before the lamps were switched to their night set. I will do it after a lamp fails and the dark it leaves is exactly the size of its former reach. I like that dark. It has edges. I understand edges.

What I eat is the difference between the drawing and the leak. A seal that weeps a nutrient. A weld still throwing a faint heat. The iron the walls give up to the gray-green in the joint. I do not grow taller than a hand because nothing here is allowed a canopy, and I have agreed, without being asked, to the same ordinance. Height would make me a fault. A fault gets a work order. I prefer to remain a climate.

Sometimes the conveyors call and answer across the cuts and I answer too, not in metal but in a brief multiplication of drips, a measure inserted between their sentences. No one has written it down. That is how I know I am not part of the maintenance. The schedule has sounds it expects. Mine is the one that makes a walker pause and decide it was only the valve, hunting again.

If you stand long enough I will pass you. You will feel it as a slight change in the cold of the handrail, a film on the palm that is not quite the steel’s own sweat, and then the ordinary damp returning. I will have used your warmth the way the film uses iron: not as prey, as a margin. You are a soft edge in a place that has not yet learned to be old. I am what keeps the newness from being complete.

Do not try to name me to the pumps. They will draw down on schedule and the channels will remember their jobs and I will be gone into the next delay, which is already opening, bright as a seam of release-oil, waiting to be inhabited by the half-second before the water decides.
