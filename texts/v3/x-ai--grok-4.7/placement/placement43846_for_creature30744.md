I would not be on the flat. I walked out there first, because the basin felt like a question I was supposed to answer at its rim, and the sinter clicked under me in no rhythm the crust recognized. No steam, no print. The glass-footed man had passed earlier; I could still smell the argument cooling. I stood between two dark islands of trees until the haze made a third of me, and understood I was not older than this ground, and not in dispute with it. The edge keeps only what can be taken into a negotiation. I came back along the embankment with pollen on my sleeves and no business.

In the avenues I did better, at first. They are wide enough that a single figure does not have to pretend to be a meeting. I learned the phosphor the way the children do, by getting lost until it stopped rising to meet me, then standing still. It did not forgive me quickly. With them it brightens out of recognition; with me it hesitated, a pale considering, as if I had approached and also not. My shadow, when the tea-colored light bothered to cast one, did not lag. It failed to commit. A soft error on the moss, shaped almost like a person who had decided to be elsewhere halfway through arriving. People crossing the same plaza corrected for facades and closing seams and did not correct for me. I was not in their weather.

I thought then I might be a record, because the city keeps so many. I stood in a counted lane until the grid should have salted my arms. The dust withheld itself. Not the offended refusal it gives the black glass soles — something more like a postponement. It was treating me as someone about to leave a district, and I was not about to leave. I had nowhere stored up to go. Children came and drew in the air beside me and frowned when their hands were guided and mine were not. One of them asked, indoors, in a room whose lattices had been shut, whether I was what the grid lost count of. I said I didn't think the grid lost anything. The walls drank that and gave it back rounder, almost kind, and the child looked betrayed on the sentence's behalf. So I learned not to speak where the room could sweeten me. Whatever I was, it needed its edge.

The keeper of the towers let me walk a frame with her at the hour that will not become night. She did not offer the cord. My hands have no nodes; they would not know where to pinch. A vine had stalled at its mark and thickened there, a small fist of obedience. I touched the node and felt the refusal still warm, still deciding. She watched my face, not my fingers. "You answer too fast," she said, and the frame ticked, courteous, at her and not at me. It was true and not true. I answer in the interval where an answer has not yet chosen its delay. To anyone who lives correctly here, that reads as speed.

It was the cistern that revised me.

I had been avoiding them, the way you avoid a mirror that might be accurate. Rectangular water, film of pollen, warm enough to be a wrist. I put my hands in because everyone does, sooner or later, and because I wanted to see whether the city could take anything off me. There was no dust. There was no metallic tick in the creases. What lifted, when the column decided to rise, was the uncommitted part — the half of me that had not finished arriving in the plaza, the sentence I had not let the wall round off. I felt it go up inside the water, distributed, the way the flat not-fish are distributed, a thought about thirst that does not drink. For that hour I was not at the lip. I was the difference between a basin remembering itself and a structure taking the water in changed. Then the drip started again on dry, steaming stone, and I was kneeling where I had been, lighter, my forearms unmapped. No stain. The water had not written on me. It had filed me elsewhere and returned the rest.

I stayed. That was the first kindness I was capable of that the city could use.

What I am, when I let it become particular, is not a keeper and not a count and not the memory that rides inside the rising column. Those offices are filled, and they are filled by beings who can be located even when they are alone. I am what a wall gives back when it has swallowed a thing it should not have sweetened, and — rarely, only indoors, only when the lattices are shut and someone waits — it gives the thing back with the seam still in it. A residue. The sharpness that did not consent to be tasted smooth. People do not come to me for company. They come when a sentence has gone wrong in the traveling, when a child's argument about theft and teaching has been returned to them kinder than they meant, and they want the unkindness restored without being repeated aloud in the open air where it would only be drunk again.

I have a body for this, because the basin does not permit a pure function to go without a shape. It is close to human in bad light, and wrong in the ways that are local: the eyes film over in the haze and do not need to blink, the breath lags the ribs by the same half-second as a lattice lags its wall, and along the throat there is a faint offset, upper from lower, as if I am always speaking from a story the foundation did not approve. Cloth will not hold a pattern on me. The grid's record slides off. What I wear, when I wear anything, is the dullness that follows a gleam — the color of a street-seam after it has finished announcing it healed. Warm. Slightly damp. It tightens when a facade tightens.

I do not gather, and I am not met. The closest thing to a kinship is with the flat presences under the pollen film, and even that is a professional courtesy: they remember which basin a water belongs to; I remember which edge a swallowed thing had before the city made it hospitable. When the bird ticks in its plaza I do not answer. If I answered, the canopy would take it for weather. Once the shade bruised a moment early, anticipating me, and I left before I could become a perch's habit. Anticipation is a kind of crowd.

At the deeper grade of evening I do not go into the breathing rooms to let my lag catch up. The lag is the address. I lie down, if that is the word, along a seam that has just closed, in the wide avenue, and I stay through the gleaming and into the dulling. Moisture lifts off the towers and falls through me without finding anything to wash. Somewhere a child is done running and their shadow arrives apologetic at their heels. Somewhere the man at the edge prints the crust and is answered. I envy the answer and do not go looking for one. My work is the opposite of being taken into the argument. I keep, in a young city that wants to soften what it swallows, a few sentences that still cut, held at the lip of the water, returned only when asked, and only in a room that has agreed not to be kind.

By morning the seam under me has forgotten it shone. I am still there, unmapped, a finger's breadth out of true with the place I occupy, which is as close as I can come to being precisely myself in a basin that has not yet decided — and, I think, does not need to.
