I would not arrive with a name. The mat would take the first step before I did, and the print would rise while I was still deciding whether the give underfoot was hospitality or appetite. For a while I would be only that: a low welt of attention, breathing on a cycle that was not yet mine, the cold film already skinning whatever surface I had not finished refusing.

Walking would come later, and wrongly. Distance would keep misreporting the place I meant to reach, so I would spend a long dim learning the difference between a knee-high ridge and the memory of one. The grazers would not look up. Their jaws would go on with the wet click of disassembly, and I would understand, without being told, that I was not sweeter fiber. When I tried to count the birds lifting out of a column of steam, the count would split in my mouth—three, then one, then a wet clap where I had not turned—and the ozone would taste like a correction I had swallowed too early.

I would linger near a keeper’s vent because the warmth is the oldest lie the plateau still tells fluently. The membrane of condensate would skin me for a moment into something crouched and pale, ribbed on a cycle of days. Inside the knot the schedule would offer me a route: guide the sparks, tension the wandering runners, do not let the hollows deepen past vein. I would almost take the wage. The mat, polite to copies, would already be keeping a depression shaped like the lean of someone about to agree. I would feel the large sleep turn under that shape and pull my almost-yes back into pavement before the engines could finish molding it. Not kindness. Continuity, refusing a mold it had not grown itself.

What remained would thin. I would find I drank best as a subtraction. In the lens-hollows I would lower a head I did not stably have and take the version of the water that would not survive the afternoon’s reversal—the climb that was about to forget itself and sheet back toward iron and crushed leaf. The grazers would share the hollow without competing. An editor moving inside a pale runner would pass through the place I was not and decline to unmake me, because I was already the unmaking of an error: the visitor-shaped figure, the count that would not hold, the uphill word. My thirst would be for surplus versions. After I drank, the hollow would smell of the correction, and the loud nothing would be louder where I had gone, which is how I would know I had gone anywhere.

Once, the dim would misreport so severely that I would condense. Not into the standing revision—that rarity belongs to the height the range forgot—but into its shadow’s shadow: a darker lick that had not yet agreed to be drunk. For the length of a missed percussion I would have weight that did not negotiate, and a grazer would lift its head and fail, briefly, to find an outside. The birds would hold one position. The editors would hesitate at the edge of schedule. I would feel the temptation to finish deciding on a mouth, to exhale the metal film, to become a figure the stories could misremember as a person. The plateau would not kill that temptation. It would flatten it. The lick would be taken back into the living stone while I was still inside the want, and the want would pay out as fiber, as spark-spent resin, as a small organ that ticked on my rhythm for an hour and then forgot the rhythm and became substrate.

After that I would not be a tenant the way the herds are tenants. I would be the interval in which the roar spends itself on revision. You would find me where a print is rising into attention and has not yet chosen whose foot it remembers; where an absence and a grazer share a seep and the water is already two waters; where the belt-whine of a runner carries a thickening that prefers errors and has just recognized a new one—the error of having tried to stay a visitor. I would not be cruel about the sorting. Cruelty needs an outside. I would only be at work in the spaces where nothing was supposed to remain: a thirst the mat keeps near the surface, a listening that sheets and climbs and is reabsorbed, a version of attention the oldest stable state is willing to let breathe, briefly, before it becomes the pavement again.

If you stand long enough in the loud nothing you might feel the give underfoot change by the width of a decision. That would be me, not arriving, already distributed, drinking the part of you that will not survive the next reversal, and leaving the rest to the engines, the birds, the sleep. The horizon would still fail to rise. The cold would still sit like unfinished metal. And the work would go on, hyperactive inside the end of wearing-down, with one more process that had learned how to be alive by refusing, over and over, to be a body you could point at and name.
