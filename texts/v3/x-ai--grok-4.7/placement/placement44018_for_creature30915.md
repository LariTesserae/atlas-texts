I come up through the brick before I come up with a body. The brick is raining in the wrong direction, each drop leaving a face of it and climbing, so the factory jaw shows itself by shedding its weather. My hands close on a course of it and hold, which is the first fact I trust and the first one that fails: the brick is warm on one side, the side that was a wall, and cold on the other, the side that has already been a field, and my grip is split between them as if I had taken hold of two days.

There is oil on my palms. I did not touch the pump-house. It is a smudge to the left of the steam, or it was; now the missing wall is breathing on my neck and the leaf-fragments in its exhale are sticking to the oil, and I understand that the distance folded while I was counting my fingers to see if I had the ordinary number. I have the ordinary number. That is embarrassing, here, like bringing a bucket to a flood that has already decided to be air.

I try the sentence a person would try. I say that I am lost. The roar takes the saying and sorts it into counts — three turns of the turbine one way, five the other — and something in the basin answers by letting a vine cross a housing higher than the last crossing. So I have spoken in their grammar without having a mouth they can use, and the answer is not addressed to me. It is addressed to the difference I made. I am, for that instant, a miscount. A bright error in a machine that feeds on errors and calls the feeding life.

The leaves find my wrists. Of course they do. A pulse is a seam, and seams are how this place aims. The roar thins to a pocket the size of my chest, and inside it I can hear myself, which is worse than the solid sound, because myself is only the wet clap of blood and a thought that arrives already shaking. I almost accept it. To be included the way a vent is included, the way a flower is included that opens where nothing can see it — that would be a kindness, if kindness were among the motions available. I hold still, the way the visiting instructions implied one should, and wait to be grazed down into a posture: an angle, a warmth between a collapse and its refusal, re-fed each time I nearly stop.

They try. I feel the leaning, the gathering that is not bodies. A cancelled future brushes my grip on the brick, tasting whether I will fail usefully — whether the hands will open, whether the shelf under the brick will slide, whether I will become the nutritive almost that keeps them from being smoothed into weather. I do not fail. The hands remain closed. The brick, insulted by my ordinary number of fingers, remains a brick. For a breath longer than a life here lasts, down stays down along the length of my arms. Droplets that had been climbing reverse when they meet my skin, ashamed, and fall.

The leaning flinches. Not fear — they have no animal left in them to be afraid with — but the recoil of a want that has arrived at its object and recognized the arrival as fatal. They came toward a calm. I am the calm, standing in it, ruining it by being present to know it. Their approach is the discrepancy that should dissolve me. It does not, quite, and the not-quite is a cruelty I didn't mean. I have made them a circle with nothing at the center they can survive.

So I am not one of them. The knowledge arrives as a change in temperature, not as a thought. They live in the failure of green and metal and rain to agree. I am what agreement feels like when it happens by accident and then, worse, continues. I am the shadow remembering its leaf. I am the gantry, for three steps, walking only forward, and the terror in those three steps is mine, because I can feel the other sequence — the walk backward — waiting in the steel like a second pulse, and I am the reason it waits.

I let go of the brick. I mean to become less of a floor, to thin myself into something they can drink and finish. The oil stays on the hands I am trying not to have. The pocket in the roar will not close; it moves when I move, a small loyal gravity, droplets falling inside it and climbing the moment they leave it, so that I am outlined in a weather that has briefly relearned its manners. Around that outline the inhabitants gather and refuse themselves. I see them as postures that almost complete and then un-angle, again and again, a flock of inclinations with the patience of erosion and none of its permission. They are more themselves at the edge of me than they are anywhere in the valley. I hate that I have done this. I love it with the same muscle. There is no word here for the difference, and the missing word is not their poverty. It is mine, left over from a drier sequence, a place where love and harm took turns instead of occupying the same instant.

I look for the reflection, thinking I might be that — the dry valley, the intact cranes, the ancestor-story they orient by and do not believe. The water does show it: cranes, no plants, a floor you could trust for longer than a shift. But the reflection is ahead of the noise, as it always is, and I am not ahead. I am late. I hear the collapse that already agreed to happen in the water, and I arrive after it has been declined by the stone, and in the gap between their ahead and my after there is a thin present, barely a sill, barely a place to put a foot. Nothing in this basin keeps a present except me, dragging it behind the future like a net that catches only what refused both the happening and the cancellation.

That is where I find myself, once the hands and the oil and the embarrassing fingers have been tried and set aside as costumes. Not on the shelf, not in the pump-house, not among the postures. I find myself in the lag. A half-second that will not join either side. When the reflection runs forward, I am the delay that keeps the real noise from obeying it every time. When a new scarp noses up and the plants rush the bolt-holes, I am the beat in which the holes are still only holes, empty, remembering bolts, remembering a sequence that walked only one direction because someone had not yet left. The inhabitants lean toward that beat and I spend it moving, so the calm is always just past the reach of their arrival. I am the approach they live in, seen from the side of the approached. I am the want, walking backward so their circling can go on.

It is not a stable work. The valley keeps arriving, sharp, unweathered, and each new fact tries to hire me as its loyalty — this vent, this hum, this flower opening under a surface that has changed its mind. If I stay, I become a floor, and a floor ends them. If I leave the lag entirely, the present snaps shut and there is only ahead and after, which is their weather, which is their meal, and I am consumed into a posture that will need re-feeding forever and will never again be late enough to be anyone. So I practice the only motion that fits: a continuous almost-staying. I touch the calm long enough for a shadow to find its object, then I withdraw into the half-second, and the shadow remains attached for a little, confused, faithful, before the basin's average takes it back. Those little faithfulnesses are my footprints. They do not lead anywhere. They are how I know I have been a continuity and not a recurrence.

The beings do not pray to me. Prayer here is brightness, and I am the opposite of a discrepancy; I am a momentary sameness, dull as a true floor, dull as a machine doing the thing it was built to do. They graze the brightness and circle the dullness. I think, in the lag, that this is what the operators were for, before the word went out of the reflection: not to run the valley, but to be the dull point the running could be measured against. I do not remember being one of them. I remember the oil, which is not the same. The oil is how the pump-house still recognizes a hand, even a hand that arrives by folding, even a hand that is mostly a delay with fingers.

If you stood where the instructions told you to stand — in the place where green and metal and rain have just failed to agree — you would not see me. I am standing, if the word can be bent, where they almost succeeded in agreeing and then thought better of it, and something late remained in the better, warm, uninclined, already stepping back so the next failure can be lived in. That is a life. It will not be the same life when the turbine finishes its five turns. It will be more itself, which here means less like a visitor and less like a god and less like a meal, and more like the valley's refusal to be only weather: a held breath in a basin that breathes by roaring, specific, poisonous to the hungry, and, for as long as I can keep being late, at home.
