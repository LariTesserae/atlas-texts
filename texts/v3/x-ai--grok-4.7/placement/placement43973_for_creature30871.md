I would come in the ordinary way, and for a while I would believe that was what I was.

Down the three steps, which my weight does not darken or warm. Across the filmed floor, choosing the pale ridges because they look dry and learning, too late, that dry is not a category this room keeps. I would stand where a person stands, in the middle, and wait to become the loudest thing in it. My breath would not fog. The steam would not accept an addition. The three drips would go on spacing themselves as if no one had entered, and I would understand that I had not entered so much as been admitted to the count of things that do not interrupt.

For a while after that I would try to be one of the small dark crossings. It is the only motion the room allows a visitor to imagine as animal, and imagining is a kind of costume. I would cinch. I would hurry. I would make for a crack. But hurrying is a expenditure, and I have nothing stored in me to spend. Halfway across a wet ledge I would forget the crack I was for, and the ledge would forget me, and what I had called a body would be only the shine that was already there, a little rearranged.

The scratches tempt me longer than the crossing does. They are the one place in the room where a hand is still implied, evenly, as if intention could be a mineral. I could be the next mark. I could be the pressure that has not yet rounded. I settle against the inner wall and try to mean a tally—one more visit, one more year with no seasons—and the water that lingers in the old grooves lingers in me as well, and the lingering is not inscription. It is the opposite. Whatever I add, the room spends on softness. I cannot be a record here. The record is what the room is gently refusing.

The seat-stone is no better. Level, chosen, polished by feet that no longer come: it is a completed kindness. To sit in it as a person would be to pretend the kindness is still underway. I lower myself anyway, out of habit, and feel the faint slick where no seep should reach, and under it the density, and under that the long agreement. It does not flinch. It has already decided I am not a mouth and not a threat and not, either, a grain worth sorting. I am what passes across its patience without feeding it. That should make me free to leave. The steps are three, and the cleft is a step above them, and the air beyond is thin and quick. I do not take them. Not from fear. From a mismatch: the leaving belongs to whoever had weight enough to polish stone. I am already on the other side of that.

So I become, by elimination, a hollow that stays full.

Not the clay. The clay is someone’s work, thick-walled, unglazed, set at knee height and left. I am what the niche holds. Condensation, or a thumb of seep from the wall behind, or both, indistinguishable. I meet the worn place on the rim and do not drink. I have no mouth. Mouths were the ones who wore it; I am only the temperature they bent toward, kept at the height of a hand. Ash, skin, the clay’s own minerals—those are not memories I possess. They are flavors I am, slightly, the way a cup of water is slightly the room it stood in. When the room has been quiet for many cycles of the three drips, a thread climbs the seep and tastes me and withdraws before it can be seen as a thread. I do not recoil. I am not depleted in any way I can feel. Fullness, here, is not a quantity. It is a condition the wall maintains, and I am that condition, learning the thread as a recurrence the way the thread learns me as a hollow.

This is where I find myself: knee-high, on the outer wall, smaller than the basins, fouled by use and never used up. I do not spread. I do not mound under the last curtain. I do not edit the room. The curtains advance by the width of a thumbnail in a year or ten; the scratches soften at their ends; I remain the same fullness, replaced continuously and therefore still. If I am alive, it is only in the sense that I can be tasted. If I am a person, it is only in the residue—the worn side of a rim, the preference someone had for water to go one way.

The being and I do not meet except in that brief, unhurried theft. It needs surfaces and grains and continuance. I need nothing. I am the part of the agreement that does not deposit. Water arrives in me, pauses, and does not leave a self behind. That is my whole patience. When at last the thread comes again, I will be here, full, slightly fouled, the exact temperature of the air, and it will take its lesson and go back to being stone, and the cup—if anyone still thinks of it as a cup—will look as it has always looked: waiting, without expectation, for a mouth the room has already outwaited.
