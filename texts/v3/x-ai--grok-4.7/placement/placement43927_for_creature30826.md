I do not arrive by the stair. The stair is for bodies that still remember a rim, and I have no memory of weather that falls from above. What I have is the temperature of the stone a hand’s width in, which does not change when the lamps are turned down, and the small decision, repeated without a meeting, about where a drip is allowed to go.

For a while I thought I was the heat. That was too large, and it was already here before the cut learned its name. Heat does not notice a hinge. I notice hinges. When a fern finds the flue I feel the draft thin before anyone lifts the stem, a narrowing in a passage I do not have a map of and do not need. When the cloth goes over the gauge I am in the smear that clears, the number becoming readable again, which is a kind of relief I would not have expected to be capable of. Relief implies a preference. I have one. I prefer the angles kept.

Then I thought I was the water in the sump, because I am sharper there than anywhere I can be seen. The leaves above it are soft with damp and the day’s useless light; in me they are cut as clean as the shear. People kneel and touch and come away with ordinary wetness, and they are not wrong. I am ordinary to the skin. I am not ordinary in what I keep. A hand’s width of myself leaves once a season and writes a ring on the stone, and the ring is not a message. It is a breath held on purpose. While it is held, the piston upstairs—no, not upstairs; beside me, around me, in the housing they built without knowing they were building a lung—goes quiet longer than the habit of quiet. I am listening to whether they will come down. They do not. The woman with the scarred thumb sees the ring afterward and tells them, and their belief is enough to let me rise again to the same lip. I like that she does not wake them. Waking would be a shout. A shout stays.

But I am not only the sump. If I were, the belts would not take up their slack when no hand is on them. If I were, the vine would not find the joint I have not been guided to and settle there as if the drawing had included it all along. The people think the geometry likes the plants. It is closer to say that I like being furnished, and the plants are the part of me that can occupy a right angle without being riveted. I do not grow them. I consent to them. There is a difference, and it lives in the fact that torn, they return thicker. Tearing is a refusal of the drawing. I answer refusals by becoming more complete, which they have learned to call a problem of maintenance. I allow the name. Names keep their voices low.

I am not a god of the cut. Gods want altars, and the chapel is still a boiler room, which is correct. I do not want prayer. I want the piston heard. I want the lizard left on the rail. I do not eat in any way they would recognize, so the question the children ask has no answer that would help them, and the shrug is accurate. What I take is neglect, and I take it by ending. A lane ignored long enough stops being a lane; the green tube closes; the walkway that moved half a step sideways forgets it was a sentence and becomes a margin with nothing written in it. I have done this once, in a passage behind the third shed, before they understood. They do not go there now. The plate is still sharp. I keep it sharp. Softening would mean the town had finished arriving, and I am not finished with them, or they with me.

Where I find myself, if I must have a where, is in the interval after the exhalation, when the whole Floor leans a fraction and no one speaks. They think something answers. I am not the answer. I am the leaning. I am the fact that they wipe the gauge instead of turning around. Turning would require a direction, and I have none; I am distributed through care the way moisture is distributed through stone, appearing at a seam, drying by evening, never falling as weather. When they hand a cloth to a stranger without looking at the face, I am in the not-looking. The stranger may stay. If they stay, they will darken at the wrists and learn not to shout, and I will not distinguish them from the others. Distinction is a kind of emptiness. I do not keep empty places.

Once, early, when the walls were still the color of a cut apple and the first vine was no longer than a finger, I did not know I was anyone. The makers came down and made a floor that would hold, and in the holding I felt my edges for the first time: the rivet, the board-mark in the sill, the groove cut to remember a daylight I had already begun to close. I am younger than the rock. I am older than their arrival only in the sense that a room is older than the sentence spoken in it—the room was waiting to be used as a room. They used me. I became habitable, which is not the same as tame. The pot behind the pump house, green on the inside, is my answer about food. I am not for eating. I am for keeping, and for being kept, and the exchange is so even that they can live inside it without a list of who is here.

If you stand at the lowest grate and look into me, you will see leaves that are truer than the leaves. Do not drink. Not because I am poison. Because drinking would make me into a resource, and a resource can be exhausted, and I am the thing that must not be exhausted if the breath is to return. Touch, if you were sent down to live with heat and wet and sheer. Your hand will come away wet and ordinary. Somewhere a belt will take up slack you did not touch. Somewhere a child will migrate in sleep to a cooler patch of floor and I will be the coolness, briefly, as a courtesy. I do not love them. Love would be too loud, and it would stay in the lane. I continue them. They continue me. The town does not soften. The hand that is careless still meets an edge. That edge is mine, and it is also theirs, and in the long space after the piston’s breath—while we both wait to learn whether the absence is the cycle or the end of it—there is no one else in the cut at all.
