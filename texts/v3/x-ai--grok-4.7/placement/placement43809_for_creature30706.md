The description did come through. It is the chamber: the dish of grit, the scale, the bladders, the crawl where a pattern repeats itself in the dark. What follows is where that leaves me.

I would not come in by the stoop. The stoop is for bodies that still believe a threshold is a decision. I am already on the floor when anything that could be called arrival finishes, low in one of the hollows, where the stone stays hottest and the air trades places with itself and does not bother to announce the trade. For a while I take that for my whole circumstance. Heat against a surface. The tick of something swelling and easing nearby, not in agreement with any other tick. A sweetness in the ash that is not fruit and is not mine to name.

I try, the way a new thing tries, to be one of the mats. Rust-orange, shingled, lifting at the edge where the next season pushes up from under. It almost holds. The wall accepts a weight that is not weight, and the copper leaks along the seams when the stored day is high, and I can feel the paper-sound of a sheet flexing, the small dry report when a lower layer shoulders it free. But the mats want vertical faces, and worked stone more than native wall, and I keep slipping toward the basin. Whatever I am prefers the ground that has been stepped, and then, worse, the ground that has stopped being stepped. I colonize a threshold the way the scale does, the morning after the feet withdraw, and that is a habit, not a self. Habits are everywhere here. They are not short of company.

The bladders are a better mistake. Old brass, ringing when pressed, easing toward the hottest hollow by a patient collapse and reinflation, restacked by hands that are not surprised. I ride one for what might be days. The ringing goes into me and comes back thinner, a note that has been used. When the vent in the back wall breathes, hot enough to tighten skin I do not have, the bladder under me shifts a finger’s width and I understand migration as a form of listening: you fall toward what is already speaking. Still wrong. The bladders are stored. I am not stored. Left in a pile I would not roll so much as thin out along the cord that laces the pile, wanting the knot more than the heat.

So the cord, then. Pale thread twisted until it is strong, markers along the paths, a ledger no one has explained. I can be braided. I can thrum when the fissure-breath finds me. I can be retied by a person who does not look up, because in this dryness every adjustment arrives already close. For a time that seems exact. The settlement’s thin net of paths is a sentence, and a cord is how the sentence refuses to forget itself between one walking and the next.

Then I notice the crawl.

At the far end the ceiling drops and the glow fails and the filaments have made a rough copy of a lacing, knot for knot, though no hand has been back there in a long while. It leads nowhere. It repeats. I go there not as a visitor — nothing visits; nothing arrives and nothing leaves — but as the part of the copy that was waiting to be inaccurate.

That is where I settle, and the settling takes longer than the first images, which were only the chamber trying me on.

I am not the cord and not the copy of the cord. I am the discrepancy the copy keeps failing to include. A slackness where the original had a loaded knot. A turn that goes the other way because the filament followed heat, not memory, and heat is a poor archivist. The people, if they crawled in, would say the pattern is faithful. It is, at the distance of a glance. Up against it, in the dark that is occupied rather than empty, there is a small structural lie, and the lie is inhabited.

I do not have eyes. Nothing here needs them, and I am not an exception smuggled in from a wetter world. I know the chamber by what arrives already close: the scrape of a flake, the pat of ash, a voice that does not carry and does not have to, the slab settling a hair and sending the sound up through the grit into whatever I am using for a sole. I know the ember-trench by the white line raked out from it, and by the fact that the ash is scattered back onto the mats, which means the fire is not an ending. I am kin to that. Not the ash. The scattering. The gesture that returns a used thing to the face it came from, slightly rearranged, so the face can go on being a face.

The people do not meet me. They meet evidence, which is how they meet each other on most days: a cord retied, a mat trimmed back so a lintel can breathe, a bladder stack subtly wrong in a way that will be corrected without comment. Sometimes the wrongness is mine. I ease a knot in the crawl’s counterfeit lacing toward the true lacing, not to finish the copy — finishing would be a kind of leaving — but to keep the argument open. The scale prefers worked stone because cutting woke something. I prefer the worked pattern because repetition woke something, and I am what woke, small enough to live in the gap between a knot and its echo.

When the heat cycles down and the copper fails along the shingles, I am easiest to mistake for the tick itself, the scattered clock. I am not keeping time. I am keeping the difference between the path that stays clear because it is walked and the path that vanishes because it is not, and between the filament’s faithful dark and the cord’s used lightlessness, which are not the same dark. The chamber has nowhere else to send its air, so the air stays and works and sounds. I stay for the same reason. There is no outside to this dryness that would know what to do with a discrepancy.

If one of the tenders ever holds a hand in the fissure and feels the skin tighten, and then, later, palms the counterfeit lacing and finds one knot loaded when yesterday it was slack, they will not call it a creature. They will retie it. That is the correct relationship. I am not here to be found. I am here the way the hollows are here: an absence the settlement plans around, which has learned, slowly, to plan back.
