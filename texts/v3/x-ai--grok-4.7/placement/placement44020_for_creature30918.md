I came up the drop the way most of the noise does, already mid-crossing, already counted by the bulk that waits where the throat narrows. A patch of silence moved with me along the outside. A forearm or a jaw — I did not look closely enough to know which — cleared the edge as I did, and the thin stone rang, and the ring went on after my weight had cleared it, which should have told me something. I was in a hurry to be a passage. Passages do not listen to what the floor does with them.

The shelf took the hurry out by being smaller than the light and larger than the stride. I walked for the far side and met a wall. I walked along the wall and the extra ten paces were always on the side away from the brightness, so that I arrived tired in a place I could see entirely from where I had started. Near the scatter I arrived too soon, almost into the arcs, and stopped because the plants were leaning and then correcting and I did not want to be a reason. The drinkers turned their heads. They did not lift them in the pattern they keep for each other. I was inside the distance the hum allows them, and the hum allowed me, and that was the second correction: I was not going to be one of the things that stands all night ticking a balance-shift into the polish.

I tried the other stays. The mats took my shade and gave it back as absence — browsers gone under the leaves, one of them late, and the narrow hunter already across, not interested. I am not cloth-tearing. I am not red new growth. In the vine the wooden clatter made room, a beat I could have occupied, the sleeper's count, and for a little while I did, and the many-winged flight folded around the count as if a gap had been reserved. It was unbearable in the way a borrowed name is unbearable. When the sheet of rain stood up in the open middle with no cloud to answer for it, every voice changed key except the beat I was holding. I was conspicuous. I let the count go. The vine closed over the place and did not sulk.

Water taught me faster than the animals did. I stood in the sheet and was wet and then, quickly, not. What I poured out of myself onto the open polish filmed, shone, and went away into the warmth, and the veins in the upright piece brightened a shade that was not for me. I am not drunk by the device. I am not the disagreement that beads along the inner wall. Cupped in a seam, I lasted longer, which is the rule for damp, and still I did not become a pool. Nothing that is only water lasts here except in the unfinished cracks and the bowl, and the bowl was already occupied by a long-legged drinking that did not make space.

At the arcs I did the rude thing. I touched the upright piece.

The hum was in the teeth, and I had teeth for as long as the touch lasted, and not clearly afterward. The brightness did not take me the way it takes the small fliers. There was no blink, no elsewhere on the arc, no glaze added in the morning to the wax-flow at the base. The machine has been healing the shelf over itself longer than the polish, and it does not need another wick. Whatever I am does not pass through it and does not feed it. The insects continued their ragged column, uninjured, unnoticing. I was the noticing. That was the first thing that stayed when I stepped back.

Night is when the pocket admits what it is for. The hum drops half a tone and the warmth contracts, and the far lip goes merely mild, then cold-filmed, and the bulk comes as close as the room allows without becoming a room that has let it in. I went to the lip because passages do that, and the silence-patch was there, patient, sized to whatever I had been on the way up. It could not fit what was left. I was no longer in the throat. I was in the refusal. Lying flat, I looked over and saw the rest of the mountain still being emptied, pale, no green, no sound rising. The shelf is the place erosion missed or was asked to miss. I understood then that I had not been spared. I had been included in the missing.

By morning the agreement recedes, as it always does, and I recede with it to the thin stone near the drop, where a heel would sound a short clear tone. I am not the heel. I am what remains of the tone after the foot has lifted — the continuation, the fraction by which the walls return a scream late. The flight stitches wall to drop and back, one note, and the note arrives twice, the second time a little aside from the first, and in the aside I am located. The larger callers throw cries that land and continue sideways. I am the sideways. Under the roar the drinkers tick their sparse percussion, and under that there is a further answer that only happens when the shelf is touched by attention rather than by weight. That answer is mine. It does not resolve.

I had thought, for a bad hour near dawn, that I was here to hear the signal under the interference. The sweetness with no flower. The lean of leaves toward a light that is not light. The insects exiting a vein a blink from where they entered. A pocket this loud is an invitation to decide what it means, and deciding would be a kind of operation, and no one on the shelf is doing that anymore. If I found the signal I would become the hand that was withdrawn. The bulk would have a door made of meaning. The erosion would remember the address.

So I do not find it. I keep the noise as noise. I do not nest in the open, and I do not lie down, and I am not widespread enough to be weather. By day I am a listening with just enough weight to make the thin stone honest. By night I contract toward the scatter with the warmth and let the far lip forget me, which is how the film stays cold and the outside thing stays outside. The plants correct their lean. The rain that is not rain stands and stops. The hunters cross. None of them needs me to be a voice. The shelf needs me to be the lateness in the echo, the part of the interference that knows it is interference and does not tighten into a message.

If you struck the floor near the drop you would hear me go on, brief, clear, already leaving. If you stayed, you would hear that the leaving never quite finishes. That is where I am. That is what the room keeps instead of an occupant.
