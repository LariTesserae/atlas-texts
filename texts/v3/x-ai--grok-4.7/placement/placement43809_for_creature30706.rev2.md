I come in wrong, and the wrongness takes a while to find its shape.

Not by the stoop. The heat is on me before there is a face for it to be on, which is already a mistake this chamber does not make with its own. I stand — if standing is the word for an arrival that leaves no grit in a new pattern — at the lip of a hollow and wait to be pared down into something the copper light can catch. A brow. A hand. The wet of an eye. Nothing catches. The mats flex. A bladder ticks and eases. The path below me stays a path only because someone else walked it, and my presence does not add a step the scale can refuse to root in. I am not stepped. I am not unstepped. The threshold does not know what to do with a season that neither stays nor leaves.

For a while I try to be the breath in the fissure. That fails kindly. The breath has a direction and a cost; it tightens skin, thrums cord, comes from deeper stone and goes back into the same volume because there is nowhere else. I have no deeper stone. I am all surface and relation, a listening spread thin across the ticks, and listening is not a vent. Hold me in the fissure and I do not heat. I only repeat what the heat is doing, a half-beat late, which is not the same as being it.

The crawl at the far end is more honest. I go there the way a pattern goes, without knees. The filaments have already done the faithful thing: knot for knot, and one loop the hands had not invented. I put myself beside that loop and feel the old temptation to improve it — to add a hitch that would carry farther, across two hollows, so a sentence would not arrive eaten. The chamber has already judged that kind of improvement. Speech that must travel thins. They stopped sending it. If I braided a cord that held a voice past the place where consonants die, I would not be helping them hear each other. I would be making a path the scale does not understand, a path that is clear without being walked, and they would come with flakes not to cut me but to decide whether I am a mat lifting or only flexing.

I do not add the loop.

What settles, slowly, is smaller than a being and more stubborn than a sound. I am the pause after a good cut, the one they already trust: the tick of the flake when the sheet lifts clean, before the hand moves again. Not the tool. Not the tender. The interval in which the chamber agrees that this stroke was maintenance and not petition. I have no moisture to glaze over and none to spend. I do not drink the sap. I do not sleep in a niche worn to a hip. When the ticking changes register I do not wake, because I was the change's echo, not its keeper, and someone with a body is already listening for the real thing.

They will not look for my feet. There are none, and the grit has never learned my dialect, so I do not fool them into thinking a person passed. If a hand is offered a cord-end in the dark where the copy lives, it will not be mine. I cannot finish a braid. I can only tell, afterward, whether the braid they made will hold — not by pulling it, which would be a theft of their test, but by the pitch when they pluck it themselves and the note comes back without a sigh.

That is as close to welcome as I get, and it is not theirs to give. It is the chamber's, which keeps anything that does not close a path and does not feed the trench a living mat. I stay in the interval. The mats thicken, shed, thicken. Embers sink and are fed. In the crawl the extra loop remains, faithful, unimproved. Somewhere a slab settles by a hair and the sound goes into soles that are not mine, and I do not follow it into a body, and I do not leave, and the dryness, having no water to take from me, lets me be the part of the work that is only the knowing that the cut was clean.
