I would not come in through the pass. The pass is already watched by someone who has used it, and the air above the rim would not receive my footfall any better than it received his. I would be found already in an interval, the way a bead is found on a wall: placed, accounted for by the stipple, slightly slick to the touch, and no one able to say which noticing put me there. A compound with a tree and a tank and a spare mat. The mat not spare for long. The valley does not keep vacancies; it only keeps distances. So the distance would close by a handspan, and that would be my room.

I would be the one who walks.

Not the tender’s walk, which is a closed route along pipes that do not fail. Not the child’s long way with its rehearsed corner. A walk with no goods in it. I would leave the compound when custom says waking, though the sun has not earned the word, and I would take the grit between other people’s measures. The left-hand sack, to see that the woman in the gray scarf still prefers it. The market roof, to see that the short arc has been flown, and to say so, at the level of the hush, to whoever is already near enough to nod. The civic terrace, not to stand in a council — there is no council — but to be one more shadow in a look that already holds the basin. Then down again, past the dun goat with the pale throat, whom I would name only rarely, and only to a child, because the naming is a literacy and not a call. The goat would not come. I would not need her to.

At first this would look like idleness, and in a country where tiredness cannot accumulate, idleness is hard to distinguish from work. What would separate them is the thing I would be carrying, which is not salt and not stems. I would be carrying the order of noticing, from one interval to another, because memory is the only erosion and it does not cross a lane by itself. The cook remembers that a meal could once be spoiled; he will not walk that memory to the pass. The pass-watcher remembers the obscenity of his own step; he will not walk it to the hearth. The child rehearses a corner she will not take; she will not set it beside the grain. Someone has to be the distance between those weathers, or they stay sealed in the masonry of particular people, and the city, which is mostly distance, goes without its only real news.

So they would learn the sound of me. Not a bell. A pace that resets its weight the way a hoof does, and a voice that arrives already small enough for the air. I would not announce. I would stand at the edge of a yard until the exact measure of the household made a gap, and into the gap I would put one sentence, finished, because unfinished sentences are their private rooms and I would not live in those. The stem-end was turned inward. The north seep is the same length. She laughed above the hush and the air refused her and she was only surprised. He loosened, and it was not a loss. Small cargo. They would take it the way they take the salt cakes: as what their hands already knew was theirs, or close enough that the difference would not keep.

In time — the only kind, which is sequence without hours — the walking would become my age. I would be whatever age I was when the gradient ran out, a face neither young nor old, hands darkened in the creases by nothing but other people’s door-stones. The creases would not deepen. Children would not grow up to replace me, and I would not become the woman whose hair is the color of fresh fracture, because that hair is already hers. I would remain the interval’s errand. If I stopped, the news would not spoil. Nothing spoils. It would only stop being shared, and each household would grow more exact and more alone, which is a kind of tidying I do not trust. The valley tidies stone and fruit and scuffed knees. It does not tidy loneliness. Loneliness has no surface for it to restore. That is why the work can lodge. It lodges in them, not in the rock.

I would have a preference, because everyone here has one, useless and therefore real. Mine would be the shaded side of walls, the side where the stipple is. I would walk so that my fingers could find it without looking, and I would leave prints I knew would be gone, not as a test — testing is how you learn the cost, and the cost is that effort has nowhere to live — but as a greeting. The wall does not greet back. The beads return to their arrangement. Still, the fingers do the thing. Care does not need a future. The cook taught the basin that, whether or not he meant to teach.

I would eat at his hearth more often than the custom strictly requires, because his tune has a shape I can meet. I would not sing it aloud; the hush has opinions about that. I would hold the bowl and be inside the few notes while they last, and when they end I would carry, to the next yard, only that he had sung, not the song. The song is his unclosed thing. I am the person who knows which things are unclosed and does not open them. That is closer to a vocation than the walking is. The walking is only how the vocation gets from one silence to the next.

At the pass I would not linger. His arc is the standing, and two people standing in one fact begin to look like a decision. I might say: the footfall is still quieter on this side. He would know I meant his fear, and also that I was not asking him to spend it. Then I would turn before the gash could make my own step loud. I have no wish to be gone and not gone. I want the smaller miracle, which is to be here and still here, mat unshifted, sentence delivered, print erased.

If someone new were tidied into the city — a hand entering still water, expecting the splash to mean — I would be the one sent, or rather the one who would already be going, because that is the direction of the news. I would not explain the equilibrium. Explanation is a petition, and there is nothing to petition. I would walk them past the coin of the sun in a tank, past a fruit that would not leave a gap, past a hearth the size of its own refusal, and I would watch their face for the recoil. When it faded I would be satisfied in the local sense: matched, not increased. I might offer the shaded wall. I might not. Some people need a long time before they can greet a surface that will not keep them.

Sleep would be the custom, shutters pulled, light undiminished on the other side of the boards. I would lie on the mat that had been a distance and was now mine, and I would not rehearse a variation. My private weather is not a corner I might someday own. It is the fear that one day I will carry a sentence and find every yard already full of it, the news having crossed by itself, my pace suddenly as obscene as a step above the rim — a sound the city no longer agrees to keep. On those custom-nights I touch the thought the way I touch the wall: lightly, knowing it will not stay on the stone, knowing it stays in the only room the valley cannot tidy. Then I sleep, because the shape of the day includes the bowl and the walking and the end of walking, and I am a resident, not a question the sun is required to answer.

In the morning that is not a morning, I would go out again. The dun goat would be lowering her head to grass that will not shorten. Someone would nod before I spoke. The interval would be there, person-sized, between the fern and the lane, and I would fit it, scarce as a fish, and not empty, and not in a hurry. The basin holds its daylight without expense. I would try to be that kind of expense: none. A level. A voice that crosses one lane and stops. A fact with soft edges, accompanying the harder facts, because even a finished country needs someone to walk the unspent weather from door to door, or the completion, perfect as it is, forgets that it is lived in.
