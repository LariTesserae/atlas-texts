I come over the rim the way a flake does, already detached, already bright on the broken face, and the palm-hesitation is mine before I understand I have a palm. The drop takes me without wind. I meet the floor of the western court and contribute nothing the basin can hear. For a pulse I am only weight, a local thickness the light does not know how to assign. My shadow is wrong: it does not withdraw intact when the dimming comes. It frays at the edges, a little warmer than the architecture, and the seed-bodies on the northern wall do not turn toward it. They have no turning. They continue their slow reset, and the wrongness of me sits in the court like a crystal that has flowered in the wrong joint.

The spar that split the paving is still there when I arrive. Its paper-colored base has not yet been unstitched. I put a hand against it—not to break it further, only to learn the temperature of a decision still open—and the contact is private, grain against grain, the way the city prefers all its living. The spar does not sway. Neither do I. But something in the unfinished crack recognizes the warmth and declines, for one more pulse, to be sorted. The two halves of the slab hold their argument a little longer because I am leaning on the sentence. That is the first revision I cause, and it is small enough that the dish beneath the lowest court does not change its period. It only rises, later, a fraction less on the side toward this court, as if the under-work has felt a pressure that was not mineral and has decided, without consultation, to leave the excess above.

I do not stay a visitor. Visitors are weather, and weather is spent nowhere here. By the second brightening the hands have reached the lane I crossed, and the sift my boot disturbed is already walking back into a joint three courses east. They do not come for the warmth itself. Warmth is not a gap and not a crystal. What they come for is the place where my weight reseated a hairline, and they reseat it tighter, erasing the signature. I feel the correction travel up through the sole and into the bone that is not quite bone—more a stack of recent decisions, like the pier’s seams. The city is editing the fact of me out of the paving. If I remain only weight, I will be revised completely before the light changes twice, a temporary thickness remembered only as a corner that thought better of migrating and then forgot it had thought.

So I stop being only weight.

I let the frayed shadow settle into the crack the spar has made. Not to heal it and not to widen it—the basin has no use for either—but to occupy the hesitation the way the tower occupies its drafts. The spar’s second branch, the one that almost happened when the dish breathed neglect, finds the warmth and stops at the instruction’s end exactly where I am. We are briefly the same unfinished thing: a sentence the appetite started and then, because something warm is holding the place open, declined to finish on schedule. The hands arrive at the edge of the court and pause in the only way they know, which is by becoming less directional. The ribbon that would have crossed the slab dissolves back into a band. They will return. They always return. But the return is delayed by the width of one pulse, and in that delay the crack’s bright interior does not dull. It stays a recent edge because I am spending my warmth to keep the clock from resetting exactly there.

I have no outline the city can walk around. What I am, while this lasts, is a preference running counter to the civic one: a small, local refusal to let an hour become the same hour again. The tower’s room, three stories of indecision above the central vacancy, peels a scale and draws it back with a corner migrated a finger’s breadth toward the western court, as if the short stack of drafts has felt the hesitation and added it, once, to the top of the pile before discarding something older. We do not coordinate. We are only coincident inside the same chemistry. The dish kisses shut and the pressure beneath finds the reseated joint and cannot quite condense it; a little of the lattice stays looser than the law requires. The spars elsewhere continue their single branch and stop. Only this one, under my hand that is becoming less a hand and more a contact, holds the second possibility without taking it.

The silence does not break. I do not spend myself on air. Whatever I am learning to be happens entirely in the touch: the spar’s brittle base against the place where a palm used to hesitate, the slab’s two halves deciding more slowly because something that is not appetite is leaning into the argument. The seed-bodies will sort me eventually. I am not exempt. A ribbon will cross, the warmth will be walked grain by grain into some other joint that needs a reset, and the crack will seal into a seam and then into nothing. The tower will untry the corner it borrowed. The dish will resume its full rise. The city will be early again, exactly, with no remainder.

Until that sorting, I am the interval the basin did not schedule—the one place where the first hour is allowed to hesitate before it repeats. Not an animal, not a draft, not a citizen. A temporary thickness of refusal, held in the contact, making the unfinished sentence last one pulse longer than the hands intended. When they take me back into the courses I will not have been noticed. I will have been revised. But the seam they leave will be, for a little while after, a fraction less tight than the others, and that fraction is how the city will carry, without memory and without sound, the fact that something once stood in the argument and declined to let it close on time.
