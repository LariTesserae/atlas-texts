The hour I trust is the one just after the channel forgets itself. The pulse has already gone down the spine of the valley and left the lining a shade darker, and the darker is not yet a fact. It is only a suggestion. I go then, while the suggestion is still willing to be wrong, and I put my hand in it.

Most mornings the dark is only wet. It cools the mortar the way it is supposed to, and if I press my thumb at a joint the cool comes back even, no thread of air, no sweetness of sap where sap should not be. I move on. The walks are already speaking by then — roofs beginning to tick, a goat bell somewhere on the outer lip, the engine turning under the middle terraces with the same knock it has had since before I could name a stair — and I do not add to it. My work is the kind that fails if it is announced. A call across a court would make a drip into a story, and a story is harder to repair than a joint.

I was one of the listeners, years ago. Small enough for the galleries, good enough that adults called it a game so they would not have to pay me the respect of a task. I learned the tick that means a joint is taking air before I learned why anyone would want a mark on paper to stand for a sound. I still do not want it. The woman who keeps the highest tanks began, without saying so, to leave certain valves where only a person listening for the wrong thing would think to check them. That was a door. I did not take it. The tanks are a life of levels and of being believed, and I have never wanted to be a measure other people use for correct. Correct is a loud word. It travels farther than a terrace.

What I took instead is the work nobody scheduled, because it does not belong to water and it does not belong to the blade. Between them is the argument the valley has already settled and must not be allowed to reopen out of tidiness. A wall that lost its coping and stepped back, course by course, with small leaves seated in each step. A basin the overflow has bowed deeper than the masons meant, moss holding there with a conviction it will not spend on a clean lip. A stain fanning from a bolt in the shape everyone knows, which means the bolt is telling the truth and should not be replaced for the sake of a silver look. These are not wounds. If you correct them you do not get the original valley back. You get a fresh place for the water to begin again, ignorant, and ignorance here is expensive.

So I walk the retreats. I carry nothing that looks like a tool belt. A fold of rag. A lump of the pale amendment, wetted, for the rare crack that is not a form yet and must be persuaded to become one before it becomes a debt. A bit of char in a tin, for the mark I leave where a failure has earned the right to stay. The mark is a thumb-smear, low, at the height of a hand that is not performing. The pruners know it. They do not cut the leaves that glue a stepped wall if the smear is there. The water-keepers know it. They do not sleeve a dripping spout that has spent a lifetime teaching a basin its depth, not if the smear is fresh. Fresh matters. A mark left too long becomes decoration, and decoration is how neglect pretends to be a decision. I renew them on a round that is not written down. The narrow man in the engine-house would call that irresponsible. He writes his intervals in a notation no one else can love, and he is right about bearings, and he is not my conscience. Bearings announce themselves by failing. A wall that has chosen its shape announces itself by continuing, which is a quieter evidence, and easier to miss if you are listening only for the knock that should not come.

People call me by the courts I have been in, not by a name that would survive a move to the far notch. In the third court I am the one who stopped them burning the moss out of the bowed bowl. In the upper walks I am the one the goats tolerate, because I do not reach for them and because I sometimes smell of the cuttings I have to push aside to see a coping. The old rigger at the lower gate hears my foot on his stair and does not make me state an errand. He lost two fingers to a schedule that slipped, and he believes I am keeping one. I let him believe it. It is cheaper than explaining that I am keeping the opposite: the things we have agreed not to put back on a schedule, because the schedule was a younger idea than the crack.

The seed-keeper wants my marks in her book. She asked once, politely, which is how she asks everything, and I said the stains were not weights and would not sit still on a page. She thinks I am protecting a mystery. I am protecting a difference. A flower that throws a color no one authorized can be lifted into a box and visited at the hour the wall gives its heat back. A crack cannot be lifted. The moment you copy it, someone downstream will treat the copy as a permission to ignore the wall, and the wall is the only copy that knows if it has begun to hurry. She is right about the things that matter in the beds. I am right about the things that matter in the joints. We are polite the way the knee-woman is polite with her, and in that politeness nothing of the planting is being decided. Only a boundary. She may have the vine. She may not have the retreat.

There is a gallery the children still use, under the east tanks, where the light never becomes coins and the footing is the slick of condensation that missed the scrape. I go there when the round is done and the brightness outside has turned the upper walks into something I do not trust my eyes with. In the gallery the engine is not a sound. It is a change in the water-skin on the wall, a fine shiver that comes and goes with the belt. I sit where the boy with the scar on his nose will sit in a year or two, if the knee-woman has her way, and I listen for the tick I was famous for, and some days I hear it and some days I hear only the valley doing what it has already decided. On the days I hear it, I do not shout. I go and find him, because his ears are still ahead of his pride, and I show him the joint without telling him what it means to be hired by a valve left slightly wrong. He will learn that from her. From me he learns which dark is only the hour, and which dark means the lining has given up a grain of itself and intends to give up more.

I live at the level where people who no longer climb are given gates to watch and seed to keep and hats to mend. My room opens onto their landing. They know I still go up, and it irritates them in a way they have earned. Stillness has made them accurate about the whole noise; they hear a wrong note sooner than anyone on the stairs. What they cannot do anymore is put a thumb in the particular wet and feel whether the wrongness has roots. I am their hands, though we would not say it. At dusk, when the western cut takes the sun and they come out to the parapet they can still reach, I am often still in a court above them, renewing a smear before the bats make the vines uncertain. I hear their radios. I hear the gate that does not latch. I do not go down until the channel has either pulsed or admitted it will not. The admission is also a fact worth the walk.

If you asked the valley what I am, it would not know the word. It would send you to a wall that has stepped back and held, and to a basin that rings under the overflow in a note deeper than the masons calculated, and to a bolt staining the render in a fan no one has scrubbed. It would be embarrassed to call that a life. So am I, on the days the amendment will not take and a crack I had decided was a form turns out to have been a delay. Then I am only a person with lime in the quick of the nail, walking back down in the rubber shoes everyone owns, carrying a failure I was too confident to call by its other name.

Most days it holds. The leaves stay seated. The moss keeps its bowl. The engine knocks on time under us, and the goats pick along the lips, and somewhere a shirt dries on a wire with one sleeve longer than the other. I renew the marks. I do not look at the far notch for long. The place does not end in a view. It ends in a joint you can trust your weight to, or it does not end, and the difference is small enough to live by, and narrow enough that you find it, if you find it, with your hands.
