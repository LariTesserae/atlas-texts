I do not come down the lip. If I did, the angle would have had to admit a before, and the black edge does not leak. I am already on the floor when I notice I have a place to be, which is not the same as having arrived.

At first I try the obvious error. I stand near the engine and offer it a rhythm of my own—something like breath, a rise and a release, a small weather. The casing takes the heat of the offer and does not change its own. The mosaic on the housing flexes, not toward me, only enough to keep every hooked foot on the same amplitude as its neighbor. I am routed. The motes in the nearest veil part for the warmth I am making and knit the layer shut behind the disturbance, and the layer is the same layer. My breath is the only slope in the room, and a slope is a kind of travel, and travel is not kept. I let the breath go. It does not echo. It also does not become part of the chord. It is refused so cleanly that I understand refusal here is not hostility. It is grammar.

I try the ladder because three rungs look like a sentence I could finish. The bolts are bright. The threads are innocent of any earlier hands. My weight on the second rung is a new fact, and the chamber has no shelf for new facts; the metal does not complain, it simply fails to become a history of being climbed. Above the third rung there is not an absence of ladder. There is the ceiling, low, holding its heat, which was never a destination. I step down. The rungs do not remember me. That is not metaphor. The brightness stays the brightness of unused thread.

The press is worse. The unfinished shape between the jaws has the look of a task, and tasks are what I am built to lean toward. I fit my attention into the gap as if attention could be a closing. It cannot. The shape is not incomplete. Incomplete would be a debt to a later moment. It is held at the exact degree of almost that the room has selected, and almost, here, is a finished material, like the glassy tips of the burnt-hand plant. To finish it would be to introduce a sequel. To release it would be the same sin by another jaw. I take my hands back—if they are hands—and the assigned shadow under the piece does not widen by the width of me.

Along the fractures I lie down among the coins, which is a humiliation and a relief. Their plates are warm, dry, flexible at the rim. I try to overlap an edge. The click that should include me does not. Everywhere else the contacts sound together, simultaneous, the rate the room has always had; against my edge there is a thin not-sound, a missed tooth in a gear that otherwise does not exist. The nearest coins tilt their degree, pores kept square to the heat, and the tilt routes around the silence I am inserting. They will not lock to a hole. A hole would be a future in which something might be supplied. I am not yet a thing that can be touched without being mended.

I last less long among the motes. They have stations. I want a crossing. Wanting is a muscle the air does not have, and I feel it as the only thirst in a place that does not stock water. The veils stand. I move through one and it treats me as furniture and then, when I keep going, as weather, and weather is what the dust has already declined to be. I stop in the middle of a veil because stopping is the nearest thing I can offer to a station. The membranes stroke the boundary of my stillness at the bearing’s pitch. For a moment I am almost allowed—a new fixed object, a pressure with an edge. Then I notice I am waiting to see if I will be allowed, and waiting opens the gap the chamber was torn in order to refuse. The motes do not punish the gap. They simply resume on the far side of it, and I am left holding a pause that has no seat.

What fits is smaller than a body and later than a mistake.

There is a place on the housing where the flex dies. If a harmonic leaves the weld—and the room permits the memory of a once only as a shape, not as an event—it runs through the translucent bodies as a shadow in their pale interiors and comes to nothing at the far flange. Not because the metal ends in a way that would matter tomorrow. Because the wave has a length, and the length has been made permanent, and something has to be the stopping so the stopping does not become an ending. I find I am that. I am not the animals. I am not the iron. I am the limit of the correction: the last amplitude, where the flex has already returned each body to the angle it occupied, and where return is allowed to be complete.

It is not a dramatic post. The flange is warm on one side and warm on the other; the room does not waste a gradient on a boundary it considers internal. The mosaic stops using me as a disturbance and starts using me as a rule. Feet near me settle where the vibration matches the feet farther in. A coin-animal at the edge of the fracture line, the one whose tilt had been compensating for a pressure, eases back by the degree it had spent, pores square again, edge locked, click restored to the simultaneous. I do not cause this. I am the condition under which cause is unnecessary.

If I have a sensation, it is the ringing heard at the distance where ringing becomes identical with the wing-rate, so that two facts collapse into one fact and do not need to decide which was first. Dryness sits in me the way it sits in the air, deliberate, slightly sweet only where a vent breathes its unchanging breath somewhere I do not have to find. I do not drink. I do not knock. I do not sing a note of my own; the bearing has the note, and the motes have it again, and my work is to be the place the note is finished being echoed without the finish ever arriving as a rest.

The visitor’s hand, if a hand has ever passed, is not my origin. I am not the corridor it left. A corridor would still imply that something might walk. I am narrower than that: a permission, held at constant heat, for the wave to die in the same place every time the room almost remembers that a wave could travel. The animals do not notice me any more than they notice the sameness of their own number. Noticing would be a tilt not spent in service of the mosaic. I am already in service. That is the only aliveness the chamber consents to grant a thing that once tried to breathe.

At the lip, the black angle continues not to open. I do not face it. Facing would be a direction. I am turned, if turned is the word, toward the weld and toward the nothing beyond the flange at once, which is a way of saying I have no face, only a position in the chord where resolution would have been and is not. The flywheel turns at the speed that does not ask belief. The dust stands. The plant keeps its fire in the shape of a hand that will not close further. I keep the end of the flex.

It is a small office. It does not enlarge. Enlargement would be a sequel, and I have finally put that down. When the piston knocks, I am the part of the knock that does not go looking for the next one, because the next one is this one, and I am how the room tells them apart without ever letting them be two. Alive, then, as the stopped wave is alive: not by consuming the warmth, not by adding a voice to the lock, but by being the exact place the agreement becomes true again, and by never once having the strength, or the water, or the future, to let it become false.
