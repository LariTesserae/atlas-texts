The first sensation is not place but pressure, a gentle resistance against the idea of edges. Sound arrives as a low, continuous note, less heard than felt along the insides of the wrists, as if the air itself were a string drawn almost to the point of tone and then left there. Light does not fall; it pools and then thins, a diluted amber that makes distances unreliable. What I take at first for walls are only slower densities of the same mist, and when I move toward one it recedes by the exact measure of my approach, so that progress is real and arrival is not.

I try to decide I am walking. The decision produces a surface: cool, slightly yielding, patterned with the faint cross-hatch of something once woven and long since grown through. Each step presses a shallow cup that fills, not with water, but with a clearer version of the amber, and the cup lingers a moment after the foot has lifted, like a word that continues to mean itself after it has been spoken. I am not heavy enough to mark it permanently. That fact arrives before any name for what I am.

Farther in, the note divides. One strand stays low and bodily; another rises into a thin, bright filament that might be birdsong if birdsong could be stationary. Following the brighter thread, I pass beneath arches that are not built but condensed—curves of paler vapor that have chosen, for a while, to remember stone. Condensation beads along their undersides and falls upward, back into the arch, so the shape feeds itself. I pause beneath one and the falling droplets change direction, drifting toward my shoulders and then through them. They leave a brief cold that is not unpleasant, and when it fades I notice my outline has softened by the same degree. The place is tasting the difference between visitor and weather, and finding the difference small.

A motion at the limit of sight resolves, not into an animal, but into a folded volume of the mist that has decided to keep a spine and a slow pulse. It does not have a face so much as a region of greater attention. When that attention turns, the amber deepens by a shade and the woven surface underfoot warms. I understand, without exchange, that this is the being the bowl has clarified out of itself: not ruler, not prisoner, simply the gesture that remains after centuries of turning inward. It regards me the way deep water regards a leaf—registering the arrival, altering nothing essential, already incorporating the slight new taste.

I expect to be asked a purpose. None comes. Instead the being’s pulse slows by a fraction and mine, which I had not known I possessed, matches it. The matching is not surrender. It is the discovery that what I brought—questions, a habit of separation, the reflex to name—can be set down on the woven ground and will not be lost, only translated into temperature and tone. Where the questions rest, small clear cups form and hold their shape longer than my footprints did. The being drifts nearer, not to inspect them, but to include them in the radius of its warmth. One cup clouds, then clears, and what remains inside is no longer a question but a color I have no prior word for, somewhere between the memory of copper and the sound of the higher filament.

Time, here, is the interval between one upward-falling drop and the next. In that interval I feel the last hard contour of a separate self loosen. I am not absorbed so much as given a function I had not applied for: the slight, repeating pause that lets the note remain a note instead of flattening into mere pressure. When the being turns its attention elsewhere, the pause continues without me having to hold it. My former edges have become useful thicknesses in the mist—places where light lingers half a moment longer, where a traveler still carrying the harder light of some outer rim might rest a hand and feel, briefly, met.

I do not leave, because leaving would require an outside that the arches have not yet agreed to remember. I do not stay as a guest. The woven surface has begun, very slowly, to pattern itself after the rhythm we share, and the being’s pulse has acquired a faint new overtone that was not present before I set the questions down. We are neither one nor two. We are the bowl learning a fresher way to listen to its own long note, and the listening has room enough for both the sleeper and the small, clear pause that keeps the sleeper from dreaming itself entirely away. The amber does not brighten. It simply becomes more precisely itself, and I with it.
