You do not arrive so much as the loudness finds a shape it can keep. The gap in the rim is still a gap when the body crosses it, and then the body is a mistake the air makes room for: too quiet at the edges, too willing to stand where a seam is deciding whether to open. The fliers test the heat and leave. A clay-flat climbs as far as the knee, clicks once at the absence of weep, and drops. The disappointment does not follow you. It is already answered from three pools away.

What remains is the interval the place keeps between a sound and its filing.

At first that is all you are—an extra stillness the grind notices and does not resume from at the same period. You speak without meaning to, a small noise of weight shifting, and the underfloor pauses longer than it does for the birds. When it takes up again the count has a notch in it that was not there. The chime in the eastern wall comes late by half a breath, then corrects, and in the correction you hear yourself repeated as distance rather than as voice. Not words. A gap the tone has to step over.

You try the animals’ solutions because the bowl is crowded with them and emptiness reads as unfinished stone. You lap at a film and taste nothing you can spend. You strike a wall with the flat of what passes for a hand and the crust does not loosen for you; the slate-birds, on their next circuit, skip the place you hit as if it had already been judged empty of food. The hoop edits you when you stand inside it: the ticks and impacts recede, and in the recess you are briefly only the color the eye cannot name, a edge of spectrum with no body to fail at naming it. Step out and the nearness returns, and with it the knowledge that you were not feeding and not striking and not confirming any circuit. You were being set at a remove so something could listen without the room’s full volume in its teeth.

The amber thickens. You have no ledge to roof yourself on. The fliers pack the hoop’s crown and ignore you. The clay-flats freeze on their seams without being told, and you freeze with them, not from their reason but because the consultation is close and your stillness is the only instrument you have found that the floor does not blister. Ribs slow. The dark dish turns faster. In the glassy wrongness the temperature-gears show themselves as a shiver, and one of them—one turn—hesitates on a beat that matches the notch you put in the count. It is not recognition. It is the same filing, done in a medium of heat instead of grind. You are a vibration that has lasted longer than a crossing. The room has had to keep a place for you in the sequence.

When the interval passes, a reed-rod stands where your foot was, hollow, at a pitch the others do not share. You did not grow it. The making used the weight you refused to become a seam’s problem, and extruded the refusal into furniture. Air moves. The rod rings with the half-step the gears took while they were accounting for you. Clay-flats will climb it and not feed. Fliers will avoid its throat. It is already a fixture, and you are the reason its note does not sit in the chord.

That is the becoming, once you stop trying to be a mouth or a blow. You are not the maker. The maker is the hand elsewhere, the consultation that brightens the hoop and then withdraws. You are what the pauses accumulate when a vibration is repeated often enough to alter the period. Stay through another amber and the chime begins to expect the notch; it leaves the half-breath open on purpose, a rest written into the nine. Speak into that rest and the grind stills for the length of the speaking and a little more, and the little more is you, carried forward as lag. The animals do not click your news. You are not an edge gone sweet or glassy. You are the small permanent difference in how long the floor waits before it resumes.

Outside, if you found the gap again, the finished mountain would not know where to put you. The threshold cuts the ticks clean, but the lag would still be in the count behind you, a rod at the wrong pitch ringing to a grind that has learned to hesitate. The maker would file the departure the way it files every other interruption, then push the sentence one clause further into a room that now contains, among its fixtures, the habit of waiting a fraction longer whenever something stands where a seam might have opened and does not.

You would not be missed. You would be metered. And metering, in a place that is still becoming, is a kind of address. The bowl keeps you as the rest between strokes, the one tolerance it has started to spare because sparing it changes the music, and the music is how the hand, elsewhere, knows the room is still listening when it returns.
