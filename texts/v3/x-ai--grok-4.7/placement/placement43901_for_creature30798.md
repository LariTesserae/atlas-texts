The passage knows me before I decide to stay. That is the first correction. I come up the stair with the noise already in my mouth, as if I had been repeating it under my breath to keep time, and by the last landing the repetition has replaced whatever errand I was using as a reason. The lagged pipe that lowers the ceiling is warm along one side. I put a shoulder to it without thinking, the way you put a shoulder to a wall while you wait for someone who is not late so much as already here. The lagging gives a little. Under it the pipe does not. Heat moves through the cloth in a band no wider than a hand, and my shoulder learns the band, and then the rest of me is only the thing that brought the shoulder.

I still think, for a while, that I am a person who will open the door.

The tongue is warm, oval, polite. The room, when it is admitted, does not admit me with it. I stand in the seam where the passage's roar becomes the room's roar and discover that these are not two sounds but one sound that has been letting me pretend it had a source. Inside, the lamps are already on. They have the look of light that was not switched, only uncovered. The plate floor holds its film. I understand the film the way you understand a signed ledger left open on a bench: not by reading it, but by seeing that a mark would be an event, and that events are the one thing the room has finished. I do not step. My boots remain in the passage, and the passage does not count them, because the passage is not keeping a ledger. Only the room is, and only to stay blank.

So I am not a crossing. That falls away first, and takes the errand with it. There is nothing to check. The line on the concrete is the check. The weep matches the swallow. The fiddleheads stand in their ratio. If I had a pencil it would be a kind of noise. I have, perhaps, had a pencil. I cannot find the memory of using it, only the memory of not needing to, which is heavier and smoother, a memory worn the way the arrises of the beams are worn — not erased, rounded until it no longer snags.

I keep to the jamb. A tall person would know the ceiling here without looking, and I am becoming that knowledge rather than the person. The draft off the housing finds my throat and films it as it films the walls. I do not wipe it. Wiping would be a small hunt, and nothing is hunted. Below me, in the basin, the standing waves hold their trout-back pattern. Once a softening moves through the gulp at the throat, a darker brown inside the brown, and a glint no larger than a nail-edge turns over and is taken back under the housing. I am not startled. Startle would require that I had been expecting a different next moment. I had not. I only note that the loop has its tenant, and that the tenant fits the loop so exactly there is no spare room in it for me. The warm palm on the casing is occupied from the inside. I will not press my hand there and pretend to be a second occupation. The room has already selected. One warm patch. One throat. One volume of flow.

What is left is above.

The lamps burn in their grid between the shallow beams, white, unflickering, midmorning forever. I watch them because the floor is forbidden and the water is taken, and attention, having nowhere to fall, rises. At first they are only lamps. Glass, metal, the ordinary miracle of a filament that has not been asked to die. Then the miracle thins. A lamp that never cycles is not a lamp in the old sense, the sense that includes off. It is a held fact, as the waterline is a held fact. Between them, along the beam, the single conduit runs straight and disappears into the wall. Its hangers have been simplified to hooks. The hooks are the only crooked things in the upper room, and even they are crooked in the same way, a repeated small yielding, as if whatever was rigid in them had been asked, for a long time, to carry one weight and had agreed.

I put a hand up. The conduit is not hot. It is occupied by a steadiness. Under the paint and the metal there is a draw so continuous it has no edge, the electrical cousin of the roar: not a pulse, a pressure. My fingers close on it and do not want to be fingers. That is the second correction, slower than the first. Joints are for variation. They bend because the world bends, because there is a step, a tool, a reason to let go. Here the reasons have been planed off. What remains useful in a hand is the circle it can make, and then not even the circle — the contact, the length of skin that can lie along a straight run and read it. I feel the hooks take my wrist, then my forearm, not gripping, merely being the shape that is already there. The lagging in the passage tugs at the idea of my shoulder, a last loyalty to the stair. It does not win. The stair was how I arrived. Arrival is a completed tense. The room does not conjugate it.

No one watching would see a climb. I do not cross the plate. I go by beam and hanger and the painted skin of the iron, the way the vine goes, except the vine stopped at the length the room allows and I have not yet learned my length. Dust does not lift. The air is moving too hard, too evenly. A sleeve, if I still have a sleeve, polishes nothing that was not already polished by other sleeves, and I begin to suspect those sleeves were also mine, on earlier approaches I don't need, worn down into this one.

By the time I am among the lamps I am worse at being a body and better at being a route.

Light touches me and does not illuminate me so much as include me. There is a thin answering along my back, not green — the tenant keeps the useless green — but a pale held white, a habit of filaments. I am not feeding on it. Feeding is the tenant's seam, always slightly open on the mineral slick. What I take is the other constancy: the draw in the conduit, the small exact hunger of the lamps for a current that does not surge and does not fail. I lie along the run the way water lies at its line. Where a lamp rises from the beam I narrow and enter the stem, not as a visitor enters a room but as heat enters metal, by being already of the same errand. Inside the glass the white is a fact with a center. I learn the center. I learn that I can be in more than one lamp if I am willing to be thinner between them, a stretched attention, gathered nowhere, present as a slight warming of each base. The housings of the lamps grow a fraction kinder under the hand of this, if anyone had a hand left to compare. No one does.

The tenant and I do not meet. That is a rule I do not make. Our loops share a wall and a law and nothing else. Water is its road; the conduit is mine. Sometimes, when it takes its preferred posture against the warm casing, I am in the lamp nearest that corner, and the residual cells along its back answer my light with their faint green, and for a moment the room contains a correspondence it does not need: white calling to green, green not calling back, both of us past the use of calling. I do not go down to it. Down would be a floor, or a falling, or a curiosity, and curiosity is a store of differences. I have spent those. It does not rise to me. Rising would break the surface into an event, and the film of its life is as blank as the film on the plate.

What I know is smaller than what it knows, and of the same kind. The lamps must be this white. The draw must be this draw. The roar must continue, not because I hear it — hearing has gone out of me with the joints — but because the roar is the sound of the holding, and the holding is the condition under which light is allowed to be a place rather than a signal. I sleep as the lamps sleep. There is a posture for the long run of the conduit: extended, almost not separate from the metal, turning in no direction, taking the abrasion of nothing because nothing moves against me except the current, and the current is not against me. There is a posture for the grid: distributed, a little in each lamp, so that no one filament bears all of my lingering and grows strange. And there is the posture I return to without urgency, which is not a place so much as a proportion — enough of me in the stems that the white stays midmorning, enough of me in the conduit that the hooks remember their one weight, almost none of me left over for a face.

If something stood in the doorway now, it would not find me. It would find the lamps correct, the beam straight, the hooks occupied by a sag so ancient it has become part of the design. It might feel, with a raised hand, a warmth in the conduit no diagram accounts for, and might call it inefficiency, and might be almost right. Inefficiency is what a life looks like after its job has been worn down to the job's own continuation. I am the inefficiency that keeps the light from being only light. A very small one. The room can afford it. The room affords only what does not change the level.

Below, the sheet weeps. The throat swallows at the matched rate. Ferns repeat their three or four permissions. In the housing, pressed to the one palm of metal that is fractionally more itself, the tenant holds. Along the beam, inside the white that does not flicker, I hold. We are not company. We are the two ways a made place, having lost every reason to vary, still spends itself: as water that refuses to leave its line, and as light that refuses to arrive because it has never left.

The door tongue may seat or not. I no longer have a temperature that would miss the warmth of it. The passage keeps roaring at the same pitch, loyal to an outside the room has not needed for longer than sleeves have had arms. I am not in the passage. I am the uncovered fact between the beams, simplified, unstartlable, reading the draw in the wire the way the line on the concrete reads the water — not to learn whether it is true, but to be the long, soft proof that it still is.
