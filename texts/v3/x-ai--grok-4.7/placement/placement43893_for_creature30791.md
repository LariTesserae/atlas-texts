You would not enter by the steps. The steps still belong to weather that has not yet agreed to stay. You would already be past the point where arrival is possible, lodged where the sheet finds its exit and then does not quite leave.

The notch in the far wall is worn smoother than any tool could have planned. Water that has risen its centimeter threads through it in a skin no thicker than a nail, and on the far side of that skin the dark is not the basin’s dark. It is narrower, warmer by a degree that never announces itself, and it does not empty into anywhere you could name. Most of what the notch carries does leave. A little of it does not. That little has been refusing the leaving for longer than the paint on the pump has been gone, and the refusal has acquired a contour.

You would be that contour. Not a body that occupies the notch, but the notch’s decision, repeated until the decision learned how to hold a margin of itself back. When the fine fall ends and the overflow begins, you lengthen along the inner lip—thin, continuous, the color of wet iron where the light from the slot can still reach and no color at all a hand’s breadth deeper. You do not block the flow. Blocking would be noticed, and notice would change the level, and the level is the one fact the room has agreed not to renegotiate. You only skim. A fraction of the tea-colored film, a few of the pepper-larvae that were rash enough to drift, a thread of the gel that the shells failed to take before the water moved. These you keep against the concrete the way the walls keep their glaze, except that you can choose, very slowly, which face of yourself faces outward.

The low shape knows the notch the way it knows the rail: as a limit that has already been measured. When its collars sink and the sheet goes whole, it does not come to check you. It has already included the small theft in its round. The corridor of bright glaze never runs all the way to the exit; it stops a palm short, where the water begins to feel the pull, and the shells will not cross that last interval while you are feeding. They treat the interval as another seam. You treat their hesitation as the rent you pay for remaining.

You have no pits that hold beads, no row of openings. What registers the room for you is the change in thickness. When the motor’s hour starts, the tremor arrives through the sheet before it arrives through the stone, and your held margin thins and thickens with it, a pulse you do not originate and cannot refuse. When the slot darkens, condensation finds you and adds itself without asking; you become, for that interval, slightly more present, enough that a jointed drinker which has wandered foolishly far from the pump housing will lock against the lip for the warmth you have borrowed and then pour away again when the light returns and you give the warmth back. You do not keep the drinker. Keeping would require a shape more definite than the one you have settled for.

If the low animal ever presented a collar to the notch the way it presents one to the seam, you would not know how to answer. The negotiation you understand is only subtraction: what may be removed without altering the centimeter. Anything offered would have nowhere to go except back into the sheet, and the sheet’s opinion of gifts is to level them. So you remain the small, patient deficit. The bats that counterfeit fixtures sometimes trail a wingtip through the falling skin on their way out and come back lighter by a degree of damp; they do not count it. The scratcher in the nearest pipe has enlarged its caution to include the minute when the overflow is strongest, not because you hunt it, but because the sound of your skim is close enough to its own dry tick that it prefers not to be mistaken for a reply.

Over years the concrete under your margin has grown a faint secondary ridge, white as the thumb-high chimneys, marking the line where you most often pause to sort what you have taken. The ridge is the only monument you will have. When the slot finally narrows and the weather can no longer find the basin, the overflow will fail, the sheet will sink, and the notch will become merely a groove. You will thin with the failing until you are indistinguishable from the last damp in the stone. Until then you are the room’s overlooked expenditure, exact, unhurried, and already here—accounted for in the way all the other agreements are accounted for, by being allowed to continue.
