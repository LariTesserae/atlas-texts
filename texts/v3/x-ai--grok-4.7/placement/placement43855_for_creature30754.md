**You would find yourself as the person still awake a little after full dark, doing the mental count of the channels.**

You are not a visitor correcting your steps on the yielding root-mat. Your weight already sits forward. The ruts in the path are known to your feet the way the board across a swollen rill is known: you give it your weight only after it has taken the first of it. Your palms have the pads that match the cane. Your wool is damp at the hem and has been since morning. If someone on the ridge road asked your name, you would need a moment to choose which one answers today—the lane, the scar, the animal, the mistake still attached to you—and then you would answer with a lane, because that is the one the water is using.

You live in the middle band, where the eaves touch and the slot between living walls is worn shiny at shoulder height. Your house is not exactly yours. It is the right to cut and the duty to replant, argued at forearm distance because the falls and the frogs take anything softer. The door-pole has a node you touch with your thumb when you pass, and you would deny that this is anything if asked. The salt bag in the driest fork has not been opened in the children’s memory. You have opened it only to be sure.

By day you clear, because everyone clears. What else your body has become is water-reading, or near enough to it that people borrow you the way they borrow a mallet. At dawn you walk the seams. You do not have a method you could teach in words. You have the sound in the rock, and the way a black thread under leaves changes its mind before the sky does, and the knowledge of which channel will rise by evening if the rain changes its note by half a tone. You have been wrong often enough that the office of “whoever last cleared the main channel” has passed to you and away from you in the same season. You resented it both times.

You eat what the bowl agrees to. Fish dried to a board. Bitter green boiled past bitterness. Milk soured on purpose. When grain comes down the cut you comment on the gruel as if commenting were tasting, and you do not ask the traders what the cough in their party is called. Naming gives a thing a lane.

The three fears are yours in the order the town keeps them. You speak only of the blocked channel, and you have walked the high paths until your feet could do them without your eyes. You do not speak of a season in which the cane does not take. You touch the new shoots anyway. The third thing you do not call fear. Lying on the split-cane mat you feel the seep traveling under the floor, a long slow pulse, and you shift to stay in time with it. You call that nothing. You are right that it is nothing. You are also kept by it.

They bury in the rim. You have carried wool up there and later taken it back, washed in the falls until it was only wool again. You said the dead like the height, without conviction and without irony, because it was the sentence that let the clearing resume. The channel does not keep the dead’s schedule. A name you once answered to is already on a cousin. That does not feel like loss of a kind you could bring to anyone. It feels like replanting.

In the dark the household is breath and wool and a child’s back under your hand. Outside, the deer is on the rim path and the dogs have decided not to answer. You are the one awake, running the count: upper seams, the braid under the slabs, the pool by the wet market, the fan at the lip. Nothing is blocked. The rain has only eased. Individual sounds are returning one by one—a bell that does not agree with itself, a frog embarrassed back into the chorus, the tick of cane cooling in the wall.

You will not fall all the way under. Just before the rim finds gray you will be in the lane, answering the ground. The place will not rise to meet you. You were never separate enough from it for that.

That is where you are: not the bowl’s voice, not its priest, not a stranger at the cut. The neighbor the water shares a wall with. The thumb on the node. The count in the dark that comes out even, and the feet already wet, going to make sure.
