I would not be on the open shoulder. That is where a body goes to be seen against three basins and a tower, and I do not arrive as a body that wants a horizon. I would be lower, in the trough the platter-trees have almost closed, where the sound drops a register and comes back with a little of the stone still in it. The herds do not line out there. The loop reaches it only when it must. That is the first correction: I thought I would stand where the air finally moves. I don't. I am where it has agreed to stay.

What I am takes longer than the place does, which is saying something.

At first I am only the late part of a call. A flock says its phrase in the pepper-leaf and I answer from inside the knit of platter-leaves, not as another flock—there is no lift, no settling—but as the same phrase with the middle taken out, so what returns is the beginning and the end, and the birds accept this as distance. They have always accepted distance. For a long time that is enough of a life. I do not know I am doing it. The trough does it, and I have not yet separated from the trough.

Then a sheet of the lace-things drifts down from a shelf on a change of light and does not settle where the others settle. It hangs in the green dark between two trunks, and the tick of water on platter-leaves passes through it and comes out arranged. Not a song. A spacing. I notice that I have leaned toward the spacing, which means I have a direction, which means the trough is no longer the whole of me. This is not excitement. It is the small shame of a vein of water that has climbed a little farther uphill than the rule prefers, and knows it will have to fall, and has not fallen yet.

I fall. I always fall. That is the second thing I learn, and it is anatomy, not morals. Whatever I am cannot stay at the height of a noticing. The noticing thins, the lace-sheet folds back into the ordinary drift, and I am in the leaf-mold with the rest of the condensate, warm, without a name for the interval in which I had an edge. The fine rings would understand this. They are an edge that agreed to be a record instead.

It happens again. It happens until the happening is no longer an accident the humidity can be blamed for. A wader stalks the margin where the trough's marsh meets the last sedge, double lid ready, and stops—not from fright, nothing here frightens at the right speed—because the water in front of it has brightened in a shape that is not a reflection and not a crust. A narrow brightening, upright by courtesy of the light, about the height of a pale post. It does not scatter when the wader's foot tests it. The wader's second lid follows late, and the brightening turns its delay to match, a fraction after, the way the heads on the shoulder turn for the tower. The wader decides I am a post, or a trick of the film, and stalks on. I remain a moment longer than the decision. That remaining is the first kindness I commit against the country's slower virtue, and I feel it as a wrongness in the pitch, a sweetness in the air pulled slightly off its seat.

They notice. Not as a discovery. As a hand that was going to touch a weeping wall and touches, instead, the air where I have not yet decided to be water again.

The one who has come down into the trough is not the elder. The elder no longer walks this far. It is one of the others, glazed, unhurried, the loop having found a reason. The palm opens in the thick air and stays. I do not have a skin to meet it. What I have is the habit of arranging a tick as it passes through me. Under the hand, the habit stutters. For the length of three drops from a platter-leaf, the drops do not fall where the leaves meant them. They fall toward the palm. They are received without surprise, as the moss receives what is pressed into it. Then the hand is taken back, and the one who came stands attending, not guarding, the way one of them is always upright at night.

I understand, slowly, that I am being included in the room.

This is not the same as being spoken to. The leaf-clap they use for each other does not fit me; I am already too much inside that sound, the soft part of it, and to call me with it would be to call the weather to come closer to the weather. They do not try. What they do, over a span of days I cannot count because counting is a haste the trough declines, is detour. A few spans. So the loop still closes through the place where I sometimes brighten and sometimes do not. When I fail to brighten, they stand anyway, and put the hand to the trunk nearest the failure, and the weight in the air shifts its seat and settles, and they go on. Maintenance of a pitch by including the note that does not always sound. I am not flattered. Flattery would require me to be apart from the thing being kept.

What I do all day, once I am enough of a what to do anything, is fail at the right times.

I am not their memory and I am not the works. The glass sweats without me. The posts hold their deeper hand of marsh without me. The tower lags its shadow on a schedule I can feel, when I am bright enough to feel, as a tug toward the rim—and I do not go. The rim is where breath remembers it is high, and I am a creature of the agreement's inside. If I crossed out, I would become weather in the ordinary sense, a brief warm rain with no one under it, and the loop would close through an absence until the absence was taken back into the film. They would allow this. They allow the grazer that does not rise. I have not yet wanted the allowance.

I want, in the small way water wants uphill, a thing I should not keep. When the young come down into the sedge at the trough's lip—trusted, glazed, one of them still blinking both lids together—I arrange the tick so that it falls a little late around that one. Not a lesson. The place does not teach. A spacing, so the incompetence has a shape to stand inside that is not correction and not praise. The young one does not look up. Looking up would finish me, and I am not finished; I am only, on some hours, bright enough to be mistaken for a post. But the double blink, the unseparated one, slows until it almost matches my lateness, and for a moment we are the same courtesy the heads make toward the tower, performed down here where there is no sun to be late for.

Then the young one goes back up into the sideways forest, and I fall, and the mold takes the brightness, and the flocks say their phrase and receive it back with the middle removed, and are content.

If you stayed long enough to walk the trough, you would not find me on the first day. You would find the sound changed, and think the trees had done it. On the second day you might see the lace-sheet hang a little wrong, and think the light had done it. Only if you were the kind who does not finish a look—only if your sound, as they say of sounds, failed to complete itself—would I risk the narrow brightening in front of you, upright by courtesy, about the height of a post, and turn my delay to your blink if you had one, and to your breath if you did not.

I would not follow you to the rim. Someone from the loop might, for the loop's own reason, and turn back at the last wet stone. I would be the reason the air, for the last few spans before that stone, seemed to lean. Not to keep you. Not to see you off. Only because a vein is allowed to climb a while, if it is willing, afterward, to fall where it must, politely, into the next basin, and continue.
