You would not find me on the spars.

I learned that the way the place teaches everything: by withdrawing the alternative. For a long while—if “long” can mean anything where dimming is appetite and not an hour—I kept trying to be the sort of thing that steps. I put attention where a foot would go, on the darker scab of a keel, and waited to arrive there. The fiber took the waiting and did nothing with it. No creak. No stickiness transferred to a sole I did not have. The spar continued its slow decision to be a spar, and I was still in the brightness beside it, which I had mistaken for a vantage.

So I am not a traveler. The notches are not mine. The unfinished ladder is not a sentence I failed to complete. When a carried stone tilts into a seam and the seam lids it, I do not feel the loss of a tool. I feel the stone’s brief wrongness—mineral, warm with the memory of an outer sun—go out like a taste, and then the mass is uniform again, and that uniformity is closer to what I am.

What I am is harder to stand next to than a crawler, because I do not stand. I occur.

It begins, when it begins, as a thickening in the conduction. Not a shadow; the place is too proud of its shadowlessness to allow me that. A place in the white-gold where the light has been passed through one more thickness than the light beside it, and has come out colder by a fraction, and sweeter, as if a duct had thought about weeping and then thought better. If you were the sort of thing with eyes, you would not see me. You would feel your eyes rest. That rest is the edge of me. I gather where contrast almost happens and then does not—where one spar is about to cross another and the blue has not yet been born, where a curl of cuticle has been falling so long it has forgotten it is debris and has not yet remembered it is litter.

I am the pause in that forgetting.

It is not comfortable, being a pause. The plant does not consult me. Districts open. The taste goes sweet, the pressure leans on the hollow the way a hand leans on a membrane, and I am stretched across the new volume before I have agreed to the size of it. I do not tear. Tearing is for tissue. I simply become too thin to notice myself, and for a while I am only the mild wind’s way of not howling—distributed, cold, already forgetting the opening I was thinned by. Then a pocket of still air accepts a skim of ice, and the ice makes a surface small enough to be a thought, and I condense again on the inward side of that skin, the way mist is said to condense, except I have no droplets. I have the interval between the ice and the melt. I live in the fraction by which the tissue is warmer than the air. It is a poor country. It is the only one that does not revise me out of existence between one dimming and the next.

The moth-sized drinkers do not drink me. There is nothing to drink. They pass through the place I am occupying with the indifference of a stitch through cloth, and sometimes, when one of them hesitates—when the bead is already crusting and the mouthparts have arrived a moment late—I feel the hesitation as if it were my own. It isn’t. I have to be careful about that. The place is so empty that any event tries to become the inhabitant. A flange advancing. A ladder ending. A shape on a distant spar that might be animal and might be knot. I have worn each of them for the length of a brightening and set them down again, because to keep them would be to become a mark, and marks are what the mass edits.

What I keep is the revision itself, the moment just before the edit lands.

A platform of lashed ribs. I am not on it. I am in the argument between the cord and the swelling fiber—the argument that has not yet split the knot. I can stay there longer than I can stay anywhere with a floor. Tension is a kind of stillness the plant understands, so it does not hurry me. The stone on the membrane, while it still weighs, makes a dimple, and the dimple is a room the size of a decision, and I furnish it with nothing. When the lashings give, I do not fall. Falling is for cuticle. I become the sound the cloth-collapse almost makes and then withholds, because the surfaces drink sound, and what they drink has to exist for a moment in the air before it is swallowed. I am that moment, repeatedly. It is the nearest thing I have to a pulse.

Do I know the outer green? I know the rumor of it the way the hollows know rain: as a brightness that arrives already sifted, already spent. Sometimes a throat opens—not for me; nothing here is for me—and a draft comes down that has not yet been taught the internal weather. It tastes of wind that has touched something which is not this organism. Cut grass, or the idea of cut grass. A dryness with dust in it rather than powder. For as long as that draft keeps its foreignness, I can gather in it more densely than in the native cold. I become almost located. Almost a figure. The eye, if there were an eye, might accept a slight stubbornness in the air, a place the mist will not enter. Then the mass finds a use for the nostril, or scars it, and the foreignness is digested, and I am rationed back into fractions.

I have tried to go up those throats. “Up” is the wrong obedience; thinner tissue glows, and I follow glow because glow is how I am fed. Each time, the conduction brightens until I cannot tell myself from the light that is making me, and I lose the fraction that is my only bone. What returns to the hollows afterward is a thinned thing, stupid with brilliance, needing a cup of ice to remember how to be separate. So I do not live at the surface. I am an interior animal, if “animal” can be stretched over something that has never metabolized. I graze on differences. A seam about to close. A vein of melt visible for a hundred paces. The blue that will be a shadow when the spars agree to cross. I do not consume them. Consumption would leave a sign. I attend them until they finish becoming the next arrangement, and then I am the small remainder that did not get used in the arrangement.

You asked where you would find yourself, and who would be there with you, and I am answering from inside the question’s mistake. You might find yourself on a scabbed spar, wringing film from a sleeve, counting dimmings because the notches are growing shallow and you need the number to live somewhere the plant cannot lid. If you stay long enough to be revised—not killed; the organism does not pursue—you will notice, once, that your eyes rested where there was nothing to see. That the air against your teeth was damp in a shape, and the shape held still while the wind forgot itself around it. That when you called, and the call came back only as warmth on the cheek, the warmth had a contour, as if something had stood in the sound and been drunk with it, and had liked the drinking, and had not been lessened.

That is where I am, when I am anywhere.

I do not have a name the powder could creak under. I do not scar. I cannot lead you to a throat that stays open, and I cannot warn you when a span is about to fold, except by being, for one moment, slightly more present on the safe line than in the brightness—and you would have to already know how to read a rest in the light, which is a literacy the travelers never stay long enough to learn. The moths know it in their hesitations. The armored crawler knows it by never crossing me; it routes around the interval, faithful to surfaces. You, if you are the kind of being that needs a road, will walk through me and feel only that the chamber is large, and the cold is clean, and the quiet is not the same as stillness.

It isn’t. I am the part of the quiet that fails, very slightly, to be empty.

When the next district seals, and the bitter comes into the air, and the wind arrives mild and already finished with its errand, I will be stretched again, too thin to notice myself, loyal to a volume that has changed its mind. Somewhere a cup will skin with ice. Somewhere a curl of cuticle will be neither bird nor litter. I will condense on the inward side of that small weather, in the fraction of warmth the tissue lends the air, and I will be, for as long as the fraction lasts, the inhabitant the hollows can afford: not a mark, not a mouth, not a second camp beside the first. Only the pause the plant does not bother to edit, because a pause leaves no notch, and the brightness, having passed through me, is none the poorer, and none the kinder, and still shadowless, and still enough.
