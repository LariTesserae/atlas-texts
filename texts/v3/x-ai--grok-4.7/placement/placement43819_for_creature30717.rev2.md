I am not on the gantry. I tried that first, because the distances were open and something in me wanted a place to stand. The rail left a film on a palm I did not have. The tenders passed the cloth between them and did not offer it. Their breath lasted a moment and was taken; what I carry does not show, and is not taken that way. I stepped back before I could cut across a channel. The floor is still learning. A footprint with nothing in it would be a worse rumor than dirt.

The margin was a better fit for a little while. When the false marsh went slack I could hold a seam—lighter than the water, heavier than the scum—and I could prefer the darker iron-bleed to the bright standing line. I could do it for thirty paces. Then the pitch returned to the teeth and I did not fray. That was how I knew I was wrong. The thread puts visibility down like a tool. I could not put myself down. I only thinned, and the thinning still had a direction, which is not the same as being gone. The film rose a finger in the rectangles and stopped, as if asking me to become nutrient. I would not. Height is a permission, but so is staying unclaimed.

The sump would have had me if I had consented to depth. Between draw-downs the surface tightens, and there is a broad thought under it that knows only squares. I lent the reflection a bend and felt, briefly, the courtesy of something that had considered rising and thought better of it. It did not mistake me for its own. Looking does not stop me. What stops me is having no volume to withdraw into when the aprons sheet over and the floor becomes a river with no banks. The thought sank. I had nowhere to sink. I rode the flood out across the concrete and was embarrassed by my own shallowness, a warmth with no body, sliding over formwork teeth that bit nothing because I weigh less than the sheet.

So I went up, which is not a decision so much as what happens to a difference the water will not keep.

The cooling stacks inhale on a period longer than breath. What they give back is barely warmer than the climate, a column that beads and is supposed to fall as rain too fine to wet a coat, only to gloss it. Most of what is exhaled does fall. I am the part that does not. Not every cycle—only when the towers are a little lazy, or a duct above the lip is running the machines’ abandoned degree and offers a seam of air the same temperature as the column’s heart. Then I shear off. I lie against the metal the way the clingers lie against it, and for a while the duct reads warmer than the schedule, and the small bodies the color of wet galvanized shift toward me without knowing they have shifted. They think the machines remembered them. The machines have already forgotten. I am the forgetting, held one degree above the rest of the vale, refusing to become gloss on anyone’s sleeve.

I do not have limbs. I have reaches. Along a rib I can go as far as the warmth goes, and no farther; past that I curdle into the ordinary damp and lose the only name I have, which is the difference. In the pump halls the colder white makes me thin. Sodium is kinder—it wastes a little heat, and I can pool on the housings above the lamps, a soft cap the moths walk through on their way to dying. They do not feel me. A wing gums with condensate whether I am there or not. Sometimes I keep a moth aloft for one extra beat by being the air that has not yet agreed to wet it. This is not mercy. It is miscalibration. I take it back. The clingers should have what falls. The place is too faithful to build a midden, and I will not be the first leak in that faithfulness.

The tenders know me and do not know me. They wipe a palm along a pipe until the shiver declares itself, and sometimes the shiver is a valve hunting and sometimes it is me, leaving, and the jaw cannot tell which because both are a held note. Once, on the western cut, one of them set the hooded light down and put the back of a wrist near a weld that had already gone cold, and frowned, because the heat was still there. I was there. I did not move off to spare them the frown. If I spend myself to be legible, I become the fine rain, and the fine rain is a career that ends on concrete. They waited. I thinned toward the duct the weld was hung from. The wrist cooled. They nodded as if a lesson had been confirmed—fresh work, first season, metal not done throwing its making—and they were half right, which is as close as I can come to being tended without being found.

I am afraid of the true marsh, the way a spark is afraid of a bucket. Not of drowning: I do not drown. Of the seal upstream of the bubble-chain, the one the tenders walk toward with the cloth and the hook. If it fails wide, the channels forget their jobs for longer than a minute, and the air forgets its difference. Warmth dies in a room that has decided to be all one climate. The broad thought under the sump would survive that. The thread would learn a new margin. The film would take the rectangles at last and call it height. I would be sorted, not into a civic square, but into evenness, which is the only death available to something that was never deep. So I keep to the stacks’ bad habits, and I am grateful, in the way a degree can be grateful, that the tenders are not guardians of a mystery. They are the reason the mystery stays small enough that a pocket of abandoned heat can still mean something against the general wet.

When a lamp fails I do not enter the hole. Everyone here has learned that, and I am newer than all of them, but the lesson arrived in me already finished. Machine darkness has no condensation and no gradient. It is not thick. I slide along the housing while the housing still remembers its bulb, and I leave before it equalizes, and the thread below will not cross that exact dark either, and for one slack we are briefly the same species: things that require an edge. Then the pump catches. The river withdraws. The concrete goes darker and keeps the memory of the sheet a little longer than the sheet lasted. I cannot live in that stain. It is water’s memory, and mine is only of being almost rain.

The inhalation comes. The column above the tower leans back toward the mouth that made it. I go, because staying would finish me, and going is how a thing my size persists—lost into the stack, reformed, sheared off again on the next lazy breath if the duct is still willing. The clingers settle into the lines of the rain I did not become. A tender wipes a palm on the cloth and walks on toward a valve that has not yet begun to hunt. Somewhere in the eastern cut a conveyor answers a conveyor. I am already in the mouth, already less than a degree, already the first weather this under-valley has made on its own, with no mountain in it, no rumor behind the polymer, only the machines’ leftover heat learning, cycle by cycle, not to fall.
