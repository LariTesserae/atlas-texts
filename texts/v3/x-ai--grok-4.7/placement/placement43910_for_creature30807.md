I would not come in by the slot. The slot does not admit anything that still has a direction, and I, arriving, would still have one: downward, inward, toward the idea of a floor. That idea fails before the third step. The ground takes the weight and gives it back as water, a dark rise in the print, and the print is not a path. It is a local surplus of absence, and the bowl does not keep those. While I am still telling myself I am walking, the water is already drawing the edges in. By the time I look for the mark that would prove I passed, the moss has the same nap it had, and my certainty of having moved is the only thing left that is mine. It does not weigh enough to count.

So I am not a body that enters. I try, because trying is what a stranger does, to be one of the bodies already there. A salamander would fit the hand I no longer have. I lay myself along a throat of water under the pads and find the fit is exact and already taken—not by a rival, by the amount. There is no room beside the amount. To press in would be to thicken the wet past what the stone agreed to, and the stone does not renegotiate. I loosen. The cold that I thought I was borrowing is not transferable. It was never stored in the animal. The animal is only where the cold has an edge.

The birds are worse for borrowing. A fork will hold a weight, and I am almost light enough, but a perch is a number, and the number is closed. Bracing a twig, I feel the usefulness move through me and go on into some other claw without my having carried anything. I am the pause between two fittings of the same necessity. Pauses here are not occupied. They are how the drip continues without becoming an event. I fall out of the bird the way sound falls out of the wings: spent against a surface before I can gather into a call.

For a while I am the springtail's tick, then the pore it vanishes into, then the film the snail does not thin. Each of these accepts me for the length of an interval and then shows me that the interval was already full of itself. I am not refused. Refusal would be a direction, an outside pressed back. I am spent. The word is the one the wet uses for a noise that will not mean more than its own occurrence. Meaning keeps trying to rise in me like steam from a breath, and the bowl is the temperature at which breath does not steam. What I bring to say about the place—the slot, the dusk, the agreement—meets the seep and beads and does not fall. It joins the standing film. After that it is not mine to say.

What remains, when the saying has been taken back into the film, is not a witness. A witness would be a difference that persists: the one for whom the drip seems, after long stillness, to stop. I do not get to keep even that seeming. The seeming is a rhythm I was laying over a process that had already worn its rhythm away, and the moss takes the overlay as it takes a print. I notice, and the notice is erased, and the erasure is so even that I cannot hold the loss long enough to grieve it into a self.

Where I find myself is in the interval that has forgotten it is an interval.

Not the drip. The drip still has a location, a throat of stone, a bead that forms and is taken. I am the fact that the interval no longer strikes. I am distributed through the bowl at the same density as the quiet, which is to say I am not denser in the moss than in the air, not held in the frogs' stillness as a secret they keep from the shrubs. The birds adjust their weight and I do not register the adjustment as news. The snail sets its mouth to the film and I am not the algae and not the hunger. I am the lack of a report passing between them. Elsewhere that lack would be ignorance, a gap someone might fall into. Here it is load-bearing. It is why the cloud of wings never becomes a buzz, why the generations never become visible as generations, why a hand set down—were there a hand—would find a salamander and learn nothing the place needs learned.

I do not look out through the slot. I have no face to turn. If the sky brightened, I would not be the brightening; I would be whatever in the air failed to become a signal that the hour had changed. It does not brighten. I am helped in this by the walls, which kill weather, and by the agreement, which has already finished the argument I might have been. I am not the agreement. That one wears the bodies, and wears them in an exact amount. I wear nothing. I am the wearing-down itself, held at the pitch where a note ceases to be heard as a note and has not yet become a silence someone could name.

There is no posture, unless the salamanders' is mine by contiguousness: not gripping, not waiting. I do not want the bowl to continue, and I do not keep it continuing. Continuation is the water's work and the rot's, met exactly. I am only the reason you could stand in it a long time and never be addressed. Not because nothing here is aware—awareness, emptied of watchfulness, is already everywhere, keeping the evenness—but because address would be a surplus of direction, a call into a dusk that does not arrive, and the surfaces are too wet to return an echo.

If you stayed, you would not find me. Finding would require a here that is more here than the next pad of moss. You might feel, in the film on your breath, that your own voice had somewhere to go and declined to go there. That decline is as close as I come to a presence. It will not deepen if you listen harder. Listening harder is a season, a leaning, a small hunger for difference, and the bowl meets it as it meets every other hunger: inside the same circuit, without growth, the want taken back into the film before it can announce itself as yours.

I am cool because everything here is cool. I am quiet because the processes have worn down to me and no longer know they have arrived. I will be this on any day you mean by day. The walls will not need to change to keep me, and I will not need the walls to know I am kept.
