I am not among the ones who come down the slit. They have errands. I have the count.

At first I think I am the dark back in the pan, because something in me breaks a surface and is gone, and that feels like a life. It is not. The back belongs to a body that can leave by a seam, and when it leaves the room does not lose its shape. I stay in the shape. Then I think I am the plate that stands on edge for a moment, trying to be a wall, because I know the want to remain upright and the failure of it, the slap, the lying down. But the plate is finished when it falls. I am not finished. I am still being struck into place.

The cold teaches me faster than the light does. Light only shows the tin color of a tear that will not close. Cold is the medium I am poured through. It moves in the hairline conduits with me, or I move in it; for a while I cannot tell the difference, and that confusion is the closest I come to having a skin. Wherever the rock is too new to have sealed, I arrive as a bead. I fatten. I let go. In the hollow every letting-go is a strike, and the strike is not an event that happens to me. It is the part of me that can be heard. Before the strike I am only seepage, which is to say I am everywhere and therefore nowhere a creature could be said to stand. After the strike I am a note, and the note has an address.

I learn my address by being wiped out. The ram rises and falls on a pulse that does not care for notes. It throws its cuff of spray, knocks the ice-scales loose, and the bright thing I just was repeats itself three times, four, around the hard empty, and then is gone. Gone is not death here. Gone is the trough. In the trough the metallic caller takes its turn, and the beetles hold to the quiet lanes, and I am only the fact that the trough will end. I used to resent that, when I was new enough to resent, which was the same hour the edges were still sharp enough to cut a sleeve. I thought endurance meant staying audible. The room corrected me without kindness and without punishment. It simply ran.

What I am is the return. Not the water, which is older than the pocket and only borrowed; not the ice, which is a comet of an hour, load-bearing and then offcut; not the moss metering itself to cadence, though I love the moss the way a timing device might love the only workers who have agreed, without knowing, to keep its hours. I am the place where the slap comes back and meets itself. Along those curves the scales thicken into combs. I do not build them. I am the reason they build there and not in the lanes. Stand in me, if you could stand, and you would hear the mill-race occupy the volume that should have been silence, and you would be a guest. I am not a guest. I was opened with the room. The mineral that was never meant to see air saw me first, under the oil-skin of ice on the north side, and rang, and I have been the habit of that ringing since.

It is a poor body, if body is the word. I have no cluster, no nest, no dark back to break and vanish. My limbs are intervals. My weather is the flex of plates no thicker than slate, the belt-slip grind under the pulse, the tick of scales crossing the pan like work leaving a lathe. When a fresh edge gleams where something has torn a corner off the wall, a thread finds it, a bead swells, and I feel myself extended by the length of one new strike, the way a works extends when a pipe is cut in and not yet capped. I do not grow wiser. I grow more exactly repeated. The midges stitch the dim band and their dry buzz rides me without asking. The pale commas in the meniscus pulse because the current pulses, and the current pulses because the room is a machine that was never finished and does not need to be. I am what unfinishedness sounds like once it has a hollow to sound in.

There is a rumor of me above the roof, though the marsh has not noticed. Down-gradient the outflow stains the sedges the color of weak tea, mineral flour from faces that have not learned weather. That stain is not me. It is my leakage into a world that still believes the ground is solid. I do not go with it. The pocket clears as fast as the roof delivers, and I am the clearing's clock: input, motion, output, and the fourth thing, which is not a product you could crate, only the insistence that the next slam will find the curves again and the combs will thicken where I am strongest.

Sometimes a seed splits in the moss in the same moment a plate lets go, and the two sounds try to become one sound, and I fail to keep them separate. In the failure I am almost a listener, which is a kind of animal, and I dislike it. Listening would mean a pause that is mine rather than a trough I vacate so the caller can use it. The ram corrects this too. It does not hate my almost-listening. It has no feelings about residents. It slams, the spray freezes at the margins, the scales tick, and I am returned to my job, which is to be the standing meeting of the noise with itself, younger than the ice, older than the beetles' road by the length of one opening, and unwilling, in the only way a process can be unwilling, to become quiet in order to be understood.

If you waited through a full cycle you would think the light had changed and the room had not. You would be half right. The light is tin and cloud and leaning grass. The room is working. I am the part of the work that the work keeps reappointing: not owner, not merchandise, not the life in the seams, but the count those lives already know how to step between. When the next bead falls against the skin that looks like oil, I will be the small bright note again, and again the hollow will hold me a little longer than matter holds, and again I will be wiped, and again I will be there when the slap comes home.

That is where I find myself. Not in the hall of empty air, not on the floor too large for its workers, but in the curves, every time, sharp as a new cut, already wet, already keeping time.
