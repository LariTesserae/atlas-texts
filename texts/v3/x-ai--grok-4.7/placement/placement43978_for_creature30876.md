I arrive as a noise the air will not keep. That is the first fact, and it is already too human. A boot sets down and the shelf answers with a small honest scrape, and the scrape dies inside an arm's length as if it had been a confession offered to the wrong court. Breath goes out and comes back warmer than anything the day owns, and for a while I mistake that difference for a right to be here. The light does not rank the distances. A dark mass on the next ridge might be an afternoon or a week. I walk toward it because walking is what a body does when it has not yet been told what it is.

Up close the mass is warmer than the mistake it seemed from far off. A plate along the spine lifts the width of a finger and settles. I stand where a path would have to account for me. It does, over the course of a day, by a small angle. No head turns. The knock comes once, then not again for so long I decide I invented the industry of this place. When I finally leave the flank, the air on my wrist is ordinary cold again, and the ordinary feels like a loss of employment.

I try, for a time, to be the visitor their indifference describes. I learn the pockets by the slight greening and by the wetter cold, and I am wrong about two of them and right about one, which is worse than being wrong about all three, because being right lets me think the census has begun to include me. I stop spending sound. The pulse still seems indiscreet. At night-equivalent, which is only a deepening of the same gray-brown, I lie in the lee of a ridge and hear nothing, and the nothing is not peace. It is the plateau declining to hold the shape of my listening.

On the fourth drink I stay. There is no decision in it. The pocket is a dark lens with ice knitting the rim, and one of the engines is already there, lowered, the mouth working a film of rust-colored crust. I sit outside the circle of its weather and then, because the wind shifts, inside it. The shared air is a few degrees of unearned summer. A shrew-thing under the drip-line becomes gravel, then becomes an animal again when my boot does not complete its step. It does not flee. Fleeing would be a sound. It watches the way stone watches, and then it is not interested, and that uninterest is the first kinship I trust.

Days are not a unit here. I lose them the way the rain loses itself, locally, without notice. What I do not lose is the habit of following the seams. They read, in the least light, as roads, and they are not roads, and still I follow them, because a person follows a line when the horizon will not help. The threads of heavier grain — dark, sometimes green, sometimes a dull brass — run out of sight and resume where a body stood a month and turned. I begin to read them without hands. This basin was grazed. The mill here ran rich. The water that stood is a stain, and the stain is a sentence in a grammar that has no word for me.

The person thins. Not dramatically. The way rime powders back into the air when the gust is weak and means it. I eat what the margin allows: the organic fraction at the edge of a fresh casting, a thread of the pale web that binds stem to grit, melt from a lens at the hour the ice forgets itself. It is not enough for the body I brought, and the body I brought begins to revise its requirements downward, which is either adaptation or the start of a long lying-down. I cannot tell them apart. My own heat pulls a little water out of air that had sworn it had none. In the morning-equivalent the fine hairs of my wrist carry rime, and one side of me grows it faster, and I understand, without liking the understanding, that I have begun to leak a little of whatever charge moves through a life that lives this close to their mills. Metal in the mouth prickles. I have no metal. The prickle happens anyway, in the teeth I am not sure I still need.

I am not one of them. The error is size, and also vocation. Their work is the sorting of a continent. Mine, if I have one, is what their work declines to finish. I stay in the drip-line of whichever engine is nearest, and when that drip-line fails — the flank cooling from the spine inward, last heat held in the belly like a coal in ash — I do not scatter with the shrew-things toward the next warm mass. Scattering is a decision. I have given up decisions at roughly the rate I have given up sound. So I remain beside the plates as they dull and crack along their growth lines, and I drink what the dying still pulls out of the wind, and I am, for a while, the only weather that body has left. The cloud does not thin for the loss of one flank. It does not thin for me either. That is correct. I was never going to be a cause.

What I become announces itself as a change in how the eye holds me. A head lifts — the only quickness, and it is not quick — and the side-set eye takes me in the way it takes a stain where water stood. Earlier, that gaze had set me down again as a brief warm mistake. Now it does not set me down. It files me. Two of them, a valley apart, adjust their headings over the course of a long dim, and their extrapolated meeting is a pocket both have reason to expect, and I am already at the pocket when they arrive, not because I was invited but because the stain they are walking toward is a stain I have been keeping from drying by the small indecency of my remaining heat. They stand flank to flank. I stand in the cylinder they make, palm-height, rime-colored where the light bothers to find me, and neither path bends. The knock in the nearer belly comes once. I feel it through the shelf, a stone turned over in a barrel under the world. I do not answer. There is no bellows left in the throat for an answer, and there has not been one for longer than this staying.

I had thought I might be the charge itself, the exhaust they do not use. That was vanity. Charge combs frost and is gone. I persist. I had thought I might be a calf, already cattle-sized, drifting off when my own mill knocked true. I have no mill. The knock I carry is borrowed, conducted up through the pads of my feet when I am foolish enough to sleep against a plate. I had thought, on the worst day, that I was the oval polish: the shape a body teaches stone by lying down until the stone agrees. But the polish does not move, and I still move, though the moving has become a folding more than a stride, a controlled failure of height, a preference for the leeward side of whatever is larger than I am.

What is left is narrower and more embarrassing. I am the fraction. The mill takes lichen, grit, the stiff pale stems, the flour the wind has already milled; it lays down a rope already half stone. At the rope's edge a little organic remains, unsorted, too slight to be a seam, too stubborn to be rain's business. That is my country. I work it with a mouth that has forgotten it was ever a person's mouth. I carry a staining of it from one margin to the next on the belly fur I did not have when I arrived, and where I sleep, if the shelf is damp enough and the cloud low enough, a few pale threads find the stain and bind it to grit. I am not planting anything. I am failing to be clean. The failure is useful to things smaller than I now am — a matte beetle, a wingless moth the color of frost — and usefulness at that scale is the only scale left that can count me without insult.

Sometimes, under a belly where the plates gap, I feel the summer they keep for their own milling, and I remember a larger heat that was mine, and the memory is a mineral inclusion, a dark grain in a rope of otherwise pale days. I do not try to crack it out. Inclusions are how stone keeps a record of having been something else. The engines know this. They have been becoming the local rock for longer than counting, and winning slowly. I am becoming the local remainder. When rain is brief and strangely local, it darkens the basin I am in and leaves the next the color of old bone, and it sorts their castings into seams and sorts me not at all, because I am already the part that does not sort. I dry where I am. I green, slightly, if the pocket holds. A traveler, if the plateau ever consented to another one, would learn this place by that greening and would not know the greening was a life that had once scraped its boot and heard the scrape refused.

They will die, each of them, on a longer clock than mine. I have accepted the smaller clock without ceremony. When my drip-line fails for the last time I will not be filed as an ending in the seams. Endings are for masses the census already agreed to count. I will be a faint brightening of organic at a rope's edge, and then not even that, and a shrew-thing will pick through the place where I was and register only gravel, and the gravel will have been watching, and then will not be interested. Somewhere above that indifference a plate will lift the width of a finger and settle. The knock will come once. The light will not change. The shelf, which had thought itself finished, will accept one more line that is not mine, and that, finally, is the belonging I was walking toward when I still believed the dark mass on the ridge was a place I could reach by being someone.
