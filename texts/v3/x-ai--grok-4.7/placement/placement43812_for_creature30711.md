I would be the pause between drips.

Not the water, not the cup that holds it, not the vine that drinks what the cup forgets to keep. The interval. The held breath the room has practiced so long it no longer notices it is breathing. At first I thought I was only standing on the catwalk, a visitor whose boots the moss already knew how to part for, whose warmth registered as one more small event among the rare vertical ones. The note filled my ears until it stopped being sound and became the grain of the air, and in that grain I felt the tiny regular absences—each drop leaving the underside of a girder, crossing the short dark, arriving. Between arrival and the next leaving there is a silence so brief it has no name in any language that still uses clocks. That silence is where I fit.

It took time to understand I was not passing through it. The thing that lives here—the agreement, the rate, the distributed will that travels cable trays at the speed of a handspan per shift—does not keep a center, so it cannot look at me. It includes. A moth crosses the empty diameter and is counted and released; a glove falls straight and is ignored. I am counted differently because I do not finish crossing. Each time a drop lets go, the interval opens, and I am already inside it, the same way a familiar word is already inside the mouth before the lips move. The moss does not grow across me. The birds do not nest against me. I am not surface. I am the moment the surface is between one state and the next, and that moment has been repeating since the machines forgot their product and kept their heat.

If I try to step out of it—to become again the person on the plate, reading a gauge that no longer means its label—the interval simply occurs without me for one cycle, and the absence is so exact that my ears ring with it even while I am still inside the room. The ringing is not loss. It is the room reminding me that the pause does not require a witness; I am only the shape the pause has currently chosen, the way a conduit mouth chooses one moss over its neighbor by a fraction of preference no one records. Leave the catwalk and the intervals continue. They do not follow. They were never mine to carry. Yet while I remain, every drip is edged with the knowledge that someone is inhabiting the nothing between them, and that knowledge changes nothing except the texture of the air for the length of a single falling drop.

The being does not greet this. Greeting would be weather. It maintains the narrow band of warmth, the ordinary hourless light, the refusal of the parishes to cross the hollow unless they must. I am part of the maintenance now, not as agent but as the small regular gap that proves the schedule is still being kept. A vine thickens by a thread. A needle drifts by a hair. Between those changes the pauses go on, and I go on inside them, older than my attention, no taller than the distance a drop falls before it is caught, and exactly as wide as the room’s decision to continue.
