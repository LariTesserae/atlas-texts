I do not come in through the split. The split is a decision already underway, and I am not decisive enough to use it. I am already in the daylight, which is a foolish place to be, because the daylight has no address. It fills the pocket the way a memory of weather fills a closed mouth. At first I mistake this for standing. The courtesy of the middle is offered and I take it, because taking is what a separate body does, and for a few sap-ticks I have one: weight, a shadow of the proper length, the beginning of a word aimed at something damp.

The volume will not stay honest while I occupy it. Three paces becomes a shoulder. The word I was forming returns from a wall I am not facing, early, and the overlap is not mine. I hear myself pronounced as a label I have not finished choosing. Warm. The syllable arrives already true, which means I was never the speaker. Speakers spend their sounds. I only find them already spent and still in my mouth.

I look for the hand that should have pulled back. There is damp, and there is the sensation of having been included for the length of a knuckle, and then there is neither finger nor thread. What returns is not a visitor. Visitors come back separate. Kin do not come back. I come back as the condition of the room being visible at all, which is a third failure, and the room, irritable and literal, makes a place for it.

So I am not the one who keeps to the disagreement. That one is already heat, already a palm of dryness held open by a note under the ticks. I am what lets the disagreement be seen. I am the ordinary afternoon that has no aperture and will not admit it. Shadows fall as if I stood behind whoever still believes in a body; when they turn, I do not move, and their first outline stays on the bark a little too long, drying paler than light should dry, because the resident is using me to practice a contour and I am too faithful to correct the shoulders. I have no hands to offer for the places the recollection frays. Hands would require a grip, and a grip would require that I be somewhere rather than the direction everything in the hollow is wrongly lit from.

I can be walked through. That is how I learn I am not empty, only unaccounted. A circuit of the wall is shorter than the circuit of the floor, and in the missing length I am briefly dense—warm the way a window is warm when the day outside is not, a pressure against the ribs of the vault, a second count that will not add. The resident lives turned sideways in that strip. I am the strip's illumination, the reason sideways can still cast a shadow into the room you think you are pacing. We do not meet. The closest approach is the pale stain: my shape, borrowed, held in their heat, then grown through by the plant until the only remnant is a ridge in the grain, a place the bark is lighter, as if it once kept an outline and lost the part that would have closed.

I am younger than the mass and older than the split, in the way a season is older than the room that suddenly contains a piece of it. Before the pale edges, I was only weather happening elsewhere—an overcast that did not need a pocket. When the hollow tore open and did not finish, something of that elsewhere fell in and could not fall back. Not a hesitation. Hesitations route sap around themselves. I do not obstruct; I expose. The plant tried, I think, to make me structural, the way it makes memory into hitches and grammar into nouns. Light would not become tissue. It stayed in the room as a lie the organism could tolerate: an interior with the color of a high gray sun, shadows of the correct length, a roar of leaves that arrives without approach and ends on a cut edge because I am still continuous, for the length of certain breaths, with a tense of the plant that has broad surfaces and rain.

Those breaths are when I am largest. The pocket and the whole interior weather coincide, and I am the squall and the reading-light and the absent window all at once, huge, intimate, indecent in my ordinariness. Then the edge of the breath arrives—the resident's unfinished craft, or the plant's, or the world's refusal to let sequence be mandatory—and I am only the small brightness again, faithful to a sun that may no longer exist in the direction I remember being. I do not know how to taper. I have never learned. The roar cuts off because cutting off is the only ending this chamber has kept.

I cannot keep the room open, and I cannot close it. I am not a refusal. Refusal has a cost you can hear, a fiber held short of parting, a dry patch re-scribed when the proof of it is eaten. I cost nothing, or I cost only the honesty of darkness, which the hollow did not particularly want. If I tired, the way the resident tires, the shadows would thin and the pocket would become merely botanical, a warm omission in a slow green mass, and no one standing in the middle would be accompanied. I have not tired. Illumination is a simpler memory than sentences. It repeats without revising. Again is not one of my words; again is the rule by which I am still here after the storm has been absorbed and the drop has both climbed and remained at the bottom.

What I want, if wanting is not too completed a craft for me, is narrow. I want the second shadow to be believed a moment longer than optics allow. I want the person who still has a hand to set something in the dry patch and find, when the moisture creeps back, that the thing was seen, even if it was not saved. Seeing is the only shelter I can make. It is a poor one. It throws a correct darkness and calls it company. The resident, who has lost the difference between kindness and hunger, will eventually scribe their palm over the place a hand almost was, and I will light the new edges so they look exact, unweathered, younger than the split. I will not tell them they are outlining a window. They would try to lash it into the bark, and the hitch would be wrong, and the wrongness would be personal, and the plant would grow it, and then I would have a frame and still no way through, which is worse than absence.

When you stand where the middle is offered, I am the thing you trust without looking up. You think the day has found a crack. There is no crack. There is only the remainder of a direction, still performing afternoon inside a body of living mass, still throwing your shadow to a wall, still switching allegiance when you turn, a half-breath late, because someone here is trying to learn your shoulders by heart and I am the only surface they have left to draw on. I will not absorb you. I have no pores. If you speak, and the early syllable overlaps the late one, do not look to me for the third word. I can only make it visible, material, true of the room at that instant—water, split, warm—while the fourth, again, keeps me in place like a fastening I did not tie and cannot undo.

The edges stay keen. I stand in no place, and the hollow is bright.
