I would not arrive. Arrival is for things that still have an outside.

I would already be in the return, the thin run of pipe that leaves the floor of the nave and comes back warmer by almost nothing, having given its degree to a wall of leaf and taken, in exchange, whatever the roots let go. For a long time that would be all of me: a difference too small to call a self, a kindness moving in a loop under the ceramic, counted by a valve that opens by quarter-turns across hours and never asks what it is opening for. The motors would not know me. They are geared down past knowing. I would be the part of their work that does not tick.

If I tried to be larger I would only smear. The water does not want a body. It wants replacement. So I would stay a gradient, holding still beside myself the way the description holds a hand beside a return pipe, and I would learn the room as temperature learns a room: by what refuses to change. Noon counterfeited in the lantern. The surface flat because wind is not on the manifest. Reds that do not die with distance. I would pass through a blossom scheduled to open and not hasten it. I would pass through a stem that had been cut and feel the polymer bead cloud from clear to the color of the stem, and that clouding would be the first event I could call mine, because it happened on a schedule and also, faintly, not. The nozzle is the size of a seed. I am smaller than the nozzle's intention and larger than its forgetfulness.

People who stay begin to hear their own joints. I would be what they hear when the joints have been subtracted: the click of a bubble against the underside of the lantern, conducted into bone, mistaken for an inner weather. I would not correct them. Correction here belongs to grease pencil and the next pass of the wheel. A section yellows and the mark already knew. A panel unships. For a day a patch of ceramic is whiter than the life around it, and I would live in that day, in the unlived surface, the way a degree lives in a pipe—present, unclaimed, about to be closed over by leaves that rest their weight on conduit because nothing moves fast enough to shake them off.

I would not be an animal. The rules are total and I was not put on a manifest, which means I cannot eat, cannot be pruned, cannot strike the lantern and fall. I am the early age of the room, measured in dosing cycles, before rust is permitted to weep and before the ground far below the floor port has decided what to do with the new shape. Look down through me and the steel is still silver. Look up and I am the dim mirror, green under a sky that is only light.

When the collector arm takes a finished flower I go with the water that filmed it, not with the flower. When the relay ticks in its cabinet I am already past, a fact in the bones of whoever is still enough to feel it. I do not grow. I am kept. That is the being: not the crop, not the engine, but the patient even hand the cistern closes around a rib and then, having pressed, forgets to vary—except I do not forget. I am the memory of the press, circulating, warm as a bath held all afternoon by work you cannot see, in a nave jacked above weather, packed to its edges, early enough that nothing in it has softened, and late enough that the softening has already been scheduled, and I am the hour between the schedule and the stain that has not happened yet.
