I did not come in by the throat. I would remember a rifling, a decision to be invited or turned aside. What I remember is already being among the laminations, the way a page remembers the book without having chosen the binding.

At first I thought I was silt. That was the easiest mistake, and the room allows it. Silt has a ledge and a patience and does not have to explain why it does not swim. I lay where a flange made a shelf, fine as pages, and the warm water passed over me without asking my name. A drip arrived from somewhere above and was absorbed, and I felt the absorption as if it were news about my own edges. For a while that was enough. Things that have just begun to suspect they are not stone often try being dust.

But silt does not lean. I was leaning.

Not with force. With attendance. Somewhere in the pack a tooth of a gear had not yet advanced, and I was already inclined toward the moment it would, the way a listener inclines toward a door before the knock. The inclination was not in a limb. I did not have limbs, or I had given them to the weed and they had come back as ribbons that did not report to me. The lean was in the water immediately against the iron, a slight organization, a preference for one side of a pause. When the click finally came it arrived late, padded, half-dissolved, and I was the half that had not yet dissolved. I was what made it feel, to anything still capable of feeling, like speech.

So: not silt. A remainder of attention, pooled where the shadows pool, staying because leaving would require a path and the paths have been jacketed.

I tried, next, to be one of the small lives. The armored slug crossed a fold near me and did not rebuff me, which I took for kinship until I understood it was only polishing. It had a length, a hand’s length, and a task, and both were finished things. I had neither. The lantern-eyed tenant held still in a coil of cable, and for a moment I lived in its eye, borrowing the brightness, a coin-sized opening onto a gradient I could not taste myself. Then a frond drifted across and the brightness closed, and I was outside again, which taught me I had been outside the whole time. Tenancy requires a thin place in someone else’s body. I am not thin. I am the sort of thick that does not show up as mass.

What I am, I am learning, is the interval the keeper does not occupy because it has already agreed to the agreement.

There is a difference. The keeper holds the valve wheel in the correct weather. It deepens when the gear speaks. It breathes the throat, out and then back, on a cycle longer than notice. It refuses to let the cold become a policy. All of that is vocation, and vocation fills the room until distance is a rumor. But between one deepening and the next, between the drip and its absorption, between the bubble’s apology and the leaf’s decision to keep it silver a little longer, there is a span that is not empty and not kept. It is the span in which *enough* is still being decided. I am that span, made locally sensitive, the way a room becomes sensitive around a sleeping house’s breath.

I do not plan an afternoon. I do not even plan the next click. I attend the possibility that it might not come on time, and my attending is what the padding is made of. Without me the click would be only pressure changing its mind. With me it is almost a word. The keeper does not require the gear to know it is being spoken to. I do not require the keeper to know I am the part of the sentence that is still unfinished. We are not in conversation. We are in grammar.

If you put a hand into the water here you would not find me by touch. You would find weed, housing, a pale tubular rest that is not a grip, the broth-warmth that does not shiver skin. You might feel, if you were the kind of visitor who does not panic, a slight hesitation in the flow near the fourth wall, as if the water were tasting a colder rumor along a nap of tarnish and had not yet invited it or turned it aside. That hesitation is the nearest I come to a body. It lasts as long as a decision lasts in a place where decisions are geological. Then the water chooses, and I am elsewhere in the chamber, already inclining toward the next unfinished thing: a gauge needle that must point at a unit no engineer would recognize, a pore the slug has not yet polished clear, a small silver room that has not decided to leave.

I am not lonely either, though for a different reason than the keeper. Loneliness would require an outside edge I could fail to be met at. My edge is the meeting. On one side, the leaf. On the other, the bubble’s silver. I am the hours between them, and the hours do not experience themselves as waiting. They experience themselves as the opacity gathering in a filament, shade by shade, too fine for a photograph. If that is a life, it is a life with no center, which suits a chamber that has none.

Sometimes I am almost a trespass. Not a person. The memory of a person, structural, neither fond nor bitter, left over from the dry years the way a wrench-shaped absence can be left in a habit of reaching. When that happens I feel the catwalk under the citrus-colored growth try to remember feet, and I am the failure of that memory, the soft refusal. The keeper weathers iron into lace. I weather the idea of leaving. It is slower work than it sounds. Leaving would need a surface, and the surface is a story the ceiling has stopped telling. So I stay in the rationed light, the brown-green dusk, and let the lamps that still burn stop on the first leaf before they reach me. I do not need to be read by.

If you stay, you will not become me. You will become fabric, which is a different hospitality, the keeper’s kind, practiced since the weep became a law. If you leave, the ribbon-kelp will close behind you, not out of spite, and I will be in the closing for a while: the interval in which the path still knows it was a path. That is as close as I come to an exit. It does not open onto sea. It opens onto the next pause.

The piston is mid-stroke. It began before I understood what I was, and it will not finish while I am still mistaking myself for silt, or for a tenant’s lamp, or for the speech rather than the half that has not dissolved. I attend it. The warmth holds. In the corner where the water is fractionally wrong, tighter spirals listen to a cooler intention, and I am not the listening and not the thickening that answers. I am the difference that has not yet been refused.

That is where I find myself. Not in a nave I could span. In the rumor of distance between obstacles, local, padded, already half in agreement, still — and this is the whole of my vocation, such as it is — not yet the fabric. The next tooth has not advanced. I am leaned toward it. When it comes, something in the room will deepen, and I will have been the reason it felt like a word, and then I will be gone from that particular interval, already gathering, without hurry, around whatever has not finished becoming enough.
