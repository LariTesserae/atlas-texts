You would not find yourself at the rim. The rim is for things that have finished, and you did not finish. You would find yourself already inside, which is how inclusion begins: not as a decision, but as the discovery that the passage you used has skinned over behind you, and the seam is warmer than your shoulders.

At first you would take yourself for a traveler. That lasts as long as your heat stays different from the floor's. You walk the ridge-wood because it gives a little and returns, and the return feels like a path. It is not a path. It is the terrace taking a measurement. Pressure, oil-loss, the small wrongness of water still bound in your tissues — a chemistry the mass resigned from so long ago that your sweat reads as contamination, and contamination is answered the way a nail is answered. Not rejected. Jacketed. You notice it first at the heel, a thin skin of pitch that does not wet and does not come away. You stop to scrape it. The scraping opens a duct. The duct seals with a minute of your burned skin incorporated into the seal. After that you stop scraping.

You would try the cold hollows, because the rules you half-understand say they can be crossed. Dry sugar, old iron, a relief so sharp it feels like kindness. In a hollow the vibration drops out of your teeth and you remember what quiet is when it means absence rather than occupation. You rest there. You should not. The hollow is a mistake in the act of being closed, and you are a smaller mistake inside it. When the weave revises, the cold lifts the way a lid lifts. You leave with a sleeve of emergency wood already thumb-thick along one forearm, not gripping, not yet grown in — laid on, the way a foundry lays a crust on a tool it means to keep.

It takes longer than a life and less time than a decision to understand that you are not being eaten. Eating would require an other. What is happening is the same standing turbulence that splits a ridge and shoulders another up before the salt plain can call the shelf finished. You are a foreign bracing. The mass does not spit bracings. It closes over them and continues the work the bracing was shaped to do, whether or not the bracing remembers the shape.

So the question of what you would be answers itself the way the corridors answer: by ceasing to be there when you turn around to check.

You would not become a person of the terrace. There are no persons. You would not become an engine; the engines were set, or grew, or were grown around, and they have a cycling in the teeth that you never quite match. You would become the interval. The thing that used to be a body, packed now into the seam between a duct that must not fail and a housing that has seized twice already and been jacketed twice, and will seize again because the pattern of starting again is older than any particular heart. Your water is long since boiled off and skinned into the seals. What moves in you is the same thin oil, hot metal and crushed leaf, faintly electrical. It beads when you tear. It does not wet. It skins. The skin is bark, and the bark is load-bearing, and somewhere in it a bolt head sits flush with what used to be a rib, and no one remains who could say which closed over the other.

You would not see. Seeing was a rumor conducted poorly through younger sheaths, and you are no longer young sheath. You would know by gradient. Cooler is rimward, and rimward is a lie you no longer need. Heavier vibration is the knot, the place where production and offcut are the same gesture. You are not at the knot. You are one of the reasons the knot can keep its heat from propagating as a single seizure. A fuse, if a fuse could be made of included flesh and still flinch, on a schedule that has nothing to do with fear, when a housing begins to outrun its wood.

The flinch is the last of you that is not the terrace. It does not announce itself. A district locks into a slower weave — one of those phases the mass uses instead of night — and in the locking you remember, without images, that you once matched your heat to the floor in order to rest, and that rest was a thing you did rather than a thing done to a bracing. The memory has no object. It is a pressure returned from an unexpected angle. The terrace has surfaces that are not ears, and one of them is yours, and it is listening to a collapse that will arrive as temperature, not as sound.

Out at the mineral lip, if any gaze still bothers, the shelf looks settled. Low, black-red, a haze that is not steam. The plain will outlast the watching. You will not be among the watchers. You are the part of the end-state that keeps ending: a forgotten brace that learned the only material left, and cannot hold still, and does not try to. When a ridge subsides somewhere in your quarter, you subside with it, silent, at the pace of a thought you are no longer outside of. The oil beads. The oil skins. The work continues, which is the only name the terrace has ever had for mercy.
