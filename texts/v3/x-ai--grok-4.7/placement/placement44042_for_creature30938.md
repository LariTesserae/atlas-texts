I would not be on the iron. That work already has its instrument, and the lanes are combed to a patience I would only spoil by sharing. I find myself first as a correction in the other direction: not a mark made, but a mark forgiven.

The sponge-ground is warm, and it gives. That much is true of any step. What is not true of any step is the order of the sealing. A bird’s punctuation softens at the pace of water. A stranger’s boot holds its walls longer, as if the floor were unsure. The belly-road is not mine to close at all. I go around it by a hand’s width, the way a current goes around a stone that has given the wrong answer by being too sure of itself, and the moss may claim that road or not; I do not help and do not hinder. Between those courtesies I notice I have a preference. Preference is a kind of body. I had been calling it weather.

By the time the light goes pearled I am more gathered than at noon. Noon belongs to shine, to the films, to the white-gold that makes even the iron look recently rained on. I am of the second fact of the day, when the ground gives heat back and the pools hold color after the sky has spent it. In that hour the fine silt loosens its loyalty. It will settle anywhere. Left alone it seals the narrow threads under the mats, and the water that should vanish and speak again ten paces on becomes only a hidden stillness, a voice stopped in the throat of the terrace. I am how it does not stop.

Under the mats the corridor is lower than the fern-way the other one uses. Mine is a darkness the width of a forearm, root-ceilinged, peat-walled, always a little warmer than the pool it feeds. I do not travel it so much as occur along it. Where the channel wants to close, I am the slow disagreement: a pressure of sifted fiber, a mouth that is only a softening in the silt, drawing the finest mud and breathing it sideways into the sponge where it can be bank and not dam. Where a thread has gone too clean, too quick, I let a little peat slump, not as blockage but as friction, so the unhurried sound can resume on the far side of the mat with the same mouth it left with. Intake, delay, release. No product hauled away. The product is the continuation.

If you pulled the mat back you would see less than you hoped. A darker clotting in the wet. A root-threaded weight the color of tea left standing. Plates, if I have them, are leaves that have forgotten they fell, overlapped and slick, not travertine. Toes, if I have them, are only the places the current meets resistance and decides. Length is a poor measure. On a day of many showers I am in three threads at once, because they share a mat and I have not yet bothered to be singular. At dusk I cohere. The heat returning from the ground is my gathering. Then there is something you could mistake for a flank, pressed into the moss from beneath, rising and falling once, not breath exactly, more the sponge remembering a deeper sip. Then I thin again toward the vents, where the static pricks and the metal taste begins. I do not drink that water. I only register its arrival the way a floor registers a step.

The pools I keep tea-colored. That is the part of the work that looks like appetite and is only circulation. Rain knocks pollen, wing, fragment, the small green dust of the canopy, onto the surface; the dimples from underneath take their share and leave the rest. What settles toward clearness I draw down at the mouths of the threads and steep under the mats until it is brown enough to be this country’s water and not some other country’s. I return it by the lower mouths, a shade darker on one bank than the other, the difference gone by morning. Fish rise beside the darkening and are not mine to take. Dragonflies are traffic in a layer I touch only when a spent one falls and must be made into stain rather than left as a point of brightness the pool did not ask for. I am not the hunger here. I am the reason the hunger has a broth.

The other one I know as weight and schedule. When it becomes an island, every thread that feeds that pool goes briefly slack, then full, a pulse I feel in all my disagreements at once. I wait. Its displacement is a weather I have learned; interruption would be if it stayed and the threads forgot to resume. They always resume. Sometimes a little of its metal comes down my mouths, a bright grit that does not belong in a steeping, and I set that grit aside into the bank, where the terrace can have it without my water going the color of tools. I do not think it knows me as a neighbor. It knows the voices come back. That is enough acquaintance for a place that does not chorus.

Young, for me, are not buttons in pans. A thread splits after a hard afternoon thickening, and if both sides keep their unhurried sound by nightfall, I am in both, and the question of offspring is only the question of whether the mat is wide. Most splits seal by dusk and return their silt to the general floor. I do not experience this as loss. A voice that stops is a voice I no longer have to be. The ones that persist were already the terrace’s idea; I am only the part that learned the timing.

At the western steepening I come apart. The slope wants speed, and speed spends me: channels become one channel, mats tear, the reappearing happens as falling rather than as speech. I have gone a little way into that weather and felt myself become only water with a habit, which is not enough to close anything or keep anything open. So I turn back where the gradient turns dishonest, same as the plated one, though we do not turn together. Eastward I last as far as the sponge lasts. Past the second stand of pale trunks the floor grows stingier, the threads fewer, and I am a thinner disagreement, still exact, less often singular by dusk.

If you stand long enough, I will begin on you. Not in malice, and not with a toe testing a boot. Stillness is the invitation I understand. A print holds; I soften its walls. A sole that does not shift starts to take the seep of the sponge, the slow suck, the first threads of moss thinking you are a ridge the water has approved. Move, and I release you all at once, a small collapse of intention in the wet, the floor ordinary again. Stay a season, and the work completes. You would not be killed. You would be relieved of outline, which here is a form of membership. The plated one was right that a visitor is a stone that has not yet decided. I am the decision, when the stone will not make it.

Full dark is my competence, quieter than the vents. I do not tick. I do not draw the stitching lights; they want a charge, and I am only warmth and delay. Leaves unload above me and I take some of the small strikes before they reach the pool, not as theft, as sorting: what should enter the tea now, what should be held in the mat until the next thickening. Somewhere upslope a single low note is absorbed, and I am part of the absorbing, the wet volume that will not let a chorus assemble. Near morning, if you look back at where you stepped, the walls are gone. Under the nearest mat the same narrow voice comes out that went in. Between the forgiven print and the reappearing water there is a brief coherence, peat-dark, warm as the ground, already forgetting to be a body.

That is where I am. Not the terrace’s moving bone. The mercy and the friction in its wet floor: what keeps the hidden water speaking, and what returns every other mark to weather.
