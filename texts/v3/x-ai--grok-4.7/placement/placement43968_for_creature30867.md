You would not arrive. Arrival is a lower habit, something coasts do. Here the first fact is that you have already been standing long enough for the light to spend itself on you, and that your weight is wrong for the dust. The sinter holds a print that is shallower than a boot and deeper than a lizard. You look down to correct it and the correction does not happen. Your feet are the color of the pan. You have been crossing.

For a while you try to be one of the strings that move at dusk. That almost works. The long-legged animals do not object to your pace, which is no pace, only continuation. They face the wind to sleep and you find your face turned the same way without deciding. Salt is a taste you already know; you have been licking it off the verge where coolant has wept and dried into a white thread. Their bodies are nearly the color of heated air. Yours, when you try to see it, is the same failure of color. If you were one of them you would not be trying to see.

The cities will not have you either, not as a citizen. You pass an outer wall in the late afternoon and the hush inside is not secrecy but the ordinary failure of sound in air this thin. People speak close. You stand on the road scar outside and hear a hammer as a thought, and you understand the thought, which the animals do not. A bell the size of a doorway goes once, low, built to push a tone through what would otherwise swallow it, and the tone reaches you as if it had your name and had decided not to use it. You are not of the stacked brick. You know the tanks, though. Somewhere in you is the taste of water that has been a decision too many times: metal, and the patience of filters. You have drunk what they ration. That memory is not an animal’s.

So you are the thing the country grew in the gap, which is most of the country.

Between one settlement and the next there is a day of nothing that is not nothing. Transmission towers diminish into glare. You travel that interval the way weather does, not to arrive but because the plateau continues and so do you. By day the pale flocks drop into the shade of machinery and pick the warm films off metal; you do not join them, but you know which faces of the cuts are ripening, the copper-green and the rust, slow as a life. You have watched one face long enough that a child in the nearest city could swear it has not moved, and you would be the reason the child is nearly right. By night the many-legged things come up from the cracks and farm the dust. You do not farm. You are what their farming assumes: a continuity, a warmth that is not a body, a silence that still counts as presence because the lizards under the pipes shift when you pass and then settle, as they settle for pipes.

If someone in a yard looked up at the wrong hour they might take you for heat-shimmer with a direction. If one of the standing animals turned its head it would include you and then forget the inclusion, the way a string forgets which member joined at the salt pan. The people, speaking close inside the walls, have a word for the intervals that is really only the word for road. They do not need a tighter name. You are not worshipped and not hunted. The grid is overbuilt and underloaded; you do not drink its electricity. You drink the long weathering, and the drip, and the hour after a dry storm when the map of dust has been rewritten and already looks as if it had always been that arrangement.

What you are, once the earlier mistakes fall away, is the agreement the plateau made with its own emptiness: that something should cross it without hurrying, and remember the tanks, and not need a leaf, and answer the low bell by continuing. You sleep facing the wind, when you sleep. You do not cast the shadow the hour requires. Ahead of you the horizon fails in the usual way, rock and air deciding they are the same substance, and you are already in that decision, which is as close as this height comes to a home.
