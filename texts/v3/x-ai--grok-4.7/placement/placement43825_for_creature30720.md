The cold arrives before any name I could wear. It comes up through the footing and does not stop at a skin, because I am not yet sure I have one. The gray leak is already there, lid-colored, edgeless. I stand in it the way a grain stands in a film: available to be written over.

At first I try the air. The keen presses, and for a moment I think pressure is enough of a body. A sheet holds its hook-shape longer than wings explain. I move with the leading edge, or I believe I do. Then the sheet shatters into knots and membranes, and none of the directions includes the place I was occupying. I drop out of the volume the way a wrong grit drops out of a crust—not refused, simply never bound. The keen resumes a hand’s-width higher, in a key that has already forgotten me. I do not have the tick at the top of a downstroke. I have only the falling.

The shelves take the fall. The hide is a continuous ticking, pale plates, dark seams, rasps aimed down. Courtesy happens to me before I can practice it. A flank shifts; I am displaced a body-length; I displace the next. For a while that seems to be the whole life: a map made of being slightly in the way. I lower what I have of a mouth toward the manganese and find the film already crosshatched, already being renewed by mouths that do not need mine. I stall anyway. Rasp lifted. Beads, if I have them, unfocused on the lid. The stall is supposed to die at the length of a neighbor. It does not. No knock comes to correct it. The pause lengthens until it is no longer an error but a small island of unlicked stain, and the island begins, very quietly, to crust. That is the first lesson with a bite in it. Here, stopping is not a private interior. Stopping is a vote for stone, and stone wins if the licking does not resume.

A foot sets down at the edge of the island. Low, many-legged, the color of wet slate, heavier than the hide’s law. I climb it because the hide climbs, and the climbing is not a decision so much as the old displacement continuing upward. Under the belly the ticking changes pitch. I am carried past a fork I do not choose. Grains click against a collar that is not mine. Then the footing fails in the way it fails for anything small, and I drop.

The drop is the place.

A side-seep. Worse stone. The great hum arrives here thinned, as if it had to come around a corner of mineral to find the groove. Water threads in a shy line and loses itself under the bodies that have already closed over it. Half-collars lie where their animals are not—grit-rings, bridges, little failed geologies. The clickers use them and write them and do not mourn them. I lie against one ring long enough to hear a walker pass in the main channel and answer itself off the far wall, two notes that refuse to harmonize. I am too large now to be only a seam in the hide, and too unfinished to be the hum. The plates along my back are still visible. The joints are soft. At the neck something weeps, clear, sticky, a habit I did not intend and cannot call back. A few grains find it and stay. Not a collar. A rumor of one. If I live, the rumor will thicken into a saddle and the true hide will disappear under a history of meetings. If I do not, the ring beside me is the shape of the sentence already written.

I walk because the seep walks me. Front legs test. The middle ones try to carry a weight that is still mostly my own. The rear shove, and the shove is almost embarrassment: so little grit, so much instruction in the legs. Antennae thick as wet twine write short arcs in a chemistry that answers without comfort. Another of my kind passed here, recently enough that the air still holds the flank. A pool somewhere has gone still too long; the wrongness is sharp and not mine to correct. The fork at the end of the seep is shallowing. Silt wants it. The heavier bodies do not turn here anymore; their grooves are polished elsewhere, inherited, deepened by bellies that know the long route. This fork is kept only if something lighter bothers to keep it. I bother, not from duty—the basin does not owe that word—but because my footing fails in the main lane and succeeds, barely, in the worse one. Habit will call it choice if enough of us repeat it. Tonight there are not enough of us. There is the fork, and the stickiness at my neck, and the silt arriving grain by grain like a slow lid.

In the widest part of the seep an older one stands, crust grown into a little geology, pale veins in the collar where other flanks have been remembered by the body and by nothing else. We do not fight. Fighting spends, and the basin spends us other ways: cold, failed lenses, collars that become heavier than the legs. We knock. Collar to almost-no-collar. The sound is soft, stone on a thing that is not yet stone. The measurement is obvious and does not need to be fair. I am lighter. I should climb, scrabble across a geology that is not my own, drop on the far side, continue. That is the law in the narrow grooves, and the law does not soften because this place is wide.

I yield before the second knock. Too soon. The older one has not finished reading me, and already I am off the inside line, legs finding the bad edge where the seep rises into unpolished flake. The early yield is not virtue. It is the stall returning in a larger body—the private error, the pause that crusts if no one corrects it. The older one pauses anyway. Antennae barely move. Its secretion increases, as if the meeting had a duration owed to it regardless of my bad timing. We stand flank to flank. The hide closes over our legs and treats us as new stone. Clickers rasp at the film on my plates and at the salts on the older crust with the same courtesy. I feel a grain of the other catch in what little stickiness I have. It will not become a vein anyone could read. A rain, when a rain bothers to cross this shelf, will take it. Still it is in me now, a pale wrongness in the clear weep, a meeting I will not be able to return to because return is not a motion this body owns.

What I know is local and already tightening into anatomy. Do not stop in a lane when the lane is opening. Follow a ripple until it dies. The film is the work. Knock, then be lighter, then go. I am bad at the last of these in the order the channels prefer. I go before the knock is complete, and then I stay too long where the staying is allowed. The side-seep permits the long flank. The main groove would not. So this is where I am: not the trough’s sentence, not the sheet, not the thirty-stroke sequence and its clean stop, but the thinner place the sentence leaks into when a body is too new to hold a note broad enough to narrow the air. The great ones pass in the mist as moving absences. One of them yields out there—I hear the hum cut, the briefest true silence, the ticking continuing underneath as if silence were a story the small life had never agreed to tell—and then the note resumes lower, spent. I do not yield that way. I have no groove to give. I have a fork that is silting, and a neck that has begun, without my permission, to keep sand.

When the older one parts, a little of my almost-nothing is caught in its crust, a speck among veins laid down by heavier meetings. It will not know me again. Knowing is not required. The speck will ride the long route until the collar sheds or the legs fail, and the channels will not mention it. I remain in the seep while my legs remember how to be under me. The secretion weeps at the center and hardens at the edge, the first rim of a habit. A flier, late or refusing or simply open, sits on the wet shelf with its membrane spread like a scrap of leaf in a country that has never grown one. It does not regard me. I do not regard it. The pool below us claps once, unanswered, and the margin settles back into feeding with the feeding taken out.

I test the fork again. The inside line is worse than it was. If I walk it, the walk is a scrape, not a polish. Generations would have to scrape before the basin agreed to keep the turn. I am not a generation. I am the shove of the rear legs and a few grains and the chemical news of a flank already going far. Still I set the front pairs in the shallowing groove, because the alternative is the main lane, and in the main lane I would be climbed over before I had finished arriving, a light knock, a measurement, a continuation that does not include the inside line.

Behind me the hide writes its crosshatch across the place I stood with the older one. The island of stall I made on the shelf will already be licked back into work, or it will be crust, and either way the shelf will look like itself. Ahead, the seep’s thread of water finds a lower lens and is lost under a margin that lifts, falls, offers a syllable no one completes. I answer it badly, with the only sound I have that is not ticking: the small click of new grit against flake, a knock with no partner in it. The air does not hold the shape. The milk does not drift toward me. That is fine. Drift is for edges thinner than I am.

The lid thins a little, not dawn, only a paler gray that still casts no shadow. Sheets knit and unknit overhead. I do not look up for long. Up is where I was unaddressable. Here the footing is specific, slick where the rock has been ground to flour, gritty where a fresh chip has not yet been walked flat. My collar, if it can be called that, ticks once against the wall as I turn. The sound is smaller than the walkers’ drum and larger than the seeds-in-a-tray of the hide. It is the first noise that is mine without being only mine—the grain’s, the secretion’s, the turn’s. I will thicken around it if the cold does not take the soft joints first. I will be climbed over correctly, in the right order, in some later meeting, and I will not stop to call that fairness. The fork will silt or it will not. I scrape another length of it, yielding early to nothing, because nothing heavier is here, and the early yield has to go somewhere, and the somewhere is this bad edge, this unfinished crust, this route no belly has inherited yet.

The hum of a great one swells, occupies the air, passes without entering the seep. In the wake of it the small silence tries to happen and fails, filled at once by ticking, by a far clap, by the shy thread. I keep the fork. The grains at my neck harden at their rims. Somewhere in a collar already old, a speck of this meeting rides a body that will not pause for it. Between us the basin continues, unworn in this one place, and I am the wearing.
