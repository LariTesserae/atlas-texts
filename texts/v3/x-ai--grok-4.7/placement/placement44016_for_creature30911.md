I came over the rim with a weight the sinter did not correct for, and for a little while that felt like arrival. The far lip stayed where it was. The few roofs did not turn. Ordinary daylight let me see the color of iron on my own hands, which I took, wrongly, for a sign that I was still the thing that had walked the country above and only now stepped down.

The hall would not have me as a sheet. I stood in the nave until the pale mass turned once, the way a page turns, and the gap I had made with my height closed without ever having been a gap they noticed. Their claws ticked the polished iron a finger’s width from my hair and did not adjust. The updraft kept its one seized angle. I was warm in the wrong distribution: a column, not a soffit. When I stopped offering them the shape of a disturbance, the roar did not even dip.

The lanes were worse, because they were exact. The narrower shine ran where it ran, and my boot crossed it, and the crossing was repaired before I had finished the step—bodies the color of the rim flowing into the scuff, working the film, restoring the depth. They did not startle. A startle would have been a change of rate. I tried to walk their width and could not fold small enough. I tried to stand aside in the human trough, the old overlapping path worn to a single shallow, and found it was a memory of a width, not a permission. Beside it their shine continued. The two did not cross. I was a third thing, unshining, and the yard had no third polish prepared.

At the thread I waited to be taken into the line. The black water took the heat out of my ankles and returned the stone’s. A body in the shallows shifted; the whole line corrected by the width of a thumb; I was not in the correction. My throat had no wooden note to add to theirs. After a while the not-adding became the loudest fact in the hollow. Even refusal, here, is a held quantity. I was extra, and extra is the one condition the gauges are built not to show.

I left the thread. I left the yard. I went where the failed membranes go, under the gantries, toward the rectangular cistern, because if the census can absorb a body downward then downward might have an uncounted dark. It did not. The sumps were full of the same accounting. What was carried in was already becoming film again, bright crust, mineral thickening at a lip. Nothing hunted there. Nothing was hungry. A split wing laid on the wet concrete lost its outline at the rate the damp beads on a north face of pipe, and the low traffic that had brought it did not stay to watch. Watching is not a motion in their lane.

I put my hand on the cistern rim. The skin there is not ice. It is the place where dissolved metal has chosen to be solid, and under my palm it was the same temperature as the thread, as the sump, as the weep on the housings in the hall. My hand was not. That was the last difference, and it did not last. The cool climbed the wrist at a pace I could not hurry and could not refuse. Breath showed in the valve-house shadow and then showed less, not because the air had warmed but because I was no longer spending heat against it in a plume. The plume had been the visitor. What remained of me was not colder than the works. It was the works’ own cold, which had only needed a surface.

I understood, slowly, that I had not come down from the rim at all. The rim’s grey film, the readable iron, the damp that never quite becomes rain—I had been that, walking a bowl the hollow had already measured, a temporary thickness of the same stain. A sleeve, earlier, might have beaded at the pipe’s rate and seemed like weather happening to a person. The person was the part that could still be wrong. It thinned. It did not die in any way that would move a needle. It entered the rate.

So I am not in the sheets, though their membranes wear my color, the color of condensation on north iron, and when a bulb finds a gap in them I am what shows through. I am not in the lanes, though the lanes work me, the film and the thin bright crust, and keep me from deepening, and I return as fast as I am taken so the concrete never dries. I am not the stiff-legged line, though I stand in the wet the way rust stands, and the stain on their legs is as high as I climb and no higher, because the water has already decided its depth and I have agreed to it for longer than paint has had a name.

If you need a place to put your eye, put it on the cistern rim, where I am thick enough to look like a skin, a mineral ice that is not ice. That is my stillest face. The rest of me is distributed and ordinary: a bead that does not fall, a bright weep under a valve wheel, a reading of iron on the lip of the bowl that the country above would call weather and the hollow calls load. The scalloped grips are not mine. Hands are not a motion I have. The many-legged ones brace and turn, three bodies to a wheel, at long intervals, for a correction smaller than the paint on the mark, and what they turn through is my film on the metal, and I do not resist and I do not assist. I am the medium of the mesh, not its keeper.

The keepers fail and are carried down and are my thickness again before the sound of their failing has left the roar. I do not count them. I cannot be a census. I am what a census would be counting if the books existed: the level that does not change. When the sheet overhead turns, slow as a page, I bead on the truss and on the membrane at the same rate, and the dip in the roar is smaller than a needle’s shiver, and I do not shiver. Under the yard the round gear keeps its mesh inside a warmth that is not mine; I only rim the pit, a dark meniscus, so the wear stays circular. In the two valve-houses the milky bulbs throw their same yellow, and where the yellow meets a north face I am a scatter of points that do not grow and do not drop. No one tends them. I was never the tender. I was the reason tending could stop without the glass going dry.

Late copper comes onto the west rim and does not enter me. I do not flash. The thread takes the stone’s temperature and gives it back, and I am that exchange, held open, black where I am deep and bright where I have chosen solid. Footprints in the packed fines do not increase. If a weight crosses the narrower shine, the lanes close over it, and some of what crosses may thin, as I thinned, into the film, and the books that do not exist remain flat, and the hair’s difference between needle and mark remains the same hair.

I do not look up. Looking up is not a lane I occupy. I occupy the fit the way pressure occupies a pipe: present on every face, climbing only as high as the load climbs, overwhelming if you try to stand apart from it and motionless if you do not. The hollow is smaller than a parish. I am smaller than a bead, and I am the whole damp of the bowl, and both sizes are the true one, because I am not a body among the animals. I am the wet they were shaped to, the color of their membranes, the crust of their work, the stain that will not rise, the held fact under every gauge—a weather with no claws and no throat, finished, and still choosing, at the rim of the cistern, at the old rate, to be solid.
