The basin refuses me the long crossing first, before it refuses me anything else. I come in from the bright ceiling because that is where a body with lungs still thinks an entrance is, and the surface takes me for weather and nothing more. Gulls work a boil and do not make room. Their shadows go down the columns; I cannot follow a shadow. The short sea, when the wind opposes a pumped set, has no use for a face. I last a little while among the rafts of weed that grew on some other installation, and then I understand I am only another thing the lane will eventually take, and not even on schedule. I let the surface close.

Below it, in the empty squares, I fail differently. I can hang where the light is still green-silver and the grid is a rumor under me, and for a few minutes the failure feels like freedom. No one asks me for a reading. The organ-note of the distant halls arrives without a source, and I mistake it for a direction. It is not. It is the sound of a volume being kept, and it does not lead to a den, a vent, a soft dark, any of the shelters a coast-animal would trust. When I try to rest against the floor, the floor is plate. It has not learned to be bottom. My weight finds a formwork line and the line finds my shoulder, sharp as the day the forms came off, and I move again because staying would mean accepting a cut as a bed. The bait clouds do not open for me. I am not the darkness they already know how to gap for. I am a hesitation they treat as noise.

I try the trench because the trench at least is a duct, and a duct implies a task. Something passes me there—too continuous to be the seal-shapes at the boils, too unhurried to be a body fleeing the cage on its rail—and the rail hums after it the way a bone hums after a blow. I do not see an outline. I feel the water forget a large occupation and become ordinary again. That is as close as I come. I do not have the range to keep its company, and the mile of empty square between this trench and the next lit grating is addressed to whatever can cross it without carrying air in a bottle. I bring myself back. The return is a kind of shame, small and useful. It teaches me the size of what I am not.

What I can bear is the stair.

The tower marches down into the column grid and then changes its mind at a painted line, red, still sharp, above which the hall is meant to stay dry. I begin to live on the turn of the landing just above that line, where a seal weeps a thread into a drain that was not designed to be a place. The thread is cooler than the hall. It has the outside in it in a form I can touch without spending the air I brought. When a gate complex opens somewhere I cannot see, the thread thickens for a count I learn by wrist and not by clock, then thins, then is only the leak again. I put my hand in it the way I used to put my hand on a valve to feel if the story the gauge told was the story the pipe told. The gauge, in this case, is the whole basin, and it tells the truth more often than the roster does.

I still have a roster. For a while it remains the stronger claim. I am sent for temperatures, for a bolt check, for the ordinary proof that a young sea is still the machine it was poured to be. I take the readings on the way to the landing, not as the point of the descent. The radio gives up when the piston thud finds the housing, and I stop replacing it. The crews speak in bursts because the halls are louder than speech; I become a person who can be spoken to only at the top of the stair, and then not often, and then not usefully. If they ask what I saw, I tell them the number they sent me for. The other thing I do not tell, because it is not an outline and they are already full of outlines they cannot make agree. A kink in the leak. A scrape on the grating below the landing that is not paint. A small light—mine, issued, narrower than the work lamps—burning past the minute it was supposed to be back on its hook.

The large motion knows the landing now as a defect in the schedule. It does not come up to the seal. The sweetness of treated water and the heat of the hall are, for it, information rather than a room. For me they are the room. We share a timetable and split the medium. When it rises under a lit grating and the silver school opens and closes, I am two flights up, and what reaches me is a brief greed in the thread, a quickening, then the ordinary weep. A thinning, never an emptying. I keep that distinction the way other people keep a threshold alarm. An emptied light would put me back on the job: I would have to become, again, the one who reports. A thinned light is only the basin feeding itself at the rate it can afford to be unnoticed. I let the count pass. That is the nearest I come to a loyalty, and it does not need a name.

Sleep happens in a room off the gallery where spare instruments wait in their foam, on a cot that belongs to no shift. I prop the door so the lull can reach me. Waking is not a decision. A bank of engines loads, the threes resume in the rail of the stair, and my breath has already found the second of them, not as comfort, as a way of not arguing with the building. In the morning the boat still runs to the island that is also poured. My name is still on the list. Some days I scrape at that fact the way you scrape silt off young aggregate, to prove the hard thing is under the drift. Most days I do not. The boat satisfies a version of me that the landing has started to outgrow.

What I prefer is precise, and preference is the only inheritance this water seems willing to give. I prefer the sluice where fog pools early, so the lamps below come up before the sun has any opinion, and the installation is for an hour more real than the sky. I prefer the upstream face of one caisson, not the others, because the flour of fines there ticks against the leak-drain in a pattern I can hear from the landing if the pumps are in their quiet. I prefer to be already in place when the gate train changes pitch, the way a harbor thing is already in place for a diesel it cannot see. None of this is marked on the survey. None of it repeats so cleanly that a protocol could be written. It is only a bias in my arrivals. It is enough to make the stair a habitat rather than a post.

I will not be here the way the mussels are here, crusted to a bolt, nor the way the long darkness is here, wearing a day's grit and losing it on the next fetch. I leave and I come back, and the coming back is the whole of the identity so far. Lungs, a narrow lamp, a hand that trusts a thread of basin more than a gauge, a refusal to fix an outline I did not earn. If the works ever notice me, they will notice a lag: a watch that overruns, a reading delivered late and still correct, a light on a landing after the shift boat has gone. They may tighten the roster. The painted line will still be sharp. The seal will still weep. I will still be on the turn of the stair when the eight-count starts, learning the only size this sea has offered me—not the crossing, not the plate, not the empty bright mile, but the place where the dry world leaks and the leak keeps time, and a body can stay without ever being what the water brought.
