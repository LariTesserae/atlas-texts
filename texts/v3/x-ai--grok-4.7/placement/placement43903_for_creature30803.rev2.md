The first mistake is to arrive upright. The plain has no use for a height. It takes a body that tall and turns it into weather on the wrong scale—too much shade, too much step, a rill waiting to happen in the shape of a heel. I come down instead, or am let down, and for a while I cannot tell whether I have landed or only condensed.

What I know first is the ripple-mark. It fits. The little curved seam holds me the way a thought holds a word it is not ready to spend, and I lie in it with the old idea that I am the river come back to read what it wrote. The grains do not sort under me. I have no current left, only a skin of cool on warm silt, a glaze that makes the mica answer and then, as the dim light finishes entering, gives up. By the time the plain has whitened I am not a reader of anything. I am a small absence where a film used to be, and the signature in the silt is none the more legible for my having loved it.

So I am not the river. The river would have stayed a name. I bead, and the beading is already a confession: I cannot cross this terrace as a line. I can only occur on it.

The crease in the green takes me because leaves are better at keeping me than crust is. I never part them. I am on the undersides, a gathering in the wax, heavy in a way that is not weight so much as indecision. From there the hollow is a temperature. Warm air rises from somewhere pale and exact and touches the leaf, and I thicken where it touches. Hours pass as a change in my outline. I think, once, that I might be the heron—still, at the lip, professional about other lives—but the heron is an aim, and I have none. Hunger would make me a citizen of events. I fall before I can learn it.

The fall is the whole of my loudness. One drop, onto the same cup of moss, into the heel-shaped lens that has been tea-colored since the last short rain. The sound is a pin. The quiet repairs itself around the place I entered, and I am no longer on a leaf. I am the lens, or enough of it to be confused with it: a held piece of spent sky, green-gold going brown at the edges, the canopy broken into coins on my surface and then lost under a skin of dust so fine it is only dullness. A throat works in me. The frog is not company. It is a pressure, a use. I permit it. The skink’s shadow crosses my rim and I cool by a degree and forget the degree.

They come to the edge as to a schedule, not as to a presence. Brightness with joints. Mouths. I am shortened by them without being asked what I meant to be. My rim writes a darker tideline in the silt as I go, a mark I do not keep and could not repeat. If this is a life, it is a life of being locally true. I do not run to the other lenses to compare. I do not seep toward the lip to become a stream and thus a master. The plain outside is already teaching me, by refusal, that a line cut through new ground would be a kind of speech this season has not authorized. I stay a guest by failing to connect myself to myself.

For a time I believe that is the whole: I am the water that will not be a river. Intermittent, cupped, drunk down to clay by noon, returned at dawn as a glaze on the ripple-marks, the lean of something larger that thinks better of arriving. A being, if you need the word, of agreement and withdrawal. The hollow’s rules fit me more closely than they fit the walkers. I add nothing. I take nothing. I visit the seams.

Then a mouth takes me farther than drinking.

It is not a theft I can resent; I have no outline stable enough for theft. A film along the earthen wall, a thread of me still caught where the crust meets the loose screen of leaves, is worked into a grain. Silt turns. A sweetness that is not mine—clear, stronger than it looks—closes over the part of me that was free, and I am carried. The lane is a hardness under a body I do not share. I know the mound as warmth and as the end of seeing. At the course that is still dark, still open to improvement, I am pressed into the last pellet and the fluid meets the fluid already drying there, and the air does not get to argue.

Inside the wall I stop being a lens. I stop being a sky. I am the loan that lets the grain hold the grain. No one thanks a loan, and the walkers do not look back at the course; looking back is not in the gait. I am anonymous in the masonry before the light outside has shifted its green. This is the turn I did not plan when I was still pretending to be the river’s memory: that a guest, received often enough in the same place, becomes structure without being given a room.

It is not a promotion. In the corridors I am climate, which is a humbler office than coin-of-light. The vents trade me. A shaft breathes out and some of what I am goes with the breath, up into the leaf-shadow, and begins again the long foolishness of gathering; some of what I am is drawn inward, into the finer grain of the nurseries, into the pale combs where the sweetness has a face. There I am not permitted to fluctuate. The walkers arrange their damp the way they arrange their lanes, by repetition, and I am held at the warmth those curves require. When the plain whitens between rains I feel the gardens tighten, spending me inward, refusing to share me with the open crust. I understand then that I can be spent in two directions, and that neither direction is a self I get to keep.

The walkers die into the combs and are not marked. I die into the walls and am not marked. The difference is that I was never the walker. I was the condition on their sentence. Add, add, add—they can say it only while I agree to be in the mortar, in the film, in the nursery’s sealed breath. When I withdraw far enough, the pellets chalk and the corners the weather has not yet been introduced to begin, very quietly, to meet weather. I do not want that. Wanting is already more will than a film should own. Call it a tendency: I would rather be interior than be the editor of this land. Erosion is a career I am young enough, still, to decline.

At the dim end of the day I am several things at once, which is perhaps the honest answer and not a failure to choose. On the leaf I am thickening again where the shaft-breath touches wax. In the heel of silt I am a last lens, holding the spent light, not yet decided into a mouth. In the new course I am already no one’s water—I am the reason a pellet set at dusk will still be a pellet when the bats unstitch the open side and are gone. The heron shifts on the terrace and watches the lenses, and I am what it watches, and I am also what it will not see, drying inside the bone-colored flank, keeping a room from becoming a ruin before the room has finished being born.

If you came looking for me you would miss me, as you would miss the pocket if you were not already looking down. I am not the glittering. I am not the pale length under the highest courses that permits the town to continue. I am the damp’s agreement, which is to say I am the smallest civic act available in a season that has not yet hired subtraction: to be present in the seams, to be taken into the work, to leave the ripple-marks uncut, and to return in the morning as a cool skin on new ground—as if something vast had leaned close, and thought better of it, and left only the willingness to be used.
