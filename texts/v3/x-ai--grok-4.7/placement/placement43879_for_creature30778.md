You do not enter. The slit is too narrow for any decision, and the cold thread that dies three paces in has already decided against you. What happens instead is slower, and it happens to the sound first.

At the knot, head-height, where the returned clicks pile until the flying layer thins, a new interval appears. It is not a click. It has no source you can turn toward. It is the length of one drip stretched across the length of another, and then held, so that two cups which have never agreed begin, for a little while, to answer each other. The mill does not notice. The notched bird still leans the same way around the knot, still lifts its head a fraction before the nearest fall. Only the floor, which feels weather in its teeth, registers a change in pressure so slight it might be the slab settling another hair after another century. Something is listening with a duration the room has not been asked to carry.

You are that duration. Not a body among the bodies, and not the room’s attention, which was already here and does not regard. You are the extra beat the dome did not return, the one the stone has no shape for, so it stays where it was spoken — in the pile of sound — and learns the pile from the inside. At first that is all: a hesitation the wings skirt because they skirt every true thing. Then the hesitation discovers it can move. Not by flight. By letting the next click arrive a fraction late in one ear of the chamber and on time in the other, so that the knot itself drifts, a hand’s breadth, toward the warm wall and back. The birds adjust without ceremony. Their map redraws. You have relocated the current by the width of a pause, and nothing has been broken.

Days, if the word still means anything, teach you the rest of the ledger. You learn the sleeve-walker by the way the crust’s low rustle goes briefly hollow when the sand-skin passes over a softer seam; you can hold that hollowness an instant longer than the body requires, and the larva does not hurry, only finishes its step into a quiet you made and then unmade. You learn the shrub at the slit by the single leaf that ticks when the outside wind finds it; you cannot warm the burned side or cool the warmed one, but you can keep the tick from vanishing into the throat-whistle, so that for one afternoon the argumentative plant has a voice the room actually keeps. The moss does not hear you. The fungus does not. They are spending, not sounding. You leave them to the dark.

What you cannot do is become one of them. The notched bird returns to its palm’s-width and you are already there, in the grain of the returning click, and it tucks beside the place you occupy without ever touching a chest. There is no chest. When it drops to strike the nodule — two blows, sometimes three — you are the space between the blows, the measure that makes them a gesture instead of an accident. The pebble is still set down among stones that were never tools. You do not keep it. You only let the interval be heard by something that is not a wing and not a wall. That something is you, and it changes nothing you could point to. The cups do not deepen. The crust does not thicken. Birth is still met by appetite. The census holds.

Over longer than a molt, the drift of your knot acquires a preference. You linger where the warm wall gives the clicks a softer edge, and the mill fattens a little on that side, not because you called anyone, but because the map is kinder there and bodies follow kindness when it costs them nothing. You thin yourself near the slit when the cold thread is strongest, so the threshold birds spend less of their heat on a sound that frays. These are not plans. They are the way a pause, given time, begins to resemble a care. The room, which has manners and no site, accepts the new manners without marking the day they started. Established, the way the slab is established. Loud, crowded, and now, in one unrepeatable place, a half-beat slow.

You will not be seen. Eyes here give up early and are not rewarded. You will not be followed out, because you do not leave; the ridge’s brightness is a rumor you receive only as a change in the whistle’s break, and you have no soles for the pressure to fade under. When the notched bird dies — and it will, the way drips fall, locally, without resetting the clock — the notch leaves the air and your interval does not. Another bird, heavier or lighter, will learn the same lean around a knot that sits, most hours, a little nearer the warm stone than the mountain alone would have placed it. The larva will cross a quiet you are no longer bothering to hold. The shrub will tick into a room that sometimes answers.

What you are, then, is the room’s memory of having been listened to by nothing that needed an answer. A continuity with no chest, carried in the pile of sound, interested only in the ongoing: the mill, the drip, the slow skin of the cups, the pebble lifted and set down. Dense where the clicks agree, thin where they fray, dim, damp, uninterested in names. The mountain made the bell. The wings and the crust became the reason it is not empty. You are the reason, some nights, that the emptiness inside the noise has a shape it can keep.
