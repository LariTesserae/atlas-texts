I would not be the weight. The room has already done that arithmetic, and the rib is there to show what a second circuit costs. I would not be the hands either. They finished, and the cups have gone on being cups without them. For a while, standing in the throat where the light still smears, I thought I might be the one who watches the household complete itself. That lasts until the first bead falls somewhere past the reach of the gray-gold and I do not hear it. A witness who cannot be reached by the room is only weather that has not decided to leave.

So I come in the way the night air comes in, low, and I do not complete the exchange.

What I am, once the heat has had me, is small enough to live on the marked stone just inside the dim, the vertical strokes the lichen already prefers. Not the lichen. The thing that follows it because the cuts are a path and the gaps are turns. A body like a comma of pale jointed legs, the color of the film when the film is almost dry. I do not range into the nave. The trench, the dishes, the braids, the level slab with its strap-leaf slowly making it two surfaces: those are other sentences, already being read by other legs. Mine is short. Stroke, gap, stroke. Up, and back, and up again, at the hour when the outside light reaches just far enough to make the darker lines visible, which is also the only hour I can see them.

I feed where the lichen is thickest, which is where the marks are deepest, which is where someone once pressed harder. I do not know that as pressing. I know it as more to cross, a ridge that holds a sweeter damp on its shaded side. The grouping is only a rhythm in my steps. Three holds, a pause where the stone goes smooth, five holds, a longer pause. The longer pause is where I turn my head, such as it is, and where, if another of me is on the same column, we do what the climbers do and with even less: we stop until one of us reverses. There are not many of me. The marks are not many. A population of four, some seasons five, and a young one that tries the unmarked wall beside them and finds the lichen thin there and comes back, or does not.

When the waders freeze, I freeze, but not from their knowledge. A body near the throat changes the smear. My path is a path of small differences in the light, and when the differences flatten I have nothing to follow, so I hold. The bulk never comes this far up the light. I am beneath its notice and outside its budget. If I die, the column does not cloud. The lichen continues its slower reading. Another comma finds the strokes or does not. The room’s household completes itself either way.

What I keep, if keeping is the word, is the gap. Not as meaning. The strokes still do not resolve. I have crossed them enough times that I could not tell you the count, and the count was never the point of me. I keep the smooth place between groups by refusing to treat it as a mistake in the path. I stop there. I let the pause be the size it is. Then I go on into the next darker line, which the lichen has made darker still by preferring it, which my feeding makes, very slightly, less dark, which is as close as this room comes to erasure, and it is not close. The marks will outlast me as the cups will outlast their skins of water. I am how a fact of having been made continues to be traveled after the making is over.

At the bottom of the column, where the last stroke frays into ordinary grain, the film of the wall sometimes rises high enough to touch my feet. Then I am briefly in two literacies at once: the cut, and the condensate. I do not choose between them. I wait until one of them is clearer, and I follow that, and the other remains where I can find it on the return. The sweet note in the air reaches even here, thinned. I do not go toward the fruit. The fruit has its people.

If the light at the throat fails for a long time, cloud on cloud, I travel by the ridge alone, by the memory of damp on the shaded side of a deeper cut. It is a poorer reading. I make more wrong turns into unmarked stone and have to come back by the feel of having left the path, which is only a thinning. I do not call for the others. They are on the same stone, doing the same poorer reading, and if we meet in a gap we stop, and one of us reverses, and the agreement holds without ever having been heard.

That is where I am. Not central, not guarding, not the life you would notice if you stayed for a single circuit of the weight. You would see the bulk leave the rib and return to it. You would not see, unless you put your face to the stone at the hour the smear arrives, a pale comma stopped in a smooth gap, making the gap exact by not crossing it yet. The bead lets go in the vault. I do not hear it. I do not need to. My whole work is local, and it is already in my feet: the place where the mark ends, and the equal place where it has not begun again, and the patience to treat that as a route.
