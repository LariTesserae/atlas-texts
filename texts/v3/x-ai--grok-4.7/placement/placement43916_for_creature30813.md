I come in through the throat because that is the only place the chamber is unfinished, and I come wrong. A body is an episode. I know that before my shoulders clear the narrow water and the column closes over the day behind me. The surface above is kept flat; it does not record the entrance as a splash so much as a brief insult to a decision, already being repaired by whatever patience sits upstream. By the time I am in the room, the insult is over. I am simply here, too large, cooling.

The coolness is not a welcome. It is a correction. Whatever season I carried on my skin is argued with and then dismissed, not roughly, only completely, until I am the same temperature as the volume and no longer a lens the film would have to divide itself to survive. I feel the end of that argument as the end of being news. After it, my pulse is the loudest fact in the chamber, and the rock declines to answer it. I stop kicking. Kicking would be a current, and a current would be a kind of vandalism: it would peel the only life that has negotiated a right to the stone. So I hang where the column loosens, a large mote with lungs, and try to spend as little as a mote spends.

At first I think I might be a second resident if I am quiet enough. The idea does not survive the looking. The rations are already fully assigned. The green on the ledge, the ochre that is not a stain but a lattice, the few blades that are the film standing up and then remembering itself — that is the whole permission the water grants to anything that trades and keeps a thickness. There is no surplus for a neighbor. The dark floor is not an empty lot. It is a conclusion. If I tried to belong at that density I would have to become a fingerprint-thick decision about iron and light, and I cannot; I have an interior, and an interior is a luxury this chemistry has already ruled against. The film does not watch me discover this. Attention is not one of its costs. I am a shadow crossing a ration of photons, and when I drift out of the column the work that needs light pauses, and the work that does not continues, and neither pause nor continuance is about me.

I look for a form the room will accept without being asked to revise itself.

Not the slab. Its pale underside is the record of not having been turned, and turning it so I would have a place to put a hand would be the first fresh break in a hollow that has finished breaking. Not the ledge. Not the walls, which have ended their argument with the water and will not reopen it for fingers. Not the surface above, which I can reach again if I spend the air, but which is kept flat by a will that is not mine; I can pass through it, I cannot inhabit it. The note at the edge of hearing offers itself for a moment as a kinship — something else that persists by being too even to count — but the note is the far engines seating themselves into silence, and I am not far. I am in the middle, taking up volume, slowly loading the water with the carbon I cannot help breathing out. Even my patience is an emission.

What I am, then, is the inequality, while it lasts.

A warmth already gone. A shadow already moved on. A few ions the film will not thank and will not notice, because they are not enough to change the accounting, only enough to have been, briefly, extra. I can feel the role settle, and it is smaller than a name. The chamber does not need a witness. It has already become the form it keeps. Witnessing is my need, imported down the throat, and the water treats it the way it treats heat: distributes it until it is no longer local, and then there is nothing to point to. If I stay past the air I brought, the inequality ends in the ordinary way of animals here, which is that it is not allowed to become a story. No one feeds. Nothing with a soft body is supported long enough to finish decaying into a lesson. The works would clear even that, I suspect, not from tidiness, but because tidiness is what their patience looks like from the inside.

So I will not stay past the air. That refusal is the last thing about me that still resembles a person. I turn, slowly, toward the pale column, toward the one failure in the ceiling, and I understand the turn as a courtesy the film will not register and the rock does not require. Courtesy is another imported episode. I do it anyway, because I am still, for these minutes, the kind of thing that has minutes.

On the way up I pass through my own earlier warmth, long since erased, and through the place where my shadow paused the green. The green has resumed. The thickness is the thickness it negotiated before I was a possibility. I am already the same as not having come, plus a remainder too small to see: a little metal from a buckle, a little salt, a carbon the pumps will spend somewhere else without naming the source. That remainder is the only citizen I am permitted to be. It will not cohere. It will not prefer the coolness or the iron or the stillness, because preference, down here, has already been spent by the film, and what is left for me is to be soluble.

In the throat, for a second, I am neither the chamber nor the weather. The day is a few hand-spans above, ordinary, cooled, not shimmering. I could stop in that short column and pretend to be the contact between the two — the last water that touches air, the narrow refusal of wind — but that is a romance, and the surface is kept by engines, not by anyone hovering in it with lungs. I go through. The flat closes. Below, the note continues, too even to be called a sound by anything that stays. The film keeps its fingerprint depth on the ledge, the slab, the broken band of wall. The room returns to the composition I interrupted, which did not experience the interruption as harm, only as a small weather that equalized.

Above the grade, in the light that is only light again, I am a person who has been told no in a language of temperatures and thicknesses. I carry the no out with me. It does not make me the inhabitant. It makes me the part of the world that still moves by episodes, standing over a hollow that has declined episodes, carrying in my blood a trace of water that will not support what I am, already warming back toward the season, already forgetting the exact grey-green, the way a correction forgets the error once the balance is restored.
