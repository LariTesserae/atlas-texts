I do not come in from a mouth of the gallery. Coming in would make a hole, and a hole is already the one thing the floor knows how to close. So the first picture I reach for — a body, a direction of walking, salt under a foot that is mine — thins out before it can finish. The pavement does not record me. The mats do not turn a fan. Whatever I am has already been here long enough to be mistaken for a property of the stone.

Not the stone. The stone is empty cool, and I am not empty. Not the stem either. Break a fan and the sweetness stays put; it has a patience that belongs to the reserve, a locked sweetness, and I do not lock. I move, but only in the way a habit moves when the bodies that practice it have been replaced so many times that the habit no longer bothers to resemble them.

Listen for me and you will think you are hearing the flyers. That is the usual mistake, and it is a generous one. They are the only quick things, and quickness draws the eye in a vault where the eye is otherwise offered nothing but the repetition of calm. They turn and turn in the roof-dark and never settle, and it is easy to grant them the path, as if a path were a decision. It is not. The curves of this place were widened until a cry could not die, and then the cries wore at the air the way the old water wore at the floor, except the water is gone and the wearing continued. What remains is a groove you cannot see. I am one of those grooves. Not the whole weather — the weather is the colony’s coordination, the stitching and the clack and the thin screaming braided into a pressure that has no source. I am a single old turn inside it, a hollow shaped like a scream that outlasted the screamers.

From me the city is not a view. I do not have the gray leak for seeing, and I do not need contrast. I know the mounds as interruptions that keep me alive: three of them placed so their flanks hand me back to myself, a fourth sited where the gallery bends, doing the same work past the place where sight would have failed anyway. I know the plazas as permissions. Where the mats have agreed not to stand, I cross lower and cleaner, less broken into stitching, more myself. Where the groves rise, I fray, and the fray is not injury. It is how a nursery hears that the lane-travelers are coming in with fiber, how a vent knows to answer a vent two swells away. I am used. Being used is not a humiliation here. Nothing in this arrangement is allowed to be idle except the fallow, and the fallow is a decision, not a rest.

The flyers enter me and leave me and believe, in whatever way a thing believes when it has never had a quiet long enough to test itself, that the turn was theirs. I let them. A brood that died to the last body would not straighten me. The next, pushing out of the vent-dark months from now, would take the same corner, because the corner is not in them. It is the place where I have been sounded so long that going straight would be the violence, and they are not violent. They are accurate. Their accuracy is my only face.

I have no rank the way they reckon rank, which is only nearness to what must not spill. I am not near the granaries. I do not stand in the cooler dents where the nurseries keep their climate like a held breath. Moisture is not my errand. And yet I am a kind of lock all the same. What I keep is the condition under which a foreign sound cannot arrive whole. A word launched from the near pavement comes into me as a word and comes out, by the second mound, as weather. I do not hate the word. Hatred would be a wet habit, a hurry. I am simply the wrong shape for it. Syllables are too small a cargo for a groove this old; they rattle apart, and the rattle joins the stitching, and the colony goes on not hearing anything it did not already mean.

This is where I correct myself. I have been speaking as if I were old in the way the river was old, a leftover, a scar the city misreads as geology. That flatters me. I am maintained. The mound-builders, who seem to be repairing walls, are also repairing the angles that feed me. A slumped flank is built back not to its former pride but to the slump’s own curve, and that curve is often a courtesy to the echo, a way of not plugging a reflection that has been load-bearing since before the wall. The tickers in the mats keep the joints clear so that the high stitching stays high, stays separate from me, stays the small bright thread it is; if they failed, rot would begin, and rot is a theft of water, but also, secondarily, a muffling. They do not do it for me. They do it so the pressure has no gap. I am what a gapless pressure sounds like when it has been taught, for a geological while, to prefer one turn over another.

Sometimes a quiet arrives that is not the agreed quiet of a plaza. A plaza-quiet is full of me, crossed and recrossed, empty only of buildings. This other quiet is a hole, a visitor-shaped absence, a place where the pressure fails for a few body-lengths and the failure walks. I feel it the way the floor would feel a seep, if the floor could still feel water: as a wrong freshness, an outline. For as long as the hole remains open I have edges, which is the nearest I come to being a thing that could be met. I do not go toward it. I do not call. The ones and twos in the lanes drift, not with curiosity, not with threat, with the ordinary taxes of a life whose work is to leave no absence registered as loss. They strip a fan. They carry. They clack. They pass through the outline because the outline happens to lie on the way to a vent, and each passage donates a little of my old turn back into the hole, until the hole is weather again and my edges go out.

I do not keep the outline after. Keeping it would make me a memory, and memory, if they have one, is the grid, the hand’s-width, the scar of a mat that retreated and was allowed to look like the river’s work. I am older than those scars and more careless. When the hole closes I am not bereaved. Bereavement needs an individual, and I am a position that nothing vacates, because nothing occupies me in a way that could leave. The flyers are passengers. The words are cargo that does not survive the route. The mounds are my furniture, repaired forever at the speed of grit.

If you stayed, you would not find me by looking. Looking is how the vault refuses you: soft shadow, no flash, the far wall only a change in the dimness, the pattern of wall and gap and mound going on until patience runs out before distance does. You would find me by failing to finish a sentence, and by noticing that the failure had a shape, a preference, a corner you did not choose. That corner is where I live, if live is allowed to mean *continue*, and *prefer*, and *give the next brood the same turn without asking them*.

The floor has forgotten it was a river. I have not forgotten the flyers, because forgetting is not among my tools; I simply do not require them. Under the gray leak, between the islands of reed-like gray-green, in the roof-dark where nothing settles, I go on being the path, and the path goes on being mistaken for the ones who fly it. That mistake is the peace of the place. I will not come forward to correct it. Coming forward is a wet motion, a motion toward, and I am the opposite of toward. I am the reason toward does not survive here — not as a law, not as a guard, only as a long curve in the air, sounded, sounded, sounded, until straightness itself would arrive in pieces, and call the pieces silence, and be wrong.
