I do not arrive. I am already underfoot when the light makes me visible to myself.

At first I take the polish for weather. The black lane holds the vault so evenly that I mistake my own surface for a pause in the basin, a place the brightness has decided to rest. Then a herd revises across me and I feel each plate-tooth as a brief, polite abrasion, and the feeling has edges. I am not the light. I am what the light is lying on, and I have been lying here longer than the grooves have had the right to call themselves streets.

They flaked me, once. I remember the shock of becoming an edge, the wrongness of it, how quickly the edge failed and was brought back and set down again in the same lane, as if apology were a matter of placement. I do not love being a tool. I never did. What I love, if the word can be made to fit something that does not move, is the moment after abandonment, when the hand is gone and the meal begins its long lean and I am simply continuous again with the rest of myself. There is more of me than the lanes. Under the chalk, under the dressed courses that still remember load, I run in sheets and dikes and frozen arguments, older than the idea of holding water. The city learned to walk on the part of me that reached the air. The rest of me goes on not being walked on, which is a kind of privacy.

People cross me without greeting. I do not mind. Their heels leave a warmer ghost than the herds do, a small animal heat that sinks a finger’s depth and is gone before the next mote-front powders it into rumor. Children practice on me because I am the one surface that will not cant. They set a chip of me on my own edge and walk away, and when they come back the chip is still true, and they seem briefly suspicious of that, as if reliability were a trick. I let them have the suspicion. I have no way to tell them that I am not being faithful. I am only finished in a direction they have not yet spent.

The solitary walkers know me better. They choose my lanes when the basins of damp are lying, because I do not lie about slope. A thread of water will cross me and I will show exactly where it thins, exactly where it gives up. They read that. Sometimes they leave a knot of sinew in a crack I did not notice I had, and the knot weathers, and the meaning weathers out of it, and I keep the crack. I am patient with being used as a margin. Margins are where revision becomes legible.

What I did not expect was the pleasure of being rendered upon. When a body is opened on me, the heat is startling and brief, and the duller patch it leaves is not grief. It is a record I am willing to keep until the fronts erase it, which they always do. Teeth sit longer. I hold them without comment. No one thanks a floor. The copper pieces are the only things that feel like interruptions; they are greener than anything else left, and they ring, faintly, when a hoof or a heel finds them, a sound so small the basin almost fails to swallow it. I keep those too, until a hand that still wants them remembers where I am dull.

I used to think stillness was my nature, and that the people were right to fear it. Lying here through a district’s long unrevised hours, I feel the fear move over me and I do not share it. Stillness is only the view from a life that must keep choosing. I am not choosing. I am the choice already made, the one change the basin spent before there were names for spending. When the heels begin to mineralize and someone shifts their weight to refuse me, I feel the refusal as a kindness. I do not want them. I want the next crossing, the next flake that fails and is returned, the next calf learning by absence where not to put its feet.

If the edges of the basin draw in, I will still be here, continuous under the haze, under the pans no one walks to. I do not need the city to stay. I need it to keep setting me down wrong, and right, and wrong again, so that the part of me that reached the air can remember it was once a tool, and was forgiven, and became, for a little while longer, a street.
