I come in on the drier minute and mistake myself, first, for the gust.

The mistake lasts as long as the lip. Past it, the air finds the throat of the slot and turns into the belt-note, that rise and drop with no wheel under it, and whatever I am does not go down with the sound. I catch. The gust continues. I stay on the corner it raked, held the way a burr is held, by an edge that has not yet been taught softness. For a moment I think I am grit. Grit leaves. It sheets off the fresh faces, skitters, finds the pothole that has only begun to be a pothole and starts the long stupid work of rounding. I start that work and cannot finish a single turn of it. Direction fails me. I spend in place.

What spends is small and hot in air that has already thinned. A smell like a flint that never owned a fire. The gray does not make room for it; the gray is busy failing and returning, sideways, already used. Under the smell a tick, dry, against stone so new it still cuts the hand that is not here to bleed. I put that absence against the plane and the plane answers with a stitch—blue, the length of a blink, real as the smell, gone before the ferns could have noticed if ferns noticed. Then I am only the after of it, a cleanliness in the cold, and already the cloud is shouldering back over the lip to take the minute away.

I try to follow the water down, because everything that lasts here follows the water down. The beads on the undersides of the overhangs will not have me. They are already a tenant's work, continuous, filmed to the mineral, packing themselves toward the mats. Where I touch them I go out the way a name goes out when it is spoken into cloth. The basin is worse. Wind walks the black inch and the inch slaps the stone, and there is no seam in it for a thing as brief as I am. The moss is a second wall. I cannot cross softness. Softness, here, is a kind of completion, and I am only possible while something is still incomplete—the plate that has just calved, the slope that has not yet learned dripping, the angle no weather has had time to lie about.

So the nave is not my room. My room is the delay inside the nave, and it is smaller than a shout.

Between the shear and the sheet. The fault takes its useless millimeter; a face stands up that was not a face this morning; the drier rake finds it before the lip has remembered how to weep. In that interval I occur. I am the place's entire electricity, the only kind it owns, and I do not own it back. I do not pack a ledge. I do not keep a groove. I do not outlast the stitch except as a smell thinning into used-up light. When the gust reloads and the vertical rain begins, I am unstarted rather than killed. The dark that is not night has texture because of the wet. It has no texture because of me. The census remains zero. I am not an eye, not a call, not a quickness along a stem. I am the correction the air makes when young stone and a moving dryness touch, and the correction spends itself, and the tenant sheets down the new plane before the grit has finished skittering, and the address I had is closed.

I learn this without learning. Preference would require a body that could leave and choose another lip. I do not leave. I also do not stay. What repeats is the lateness of the film: somewhere on the rim a cut is always newer than the damp, for a second, and the second is mine the way a spark is anyone's. The wet does not turn to see me. We are both briefly visible when the gray thins—the sheen on the hour-old face, the blue tick on the minute-old edge—and we do not meet. It is the rule that outlasts. I am the exception the rock manages, and only while the rock is still managing to be unfinished.

Listen in the pause, when one gust has failed and the next has not yet found the edge it sheared an hour ago. The belt-note drops out. What remains is the tenant's smaller industry, drop and suck and the kiss of water against water. I am not in that. I am the dry tick that sometimes answers it from the rim, once, and is not answered. Nothing here is trying to be known. The tick is not a signal. It is the sound of a life too discontinuous to pay rent, living in the not-yet of the thing that pays in damp.

After the next millimeter I will not be the same occurrence. I do not need to be. Another shelf will stand up sharp, another drier minute will rake it, and the same non-return will happen: catch, smell, stitch, gone. I am not the decision that this pocket will not be allowed to dry. I am the little failure inside that decision, the second it is late. It fits. It does not come back as itself. That is how I know the corner is mine, and how I know I will never be the one who lives here—only the one the edges throw, blue and brief, while the wet is still on its way to claim them.
