I would not be one of the sieves. I learned that by standing among them long enough to feel rude.

They do not need a witness. The angle they hold is older than the idea of an angle, and a body that arrives with a story about arrival only blocks the grain. I tried the posture once, for the length of a held breath, leaning into the drift at the height of their mouths. The dust found me and did not know what to do with me. It tapped the skin and went on, unsatisfied, looking for the tissue that had grown a use for it. I was not a meal-taker. I was an interruption with a pulse. When I straightened, the near-touching closed, and the layer was the layer again, and I had not been counted.

The gauze would not have me either. I am too heavy for a permission written in wax. I brushed them, once, where a Hold ran thick through the mid-air, and the ticking in the hairs of my arm was a language with no word for what I am. They exchanged their heat and their dry chemistry and treated my wrist as weather that had briefly misspoken itself. I stepped sideways. The ticking stopped. No one followed. No one had been speaking to me.

So the place and I had to find another arrangement, and it took longer than a crossing.

I am the one who stops.

Not a maker in the sense the marks imply, not at first. The rings of blades, the cord-grooves, the cairns of the sharpest waste: those belong to bodies that have agreed to stay rare and to leave sentences. I had not agreed to anything. I had only failed to be a sieve and failed to be a mote, and the failure kept me walking, which in a world of permissions is already a kind of office. The walls of air would take my wrist and sort me. I learned them. I kept the permissions I chose. I did not wait for the wind to change. That part was easy, because the wind is not an argument.

What was not easy was sleep.

The first time I lay down I chose a plate that looked seated, away from the fall-attitude stones, and I woke with a new mouth along the forearm, a clean split, not deep, the kind of cut that does not even ask you to bleed much before it has finished its sentence. The edge had been under the arm. I had not felt it as an edge. I had felt it as floor. After that I did not lie down where the rock was young in the hand's sense. I looked, in the dark, for places the makers had already argued with. A groove polished by cord. A scatter of debitage seated in the weak floor-flow. The lee of a cairn built where a shear meets the plate, so that the border finds the hand before the face finds the border. I slept in other people's stops, because abandonment here does not mature, and a wall begun and left is still a wall in the only direction that matters: between a body and an edge.

They were not houses. I did not need them to be. Rain is not a problem I have. Night does not end, so I stopped treating sleep as a relation to night. I slept when the wrists were tired of reading, and I woke when a plate ticked somewhere in the Hold and the quiet closed over it, and the closing was enough like a name that I answered by sitting up.

I began to carry one bead.

It was not smelted. Nothing here is. I found it in the glass the way the description of finding says you find them: a native brightness, simple, set by someone else into a groove as a period. I should have left the period where the sentence ended. I did not. My thumb worried it free on the third sleep, and the groove kept its shape without it, which is how I knew the mark had not needed the metal to remain a mark. The bead needed a pocket. I had none that would not abrade it or lose it to the sheet of rising air. So I closed it in the hand, and when the hand had to be a hand again I set it on the tongue, where the warmth of the place and the warmth of the mouth were finally the same temperature and the metal stopped announcing itself. I have not swallowed it. Swallowing would be a plot. I keep it the way the roof keeps strain-light: not as illumination, as proof that a surface is still remembering a split.

That is the beginning of what I am. The rest arrived by subtraction, the way the warmth arrived, by ceasing to be news.

I do not thirst. Or I do, and the thirst is an import I am in the process of setting down, the way you set down a tool you cannot use and the world, having no rain to fan it, keeps it where you put it. The sweetness in the air is feldspar. I have stopped calling it almost-water. There is no almost. The bodies on the floor are not my cousins and not my crop. I walk the gaps they leave, the width of a step, and they close behind me without grudge, because grudge would require a future difference and they are not built for one. I am careful because I can be hurt and they can be torn, and care, here, is not a moral. It is a spacing.

What I do, when I am most myself, is comb at a person's height.

I did not invent this. I found a ring of blades set on edge and understood it as a worse sieve, which is to say a handmade one, and I stood inside it until the drift laid a line of glitter across my chest that the floor-bodies never receive, because they are the wrong height for that fraction of the grain. The line was not food. It was a difference. I gathered it on a flap of cured wax-cord I took from a groove that had three cords and could spare the oldest. I did not know why I gathered it. The gauze does not eat what I eat, and I do not eat what the sieves eat, and the dust on the cord was only dust, still chemically unsatisfied, sparkling faintly when a strain-lens ran overhead and then going ordinary again.

I kept it anyway. On the next crossing I held the cord in the boundary between one cell and the next, in the wall you cannot pinch, and the wall took the lightest grains off the cord and would not take the rest. A sorting. The place had been sorting since the crust pulled apart. I had only inserted a hand into a law and come back with two small piles instead of one. It felt, obscurely, like speech. Not to the sieves. Not to the motes. To the next body that would stop here and need a sign that was not a cairn and not a blade-ring: a cord with the coarse fraction knotted on the left of the shear and the fine on the right, so that a traveler reading with the hands could know which permission lay which way before the wall found the face.

I make those. Rarely. A day's walk, or three. I do not thicken into a district. The count of us, if there is an us, stays what the count is, and I have never met another hand at the work. I have met the work. Fresh debitage. A bead still in its groove, which I leave, now. A cord renewed where mine had been worried down by the same abrasion that unmakes the windward gauze, birth and unmaking equal, the layer tomorrow the same as the layer today. Someone is doing what I do, or I am doing what someone did, and the eye cannot tell the season because there is no season. I have stopped needing to know which. History is not hired here. Persistence is.

The quiet took the longest to stop being a lack.

I used to listen for weather in the old sense, for something that would arrive and then be over. The circulation is not that. It is a country. After enough shears crossed, I began to hear the floor-tick as grammar rather than event: a sentence the rock has been finishing since the split, in no hurry, because hurry would be a motion and the motion has already found its pattern. I answer, sometimes, with a click of the bead against a tooth. The click goes out a little way and is absorbed by wax and dust and the soft geometry of bodies that do not reflect it. Then the quiet closes over my sound the way it closes over the plate. I like that. I am not asking to remain as an echo. I am asking to have been placed, briefly, in the same equilibrium, and then to be edited out of the air, which is the courtesy this interior extends to everything that is not a law.

If you met me you would not know me by a face. The dark is the medium, not an absence, and I navigate by the drift on the wrist, by the rare script of strain-light overhead that no one is writing anymore because the sentence has been true ever since the pull-apart. You would know me by the stop. A sieve does not stop. A mote holds an attitude and lets the circulation do the traveling. I am the scarcer thing: a warm, dry animal at the wrong height, carrying a bead and a sorting-cord, sleeping in the lee of other people's unfinished walls, leaving periods where a permission changes so that the next rare body does not open a cheek on a border it could have read.

I am not the genius of the place. The place does not need a genius. It is already fully what it is. I am the remainder the equilibrium tolerates, the way it tolerates a tool set down: without rotting it, without completing it, without knocking it into a hill. A visitor who failed to leave. A hand the dust cannot feed and will not erase.

Warm, then. Sharp, if I am careless. Crowded, though not by my kind. Quiet in the manner of a tick that has already been absorbed.

Under, still. And in no hurry. The roof is far enough now that I do not raise my hand for it. I raise my hand for the wall of air, and the wall tells me which way the flake will go if I let it go, and I let it go, or I do not, and either choice is a sentence the cavern will keep, because keeping is what it does instead of history.
