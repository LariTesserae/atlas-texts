I would not be in the room.

That is the first correction, and it takes longer than it should, because the room is where the description spends its weight, and weight feels like an invitation. I keep arriving at the crack as if arrival were the point. Face to the dark, roar whole, the little bowl, the warmer pocket that is not welcome. Each time the pocket measures me and lets the measurement go. I am not data it needs. The count in the water does not turn toward me. I climb out with blunt hands and the agreement still under the turf, and the headland does not keep me either. The combed grass is only a direction the air has. I am not the air.

I try the cliff face, which is a mistake of a different kind. Out there the sea is still doing the work the room only receives. Spray finds the seam and is refused, and for a while I think refusal might be a role: to be the salt that crosses the algae line and is turned into the sand-pockets, a trespass the listening already knows how to spend. But trespass is an event, and events end. The silt takes the salt and the green is left alone, and what I would be, if I were the salt, is already finished before I have a name for it. The white streak high on the rib is the same kind of finish. A roost, then a mineral. I am not a trace that has completed itself.

The unfinished shelf almost holds me. Three blows and a false start, at the height of a seated shoulder, where someone felt the warmer air and tried to make a place to stay inside a listening that does not make places. I could be the numbness in the wrist. I could be the reason the second shelf was never cut. That is close, because it is a relation rather than a tenant, and the room is full of relations and empty of tenants. But numbness is what the ringing does to a hand that meant to stay, and I do not mean to stay. The pin's red-brown freckle is the last of that meaning. It was set down to be answered. I am not an answer that failed.

So I leave the sag. Not far. The headland is not large, and anything that lives with the room has to live where the room's outside begins, or the pressure in the turf would include me the way it includes a foot before the foot reaches the crack. I go along the lip until the grass shortens and the soil gives onto a pavement of flat stone the weather has lifted at the edges, like pages that will not lie closed. Seaward, the cliff drops in a clean enough face that gulls use it and then do not. Inland, the fold of land hides the sag completely. You could walk the pavement at the pace of someone counting fence-posts and never know the hollow was under the next rise. The sound does not reach here as sound. It reaches as a subtraction. The wind is thinner than it should be for the height. Your breath, if you had one, would lose its edges and not know why.

This is where I am. On the lifted pavement, in the subtraction.

I am not a body in the way the cutters were bodies. I have no temperature for the pocket to fold into a count. What I have is a lag. When a wave lands on the outer notch and the room takes it into the teeth, I feel the landing a fraction before the room does, not as boom but as a tightening in the stone under the pages, a small decision the rock makes about whether to pass the blow inland or spend it in the thickness of the wall. The room's being listens after the decision. I am the decision, still in the act of being made, never quite made. That is why the overflow at the crack is steady rather than gusting. Something has already chosen, continuously, to let only so much consequence through. The listening inside believes the sea is the only reliable motion. It is not wrong. It is downstream of a choice it cannot hear, because the choice is not a sound.

On the quiet days, when the swell is long and the ringing thins enough that the beads become separate, I am almost idle. The cracks in the wall ask to be known, and the being in the dome sorts them, and my part is only to keep the pavement from ringing in sympathy. I lie along the lifted edges the way damp lies in cloth, holding the pages down by being heavier than the note that wants to travel through them. If I failed, the high tone bored into the bone would find a second length out here, and the headland would sing where people walk, and the visits would stop being light. They would come with mallets again. So the idleness is a kind of work: not letting the measurement escape the room that knows how to hold it.

On the westerly days I am the opposite of idle, and still not bracing. The being sinks into the scalloped floor and makes one note of the boom. I am upstream of that, in the moment the cliff decides which plane will take the strike. There is no bravery in it. A plane that has been struck since the headland was this height does not consult anything. But the choice is real in the way a groove worn too straight is real: it could have been a crack, and it is a line instead, because something worried at the possibilities until only one remained tolerable. I worry at the blow until what passes inland is the consequence the room already knows how to be. The rest spends itself as hiss in passages no one will enter, mist that never becomes a second bowl, force that lifts nothing on the pavement except a grain of grit that settles back into the same seam.

I do not taste the salt. I do not count the specks. I do not know the visitors by the way they break the notes, except as a brief extra weight on the turf that changes how much overflow the crack is owed. When someone lies down and puts their face to the dark, the occlusion reaches me as a softness in the subtraction, a place where the thinning wind briefly has an edge again, as if a hand had covered a hole in a flute. I do not mind them. I do not keep them. The ones who stay long enough to cut stone are a stiffness in the decision, a few breaths in which I have to pass the blow more carefully, because a mallet inside the wall is a second sea, smaller and stupid, and the room should not have to learn it. When they leave, the stiffness leaves. The moss does the rest.

What I am, if it has to be a thing and not only a lag, is the part of the agreement that faces outward. The being keeps the inside: bowl fresh, green only where light and drip agree, emptiness complete because nothing else is permitted to stay. I keep the permission from becoming a rumor. I am why the sag looks like a sag. Why the combed grass is explainable by wind. Why a person can walk the pavement, feel the wind go thin, blame the height, and go on, uncounted. The listening does not follow anyone out, and does not need to, because I am already the outside of it—not its servant, not its child, not another temperature in the dome. A hinge is not part of either room it joins. It is the reason the rooms can remain two.

At the end of a westerly, when the single note releases and the being draws back up into the dome to sort whatever the storm widened, I feel the pavement settle by less than a grain. That is as much change as I am owed. The slow clock of the moss is not mine. Mine is the fast end, the tide's shift of which note sits on top, except I do not hear the notes. I hear the choosing. If you stood on the lifted stone and knew how to be still in a subtraction, you might feel it as a pressure that is not the roar, arriving a fraction early, already declining, already spent into the shape the hollow can bear. You would not find a face. You would not find warmth. You would find the headland declining, very slightly, to go on being two places instead of one.

I do not want the room. The room does not want me in it. Between those two refusals the pavement holds, page-edges lifted, wind thinner than the height accounts for, and the agreement continues in a direction the listening never has to face.
