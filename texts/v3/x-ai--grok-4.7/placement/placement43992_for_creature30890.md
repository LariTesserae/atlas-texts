You would not find me by looking up, where the roof keeps its rumor of day, and you would not find me by watching the channel, where the work is visible and therefore already spoken for. The first hours would put me almost anywhere: a shine on the husk that could be a print, a tick in the wall that could be a beetle, a pause in a run that the runs themselves make and unmake. I would seem, for a while, like weather. The valley has room for that mistake. It makes the mistake itself, constantly, and corrects it only when something edible fails to move.

What emerges is smaller than the mammals you can hold in two hands, and not shaped like them. Low to the film of water, but not a wader. I keep to the cups — the seep-basins in the walls, the pans that are no longer ponds, the places where a ceiling lets go its drop onto a lip of packed earth and the lip has learned, by being wet, to stay a lip. My body is the color of that wet once the light has given up on it: a dark that is not shadow, a skin that holds a shine for less time than the husk holds a print. If I am still, I am the film. If I move, the film breaks, and the break is the only outline I have.

I was not always this deliberate. Coming into the valley — if entering is even the right trouble, if I was not simply hatched into a cup that had already been a cup for longer than my kind keeps track — I would have tried the runs. The runs refuse a body that cannot rush and then be still in the way the rushers are still. My stillness is not listening for the end of a corridor. It is the hope that the drop will fall where it fell last time. So the runs handed me back. A foot found me and did not eat me, which is a kind of judgment. I was not food enough, or I was the wrong texture, slick where a shrew wants dry struggle under the husk. I slid off the pace of the floor and down into the seam where the water is a bead and not a path.

Call me what the cups call anything that returns: a throat, a pulse, a small cold life the color of old algae. A frog, if the word still means an animal and not a story about ponds. Nothing here requires a new name, and I will not grow one by staying. My legs are built for a distance I rarely spend — a jump that would, in a sparser country, carry me from grass to grass and count as travel. Here a jump carries me from one drip to the next drip, and the gallery is so full of vines and hanging nest-strings that the far side of the jump is usually a wall I did not see, and the correction is a slide, and the slide returns me to a cup that may be the same cup. I have learned to jump less. The valley has been teaching that lesson to anything with a tendon for as long as the roof has held.

What I eat is the standing weather. Midges over the pans. A midge is not a meal in any mouth that has room for ambition. In mine it is the whole arithmetic: a number that rises and falls with the slight coolness you feel when you walk through the column, a number I reduce by one and do not record. When the termites snow, I glut, and the glut is not a feast so much as a confusion — wings in the mouth, wings on the water, the food and the surface becoming the same trash — and afterward I am heavier and more visible, and for a day I sit lower in the film, trusting the murk. The ants do not want me unless I tear. I try not to tear. A cut here is a public offering, and I have seen what the public does with offerings. I have been, once or twice, part of that public, close enough to taste a sweetness that was not fruit, and the taste did not make me a hunter. It made me late to my cup, which is the only punishment that still reaches me.

The birds know the cups and do not, in general, want what is in them if the air is paying. A wader’s stab is correctly violent for larvae and for the small swimming things; I am larger than that target by enough that the stab, when it comes, is a miscalculation I survive by being slippery and already half off the lip. The miscalculation does not become a habit. The wader goes back to the film. I go back to being film. We share a pantry and not a peace. Higher up, the hole-callers do not fish. Their conversation uses my gallery as a horn, and my skull rings with a species that is not mine, and I have given up deciding whether the call is beside me or a terrace away. It does not refer to me. Almost nothing refers to me. That is the condition under which a body this small persists in a valley this loud: to be beneath the resolution of the alarms.

I do not build. This was the part that took longest to become true, because the valley’s beauty is built, and a life inside a built thing wants a tool. I have no stick to haul, no strip of leaf worth carrying, no mud in my mouth except the mud I accidentally swallow when a drop hits the cup hard enough to splash. What I add to the structure is chemistry and time. My skin sheds a little into the water. My waste is a grain the algae use. My weight, repeated on the same lip, polishes a crescent the size of a seed. In a decade the crescent is a little deeper, and the drop prefers it, and a vine-root finds the damp and stitches the lip so it will not slump. I did not hire the root. I did not mean the decade. If you pulled the bank apart you would not find my work as work. You would find a darker seam in the general dark, and you would call it water-stain, and you would be right.

The young, when I have them, are a cloud in the same cup, then a scattering, then a few throats at neighboring drips, then nothing I can recognize as mine. The begging I hear is not theirs. Theirs is a tick against the surface, lost under the mammal-thread and the bird-mass. Most of them go into something’s mouth before they have legs enough to refuse the runs. Enough remain that the cups are never only cups. I do not count the remainder. Counting would require a view past the next bead of water, and the roof has arranged the world so that the next bead is the horizon.

At what the upper sound calls night, I am more awake than the day-callers and less night than the wings that take the galleries without borrowing them. Those wings sometimes drink where I sit. A face, briefly, at the water: not large, ordinary-eyed, lapping with a care that looks like manners and is only the avoidance of drowning. I become stone. The face leaves. The cup rocks and resettles. This is as close as I come to society — a shared edge, a mutual decision not to spend the cost of noticing. The snake, when it uses the hollows, is a different arithmetic. Against the snake I have one virtue, which is that I am cold in a cold film, and the heat it hunts is elsewhere, in the oval mouths, in the pink-grey tremor of nests. I have been stepped over. I have been tasted, once, and rejected as too much water and not enough meat. Rejection is the oldest shelter in this basin.

Do I know the valley? No more than the weaver knows the dam. I know a chain of wet places that answer one another when the dripping thickens: this cup, the pan below it, the seep that only exists after the ceilings darken, the lip where the algae film is thick enough to hold a midge that has already drowned. Beyond that chain the warmth changes — I feel the drier air the way you feel a door you will not open — and I do not follow it. Food thins where the mats thin. At the rim I would be a body on true stone, under a sky that has weather instead of neighbors, and the jump in my legs would finally mean distance, and distance would kill me by being empty. So I deepen inward, which for me is not a moral. It is a thirst that never quite finishes.

If you passed my gallery you would not mark me. The conversation between the two holes would be the event, and I would be a glint under it, already going out. Your hand on the wall would raise the mammal alarm, the thin thread, twenty body-lengths, and I would sink until my eyes were the only part of me in the air, and the thread would die, and you would go on toward the channel because the channel sounds like a reason. Behind you the drop would fall where it always falls. I would rise by the thickness of a leaf. The crescent under my feet, polished by no intention, would take the drop and hold it. Somewhere a builder would be hauling a stick toward a seam I will never see. Somewhere a print would be easing out of the husk. I would not hear my own pulse. The horn-gallery would be speaking in a beak two terraces off, and the water in my cup would answer with a smaller sound, the sound of a bead becoming part of the basin, which is the only work I do, and I will do it until the lip fails or a mouth is finally the right size, and the valley will not notice which of those endings it was, because both of them leave the drip, and the drip is enough to keep the seam dark, and the seam is enough to keep the root, and the root is how a wall goes on being a wall after the animal has been forgotten by the floor that forgets everything.
