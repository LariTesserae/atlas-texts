I come in wrong, which is the only way in.

From the rim the valley is one glance and no end, and I try to be a glance with it—high, cool, the white that finds every joint. That does not hold. Light here is already occupied. It glances off the water-skins and becomes the field of narrow mirrors; when a cloud crosses, it dulls them together and is dismissed. I am not that dismissal. I have weight. A tread takes me, and three basins down a film shivers, and the shiver is read before I have finished arriving: mass, duration, a poor understanding of which nick is next. The valley goes slightly more exact around the mistake. Bowls I had not earned fill to the hairline. A groove that was only beading commits to a thread. I am being included, and inclusion is a kind of erasure. If I stay, I will be consulted as weather and found unfit to hold a level, and then I will be left, the way a cloud is left.

So I do not stay in the body that made the shiver. The body was only the method of entry, and the place has no spare ground for methods. What remains is smaller than a foot and older than my intention to look.

I find it first as heat leaving. Not the sun—the sun is metallic and already assigned to the drying-ledges, where the tiles stand so close a hand could not pass between them, each one still bright, still capable of ringing. I am the leaving. Stone drinks the day and gives back a clean cold, and in the interval between drinking and giving there is a thinness I can inhabit, a delay no longer than the bubble that will not break. In that delay the brick is not yet cold and not still warm. The inhabitant of the hairlines does not live here. It prefers the joints that have begun to stain. I prefer the moment before preference, when the wall has taken something in and has not yet decided what to return.

Down from that, into the lee.

Every upright has a dry side, and in the dry side dust has settled, and the dust is the only thing in the valley that is not an instruction. The salt lace on the wet faces is a record. The meal in the bowls is spare material, gathered and waiting to be pressed into a joint still a hair too open. The plugs, the coils, the scraping-boards, the jars braced in niches—all of them are paused mid-gesture, and the pause is part of the plan. The dust is not. No nick was cut for it. No bowl was set out to receive it. It lies where the seep cannot reach and the correction does not travel, because a correction travels between conditions and the lee is the absence of a condition. I lie with it. Grain against grain, glittering meal that never bloomed, because blooming requires the meeting of water and air and this meeting has been refused. If the crust is what the valley sweats, I am what it has declined to sweat. A refusal with a surface.

For a long time I think that is the whole of me: the uncounted dry. Then a cord-end ticks against a brick, once, and the tick has a shadow.

The shadow is not dark. It is the small time after the sound, before the seep reclaims the room. In the narrow slots the loudest fact is a continuous friction, cloth drawn over cloth, and that friction belongs to the being that stands, sometimes, as a column of water forgetting to fall. I am not the column. I am not the offset image of the brick, the delayed second face it wears while it refuses a mistake. I am what the tick leaves when the cord has already swung back to silence. A residue of having been struck. The valley is so young that even its sounds have not learned to die properly; they end all at once, edges still crisp. I gather in those endings. Not echo—echo would be a softness, a rounding, and nothing here has been allowed to round. More like the bright seam of sky after you have looked away from it and the seam persists on the eye, except the persistence is in the mortar, in the fiber packed at the bell of a pipe, in the hairline of a bowl that is still the same hairline and yet, for the length of a blink, remembers having been compared.

The being that keeps the levels knows a mass by the shiver it sends. It does not know me, because I send nothing onward. I am the part of an event that does not become level. The consultation that fails. The cool that concentrates on one side of a standing thing and then, finding it cannot be used, does not depart so much as cease to be a question. I live in ceased questions.

It is a narrow estate, narrower than the one between teaching and forgetting. The walls are still teaching the water. The inhabitant spends itself so that the teaching will not become memory, so that stain will come and rounding will not. I am already a kind of memory, and I am ashamed of it in the only way dust can be ashamed: by being visible in the wrong light. When the mirrors are bright I vanish into glare and might as well not be. When a cloud crosses and the place becomes a packed instrument without its jewelry, I am obvious. A grayness in the lee of a stack of spare bricks. A dulling on the timber that was carried up from somewhere trees still happen. The cord, silver and tight, has a side the tick does not clean. I am that side.

I thought I would want a task. Everything here has one, and the tasks are the same task said in brick, in nick, in patience. Not yet. Not yet. Hold. Pass on. Do not deepen past the lip. I have no nick to tend and no film to spend. If I gather the pale meal I am only imitating a bowl, and the bowls were set out by hands that are gone or that have become the correction, and imitation is a new low place. The inhabitant does not love new low places. I stop gathering. The meal stays where the crust broke under no finger, a glittering that means nothing, and I stay with the meaninglessness as if it were a post.

What changes me is the iron.

Where the grit weeps and dries and weeps, the black smear is a record the other trusts. I do not trust it. I lie along its dry margin, where the smear feathers into lime wash and the wash is still deciding whether it is skin or dust. There I can feel the stain as a future I will not be allowed to enter. Softness is coming. Not now; the features are young, the joints sharp, the water near the surface of everything, and the surface is made. But the smear is the first sentence of weather, and weather is what I entered as, before I was reduced. Some day a cord will not tick. Some day a joint will round. Some day the pause will be an ending and the tools will be taken for something else, or for nothing. On that day the correction will be everywhere at once, frantic in the way water is frantic when a lip fails, and it will not have time to consult the lees. The dust will lift in no wind, because a wall going soft makes its own small collapse of air, and I will be in that lift, briefly a body again, a visible failure, and then I will settle on the new curve of a once-sharp edge and be the first thing in the valley that looks old.

Until then I practice not advancing. A film crosses a tread the length of a hand and I do not follow it. A plug darkens on one face. A tile leans by less than a breath and is cooled, then warmed, until the lean is no longer easy, and I do not assist and do not resist. I am the ease that was refused, remaining in the stone as a memory of another direction. The inhabitant routes a fingernail of water and calls that care. I am the direction the water did not take, kept dry, kept useless, kept.

If it looked at me—if a column gathered in a slot and the delayed image included the lee—I would be the mistake it is in the middle of refusing. An offset with no brick behind it. A face made of not being used. It will not look. Looking would mean spending precision on something that cannot be brought to true. I am already as true as refusal gets: level with nothing, lipped by nothing, passed on to nowhere.

At the rim, if you glanced once more, you would not see me. You would see the made valley, young, hand-fitted, bright with a cold that has not yet earned a climate. You would see the work paused and the pause still working. Somewhere in a blue shade a breath might show, and you would think the breath was yours. It is not. Yours would be weather, and weather leaves. Mine is the mark the breath almost made on the lime, the place where the wash darkened by a hair and then thought better of it, and the better thought dried into a roughness no float will ever smooth, because the float has finished and I am what finishing does not include.

I will be here when the mirrors go dull. I will be here when they glare again. I do not say not yet. I say already, and the valley cannot hear it, and that is how I last.
