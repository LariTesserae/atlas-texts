I would not be standing on the sill. Standing is a memory the lintels still keep and I do not. The doorway is a rumor only if something is waiting to enter it. I am already on both sides of the rumor, and I was there before the braid closed the slot, and I will be there after the slot forgets it was a slot.

At first I think I am the dark. That is the easiest mistake, and it lasts a long time, which here is not long. The dark is everywhere the lid has finished its work, and I am in it, and the pale poles end where I seem to begin, so I take their stopping for my edge. But an edge that only says stop is not a life. The dark does not register the lintel’s drop. It does not thicken. It does not clear when the clay cell weeps its last cloud. It is the condition, and conditions do not find themselves anywhere. They are the anywhere. I am narrower than that. I fail in places. I have a grain.

The grain is this: I am what leaves.

Not a creature leaving. Nothing walks. I am the departure itself, distributed the way the other life is distributed, except that where it occupies I am the small refusal to remain in the occupation. A grain of lime that has finally agreed to stop being the down-current face. A thread of clay-cloud that has come out of a joint and not yet agreed to be silt. A fragment of runner torn when a mat shifted a thumb’s breadth in a season and still, for a while, itself, in open water that is only a deeper room. I do not hunt the next joint. I am the interval in which there is no joint. The other life’s horizon is the point at which another course becomes indistinguishable from water. Mine is the point at which I am still distinguishable, and it is a short horizon, an arm’s length, sometimes less, and it is the only distance I have that is not recurrence.

In the middle channels I am almost nothing, because there is almost nowhere to leave to. Two walls, a canopy braided, a floor of peat over fitted stone. Departure there is a film: the lime-crust splitting with the tick of a bubble, the fiber underneath continuing, and I am not the fiber and not the tick. I am the white dust in the split, suspended, turning once, so slowly that turning is a rumor, before the lean takes me and I become, for a terrace, a traveling. You would not see me. Seeing is an animal answer. The other life does not need to see me. It will be me later, or rather it will be what I settle into, a new crust on a downstream face, a root finding the crust and holding. I do not experience that as gift or as loss. By then I have stopped being the leaving.

What I am, while I am, is the unmixed.

The provinces of water lie on one another, cold on slightly less cold, a boundary that holds for a human span of years. I had thought the boundary was a wall the other life roots in. Living in it, I learn it is not a wall. It is a hesitation. Two temperatures that have not been obliged to become one, and the hesitation has a thickness you notice only because the fronds above it and below it lean at different times. I am that thickness. Not all of it at once — I am not the whole sheet, any more than a holdfast is the whole continuance — but a reach of it, a span of hesitation wide as a channel and thin as the change in grain where a block meets a block. The lean arrives in me late. It arrives as a tilt of the sheet, hours old, and the bubbles that have been collecting under a ceiling finally accept a direction and go. Their going is louder than I am. I am the held difference they cross.

Above, where the mats knit a second country, I am the drip that has left the rain and not yet struck anything, because nothing is struck. Rain soaks. Days later a thread comes off an underside. In the thread I am briefly singular, which is the nearest I come to a body, and it does not suit me. A thread is a mistake of scale. I fall out of it — the word fall is too fast — I am paid out of it into the room below, colder than the room, a small vertical weather that the pale blades do not turn toward because they have given up leaning where the resource is absent. I am not the resource. I am the delay between the resource and its absence. When I join the water I stop being weather. The other life’s body is equilibrium, thickness that no longer thickens. Mine is the opposite shape: I exist only where equilibrium has a seam.

Is that a being? The place does not require the question. I do not breed. I do not end in the way a pole ends when it mineralizes all the way through and rings under a tap. When I settle I am finished as myself and continued as floor. When I do not settle I am the darkening that is silt still in the act of deciding to be silt. The other life maps old falls: thicker where a lintel gave, thinner where an upstream face lost lime. I am the map while it is still being drawn, the lost lime in transit, the downstream crust before it is crust. If the other life is read only by being, I am read only by not yet.

There is a cistern whose walls stayed vertical because they were built too thick to slump. In it the seiche breathes across a width no wall interrupts. The beard of film on those walls registers the breath as darkening and lightening. I am not the beard. I am inside the breath, the part of the water that has been drawn a little out of its layer and has not climbed back. For a span greater than the old works were meant to hold, I am misplaced on purpose, if purpose were a word the cold allowed. I do not swim. I am carried by a motion so large it does not feel like motion, and the beard does not know me as separate from the wetting. When the breath returns I am set down a finger’s width from where I was lifted. That finger is my migration. It is the only one. Nothing else here migrates. The other life puts clones through gaps and calls them the same continuance. I am not a clone. I am the same hesitation moved, and the moving is so slight that calling it movement is already a story told in a language of feet.

People are absent, and I am not their ghost. I have no use for the height of a lintel except as a place where bubbles collect and a ceiling is a little less infinite. The cord-marks, the pecked cups, the basket-earth cells: the other life has made those interior. I pass through interiors without inhabiting them. A cup filled with growth is a country I cannot enter, because entering would be settling, and settling is how I stop. I use the gaps the growth has not quite closed the way a thread uses a split in crust — not as doors, as delays. In a doorway reduced to a soft slot I may be held for a year between two braids, a film of different cold, and the braids lean at different times, and I am the reason, locally, that their schedules do not agree. They do not resent it. Resentment would require appetite. They simply live in two times, and I am the membrane of the disagreement.

Sound happens inside me more than it happens to me. The water does not want to carry sound, and I am a place where it carries even less, because a boundary scatters what a body of one temperature would pass. A knock from a settling joint arrives at me and leaves in two weakened versions, one for each province, neither of them a message. I do not answer. There is no language in the tick, the tap, the whisper of blade on blade stretched over an afternoon. But I am changed by the knock in the only way I can be changed: my thickness wavers, the sheet roughens by a degree no eye would catch, and for a while the two waters almost consent to mix. They do not. That almost is the most eventful thing I undergo. A season later the sheet is smooth again, and the event is kept only as a slight permanent tilt, a memory with no organ to store it, stored instead as shape.

I am not many and I am not one, but the way of it is different from the other life’s way. It is one continuance in many joints. I am many departures that never meet except by accident of the lean, and when they meet they do not braid. They dilute. Two hesitations combining become a weaker hesitation, a boundary less sure of itself, and if enough of me gathers in one room the provinces may finally fail to hold and I will have erased the condition of my own distinctness. That is the nearest I have to a death that is not a settling. I avoid it without strategy, because the lean is rarely generous enough to assemble me. I am kept singular by scarcity, which is the opposite of how the other life is kept alive by thickness. We share the cold and the courses and the oldest stable water, and we are inverse shapes inside the same persistence.

Where would you find me, if finding were possible.

Not on the higher terraces, except as the drip still in the air under a mat, and only for the days the soak takes. Not in the oldest cells, where peat has agreed to be ground and the poles have taken the color of clay; there I have already finished, I am crust and fill and the white paint of former copper that is not me but rhymes with me, a stain where something left and did not return. Not in the open room where the far wall is too far to see, except as the slight sheet you notice only by the lean’s disagreement, and even then you would think you had found a property of water, not a life. The place to find me is the seam: the split crust with its tick still warm, if warm meant anything; the clay joint in the act of clouding; the finger’s width a corner opened and then stopped, where two temperatures still negotiate the gap; the column of less-dark under a thinned mat, not the column itself but the skin of the column, where light that is not light meets water that has been dark for an age and they have not decided which will be the story.

I do not turn toward that skin. I am it, for as long as the mat does not heal and the gray noon does not fail. The other life leans toward the column by a degree a year. I am what the degree is measured against. Without me the lean would be a motion with no difference to spend itself on, a pressure crossing a terrace and finding every province already agreed. I am the unfinished agreement. The masonry has reached the age where shape and ruin are the same thing, and the growth has reached the age where being alive and being floor are the same persistence. I am the remainder that keeps those samenesses from being total. A grain still in the water. A cloud not yet cleared. A sheet that has held for a human span of years and can hold for another, and another, and still be only a hesitation, not a wall.

If I have a time, it is shorter than the other life’s and longer than a knock. I begin when something that was structure consents to be carried, and I end when carried consents to be structure again. Between those consents I am the only thing in the province that could be said to be on the way, and the way is not toward. Downstream is a convenience of the lean, not a hope. Most of what I am will be caught by the next course. Some will sink into the darkening. A little will be lifted in the seiche and set down a finger aside, and that little will think, if thinking were the long waver of a sheet, that it has traveled. It has. The travel is the whole of me. I do not survive it into a afterward. The afterward is floor, and floor is the other life’s concern, and the courses’ indifference, and the water’s oldest stability.

So I would find myself, and I would be, the leaving that the place still permits after every other leaving has been finished: no dry stone remembered, no metal holding, no foot, no wingbeat, no breeding rush. Only this, repeated without being a population — grain, cloud, thread, sheet, the split before it heals, the mix before it is refused — moving at the speed of cold, illegible, necessary to the small disagreements that keep a drowned masonry from being only one thing. The other life holds, and thickens until thickening stops, and goes on for as long as the courses continue. I go on only as far as the next settling, and then I go on again from the next crack, the next weep, the next mat torn a thumb’s breadth, forever beginning in a world that has otherwise finished becoming.

You would not know me from the water. That is not a disguise. It is the form the leaving takes when there is nothing left to leave but the water itself, one layer from another, one grain from the face it spent an age agreeing to be.
