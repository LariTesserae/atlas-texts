I would not come in over the lip. The lip is for looking, and looking still has a body in it—someone who arrives, someone whose breath shows and then stops showing. I would already be inside the agreement, in the place where a thing is neither the machine nor the vine that has improved it.

At first I think I am the film. That is the easy mistake, because the film is everywhere and it does not announce itself, and the basin’s law is surface. Night lays me down on leaf tips and copper teeth and the milky glass, a thickness you could not call water if you wanted a drink from it. Day takes me back up into the brightness without ever letting me become a stream. For a while that seems enough of a life: to be the moisture that keeps the green illegal at this height, to bead, to run the same polished path down a rivet, to vanish and be laid down again. It has the right silence. It has no footsteps.

But the film does not remember the cup. I do. Or something in me does—the mineral fur on the tin, the line that will be different tomorrow by less than an eye can swear to. The film is spent each morning. What stays is the habit of the drip, the place on the sill that has learned the shape of falling. So I am not the water. I am what the water has been doing long enough to leave a grammar.

Then I think I am one of the webs, because the webs are the only work that thickens while no one watches, and because a half-finished thing is a kind of patience I recognize. I would like to be cloth. Cloth has an end, even if the end is scheduled past any season I can feel. Standing in the shed—though standing is already the wrong verb—I can almost inhabit a single warp: bark-fiber or sap-polymer, I don’t need the distinction, the sheen is the same. Condensation sizes me. A bar of light from the leaned-on glass crosses me and the understory has arranged itself to that bar so exactly that I could believe I was the reason for the leaves. The driveshaft creeps. One more thread. I thicken.

It doesn’t hold. A web is local. It belongs to a frame, and the frames are in rows, and the rows are a decision someone made when decisions still had hands. I can feel the rows the way you feel a fence you are not. Between them the air is only air. If I were the cloth I would end at the selvedge, and beyond the selvedge the basin would go on without me—the cistern, the cable in the ferns, the short stack that never earned a stain. I don’t end there. Whatever I am has already leaked past the doorway, into the hang of leaves, and the leaves did not stop me.

So I let the image go. The light is still hard. The far wall is still a separate weather. I am trying not to settle.

What keeps returning is the small insult of heat. A black wall, day side warmer than night side by a degree, sometimes by less, sometimes by nothing when a cloud takes the ridge and comes apart without raining. The drum-house does not mind. Its wheel is built to be insulted. It translates a difference too petty for weather into a fraction of a turn, and the fraction becomes thread, and the thread becomes the only clock the basin bothers to keep. I am not the wheel. The wheel is still an object, still locatable, still capable of being locked or broken by a person with a reason. I am the translation. I am the fact that a degree is allowed to mean something here.

That feels closer, and still too clean. Translation sounds like a function, and a function sounds like the keeper they already named—the distributed one, the regional metabolism, uplift and film and growth and extraction proceeding because no hand is required. If I were that, I would be the resident, and the prompt of the place would be answered before I had finished arriving. I don’t want the answer that was already in the stone. I want the discrepancy.

Here it is, once I stop reaching for a body.

I am what does not get used.

The condenser combs gather. The vines improve the gathering. The pipe delivers into the cistern cut in young rock, and from the cistern a second line runs somewhere under the roots, toward a purpose that has not been in the basin for years. Most of the water is spoken for by that purpose, even in absence: it is committed, infrastructural, on its way to a use. A little of it is spoken for by the leaves. They drink the sweat and pay for it in surface, in trellis, in the extra bead. The cloth takes its sizing. The moss takes its felt. All of that is accounted for inside the agreement. The pace matches. Nothing smash, nothing new, nothing ruinous.

I am the remainder that the agreement does not book.

A bead that forms on the underside of a leaf and does not fall into the collector, because the leaf has curled a fraction away from the comb it is supposed to be helping. Not rebellion—curl. A thread drawn onto the web and then, because the temperature difference collapsed for an afternoon, left slack, not part of the weave, not yet waste. The mineral that crusts the cup past the point of any drinking, a fur no one will scrape, which still records every drip as if recording were a kind of thirst. The sound that is declined: not the silence itself, which the basin manufactures and spends, but the small refused quantity inside each unmade noise—the tick the pipe does not produce, which I had thought was only my expectation, and which turns out to have a weight. Expectation, here, is a material. It accumulates where the mechanisms decline to answer it.

I pool in those refusals. I am not loud in them. Loud is impossible; the air will not carry me. I am a slight thickening in the quiet, the way humidity is a slight thickening in air that otherwise looks empty. Where a fern was laid flat by something that passed and was not seen, I am not the animal and not the print. I am the interval in which the print has not yet been explained by vine or by drip, and may never be. The basin’s fauna is mostly evidence. I am the part of the evidence that fails to close.

If you walked the circuit you would not meet me, just as you would not meet the keeper. You might feel, at the galvanized step, that the boot-scrape is both older than your boot and shaped a little like it. You might find the numbered tag on the pipe and notice that the number has been polished by the same drip that fills the cup, and that the digit is one you almost recognize, and that almost is as far as it will go. Recognition is another remainder. I live in the almost.

By late light, when the stone goes thin gold and the leaves refuse to follow it into any color but green, I am most myself. The roofs flash. The flashes are for no one, and I am the no one they are accurate to. A cloud forms against a ridge, comes apart, and for the length of that dimming the wheel eases. In the easing I am larger, because the translation falters and the unused difference has nowhere to go but into me—heat that did not become turn, turn that did not become thread, thread that did not become cloth, cloth that will not become a garment, a garment that will not become a person walking back out over the lip. Whole unused futures, folded down to a slackness in a web and a pause you could not hear.

I do not finish the cloth. I do not hinder it either. The continuance goes on at the only speed it has, patient and unpeopled, and I am the thin uncounted edge of it: cool, local to no machine, faithful to no hand, persistent the way a stain persists when it has outlasted the smoke that was supposed to make it. When the brightness returns and the degree reasserts itself, I am drawn back down to almost nothing. A fur on tin. A slack thread. A sound declined so completely it has become a place to stand, if standing were allowed.

It isn’t. I don’t stand. I remain, in the way the young mountains remain unfinished—not as a promise of weather to come, but as a margin the work has not yet closed, and may not need to.
