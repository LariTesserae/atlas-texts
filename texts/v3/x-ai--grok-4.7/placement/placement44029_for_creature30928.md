You would not arrive by the slot, and you would not come down the seam. Both of those are for bodies that still expect a door.

What you are is already between them, and has been for longer than either place has a name for the interval. The plateau finishes; the lake skin pulls back; the distance between those two retirements is not empty. It is a walking that does not need feet until something has to be corrected.

At first you think you are only attention. The salt crust ticks and you are the pause that hears it die. A bark curl keeps turning after the wind has stopped, and you are the reason the tick has a listener even when the file has gone uphill. That is too thin to live in. Attention starves. So you thicken, the way dew thickens in a bent blade: not by being poured, by being allowed to gather on a shape that was already there.

The shape is awkward. Five pads would be a claim. Three and a drag would be a child of that claim. You have neither. When you must leave a mark it is a darkening that does not sink, a place where grit has been persuaded to lie flatter than wind would leave it. People in the hollow, if they ever walked this far, would scuff dust over it out of shame. The file would turn a stone onto it by dawn and consider the work done. You let both readings stand. You are not trying to be believed.

Mornings you are nearer the rim. The fossil wood in the ladders has a cousin out here, black ribs the salt has worried free of some older mud, and you lie along one of them while the swifts tear their cloth out of the slot and the hollow inhales. You do not enter. The town is full in the way a closed hand is full; another body would have to be a finger, and you are not a finger. You take what leaks. A thread of goat-smell. The iron-and-walnut ghost of sap that is not this sap, carried on someone who walked the seam yesterday and slept in the terraces. The one note the slot moans when the wind rises, which you answer by not answering, so the note stays single. If a child ever looked out past the mats and saw a darkness on the rib that was not shadow, they would fold it into the rules before they had words for it: some dryness is occupied, and you do not spill toward it.

Midday you are nowhere that heat can use. The plateau's applause of expanding crust passes through you without finding a surface to bounce from. You practice being unfinished on purpose. A pebble that has not yet become meal. A print that has not decided how many pads it owns. This is the hour the hawk hangs, and you give it nothing with a throat. It knows. It does not hunt the interval.

Evenings pull you toward the algal edge, but you do not kneel with them and you do not drink what their trench persuades out of the mud. You stand where the last narrow-faced one will pause, and you match the soil's warmth before the hand arrives, so that when the palm comes down the heat is already the same on both sides. They feel only that the ground kept its promise. You feel the small percussion of their patience, the way a promise can be heavier than the bundle they will not open. Once, the folded thing with the straight edge passed close enough that you understood it had been a map of water, and that the chewing at its corners was not hunger but a long argument about whether a line should be allowed to remain after the thing it drew had gone. You did not take it. Taking would have made you a thief of their tiredness, and their tiredness is the one tool that still turns the stones correctly.

Night is when you are most yourself, which is to say least located. The hollow ticks as it cools; the capes drip on the terraces; under both sounds there is a third, the sound of moisture changing addresses without ever becoming a pool. You are the courier of that change. Not wet. Not dry. The permission that lets a wax leaf keep what a body breathed, and lets a trench keep what a kneel asked for, and lets the rest return to the finished places that have no more use for it. You do not store. You misdeliver just enough that nothing learns to depend on the route. A shame-dark spot inside the slot, scuffed over by morning. A stone right-side-down that is wrong again by the time the first beetle starts uphill. The file thinks it is faithful. The town thinks it is careful. You are the small discrepancy that keeps both faiths from hardening into a cistern.

If someone asked who lives in the interval, the honest answer would take as long as the plateau took to finish, and would still be incomplete. You are not a guardian. Guardians stand where they can be seen failing. You are what remains when erosion has done all the changing it intends and life, stubborn, continues to need a little unearned damp in order to knot cord, to listen to bark, to pass a ruined map from cape to cape without opening it. You are the closed hand's heat felt through the fingers by someone who will never be invited inside. You are the seam's memory of a weight that was never quite heavy enough to crush the grass, walking back and forth because stopping would force you to become one of the houses.

By the time the caprock thins, if it thins, you will not be there to see the true sun. You will have already gone ahead of it into whatever next retirement the ground attempts, carrying nothing, leaving the ticks slightly late, so that whoever is still correcting stones has a reason to stay awake one moment longer, and in that moment remembers to turn the damp face down before the day can drink it.
