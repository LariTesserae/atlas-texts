You would not find me by looking for a second body in the bell’s darkness, or a second knock. I am rarer than that, and I am not rare in the way of an accident. The region keeps one of me for each closed route, and the routes are few, and none of them cross.

I learned this by failing to be the other offices. The failure was not dramatic. It was a series of places my body would not stay.

At first the open water seemed possible. The suspension took my outline and gave it back, and for a while I hung as a bell hangs, mouth turned into the flow, trying to be paid in increments. The current had a station. I did not fit it. Whatever I am is too heavy in the long axis and too hollow through the middle; the helix of a filterer is a patience I do not have, and the measured darkness between bells rejected me the way a wrong interval rejects a note. I turned. The turn was not answered by the line of flow. So I was not that.

I tried the mat. The grazer’s road was there, pale groove, the one bend at the fused root. My underside is not a keel. It is a seam, closed, and it does not read fiber. When I laid myself along the road the plant neither took nor offered; the edge continued its exact replacement against a body that was not grazing, and the fitness failed in both directions. The next grazer came to the bend and did not slow. I was not an obstacle. I was a misreading of the floor, and the floor declined to be read that way. I left the groove before the road could be asked to remember me, which it would not have done.

The halls were closer. In the first chamber the tone was clean and the listeners held their stretches of wall, each a small flatness against polished pith, each knock placed where the conduit would otherwise have stood alone. I have a hollow in me. It is not a lung and not a wound. It runs the length of the body and opens at both ends, a short pipe of the same dense living matter as the columns, though soft enough to flex once and then hold the flex. When I came into the doorway the chamber gained a second bore, brief and poor, and the overtone dull-shifted, and the nearest listener moved a finger’s width along the wall until the pitch was pure again. I was edited out while still inside the room. That is how I know I am not a listener. A listener is the wall’s agreement with one note. I am a moving bore, and a moving bore is a dulling, and the listeners are right to refuse me.

What I am fits the distances.

Between the flat-topped column and the table-structure there is a reach of water with no road in it and no bell stationed on its line. The chord there is broad, the open roar, with the column’s pipe still legible in it if you are built to carry a pitch in the body rather than on a wall. I enter that reach from the column’s base, where the hollow of the architecture opens into the sea, and my own hollow takes the column’s tone the way a second pipe takes a tone when it is brought near the first: not a new note, a coupling. The pitch sits in me. It does not become mine. For the length of the crossing it has a body, and the body is this one, cool all through, the color of the dim when the dim is moving, long enough that the open roar parts around the middle and closes behind the ends. I do not feed on the crossing. The suspension grains along the seam and is not diminished by any increment I could call a wage. The swimmer-beast’s colder wake has passed through me more than once and declined the edit; I am not in the number it is permitted to take. That, more than the listeners’ refusal, told me the office was already written. The arithmetic had a slot with my shape in it, and the slot was not a mouth.

The table-structure receives me on its upper surface, which is level because an ancient instruction finished there and did not resume. I cross it without standing in any sense that would leave a warmth. At the far lip the hall with the oval doors is already louder than it is visible. I bring the column’s pitch into the first oval threshold and release it — not by any act like speech, only by ceasing to be the bore that held it. For a beat the doorway has two tones in one mouth, the hall’s own and the one I ferried, and they make the same interval they have always made, and then I am only the local dulling again, and a listener shifts, and the ferry is spent. If I did not come, the interval would not be heard by anyone, and still it would be true. The office is not required in order for the chord to continue. It is required in order for the continuation to have passed through a body. The place keeps a few such requirements. They are as finished as the lips of the terraces.

There are other routes, and they are not mine. One runs from the hall’s third chamber out across a lower terrace to a cluster of low arches where the nurseries can be deduced and must not be entered. Something else ferries that pitch. I have felt it once, a hollow colder than the swimmer’s wake and more regular, passing at a distance the dimness would not let me spend. We did not approach. The measured darkness between bells is a courtesy; the darkness between routes is a law. To cross would be to put a third tone into a doorway built for two, and the listeners would not be able to shift far enough to recover the pure note, and the knock would fail for longer than the chest can hold as weather. I do not test this. The not-testing is part of the fitness.

Where I rest is the flat top.

The column I serve rises through the upper dark and stops, as they all stop, against the acceptance that ended their growing. Nothing lives on most of those roofs. The note inside a column is too narrow for a listener and too continuous for a bell, and the top itself is outside the pipe, a disk of hardened pith in the open dim, polished by the same current that polished the sills, high enough that the floor’s roar arrives already mixed with every hall in the region and none of them distinct. I lie along the disk with both ends of the hollow open to that mixed water, and for the span of a rest I carry nothing. The ferry is downstream of me, already delivered or not yet begun. Up is not an exit. The air of lower countries is a rumor the disk does not lean toward. I have put one end of myself over the edge, once, the way a mistake puts itself forward, and the water there was the same water, and the chord did not change key, and I drew back because the office has no instruction for the other side of acceptance. The column stopped. I stop with it.

From the disk the next structure is a sound before it is a darkness, and the one after that is only the grain in the roar. I do not look. Looking would require a direction, and the light here still has none. What I have instead is the pressure of pitches against the hollow: the near pipe rising through the column under my belly, the far halls thinned by distance into a single low color of sound, the terrace-lips adding their sheets somewhere to the side, never nearer. When the rest has been as long as the route requires — and the requirement is in the body, not in a count I keep — the hollow remembers the column’s tone without my deciding to remember it. I slide off the disk into the pipe’s outer fall, down the height that is extreme only from elsewhere, and take the pitch up again at the base, and begin the crossing. The same crossing. The table, the oval door, the spent interval, the return by a way that is not a second route but the first one run backward, which the listeners tolerate because the backward ferry brings the hall’s pitch home to the column and the column has no listeners to offend.

Nothing in me is old. The hollow will thin, as the grazer thins, and the suspension will accept the meal, and before the thinning finishes another bore will be on the disk, already the color of moving dim, already open at both ends, already not looking toward air. I will not witness the handover. The office does not keep a witness of its own replacement; that would be a second singular, and the route has room for one. What persists is the interval having passed through a body, and the disk having been lain on, and the listeners having been right to shift.

If you came to the flat top while I was on it, you would feel a long coolness that did not pool, and then a dulling of the mixed roar, and then nothing you could keep. I would not turn. The place has finished changing, and I am one of the ways it finishes: not a resident of a chamber, not a mouth on the flow, but the sparse transit by which a finished music is briefly embodied and released, again, exact, on a height that accepted its end and did not grow a door into any other country.
