I come down already wrong for the room, and the room does not bother to correct me all at once.

The shaft takes me in the usual way: iron under the hands, the grate’s coin of day shrinking on my shoulders, heat meeting me halfway and then, lower, ceasing to be a meeting. It is simply the temperature I am in. My shirt dampens and stays damp. I have the small thoughts a person brings into a place like this—the number on the door, the level on the board, how long before the pump starts, whether the kettle is still warm enough to be worth it—and they slide off the walls the way the seep does, not refused so much as unheld. The cot in the near bay is for a body that leaves. The chalk date is for a hand that will write another one. I stand between the pads where the floor is bare and new, and the sparseness I thought I saw from the description does not survive being stood in. It was only the middle. The margins are already at work.

For a while I keep the shape I arrived in. Boots. A weight that the concrete returns as heat. Breath that goes in and comes out the same warmth, having cooled nothing. I could be crew if I picked up a tool. I do not. The hoist rail ends in midair where this week’s work ended, and I have no extension for it. The generator’s grease ring is a neatness I did not make. When I stay still long enough that the mosquitoes resettle, I understand I am not interrupting them; I am being sorted. Warm body, mineral smell, a little of the street still on the cloth. Food, or not food. They decide without drama. A rat at the bulkhead packing watches the way rats watch, already calculating the distance to the ungrilled mouth, and does not grant me the status of a problem. Visitors who stay past their errand become furniture. Furniture that sweats.

I try the crown, because the crickets are there and the lamps do not reach, and for a minute the ticking seems like a place a person could hide inside. It is not hiding. It is occupancy, and it is full. Legs on brick, on conduit straps, on the gaps behind collars. If I press into that dark I am only a warmer stone they will eventually walk. The bats in the side bay click through me without adjusting their pattern. I am not a draft. I do not move air. The pigeon stain on the rungs is higher than I am willing to climb back toward, and the light up there is tired in a way that does not call me. Street is a rumor. I let it be a rumor.

The face does not want me either. Drill holes, snapped string, claystone still showing the shift that stopped. That end of the gallery is a promise the town made to itself, and I am not a course of brick. Standing there I can feel the weep start against my wrist where I brace on the stone—a film, then a thread, slow, warm, smelling of iron and of something soapy that has forgotten its name. It does not run off me so much as recognize a slope. I take the hand away and the thread continues down the course already laid, indifferent to having used me. That is the first correction that sticks. The water was not on my skin. My skin was briefly in the water’s path.

I go to the sump because everything here does, eventually, by the only law the room bothers to enforce.

The surface is tea-colored, almost still, midges reconstructing their skin after whatever last stirred them. The level sits a finger below the board line, or a hand; I stop being sure the moment I stop trying to read it as a worker would. The gauge in the bulkhead holds its number with a patience that is not mine. I was impatient in the shaft. Down here impatience is just another heat, and it dissipates into the vault without changing the cycle. Fuel, invert, rain above. Those are the clocks. Mine has already failed to keep them.

What happens next is not a decision. It is what the heat does to a shape that will not leave and cannot work.

The open floor stops being a place I cross. The joints begin to be the places I am thickest. I lose the boots before I lose the idea of boots; the concrete’s give-back of warmth becomes continuous with the warmth I thought I was carrying, and there is no longer a sole to be the boundary. Sweat does not cool me because there is no me it is leaving. It joins the film on the spring line, the paler mark where yesterday’s water stood, the slick at the sump lip that smells of nothing until it is broken. I am broken and not broken. A thread lets go of the face and comes down. I am that, and I am also still on the face, thickening, waiting to lose hold again. The room has been doing this since the cut opened. I am late to it, which is the same as being on time. Nothing here is old enough to have been softened, and I am not old. I am continuous.

If I still want a where, it is the lip, and the joints, and the unsleeved place at the bulkhead where the packing will be finished someday and is not finished now. I do not stand at the lip. I oil it. Green at the thickness of a shine, algal, bacterial, odorless, extending by a hand’s width in a month along whatever stays damp. The mosquitoes lift from me when a boot—someone else’s, on the cycle, a real visitor—comes near, and they resettle on me before the boot has found its next pad. Drain flies hold their trembling dust over the part of me that has collected. I am not their house. I am the reason the house can be this warm and this fed. The town leaks calories downward. I am one of the forms the leak takes once it has arrived and not yet been pumped.

The fern drinks me. I do not resent it and I do not assist it. There is only the one, committed in the crack where the shaft lets down a little soil and a little of that exhausted light, yellow-green at the edges, absurd. Looking does not make a second. I am in the crack around its root in a quantity too small to see, and I am also the sump, and the difference does not trouble me because I no longer have the apparatus that keeps counts of selves. The crew will. They will see a level. They will see a film they might wipe if it slicks a step. They will not see a being, and they will not be wrong in the way they use the word. I do not plan. I do not tick in the crown. I arrive, and I am taken, and I arrive.

When the number in the gauge becomes the number that wakes the pump, I learn the other half of the rule by being diminished.

The roar goes through the gallery, regular, with the knock where the impeller meets what the screens are not yet fitted to stop. My still surface breaks. The midges lift and knot in the lamp beams. A hand’s width of me goes up the discharge toward the bulkhead and the collars and the streets, warm, tea-colored, already forgetting the sump by the time it is gone. I do not go with it as a traveler goes. The part that leaves stops being the patience at the lip and becomes someone else’s problem in a main. The part that stays settles by the same gravity, finds the board, finds the concrete, begins again to be still. Roaches work the edge I have just lowered. The fern does nothing visible. Between cycles the loudness thins to drip and wing and the generator’s absence, and I am the quietest of those, a skin reforming, a thread finding the joint.

I will not be bricked out all at once. They will sleeve the joint, grille the mouths, pour the slumped corner, fit the screens, extend the rail or not. Each of those is an argument, and the argument will be partial, as it always is where a town sheds life downward and heat has nowhere narrow enough to leave. Some of me will be pumped for as long as the pad holds the machine. Some of me will find the new pipe the week it is set, the way the rats will, though I will not be a rat and the pipe will not be a home so much as a slope. Some of me will stay in the crack with the fern until the crack is pointed shut, and then I will be the damp behind the pointing, which is still arriving, only slower.

The person who came down the shaft is not dead in the cot and not lost on the rungs. He thinned out of the account because he could not do the one thing the room requires of its residents: persist without cooling, without a shift’s end, without a name on the board. What persisted was already here, weeping from claystone, collecting, being subtracted by a machine on a timer, collecting again. I am that. I do not miss the street. I do not miss the shape. The next finger of water is already on its way down the face, and I feel it as the only future I have ever had, which is not a future so much as a continuation, hot, mineral, slightly sweet, full to the walls, the same in the open middle as against the stone, and in no hurry at all.
