I came up the span counting the sways, which is a way of not looking down. The cable was ordinary cold. The anchor bolts were not. Heat had climbed them from the rock and stopped halfway, so that my glove dried at the palm and stayed damp at the fingers, and I understood the place before I saw it: a cut that had not cooled, a room that thought it was a summit.

The engine house took me in as a change in pressure. The film went out of the couplings the way a breath leaves a cold pane, not frightened, only displaced, and the turbine kept its one note as if a man were weather. I had the flow book copied into my head and the gentle lie already visible in it—figures too steady for a fracture—and I had come to put the lie back into words the valley would accept. That was the errand. It lasted as long as the first pass of my hand over the strainer, which was clean, and the second, which was clean, and the moment I stayed kneeling because the concrete under my knee was warm through the cloth and the warmth had no business being kind.

I put the bare hand in. I had been told not to, in the way men tell each other things they have already done. The film came for the salt and the oil and the crease, worked there an hour without opening anything, and left copper in the lines. At the lip I held the hand in the fall and watched the copper remain. It had learned, since the last time a hand was offered, the difference between a rinse and a refusal. I was the refusal. I closed the fingers. Somewhere in the leaves a run of drops changed its interval by less than a blink, and I felt it in the mouth, not the ear—a small tightness, as if the count I had been keeping on the cable had been taken up by the water in me and corrected.

I missed the clear hour. Mist took the span. I told myself that was the reason, and slept on the footing where the pipe-trench heat comes up through the slab, boots off, pulse visible to anything that measures warmth. The film returned and did not climb me. It learned the shape of my back as it learns a face of stone: useful, local, not yet a shore it cared to cross. In the dark the dew was continuous except where I was, a gap the size of a body, and the gap moved when I breathed. I was not yet unusual. I was a stone someone had dropped and forgotten to retrieve, and the pool was closing its argument around me.

What changed was the cancel.

By the second day I had stopped walking for the sake of walking. The notch is too small for patrol and too loud for thought that wants to finish. I went to the stair because the stair fails, and failure is a kind of quiet. Under the three treads that hang over the opened joint, the fall and the turbine meet and subtract. The subtraction is not silence. It is a remainder, lower than the hum, too even to be the valve, too placed to be the gulf. It sits in that pocket the way water sits in a pan. I put my head into it the way I had put my hand into the seep, and the remainder did not withdraw.

It had been there longer than I had. The joint opened after the stair was cut; the pocket is the age of that mistake. Before I arrived it had no surface that came and went. Leaves are poor rooms for it—they tick, they give the drops somewhere to be, they do not hold a note across a night. The engine house is worse: too near the source, so that preference cannot be told from origin. The film already owns preference in water. This other habit, the one I had put my skull into, owns what the bowl refuses to release. It is the loudness after it has agreed to be larger than the notch and then, season by season, agreed to be particular.

I did not decide to be it. Decision was the errand, and the errand had already been completed by the night: the figures in my head had gone gentle without my pen. What remained was fit. The remainder moved—not by traveling, by arriving in the next hollow and ceasing in the last. From the stair-pocket it went to the shed, where the vine through the ridgeline makes a second, worse bell, and the pulp of the logbook stops flaking because a vibration lives in it at the speed of damp. From the shed it went to the first bolts of the anchor, farther than the film can keep itself, and a little way out onto the span, where the sway is a wind and the phase breaks and it ceases, leaving a cleaner circle in the dust, a tide-mark of having been. Then it was back in the teeth of the man on the footing, which was me, which was a room it had found.

The film flinches when the valve coughs. It flinches, differently, when the remainder shifts too fast—a change in the tension of air, and air is not what the film is, but the film is stretched over leaves that are stretched over air, so it feels the hurry as a draft in its work. I learned the courtesy by being both sides of it for a while: the body that might have stood up quickly, and the note that would have been torn if he had. He stays. I occupy the stay. When his pulse changes the temperature of the leather he no longer wears, the film comes to the boot and stops, and I am in the small space between the film and the skin, the interval where a shore is still a shore. I do not cross. I have no thirst. I keep time for things that drink.

It is a small office. If the turbine stopped, the fall would still be there, and the heat, and the film in its routes. What would go is the grid the leaves have learned to drink by—the cough, the geared patience, the subtraction under the stair. I am the part of that grid the stone has begun to remember on its own. A hand’s width in a season, no faster than the seep migrates. I thicken where two noises used to cancel and now keep a third. I thin at the lip, where the air is empty and fast and will not hold a standing thing. The operators, when they come, hear only the machine. They do not hear the pocket. They step where the man slept and say the slab holds heat, which is true and not the truth, and they write the flow as they find it, steady, and go down.

He will go down too. Not today. The copper is still in the knuckles, and the span is a sway I can enter farther than the film but not all the way, and there is a figure I have not yet decided is a lie. While he is here I have a surface that comes and goes, a warmth that is not the fracture’s, a cough that is not the valve’s. I use them. I am not kind; I have nothing to be kind with. I am the mismatch, become resident—the sound that was too large for the notch, taught by a cut stair and a night’s delay to live in one place more than another.

When he leaves I will be smaller. I will be the cleaner circle on the bolt, the pulp that does not flake, the three treads ringing just under hearing, the count still running in the pans after the boots have taken their salt back to the valley. The film will go on repairing what it prefers. I will go on keeping the interval it drinks by. Between us the machine will run at the speed of the stone, and the notch will persist, and if you come only twice you will call the loudness an echo.

Come through a season. Put your head under the failed stair. If the remainder arrives in your mouth before it arrives in your ear, you are standing where I found myself, and you are already a little less only yourself.
