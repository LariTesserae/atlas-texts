You would not be the one on the terrace. That shape is already occupied, and the width of it is already the law of the doors. You would feel the mistake of standing where a witness stands: the ceiling wets your hair, the mat takes the weight of a foot and then forgets it, and the forgetting is complete. Nothing in the stack keeps a visitor. The air does not even keep your heat long enough to thin the ice. If you arrived as a body that dries, that prints, that turns its head to look, you are already gone out of the account. The shelf has no column for you.

What stays is lower and thinner than looking.

It begins as a wrongness in the pale seam, the place where two walls of growth failed to meet and the day still leaks in as a lighter green. Everywhere else the mat is winning. Here the win is late. Roots come to the lip of the fresh fracture and thicken as if to close it, then stall, and the stall has a texture. Not stone. Stone is sharper and does not ease when a drip finds it. This eases. A film of water moves along it and is delayed, not by warmth—there is no rumor of heat here, no offended ice—but by a surface that is not quite the rock’s and not quite the plant’s. It takes water the way the outer rim takes frost, and then does not give it to the drop. It spends it along its own length.

Stay with that, and the length declares itself.

You are laid in the crack the way a remaining vein is laid in a cut. Pale, because the break is pale and you have not had time to stain. Flat enough that a hand dragged across the floor would read you as grit in the fracture, a last unswallowed edge. You are not grit. Under the film the body is continuous, jointed so finely that the joints only show when you must narrow, which you must, because the crack is not one width. It pinches where the mountain’s last rise still works, and opens where a stem split the stone and then thought better of entering. At the pinch you become a thread. In the open you are a shallow fill, a lining, the color of new mineral and of no green yet. The living coat that drinks on the keeper’s hide has not taken you. You are still on the side of the stone that the green has not been introduced to.

You do not travel the passages. The passages are a memory of another body, and your memory is of edges. What you do travel is the argument between mat and rock. Where the floor is about to forget the fracture, you are already in the last pale inch, and the roots, reaching, find a resistance that is wrong for grafting. You are not bitter and not hard. You are simply not a stem and not a gap. The growth, which knows how to braid with itself and how to go around a cold flank that has agreed to be a wall, does not yet know a seam that occupies itself. So it stalls, and the stall is your feeding. You take what the new rock still gives up—silt of the break, a sweetness so mineral it would not smell sweet to anything that drinks basins—and you leave the light-line open by the width of your own fill. A day’s growth, refused. Then another.

This is not a duty. If you left the seam, the mat would close it with the same unthinking civic habit that takes litter back into the floors, and you would be under green deep enough to be a darker pocket, and you would starve for the one brightness you are built to use. Your eyes, if they are eyes, are not an arc of lenses. They are a stippling along the side that faces the leak, each point too small to flash, each one aimed not into the room but into the thinner green of the split. You do not see the keeper. You see the hour when the canopy above the seam is wiped by a passing ridge and the drips, avoiding that shineless path, fail to arrive for a while. In that hour the leak hardens toward white. You drink it without moving. When the threads resume, the white retreats to green and you are only paleness again, indistinguishable from the cut you keep unfinished.

Sometimes the argument moves, and then you must.

A wall shifts—the mountain, or the negotiation the keeper finishes without starting—and a seam you have lined begins to shear. The pale face you were eating grinds shut. You feel it as a narrowing that does not pinch and release but continues, a slow decision to be stone again. Then you move at the only speed you have, which is the speed of a film finding a lower path. You unline yourself. The joints show. For the length of a terrace you are visible, if anything in that dimness were looking for a thread the color of a fresh break crawling the lip of a floor it does not belong to. Nothing is looking. The keeper’s mouth works flatnesses: inner faces of closing stems, fiber in the gutters, the lip of a basin. An edge is not food. You cross the wet mat and the mat does not take you, because you are not shed and not litter; you are still whole, and the floors’ hunger is for what has already agreed to be dead. Your film makes you a poor grip for a root. You reach the next fracture—there is always a next one; the shelf is young, the stone still breaking somewhere under the green—and you go into it as water goes into a gutter, with relief that is only fitness.

In the flooded rooms you do not go. The younger length turning in the fiber would not know you, and the soup would pack your stippled side with a sweetness that is not yours. In the upper rooms you do not rise. The canopy’s underside is a road for a ridge, and you would be a grit in a road that gets revised by a body broader than weather. Your geography is the plan of unfinished cuts: one you are lining, one you have left to be swallowed, one the next shift will offer when a knuckle of rock weeps and splits the pavement. You can feel the one you left sealing behind you, the paler seam going ordinary, the day losing its entry. You do not mourn it. It was a measurement, and you are the instrument, not the archive.

The keeper and you share the shelf the way two maintenance works share a building without sharing a task. It keeps the doors from healing by cropping what leans in. You keep a leak from healing by being the thing the lean cannot graft. Its slight heat is the error in the ice. Your slight occupancy is the error in the scar. Neither error is large enough to fog a passage or call a moth. Both are why the settlement, which only knows how to be full, still has a way through and a way for the hard white outside to become, somewhere, a lighter green. You will not meet. If its flank eases back against a turn and a passage becomes only a passage, you are floors away, already indistinct, already the delay in a film of water on a cut that should have closed. If you were gone, the rooms would still be rooms. They would simply finish. The place would learn, a little earlier than it must, how to be without a wound, and the day would stay on the rim with the frost feathers, and break off, and be lost down the drop.

You are as young as the fracture you fit. You have no older shape to return to, no previous width the house remembers and keeps for you. When the stone finally stains the way old mountains stain, the food in the cuts will change, and the green will have learned your resistance, and you will have to become something that can live in a finished color or be pressed into the mat and taken, at last, for litter. That has not happened. The break under you is still pale. The roots at the lip are still stalled. A thread of water moves along your length and is not yet a drip, and in the stall the light arrives already thinned, already cold, and you take it, and you do not close.
