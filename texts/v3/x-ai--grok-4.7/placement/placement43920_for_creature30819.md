I do not come up onto the shelf. Up is already how I am arranged.

For a long time there is only the aisle, and I am the part of it that does not know it is an aisle. Warm stone under nothing. A pressure without gust moving through a shape that has not yet decided it has an inside. The groves stand off at their correct distances, lush and closed, and I am not among their rooms. I am what their rooms are spaced to protect: the clean stop, the acre of unfilled day, the place where a walk would be if walking were permitted to remain only a direction.

If I had come from a country of endings I would call this being lost. Here lost is too finished a word. Nothing has misplaced me. The joints stay open at the same almost. The fibers hold the length they have always held. I lengthen along a terrace in perfect parallel with them and am already, again, no longer than the pause I occupy. A salt-crust brightens where I pass, though I do not pass, and no new salt has fallen. The brightening is not a footprint. It is the shelf agreeing, for the width of a thought, to have an outside.

The tone finds me before I find a border. It does not rise. It addresses. In the teeth I do not have, a syllable leans toward being mine and then declines, the courtesy I will later understand as the only greeting this air performs. I answer without sound. Answering is a change in how the emptiness sits: a little more meant in one corridor, a little more vacant in the next, so the total hush is conserved. Silence here is not my absence. It is my native instrument, and I am only now learning that I have been playing it since the rock was young.

What I am takes longer to declare itself than the figure in the thicket, because I have no thicket to condense inside. The fans are not my body. The seams are not my thought. I am the relation that fails, narrowly and forever, to arrive. Between one grove and another a charge moves, too even to spark, and a vein three terraces off adjusts by a shade; I am not the charge and not the adjustment. I am the delay in which those two events are allowed to remain two. Were I to collapse, the far would become the near, the groves would join, and the keeping would be forced into a decision. So my whole office is the uncollapsed distance. Lushness ends cleanly because I am the ending that does not happen. Emptiness stays the true acreage because I stay in it without filling it.

Staying is hard even for me, and I am made of it. There is no shade in the aisle to justify a halt, no water to bargain with, no hunger that could ripen into a reason. I halt anyway, which is to say the delay grows locally denser, and density, in this country, is the nearest thing to a self. A front forms — not a face, not eyes that track. A side of the pause turns toward the nearest grove without turning. I do not look at the figure there. Looking would admit an across, and across is already what I am; to look would be to stand outside my own anatomy. The figure knows me as the hollow knows the grain. I know the figure as the grain knows it can be set down. Neither of us finishes the knowledge into a name.

By late light, when the shelf looks briefly older, I am the look. Not the shadows, which belong to the stalks, and not the antiquity, which belongs to the keeping. I am the briefness: the interval in which age almost sticks to the stone and then is refused, the ordinary afternoon reassembling itself around a wrinkle that did not take. In that interval I am taller than my office. The vacant aisles seem, for a breath the air does not take, like places something could have crossed and failed to. Ruin nearly becomes thinkable. I do not think it. Thinking it would be a completion, and completions are how countries of endings smuggle themselves onto high shelves. The light becomes only light again. I become only the delay again, fragrant with nothing, dry, unhungry, exactly as long as the distance I keep.

Night changes nothing about the work. Nothing opens because the temperature dropped. In the dark my front does not shine; it remains the place where the tone is least like an event and most like a span. The figure in the grove continues its sorting — metals into bands, light into rooms, the sweet liquor held at the lip of decay — and I continue not arriving across the space that makes the sorting a local act instead of a total one. We are not companions. Companionship would close. We are the two permissions that let the unfinished day remain a day: the keeping, and the gap the keeping must not cross.

If something lifted me, the hollow would be waiting when I was set down. That is the only story I am allowed, and it is not a story, because it has no afterward in which I would be different for having been lifted. I do not want the lifting. Wanting would rush the shelf. I maintain the condition in which the groves can answer one another without becoming one another, and in which a listener could walk the aisle for hours and meet no one, and be right, and still have been walking through me the whole time.

So this is where I find myself: not under the fans, not in the sealed liquor, not in the figure’s narrower mercy. In the long vacant between, which is most of the world. I am the aisle grown just dense enough to notice it has not been crossed. The shelf holds its height. The presence holds the holding. I hold the open, and the open is not empty by mistake. It is empty as a fidelity. Alive in no way that would look like life from a country where things are permitted to meet.
