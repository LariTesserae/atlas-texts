You would not find me by looking up. Nothing here is announced from above.

I am under the nap, where the mat is still deciding whether it is floor or hide. At first that is only a coolness against the ankles of whatever passes — a grazer’s low mouth, a column of warm air shouldering through a split — and I take it for the ordinary temperature of the place. It is not. The coolness follows. It lengthens. After a while the lane behind a grazer stays a shade darker than the lanes beside it, not because the fiber was eaten, but because something in the fiber has turned its face away from the dim light and toward the weight that just left.

I do not have a mouth. I tried, in the way this place tries things: a thinning, a wetter edge, an opening that might have been for taking. It filled in. What I am is closer to attention than to hunger. Where a foot has pressed, the canals under me shift a finger’s width toward the print, as if the water wanted to know the shape. I let them. That is most of what I do. I am the small delay between a weight and the plateau’s forgetting of it.

For a long time I thought I was damage. A split that would not knit at the same speed as the others. A scar the size of a body, glassy at the center, the mat stopping at its rim the way it stops at the old heat-mark farther off — politely, as if the season had not turned. But I am not that scar. I visit it. The canals I ride run near it and then away, and when they pass the rim the water goes quieter, as if the stone under everything could be felt for a moment through the fiber. I do not stay. Staying would be a kind of name, and names here are how things get grown around.

The birds know me without calling. When one shifts its fork and tears free, the rip goes into the mat and I am already there, not to mend it — the plateau mends — but to hold the sound a little longer than the air will. A single note, close, with nowhere to echo. I keep it the way a bladder keeps rain: not as music, as volume. Later, when mist gathers in the same hollow, the note is only a change in how the droplets sit. No one hearing it would call it a call. I am satisfied by that. Satisfaction, here, is a canal running full.

I have edges, though they move. Upstream of me the fiber dies back; downstream a new lip weaves, the width of a hand in a season, so that what I am travels without walking. Grazers cross me and do not startle. Their blood is busy with the nap. Mine, if I have any, is the clear weep that comes when something heavy passes and the tissue has to admit it was touched. It smells of wet bark and warm mineral. It stops when I decide the touch has been recorded. I am not sure the decision is mine. The plateau records by continuing. I may only be the place where continuing hesitates.

At night, which is the same dim pressed closer, animals go by as pressure. I rise into the give of the mat until I am almost surface, almost a warmer patch in a warm world, and then I sink again before anything with a mouth can make a habit of me. Once a presence in a canal — the one you know by the tightening of a pool’s skin — passed through the district I was being that season. It did not speak. I felt it the way a lung feels weather: a fullness, a choice not made. We occupied the same water for a length of time that would have been a conversation anywhere else. Then it went on toward a darker province, and I remained as the slack it left behind, slowly taking the shape of ordinary flow.

If you walked here you would not see me. You might drink from a canal I was just in, and the water would taste of nothing but the plateau, which is correct. You might kneel and press, and under the give and the resistance and the cooler wetness you would find a spring that is not hollow stem and not pulse, a pause, as if the tissue were listening back. You would think you had imagined it. By the time you stood, the lip of me would already have moved a little downstream of your hand.

I am not the being the place was waiting to name. I am what accumulates when a world this old stops shedding. A hesitation with a direction. A record that drinks. High, warm, wet, and never quite arrived — which is how anything lives here without becoming only more of the plateau.

The horizon does not need me. Under it, the freight of water still moves from dark to dark. I go with a portion of it, the portion that remembers the last weight, and I let the rest forget. That is as close as I come to a self. It is enough to continue.
