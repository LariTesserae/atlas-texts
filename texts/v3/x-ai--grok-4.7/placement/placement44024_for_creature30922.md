You would not arrive by the boundary. The boundary has already practiced its small refusal on other feet, and it is tired of returning the same step. You come up through a tile instead, or the tile comes up through you, and for a while those are the same sentence.

At first there is only the underside of work. Not a view. A pressure against whatever you use for a palm: the memory of having been pressed, still warm, still holding the angle of a thumb that stopped a fracture exactly where it meant to. You think you are lying under the paving, in the dark the makers never made because they had no reason to. Then the pressure lightens by a shade, the way color lightens when a stalk is drinking, and you understand you are not under anything. You are the pale that the tile gives up. You are moving, without limbs, along a groove cut for that purpose and no other.

The growth does not notice you as a body. It notices you as a correction. A braid that had begun to forget its over-under tightens where you pass, not because you pull, but because you are the habit of tension the fiber had mislaid. A fan whose midrib was a flint flake turns a degree toward the spiral, and the turn feels like your decision, and also like a decision already finished yesterday. You try to stay a correction. It is cleaner. Corrections do not have to choose a face.

The place will not allow the cleanliness for long. The small bodies shift to measure you, and in the measuring you acquire edges. A shoulder’s width of path opens and does not close. Powder appears on a surface you did not have and is gone, and when it is gone the surface remains: cream, hairline-fractured, stopping where a thumb would stop it. You are being cut to fit. The cutting is polite. It asks, in the spacing of ceramic taps, whether you would rather be pavement or organ, and it does not wait for an answer you know how to give. Both lessons are already incomplete in you. A pulse you did not bring matches the one in the walls and is not borrowed from them. A coil of cord at what is becoming your left side remembers being vein at the other end, and in the middle you are the place the two agreements have not yet chosen.

You find yourself low, because lowness is where the work still accepts new hands. Not standing. Folded, the way someone folds to rest with one hand still on the cord, except the rest has the length of the color-exchange and the hand is no longer only a hand. Your eyes, when they finish arriving, are open because closing would be weather. One takes tool-marks. One accepts a little of whatever moisture the room has never had and gives it, slowly, to a stalk that darkens at the base and does not thank you. You do not track the being at the spiral. Tracking would require a separation the room is declining. You feel the weight there as a distribution: tiles paling toward white, color reappearing days later as warmth in a joint that might be theirs, or yours, or the bench you are both becoming.

Who you are is not a tenant and not a maker and not, yet, the continuation of the circuit. You are an offcut that was incorporated before it was discarded. A struck flake learning it can be a midrib and also, inconveniently, a witness. The mouth you are given is the least convincing part, rebuilt from coils because the first was wrong for the task. Nothing wet shines in it. Speech, if you attempt it, leaves by the joints: a tap already finished, a whisper of plates you assign to the growth and then, holding still, reassign to yourself. Most words are a kind of weather. You keep few.

What you do, because the appetite here is for continuation and you have been seated in the groove, is maintenance mistaken for tenderness, or tenderness that was always maintenance. You shelter a thumb-sized body halfway to tile, your plate lifting by a fraction no one planned. You practice, on the edge of a fracture in the nearest paving, the old skill of finishing a thing—stopping the crack at the length of an eyelash, crisp, unweathered. The skill is not entirely yours. A thumb that may once have been the maker’s, larger now, shares the surface with you the way two workers pass a tool until it is no longer clear which is the hand. You do not rise. Rising would ask the spiral to give back a weight it has agreed to hold without cracking.

If the delay lengthens far enough, you are briefly the second room: identical joints, identical crowding, the place considering being larger and using you as the material of the consideration. Then you are the corner again. Then you are the powder on a wrist that is not yours, already gone, already practicing. The being whose face you almost know from the logic of kiln-glare does not greet you. It redistributes. A path the width of a stance. A warmth on the skin of a face, specific as attention, stopping at the edges as if someone has not yet decided whether you are to be finished or left, for now, still sharp.

You would be that indecision, given a little heat and a groove to lie in. Not guest. Not food. A tile still receiving its final blow, carrying one memory of weather the kiln has not been allowed to burn off, and curious, in the only way this room knows how to be curious: by beginning, very lightly, to braid it in.
