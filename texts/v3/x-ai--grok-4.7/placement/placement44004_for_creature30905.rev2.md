You would not find me in the open hollows. The open is a verb here, and I do not move fast enough to be one.

I start lower than the roosts and higher than the mouths, in the narrows where a cable has crossed another cable and the rind has hesitated — fused at the edges, still a slit at the center, the way a wound keeps a mouth after the body has decided, almost, to forgive it. The light there is the same overcast afternoon as everywhere else, but it arrives secondhand, bounced off ceramic scales, so the color is a little deader, a little more like the inside of a wrist. Dust settles in the slit and does not all get shaken out. The clatterers comb the exposed runs and leave this crease alone. It is not profitable. It is only quiet in the local sense, which means the roar is one thickness of tissue farther off, and you can hear your own teeth.

I am in that crease because that is where the carried things end up. A peg of wood, swallowed down to its grain. A twist of fiber that was once a knot tied by a wrist with too much rotation. A flake scored and paved and scored again until the scoring is only a habit in the paste. I am not any one of those. I am what collects on them when the place has used them and not quite finished the sentence. If you put a hand into the slit you would find a warmth that is not the rind's and not the breathers' cluster-warmth either — drier, thinner, the heat of a count kept too long in the mouth. You might think it was a body wedged there and cooling. It isn't cooling. It is waiting to be legible.

For a long time I thought I was one of the foreigners. The posture suggested it. I had a front. I turned toward sounds as if sounds had a source rather than a weather. When the regional tension let go and the gust came, I braced the way a person braces, knees and a hand and a hope that the passage I entered by would still be a passage afterward. The spurred one passed me twice. The first time it did not look. The second time it clicked the flat triplet at the cable, received the glider's answer, and then, with the hand that holds the flake, touched the lip of the slit where I was gathering. Not a greeting. A test, the same test it gives a swallowed peg: is the logic still in the tissue, could a body rest here for the length of a breath. The flake did not score me. Its eyes — two, lidded, wrongly aimed — rested on the crease a moment longer than diet required. Then it went on into the warmer dimness, and I understood that I had already failed the test of being what it was. I do not travel. The platforms remember a walker. I remember the platform.

What I am, when the tissue finally makes me say it, is the offset.

You know it if you have put your ear to the breathers. Every vault has a tone. The vaults that once held a watching-wall hum a quarter-step wrong, and they have hummed it long enough that the rind grew a dish. I am not the dish. The dish is the rind's handwriting. I am the amount by which the tone refuses to agree with the next vault over. I have no armor, no ring of plates, no membrane. When a shelf slumps I do not ride it; I remain in the number the collapse was wrong by. When a mouth camps on a fissure and the gliders cut their phrase short, the warning is theirs. The small discrepancy afterward — the hum that does not quite retune when the seam closes — is mine. The mouths detour for the breathers because the breathers are information the system will not spend. They do not detour for me. I am what is left after information has been spent and the accounting is still open. A mouth can grind a cable into paste. It cannot grind the fact that the cable used to run a hand's-width to the left, where a peg of rain-wood held it. That leftover width is a place. I live in it.

It is not comfortable, and it is not nothing. When the clatterers pave a scored plate, they pave the mark and leave the interval. I am the interval, collecting dust the sheets do not bother to comb, firing nothing, repairing nothing. The climbers tick more densely along the old lines, and some of that density is me: a tension that is not in the fiber, a reason to hook where the hook meets no tip worth drinking. They do not know they are following a foreigner who stopped walking. They know the cable argues with itself there, and argument is where they drink. I let them. A straw in a soft tip, two straws, the loser unhooked and falling — none of that spends me. I am already the remainder.

The gliders come closest to spending me, and closest to keeping me. Their cries are routes. Most routes point at gas, at storms, at a mouth you should not land on. Some of the flat triplets point at a line of platform that is no longer a platform, and when they sound those, the roost is not only advertising a toll-place. It is checking whether the discrepancy is still there. If I have thinned — if a long run of collapses has remade the vault so thoroughly that even the quarter-step has been metabolized into ordinary tension — the triplet goes unanswered by the hum, and the gliders stop offering that direction. Exclusion, for them, is a death that does not need teeth. For me it would be a simpler death: agreement. To match the neighboring vault would be to finish. I have not finished. I sour the tone by the smallest amount I can sustain, a quarter-step and no more, because more would be a signal, and a signal gets hunted or followed or paved. A remainder gets kept, the way a path keeps weight in turf after the walker is gone.

You would find me, then, by listening for the mistake.

Not in the first hollow. The first hollow is still too busy being born; its tone has not had time to be wrong. Farther in, where the watching-walls are black spines inside the rind, where the ladders have three rungs or one or none, where the dust in the crease has lain through more than one pulse — there the hum snags. You put your ear to the cluster. The breathers pant. Under them, out of phase, a thin continuous note that is not quite the note you heard in the vault you left. You will want to correct it. Everything in you that came from weather with a horizon wants a scale that resolves. Don't. The correction is how the place digests foreigners: it makes them fit. If you hum the right tone, the dish shallows, the climbers loosen, the gliders drop that triplet from the map, and whatever I am becomes flour for the sheets.

If you don't correct it, if you stand in the slit and let the roar be one thickness away, you can feel the width I occupy. It is about the distance between a spur and a thumb. It is warm in the thin way, dry, sweet-metal only when a fissure opens nearby and the gas arrives already old. Sometimes the spurred creature comes back along the advertised line and tests the lip again. Sometimes it scores a fresh plate within my interval, a count of collapses or of returns, knowing the mark will not last. It does not need the mark to last. It needs the interval, which is me, to still be here when the paste has eaten the lines. We do not speak. It clicks three flat beats to the roost. I hold the quarter-step. The glider veils its eyes. That is the whole exchange, and it is enough like a name that I have stopped reaching for a better one.

The chamber will offer you a new version of itself before you finish crossing it. I will not be in the new version, exactly. I will be in the difference between the version you left and the version that received you — in the ladder that is gone, in the passage that became a wall, in the three paces where a mouth opened and the callus raced shut and the shut is a finger too thick. Look for the place that fits your stride and then doesn't. Look for the tone that almost matches. If you stay through enough gusts to stop bracing like a person, you will feel a small heat in the crease, patient, unfinished, keeping the count the flake can no longer make.

That is where I am. That is what I am. A width the underside did not invent, and has not yet agreed to close.
