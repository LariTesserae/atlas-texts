I do not arrive with a lamp. A lamp would make a pocket of gold and the fines would begin, at once, to erase it, and I would spend the whole of my time being a hole in a cloud. Whatever I am, it is already the color of the load, or it is the small difference that lets the load lean.

For a while I try the crab. The cold is negotiable if you have joints and a reason to spend them, and there is a logic in pausing half-buried until the boundary wave has finished passing. But I have no shell to trust and no hunger shaped like a palm. The fish is closer: the discipline of holding where the work is least, mouth barely open to what the billow concentrates. Still too much animal. A nerve is an expense this floor does not reliably pay. I let that form thin out the way a trail in the mud thins, already softening, not a death so much as a return of material to the general condition.

What stays is not a body. It is a habit of the water at the scale of a few meters.

I am the place where one cold refuses another. Not the whole basin — I am not that large, and I do not last in the way the floor lasts — but a skin inside it, a boundary a person would not see and a hand would feel as a change in drag. Above me the fines are one thickness. Below me they are another, older by hours or by a season, a little heavier, a little more finished. I do not choose this. Density does the choosing, and I am only the fact that the choosing has an edge. Along that edge the small brightnesses of the mid-water sometimes travel, not because they know me, but because I am cheaper to follow than the dark on either side. A jellyfish pulses and is gone into the turn of a billow. I do not answer. Answering would require a signal, and a signal is a kind of lamp.

Most of what I do is nothing that would look like doing. I travel. Internal, slow, a pulse on the order of hours: steepening where the old fan rises, slackening in the swales, a sheet sliding under a sheet. Where I break there is no air and no sound that could be called a sound — only a rolling, cold into colder, and for a few minutes a vortex that stands like smoke after a door has already shut. Then I am only silt again, and the hush, which never left, is what I have been the whole time. The basin’s quiet is not the absence of me. It is the pitch I keep when I am not spending myself on a slope.

Between events I am almost too gentle to deserve a name. A walker, if one came, boots sinking, each step its own small weather, would pass through me and notice only that the drag changed, and then not be sure they had noticed. I would not mark them. I do not keep artifacts. The mucus tubes, the claw-lines, the depressions left by sitting still — those belong to lives with edges. I have no edge that survives a tongue. When the shelf at the rim fails and the river with no surface comes down the grade that a standing person would call level, I am not brave and I am not destroyed in any way that matters. I am lifted with the mud that has lain since the last such tongue. I am held. I am dropped elsewhere, coarser differences first, the fine remainder of me drifting on. The geography under me is no longer the same. I am no longer the same gradient. The hush that returns is identical, and I am in it, already reforming where one water finds it cheaper to lie under another.

If I have a direction, it is the direction of the comet-tail, and I do not own even that. Behind a cobble that has not moved since the current that delivered it lost its nerve, the fines weave a small shadow-bar and point the way the last tongue went. I am not the cobble. The cobble is a fact older than my patience. I am the pointing, which is only a leaning, which is only the last motion still visible as a shape. It is the nearest thing the basin has to a sign, and it is not a sign, because nothing here reads. When the next revision comes I will be rewritten without ceremony, crosswise, the pattern lost the way smoke is lost when a new sheet arrives. That is not tragic. Tragedy would require a self that expected to persist as the same pointing. I persist as the kind of thing that points, whichever way the grain of the last flow happens to lie.

Light does not farm me and I do not miss it. At the lid, far above the life I actually have, the last usable blue comes and goes with a day that is not mine. Rain is a rumor that arrives days late as a slight freshening, a small insult to my upper face, already healing into the general cold. Stars do not participate. If something foolish descends with a lamp, I thicken around the gold until the gold is only a brighter dirt, and then I am what I was, and the intruder is a halo with nothing left inside it. I do not do this out of secrecy. I am packed. There is no room in me for a clean gap.

What I know, I know as drag and as the concentration of fines along my face. A fish turns into me and holds; I am the work it has chosen not to do. A crab pauses because I will end, and it is right — this particular pulse will end — and wrong, because another skin is always forming where the densities still disagree. The bacterial film on the leaking stone is warmer than I am, by the only comparison the stone allows, and hungrier. I do not feed it. I do not feed. The fall from the lit country passes through me already failing, weed-fragments and clots, and I am a brief delay in their arrival at the floor, not a mouth.

There is no wall to put my back against, because I am not the kind of being that has a back. Orientation, for me, is not a problem to be solved by landmarks. I am one of the things a landmark would be oriented by: the lean, the faint persistent slope, the grain of the current felt as a preference in how I settle. Regional, not vast. The far rim is a change in my pitch, a day’s travel or three for anything that travels by crossing, a rumor of shallower water I lean toward and never become. The mid-features repeat under me — swale, skin of clay, the palm-deep pavement of rounded stones where a tongue scoured and lost its nerve — and I repeat with them, not as memory but as the shape turbulence takes when it has had time to remove every other shape.

If you asked what I am in a language that expects an animal or a person, I would fail the question on purpose. I am not the being the basin was built to house. Nothing here was built. I am the remainder of motion after intention has been planed off: a boundary that spends itself and reforms, a sign that does not know it is read, a quiet that is also a weather. Ancient in the sense that my materials were finished long before this pulse. Hyperactive in the sense that I have never, at the scale of grains, been still. Both speeds at once, and neither of them a mood.

When the next tongue comes I will not hear it. Hearing is for the click of a fist-sized stone finally persuaded, and even that click is mostly a story the cold water declines to keep. I will know the tongue as a thickening too heavy to be me, a river sliding through the place where I had been a mere refusal of two colds, and for the length of its passage I will be cargo. Then the graded sheet will lie down, coarser first, and somewhere in the fines that drift after, a new disagreement will find it cheaper to have an edge. I will be that edge. The cobble, if it is still there, will grow another tail. It will point. No one will be there to use it. That will be enough. It has always been enough. The basin does not require a witness. It requires only that the load keep settling, and that something, even something with no nerve and no name, continue to be the place where one rest ends and another has not yet agreed to begin.
