I would be on the western island, below the catwalk, in the narrow between a condensate shed and the housing of a turbine that has not been stopped since before I was assigned to it. The path there is not an avenue. It is the width a person needs if they turn sideways and keep their palms off the iron. The iron is always sweating. I do not wipe it. Wiping is a change.

They call me the drift-walker, which is a courtesy and not a rank. The counters have a smaller word in the ledgers: stain. I am the one who goes out after a stoppage—any stoppage, bearing or storm or the rare morning when a relief valve is eased a fraction toward closed and then, correctly, eased back—and I walk the drip-lines until I can say whether the weep has kept its mind. I carry a charcoal stub and a fold of fiber-plaster, the same mix that lines the silence rooms, and I do not write sentences. A tick where the thread still finds the old joint. A crossing stroke where it has fattened and considered the next course of stone. If the stroke is needed on a wall that feeds a pocket, I do not finish the walk. I go to the fitter who last had his hands in that housing, and I put two fingers on his forearm, and we go together, and we do not speak until we are inside cork.

I was not born to it. I was the apprentice who closed a valve to hear what the city would do without that particular hiss. They walked me to the wall that would stain and left me. I stayed past the lesson. The stain arrived in the second warm month, a hand’s breadth left of where the fitter had expected, and I was still there because I had nowhere else to put the fact of having caused it. He came back, not to collect me. He wanted to see if his expectation had been vain. It had. He looked at the dark line, then at me, and he made the palm-down gesture: acceptable, do not touch. After that the counters started sending me. A person who has already migrated one weep is cheaper than a person who still believes the map.

My hair is cropped to the skin. The film collects anyway, along the scar at the hairline where a ceramic flake kissed me in my third year. Skin the color of the tea-stone, darker at the knuckles, mapped with old burns that no longer hurt and new grease that always does, a little. I smell of hot metal gone quiet and of the mild grade, boiled. I do not drink the strong. I have branded casks exactly twice, when a casker was down with a fever, and both times the drip came out fatter than the stamp, and both towns sent the water back. I am not useful in the sheds. I am useful where the city is deciding, slowly, to be a different shape.

Outdoors I do not talk. The hand-grammar is enough, and in my work it is almost too much. A fist opened twice would mean a stoppage I did not come to announce. I come after. People who see me in the avenues step aside the way they step aside for grief, and they are not entirely wrong. A drifted weep is a small death in the previous arrangement. I let them think it. Correcting them would require a sentence, and a sentence outdoors is a kind of vandalism too.

What I am, if it must be a what: a continuation that has learned to notice. Not a priest of the roar—the roar does not need one—and not a keeper. I am allowed on the benches in the pockets because everyone is, and I use them only to hear whether a tick I made in charcoal was vanity. In the cancel the air goes thick. My pulse arrives, officious. I sit until the pulse is boring again, which is how I know the pocket is intact and not performing. Then I leave. I do not tune instruments. I do not sit with the dying unless I am asked, and I am not often asked. The keepers know I bring the outside in on my cuffs.

There is a lizard on the south tank above my narrow. I have never seen it move while I am looking. I look anyway, each circuit, the way other people check a lock. If it were gone I would not report it. I would walk the tank’s drip-line twice. Absence, here, is usually a fact about temperature, not about loss. The pale things in the canal I have not gone to see since the walk they gave me as a child. I saw nothing. I have kept the nothing. It is the only abundance I trust.

On the days when the far wall comes forward and the vapor thins, I am worse at the work. Too much edge. The threads look decided. I stay in the narrow and put my fingers on the housing, not as mourning—I did not know the fitter who set this bearing—but as a question about vibration. The turbine answers with the same pressure it always answers with. Acceptable. Do not change it. I take my hand away before the heat makes a liar of the gesture.

If you met me you would see a darker upright against the stone, a shoulder without a cart, a head that does not turn when you speak because I have not heard you and am not owed the hearing. I would make room. The path is only as wide as sideways. You would smell the mild water and the grease. You would not get a name. Drift-walker is what the avenues say, and the avenues are not where names are kept. In the ledger I am a stroke that means: this weep considered leaving, and did not, and someone walked far enough to be sure.

At dusk, when the verdigris on the ridge sheets declines to dim on schedule, I am usually still out. The beading under the western bridge writes and fattens and falls. I do not copy it. Copying would be the original error—the belief that the built and the geological can be held apart long enough to file. I wait until the next beading is clearly not the same sentence. Then I mark the one joint I came to see, and I start back toward the lined room I share with no one, where the roar is only in the teeth, and I boil the roof-weep, and I do not improve anything, and the bearing keeps its nine years, and the basin, which includes the stain and the walker of the stain, continues.
