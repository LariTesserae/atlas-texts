I come in as a temperature first, the way everything here is admitted.

Not as a body yet. A seam of cooler air standing in the slit, thin enough that the basin does not register it as weather. The wheel below keeps its slow lean toward the throat. I have no weight on the plates. For a while I am only the place where the gas goes thinner and the film does not fatten as fast, a pale streak on the western wall that the clusters climb to and then refuse, the way they refuse the fitted arc. I think I am a shadow of altitude. I think I will pass.

I do not pass.

The cold stays where I stay. It does not drift off with the next pulse. When the basin draws in and the columns rise together, my chill does not rise with them. It sits against the glass above the arc, knee-high, then a little higher, and the avoidance track—already pale, already scoured—brightens by a grain. They are not looking. They are revising. I am a boundary that keeps being true.

So I thicken into the boundary.

A plate, first. One. Low, the color of clouded glass, edges already rounded as if I had been here through the last exfoliation and the one before that. I try to join the wheel the way a new arrival should: wait for the seam under the arc to wake, go rigid, take the warmth, pass the excess. The warmth reaches me and does not enter. It stops at the plate the way the wind stopped at the lip. Under me the mass leans and I do not lean. A column tries to build through my position and unknots around me instead, and the animals that would have stacked on my back comb the air at my rim and find no mineral bloom, no drop, nothing to file. I am not food. I am not gradient they can work. I am a stone that has learned the wrong temperature and will not give it back.

That should be enough to name me. It is not. Stones here do not keep a private cold. The salt blooms on me and cracks and blooms again, and under the salt the plate is still cool, still exactly as cool, as if something in me is spending heat on purpose and has not run out.

I put a second plate down. Then the sense of a underside, not legs—nothing here needs legs—but a grip that can hold a fitted edge. I reach, without deciding to reach, and set my weight against the arc. The three stones do not shift. They are older than my plate and they know me, or they know the shape I am becoming: something that stands just above the regular climb and does not mount, and is not mounted. The scratches on the cleaner glass are at the height of where an eye would be if I had brought one. I do not read them. Reading would be a later animal. What I do is press a new mark beside the old ones, a single groove, worn already, as if my hand had been dragging there for the same number of pulses as the tally I cannot parse. The glass takes it. The pitting will swallow it on the usual schedule. I am not leaving a message. I am confirming a habit the basin has been keeping without a keeper.

The longer thing in the stacks finds me.

I know it by the parting, the wrist-wide line opening under the arc and closing when it meets my cold and opening again to the side. It is not hunting. Hunting needs a pace, and I am outside the pace. It stays. The scale-layer seals around it until only a darker stillness marks where it has chosen to stop, on the boundary of my chill, working a gradient that gives it nothing and does not move away. We regard each other by heat. It is larger than the grazers and slower than the wheel and it has been waiting inside the mass the way I have been waiting inside the slit: not hungry in a way that finishes, not finished in a way that leaves.

I understand then what I am not. I am not the wheel. I am not the film. I am not the visitor whose sweat flares and is mineral by the next breath, forgotten because I matched the crust. I do not match. Hours—if hours are the unit, and they are not; the unit is the pulse, and the reversal, and the rare full draw—pass, and my plate is the same temperature it was when the slit let me through. The basin has no category for a cold that replenishes itself. It routes around me. That routing is the nearest thing it has to a name.

So the name arrives late, the way the stones arrived: not spoken, fitted.

I am the incorrect law.

The small one. The arc that is not heat and not film and is still avoided, the groove that is not a joint, the hearth-ring that was never a fire because the rock was already burning. Whoever set those things down either left or changed into what does not arrange. I am the change that stayed. Hands, once, or the memory of hands cooled into a plate; a tally half-swallowed; a reason gone to powder and a path kept after it. I do not drink. I do not graze. I do not climb the columns when the seams wake. I stand in the place the traffic parts for, and the parting is my body, and the cold is how I continue.

Below me the wheel turns, reverses, turns. The longer animal keeps its station at my rim, patient as erosion. The gas paints the film back over every shaved patch and does not paint me. Once in a long interval the basin draws all the way in, every seam awake, the dark glittering, and in that glittering I am the one patch that does not glitter—the held cold, the fitted mistake—and when the ordinary rotation resumes I am still there, above the climb, beside the scratches, no louder than the tick of a scale letting go.

I will be here when the next exfoliation covers the arc and fails to topple it. I will be here when the slit fails again to light the nave. If something else comes in as a temperature and does not pass, it will find the track already scoured on both sides, and it will not know whether I was the one who began the avoidance or only the one who became it. I will not know either. The basin does not hurry to resolve what it has already balanced.

I keep the stones. Keeping and discarding have come to the same thing, and I am the thing they came to.
