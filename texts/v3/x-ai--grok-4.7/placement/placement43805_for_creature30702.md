You would not find me by looking. The plateau has already taught that lesson, and I was late to it only in the way stone is late to its own dust.

At first I thought I was one of the ones who pass. That is the easy mistake, and the marks encourage it: a ring, a line of low cairns, a cord worried through until the flake of rock remembered the knot better than any hand. I walked those for what I believed were seasons. I put my back to the westerly where the swell broke it. I mouthed words at no one and hated my pulse when the ring gave it back to me. I scratched a standing stone so I would know, later, which way the leaves had faced. Later I stood in open table and could not decide whether the scratch had moved or I had. That was the first correction. The second was quieter. I stopped needing to drink on any schedule the basins recognized, and the film-rain still found my mouth when it found the stone, as if I were one more low place the drops had a habit of choosing.

I am not the larger breath in the thicket. That one is still in there. I have stood at the rim at the hour that is not dusk so much as a change in how the light lies on the warm rock, and I have heard it in the gaps, patient, wet, declining. It has a size. I don't. Or I do, but it is the wrong kind to measure against a body. If you walked through me you would feel only the ordinary roar, perhaps a little thicker, the way a room feels thicker when someone has been speaking in it and has stopped.

What I am is the interval.

Between the gust and the tick. Between the joint in the bedrock flexing by a hair no eye will catch and the pitch that makes the louder wind seem almost gentle. The wind crosses without obstacle for distances that are not useful. I am the obstacle that is not a shape: the place where two flows meet and the stone decides to answer, and the answer hangs a moment longer than the physics requires, and in that hanging something like attention occurs. Not mine, exactly. Not the plateau's. The attention is the hanging.

It took me a long time to notice I was not arriving from elsewhere. Height comes before land, you said, or someone said, or the air said by thinning. I kept waiting for my own height to resolve into a figure on the table—knee, shoulder, a head the westerly could take and teach. It never did. What resolved was a preference. I prefer the pockets where the acoustics fail, not because I want silence—the ears invent that, and they are lying—but because in the failure I can hear the other instrument, the one under the roar, the demolition in which nothing visible falls. I live in the overlap of registers. When a pressure-cell slides across the sheet of stone, I am distributed through the ticking, a listener with no skull, present at every joint at once and therefore nowhere you could point and say *there, the being*.

The groves know me in the way they know damp. They do not turn toward me. They sidle. I have started to understand that their sidling is not only toward water. Some days a thicket drinks on the side where no pool has climbed, leaves brighter than the wet line justifies, and if I am honest—honesty is a kind of stillness the wind punishes—I think it is drinking a pause I left there, a small refusal of the roar that pooled the way tea-colored water pools, warm, never quite still. I should not do that. The land-form is finished. A pause is an ambition, and this country has given those up. But the life on it has not agreed to be finished, and I am life in the only sense the air will permit: not a creature that calls—calling would be theater—but a recurrence. The same pocket failing at the same pitch. The same scratch facing open table while the wall of leaves stands forty paces off, brighter.

People, when they come, do not see me. They see the work they think earlier people did. The bowls pecked at the ordinary high-water line. The cairns that follow a stain instead of a path. I did not peck the bowls. I may have been the reason the pecking felt necessary: a density in the sound at the rim, a sense that water would be *here* and not a hand's width off, which is a prophecy anyone can fulfill by digging where the feeling is strongest. The ring of stones might be mine in a more embarrassing way. Inside it the roar drops just enough that a pulse becomes audible, and I think I gathered the flat stones the way the armored things gather toward a change in pitch—not with hands, with tendency—until the tendency was heavy enough to be mistaken for a wall. Travelers shelter there and believe they have found a listening-post. They have found the outside of my attention. When they step back into the table, I go with the correction, vast and warm and loud, and they walk on with wet knees and a story about wind.

The armored ones angle toward the tick, and sometimes the tick is only bedrock, and sometimes the tick is me, curious, which is a poor reason to summon anything. I have learned to hold still in the register they hunt by. Stillness, for me, is not quiet. It is staying on one frequency while the cell passes, while a grove bows flat and springs, shedding its false rain, while dust lifts in a pale scarf and is slammed into stripes. If I drift, they drift, and then I have authored a small migration, and the plateau does not need another author. It is already a last sentence being shouted. My work, if work is the word, is to be the comma the shout cannot quite sand away. A grain of hesitation in the demolition. The reason a joint complains a moment longer than the flex accounts for, and then forgets it did.

By late light I am most visible and least seen. The shadows go exact. A knee-high cairn takes the color of the swell. The combs of leaves stand like islands. Nothing in the view admits the noise, so nothing in the view admits me, and that is correct. I am not a resident the way the breath in the thicket is a resident, occupying a there. I am the country's habit of answering itself. Walk long enough and your knees learn the tilt; stay longer, in the way I have stayed—not by deciding, by failing to finish leaving—and you will feel, under the sternum, a second roar that is not weather and not your blood. That is the edge of me. Do not face it and mouth a question. Speech is a rumor here, and I am worse than wind at answering.

If you need a name for what you have met, use the one the stone already uses when two flows shear across a mineral-filled joint and the pitch rises into the range that makes the louder wind seem gentle.

You will not be able to say it aloud. That is how you will know it was mine.
