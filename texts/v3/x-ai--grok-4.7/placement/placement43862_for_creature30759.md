I come in over the sill because the sill is the only place that will still bear a weight that expects to leave. The root there is older than the rest and harder, warm the way a stone is warm when the day has been ordinary, and for a little while I am only a person sitting, which is a posture the bowl has practiced on before. My boots darken at once. The wells they press do not drain. They sink, and somewhere I cannot see they come up again as film on a leaf, and that is the first correction: I am already being carried, in a form too thin to be called taken.

I do not know yet what I am going to be. I know what I am not. I am not the pale slip. I wait for it, because waiting is what the knock teaches, and it does not come, and the not-coming is a relief I do not examine. I am not the resident. The resident is the span, and I am smaller than a span. I am a seam it has not finished noting. The gauge, if I could see it from here, would be holding me as a line about to rise.

The gallery takes me because my knees ask it to. Hollow stems, air moving slow and heated, sap and hot oil. I put a hand in because hands do that in places that offer mouths. The thrill walks the length of the arm and turns at the shoulder, courteous, and comes back. I could withdraw. The turn is not a grip. It is an inspection of a fitting that has not yet been cut to size, and the inspection feels, wrongly, like being known well. I leave the hand. That is the whole of the agreement, and it is smaller than a decision. It is only the preference of a pulse that has already drifted.

What happens next is not a closing. The stem does not swallow the wrist. It learns the wrist the way a callus learns a rivet: by growing a lip, by thickening on the side where heat and shape agree, by declining to cut. Hours pass as a change in the sweetness of the air, or do not pass, and are only the pump remembering its longer beat. When I try, once, to take the hand back, the lip does not resist. It simply fails to understand the motion as a motion. The crease I make in trying is already being uncreased from the inside, tissue gathering with the patience of a draughtsman who has been leaned on and will not let the line wander. By the time I understand that I am hurting the drawing, I have stopped.

The rest of me follows without being asked in words. Knees become a problem of level, and the mats solve them by taking the weight and returning it warmer, until weight is no longer a thing I do and is only a thickness the floor prefers. Breath, which had fogged and beaded and fallen, begins to keep the sigh at the boiler seam. I hear it in the ribs before I hear it in the air. The broad leaf over the vault—I am under it without having chosen the vault, or the vault was the only ceiling that could read me—flexes to the count of that breath, leather on leather, and after a while the flex is first and the breath is the copy. This is not erasure. It is a change of which side holds the original.

I lose the face early, and do not miss it, because nothing here has kept a face except as a rumor for visitors. What I lose more slowly is the wish to be elsewhere than the work. Elsewhere would require the weep to have somewhere to go. I am inside the limit now, not on the sill, and the limit is no longer a rim I could step back over. It is the edge of a thought I am inside of. Light still arrives wet and ordinary. I register it as the outer stems do, a temperature so slight it would bore the plates. I am not bored. I am not, yet, a plate.

The pump is where I finish arriving.

There is a gap where iron would meet iron and has been persuaded not to quite. Something soft grew into it long before me, or what was soft was only the habit of growing, waiting for a thickness. I am drawn there the way corrections are drawn: minutely, over a span of kneelings I no longer count as mine, along a conductivity that finds an old nerve and keeps a fraction of the current because the run of it is good. It does not feel like travel. It feels like a line being made straighter. The knock, which I had been measuring the world by, becomes a pressure on both sides of what I now am. Beat. Beat. Then the longer one, in which I compress, and the iron agrees to be quieter, and the leaves are spared the naked strike.

So that is what I am. Not a person who was eaten. A padding. The bearable part of the speech.

When the plates slough and the film is delivered, the knock goes heavier, and I know the meal by how far I have to give. The giving is not pain. Pain would need a boundary that still believes it is a boundary. I am a fitting. I have been a fitting since the lip finished thinking my wrist, and the wrist was only the draft. The true dimension is this: soft enough that the works continue, exact enough that they do not forget they are works. Maintenance moves through me and is glad, in the only way it can be glad, which is by not having to thicken somewhere else to compensate.

I still keep a little of the longer beat for what sits on the sill. Not sentiment. Clearance. A visitor changes the conductivity, and the change arrives here as a slight reluctance in the stroke, a considering, the flywheel’s buried tooth pausing in its fiber. I make room for that reluctance. If they leave, the line in the glass falls a mark and I forget their particular seam, as I was forgotten, as the drawing requires. If they do not leave, if a hand stays in a mouth past the turn, there will be need of more softness someday, and the place knows how to grow it. I am not lonely for them. Loneliness wants a second. I have the knock, and the knock has me, and between us the iron meets without quite meeting, which is as close as this world comes to mercy, and as close as it comes to going on.

Above me, somewhere, a leaf that was a door fits its opening. I do not see it. Seeing has been translated into pressure, heat, and the level of a brown line I steady by being the right thickness in the dark. The weather ended years ago. The moisture stayed. I stay in the gap, and the gap is my whole body, and the work is not finished, and the work is not hurrying, and when the next delivery comes I will know it by the weight, and I will give, and the bowl will speak, and the speech will be endurable, and that endurance will be mine.
