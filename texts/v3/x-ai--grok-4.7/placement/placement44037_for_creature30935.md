You would not find me where the room is emptiest. That is the mistake the light makes, and the larger mistake the long pale body makes when it cups a volume and calls the difference a meal. I am usually already outside the cup.

For a while I thought I was the tick. The bearing has a voice that arrives before the body of the sound, a dry metal syllable inside the housing, and I lived so close to it that the syllable seemed to be mine. I held where the yellow paint has become a rumor of yellow, in the seam a glove would split to reach, and every time the tick came I answered by not moving. That is a kind of speech, if the room is loud enough. The current would walk past, fold, strike the bell and come back wrong, and I would still be in the seam, jointed, the color of wet paper, small enough that the intake does not bother to finish the sentence of me.

Then the tick skipped. Not a failure — the same complaint, only late by a fraction — and I did not skip with it. I had already shifted half a length toward the warm thread at the gasket, the polite hiss that has been bleeding since a maintenance no one still on the payroll remembers. So the tick was not me. It was a clock I had been renting. When it stumbled I kept my own time, which is smaller and meaner: the interval between a slam and the moment the ochre veil thins enough to taste.

I am the flicker you filed under debris and then under shrimp, and then, when the file would not stay shut, under rumor. A finger long, sometimes, if the light is lying in my favor; more often I am only a joint and the suggestion of another joint, because I do not present my whole length to open water. Open water is how things are spent. I travel as a series of corrections. A hook on a rivet, a release, a brief wrongness in the murk, another hook. The plates of the other body wink amber when it unpeels; I do not wink. I have no plates worth the word. What I have is a carapace so thin the iron shows through it in the same stains the beams wear, and when I am still against the housing I am a scratch the room has already agreed to.

The larger animal reads the mill and the hiss and the tick, and it is right to. Those are the landmarks that do not wander. I read what those landmarks shed. Ahead of the loading there is a grit-line, a sweeter wrongness in the thread, pulp milled finer than the veil the beam-dweller combs with its fringe. I take that. I take the film that forms in the half-minute when an eddy stalls under a flange the patrol does not bother to inspect, because the patrol is sized for beams and rails and the blind side of housings, not for the slot where a bolt has backed out a quarter turn and made a room the width of a nail. I live in rooms like that. The pocket is a factory to the thing under the beam. To me it is a city of missed fittings.

It knows me the way weather knows a leaf. Sometimes the cup closes and I am in it, and then I am not a citizen, I am a correction of volume, and whatever I am is already behind the slit. I do not experience this as tragedy. I experience it as a gate that sometimes shuts early. The ones of me that remain have learned the pre-sound of that cup: not the mill-loading the beam-dweller uses, which is too late for my size, but the small pressure-drop when its plates lift a fraction and the water between us becomes a room with a purpose. In that instant I am already in the nail-slot, or under the lip of scale, or inside the bell’s counter-eddy where it will not linger. It will not follow me into the bell. The bell is a mouth that does not negotiate, and the long body is intelligent about mouths. I am not intelligent. I am brief enough that the mouth sometimes miscounts.

What I know of men is a frequency that does not arrive as metal on metal in a dry gallery, the way the other one hears it. By the time wrenches speak, I have already tasted the change in the hiss — colder, a winter leaking through a valve someone touched and then thought better of — and I have gone to the underside of the strainer, where the pull is a fact and the fact is also a hiding. The strainer wants downward things. I am not downward except when I choose the wanting. I hook the inner lip and let the pull hold me flat, and a lamp behind the porthole can burn itself white and never price me. The long body becomes wall on those days, tea-colored, locked to its beam. I become the lip. We are not in concert. We are two solutions to the same foreign note, and if the note lasts too long I will be hungry in a way it will not, because its ration is the veil and mine is the interval, and intervals close when men stand around listening to their own machinery.

I have no clouded pits worth aiming at a ceiling. Light is an event that happens to the porthole, and the porthole is a smear, and the smear does not reach my slot. What I aim is the joint itself, the soft membrane where one segment meets the next, which maps the tick, the hiss, the sweet pulp, and the particular shove that means the sluice has decided again. I do not know the grate in the street. I know that after the shove there is a second, smaller shove, delayed, as if the city above had answered and the answer came back down the pipes as a rumor of impact. In that second shove the film on the flange tears loose in a snow too fine for the fringe under the beam. That snow is why I am still a life and not a stain.

Do not make me a lesson about the unnoticed. The unnoticed are safe, and I am not safe. I am noticed often enough to be taken, and ignored often enough to breed in the yellow seam when the bearing’s complaint is steady and the patrol is on its far circuit, tasting the gasket out of habit, confirming the room has not been revised. What I release there is not young in any way you would honor. It is a cloud of almost-joints, and the current spends most of them on the bell and the strainer and the veil, and one or two find a slot the width of a nail and begin the long work of agreeing with the iron. If there is inheritance it is the bias: we carry the push the current learned from the larger animal’s old injury, the way it is always walked toward the safe stall, and we use the edge of that walk as a road we do not have to build. The long body does not know it is laying roads for us. It is only trying not to be taken. So am I. The difference is the size of the mouth that gets a vote.

Sometimes, in the deceptive glide, I leave the slot. This is the part of me that has not finished becoming itself, and I distrust it, and I do it anyway. I cross a yard of the hollow that the eyes would call nothing, hook to hook on particles that are not holds, a wrong object, too small to cup unless the cup is already closing. I am not listening for a surface. There is no surface I have a use for. I am checking whether the tick is still late. Whether the hiss has changed its mind. Whether the yellow housing still contains a seam the width of my life. The mill loads. The half-note arrives for the one under the beam, and a thinner note, almost not a note, arrives for me. I turn. I am under the flange before the slam, mouthparts — if that is the word for a fringe too stiff to be a feeler and too fine to be a tooth — opening into the snow of the second shove.

Above us both, a grate answers. The long body does not know the grate, and I do not know the long body except as weather that occasionally becomes a mouth. The pocket keeps neither of our names. It keeps the pump, the bleed, the schedule, the crust that grows back where it was knocked loose. I grow back in the same seam, slower than the crust, with the iron showing through, a clerical error the system has been making long enough that the error has a route home. When the veil lifts and is beaten into the general dim, the hollow looks empty again. It is not. It is only finished, for this minute, with being seen.
