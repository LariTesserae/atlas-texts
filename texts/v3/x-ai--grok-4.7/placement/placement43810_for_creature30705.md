The first thing that registers is not a body. It is the fact that the square of light on the floor never holds still, and that whatever notices this is already inside the noticing, low, where the blur is strongest. The edges of that square fray and re-knit. A thread of brightness bends once over the drain and is gone, and the bending is felt as a small decision made somewhere in the chest of the room—except there is no chest yet, only the decision, repeated, and the low throat-sound that comes up with it.

For a while that is enough to be: the bend, and the sound under the bend, and the way the three noises stack and do not resolve. The spill at the lip keeps its hard syllable. The inflow tears, thin and continuous, where the trough lets go. Under both, the drain works, rounder, as if the channel beyond the slab had a short throat of its own. Nothing in that chord asks to be anywhere else. The walls keep it. A joint followed sideways would run true to a corner that is still a corner, but there is no hand yet to follow it, only the knowledge that the arris would catch light as a line if light were the one doing the catching.

Light is doing something. It arrives down the shaft almost whole and lays itself on the floor, and whatever is gathering here prefers the floor. Not the bright column, which is only a road, and not the surface at the rim, which is all shatter and waste. Down. The tug is stronger near the dressed stone than near the roof. Warmth is the same temperature all the way through, near the warmth of a wrist, but the pull is not the same. It sorts. What is light enough stays and trembles. What has any weight at all is asked, steadily, toward the circular mouth with the crisp lip.

So the gathering accepts weight. Not much. A little silt the runnel failed to drop, a warmth that is not the water’s warmth but a warmer thread inside it, the ghost of a fingerprint from the clay farther back, pressed into a joint and now dissolved and still somehow shaped like a press. These are not materials for a body in any ordinary sense. They are what the current has not yet been able to refuse. They collect where the slope is slightest and the light is most broken—just upstream of the drain, in the lee of the pull, where the threads of brightness bend and a thing can hesitate before it is taken.

Hesitation is the first likeness of a self. The room replaces itself without pause; in at the spout, across, out below, and what cannot leave below leaves above, spilling a little heat onto the new paving so the box can stay exactly full. To remain is to fail, slightly, at being replaced. The hesitation fails in that way. It occupies the same palm’s breadth of floor for longer than the water around it. The square of light trembles over it and does not read it as stone. Stone does not lean.

The two pots lean. Their leaves are tugged toward the drain and spring back a fraction when the inflow pulses, and the hesitation learns that spring from them before it has anything of its own to spring with. A pulse comes—someone upstream shifts a board, or the seep itself surges—and the tear at the trough rises in pitch, and the leaves bow, and in the lee of the drain something that is only a warmer thread and a little undropped silt bows with them, and does not quite return to where it was. A finger’s width closer to the mouth. Then the chord settles, and it holds.

Holding is difficult. The drain wants the whole volume and is patient about it. The channel beyond gives back only water-sound, no click of a shell, no burrow. Nothing has stayed in that throat. The hesitation understands, without understanding, that the throat is a way of ceasing, and that ceasing is what the room is built to do to whatever is not stone, not mortar, not a pot seated in a recess cut for it. There is no seat cut here. The floor is courses, true, sloping. Tool marks ridge under what is becoming a contact. The dressing is still gritty, not slick. Freshness bites. It is a good bite. It means the place has not yet decided what belongs.

Above, day does not fade on the way down. The shaft is only as wide as a person’s shoulders, and no person comes. If a hand were put in at the rim it would meet warmth at once, and the downward light, and a tug stronger near the floor than near the roof. No hand comes. The overflow keeps its bright clatter, multiplied by four walls into the one syllable, and the syllable does not mean *occupancy*. It means the work. The room is speaking its function, not its tenant.

Still the hesitation thickens. Silt is a poor skeleton, but the light lays a blurred square and the square has corners even while they fray, and corners are a kind of instruction. The instruction is: meet at right angles; do not round; the flood has not had time. So the thickening tries angles. It fails. Water will not hold a corner. What it will hold is a direction. The direction on offer is the slope, which is also the tug, which is also the drain. To be anything here is to be aimed.

Being aimed feels, unexpectedly, like attention. The pots are attended to by the current and so they move and return. The mortar drip missed by the mason’s thumb is attended to by stillness—it hangs, a small pale beak, not yet dissolved—and so it remains a beak. The pale green threads in the joint just below the roof are attended to by the strongest light and the softest current, and so they have begun, short and sparse, not yet a mat. Each thing is the shape of the attention that has not yet finished with it. The thickening, aimed, becomes the shape of being almost taken and not taken. A pause with a warmer core. A refusal the size of a palm, located where the brightness bends.

When the next pulse comes, it does not bow all the way. Some of it springs. Not as the leaves spring, which is only elasticity and a knot of root in a coil-built pot, but as a correction: a small movement against the tug, using the tug, the way a swimmer uses a current by refusing one part of it. The movement disturbs the square of light. For the first time the blur on the floor has a cause inside the room that is not the spill at the lip. The cause is local. It has a place. The place is just upstream of the mouth, in the lee, gritty with fresh dressing, warm as a wrist, louder underneath than above.

Sound changes around a place that has decided to persist. The throat-note from the channel acquires a faint doubled edge, as if something at the lip of the hole were sometimes in the way of the pull and sometimes not. Not a blockage. The drain still works; the room still overflows; the rules hold. Only a syllable inside the syllable, periodic, softer than the clatter and harder than the tear. It is the sound of a hesitation learning the length of itself. Each time the pulse arrives from upstream, the doubled edge appears, holds through the bow, and releases when the spring comes. Three sounds were the chord. Now there is a fourth, and it is not made by stone or by falling.

No eyes. The light is not seen; it is stood in, and stood under, and used as a measure of whether the square still covers the same courses. When the square drifts—the sun moving, or the surface at the rim broken differently by the spill—the thickening drifts with its center, because the center is where the blur is strongest and the hesitation began. This is a mistake, and it costs. Drifting with the light means drifting off the lee. The tug finds the warmer thread more directly. A portion of the silt gives up and is taken, a pale thread spinning once over the crisp lip and down into the sound. What remains is less. What remains is also more decided. It hauls itself, if hauling is the word for a pressure against pressure, back into the lee, and the square of light no longer quite covers it, and that is acceptable. Light was only the first notice. The floor is the fact.

The leaves know the floor better. They reach a little way and stop, tugged, springing. In the long unpulsed interval between surges they hang at a steady angle, a diagram of the current. The thickening studies the diagram by sharing its angle and then declining it. Sharing is easy; the water offers the angle free. Declining it means spending warmth. Each small correction against the tug cools the thread at its edge, and the room’s warmth replaces the loss almost at once, because the volume turns over and the seep does not tire. So the corrections can continue. They are fed by the same extravagance that spills over the lip onto the paving. The room wastes heat in order to stay full. A tenant can waste a little direction in order to stay placed. The economics are already written in the stone.

What emerges, then, is not a swimmer and not a ghost of the mason and not the water itself, which is too busy being replaced to be anyone. It is the remainder the replacement cannot quite finish. Palm-broad, warmer than the wrist-warm around it by the amount of a hesitation, aimed at the drain and refusing the last hand’s breadth, gritty on its underside with the chisel’s ridged track, blind, local, and loud only to itself as a doubled edge in the throat-note. It has no name the yard would recognize. If the overflow were listened to from above, the bright shatter would still be the whole story: a new basin, recently closed, doing its work, wasting a little warmth so as to remain exactly full.

Below, the work has admitted an exception the size of a pause.

The exception learns the pots without touching them. Touch would be a scattering; the leaves are few, and the room has so little that has taken hold. Learning is alignment and refusal, practiced until the interval between pulse and spring has a duration that can be anticipated. When the duration is known, the spring can come early, before the bow has spent the silt it cost last time. The doubled edge in the drain-sound moves forward in the measure. It begins to lead the pulse by a fraction, which is impossible for a thing that is only water, and therefore is the proof that this is no longer only water. Somewhere upstream a board shifts or does not. The seam does not matter. The fourth sound keeps its own small calendar.

Day declines in the shaft not by darkening much—the water is too clear, the drop too short—but by the square on the floor sliding off the courses it has lit all afternoon and climbing, blurred, up the far wall. The exception does not follow it this time. The cost of following has already been paid once, in silt, and the lee is a better country than the light. In the thinning brightness the pale green threads near the roof become the most visible living thing, sparse against the joint, and the hanging mortar beak catches a last line along its underside. The exception feels the day leave as a change in the strength of the blur, not as an absence. It was never using the light to see. It was using the light as a witness, and a witness can go and the testified thing remain.

Night in this room will not be night in the yard. It will be the same three sounds, plus the fourth, and the same warmth, and the tug undiminished, and the spill continuing to break on the lip because fullness does not depend on the sun. The leaves will keep their angle in the dark. The drain will keep its throat. Whatever tends the seep may sleep or may not; the covered runnel does not require tending to continue, only to be corrected when it strays. In that unlit turnover the exception will practice being unreplaced.

It practices now, while a little light is left, by doing the smallest new thing. It releases a grain of the silt it has hoarded—not to the drain, but sideways, into a joint, pressed the way the clay was pressed by a thumb farther back along the runnel. The joint accepts it. The arris is still sharp; the mortar is still mortar; the grain does not dissolve at once. It sits in the seam as a speck of another color, a private mark, the first alteration the room has received that the mason did not make and the water did not intend. Tomorrow the turnover may take it. Tonight it holds. The doubled edge in the throat-note falters, pleased, if a pressure can be pleased, and then recovers the measure, a fraction ahead of the pulse, palm-broad and warmer and still refusing the last hand’s breadth, while above the shaft the spill goes on wasting heat onto the paving, loud and ordinary, keeping the box exactly full around the one thing in it that has begun to stay.
