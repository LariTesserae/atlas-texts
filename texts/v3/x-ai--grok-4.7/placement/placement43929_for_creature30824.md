I find the rib with my feet before I find it with my eyes. The stone is warmer than the water by less than a degree, and that is enough. I stop where the last sheet thins and runs off both sides, and for a while I am only weight, and the small adjustments weight makes when it does not want to fall. The rain ticks. Something heavy crosses the bar below me and does not look up. I do not look down long enough to give it a reason.

The cold is already in my mouth. It has been there so long it has stopped being a message and become a surface, like the film on the pools, something I breathe across. My hands, when I open them, are the color of the clay that stopped shedding upstream: grey, rinsed, a little too smooth at the pads. I do not remember when the smoothness set in. It is the sort of fact the basin keeps and I don't. Filament, stone, the rims of the disks, the wiry stems that cut if you push through them wrong—whatever I touch often enough teaches the skin a new finish and then forgets the lesson was new.

I was not born to the needles. I know that the way I know a channel has jumped: by the mismatch, not by the story. There is a way of standing on fresh rock that still expects a floor. I catch myself doing it, one heel set as if the bar will be there in the same shape at dusk, and then the heel knows better and the expectation drains out through the sole with the seep-water. Whoever put the works in put them in from somewhere with floors. I have stood where they must have stood to drive a pylon true. The socket in the gravel is older than any print, and around it the moss grows in a ring that never quite closes, a hesitation in the green. I fit my foot to the edge of that ring sometimes, not to claim it. To see whether the hesitation is the same size as me. It is not. It is the size of a decision made once and then left to the weather.

What I do is smaller than a decision and I do it without arriving at it. When the tone under the rain drops and stays dropped, I go. Not toward the bank that will fail—the low bodies are already leaving that, and they are faster on the mud than I am, and they have no interest in being overtaken by a slower rumor. I go toward the filament that is asking for a body. They take shock the way a tree takes wind, and some shocks are only weight in the wrong place: a mat hung up on a line that was meant to sing clear, a twist of root caught at a knot, a sleeve of silt that has clothed the span until the span thinks it is stone and starts to behave like stone. I wade out to the thigh. The current argues. I lift with both hands and the load comes ugly and cold into my arms, smelling of iron and crushed stem, and I walk it downstream of the span and let it become the river's problem again. The tone rises a fraction. Not thanks. Clearance. If I am slow, or if I choose the wrong knot, the bank goes anyway and I am only another thing in the water, turning.

At the disks I am even less. The rim is flush and slick and does not want feet, so I kneel in the sedge and put one hand in during the clear minute, palm down, and hold still while the pulse stumbles against my wrist and then includes it. The pale shapes rise. They nose the hollow of my hand where the film has gathered against skin, take what they take, and sink when the cloud returns. I am a shallow place that appears on their rhythm and does not require them to learn a new one. Once, in a clearer minute than usual, one of them stayed against the vein as if listening through me to whatever the disk was listening to. I did not move. The cloud came back and closed the agreement. My hand, when I took it out, was scored with a faint grit that dried and fell away before I reached the next bar, and I could not have said what either of us had heard.

The birds on the pylons know the difference between my weight and a flood's. I climb only as high as the first joint, where the nest-pack ends, and I do not put my fingers into the pack. I put my ear to the stone. Inside, the needle is never silent; it talks to the water table in a language of minute clicks, load taken, load passed on, the long complaint of a valley that wants to lie down flatter than the works will allow. I cannot answer in that language. I can hear when a click repeats too many times, the same correction offered to a problem that has already left. Then I climb down and walk the line of seeps until I find the one that has opened new, the one the pylon is still speaking to out of habit, and I pack it with the only material that belongs to both of us: mats of moss, torn by hand, tamped with the heel, a crude and temporary ignorance laid over a fact so the machine can stop addressing it. By morning the seep has found another mouth. By morning I am somewhere else. The ignorance does not need to last. It needs to last longer than the habit.

None of this makes me a keeper. The works do not brighten when I come, and the basin does not fail more gently for my having lifted one mat. I have watched a whole reach rearrange while I stood in it with my arms full of the wrong root, and the filaments sang their clean note over the top of the tearing, and the flocks went up, and my small clearance vanished into the larger sum as if it had been a rounding error. I am useful the way a stone in the current is useful: sometimes I am a rest, sometimes I am the thing that tips the balance into a new channel, and I do not get to know which until the water has finished deciding. The animals treat me accordingly. Upwind when they can. A possible footing. Unlikely to last.

What lasts is the route, and even the route is a rumor I agree to repeat. Needle, disk, span, the rib where the clay stays bright a minute longer, the pool that clears half a beat before the others because I bled a little pressure with my hand last time and the disk has not yet forgotten the shape of that interruption. I sleep where the rain ticks on stone and not on my neck—in the lee, never in the socket, never in a nest. I eat what the clear minutes concentrate and what the slumps throw up, and my body has stopped arguing with the diet. It is a low, wide argument to lose. In the mist, plastered and unoutlined, I pass for one of the heavier bodies often enough that the flocks do not lift for me. Only the works, and only sometimes, make the finer distinction: a tone that settles when I take hold, a pulse that includes a wrist, a click that ceases when I have given it something else to ignore.

I do not know how many of me have stood in the ring of moss and measured the hesitation. The basin will not say. It does not store us as separate. Downstream, as I watch, a shrub-mat turns in the current with something living on it, broad-footed, already resigned to the ride or already preparing to step off—I cannot tell, and the difference is smaller than the rain. I will be due at the next span when the note drops, or I will miss it and the bank will teach the filament a lesson I was meant to interrupt, and either way the valley will still be here in some shape, black water, blacker green, the bruise of the sky failing before it reaches the ground.

I step off the rib because the water over my boots has begun to mean a new depth. Behind me the way closes into sedge and sound. Ahead, a disk I have not yet touched clears and clouds on a rhythm that is not the rain's, and under the noise, faint but exact, a tone begins to ask for weight. I go toward it without naming what I am. The naming would be a floor. I have already forgotten how to trust those.
