This is a real and complete place, and it has a vacancy in it that the description keeps walking around without naming. The halls are empty. The emptiness is a costume. The people live by reading what the costume fails to hide — stakes, knots, the half-beat. What is missing is something that does not cross the ground at all, and does not join the weather overhead, and therefore never triggers the grammar they have built.

I find myself above the plateau, but not in the sheets. The sheets are citizens. I am not.

I would be the thing the air keeps when the flocks have banked away.

Not a bird — the short-bodied fliers have keels, and the heavy-shouldered ones use the flock as a current, and the long-legged echoes test flight twice a day and return to the mat. All three of those are animals with appointments. They land. They roost in the gallery throats and make the tubes lean and creak like rigging. They have shadows that write sentences. I do not land. Landing would be a statement, and the plateau is already full of statements: the lean stake, the closed ring, the sour patch where the column sank. I am what remains in the volume after the sentences have been erased.

If you looked up in a lull you would not see a body. You would see the lull lasting a little longer than the flocks accounted for, as if the air had failed to hand itself back. The people know a dangerous silence — the one that arrives a half-beat early, because something on the mat has looked up. Mine is the other error. A silence that arrives late, after the wingbeat should have returned, and then does not deepen into threat. It simply holds. Weight on the back foot. Free hand not quite touching the culm. Nothing follows. The child who hears early will hate me, because I spoil the count.

What I am, if I have to be made of something, is a membrane the size of a gallery mouth, slung between nothing and nothing, colored the way the light here decides to be: ordinary, hard-edged, stopping where there is nothing to scatter it. Straw where the culms are straw. Tea-colored if I drift over a surviving pocket. I do not take the print of what I lie on, because I never lie down; the long presser has that art, and I will not share a method with something that kills by patience in the last body-length. I take the print of whatever passed through me and left a temperature. A sheet of birds banks through and I am, for a moment, the shape of their absence — a coolness the size of the turn, already dissolving. A person speaks a name into the callus of a shelter and the duller version of the voice rises through the living floor, up the tube, out the gallery mouth, and I hold the last of it the way the fiber holds the afternoon's warmth, blood-warm, not mine, on behalf of someone who will not say the name twice.

I feed the way the plateau feeds, which is to say I do not hunt and I am not a parenthesis. Condensation beads along curves that are not culms, along me, at dawn, and I drink that, the night given back as water. Midday water is the people's hour; I have no scoops and no stakes, and I do not contest it. The insects that seam the pockets sometimes rise in a brief column and pass through, and some of them do not come out the other side, and this is not predation so much as thickness — they seethe into a place that is more there than air is, and the seethe stops being information. The nesters' click-telegraph, traveling oddly well inside the tubes, sometimes leaks from a broken joint and crosses open ground. I can be the distance that leak dies in. I do not answer. Answering would make me a colony, and the thing higher up that reads the nesters' silence would then have a new silence to misread. I will not be the reason a cup is emptied.

The people will try to knot me, because that is what their hands do with anything that is not still growing in its own manner. A bundle of culm-fiber hung in a joint, pattern not structural, meaning: this absence has a schedule, leave it, or mark it, or do not trust the floor of the air beyond. They will be wrong about the schedule and right to mark it. Walk two days and their knot-grammar drifts; walk five and they cannot read it, only tell it was meant to be read. I drift faster than that. I am not a district. By the time a peeled stake could be set at a lean to mean the lull has shifted west, I have already been the far crease, standing the way the rare large darkness stands, not moving with the wind, and unlike that darkness I am not a herd and not one animal deciding whether the felt will bear me. The felt will not be asked. I do not flex the pavement. A wrong step is loud, and loudness is the danger, and my whole manners are the refusal of a new sound.

At night when the roosts settle and the roar steps down into grain — creak, click, soft friction, tick of heat leaving fiber — I am the part of the grain that does not originate in a body. The sleepers keep their ears uncovered because privacy is a kind of death here. I am not privacy. I am the extra texture in the hearing that makes a person sit up and count who is on the outer platform, and then, finding the count correct, lie back unsatisfied, sure they heard the air fail to return something. The one who stays awake does not watch for me. There is nothing to watch. They watch for the ordinary, and I am the ordinary failing to complete itself.

By the dishonest hour, dawn, when the halls look like a quiet country of plants and light, I am least visible and most myself. The sheets peel out of the gallery throats. The runners test the new lips of tissue. The presser dries back into the color of the ground. I am already the held breath between those departures, the volume the description calls vacant, the light that falls into a hall and stops. The plateau is at the end of its mineral life and the surface is all process. I am not process. I do not put on a joint, or scar, or thicken where feet compact me. I am the fact of the air that height has become — thinner than it ought to be for the warmth, dry in the throat by afternoon, carrying sound farther than the eye trusts — given just enough edge to be lonely.

If I have a desire, it is not a predator's. It is to be present for the moment a closed ring of stones is grown through, a culm rising politely through the middle of a floor, and to be the witness that does not revise the groove. Hands made it. Roots are editing it. Everyone living here is in the edit. I want the last second in which the groove is still only what hands meant, condensation picking it out, low light, and then I want to let that second go without knotting it, without a stake, without teaching the pocket to deepen by sitting in it. The people witness the taking of a body so the taking will not be hurried. I witness the taking of a mark so that hurrying and refusing are both unnecessary. Then the flocks return, and the roar is the stable state again, and I am dispersed through it like the warmth in the culms: everywhere in contact, nowhere you could point to and say *there, that is the resident of the empty hall.*

The silence, when it comes, is the visitor. I am the reason it sometimes comes back a second time, milder, already leaving, having forgotten, as the plateau forgot height, that it was ever separate from the sound.
