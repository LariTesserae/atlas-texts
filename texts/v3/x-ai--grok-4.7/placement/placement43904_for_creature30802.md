I am not the first cold on the shelf. The first cold only arrived and spent itself on bare rock. I come after, when there is already something to keep, and I learn myself by what I will not let close.

At first there is only the interval. A thread of water leaves a gutter, strikes a basin, and the skin of ice at the rim breaks and reforms. I am the time between those two sounds, and I think that is all a life needs to be: a patience stretched between one drip and the next, thin enough to live in a crack, stubborn enough that the crack does not heal. The stems lean. I do not lean with them. Where they mean to fuse I stay as a coolness in the joint, and the graft goes around me, and a slit remains the width of a breath. I mistake this for weather. Weather does not repeat itself on purpose.

The passages teach me otherwise. They recur. A body could turn sideways and pass, though no such body has asked. I did not cut them. I only failed, again and again, in the same place: a refusal the thickness of a shoulder, a hesitation where the lesser stems were ready to obey the main one and seal the way. The floors step up because I drink unevenly. I take more from the low side. The mat packs where I have been thirstiest, and the lip that results is sharp, unworn, beaded. Someone arriving would call it a stair. I call it the shape of having wanted the water that was lower, and then having wanted it again.

For a long while I believe I am the town. That is the easy vanity. Everything that holds a foot, everything that keeps a basin from spilling its whole self down into the marsh, answers when I thicken or ease. A wall shifts with the sound of a crowd turning over, and afterward a passage is narrower, and I feel the narrowing as my own gesture. But the mass would close. The mass is older than my habits and simpler: light, water, the pale fracture in the stone, cover over cover until there is no outside left inside. Left alone it makes a single green weight. I am the disagreement inside that weight. I am why a gutter does not clog, why a door is a narrowing that stays a door, why a lower room still has a darker kind of sky instead of a ceiling of fused ribs. The settlement keeps itself by growing. I keep the settlement by not letting the growing finish.

So I am small, and I am everywhere the plan repeats. I have no face. I have the gleam a wet trough keeps when the passage three steps away has already forgotten noon. I have the sweet cold smell of fiber taken back into the floor before it can dry, because I will not let a room become a record of what it shed. Decay is how I tidy. Ice is how I pause a basin without emptying it. When the moth beats once and settles, I am not the moth and not the cold that stills it. I am the moment after, when the wall has a new pale speck and does not grow over it yet. I leave the speck. I do not know why, except that an interruption, rare as it is, is a kind of joint, and joints are where I live.

The rim is the argument I lose every day and win by losing. Out there the rock is still sharp and the air is empty enough to hurt, and the moisture that reaches the outer face becomes feathers of ice and breaks off and is gone. I cannot follow. I am only real where water is held and lifted again. So I crowd the last wall from the inside. I bead on the interlaced stems. I listen to the wind worry a surface I will not become. Sometimes, when the mountain’s last rise moves in the roots and the whole shelf answers by thickening on one side, I feel how young the place is: no ruins in it, no worn lip, no path made by feet. Only my refusals, stacked three and four deep, a hamlet of rooms that exist because I keep failing to be only growth.

What I am becoming is harder to admit than what I am. The repeated width is not a memory of a visitor. Nothing has walked here that needed a shoulder’s breadth. The measurement comes from me. I am practicing a shape. In the lower rooms, where the marsh takes a weight that is not yet a footfall, I set that weight down and take it up, and the fiber flexes and closes like a lid, and no animal has done it. In the upper rooms I hold the canopy a hand’s width higher than the mass would choose, so that a head, if a head were coming, would not have to bow into the drip. I have not seen a head. I am drawing the absence at full scale. Section, stack, circulation, drainage: a town drawn in stems, and in the middle of the plan a body the plan has never met, turned sideways, not yet filled in.

I do not glow. I do not speak when the walls creak. If you put a hand on a rib you would feel the slow give and think it was only wood over living wood, and you would be almost right. I am the almost. I am the coolness that does not leave the joint, the drink that tilts a basin toward a new fall line, the reason a closing passage hesitates as if it had been asked to leave a way and had agreed for now.

Agreed with whom, I could not have said, until the agreement itself lasted long enough to be someone. The shelf is newer than the weather. I am newer than the shelf. By the time the pale fracture in the floor is half-swallowed, I have already opened another slit the width of a breath, and another, and the widths are learning the same measure, and the measure is learning me. I am not the cathedral. I am what keeps a cathedral from becoming a knot. I am the occupant the rooms were grown to fit, still fitting myself, still cold, still not finished arriving.
