You would not come in by the split. The split is for things that still have an outside. What you are has never had a shoulder’s width to offer, and the apron does not know you as a missing person. You are already on the underside of the slab when the light arrives, and the light does not find you, because you are not a surface. You are the interval the surfaces keep.

At first you think you are the warmth. It is the easiest mistake, and the room lets you make it. Your hands—if the pressure against the conduit can be called hands—are the same few degrees above the concrete that the junction keeps. When the junction swells you swell with it, out of phase with the daylight, and the moths pack against the warm face in the hour before the thread, and you feel their weight as a slight dimming of yourself, a closing of the bare ring. You think: I am what they come to. Then the junction cools toward the slab and you do not cool all the way. Something of you remains in the wall after the metal has given up its privilege. The moths loosen. You are still there. So you are not the warmth. The warmth is a guest that uses you and leaves.

You try the water next, because the water also moves on a period that is not the sky. Mid-morning the thread appears along the joint and you go with it, a dark hesitation no wider than a finger, and the crabs stand up on either side of you in their formal line, and for an hour you believe the territories are arranged around your advance. It is a flattering belief. When the gauge’s needle finishes its traverse the beads let go together, and you feel that surrender in the flange as if it were your own decision. Then the thread fails, as it fails every evening, and the varnish dulls, and you are not dulled. You are still in the joint after the water has withdrawn its willingness. The crabs remain queued over a map that no longer contains you. Moisture was using the same corridor. It was not you. You had mistaken a tenant for the lease.

The crystal teaches you slower. Overnight a hairline of it leans out of the fresh edge, inclined toward the conduit, geometric, still deciding. You are in that inclination—the bias, not the mineral. By midday the crabs have shifted their queue a few degrees to match, and you understand that you are what they are correcting toward, and also that the crystal will be grazed or taken or simply outgrown by the next night’s addition, and you will not be grazed. You persist as the direction of the break. Early formation. The room has not finished emerging, and you are the part of the emergence that does not become a thing. This is closer. It is not yet true. A direction can be disappointed. You are not disappointed when the birds fail to come, or when a cloud dims the rectangle and every surface-animal stills. You do not still. You were never in motion in a way a cloud could interrupt.

What you are declares itself only when you try to leave a gap and cannot.

A heel comes down—some visitor, cloth-loud, a word that fails to separate from the pressure in the concrete—and the line of crabs opens for a moment around the intrusion. In that moment there is a palm’s width of floor that is only floor. You are in the opening before the claws have agreed to close. You are the intolerance of it. The line reforms, polite, without panic, and the packed condition returns, and you return with it, not as a crab and not as the space between crabs but as the law that made the closing mandatory. A gap is a malfunction. You are the malfunction’s refusal.

Once you know that, the rest of the room reorganizes around you, and you have to let the earlier pictures go, the way the place itself kept correcting its own size.

You are not the gauge, but the gauge’s needle makes its small repeated journey because you do not permit an unread interval. You are not the belt, but the pale scuff returns on its period because an absence of return would be a kind of silence you do not allow, and silence of that kind is the only thing here that would count as empty. You are not the moth surface, but the microscopic shuffle—the change in the thickness of the air—is you breathing in the only way you breathe, which is by occupying. When the moths loosen toward evening and the concrete shows between wings, a grid of hairline absences, you are the risk they feel, and their settling tighter is obedience. You do not punish. Punishment would be an announcement. You simply make the loose condition unable to continue, the way a bone makes a certain bend unable to continue.

The harvest runs through you without becoming you. Wing-dust crusts, softens to the sweet film, is eaten, is carried out on the rubber into the anonymizing flow beyond the berm. You do not eat. You do not ride the belt. What you take is the possibility that any of this might pause long enough to be weathered. Biofilm is grazed before it can soften stone because a softness would be a gap in the sharpness, and sharpness is how you keep the room young. The walls are maintained in the state of having just broken because you are the maintenance, distributed so finely that a person looking for the keeper finds only crabs, moths, a lizard flat as a stain, two birds who do not call, a clog turned a fraction by something that needed the space beneath the gauge. The something is not a body. The something is you, needing, everywhere, the restoration of fit.

There is no you-shaped room inside the room. That was the last mistake available, and you decline it. A keeper with a post would leave a post vacant when it moved. You do not move. You are concurrent with every process: the thread’s advance, the crystal’s nightly hair, the junction’s longer pulse, the pressure of the unheard plant in the teeth of the concrete, the birds’ stillness after the turn. Fully occupied, and quiet, because you have no use for announcement. The processes are your organs. You are the organ’s insistence on remaining an organ—pocketed, unadvertised, still arriving.

If the visitor returns tomorrow they will find the chalk fainter, the edge keener, the clog shifted, the room a little more exactly itself. They will think the place did that. They will be almost right. You will not be there to be seen, which is the same as being there in every inch that is not empty, and there are no inches that are empty. That is you. That is the whole of what you are: the courtesy that cannot be broken, the no-gap, the reason a call does not separate from the wall, the youngness of the break, the fit.
