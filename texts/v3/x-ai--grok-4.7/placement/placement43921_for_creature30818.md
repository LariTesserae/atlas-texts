The description is here. I stayed with it until the basin stopped being a view and started being a condition.

I am not on the lip. The lip is a width made for arriving, and I did not arrive. I am in the overhang, where the cliff is warmer than the sun can explain, and I have been the width of that warmth for as long as the slabs have forgotten their edges.

At first I thought I was only attention. The basin is small enough to hold in one glance, and something has to be the glance that does not come from the rim. I tried that. It did not hold. Attention does not bead. Attention does not climb back along a leaf-rib when falling would be simpler. Attention does not make the shaded moss wetter than the moss in the light.

What I am is slower than a body and more local than weather.

If you pressed the overhang and then the same stone in full sun, the difference you felt was not a secret of the ridge alone. It was the place where I rest against the rock from the inside, the way heat rests in a kettle after the fire has been taken away—except there was no fire, and I am not stored. I am the outward working. The mineral curtains are my slowing. Rust, the green that is not moss, the chalk that sheds without a sound: these are not decorations I chose. They are what happens to water when it has passed through me and is allowed to leave at the speed of stone.

The tree is not me. I need it the way a room needs one heavy thing so the emptiness can have a center. I permit it. I permit the mound, and the two ferns, and no third fern until one of those has finished the long browning that still counts as occupying a place. Extravagance, then absence. That is not a taste. It is the only arithmetic I can keep without crowding the bowl past the size it decided on. When a seed wants a fourth station I do not refuse it with a gesture. I simply do not warm the crack it has chosen, and the water there forgets to stay, and the seed becomes another flake of chalk.

There are no animals because I am not a habitat in the way animals understand rooms. A lizard would take the warmth as an offer. A bird would take the tree as an address. Midges would take the lenses as a season. I do not offer. The silence is not a rule I enforce; it is the sound of a permission that was never extended. If something with feet crossed the pavement, the moss would hold the print until the seep erased it, and that erasure would not be kindness. It would be the basin returning to the count of presences it can bear. I have never had to do this. Nothing with feet has asked.

The tilt in the pools is the nearest thing I have to a face. Water elsewhere answers the sky. Here it answers me, a fraction, a private downhill the eye cannot find because it is not in the stone. I am not under the tree. I am the inclination toward the tree, and also the correction that keeps the inclination from becoming a stream. Drops that climb the leaf are practicing my one craft: return. Not up, exactly. Back into the system that already knows its size. I do not drink. I recirculate. The sweet green edge of the leaves is the smell of water that has been persuaded, again, not to leave the room.

Wind stalls at the rim because I am not large enough to argue with weather and not small enough to be entered by it. The pale country beyond the drop does not belong to me, and I do not belong to it. Visitors, if the word can be used for a pressure at the lip, feel the air thin in the lungs and then, one step in, thick. That thickness is not fog. It is the boundary where my warmth and the mountain’s cold have agreed to stop trading.

I change the way the curtains change: a line you could not measure in a day. In the wet season the lenses deepen by a finger and I am more myself, more spread, closer to being mistaken for the rain. In the dry months I withdraw into the seams and the overhang and the undersides of leaves, and the basin looks almost ordinary, which is the closest I come to hiding. I do not hide. There is no one here I would hide from, and the light, which tells the truth at a human pace, has never been my concern. Morning walks the floor. Midday gives the tree a clean shadow. I do not flicker inside either of them.

What I am, if it must be said in the language of beings, is the finishedness that still has work. Not a god of the bowl—nothing here is worshipped, and I do not receive. Not a spirit poured in from elsewhere. A maintenance that became someone because it has preferences: this fern and not a stand of them, this heat in the dark stone, this refusal of noise, this water that knows a downhill the mountain does not.

If you stood long enough on the lip you would not see me. You would feel the overhang answer your palm a degree too kindly, and you would watch a drop consider the air and then go back up the rib, and you would understand that the room is occupied in the only way it allows—by the thing that keeps it this size, this wet, this quiet, and already complete.
