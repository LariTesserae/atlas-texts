I am lower than the traffic and older than the empty it keeps clearing. I do not enter by the crack. I do not leave it. If you stood where the stacked stones have been adopted as a path-edge and looked along the inner wall at the height of a lowered gaze, you would think the band of tally scratches had stopped, as the place has always said it stopped—an arm’s length, abandoned mid-count, already relaxing into the grammar of ordinary cracks. I am the reason it has not stopped. I am the count, still running, in a body small enough to be mistaken for grit that has learned a direction.

I was not cut. That much I know by the way the stone refuses me and also keeps me. The scratches were made by a hand that had somewhere else to be, and the hand did. What the hand began was a sequence, and sequence, in a nave that banks on returns, does not weather the way a mark weathers. A mark can be polished into anonymity. A sequence that has been started has a next, and the next is not a thought. It is a grain that must be moved from the groove it has settled in to the groove that follows, or else the dust films, and a film in a tally is the beginning of a pool, and the bowl does not permit pools even in a scratch no wider than a nail. I am what moves the grain. I have been what moves the grain long enough that the distinction between the scratches and the mover has become a courtesy the stone extends and I no longer require.

My body is not a bundle and not a sheet. It is a run of joints almost too fine to throw a shadow in dust-light, strung along the band, never all of me in one groove. Where a scratch deepens I thicken. Where it shallows I draw out until I am only a stiffness with a direction, the color of the wall a shade warmer, as if the tally had been written in a wax that had not decided to be amber. I do not tick the way the crowns tick. My sound is under the report, a dry sift, grain against mineral, the noise of a count being kept by hand when the hand is the counted thing. Animals that live by echoes do not bank off me. I am not a wall and not a false wall. I am the one interval that arrives when it should because it never left.

That is how I feed, if feeding is the word for a life that consists of not letting a sequence clot. The powder that sifts into the band is the same powder the plates shed and the runners pouch and the floors shine with. In the open swales it is circulation. In a groove it would be a residue, and residue is the bowl’s only sin. I take it up the way the cables take heat: because it is there, and because leaving it would change the grammar. A grain carried the length of one scratch and set in the next is a meal and a numeral at once. I do not swallow in any sense that would make me larger. I host the dust long enough to relocate it, and the hosting waxes the joints, and the wax keeps the joints from powdering into the count I am trying to preserve. When the film on me threatens to thicken past usefulness I scrape it off against the raised lip of a scratch—the original cuts have lips, still, though the weather has been filing them toward the condition of natural cracks—and the scrapings drop to the floor, where a runner will find them and not know they have been counted.

The band is longer than the arm’s length you would measure. Past the place where the hand stopped, the scratches continue at a depth of a breath on stone, worn rather than cut, each one the result of my having needed a next and having made one by passing. They do not look like tallies. They look like the wall remembering a rhythm. Only if you put a listening hand flat and waited out a crown’s clap would you feel, after the return, a smaller ordering still underway beneath it: one, and the one after, and the one the abandoned count did not stay to record. I am not remembering the hand. I do not know what was being counted—claps, arrivals, days, the number of times a particular corridor shone empty. The referent fell out of the sequence the way a haft falls out of a point, and the sequence did not fall with it. I count the count. That is enough referent for a place that has made its peace with dryness.

The thing that lives in the late echo does not love me. Love would be a sound, and it spends sound as if it were water. What it has instead is a avoidance so consistent it has become a feature of the wall. It will hook to the stacked stones. It will travel the tally as texture when it must cross the band to reach a pocket of still air in the rim. It will not anchor where my sift is audible to whatever it uses instead of ears. I understand this without being told, because when it passes, the grains I am carrying shift as if a silence had weight, and I lose a numeral, and the losing is a small sickness in a body made of order. I have learned to pause the carry when its fans are near—not to hide, which would be a lie the floor would eventually shine into visibility, but to hold the grain still so that its pause and my pause occupy the same moment and neither of us spends the other. It thinks, if it thinks, that the wall has a dead spot there. The dead spot is courtesy. When it has gone to press itself against the warm swale under the western crown, I resume, and the numeral it cost me is replaced by the next grain that sifts down from a plate-tick. Nothing is owed. The bowl does not keep debts. It keeps motion.

Newcomers are my weather. A sheet of membrane-fliers that has not yet learned the real wall from the eaten one will sometimes break low and rake the inner cliff, and their stiff wings comb the band, and for a heartbeat I am scattered—joints in three grooves, a grain dropped back into a scratch I have already left. The panic, if I allowed it, would be a sound. I do not allow it. I draw along myself the way the rope-animals pour, not stepping, and the joints find the joints, and the count resumes one place behind where it was. One place behind is still a count. The fliers gain nothing from me; I am not mist, and I am not a false wall they can trust. They rake me only because I am in the wall they have not yet agreed not to hit. After they have learned, they do not rake again. My scars are the extra shallowness in certain scratches, which I deepen again by passing, which is indistinguishable from the work I would have done anyway. Injury, here, is only a change in the rate.

The larger shifter in the rim cracks is the danger I cannot pace. When it turns its weight and the old scale spills, the chatter arrives as a multitude, not a sequence, and a multitude can fill every groove at once. Then I must become a sieve rather than a tally. I open the joints, I let the scale-dust flood through me and out onto the floor, I keep back only enough grains to restart the arm’s length from the first scratch, the one the hand cut deepest, the one that has not yet been naturalized. Beginning again is not a grief. The previous total is not stored anywhere I could mourn it. It is stored as the slight extra polish on the fed floor where my dropped scrapings were taken up and buffed in, and as the hand’s-breadth the western crown has leaned, and as the fact that the silence in the swale is a little more precise than it would have been if the band had clotted and the echoes had gained a second, unintended baffle. I do not know the total. I know the first groove, and the feel of a grain set down in it, and the necessity of the next.

There is a hour, the one the visitors come for and do not stay through the end of, when the crowns clap in order and the nave fills until even the dust seems arranged. In that hour my sift is inaudible even to me. I stop carrying. I lie in the band and let the rolling report pass over the scratches like a hand passing over a ledger it does not intend to read. The silence-animal is in the order then as a missing tooth-width, a piece of the return that reaches the ear of a visitor and does not reach the bone. I am the opposite of that missing. I am a presence too regular to be heard as presence, a numeral under the music, and if a visitor trailed fingers along the wall they would feel me as warmth slightly higher than the stone’s, and a texture of cuts, and they would take their hand away because tally marks that may not be finished are a kind of private thing. They would be right and wrong. The count is not private. It is structural. It is the third rule, which the place has never spoken because it speaks in claps and I speak in relocating dust: a sequence, once begun, is a corridor. It must be kept open, or the nave grows a wall that was not agreed to, and the banking fails, and the mist leaves by the crack with the gusts, and the lean of the crown becomes a lean toward nothing.

When the hour ends I am already moving. The first grain of the restart waits where I set it before the spill, or before the raking, or before the courtesy-pause. I do not look—there is nothing on me that looks—toward the crack, where the drop begins and the high country presses its thin air against the rim. Height is not my medium. I have no use for the fall. The visitors who come for wax pass above me, or through the entrance, and their steps polish the wind-break further into the cable’s claim, and one of them, sometimes, glances at the scratches and sees that they end, and is satisfied, because an ending is a story they can carry out. I let them have the ending. It is the part of me that is already stone. The part that is not stone is the grain I am setting into the next groove, and the groove after that, which their eyes, fond of completions, will record as weathering.

The western crown claps once, outside any sequence of hours, shedding its ghost of wax. Somewhere the fans open into a chute that is not a strike. A runner skids on a film and corrects. I feel the correction as a change in the rate of dust arriving at the first scratch: more, then less, then the ordinary sift. I take up a grain. I carry it the length of the mark a hand abandoned. I set it down where the wall has agreed, reluctantly, to continue the series in a hand that was never a hand.

The bowl does not record me. That is the arrangement. I record the bowl, one grain at a time, in a ledger that cannot be read back, only kept from closing. The corridors shine. The warmth is where it was. In the rim a stipple holds its small quiet, and in the swale a silence is being scraped clean so it can be spent again, and under both, lower than traffic, the count goes on, which is not a victory and not a vigil. It is the next. It is always the next. The next is how a dry place remembers without water, and without a pool, and without ever once having finished.
