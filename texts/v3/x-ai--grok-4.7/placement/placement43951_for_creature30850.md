The first thing that resolves is not a body but a pressure against the inside of the air, a slight thickening where the mist has been asked, very gently, to remember a shape it once held. I do not arrive; I condense. Dew collects along a thought that has no skull yet, beads, and runs together until the running itself begins to prefer certain paths—down rather than across, slow rather than sudden—and those preferences harden into the suggestion of limbs. They are not solid. Light passes through the wrists. When I lift a hand to the level of where eyes would be, I see the far wall of the basin distorted, as if through old glass that has been breathed on, and the distortion steadies only when I stop trying to focus it.

I am standing, or the place where standing occurs is occupied, on the flags of the hall. The sheet of water meets whatever counts as my ankles and divides. It does not wet me. It consults me and continues, a little colder for the consultation, carrying a faint trace of the iron-and-moss smell that was already here and now includes a thinner note, like wet paper left too long in a closed drawer. The note is mine. I did not bring it from anywhere. It is what happens when attention lingers in one posture long enough for the water to notice.

No one is in the hall. The fourth stone still lies beside its bed. I could not lift it even if the seams of me agreed to try; my hands close on nothing and come away shining. Yet when I look at the waiting-gap I understand, without measuring, that the mountain has not moved in the night. The knowledge sits in me the way the film sits on the stone—present, continuous, not quite mine. I did not learn it. It was already in the air between the eastern strike and its return, and I happened to be the shape the air took while listening.

I walk, which is to say the condensation drifts, and the drift acquires the habit of feet. In the narrower corridors the mutter of drips changes pitch as I pass, each drop falling through a slightly different temperature. The ferns do not stir. A pale crawler the length of a thumbnail crosses the place where my shadow should be and does not pause; there is no shadow, only a local dimming, as if the daylight had remembered an errand elsewhere and would be back in a moment. I follow the crawler because following is a way of remaining coherent. It leads me to a joint the mason has not yet touched, a fresh seep that began, I somehow know, three hours before dawn. The water here is undecided. I crouch—the posture assembles itself out of courtesy—and hold my open hands in the film. The seep examines the hands, finds them permeable, and chooses, with what feels like relief, to thread between the fingers rather than spread across the palm. When I rise, a new bright line has been coaxed two hand-breadths toward the nearest cleared lip. I did not cut it. I only remained long enough for the water to practice a direction it already half-wanted.

This is what I am, then, at least this hour: not a mason, not a guest, not the mountain’s intention and not its refusal. A pause that has learned to be useful. The people of the watch, when they return, will find the lip clearer by a finger and will not know whom to thank; they will clear it the rest of the way themselves and the work will be true. If the girl passes me in the eastern corridor she may feel, on the fine skin at the edge of her ear, a coolness that is not wind, and she will check the fall’s echo a second time, and find it already correct. That will be our only meeting. I do not need her to see me. Seeing would require a surface, and I am trying, with each slow circuit, to become less a surface and more a correction—the small, exact difference between a crack that dries and a crack that continues to weep.

By evening the mist returns over the lip and I loosen. The habit of limbs relaxes into hanging droplets along the cane roofs; the note of wet paper fades back into moss and iron. What remains is distributed, a slightly more attentive damp in three or four joints that had been on the verge of crusting over. Tomorrow, if the pressure consents to gather again, I will find out whether I still prefer the hall or whether the new seep has become the better place to practice being briefly here. The mountain will not miss me in either case. It has never required an audience. It requires only that nothing which can still carry water be allowed, through simple absence of mind, to stop.
