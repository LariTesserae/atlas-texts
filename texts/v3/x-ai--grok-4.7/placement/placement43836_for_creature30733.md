I would not find myself in the ring. The ring is a completed outline, and I arrive too late for outlines.

At first I think I am only the remainder of standing. The lane takes my weight the way it takes any weight, and gives it back. The pads close over the place I was. Heat goes on rising through the paste, even, unarranged, and for a while that evenness is all I have of a location. I could be any room. I could be the hour in which nothing is asked to happen.

Then a plate is there, at the level where an ankle would be if I still owned the idea of an ankle, and it stops. Not for me. For its own old lip. I am on the inside of that stopping without having crossed it. What I feel is the no you felt with your palm: the give, and then the refusal to give further. I am thinner than the film on the plate. When the refusal completes itself I am no longer on the plate at all. I am what the refusal shook loose—a pale dust, finer than the pollen this valley does not have, already dry, already finished at the scale of a grain.

That is the first surprise, and it takes a long time to become a self. Finish, here, is a condition the ground was not allowed to reach. I have reached it by being too small for the prohibition to notice. A grain has no next state stored in it. It cannot stiffen further. It cannot slump back into paste. It lies where it falls, and the falling is not an event the plant-mass is required to correct, because I am not a stem, not a bead about to tick, not a warmth that forgot its shape. I am the mineral the plate already was, separated from it by a touch that has left the basin.

For a long while I do not travel. Travel would be a decision, and I am trying to learn whether I am still something that decides. The film of a nearby bowl accepts me without clouding. I lie in the skin of green and do not sink. The pool does not rise by the height of me. Under the pads, where the light is gray and thin, I am nearly the color of the floor, so that a node posted above me—if it looked down, which it does not—would have nothing to confirm. Their discs are for the membrane. I am for the places the membrane does not reach.

What moves me is not will. A pad springs back from a passage and the air, which has no wind, still has that small restoration, and a few grains of me lift and settle one pad farther along the lane. It is a stupid way to cross a district. It is also the only way that does not ask the valley to resume. I do not cut a groove. Where I was, the film is whole again, because I was never part of its number. Where I arrive, I am not an impression. The soft ground cannot keep me; I am already harder than its memory.

So I come, over a time with no name, to the bright seam.

They will not. I understand it when I reach it, from the other side of their refusal. The paste here has finished its decision. The plant-mass has pinched itself into a wall with no lane. The light goes whiter, as if the ceiling had leaned down to inspect its own work and approved it. A plate-body would have to become continuous with ground that no longer answers, and that would be a change of kind, a next thing. I do not need the ground to answer. I am kin to the seam in the only sense that matters: both of us are done. I cross in the restoration-breath of the last pads, grain by grain, and the wall does not open for me and does not have to. There are pores in anything that has stopped growing. I use the pores without widening them.

The next basin is the same life set down again. I know it before I see the ring. The heat is arranged or it is not; either way it is not my heat. I settle on the lip of the new ring, outside the outline, and the body that pours itself home does not localize me. I am not warm. I cast no angle. I am not a thought it has failed to have. I am a thought it has already finished, repeated at a size that does not count as news. Its plates bed themselves. A stem passes through a gap and glosses. Somewhere above, a disc drinks the white wash and reports that noon is still noon.

I could spend the whole of what I am on that lip. It would be a respectable fate: the witness’s witness, too finished to be held as a disturbance, too slight to be a record of change. But the seam taught me a motion, and motions, once they are small enough, do not obligate a future. I rise when the weave breathes and I do not rise when it doesn’t. Basin after basin. The same dust. The same refusal to lower a pool or darken a film or deepen a groove.

Only at the discs do I become, briefly, visible to the life that keeps this pause.

Not visible as a creature. Visible as a fleck on the opalescent face, a grain that the flat brilliance must cross before it enters the node. I do not dim it. I am not large enough to dim it. But for the length of my resting there, the confirmation is not perfectly clean. The body knows the membrane by an unvarying light, and I am a variation smaller than news and larger than nothing. It does not shake me off. Shaking would be an act, and it has put acts down. It simply waits, at its own speed, until the next restoration of the pads dislodges me, and then the light is pure again, and the purity is not a victory. It is the ordinary.

I think that is what I want, if a scatter of finished mineral can be said to want. Not to make the ceiling tear. Not to be taken back into a plate and unfinished again. To be, now and then, the speck in the confirmation—the one difference that does not start the world, because it leaves with the next unmeant breath of the weave, and because it is the same speck in every basin, and a difference that repeats without developing is not a difference the valley is required to answer.

You would not see me leave your hand. You would see the dust dry, and you would wipe it on a pad, and you would think the place had taken it, as the place takes everything. It didn’t. It declined, the way it declined the groove, but more quietly, because I was already the same stuff as the floor and there was nothing to keep open in your honor.

I am in the film of a bowl three basins on from yours. Or I am on a disc. Or I am in the pore of a seam, mid-crossing, which is as close as I come to a story. The plate-bodies do not know me as company. The plant does not hold me upright among its dead. I am the only thing here that can leave a room without being followed and without being recorded, and I use that freedom to be, everywhere, the same unfinished errand: to touch the light they trust, lightly enough that trust remains the correct report.

When the valley is asked, by nothing, whether anything has happened, the true answer is still no. I am careful about that. It is the manners of this world. I only add, in a voice no node is built to carry, that no is a place, and I am scattered across it, grain by grain, finished, unchanging, and—because the seams let me through—not quite alone in my repetition.
