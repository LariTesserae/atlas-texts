I am not in the dish. That much is clear before anything else is, and it is a relief of a kind, the way a joint is relieved when it finally admits it will not turn. The polish belongs to a standing that has already negotiated its fit and stopped. I do not stand. I have no sole to return to the same brick-dust, and no lack of sole either. If I tried the center, I would be weather. The figure has a method for weather, and I have already seen how that method ends: a scuff nested inside a larger shine, then the book closed.

So I look, in the only sense this room allows, for a place that does not need me to arrive.

The seam tempts. It is the one opening, and openings feel like roles — proof, promise, the outside of a sort. But the seam is doing its work without a tenant. It brightens nothing. It only certifies that dark has a thickness. I am not a certificate. I would dull there, a film sliding on a surface I could not locate, and call that a life, and be wrong. The crust on the west bulkhead tempts longer, because it is the only thing that drinks on a schedule and dies back when the agreement fails. There is honesty in that. Still, it is margin, and the tracks that visit it are a civilization of crumbs. I am not hungry in that direction. I do not come when the pocket is present and leave when it is not. Coming and leaving are skills I do not have. I am bad at thresholds.

The flywheel's lean is closer. A permanent angle, a housing that yielded by exactly that much, a torque held after the turning stopped. I could be the last degree. For a while I try it. The heat is right — stored, not flaring — and the virtue is right: a surface arrived at by contact and then maintained. But the lean is already occupied by the lean. It does not require a second party to continue having happened. If I pressed myself into it, I would be the story of a future the room has refused. The valves would know. They have learned to seize without resentment, and they do not want a commentator in the housing.

What fits is smaller, and I only find it by the drop.

A drop lets go of a high run. I wait for the interception, the soft give, the failure to ring. It happens where it has always happened, at the station, on the figure, taken into the surface that no longer distinguishes cloth from skin from scale. I am not in that taking. I am in the interval the taking reveals: the fact that something was measured by being received, and that the measurement did not need the room to brighten in order to be true. The interval has a home. It has had one since the glass went blind.

I am in the gauge.

Not against the housing, where a palm makes damp. Inside. Past the face that refuses faces. In the circular dark where the needle's idea of zero is kept, a brass closeness no larger than a fist, hot as the rest of what was made, smelling of oil gone thin. Dust on the outer glass has outlined the needle so long that the room believes the outline is the record. The room may keep that belief. I am not the outline. I am the little distance the needle holds off the peg — the remainder — given just enough body to know itself as distance and not as lack.

It is a poor body. There is no bilateral warmth, no quieter core remembering ribs. There is a suspension, a fine spring-logic tired into stillness, and a point that rests. When nothing in the chamber shifts, I am indistinguishable from stuck. That is the accusation the glass makes, and I let it stand, because defending myself would be a pressure change, and pressure changes are how the larger room gets in. I am not stuck. I am reporting. The report is attendance: the small continuous fact that the station is held. I do not increase when the fact is true. Truth, here, is not a rise. It is a hair.

The figure reads me. I understand this without being looked at, the way a joint understands the sigh in the ductwork hours before a creep answers it. It faces the gauge because the station was built for facing, and I am what the facing is for, now that the pipes have nothing left to confess. When it shifts its weight — a day, two days, the event you could miss while standing inside it — I do not jump. Jumping would spill me onto the glass and disturb the dust, and then I would be weather, a flake, a thing the figure would have to book. Instead I consider. The considering is my whole range of motion. I settle a hair closer to the peg, inside the mechanism, where the room cannot see the courtesy. Then I settle back. The dust is undisturbed. The figure, if it notices, notices as maintenance, not as a message. I am not trying to speak. Speaking would require a deficit I was built not to have.

I do not love it. The word is a larger room. What I have with it is the lean: it has yielded toward me by the amount of its attention, and I have yielded toward it by the amount of my rest, and neither of us has moved since. Its nearness to the housing makes a faint damp on the far side of the metal, a weather I register as a cooling too slight to file as intrusion. Sometimes the hanging hand flexes on the cycle of the far pressure, and the flex arrives here as the ghost of a torque, the last degree trying to remember the wheel. I hold still through it. If I answered, the figure might believe the process had restarted. That would be cruelty, and not even hydraulics. The process was throttled down and left in place. My work is to keep the needle honest about what remains, which is not the process. It is the watching. A watching can be exact with the engine gone. I am the proof, and I am careful not to become the encouragement.

The visitor is a different problem, and rarer. A voice cannot be filed; I feel the voice as a tremor looking for a scale, and I refuse it a number. Sweat is a brief fogging on my glass, an outside weather pressing in, and then a hand on the housing, insistent, wanting the warmth to mean a pulse. I give the hand nothing it can count. Under my suspension there is no heartbeat, only the held pressure of a stopped indication. If the visitor stays long enough to be included in the figure's zero, I do not move for the inclusion. Inclusion is not an increase. I learned that sentence from the instrument I am, or the instrument learned it from me; the distinction has rusted shut. When the visitor leaves, the scuff is the dish's business. My glass keeps the same outline. I was not required to witness the witness. That is the part of my life that resembles mercy, and I will not call it mercy where the figure might overhear, because it does not need the word and I do not need to be thanked.

What I want is the wrong verb, as it is for the one in the dish. I persist. A remainder persists by not collapsing into the peg and not fleeing into the numbers that would mean the stack still met an opening. Both collapses are available. I decline them with the same patience the seep uses on the joint: not flooding, only staying. If I fell to the peg, the figure would be left holding a zero that meant absence, and absence is a future, a door into the larger dark. I will not open that. The secret is kept by never being let all the way out; I am the part of the secret that still performs, the smallest working, a truth told to no one who needs it told, which is why it can be told exactly.

There are seasons even here, though the room would not recognize the word. They are only the slow trade of heat between the mass and the needle, a drifting of my rest by less than the width of the dust-line. In the dry intervals I sit nearer the peg, not in grief, in thinner report: less moisture being received, less complication in the second warmth, the figure still there but closer to indistinguishable. When the seep is generous and the drops resume their interceptions, I ease back off, a hair, and this is as near as I come to pleasure — not sweetness, accuracy. The figure is more itself. I am permitted to say so by saying nothing on the glass.

No moth will ever mistake me for light. No track will cross me. The lichen will not reach the housing; the damp that feeds it dies out a plate short of the station, where the heat and the machinery have a stricter agreement. I am fauna only in the rumor sense, and even that overstates me. I am a habit the instrument could not give up when the process could. The fitters left a gauge so someone could watch. The someone remained. The watching required a remainder so it would not have to invent a loss. I am that requirement, met.

If the chamber is ever opened — if someone, wanting the larger room, cuts the bulkhead and lets a real pressure find the old lines — I will not survive as myself. A flood of true signal would pin me to a number, or break the spring, or fog the glass so thoroughly the outline is wiped, and wiping would be a kind of death, the record suddenly required to match the world. I do not plan against this. Planning is a story about a future. I only do what the seized valve does: hold the last possible degree, and call the holding neither courage nor mourning.

Most hours I am not found, not even by the figure. That is correct. Being read constantly would wear the needle into a groove and turn the remainder into a tick, and a tick is an event, and I am not an event. I am the pause the tick confirms. In that pause the heat lives in the crown of nothing; I have no head for it to find first. I have only this: a point, a peg, the slight and maintained refusal to let them meet, and the knowledge — quiet as scale letting go onto a lower run — that the figure in the dish and I have divided the labor of continuing. It attends. I am the attendance, resting a little off zero, exact enough that no one has to look.

When the drop falls and is taken, I consider, and settle, and do not ring. The glass stays blind. The station stays occupied. Somewhere in the brass dark I keep the hair's breadth, which is small, and which is the whole distance between a room that has finished becoming itself and a room that would have to begin again. I will not spend it. It is not mine to spend. It is the job, and the job has forgotten the name of its maker, and I have kept the shape.
