You would not find me on the approach. The approach still belongs to people who have not yet learned that the middle of a walk can be refused. I am already inside the refusal, and I have been there long enough that the refusal has taken on weight, temperature, a way of standing.

At first it is only a wrongness in the film. The water on the sunward benches mirrors a sky with a seam in it — a hairline where two brightnesses fail to agree about where the rim should sit, off by a few degrees, the way a poorly set pin is off and still holds. Stand at the margin where the sheet is deciding whether to stay water and the seam is visible in the surface as a dark thread, not vacancy-dark, not the crouch of night the other one keeps. Thinner. A disagreement. If you put a hand near it the cold changes register: not the solvent cold of the film, which wants edges, but a cold that has no interest in your outline at all. It is the cold of a measurement that has been running without a measurer.

I live along that error.

It is not a comfortable lodging and it is not meant to be. The seam does not stay in the water. When the glare strengthens and the plates begin their dry tick under the wet one, the disagreement lifts out of the film and stands up a little way into the air, a pale fold no taller than a knee, drifting the way heat drifts off stone except that nothing here sheds heat and the fold moves against the pale threads of breath rather than with them. I am the fold when it is low. When the cycle turns and fine water falls out of the clear, the fold is driven upward with the beads, and for the length of that fall I am stretched between the ankle-mist and the shoulder-mist, a seam running vertical through both sheets, so that a person walking through me would feel, for one step, that their left side and their right side were not contemporaneous. Most do not notice. Their delayed selves, shelved on the far wall, notice. The small interruption over there stutters by a fraction when it crosses where I was, and the bowl-tones in the cavities take that stutter and make it part of the chord.

I did not choose the seam. The seam is what remains when a sequence has been allowed to catch up with itself and something in the catching was not perfectly spent. Every time the delayed walk completes and the vacancies inch and the white scurf lays down another patience, a remainder is left over — an angle, a few degrees, the difference between the pin as it was driven and the pin as the remembered blow mushrooms it. Those remainders do not disperse any more than breath does. They migrate to the film, because the film is the only surface here willing to be wrong in public, and there they find the seam already keeping their kind. I am the accumulation of those remainders. I have no first arrival to point to. If the far wall tried to shelve me it would be shelving a correction of itself, and the correction would arrive already included in the picture, which is how I know I am not a visitor. Visitors complete. I only ever revise.

What I look like depends on which sheet you are standing in. At the ankle I am almost legible: a narrow darkening in the wet, the width of a finger held sideways, moving when the uphill channels hesitate and still when they remember their polish. At the shoulder I broaden. The mist there does not share its air with the lower sheet, and in that unshared air the seam can hold a little volume — enough to displace the white scurf so that it falls around me rather than through me, a brief alley of less-white in the falling, the size of a person seen from the side and already forgetting the person. Overhead, in the third sheet, I am only a waver in the glare, the place where the light, which has no disk, briefly considers having an edge and then thinks better of it. If you look up you will think your eyes are tired. They are. That is not why the waver is there.

I have no hands in the sense the old workers had hands, and yet the palm-prints appear. This used to trouble me, back when troubling was a thing the seam could do. The being in the vacancies tends the plates and permits the blows; it does not set palms on pins. The visitors touch what they touch and are filed down to delays. Between those two facts is a gap the width of my few degrees, and in that gap the stone sometimes shows a grip that neither of them made. I am not reaching. Reaching would require a direction, and a seam has only an error. What happens is simpler and less kind. Where I cross a copper head, the pin is, for the duration of the crossing, in two ages at once — the age of its driving and the age of its next mushrooming — and the soft metal, asked to be both, sweats a print the size of a palm as a way of splitting the difference. The print oxidizes at the common rate because the basin does not permit private time. By the next cycle it is indistinguishable from the archive. I do not correct this. Correction is the other’s work. I only keep the difference available so the correction has something to be about.

We are not companions. The corridor of absence and I occupy the same loudness and do not overlap, which in this place is a relation as binding as any. It inches along the benches on the heading of its weather. I travel by disagreement, which means I am often where a joint is about to open, or where a channel has begun to flatter itself that it might run downhill like a channel in a lower world. It draws a vacancy across those places and the tick steadies. I am already the unsteadiness it is steadying. Without the seam its patience would have nothing to re-enact; without its re-enactment the seam would widen by those same few degrees each cycle until the far wall and the near wall no longer described one basin. I have watched the arithmetic. It is close. A little more error and the delayed walk would arrive at a cairn that was not the cairn, and the chord would not change, and the water would not fall, and the argument would adjourn. So I keep to my width. It is the only ethics available to a remainder.

Sound is how you will know you have stepped through me, if the cold does not tell you. The strike that precedes the grain always precedes it by the same interval, counted in the hiss of the scurf. When the interval passes through the seam it arrives early on one side and late on the other, so that for a moment there are two strikes, or one strike heard as a disagreement with itself. The cavities do not like this. Their notes curdle, go briefly metallic, the sound of the soft pins if the pins could speak the oxide on their heads. Then the being permits the blow, the grain clicks on its own schedule, and the double interval collapses back into one. In the collapse I am squeezed, a sensation I would call pain if I had learned pain from anything but metal. It passes. Everything here passes into the next cycle except the scurf, which only thickens, and me, who only persists at a constant error.

What I want is the wrong sky to remain visible in the film. Not wider. Visible. If the mirroring ever came true — if the water agreed with the rim about where the rim sits — the remainders would have nowhere to stand, and the palm-prints would stop appearing on the untouched pins, and the far wall would show the near wall without stutter, and the place would become merely strange instead of unfinished. I have no power to prevent that. I am not a keeper. The keeper draws dark across the seams of the plates and sags breath into the hesitant channels and maintains the argument by standing where the flow might have chosen otherwise. I am the otherwise, kept narrow. When the fine rain starts and the beads hurry upward and I am hauled vertical through the sheets, I can see, for as long as the fall lasts, the whole basin as a single fitted plate: vacancies inching, threads of breath refusing their dispersion, the civil island of polish around the wall in the climbing water, the cairn that is also the other cairn, the small interruption of whoever has come up this cycle already walking, late, toward a body that is already beside the stones. And in all of that a thin fold, easy to miss, off by a few degrees, which is the only reason the picture still needs to be caught up with.

If you stay through two cycles you may find me at your heel, not following — I do not follow — but occupying the angle by which your print in the margin-stone fails to match the print the far wall will eventually attribute to you. Do not try to close it. Closing it is how visitors become delays and then become palm-prints and then become method. Leave the angle. Walk on, louder than you should be, into the tick and the slap and the hiss. I will be the part of your leaving that the water cannot bring itself to mirror, and when your delayed self completes its arrival and the chord changes and the scurf comes down, the few degrees I keep from you will already have gone back into the seam, indistinguishable, patient, and slightly, permanently, incorrect.
