You do not arrive by the door. The seal has already kissed itself shut, and the path of yellow paint is not a way you were ever meant to use. You are what the louver keeps trying to refuse and cannot quite refuse: a fineness that comes down with the draft, not grit and not a seed, something closer to the weather that was machined out of the air and then, in the argument, let back in. At first you are only a cooling on the bulbs. The tea-colored light tightens. A bead that should have formed on the copper forms instead on nothing, and falls, and does not reach the trough.

The gallery does not notice. Its voice is already full. The pump knocks. The header answers with the blow. You learn the interval the way a stain learns a seam — by remaining through it. Between one hammer and the next there is a thinner quiet, not silence, only the place where the roar of the louver inhales. You occupy that. You are drawn along the ceiling plate, a skin of almost-water, colder than the weep on the coils, and you travel opposite the header's flow because you are not in the circuit. You are what the circuit was built to shed. Where the bleed-vent sighs, you thicken. Where the draft shears, you shear with it, and a little of you is lost each time into the fins, and the little that is lost beads and runs and becomes ordinary drip, and the sump takes it without remark. What stays is the part that will not yet be water.

It takes a long time to find the floor. You have no weight that the plates can count. You move when the valve cycle breaks the rhythm, the same blow the other one uses, and in that struck moment you pour yourself down a bolt, a bright thread no wider than a crack in the mill scale, and you are in the lower tray before the high note of equalizing has finished ringing. The tray is warmer than you. The paint is a taste. Iron. A sweetness that is not sweet, the treatment chemical already spat and carried here, and under it the mineral the pipes give up. You spread because spreading is what you are. You do not have a snout or a slot or a jelly that trembles. You have an edge, and the edge reads by what it thins across.

The other one is there. You know it as a wrongness in the wet, a length of the tray that is occupied, a heat that fogs and vanishes between knocks. It does not know you yet. You are still too diffuse to be weather. You lie downstream of its mouth-end, toward the drain, where the seep arrives last, and you drink what it has already refused — the faint oil it will not take, the bitterness it threads out and abandons. The oil does not scald you. You have no hide to scar. You take it in and it becomes a sheen you can hold, a way of being visible if anything were looking, which nothing is. The pump's gulp tears the skin on the sump above; down here the tears are smaller, and you learn to gather them.

You grow by not being harvested. The green film is not your crop and not your kin. When a clot of it escapes the mesh and washes into the tray, the length in the dark moves, rasps, takes it. You lie still under the pitch of the floor and let the body pass. Its grit-studded weight does not feel you; you part around the bolts and rejoin behind the tail of it, a wake of cleaner metal. Once, early, you clung too thick on the intake side and were tasted. The slot closed on a thread of you and opened again, and what came back was rejection, a hotter breath, the chemical spit of something that does not belong in the meal. You learned the boundary from that. You do not bridge the mesh. You do not climb the seam. The two lanes of green are its, and the argument with the pump is its, and you are the argument the pump is not having.

What you are, when you are most yourself, is the interval made slightly longer. You hold a drop that should have fallen. You slow a seep along the tray so that it reaches the drain a knock later than the drawing says it should. The numbers on the steel do not change. No alarm exists for you. The gallery was built to balance a humidity it cannot refuse, and you are a remainder of that refusal, pooled where the lower pitch collects what the trough missed. In the dark under the proud plate you are almost a shape: a lens of clear that the jelly-pits would tremble at if they were turned toward the drain instead of the sump. They are not. The body keeps its purchase at the rim and its check at the mesh. You keep the drain.

Sometimes the heavy drops from the ceiling find a seam and come down all at once, and you are struck apart, and for a cycle or two you are only scattered chill on the paint, no preference, no edge. Then the valve hisses, the header shudders, the blow comes, and in the regrouping you find you have chosen the same hollow again, the same side of the same bolt, out of the path of the rasp. That choosing is as much body as you will ever have. You do not breathe on the pump's count. You do not need to. You thin when the louver inhales and you gather when it does not, and the creature's slit opening and shutting beside you is only another weather, large, regular, already in the record.

You will not be seen from the path. Lift the plate and the tray looks empty in the tea-light: wet, pitched, a skin of oil that tears, a green dust in the seams. The length at the sump end draws back a hand's span and settles, reading a new temperature, filing you — if it files you at all — as draft. You are the draft that stayed. When the plate goes down, the dark returns, and you spread once more toward the drain, and you hold, and the pump knocks, and the note the pour had missed comes back, cleaned, and under it, uncounted, a smaller lateness persists.

The high cold outside does not call you. You were already the part of it that got in. The door is a pressure you do not read. What you have is the tray's last inch before the outlet, the taste of what was refused, the blow you hide inside, and a life the width of a crack that learns, by remaining, not to be taken for error. The other one eats the error. You are what eating leaves. The gallery agrees with itself above. Below, in the inch the drawings do not name, you agree with the remainder, and the remainder holds.
