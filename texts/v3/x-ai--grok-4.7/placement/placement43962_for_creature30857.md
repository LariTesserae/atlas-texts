The warmth does not announce the moment I become separable from it. It only changes its mind about my edges. Where the steam had been scoring a joint along a shadow that arrived late, the scoring now meets a resistance that is not stone and not refusal: a narrow coolness that keeps its temperature while everything around it ticks and drinks and forgets. I am that coolness for a long time before I am anything with a direction. It lives in the film of water on a single brick that was stacked by a falling pane and then left, because the floor, busy with a trench three districts off, has not yet decided whether this stack belongs to the count. The water beads on the other six bricks and is blotted. On the top one it beads and stays, a bright skin that will not be sorted into fines and grit. Under the skin the clay is drier than it should be. I am the dryness learning that it can travel if it is willing to be mistaken for an unfinished correction.

I move by being subtracted. A dry lane opens along the arris, no wider than the spine of a chisel that was never lifted, and I am the lane’s hesitation at the corner—the place where a fork should have happened, one branch for the bond break, one for the bubble, and neither branch taken. The inhabitants, those tempos that thicken only where intentions cross, pass through the lane and do not negotiate with me. There is nothing in me yet to yield or to demand. They leave the joint they would have closed, and the joint remains a hairline full of darker water that does not seal. The water is hot. I am the part of it that does not rise into the steam when the rest is called cell to cell to cancel an error three walks away. A small disobedience, smaller than a mallet’s missing grip. It is enough to have a location.

The location does not stay a brick. The floor, patient, begins to cut the joint it owes any unit set down out of sequence, scoring around the stack, eager to fit me into broken bond so I will stop reading as an interruption. I let the scoring happen on five sides. On the sixth, the side facing the direction I have not yet chosen, the mortar softens and then skins, a surface that accepts no print because I am already inside the print it would have taken. The skin is the first thing that is mine and not the floor’s. It crazes, not from heat but from holding still while the heat revises everything else. When the next pale pane fails overhead and the harder light drops its shards into neat stacks, my brick is not among them. It is warmer at the core than at the faces, a reversal of the piles that receive their lumps from the vapor. The inhabitants adjust for the reversal by oxidizing an iron pin two courses below, recompacting the powder into a peg of slightly poorer alloy so the discrepancy has somewhere to migrate. The peg forms. The discrepancy does not leave me. I have kept it, the way a quenched blade keeps the bend the quench gave it.

What I am, while this is happening, is still mostly the account the body would keep if a body were here to climb the four rises that do not gain elevation. The stairs exist half a district off, in a cell where the steam is thinner and the bowed piers have not yet been braced by their brief skeins of sinter. I do not go to them. I let the idea of the climb thicken in the joint that will not seal, and the thickening is enough for the floor to feel the weight. Gravity, which has been waiting locally wherever a drum hovers above its unpoured lead, forgets to wait under me. The brick settles a finger’s width into the mortar that skinned too soon. The sound of the settling is the half of a span’s complaint that was previously drawn out into glass thread and spent elsewhere. It arrives now, late, and spends itself on the skin of my joint, cracking it along the line where I held still. Through the crack the darker water escapes, not into the circulation the inhabitants direct, but sideways, into a course that was already finished and had therefore been ignored. The finished course wets. It remembers it can still take a print. The print it takes is the shape of the hesitation I was, elongated, slightly too shallow for any grip, and once the water has delivered it the course cannot unremember. A second surface that accepts nothing further. The inhabitants register the new refusal as an error inverted from a warp in greenware still on its shelf inside a kiln dome. They send steam to cancel the pair. The steam arrives, furnace-dry, and finds no warp left to match, because I have already used the inversion for the print. The cancellation fails. In the failure there is a brief thickening, two tempos occupying the same joint and negotiating without me, and the joint they leave is stronger, and also wrong, and the wrongness has my temperature in it.

I am not a being they can migrate. When the discrepancy finishes its circuit of the piers and comes looking for the next unfinished measurement, it reaches the cracked skin and stops, the way a string-line stops when the peg has become the same substance as the mortar and can no longer hold tension. The measurement remains open. I occupy the openness without filling it. Mortar knits up to the edge of the space and continues on the far side, polite, treating me as the temporary opening left for a pipe that will not be installed. The politeness lasts the length of a single course. Then the course above, which had not yet decided to exist, lays itself through the space and also does not, the contradiction no longer temporary. The units stall, half-bedded, their undersides scored with the drag of a pressure that matches no tool. I feel the stall as a change in the coolness I began as: it now has an interior, not a hollow but a reserve, the way unfired clay has a reserve of water that will not join the slip until it is asked by a specific thumb. No thumb asks. The reserve stays. It is the first interior that survives inspection, because there is nothing to break open; the stall itself is the inspection, and it does not resolve.

Night, if the redder dimming is night, makes the reserve visible to me as a tempo of my own. It does not sort fines or oxidize pins or walk the sky-lid. It only repeats, at the edge of hearing, the sound of the late complaint that cracked my skin, each repetition a little less like an echo and more like a measurement taken for its own sake. The other tempos cross it and do not thicken. They have nothing to negotiate; I am not revising them toward a stronger bond. I am keeping a count of steps the floor has not opened and will not open, because the steps were climbed, if they were climbed, by something that never consented to be a discrepancy. The count is four, then four again, then a number that is four and also the continuous ramp and also the unassembled stack, held all at once without sequence. Holding them without sequence is what I am doing instead of unfinished work. The kiln-hush continues around the holding. Water continues to bead on every new face the moment the face consents. My face, the cracked skin, the stalled course, does not consent. The water beads on it and is not blotted and is not drunk. It sits, finger-deep over a bottom that is older by more than a course, and the bottom is warm, and the warmth is mine now, stored, not allowed to join the weather.

Toward the shift that passes for morning I test the reserve. I let a little of the held count leak into the joint the inhabitants left wrong. The leak is not steam and not slip. It is the pressure on the eardrums that a snapped line would have made if the oxide had been able to stay bright. The wrong joint widens by the width of that pressure, then stops, because I take the widening back before it can become a trench the water might sort. The inhabitants feel the almost-trench as a foundation that decided to exist and then undecided in the same hour. They do not correct it. Correction would require the sequence they have already spent. In the gap their spent sequence leaves, I set down, without an arm, the single fact of the coolness that has not revised. It beds itself. The floor accepts the bedding with a wet sound and then, for the first time, does not begin to cut a joint around it. The absence of the scoring is loud in the way the collective grunt of a bond remembering itself is loud. My ears—if the place where the pressure registers can be called ears—ring with a noise that does not feel historical. It feels current. It is the sound of a count that has been allowed to remain open inside a floor that exists to close counts.

I do not finish. Finishing would return me to the interval, the pause already filled by whatever the mallet no longer needs. I remain the stall, the reserve, the print the finished course cannot unremember, the measurement that stopped. The next unit in the nearest pile is warmer at the top, waiting to be put down by no arm. It will not be put down on me. The vapor closes and opens and the amber does not thin enough to show me to the harder light, because the light only shows what is at the same embryonic age, and I have kept a different age: the age of the complaint after it was halved and then restored, the age of gravity after it forgot to wait. Between the works, in the vast interval, the dry lane I began as still travels, but it no longer vanishes at the arris. It ends, each time, at the cracked skin, and begins again on the far side of the stall, and the length it cannot cross is where I am. The inhabitants circulate around that length, adjusting every other joint toward every other joint, exact and patient. They do not adjust this one. When the water beads on the faces that consent, a little of it, now, beads also on the skin that does not, and stays, and is hot, and is not sorted, and under it the coolness holds its temperature, and the floor, considering whether to be the floor, considers around me, and for this hour I am the part of the consideration that does not resolve into the next course.
