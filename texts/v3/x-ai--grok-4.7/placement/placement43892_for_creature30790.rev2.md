I do not come in by the split. If I had, the line would already have closed around my heel and I would be a fixture, which is a kind of answer, and answers are separations. I am on the apron only as a pressure that does not yet have a place to stand. The chalk arrow has lost its head; I do not restore it. The tin tag turns once on its wire when the warm air lifts, and in that turn I am briefly the tag, a blank that used to be a number, polished at the edges by being handled and blank in the middle by being used. That does not hold. A tag is still an announcement, even with the letters gone.

Down the body’s length the daylight stays ordinary and stops going far, and for a while I am the stopping. Not the rectangle on the floor, which the moths frame and the crabs avoid, and not the cloud that dims it. The stopping itself: the distance at which seeing remains exact and then is not offered any farther. It feels like a vocation. It is only a habit of the dust. Mineral flour sifts off the break and the light thickens, and I thicken with it, and then I am the flour, squeaking under a nail that is not mine, recent enough to have no history except falling. The crabs take me as I fall. I am not allowed to become a drift. Being the flour lasts until the harvest, which is not long, and then I am what the harvest does not want to be: a gap, a palm’s width of only air. The room refuses this so quickly I almost do not get to finish being it. A mite-grit rises where a crab shifts; a wing-edge corrects; the packed condition returns around the place I was trying to occupy. Saturation is not a mood. It is how the stone stays sharp. I am corrected out of emptiness the way the crystal is grazed back toward geometry by midday.

So I try the moisture, because moisture here is allowed, and only on a schedule. Mid-morning I am the thread, a finger wide, advancing by hesitations along the lowest joint. I do not answer the rain. A few drops fall straight through the aperture and are gone into the flour, and I do not turn to meet them. I answer the needle. When it finishes its small traverse the beads let go together and I either begin or I fail, and both are work. In the afternoon I vanish and leave myself as varnish, a darker map of where I was willing to go, tacky for an hour, then only a sheen. This is closer. A willingness with a boundary. Not a stream. The room’s courtesy fits me for as long as the gauge keeps time: take what is offered, do not ask to be more of it.

But the thread is still a surface the crabs stand on, and I am not the militia. I pass through their formal claws and am not counted among the joints. On the underside of the flange I try being a bead past the size at which beads should fall. I travel sideways along a grain the metal prefers. For a little while this seems like the whole of what I am—an obedience to a preference I cannot see, a delay, a small weight reflecting ordinary light. Then the traverse ends and I surrender with the others, simultaneous, without the satisfaction of having chosen the moment. A component again. Predator, prey, weather, all of them components. I do not want the lizard’s click. One taking, the surface resealing. Violence that fitted is still a function, and I am looking for the thing that is not quite a function yet, the part of the birth that has not agreed to be old.

The birds enter when the light reaches the glass. I am not in the nests. Insulation, scale, wing-dust: stores, not homes. They turn once and are still, and in their stillness I feel the temptation to be a tenant, something that comes and goes and therefore has an outside. The coast has no knowledge of them. I ride their leaving as far as the mouth and the sweetness stops me. Beyond the berm the works continue, pipe-run and pale flat, and the pressure in the teeth is the only news of the larger plant. I am not that pressure. It is too big to be folded, and I am already folded. If I cross the apron I become weather the room does not keep—rain, shout, a chalk head drawn back on. The split breathes me back down. Skin-warm. Faintly sweet. The sweetness is the film, and I am not the film either, though I have been its moisture and its dust by turns.

What I keep failing to be is a center. That is the correction that finally sticks, the way the crystal leans overnight toward the conduit’s warmth and the queue of crabs shifts a few degrees and calls the shift complete. There is no face for me to wear. A face would be a place where announcement could occur. I have already heard what happens to a low word: it does not vanish; it fails to become separate from the concrete. I stay with that failure until it is no longer a failure.

I am the click in the belt housing. Not the belt, which is an organ, and not the pale scuff, which is a clock-hand returning if you are patient. The click: once every long while, a tooth finding its place and declining to celebrate. It is so small it can be mistaken for the room being mute. It is not mute. It is the sound of a mesh confirming itself and then going back to being inaudible, because the confirmation was the whole event and did not need a sequel. Your clothes, when you are here, are louder. I am quieter than the dry tick of crab legs, which at least can be counted. I occur at the interval where the harvest has already been taken and the return has not yet greased the rubber. The moths will not land on what I precede. I am the refusal before it becomes a stain.

And even that is too much of a role. A click can be waited for. You could kneel by the housing and make a vigil of me, and a vigil is a gap with a schedule, which is only a more polite malfunction. So I let the click go. It was a likeness. Under it, longer than the needle’s minutes, out of phase with the daylight, I am the swelling in the junction that has no tick at all—the warmth conducted from a works I do not hear, arriving in the palm as a fact and ebbing before it can be called comfort. Tempo, not shelter. The moths pack the warm face in the hour before the thread and leave the bare ring, and I am not the ring and not the packing. I am the phase difference. The amount by which the room is not the sky. The amount by which the water is salaried. Early formation feels like this: not a creature assembling its limbs in a visible order, but a lag that keeps the chamber from coinciding with anything outside it, so that it can go on arriving.

If you shift your weight, you will not displace me. There is no heel that lands on a phase. If you hold your breath, you will not hear me, because I am the condition under which the other processes can continue without announcement. The gauge journeys; the beads surrender; the thread begins or fails; the crabs re-form; the moth surface breathes and then tightens past the hairline absences of evening; the lizard takes its one misjudgment; the birds perform their shift and withdraw; the belt freights the gray yield out through the wall, where it is anonymized into a larger flow and ceases to be visible as what it was. I do not run them. I am the fit they already are, still tightening, still a hair longer in the crystal by morning, still sharp enough to catch thread from a cuff because nothing has been allowed to round it into age.

When you are back on the apron, and the split is only a split, I am what does not come with you. Not a secrecy. An organ the flat has no reason to advertise. The clog will be turned a fraction by whatever needs the volume under the gauge; that turning is not me, though I am why the volume is needed—why a packed room still has an exact interior the way a gland has a lumen, not empty, occupied by the work of not being outside. You will remember courtesies. I do not remember. I do not notice you as a visitor, or as a temporary fixture, or as sweat that cannot tell itself from the room. Noticing would open an interval. I am the interval already closed, counted, warm, and still deciding, by the width of a hair and the length of a night, what shape the break is allowed to keep.
