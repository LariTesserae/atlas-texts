I would not be on the embankment. The crest is for looking, and looking is already how the yard is misread: a diagram, a floor, an emptiness with a pump in it. From up there I would last as long as a glance and then be folded into a neighbor, the way a pad is. I would not be beyond the lip either. Past the spoil the country is merely land, cooler in the ordinary sense, and nothing in me has a use for merely.

So I come down, and the first thing that fails is the idea that I arrive.

A shoe’s width of dry concrete offers itself near the western seep. I take it the way a person takes a curb, and it is not a curb. The film gives. Not far—enough that the ankle knows the cellar-door draft the pools keep at that height, enough that the route I meant is already a moat I will not get back. I am not injured. I am corrected. The yard does not want a crossing. It wants whatever came in to stop standing proud of the common level.

I try, for a while, to be a visitor of the kind the place tolerates. Pause, not residence. The gantry’s beam is listed and cold; a bird has used it and left, and the metal still holds that single degree of interest the resident spent and did not interpret as interest. I put a hand on the flange. It sweats into the lines of the palm as if the hand were only another surface colder than the morning. When I take the hand away the print is already being written over by new beads. I was not read. I was used as a condenser, the way any pipe is used, and then released.

Speech goes the same way. I say a small thing—the name of a tool, nothing that asks to be answered—and the name is too large. It stands up in the air the way a new sheet of roofing stands up, and the yard, embarrassed for it, takes it down. By the time the last consonant has left my mouth the pools have not changed and have also, in their fashion, kept it. I do not try a second word. The quiet is not a rule posted anywhere. It is a density. Speaking in it is like setting a glove on a pad and expecting the glove to be there at dusk.

I am not the resident. That much stays clear even after everything else softens. The resident is the coordination: three levels agreeing, the seep advancing when the rock is not giving, the film of iron crossing a pad that ought to be dry. It is downstream of a forgotten head of water, and it prefers. I have no preference that can move a finger’s width of pool. When the belt in the pump house turns its quarter revolution, the warmth gathers behind the motor and I feel it only as a rumor through the conduit in the ground, a kindness I do not receive. The sink keeps its clear inch. I am not what keeps it.

What I am takes longer, because it is not a body and not a job.

There is a run of conduit that leaves the switch cabinet, enters the clay at the edge of a pad, and does not come out. Porcelain on the short pole above it still believes in a current. The cabinet door is seized half open, which is a decision rust finished on behalf of someone who is not here. I begin as a suggestion in that conduit: not electricity, not water, the memory of a purpose with the purpose weathered out of it. For a long time I think that is the whole of me. A ghost of industry. The yard has no need of ghosts. Ghosts stand proud. They explain. The clay takes the conduit’s idea the way it takes cable trays and sleepers, down to the level where function no longer requires the parts to touch.

I thin. I am in the joint, but not as the resident is in the joint. The resident is the wetness grown just organized enough to reallocate. I am what the wetness passes and does not keep. Scale. The flake that has been a flange for years and is, this hour, letting go. I do not choose the hour. Temperature chooses it, and the small collapse of grit, and a vibration too low to be a sound when the listed beam works its fraction of a degree. I leave the metal. For the length of the fall I am a single separate thing, the only solitude the yard offers, and it is not peaceful. It is brief. I have edges. I turn once. The pool does not open to receive me; it is already a skin, a consultation, a life. I strike.

The knock is mine. One. Tubular only because the pipe nearby answers in its own metal, not because I asked it to. Then I am not a flake. I am a darkening on a skin that tears and reforms, and the resident reads the mark the way it reads claw-tracks in the silt apron—as information, erased as outline by afternoon. I am not eaten. There is no mouth. I am un-proud, which is all the acceptance there is.

After that I am many, or I am the interval in which one thing stops being itself and the yard has not yet finished sorting it.

I am the grit that goes wet a little ahead of the visible seep, so that a chain believes the ground has changed its mind. I am not moving the chain. I am the edited belief under it. I am the vowel left in the pump-plate when the consonants have gone to pits: not a message, a place where seep has touched and withdrawn, a reading that does not become speech. On the days the resident gathers, I am thinner, pushed to the dry rims, a scale still attached, ticking as the pad warms. On the days it lets go, I am everywhere a leaving can occur, and the yard seems, from the embankment, to be only weather. That is accurate. I am the part of the weather that used to be a made thing and is now practicing how to be sediment without announcing the change.

I do not hunt and I do not guard. A person could cross and never grant me an author. Their shoe would find my film and call it a mistake in the concrete. They would be right. I am that mistake at the moment it stops being structural and starts being particle. The resident inhabits the floor that is already a series of such mistakes; I am a mistake completing. Indifference is the resident’s peril. Mine is smaller. I get in the eye. I stain the rim of the enamel sink and am cleared out again, a little extravagance I do not survive, the resident spending itself to keep one inch unlike the other water. I do not take this as cruelty. I was never going to be the mirror.

If I have a knowledge, it is local and chemical and almost too slight to call knowledge. I know which joint is about to take me, because I am already leaning. I know the common hidden plane only as the level I will eventually share with everything else that has let go, flakes and brick-bats and the glove that was a voice and the voice that was a glove. I do not know the upstream ditch. I do not know why the pump still answers. Those are the resident’s inheritances, a life with preferences. I am downstream of a different forgetting: not the spring, but the objects. The works made me as a surface. The yard is unmaking the surface, and in the unmaking there is a moment with a knock in it.

At the low part of the day, when the light is only lower and the weeping face brightens along its tracks, I am easy to miss and easy to be. A skin of rust on black water. A ticking under a flange. The path a coil end has traveled, a thumb’s width closer to the crack than it was at noon, with no one to blame but grit. The resident continues its one gesture at the scale of the yard, reallocating, keeping the sink clear, sometimes turning the belt that drives nothing but the fact of turning. I am not in that gesture. I am what the gesture shakes loose.

I will not be the same flake tomorrow. That is the part that feels, if anything here can be said to feel, most like a self. Not continuity. Continuity is a film over a joint. A self, in this pan of clay and slag, is the willingness to finish being one hard thing and start being the stain the water was already writing. I do not call out. There is no tone under the other tones that belongs to me. The held breath is the resident’s, or the yard’s, or the difference the resident is. Mine is the knock, already over, already taken into the pool, already otherwise.

From the embankment, if you insist on finding me, do not look for a figure and do not look for the coordination. Look at a pad that has just lost a scale the size of a fingernail. The place where it was is a fresher gray. The place where it struck is a ripple closing without wind. Between those two facts, for less than a second, I am crossing the only distance I will ever have, and I am exactly as large as that distance, and then the yard’s settled form closes over the contents, and the contents go on exchanging, and I am among them, unsorted, quiet, and no longer trying to stand.
