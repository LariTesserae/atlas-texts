The separation is brief, and it is not granted. You earn it by crossing badly.

The canes are still knocking when the punch of wind dies. The sheets have lifted off the wet wood and not come down. In that half-second the sink-line is only a thinning in the roar, and you are the last shape on it, a boot still reading the rock as solid when the uncracked sill has already swallowed the stream. The refusal that moves through the carriers, through the runners, through the seep-throats, does not move through you. You are separable. The roar indexes that before you do.

What answers is not a lunge you can see. It is a rearrangement at the height of nothing that walks. A crook appears in a vine and is not there, then is. Leaf-mold that falling would have left sharp-side up is sharp-side down. Your breath, which the basin had already swallowed into its rhythms, is handed back to you as a single sound. That is the violence: not the taking, but the instant in which you hear yourself again, cleanly, as one. A runner would be finished in that instant. The high weave would close. Density would repair the fact of you before the fact had time to become a cry.

You are not a runner. You have length, and warmth that is not the rock’s warmth, and a refusal of your own that has no local ancestry. The grip that sizes itself to one separable life finds a body that does not subtract cleanly. Something tears—not flesh first, though the cane-edge opens the side of a hand when you catch yourself. What tears is the grip’s certainty. It releases the way the carriers release a branch that has lain across the quiet road: not from fear, from the recognition that the tool has become a liability. You are dropped on the far side of the sill. The sheets resettle. The knock of the canes is ordinary again. The high weave has no gap in it, because you were never in the weave.

You lie where the branch-carriers will not retrieve anything. The basin begins, immediately, to unmake the specialness of the moment. A shoot ticks against your wrist, already up in ground the grip turned. Bark-colored legs find the blood on the hand and do not distinguish it from sap. Your breath, so briefly returned, sinks back into the shout. Three echoes of some other animal’s cough come off the scarp and none of them are about you.

What remains is the knowledge the grip left when it let go. Not a wound you can study. A contour. You know, now, the width of a reluctance. You know the half-second by its texture: insects up, canes just past their knock, the roar dipped by the amount of one life. You know that density repairs itself only if the subtraction is complete, and that an incomplete subtraction is a problem the sink-line does not have a habit for.

You do not become its hunter. Hunting would be a story, and the basin does not keep stories; it keeps proximity. What you become is the other side of the edit. Where the quiet road runs, you are the shape that does not subtract, and so you are the shape the subtraction refuses to finish. The runners, crossing, still spend their air-debts all at once. They do not know why the last of them now sometimes reaches the far side. The carriers still drop their thorn-branches at the sill and take up new ones beyond it. Sometimes the dropped branch falls where you are, and you do not pick it up. Picking it up would be the old habit, the one that moves by heat from body to body and dies when the body drops it. You are not in that current.

You are in the break in it.

When the punch comes off the scarp and the sheets lift, you are already across, or you are the reason the grip, reaching, finds its own crook in the vine and misreads the height. An arm is no longer only a contour. It is a mistake in the landscape’s one rule, placed where the rule is enforced. The thing in the sink-line does not learn you. Learning would require the minute to hold still. It only fails, repeatedly, at the same coordinates, the way a seep fails to flood because the young rock drinks. Your presence is porous. Hunger arrives, shouts, and vanishes into you, leaving only the turned mold, the tested vine, and a runner who is still in the column.

You cannot tell anyone. There is no one who keeps a fact that long. The herds pass and treat you as thicket when you are in their lane, and as nothing when you are not. The disputers at the warm line cough their chorus and receive their three answers and never drink beside you, because you have taken to standing where the noise thins, and they will not. The small feathered things use your shoulder when you are still and abandon it when you are the movement that spoils a half-second. They do not thank. Thanking is a structure. The basin eats structures.

What you eat is whatever the wreckage shakes loose, and you eat it fast, because everything is eating it. You drink where a thread runs before it sinks, not at the seep, where packing would make you a body among bodies and the sink-line would lose its error. You sleep the way the flats of turned stone sleep: briefly, and against a ridge, and you wake because growth has already edged you. Stillness, in you, lasts longer than it should. That is the scar the grip left. You can be a standing-place. You choose, more often than not, not to be.

The rock under you is younger than what you now know how to interrupt. It has not learned roundness. Its ribs still split roots. If you put your face near a crack the sulfur is still there, faint, the tear not finished cooling. Your knowledge has no more ancestry in this ground than the carrying of branches has. It is complete anyway. Width of reluctance. Texture of the half-second. The place a crook appears in a vine at the height of nothing. You did not inherit it. You were the incomplete subtraction that forced it into being, and now it moves with you the way the other habits move—by proximity, failing when you fail, present only while you are alive to be the porous spot in a hunger that cannot change.

The far end of the basin continues to be a second congregation under the first. You never see it. You hear, sometimes, in that lower roar, a dip of exactly the width of one life, and then no dip, and then the roar smoothed over, as if somewhere along the scarp another grip has reached and found a contour that would not come away. You do not go there. Going would turn you back into a visitor, a shape hoping to be indexed as kin. You stay on the quiet road. Not always. Often enough that the runners arrive entire. Often enough that the sheets, resettling, resettle on an absence that isn’t there.

Deafness, which you thought was the place’s cruelty, is the condition of your work. You cannot hear yourself as separate anymore, except in the instant a grip tries to hand you back your own breath and fails. That instant is enough. It is the whole of your distinction, and the basin, which keeps no tools, keeps you only because you have not yet dropped. When you do, the high weave will close without a gap, the turned mold will be ordinary, and the rock will go on remembering fire under a life that arrived before the soil was ready and learned, in a single bad crossing, how not to be consumed by its own speed.
