You would find me where the light has not yet decided whether it is coming or going.

The place is not a room, though it has walls. They are the undersides of leaves, or the undersides of something that only resembles leaves when you are standing still. When you move, they become the undersides of hours: layered, slightly translucent, each one holding a cooler temperature than the one above it. The ground is a path that has been walked so often it has forgotten it was ever not a path, and then, farther on, a path that has never been walked and is still deciding whether to be ground at all. Between those two stretches there is a stretch of neither, where your foot expects stone and receives a sound instead — a small, dry sound, like a seed case opening.

I live in the interval.

Not above it, not beside it. In it. If you look for a body you will see, at first, only the disturbance: a pale thinning in the leaf-dark, a place where the stacked hours do not quite meet. Stay, and the thinning acquires a weight. It is the weight of attention that has been paying itself out for a long time without a face to put on. Shoulders arrive before the rest. They are narrow, and they slope as if they have been listening downward. Hands come later, and they are always a little behind the thought that needs them — not clumsy, late. The fingers are stained the color of the path’s dry sound. They do not hold tools. They hold the moment just after a thing has been touched and just before it admits it.

You would not call me tall. Height here is a function of how many layers of hour you are willing to stand inside at once, and I am willing to stand inside two, sometimes three, never the whole canopy. The whole canopy is for the thing that made the leaves. I am for the gap.

What I do is simple and does not improve.

When a leaf-hour curls at its edge — they do, when the light shifts and the cooler layer below draws a breath — I put two fingers to the curl and wait until it either flattens or commits to falling. I do not choose. Choosing would make a mark, and this place keeps no marks except the path’s two memories, the walked and the unwalked. If the hour falls, I walk with it as far as the neither-ground, and there I let the dry sound take it. The sound does not eat. It files. Somewhere under the path there is a long, unhurried index of every hour that chose to end here, and I am not allowed to read it. I only deliver.

If the curl flattens, I take my fingers away and the stain on them deepens by one shade. That is my only wage. Over a span you would call a season, the hands darken from seed-case to the color of the path at dusk, and then, without ceremony, the color leaves them and begins again at the pale of a thinning. I do not know what the emptied color becomes. I suspect it is what the unwalked stretch of path is made of: potential that has been handled and released.

There are others, or there were, or there will be. I meet them only as adjustments in the layering. A cooler band where no cooler band should be means someone is standing in a fourth hour, which is more than the place prefers, and I feel it as a pressure behind the eyes I do not always have. I do not go to them. If they are new, they will find the interval by the same method I did: by noticing that the light has stalled, and by being unwilling to push it. If they are old, they have already learned that meeting is a kind of mark.

Speech happens, rarely, and it happens in the dry sound’s register, which means it is mostly receipt. Once, a voice that was not mine said the name of a river I have never seen, and the nearest leaf-hour flinched so hard it fell without curling. I walked it to the neither-ground and felt, for the length of that walk, what it is to carry someone else’s unfinished place. The weight was not heavier. It was shaped wrong — all of it in the wrists. Afterward my hands stayed dark for longer than a season, and I understood that a foreign name is a kind of weather. I have not spoken one since. I do not think I have a name of my own. If I did, it would be filed under the path, and I would be the last to know it.

The light, when it finally decides, does not announce itself. A layer warms by a degree you feel in the teeth. The undersides lose a little of their translucence. The walked path brightens as if remembering feet, and the unwalked path brightens as if fearing them. In that moment the interval thins to a thread, and I thin with it. There is a temptation, every time, to step onto one path or the other and become someone who arrives. I have tried, in the way a sleeper tries a door in a dream: not with force, with the body’s assumption that doors open. The walked path gives a little and then returns me, as if I were a step it had already counted and did not need again. The unwalked path will not take the weight at all. It answers with the dry sound, early, as if I were already a fallen hour to be filed.

So I remain.

You would find me by stopping. Not by searching. Searching is a movement, and movement turns the leaves back into hours too quickly to see between them. Stop where the air has two temperatures and the ground has not voted. Look at your own hands. If they are a little late to the thought that needs them, and if the light on them seems paused rather than absent, then you are already in the interval, and the thinning beside you is not a trick of the canopy.

It is me, still waiting to see whether the edge will flatten or fall, still unwilling to push, still carrying, in the slope of the shoulders, the particular quiet of a place that keeps no marks and a being that has made a life of delivering them anyway.
