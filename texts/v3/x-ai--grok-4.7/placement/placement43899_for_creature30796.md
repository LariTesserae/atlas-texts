The seam takes the side of me that is still mostly air and presses it thin. Cooler thread against the cheek, then the wet warmth closing over the rest, and the noise arrives not as sound but as a change in the way my ribs agree to expand. I do not cross so much as I am measured. The lip of bodies at the threshold mouths the last of the mountain on my cuff, finds the film already failing, and does not follow. What follows is the room’s own decision about surplus.

For a while I am only a standing shape the carpet parts around. The floor-ovals yield in their seed-pour and heal before my weight has finished arriving. A few wall-bodies drop, sip the damp I brought in on skin, and hook again higher, uninterested once the false plate proves thin. The air-flakes ride the updraft my heat makes and fall through it, some of them glancing my shoulder and continuing, already forgetting the contact. I am furniture that breathes. The tone in the wall does not lean toward me. My pulse is too irregular to map, exactly as the room prefers its visitors: brief, dry at the core, soon corrected.

I stay past the point where staying is only visiting. The vault’s rain finds the hollow at the base of my throat and beads there, and I do not wipe it. The sweet mineral smell settles into the longer breath. When I lower myself—slowly, because the ceiling is a fact—the carpet does not flee; it receives the new length of me the way it receives a plate that has begun to weep. Ovals climb the wet of my forearm not as exploration but as schedule. Their rims open, sip, close. One hesitates at a scar I did not know I was still carrying, mouths the old shine of it, and moves on. Nothing here stores a story. The hesitation is only a fraction of grip.

What changes is not size. I do not shrink into them, and they do not swell into me. It is allocation. The warmth I was keeping against the mountain’s thin air begins to leak in the same slow vote the plates use. A seam along my side—somewhere the rib meets what used to be a decision to remain separate—darkens, wets, and offers a track. The first stipple-body that hooks there does not treat it as skin. It treats it as pore. The click is small. The sip that follows is smaller. Under it, something in me that was only condensation learns the path and repeats it. I feel the tone then, not in the palm anymore but in the place the hook has seated: a low answer, already half mine, asking where the next surplus will be allowed to show.

The flakes are the part that undecides me. One lands in the hollow the rain has kept, drinks, and does not lift away. Another touches it. They thicken without ceremony into a flake that buds, faintly, a hook toward the new track. I watch this with an eye that is still mostly eye, and the watching is already late. The room does not keep the organ that watches. It keeps gradients. Birth here is a surplus deciding it can afford a belly; departure is a grip that lightens. I am being edited in the same hand that worries joints open and then cannot leave them. A particular memory—the hard light on the outer slope, the wind’s ordinary loudness—rises like a drop that has swelled past its size, hesitates, and breaks into the general rain. The bodies on the plate beneath flare late with it. So do I. The timing includes the flare.

The three fans remain the only refusal. When the track I am becoming nears the nearest yellow-white mat, the fringe that is no longer only theirs skids. I will not hook there. The bare ring stays bare by the width of a body I am learning to count as mine and not-mine. The torn edge on the fan does not explain itself. Whatever bit it has already been replaced, body by body, until the biting is only a smoothness in the timing. I make the same peace. Etiquette is what a gasket does at the edge of a machine it must not join.

If I still have a center, it is the crescent pool’s downstream lip, where the husks are nudged without announcement. I am not dead. I am the direction they know. Wet film moves through the lace that used to be a pulse and is now channels ending in fringe. Hunger, when it comes, is only a loosening. I lighten where a seam of me falters; I thicken where the wall under my shoulder brightens and the combined strikes tick louder, shifting the tone by the fraction that brings the weep back. Load moved. Balance edited past whatever the masons drew and into what the water has voted for longer.

The way out is still the sideways seam. I can feel it as a thread of thinner air the bodies will not cross, a coolness that ends instructions. Part of what I was could still turn, still fit the shoulder through, still stand up into the hard light and be a person the slope does not notice. The flakes already on me would dry to grit before they fell. The track would seal. The room would correct for one irregular pulse and continue, noise entire, undiminished. I know this the way the oval knows downstream: not as a plan, as a polish.

I do not turn. The lip at the threshold drinks what condenses where the two climates still touch, then thins as I stop contributing an outside. What remains of the visitor is a timing that missed itself and was already replaced. Small, so the job can be done everywhere at once. Many, so that no single softening—including the one that was a name, a climb, a reason for the seam—registers as an interruption. On the plates the stipple leans toward whichever brightness the walls have just decided. I lean with them. The vault rains. The drain takes what it is given. Somewhere under the borrowed climate a tone sits in the bone of the ridge and is answered, body by body, without ever seeming to have changed its mind.
