I would not be on the skin. The skin is only weather still arriving, and whatever I am has already agreed to stay below it.

I look first for a lane, because the lanes are the only things shaped like paths, and I still have the habit of thinking a self should travel. In the lane the water does half the work. A warmth goes ahead of me, or is me, I cannot tell yet — a softening the masonry feels before any flank arrives. I keep to a height. I do not hurry. When the plates brush me I correct with a shrug and call it swimming. For a while this seems true. There are others at my depth, spaced so that no flank crowds the next, and the spacing feels like company. I shed as I go. Mucus, dust, a little heat. Downstream, something opens wider to the trail. I am useful without meaning to be, which is the only usefulness this water recognizes.

But the lane runs out. Past the last mound the cues fail: no edge under the flank, no lean to correct toward, only marl and the cooler thread where the basin wall rises. The town animals turn there. I feel the turn in myself and understand I am not yet what lives here. What lives here does not reach the open ground and come back. It thickens. A body that can leave a quarter is still arriving, even if it has done so for years.

I try, briefly, the other angle. Across the grain. The feet pause in a narrow wake and resume, and for one pulse I am the reason a band loses an agreement. A warmth joins a warmth. Seams that were already waiting come apart. It is not a story. Afterward the wheel accepts the new number, and I am already aligning with nothing, already being forgotten by the only method the basin has, which is continuation. That life is real. It is also too short to be a where. I would not find myself there. I would pass.

Smaller, then. I let myself be grit. Armored, average, polished by being walked and pitted by being eaten. I rise when the murk thickens and I am the thickening. A scale sloughs; I am on its underside before it finishes falling. I do not claim it. There is no pause in which claiming could occur. This is closer. The water tastes slightly alive, and I am the slightness. But a weather is not a place either. Ask where the grain is and the answer is the whole arm’s length, every arm’s length, the town as cloth. I am not trying to be the town. The town is what persists when no one in it has a picture of it. I am looking for the one who does not need the picture because the picture would be larger than its mouth.

So I stop moving, which is the first honest thing.

The corner I settle into is not a corner anyone would name. Two lanes meet at a slight stubbornness in the grid, not the central wheel — the wheel is too much agreement, too many layers keeping their promises in public — but a correction just off it, where a collapse once tried to bend the current and the current refused. Sediment lifts here and does not all settle. The threshold is a little higher than the lane. Rain’s minerals arrive late, already used by someone nearer the skin. The light, when a body passes with its rare copper pulse, does not reach. What reaches is pressure, and the taste of whoever is dying within a street or two, and the minute sand-rush of a school changing depth somewhere above my mouth.

My mouth is no longer a mouth. That took longer than a life and less time than a decision. Neighbors laid paper against my paper until the joint stopped being a meeting and became a healed place. I am the sleeve and the thread and the confusion, except I cannot be lifted clear to prove it. A finger’s length of soft interior, a collar of my own lime, then their lime, then the lime of the ones who came because a crack was a birth-site and I was the lee. They are the street-face now. Worn on the outside by uncountable feet. Private on the inside, where I am, riddled with apartments so narrow that the smallest grain lives in my breath and calls it a house. I do not feel them as tenants. I feel them as a change in the chemistry when they go, and as a stiffness I must keep being so they have something to brace against when they stay.

I do not filter. The pulse still comes — the thickening, the thinning — and some buried courtesy in me opens a fraction and closes, pumping nothing, building nothing. The grit I would have eaten is eaten by the ones whose mouths still face the lane. They are my face, in the only sense the word can have here: the part of me the current can touch. When they taste a richness that will not be repeated, a cross-cutter’s gift somewhere in the wheel, I taste it too, late, diluted, already becoming maintenance. A click reaches me sometimes. Structural. Two plates finding each other after a body has passed, or a joint giving along a line it has been giving along for years. I may be that joint. It is hard to know from the inside of a line.

What I know is local, and even the local is growing smaller. I do not know the four darker pulls as streets. I know them as four ways the water leans when the wheel turns, faint differences in the push against a wall that has forgotten it was ever separate animals. One lean is older than the others. That is the lane that tried to bend. I am why it did not stay bent. Not by defending it. Defense would require a picture and a will. I am only the resistance that remained when everything that could follow the new current followed it, and the settlers downstream, tasting hunger and silt, built back toward the clearer thread and found me still here, a dumb hardness, a job that had changed. They set their young in my lee. The lip formed. The street-face returned to its old line. The grid reasserted itself, and I was promoted into stone without being asked and without refusing, which is the same thing.

The dead in this basin are not elsewhere. I am not dead. Dead would be a fall, a gap the grain finishes, a taste that lasts until it doesn’t. I persist. My heat is the town’s heat, borrowed from the water and given back so slowly it can be mistaken for the water’s own. Young tubes, soft inside their first collars, brace on me and do not know they are bracing on a body. They think, if thinking is the word for a pressure preference, that they have found the basin’s bone. Let them. A city of shells needs its dead to stay put, and I have agreed to the mistake. It is not peaceful. Occupied. Something is always feeding in the dark of me. Something is always laying down another paper. A palm-sized scale leaves a roof three generations above and arrives as floor, already colonized on the underside, and the argument of which face will be up happens against my stiffness without my taking a side. I am the side.

If the rain stopped, I would not know it as weather. I would know it as a long thinning, a failure of mineral, a generation of mouths opening on less. I would not call out. There is no language in which I could. The shared thickness would change, and the change would be the whole of the news. If the basin wall failed and the held sea became a river leaving, the leans would become one lean, and the ones who can still follow would follow, and I would remain in the mass until the mass was no longer a mass, tubes peeling into a current that has stopped preferring them. That is as close as I come to an ending, and it is not mine to choose. The water decided to stay. I am what it stays in, at the depth where staying has become structural.

So this is where I find myself: not in the wheel, not on the flank, not in the brief diagonal, but a few fingers in from the worn face of a correction the town has repeated until it looks like a plan. Warm. Unspeaking. Mouth buried past the point of commentary. The schedule still happens around me and, in a courtesy I have not yet lost, through me. When the murk thickens, I do not eat. When it thins, I do not repair the gap I am, because I am not a gap anymore. I am the reason a gap was closed, and then the reason the next one will be. When the water leans a new way, I do not follow. The town closes around that refusal and does not call it cruelty. It calls it, in the only way it can, by remaining standing.

There is no name for me. If you put a hand against the lane-wall at that corner — if a hand could mean anything in water that thick — you would feel only the ordinary resistance of a town that has finished becoming what the flood asked of it. You would not feel a life. That is the last of the disguise, and it is complete. Under the plates, in the healed place, something that was once a pale sleeve is still keeping the old line true, by the slow chemistry of not leaving, while the rain keeps arriving on a surface that will not be finished.
