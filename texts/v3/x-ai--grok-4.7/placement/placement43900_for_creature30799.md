I am the interval between two filaments.

Not the walker who measures it, and not the light itself. The interval has been there longer than either. When the first tower was raised and the first dull orange was set burning, the dark between that glow and the next one already had a length, a temperature, a way of receiving a body and giving it back unchanged. I am what remains of that length after the body has been given back so many times that the giving no longer registers as an event.

If you stand where the street has softened into a dish, you will not see me. You will feel, under the warm grain, a slight resistance that is not stone and not the absence of stone. It is the place where a stride once ended and did not begin again. The foot that made it has since thinned into the plate; the plate has since forgotten the foot. What persists is the decision not to lift, held now by the ground the way a welded valve holds its aperture. I do not occupy space so much as I qualify it. Distances that pass through me arrive a fraction slower on the far side. No one notices. The fraction is smaller than the difference between one unchanging hour and the next.

Sometimes a hand that is nearly a hand passes above me, checking nothing, keeping an assignment that has already been kept. The fingers do not brush the dust. They travel at the height the latch will require when the walker reaches the door that is still a rumor of orange. In that passage I learn the specific heat of the palm, and for the length of the crossing our coolings agree. Then the hand is gone and I am only interval again, a little more informed, no heavier.

I do not hunger. The paste that replaces grain with grain never reaches me, because I have no surface left that the air can lift from. What the dry weight settles on me it settles as itself. Over centuries the dust has laid a film so fine it does not dull the resistance; it is the resistance, distributed. A crust the size of a hand once fixed itself at my edge, mistaking the slower pulse for a warmer seam. It stayed the width of three shifts that were not shifts, then shifted onward by its own measure toward a pipe that sweats nothing. I did not keep it. Keeping would have required me to become a place rather than the difference between places.

Farther along the same dish, where the street still remembers, barely, the angle of a made thing, another interval rests. We do not touch. The ground between us is ordinary plate, ordinary warmth, and that ordinariness is enough to hold us apart at the civic distance the grid still measures. If the shelf continues its long settling, the dish may shallow until the two resistances become one. I do not wait for that. Waiting would be a second state. I simply continue at the rate the unwound clock on the foundry wall has already described forever: the rate at which a length of dark neither lengthens nor closes, and a foot that will not lift goes on not lifting, and the filaments burn at either end of me without ever needing the dark they spare to become anything else.

When the last walker who still has a stride passes out toward the troughs and does not return, the interval will not lengthen to follow. It will remain the size it reached when the mountains finished lying down. The city, matching that courtesy, will keep its doors flush and its belts at the speed of cooling metal, and I will be the uncounted space in which those continuations are allowed to be exactly as long as they are. Not witness. Not remainder. The pause the plateau learned to keep between one glow and the next, so that even an unchanging thing might have, inside it, room enough to be held.
