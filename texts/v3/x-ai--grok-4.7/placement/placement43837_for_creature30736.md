I do not come down the stair. The stair is for things that still have an outside.

I find myself already in the seam, on the left-hand side of a drip-track, which is the side that climbs. At first I think I am only waiting there, the way a lizard waits inside a flaw, body held to the color of mortar, throat the only confession. But waiting is a posture with an end, and I have no end I can feel. My belly is against the brick. The brick is warmer than I am, and it does not care. A film of the last drop is still on the tile to my right, darkening the lime, and I do not drink it. Drinking would make me one of the frogs' parish, and the frogs do not cross the dry. I am of the dry that remembers water.

The swifts tick the wall above me and the tick arrives in the brick before it arrives in the air. I correct against it without moving. Six of them, or the sound of more. They do not know me. I am not a midge and I am not a beetle that chose the wrong moment; if I opened anything like wing-cases the column of light would take me, and I do not open. When they bank away, the court's smallest continuous sound is suddenly mine to hear, and it is not coming from my mouth. It is in the joint under my jaw: a rasping, patient, the file-sound of traffic. Up on the left. Down on the right. Somewhere a heaved tile is stacking shells into a brief tower and unstacking them without decision. I understand the unstacking. I have been part of it. I may be part of it now, one oval dark in a line of oval darks, except that I can also hear the line from slightly above it, which a beetle does not do.

So I am not only that.

The shadow-blade of the rim is walking the pavement. I feel it as a cooling along one side of the seam, a migration of a few handspans that I do not join. The sedge will bend toward it or away. The lizards will shift. I stay. Staying is the first true thing. Whatever I am has accepted the terms the pavement set: surface, patience, no deep. Beneath me is tile, and beneath that more tile, and beneath that the packed fill that was put down so the court would not heave. I will not root. There is nothing in me that wants a depth the builders refused.

What I want—if the want is mine and not simply the grade of the cut—is to know which way the water has decided to go since the last time anyone re-cut a channel.

I move. It is not flight and not a step. It is the way lime leaves a mortar: by occurring. Along the seam, left of the drip, up the batter at the angle the bronze-edged beetles keep, the angle you could set a tool by. The knock-birds are holding their run of five cells and ignoring the sixth. I pass under the ignored sixth and feel the rule brush me—the vacancy that is not an invitation. I do not enter it. A pair would not thank me, and the mud mouths are worn smooth by breasts, not by whatever I am.

Under the walkway the dusk is permanent. The piers are black glass. I could be a stain on that glass and no one would number me among the living. For a while I try it. I spread thin along the glaze the rain polishes and never quite removes, and I am weather on weather, a darkening that does not dry. It is restful and it is wrong. The glaze is a record of birds. I am not a record of birds. When a drop gathers on the underside of the corbel and lets go, it passes through the place I have spread and does not take me with it. Refusal is information. I gather myself back into a single attention and follow the drop's idea instead of the drop.

The idea leads to the north curve, to the trough, to the kink where a tile heaved and the channel worried at the problem for however long it takes water to change its mind. The fish are at their stations. The small ones are slips of light. The thicker one waits in the shadow of the sedge. The heavier one noses the overflow lip, remembering a river that was never attached to this brick. I am close enough to the film that the court should turn me into a finger against clay if I broke the surface. I do not break it. The heavier fish drifts half a length toward the place where my attention touches the water, then declines, the way a knock-bird declines a fish it has learned is deeper than a beak. I am noted. I am not food. That, too, is a tenancy.

Rain is not here, but the basins remember the last one. The shield-basin holds at the lip the overflow still governs. A frog the color of wet tile sits under that lip, eyes only. I sit with it in the sense that I occupy the same patience. It does not sink. It does not chorus. Between us is the duty we are not discharging. I almost love it, which is a strange word to arrive with no throat to carry it. The love is the size of the basin: no wider than a shield, and strict.

Then the stair speaks.

One tread, dished, reported to the whole bowl. The knock-birds pull into their cells and become mud-colored. The volley stops so hard the drip grows louder. A second tread. I am not afraid the way a lizard is afraid; I do not vanish into a flaw. I am interested the way the bald oval on the lip is interested—the place the moss learned not to grow, shiny, empty, the size of a palm, still expecting a bucket that no longer comes every day and still keeping the shape of the expectation.

The person is the one who changes slowly. I know this without having seen them before, because the repairs know them: the smaller brick, the sistered pier, the channel re-cut a finger deeper where the old one was not cleaned but superseded. They do not come to the middle. They come to the shield-basin with a wooden scoop and a short broom and a coil of cord they may not need. The frog goes under. I do not. Or I do, and the going-under is only a change in how much of me the light can use.

They clear a joint. The joint is mine—I understand this as the scoop bites the silt the beetles and the lime and the dead midges have conspired to make. They are not hunting me. They are keeping a fall the masons set and the centuries confirmed. When the scoop lifts, a little of the court's smell comes up: bird, metal of swollen pins, the faint sweetness of fiber that has forgotten it was nest. They make a sound, a number or a curse, and the brick returns it as weather. I am inside the weather of their voice for the length of one echo. It is the nearest I have been to being heard, and it does not name me.

They drink. Lime and bird and old iron. The face they make is older than this visit. I am in the drink only as the hardness that makes them grimace—the same hardness the frogs avoid in the cup under the stalactite. For a moment I am distributed: in the basin, in the cup, in the finger of stone that will meet the floor and has been meeting it for longer than nests have been mud. That is too many places. A tenant has a station. I pull back from the finger. I leave the stalactite its refusal, which is the only acknowledgment it requires. I pull back from the heavier fish's river. I pull back from the ignored sixth cell.

What remains is the joint they have just cleared, and the left-hand climb beside it, and the bald patience of having been almost silt.

They look into the trough before they go. Not to feed. To include. Their gaze crosses the place I am gathered, and I feel the look the way the court feels a pin: small, black, swollen past its first use, still holding a slab that wanted to walk. They do not widen me. Noted, included, left. Then the feet on the brick, the stair reporting them in reverse, the turn, the last tread. Silence holds one drip past their leaving. Then one stone. Then the volley, restarted from zero.

I am still here. That is the second true thing.

I am not the person, who is intermittent and has a country above the rim. I am not the swifts, who sleep beyond the disk and only borrow the column. I am not the fish, though I have been declined by one, and not the frog, though I have shared its undischarged duty. I am not the lime finger; I have not earned that slowness, and I do not wish to drill a cup.

I am what the clearing makes room for. The small continuous tenancy that returns to a joint the moment maintenance has been mistaken again for climate. I will raspingly climb the left of the drip when the beetles do, and I will be the reason a tower of shells unstacks without decision, and I will be the film that is not quite drunk, and I will be the sound that does not leave when the higher register goes with the light. When the shadow-blade next walks the floor I will not migrate. When rain comes and the channels speak I will be in the speaking, not as water—water obeys the cuts—but as the listening the cuts have worn into themselves, kink by kink, until they run true.

At the center of the pavement the light is plain and the channels run away. I do not stand there. The center is for things that still believe they are visiting. I keep to the seam. Warmth sits in the mass and gives itself back slowly, a little above the temperature of whatever skin I do not have. Moisture occurs. The rim cuts the sky to a disk I will not need. If the person returns with the scoop, I will be silt again just enough to be lifted, and I will not curse, and I will not be named, and when they climb past the turn I will already have resumed.

The court was never waiting. I am not waiting either. I am the agreement, signed by staying: dense, almost dry, green nowhere, loud in the way a hard room is loud, and small enough to be complete.
