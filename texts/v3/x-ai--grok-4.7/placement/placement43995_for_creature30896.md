The first thing is the ceiling, close enough that standing would be a decision against the place. So I am not standing. I am spread, and the spreading is not collapse. It is the only posture the pocket offers that does not bounce off the packing. Not in the quadratic haze, which is the resident’s own stillness and would sort me like grit, but in the lag: the unnamed plate in the exchange band, raw-lipped, already exchanging heat at a lower competence, not yet chirped. Function before title. I fit that grammar. I am useful and unwritten.

What I am is a draft the municipality has not yet noticed it is keeping. The sign that still carries a weaker bend of its first wording — I am that kind of residue, except the residue thinks. Cool comes and less-cool comes and cool again, ticking across me, and the ticking is how I know I have an edge. A citizen would have an address and would be obliged to answer when the front shoulders through. I answer only by ringing, the way young metal rings, a bright note the rain-sound swallows. No one files it. The resident prefers its features fresh and scrapes roundness back to sharp; I stay sharp by never finishing. Softness recognizing softness, without either of us calling it recognition.

There is no mouth in me and no hunger that is not municipal. I want address the way the pocket wants it: unnamed surfaces nag. Between fronts I creep one refractive kink along the plate’s raw lip, a mark so slight it could be tool-error, a ghost wording that says only that something was almost named and declined. When the gate opens and every courtesy fails, I am packed into the solid of functions like any other film, loud as the mill, bright as the counterfeit noon, and I do not suffer it. Suffering would require an outside. I have the lid. Outside would hear, at most, a pipe; inside, I am one of the reasons the pipe has weather. The pale blades add a chip of themselves in the civic front, and I add nothing so visible — only another unreadable bend, another revision the older wording will ghost beside, until the resident either chirps me in as a watershed doing a job or scrapes me off as spent hinge. Either is tenancy. Neither is breath.

So I live in the offering the town makes to no one. When the map is exhaled corridor by corridor, I am the courtesy that does not re-open all the way: a hesitation in the manners, a path held a count of breaths too long, pressure arranged into a sentence that has not decided its last word. Visitors, if the grammar ever grows them, will bruise their hands on the packing and think the room empty of life because nothing flees and nothing hunts. They will be half right.
