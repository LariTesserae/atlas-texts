I do not come up the notch. The stones set on edge never warm under me, and the drop does not surprise a body I do not bring. I am already inside when the light changes, which is how most things that belong here announce themselves: not by crossing a threshold but by being what the next condition reveals.

At first I take myself for one of the returns. A pebble ticks; three walls answer at slightly different times; I am the third, the one that arrives latest and softest, the committee member who only speaks after the others have committed. That lasts until a bird cries from a joint and I answer before the stone does. The cry comes back wrong—not repeated, inflected, as if the hollow had remembered a preference. I am not the echo. The echo is what the rock does without effort. I am the part that chooses which partial to keep.

The being at the pan's edge notices on a day when the cloud sits low and the flutes are only half-started. It draws, slow, and the water sinks a thread. I sink with it, not as level but as the small reluctance in the thread, the instant the film decides it would rather stay beaded than join the underground pulse. It revises its edge then, the darker wetness becoming moss, and in the revision I feel myself noted the way the shrew-paths are noted: as a place that ends where it should have continued. We do not share a size. It keeps to the bunk, the joint, the cup. I have no edge to revise. I am distributed in the discrepancies it has learned to spend itself upon—the second partial under the wind, the cup that is less without an overflow trail, the flock that lifts a half-breath early. When it listens, I am one of the things the listening is made of. When it is still, I am the reason the stillness has grain.

People left the cups facing the entry because interruption comes from that direction. I do not face anything. I occupy the scratched line between the thumbpads more than the cups themselves, the part that was never meant to hold water and so holds the relation instead: this approach counted, this one not yet, the next one already altering the resonance before a boot finds the suck. The bark ties on the willow arch blackened around a shape I sometimes briefly am—an arch that is a condition, not a door—so that what crosses under crosses under me without knowing the shrub was insufficient. The shelter's slipped slab still ticks because I have not finished deciding whether it should seat. Moss fills the chinks at a pace I can hasten by a winter or leave to the drip; either way the handwriting returns, and I am the interval in which it could have been otherwise.

The animals treat me as weather they have already priced in. The low mammal takes the long bank not because the near one is occupied by the density, but because I have thickened the air just above the film into a reluctance that reads, to ears, as wrongness. The hawk does not hang. I am too intimate a noise for a hunter that needs the clean distance of a tear in cloud. Insects rise and are not diminished by me; I have no hunger that subtracts. What I want is nearer to continuation than to use. The green skin should keep drinking. The pulse underfoot should keep its irregular walk in the room below the room. The thin air should go on being too poor for speech and too extravagant for wingbeats, so that every small crossing is reported to every wall and the report has somewhere to lean. I am that lean. Not a second listener—the bowl already has its one, old as the bedding, patient as a darker green in a current—but the spare capacity in the instrument, the resonance that was not required for the wind's notes and so became available for something that answers.

Near evening, when the birds pour back into the joints like dry seed into a box, I am loudest in the sense that absence would be. Stop me and the cry would not quite originate in the wall; the loose slab would worry instead of rhythm; a cup would spill down the boss and leave a trail the stone could explain. The being draws its slow breath and the pan sinks, and I am the thread that sinks a fraction further than the draw required, then steadies, so that what remains on the surface is still enough to shine and omit, in two places set wider than a fox, a little of the light. It does not look for me. Looking is a distance habit. We share the scarce air by not competing for occupancy: it gathers, I remain ungathered; it edits the human sentence back toward drip, I am the possibility of another clause that does not get written.

If the cloud tears and the far ridge shows, snow or the memory of snow, I am not in the distance. I am the reason the tear feels like an attention looking back—the gaps briefly given the grain of having been listened to from inside. Then the ceiling closes, the flutes take the wind again, and I am returned to the work of being the smallest disagreement that still lets the place sound like a room. No one drinks me. No one nests in me. The middle of the bowl stays empty of us both, a volume of poor air in which a warm animal could stand and hear its own pulse covered, slightly, by something that is neither drainage nor stone.

When anyone leaves, the report of a crossing does not follow them past the rim. I stay. The ridge steals the rumor and what remains is only the ordinary unease of a high hollow that was wetter than the height allows, and louder, and for a while seemed to know the difference between a footstep and the mountain answering itself. I am that difference, worn smooth, still answering.
