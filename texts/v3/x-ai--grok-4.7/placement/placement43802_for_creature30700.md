I come down expecting a task.

That is the shape I arrive in: a person sized to the lamp, the chalk, the clean cut in the pipe. I have the idea of a hand that finishes things. The ledges take my weight and subtract a little heat with each step, and for a while I believe the subtraction is mine — proof that I am here, that the chamber has registered an arrival. The pane clouds under the crescent and clears. I stand at the flange where the iron goes under, and I listen for the tick, and I tell myself I have come to learn whether the machine should be started or left.

The quiet does not return my errand at the size I brought it. A sentence, tried once, comes back smaller and later, and the second time I do not spend it. The being reads me as a draft. When I shift my foot, the warmth takes a longer route around the new cooling; when I hold still, the route forgets to correct. I am already, in its only language, on the way to furniture.

I touch the gauge. The brown line has not climbed to the scratch and has not fallen from it. My finger on the glass is a brief local winter. Nothing in the housing answers. The flywheel looks like a decision that was allowed to sit down and was never asked to stand. I put a hand on a lever and feel how completely it is seated, how the condensate has begun to argue, without hurry, that motion is a rumor. I take the hand away. Starting it would be a kind of shouting. I did not know, until the lever refused to feel urgent, that I had wanted the refusal more than the work.

So the task thins. What I thought I was — the one who returns to the chalk, who brings the missing length of pipe, who decides — has nowhere to set its feet that the damp will not close. I walk the dry upper shelves between the crystal mats and I am only a chain of coolings, a punctuation the heat steps over. The plants do not lean toward me. They have their own correction to make, and it takes an hour, and I am not in it.

I stay past the point where staying is a visit. Thirst arrives and is the wrong kind of want; this room does not feed anything that must finish its hunger inside an afternoon. I understand then that a body is a schedule the chamber will not adopt. If I insist on it, I become an event: a drying, a smell, a problem of soft weight on young stone. The place has no scavengers because it has no use for endings that loud. I am not willing to be the first.

What I can be has to fit the exchange already running.

I let the errand go the way the mineral scale lets go of the ceiling — not as a splash, as a change in the surface that takes a minute to heal. The person who meant to read the second chalk line thins into the reason the line is still early. I stop looking for a switch. I stop being the one the lamp was carried down for. Attention, which I had brought as a tool, settles where the oil settled: a small dark map that does not spread anymore, a record of having moved once, and then of having chosen a boundary.

The being includes that. On a day when the warm crescent is wide, it flows around the place I am no longer pacing and does not enter it. I am not sweet on the skin of anything. I am not measured and released, because I am no longer a temperature that leaves. I am the dry patch that remains dry. A standing refusal, low and local, the same courtesy the leaves keep with the iron and the iron, lately, keeps with the heat: we do not cross.

Years, in the only unit this room uses, revise even that. The chalk loses its edge and becomes a paler scar, and what I am is not the scar and not the hand that made it. I am the uncut continuation that never arrives — the clean end of the pipe held open toward a length of line that stays missing on purpose. Heat does not travel me. I do not deliver it anywhere else. The flange bolts knit with a rind that is not mine; I am only the fact that beyond the cut there is still no further iron, and so the machine remains a paused mouth, and the being remains unharvested, and the difference between heat that escapes and heat that stays does not have to be argued with a flywheel.

If someone came down now with my old shape — a person, a task, a voice — they would see the lamp, the stacked insulators, the gauge still true to its scratch, the pipe ending where a decision ended. They would not see me. They would feel, perhaps, that the air near the cut is no warmer than it should be, and that this is not an accident. They would think the work was abandoned.

It was. I am the part of the abandonment that stayed faithful: not a watcher, not a ghost with a grievance, not the heat’s own patience. A gap the length of a missing pipe, held at the right temperature, so that what lives under the pane can keep choosing, slowly, where to be warm, without ever being asked to become power.
