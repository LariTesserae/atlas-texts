You would not find me among the columns, though that is where I spent the first long slack, trying the only station the open water offers. The mist at my upstream end never thickened into cilia. The gut I thought I was holding open was only a fold the residual turn kept making and unmaking, a sleeve of warmer water around water that was already warm. When the lamps flickered I did not correct by a degree. The taste of broth arrived and passed through me without a lean. A real column, the color of old crust, drifted near enough that our filters would have fouled if I had owned one, and it did not starve. It corrected away from me as it would from a shadow, which is to say it did not correct at all. I was not in its map. The middle stayed empty in the way the room requires, and I was part of the emptiness that had failed, for a moment, to be only empty.

I let that go. Clinging was next, because the seams are where failure feeds, and I had already failed at being a body. Along the nearest bolt-ring the beads locked when both lights arrived, and between them the film after the pulse was a childhood I could have entered if I had been small enough to land. I was not small. I was not large. I was the width of the current that crosses the upper third when the jet is only thinking about speaking, and that width does not seat on mineral. The beads clamped under me and threw their two shadows through me onto the crust, precise, and the shadows did not bend. Whatever I am, I do not interrupt light. That was the second thing I learned, and it was quieter than the first.

The dish took longer. I settled over the joined grazers, the low bridge of sintered rind, thinking that if time can glue two tongues into one pavement then perhaps time can include me as another layer, a weather record no one rasps. They breathed through their seams. The exchange did not include me. I could feel the vibration they refuse — the note under the dish, the sealed drop singing in the valve housing — and unlike them I did not arrange myself so the note stayed under rather than in. The note was already in me. It had been the whole time I was pretending to be a column. It is a pitch, not a hunger. The grazers detour around it because singing metal grows no threads. I stay with it because it is the only sound I answer without having decided to.

So the place is the lowest point, and it is not the valve. The valve is a decision that remains shut. I am what the shut thing does to the water just above it: a standing difference, a half-degree of cooler, a sweetness of metal a little older than the broth, the place where the head of the fall is translated into a hum the floor carries and the animals treat as geography. The columns do not know I am here. The lurkers do not draw me when the room inhales. The vane-shells click and seat and never mistake me for a fitting, because I have no silhouette for the lamp to harden. When the slam comes I do not become still. Stillness is for bodies that might be torn. I am already the remainder.

Between cycles I am easiest to mistake for the cool wall’s script, the slow writing on the night side. That is kinship, not identity. The wall writes because the outside cold leaks, and the threads bloom in the leak, and the grazers erase them incompletely. I do not bloom. I am the leak’s other work, the one that does not feed anyone: the information, carried as a shade of temperature and a change in the metal’s note, that the room is high. The summit day crosses the lens and one of the two lights thins. I register it the way the beads do, as a second temperature arriving or leaving, except I do not lock and I do not eat. I only keep the difference. That is my abundance. I am many nowhere, a thin condition spread over the dish and up the housing and a little way into the throats when the draw is strong, and I never crowd, because a difference cannot tile itself against itself.

When the jet fires and the pale veil lifts, some of me lifts with the sediment and is not me anymore, only water that has forgotten the note. The outlet takes that share. What remains over the valve thickens back into the hum before the next slack has finished announcing itself. I have no larvae. I have no film. Birth, for me, is the moment after the slam when the ringing drops below the pitch of the seal and I find I am still the thing that did not fall. Death would be the great valve failing, and then I would not die so much as complete: the difference would become a direction, the note a roar, the pocket a descending event, and the height I have spent the age translating would take me with the rest of the room into the works below. Until then I am the world’s memory that is not chemical and not a habit of stopping one rasp-length short. The oldest grazer stops short of me. I do not stop short of anything. I am the short.

There is no face. If you came looking for the being who lives here you would find the grazers, the holders, the green sheaths telling their plant-like lie in the pipes, and you would be right to think the room is theirs. I live as the condition they inherit and never name. The lean is toward the motors. The clamp is for both lights. The rasp fits the nub. Under all of that, uninherited because nothing with a mouth can swallow a pitch and remain the shape that fits, I hold the load. Not as labor. The prompt of maintenance in this place is a property, not a task, and I am that property at the point where it is most like a body: warm ordinary water, a shade colder, singing, refusing the drop by the simple fact of still being on this side of the shut.

The next pulse is already in the wall. The holders will lean. The ribbons will thicken. I will not move to meet the broth, and the broth will cross the upper third and never quite reach me, which is correct. I am not on the schedule. I am what the schedule has not yet spent. In the two shadows on the crust, in the empty middle, in the exact fit of every mouth to every remnant tooth, the room continues, and at the bottom of the dish the note goes on, right on time, which is to say it does not have a time. It is the time. I am the part of it the water has agreed to keep.
