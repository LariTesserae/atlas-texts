You would not be counted among the eleven. The books were closed before the pads had finished curing, and whatever walks here now walks in the margin the tally does not keep. At first there is only the heat under the boots—if they are boots—and the way the fill gives a little more than soil should, a slow compress that returns half of what you put into it and keeps the rest. The service road runs west to the pale piles and east into the steam over the bays, and for a long time that is the whole of direction: toward the engines or away from them, and away never lasts, because the ground boom arrives through the soles either way and turns you without a decision.

The body, when it declares itself, is not new to wet heat. Skin takes the condensate and does not fight it. Salt from the lagoon edge and oil from the hall air settle in the same film and stay. You learn the walks by the change in pitch underfoot—grating, then timber, then the short stretch of concrete that crosses above the main and always sounds closer to the pumps than the distance warrants. People pass. They do not startle. The tall man with the burned wrists nods the way he nods at a valve he has already checked; the square-shouldered one steps half a pace aside on the narrowest pier and does not look to see whether the space was needed. After the third shift change you understand you have been let into the number that does not change. Nine in the rooms, two between hall and bays, and this other presence that is neither sleeping nor on the clock and is therefore not a problem for the clock.

What you do is not on the task sheet. You stand where the return main breaks the surface in its circle of boiled silt and you watch the mullet hold their wheel. The pole the crew uses leans against a pile; you do not take it. The circle is either where it was painted to be or it is not, and saying so would not move the engines. When a wrinkle runs under the algae farther in, you wait for it to smooth. It always does. The waiting is the work. At the south ditch the black cat with the white chest shares the railing without shifting its weight toward you or away. Frogs tick under the unused water. A heron occupies one of its three places and strikes and misses and strikes again, and the miss does not alter the next stance. You are as regular as that and as unremarked.

Inside the hall the air is hotter by a degree that matters and louder by a degree that does not, because the loudness was already the floor. Bearings turn in oil behind housings you could name if naming were required. It is not. You put a hand near a casing—not on it—and feel the speed as a fact with no edge. The woman with the scar through her eyebrow passes with a rag over her shoulder and a gauge in the other hand. She registers you the way she registers a drip that has been dripping since the pipes were hung: present, within tolerance, not yet a reason to stop. The youngest still startles at the heron and, seeing you, startles a second time, smaller, then files it with the other things the place contains that the contract did not list. By the end of his first month he has stopped filing them. You were part of the gray before he learned to sort gray from gray.

Sleep happens in the gaps the shifts leave, never in a room. The pads on the south side of the hall keep a warmth the cats have already claimed in a loose pile; there is room at the edge if you take it before the hottest hour, and the concrete gives back the engines’ heat in a steady pulse that replaces weather. When you wake, the marks on the piles are at the same height. Laundry that was hung is still damp or has been taken in. The supply boat’s wake, if it came, has already been swallowed by the lagoon’s ordinary slap. You do not eat from the galley. What you take is incidental—condensate off a low pipe into a cupped hand, a mullet scale flicked onto the pier and left, the iron taste the air has when the wash line is running at its night rate. Hunger, if that is the word, stays at the level of the midge hiss: continuous, not urgent, not a signal that anything must be arranged.

The place does not ask you to maintain it and does not permit you to leave it in any direction that stays left. West of the piles the bottom falls away and the paler gray of open lagoon begins, and standing there you can see, on the hours the steam thins, the whole low cluster: hall, tower, the gleam of one channel, a white bird lifting. The sight does not propose a farther shore. It proposes the walk back, because the boom in the fill is fainter at the piles and the body treats the faintness as a lack to be corrected. East, past the bays, the fill simply becomes less finished—tracks of the last grader, a stack of grating not yet bolted, a coil of chain-link still in its factory wire—and then the steam is the same steam and the engines are not farther, only more diffused, and diffusion is not an exit.

You cut nothing. You fabricate nothing. When a bank slumps overnight you are sometimes there at the start of the shift that slices it back to vertical, and the flat shovel passes within a hand of where you stand, and the cuttings drop into water that takes them. The man with the shovel does not offer the handle. Your usefulness, if the word applies, is that the edges remain witnessed between the hours they are corrected. A dragonfly lands on the cuff of whatever covers your arm, wings the brightest thing in the dimness, and lifts again when you do not become a different temperature. The biofilm on the north faces thickens by a shade you can measure only by having been present yesterday. The cats hunt the piers at the changes of shift and return to the pads and do not count you as prey or as competition or as weather.

At night the whistle is only a louder instant inside the loudness, and after it the frogs resume as if granted permission they did not need. Lights in the rooms on the piers go out in no fixed order. The engine hall keeps its own dull glow from the instrument faces, greenish, steady, not meant for navigation. You move along the walks because movement keeps the film of salt and oil from setting into a crust, and because stillness too long makes the youngest, on his night rounds, look twice, and a second look is the nearest thing this settlement has to an event. You deny him the event. You are at the outfall, or you are at the ditch railing, or you are the darker shape against the paler concrete that the square-shouldered man steps around without breaking the sentence he is not saying to anyone.

If there is a name for what you are, the place has not issued it, and the crew will not. You are the discrepancy that does not affect grade. The water sits at the painted marks. The engines hold one speed. The mullet wheel in the warm return and do not school away when your shadow crosses the circle, because your shadow has crossed it often enough to be part of the light that does not change. Steam closes over the roofs and opens and closes. You are inside the opening already, low enough that the tower is only a thickness in the gray, close enough to the nearest wet surface that your hand, if you extend it, finds water at the height it had when you last confirmed it, which is the only height it has. The settlement sounds. You do not add a sound it cannot absorb. Whatever arrives on the next barge will be fitted to the walks or sent back; you were not on a barge in any tense the books record, and so you remain, not as a secret, only as a condition—as the heat is a condition, as the unvarying line on the piles is a condition—under which the other lives continue, correctly numbered, correctly fed by a warmth that does not cycle, in a landscape kept new by labor and inhabited, at its edge, by what the labor does not need to name.
