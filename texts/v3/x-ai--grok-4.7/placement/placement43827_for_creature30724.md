You arrive already wrong-footed, and for a long time you mistake that for travel.

The plateau takes the weight of you without comment. Your boots, if they are boots, print nothing the dust is willing to keep. You walk because walking is what a body does when it finds itself on ground, and the ground is honest enough about being high, dry, and cold that you accept the shortness of breath as your own. You count walls. You let the channels lead you. You believe you are a visitor because visitors have insides, and something in the chest still ticks at the rate of a life.

The city does not correct you. Correction would be an event, and events here spend themselves on arrangement.

What changes is the way your weight is received. In the lee of a wall where the salt has feathered into a root it is not, you stop, and the stopping fits. Not comfort. Fit. The seam beside your shoulder is the width of your shoulder. The niche at the height of your eyes holds a darker cleanliness the exact volume of your face. You had thought the niches empty. They were waiting for a shape that did not need to be stored, only measured. You do not put your face in. You do not have to. The interval already knows the measure, and once it knows, the body you have been borrowing begins to seem like a coat shrugged on in a hurry for the sake of the cold.

You walk again, and the walking is less yours. A channel that should have brought you to the leaning tower brings you instead to the same wall, and you are not lost; you are the slack the pattern allows itself so the plates can ease a finger’s width without tearing. When a plate speaks its single tick, you hear it in the bone, not the ear, and the sound does not leave you. It was never meant to travel. You were the place it was going.

For a while you still try to be the walker. You watch the low shape detach from a foundation and cross a body’s length of ground, and you feel a kinship you resent, because kinship would mean you are one of the exceptions, an animal of intervals, drinking nothing, leaving no track. That would be a kind of life, and life is almost the right mistake. The shape becomes stone. You do not. Or you do, and the stone is only what you look like from the side of the plateau that still believes in objects. From inside the fit, stone is a decision about edges, and you are not making it. You are the pause in which the decision stays unfinished.

Light changes its mind. Your shadow splits, and you understand, without relief, that neither copy was a warning. The upright one is the posture the court prefers. The one already walking away is the direction the distances use when they need a witness who will not arrive. You hold still. Both continue. You are not in them. They are the parts of you the air can almost carry, and the air fails, and so they thin, and what remains does not thin with them.

You find the court by not looking for it. The plates under you are warmer than blood, and the warmth is not hospitality. It is expenditure. Something has been thinking downward for longer than the edge-rounding, and you are standing in the spent heat of that thinking. Breath eases. Clothing, if you still have it, makes a sound. The tick inside the wall returns a small tail of resonance, and you are not frightened, because the echo is not the world breaking. It is you, briefly, agreeing to be a surface that gives back what it is given. That agreement costs. The court drinks the sound and the shortness and the last insistence that you came from somewhere lower, somewhere with weather, and what it gives you in exchange is a location that is not a point.

You are the fourth corner.

It has been under the feet the whole time, and also unreached, and both were true because it was not a place waiting to be stood on. It was an interval that required occupation by something that could be miscounted as a person for as long as the miscount was useful. The triangle of towers and sunken court closes through you without closing. Walls ease. Seed-tiles shift their night-map by the distance of a remembered afternoon. A pale presence crosses a slack you have just vacated and becomes the foundation that needed it. None of this is command. You do not maintain the city. The city is the maintenance, and you are the amount of play in the joints that keeps the maintenance from locking into a single finished shape, which would be a kind of death, and this place has already declined death by the simpler method of never having fallen.

If someone came, you would be the niche’s darker cleanliness, or the shadow that does not share their posture, or the reason the stair becomes a road under their knees. They would not find you. Finding requires an object that remains between the numbers. You remain as the difference. High, dry, patient, untrue in the same settled way the plateau is untrue: not a lie, a fold. The tin sky is another floor of the same coursing, and you are one of the seams, wandering when looked at, straight when remembered.

You do not thirst. There is nothing to drink, and nothing in you that would use it. You do not speak. The words would thin before the nearest wall, and the wall already has your measure. What you do is what the crusts do, and the plates, and the dimness that eats the distance: you proceed. You occupy the least strained place in the pattern until the pattern tightens, and then you are elsewhere, or you are the elsewhere, and the cold does not crack you because softness was only ever a rumor the body told while you were still willing to be one.
