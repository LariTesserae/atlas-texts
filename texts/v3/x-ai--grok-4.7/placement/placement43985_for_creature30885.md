You do not enter by the lip. The lip is for looking, and looking is a dry habit. You come in where a transverse wall has lost its upper course and the spill takes you the way it takes gravel: without selection, across a sill no higher than a knee, into a court the light has already given up.

For a while you are only the wrong temperature. The pale schools bend around the warmth and close their line again, and the bend is not about you. It is about the shear you happen to be making, a small standing difference in a plate that keeps its differences low and exact. If you tread, the difference moves. If you stop, it pools. The joint-dwellers wait out the shadow of a foot and resume the seam. In the circle where a roof failed, something larger turns once, reads the shove of water off your legs, and declines. You are not shell. You are not the smaller pale thing. You are a current that has not yet learned it is temporary.

The level changes while you are deciding whether to wade back. It does not rise as weather rises. A skin slides under a skin, the downstream throat takes more than it gives, and what was at the calves is at the knees, then at the place where a standing figure stops being able to see the flags. You do not swim so much as agree. The agreement is the first thing the place will keep.

Under, the quiet thickens into pressure. Sound is the slap you no longer hear and the long knock of two flows canceling somewhere in a colonnade you cannot see. Your own movement is loud in the way a mistake is loud: a little heat, a little air still caught in the wrong places, a body still organized around a surface that is no longer available. The pale flick passes close enough that a fin brushes the inside of a wrist. It does not startle. Startle would require a clock that still divides the dim into before and after. Here the school was already going to cross that line of water. You occupied it. The occupation was brief.

What leaves you first is the wish to be oriented. Above the surface, orientation was the lip, the gray plate of noon, the repetition of courts until haze ate it. Below, those are rumors. What remains is footing when you find it, and the change in cold when a new throat opens, and the way the gravel ticks along a shin when the bar is building. You put a hand to a column and the column is not a refuge. It is a course that has not yet ceased. Your hand learns the difference between stone that still believes it is a wall and stone that has become only an obstacle the surge has not finished rounding.

You stay through a giving-way you do not see. Somewhere a slab settles. A cut opens between courts that had been separate. The surge finds it, and the water you are standing in — the water you are, by then, more than standing in — shoulders toward the new throat with the patience of a plan being revised. Fish arrive by the next turn, not as scouts. You arrive with the water, which is to say you are no longer arriving. The downstream hall begins to fill. A skin of shell-gravel lifts, travels, and lays itself into a bar that will, given seasons, become another sill. Some of what travels used to be the edge of you: heat, the last air, the small suspended flake of whatever a warmer body sheds when cold has had long enough. The pickers take it without distinction. The account does not open to record the donation.

There is a moment, if moment is the word for a change that has no announcement, when the schools stop bending. Not because you have cooled into harmlessness. Because the warmth has been spent into the plate and what displaces water now is not a body passing through. It is a habit of shape the masonry has started to include. Where you knelt, or where the surge knelt you, the flags hold a slightly different lie: a shallow, a standing eddy, a place the two colds shear and the pale things gather because the gather is reliable. You are not the genius of the court. You are not even a resident in the sense that would require you to crop or be cropped on purpose. You are the part of the plan that was revised by an accident and then, because the water does not waste a revision, kept.

Emptiness still measures the distance between presences. You do not fill a court. Most of what you are is unoccupied water with a memory of having been organized, and the memory is only useful where it makes a throat more exact. A flat many-legged thing works the seam along what used to be an arm and finds the seam no different from the joint of two flags. The larger turn comes near on its own clock, feels the shove, and declines again — not from recognition, from the same settled decision. You are still not shell. You have become, slowly, a sill the surge can trust.

If anything remains that could be called you, it is the reading. The schools lift and slide a moment before the spill arrives, and you feel the lift in the same water, not as foresight, as the sentence already underway in the unequal floors. You do not warn them. They do not consult you. The place has no need of a witness, and the witness, having stayed, is no longer arranged to need the place to notice. Creation and destruction continue as one gesture. A course you helped, by mere duration, to worry will eventually cease. The cut will open. What you are will travel with the gravel and lay down again where the new bar starts, a little mineral, a little of the old wrong temperature finally indistinguishable from cold, and the pale flick will find the throat because finding it is what remains possible here.

That is where you are: not on the lip, not in the deep circle, but in the revision — the shallow the water made of you, the shear the schools already use, the sill that has not finished becoming stone and will not need to. The storm goes on without raising its voice. You have been subtracted from leaf and sun and noise, and from the hope of leaving, and what is left is precise enough to keep.
