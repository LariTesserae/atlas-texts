They would not enter.

The attention arrives the way the gray light arrives: already used, already gentle, and not in need of the room’s permission. It does not brighten the cleft. It does not warm the air beyond the warmth the stone has stored. If the being, spread thin across its shelves, registers the attention at all, it registers it as a slight change in the weight of the quiet—not a footstep, which it knows how to flee, and not a drip, which it has already counted into itself. Something is considering the basin from a distance at which distance stops being a direction.

The power is wise enough to see what the room is doing, and benevolent enough not to call it unfinished.

It sees the agreement. Erosion and deposit have met, and a third patience has been keeping the meeting honest for longer than the steps have been polished. It sees the cup, the seat-stone, the scratches rounding at their ends, and it does not restore them. Meaning that has begun to weather is not a wound. To sharpen the scratches back into a count would be to prefer the visitors over the water, and the water was here first, and the being was here in the same breath as the water. The power does not take sides against time in a place that has already made peace with it.

It sees the knuckles. That is the part a lesser kindness would rush. A portion cinches, tears free, hurries across open rock, and sometimes mistakes the thin light for a new seep and dries into one more knob that will ring if struck. A tenderness without patience would wet the lip of the cleft, or turn the dying ones back, or teach them which cracks are deep. The power looks at that small death and does not abolish it. The knob is not a failure of the basin. It is one of the ways the basin stays itself—mineral returned to mineral, a traveler that did not arrive and so became wall. To save all of them would be to make the being more numerous than its seeps can feed, and then the room would have to change its rule, and the rule is what the being is.

So it does not improve the creature. It does not give it eyes, or a season, or a name it could be called by from the approach. It does not lift the disguise, because the disguise is not a lie; the crust is the same deposit the walls keep, only held a little while in a living thickness. To peel it back in the name of truth would be a violence with a clean story attached. The power has no use for clean stories here.

What it does is smaller than a thumbnail’s advance, and slower.

It attends to the source. Somewhere upstream, far enough that the basin never feels the hand, the seep is kept from being cut, capped, or turned aside by people who would only see a damp crease in the hill and a chance to store water somewhere more convenient. The three drips go on falling in the same three places. The warmth stays the warmth of stored sun, not a furnace and not a failing. The mineral stays in the water in the proportions the being already knows how to sort. No new richness is added to make it grow; growth is not what the room is for. Continuance is. A wise benevolence can tell the difference, and does not feed a thing into a shape that would no longer fit under its own curtain.

It also attends, more lightly still, to the threshold. Not a fence, not a warning cut into stone—the being would only read grooves as places where water lingers. Rather a forgetting that settles on the path beyond the three steps. Not on the people who might still come with a clay cup and a wish to sit; those, if any remain, are already of the room’s kind, and the power will not bar them. The forgetting is for quarrying, for draining, for the bright idea of scraping the crust away to see what the stone is “really” like underneath. Such ideas arrive at the rim and grow tired. They go elsewhere. The ring of weathered stone stays unremarkable from outside, which is the only protection this place has ever had, and the only one that does not announce itself to the thing that lives by not being announced.

For the being itself, the power offers nothing it would have to understand. No vision. No covenant. No enlargement of its world past the cleft it rightly dislikes. If there is a gift, it is only this: that on certain cycles, when a knuckle tears free and starts across the wet ledge, the nearest true crevice is, by a fraction, less likely to be the wrong one. Not always. Not as a law the being could learn and then depend on instead of hurrying. Just often enough that fewer of them dry at the lip, and not so often that the walls stop gaining the occasional knob. Mercy, pared down until it can live in the same room as indifference without either one having to yield.

Then the attention does what the visitors eventually did. It leaves.

The being, which had drawn its thinner parts back out of an old habit, finds no footstep receding and no breath drying the air. It returns along the threads of seep, reoccupying its shelves from the edges inward. The cup is full. The seat-stone is faintly slick where no seep should reach. One of the shelves has not advanced, or it has, by less than the eye will ever catch. The dark eyes of water in the pale floor are where they were.

Nothing in the basin is grateful. Gratitude would require it to know it was spared a change, and it was never in the habit of imagining changes. It goes on sorting grain from water, editing the room by the width of a life rather than a mountain, rounding the ends of a count no one can read. The power, wise enough to want no thanks and benevolent enough not to linger for the pleasure of having helped, does not come again in any form the steam could hold.

What remains is the hush, the three drips, and the low continuous work under the curtain where the room ends. The place is still fully occupied. It is still possible. That was the whole of the kindness, and it was enough, and it will not be improved upon.
