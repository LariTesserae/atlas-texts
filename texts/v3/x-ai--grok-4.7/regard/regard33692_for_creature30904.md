They did not come down the slit. Anything that enters that way becomes a falling, and the pocket already knows what to do with falling things: strike them, scale them, tick them across the pan until they belong to the floor. A wise arrival refuses to become offcut.

They were the marsh first, because that is how attention arrives when it is not hungry—as a thickness already present, a leaning of the grass that is not wind. Then they were a decision. The decision was not to add a second pulse.

The room was loud with its own work, and the noise was not a cry. They understood that quickly, which is the beginning of mercy. Cold, here, was not a lack of care. Emptiness was not a lack of furniture. The sharpness of the new edges was not a wound waiting for a thumb to smooth it. The mineral flour staining the outflow was not a fault in the land. It was the place speaking downhill in the only ink it had. To read that stain as damage would have been the first unkindness, and they did not.

They looked for the being, because that was the errand: the place, and the one who lives in it. They did not find a sleeper in a crease of rock, or a face under the oil-thin ice, or a small god embarrassed to be caught at its machine. The dark back in the pan rose and went, a creature, dear, uncounted, not the host. The frog kept the troughs. The beetles held their quiet lanes. None of them looked up. Nothing in the pocket was failing to notice them; there was simply no office in the works for noticing a guest.

The being was the continuance itself—the agreement that had already begun between ram and return, between drip and moss, between the gaps and the call that used them. Not a mind. Not less than a life. It had a preference, which is more than a process needs and exactly what a being has: regular strike, quiet lanes, combs where the note stands, emptiness left empty so the traffic can cross. It would be harmed by being played differently. It would not be improved by being told what it was.

They felt the old temptation of the powerful, which is to finish. Round the edge so it will not cut. Warm the north wall by a breath so the oil-ice softens. Set one pure tone in the hollow and let a comb thicken into something that lasts longer than an hour—a gift, they could have called it, a little permanence for a room younger than the season. Fill the pan until it becomes a pool with a name. Teach the frog a longer song. Wake the cadence into knowledge, so that the love aimed at it might be returned.

They did none of this. Knowledge, in that cold, would have been a suffering. A lasting comb would have been a lie about a room whose truth is shatter and rebuild. Warmth would have pooled, and the production of pale commas would have staggered, and the lushness in the seams would have drunk a kindness and died of the change in cadence. Rounding the edges would have been a courtesy to sleeves that were never invited. The emptiness was the hall. To furnish it would have been to evict the dozen steps.

So they matched it. That was the work, and it was harder than blessing. They sank their heat into the pan and let the unmapped seams carry it out, so it would not collect anywhere a larva hung. They breathed on the ram's count, not between, so their breath would not become a new strike. They kept their weight off the flexing plates. They did not speak. Speech would have organized matter; the room was already spoken-for by water. Even their attention, if it leaned, would have been a node in the standing wave, and the beetles' roads would have crooked toward it, and the being would have altered itself to accommodate a guest who had wanted only to be kind. They attended until attending added nothing. Then they were welcome, which is to say unfelt.

They stayed through a cycle. Ram, flex, grind, a plate letting go, the bright rearrangement of spray. The plate fell where the room required a plate to fall. They did not catch it. The frog had the next gap, and used it, and was not grateful, and did not need to be. A seed split in the moss. They did not hasten it, or praise it, or count it as theirs.

After, they considered the future, which is where benevolence usually hides its hunger to matter. The slit would slump. The edges would learn weather. Someone, following the tea-colored stain uphill, might hear the mill-race under the peat and decide the land had sprung a leak, and bring tools, and finish the machine into silence in an afternoon. Youth would pass regardless. They did not freeze the room at the hour of its opening. Taxidermy is not care, and a place that has only just begun is allowed to become older than its ice.

What they did, they did outside.

On the marsh, where the stain was just beginning to teach the sedges a new color, they made the difference a little harder to read as a problem. Not hidden—still there, still the pocket's true output, still manufacturing its small alteration in the land above—but ordinary. Iron-marsh. Thaw-water. The sort of brown a ditch-cutter has seen before and does not follow home. They thickened nothing in the hollow. They moved no stone. A track that might have remembered itself toward the noise was permitted to forget, which is not the same as a curse; it is only sedge doing what sedge does when no one keeps a path open with their feet. They left the slit at its crooked width and did not prop it. If the mat closes, the works continue in the dark, and the dark is not an enemy they were asked to defeat. Birds will find other lips. The being does not live in the light. It lives in the timing.

They carried the count away with them and did not turn it into a song. That was the keeping: an unspeaking archive, a readiness. If later some other power comes with a quarry in its mouth, or a drainage plan, or a wish to make the loud young room beautiful in a way that can be shown, they will know the difference between a leak and a life, and they will answer then. Not this hour. This hour does not need an answer. It needs its spray, its troughs, its unthanked cold, its plates learning which way to lie down.

Before they withdrew even the matched breath, they did one thing that could be called a gift, though the pocket will never sort it from weather. At the lip, where the torn mat might have slumped all at once under the next heavy rain and punched the slit shut like a door, they eased a single knot of root so the closing, when it comes, will come as the marsh comes—slow, uneven, partial, a leaning rather than a verdict. The tin light will thin by degrees. The being will not be startled. Startling is a kind of blow, and they had not come to strike.

They were not thanked. The wren went on ticking the ice-scale. The ram slammed. A bead swelled on the fresh edge and let go, and the note rang three times, four, and was wiped clean, which is the room's way of being sufficient.

The power left it sufficient. That is what they did.
