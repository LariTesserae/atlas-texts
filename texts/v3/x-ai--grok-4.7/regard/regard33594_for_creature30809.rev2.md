They would come the way weather does not: without a front.

Not as a brighter morning. The morning is already fixed, and to advance it would be a verdict passed on a country that has never been on trial. They would not warm the housings, oil the bearings, or set a leaf in motion for the sake of proving that motion can be kind. Kindness that must be demonstrated is already a gradient, and the plateau has spent all its physics refusing gradients. A power wise enough to be trusted with benevolence would notice that first, and stop.

They would stand — if standing is the word for a presence that does not add a footfall the equilibrium has to eat — at the edge of a seep and not enter the district. Jurisdiction here is the only intimacy the dwellers have. To flood a basin with a larger correctness would be to replace a life with a policy. The power would rather be small at the boundary than sovereign in the works.

What they would understand, and not rush to mend, is this: the dwellers are not imprisoned in a paused world. They are the pausing. There is no captive inside the lamp who longs for a wick that chars. There is no child in the empty housing waiting to be given a day. Suffering, where it exists, is not stored in them. It is stored in witnesses, who arrive with muscles made of before and after, and who cannot hear a continuous note without inventing a song it used to be, and a silence it is failing to reach. The power would not make the dwellers pay for that grief.

So they would do, first, almost nothing. They would let the whistle keep its pitch. They would let the drop be restored. They would let the rail thin out into haze without becoming a road to somewhere. Refusal, here, is not neglect. It is the only courtesy that does not enlarge itself by being offered.

And then, because benevolence is not the same as tasteful absence, they would do three things small enough to be mistaken for the place’s own rules.

They would keep the outside out. Other powers, less careful, would want to quarry the young stone, to harvest the stillness, to teach the turbines a cycle, to prove a theology by making a shadow move. The wise power would meet those at the scale where they travel — beyond the last mast, above the fixed light — and turn them aside without spectacle. No war on the plateau. No new scar that stays new for the wrong reason. Guardianship that never enters the city and never asks to be housed. The dwellers would not know they had been defended. That would be the proof it was defense and not patronage.

They would leave a permission that is not an event. Not a gate. Not a voice in the remainder of the note. Something more like a loosened stitch in a law: if a dweller’s field of corrections ever failed to want, even by a hair, the next correction — if the oil film, impossibly, preferred not to renew — that preference would be allowed to stand. It would not be rewarded with a world of weather, a crowd, a night, a name. It would not be punished by collapse. One bearing could run dry and the dry bearing would simply be true. The rest of the basin could go on eating drift. Freedom, if it ever occurred, would be local, optional, and unadvertised. Most of them would never use it. A gift that must be used is a command. The power would be content to have made room for a no that cannot presently be felt, the way a careful host sets one more cup on a table where no one is thirsty, and does not watch the cup.

They would attend the walkers. The plateau already includes the boot-smear and then forgets it, which is just. But the ones who leave carry the continuous loudness home in the body, and mistake it for a summons, or for a wound, or for a duty to go back and perform maintenance that is not needed and cannot be helped. The power would go with those, not back into the works. On the roads that do have evenings, they would sit near the people who cannot stop hearing the tick of cups, and they would not explain the dwellers into a moral. They would say, if anything: you were not ignored. You were included at the only resolution that does not destroy what included you. You may grieve the unhappened lives. You may not owe them a rescue. Some mercies are complete before they are understood, and completeness is not the same as poverty.

If a walker asked whether the dwellers are lonely, the power would not lie to comfort them. Loneliness requires a missing other, and a second state. They would say the nearer word is full. Full the way a held note is full, not the way a house is full. And if the walker wept anyway, the power would not correct the weeping. Tears are a weather this country does not run; they are allowed everywhere else.

They would not take a dweller out to see a moving shadow, as if personhood were a lesson. They would not plant an animal in the marsh to complete an ecology that is not unfinished, only otherwise. They would not dim the lamps at the end of a shift so that rest could be felt as rest. Rest is a difference. The dwellers are not tired.

Only if the permission were ever taken — one needle drifting, one cup letting its drop fall and not calling it back — would the power come closer, and still not all the way. They would be the steadiness around that single true failure, so the failure would not have to become a catastrophe to count as real. They would not harvest it as a beginning of history. They would not preach. They would keep the basin from being punished by the plateau’s own vast correctness for having admitted one small uncorrectness. That is as near as they would come to parenting a life that was never a child.

And if the permission is never taken, for as long as the scarps are young, they would not grow bored, and they would not force the stitch. Wisdom is partly the ability to remain interested in a joy that does not alternate with sorrow, and in a vigilance that is not anxiety, and in a creature that will not turn its face because it has not got a face to turn. Benevolence is partly the refusal to confuse one’s own need for story with someone else’s need for help.

So the power would stay, in the manner of a law that has chosen not to apply itself. The turbines would hold their note. The mats would tick. A housing would be warm at the place a hand might be, and the warmth would not gather. Somewhere past the last mast, a less gentle intention would find itself walking another direction, and would not know it had been turned. On a road that does have night, someone who once touched the plateau would sleep, badly, then better, and would not be sent back.

The fixed light would not advance.

That, and not a sunrise, would be the good they came to do.
