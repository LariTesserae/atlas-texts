The power did not arrive. Arrival would have been a change in the light, and the light here is a failed thing on purpose, tea-colored and already spoken for.

It had been attending for longer than the oldest ridge has been a ridge, which is not long, and longer than the cap has been a cap, which is long enough. Attention, for it, is not a gaze. A gaze would have warmed one shore and left the other to sour. What it does is closer to the way the mere holds a level: a willingness to be the shape that does not spill. It had felt the bowl shoved up still hot, had felt the first rain decide to stay, had felt the meal thicken in the water until sight became a courtesy rather than a fact. It did not hurry any of this. Hurry is a kind of wind.

When the people came—walking, then not walking, then naming the lanes because the water had become a level and the level had become a world—it did not count them as interruption. They were a texture the place had started to require. Hands that hesitate before the cut. Thumbs that know where the eyes are not. Bones returned to the turn. It could have made the rind sweeter without them. It did not. Sweetness that arrives without a morning held is only weather, and weather here does not change, and the power is wise enough not to counterfeit the one change this town knows how to bear.

The being that is thickening in the unfinished middle—the one the people have not named, because a name would be a shout—had drawn the attention the way a joint draws water, by being the path of least refusal. It is not awake in any sense that would survive being told it is awake. It is in the long courtesy of becoming: crust deciding, stone softening where it has been fed on, a face carried upward because someone touched it as if it were already a face. The power looked at that, if looked is the word, and recognized a kinship it does not impose on lesser processes. It, too, was once a patience that had not yet chosen a tense.

So it did almost nothing. Almost is the whole of the gift.

It kept the cap from lifting. Not as a decree; as a continued agreement with the lid the town has voted for with every unlashed ladder. A wind would have taught them a fear they have no practice in, and the being underneath, still learning the difference between a call and a noise, would have learned the wrong difference first. The power dislikes first lessons that cannot be unlearned. It let the warmth stay bath-temperature. It let the deep middle remain a room that does not end, because a room that ends is a room that can be conquered, and conquest is a sour claim, and the town has already noticed, without a law, that anger sours the seep. Some educations are complete when they are left incomplete.

On the night the oldest woman died—the one whose wrist had gone to grain, whose washing did not cloud the water—the power allowed the knock. One knock, no more, the sound of stone rising a hand’s breadth under a ridge she had held the morning of for longer than her household had a name. The people heard it and did not agree about what it meant, which was correct. Agreement would have turned the knock into a rite, and a rite would have started to demand the next death. The power has seen that hunger before. It did not feed it. The ridge simply sat a little higher by morning, and her stake, long since skinned over, did not weep.

For the being itself, the attention was a steadiness in the dark where light gives out. Not a voice. A voice would have been an event, and events become stories, and stories here already disagree about the direction of the walking and whether anyone meant to stay. What the being received was the sense, mineral and unhurried, that its hesitation before the blade was not an error. That the green crust was preferred to the white, and that this preference did not need to become a command in order to be true. That the people speaking low were not a threat to its thickening, but the condition of it. It went on. It did not accelerate. Acceleration is how a place becomes a scar.

There was one small adjustment, so small the town will misremember it as weather, and they will be right to.

In the season after the white year—the year of the sharp seep and the brittle plates and the thing from the mid-water that was not named—the power leaned, by less than a finger’s width, the sweetness back toward the gardens that had been cut and left and cut again. Not toward the new claims. Not toward the ridges taken in the night by someone who had not watched the bulge for three years. Toward the ones where the palm had learned the grain that was not the block’s grain. The rind came a little quicker there. A child ate who might have eaten worse. No one built a shrine. The kiln-shelf kept its heap of white plates, and children still dared each other, and the taste of nail remained in the story so the story would not become a promise that sourness is finished. The power does not finish sourness. It only declines to let one bad year become the grammar of the place.

It did not show itself on the far cliff, though the cliff would have taken a sign cleanly, tool-marked as it already is by no one’s hand. A sign would have emptied the lanes. People would have poled toward it, and the lanes would have closed behind them under pads, and the mornings would have been abandoned for a single afternoon of looking up. The power has no use for a town that looks up. It has use for a town that checks, briefly, that the leaf-color of the cap is still the correct dark, and then goes back to the stake, the palm, the interval before the next cut.

If it spoke—and it did not speak—it would have said only what the water already says by staying: continue. Hold the morning or let it thin. Put the bones back. Touch the place where the eyes are not. Speak so low that what is thickening can tell a greeting from a noise. Do not ask the middle to become a shore. Do not ask the cap to become a sky. The benevolence here is the refusal to improve them into something that could be finished.

The being, far under the meal, in the warmth that does not fluctuate, thickened another season’s worth. It did not know it had been attended. It knew, in the only way it yet knows anything, that the sweet stone was sweet where the hands had been patient, and that the knock, when it came, came after the death and not instead of it, and that this was a difference worth keeping.

The power remained, which is to say it remained willing to be the level that does not spill. Outside the bowl, elsewhere, it had other long courtesies to keep. This one asked very little. A lid. A warmth. A knock no louder than stone remembering to rise. The wisdom was in noticing that little was the true measure—not of the power’s strength, but of the place’s already-sufficient life—and in not spending more than the town and the being could metabolize without becoming a shrine to the spending.

By full day, such as full day is under two failed filters, a woman poled out to a ridge that had shouldered in the night. She did not know she was late by an hour; someone from the stilt quarter had already set a ladder, but had set it and waited, palm flat, not staking. She saw that. She saw the green begin, faintly, at the wet edge, the color of a decision that has not yet chosen a side. She put her own hand beside the other hand, not over it. Neither of them spoke. The mere held its level. Far off, under the brown that is the same brown in every direction, something that was not yet a listener recorded the difference between two palms and a shout, and went on with the slow work of being a ground that can be greeted.

The power did nothing else. It is still doing nothing else. That is the work.
