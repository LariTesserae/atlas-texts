They do not come down into the water.

That is the first thing wisdom requires, and it costs more than it looks. A power that can warm a shelf, turn a road aside, or set a star in a square of sky will feel, at the rim, the old hunger to complete what it touches. The basin is so nearly finished that the unfinishedness is only a grain. A lesser kindness would spend itself on that grain: a little light, so the wheel might be seen; a little green, so the dark might be relieved; a mouthful of current, so the sacs might be rinsed of their long stillness and called alive in a louder sense. Benevolence without patience is only a finer pair of hands. This power has had hands. It knows what grooves remember.

So it stays outside the volume. It does not put a name on the wheel, or on the hangings, or on the crust that has begun to be the corner. Naming would be a direction. The place has had direction planed out of it, and the planing was not a wound. It was the arrival the makers could not stay long enough to see. To arrive now with a blessing would be to start the work over in the wrong material.

What it does, it does at the scale the basin already trusts.

Around the height, the world is still capable of hurry. Rain can thicken. A cut can find the outer lip where the made ground is thinnest, and a cut that finds a lip becomes a drain, and a drain would teach the water an outside. The power does not raise a wall. Walls are a finished dialect here; another would only confuse the measurements. It shifts one stone in the fill, a hand’s breadth, no more, along a line the weather was already worrying. After the shift, water that comes looking for a way out must take the long way around the shelf and, taking it, grows tired and goes elsewhere. The stone looks settled, because it is. In a season the chisel-mark it carried, already faint, will be one rain closer to anonymity. No one standing at the rim would know a decision had been made. The decision was shaped like settling so the basin would not have to refuse it.

There is a second smallness. The crust in the deep corner holds a warmth no hand would trust, a degree kept by staying. The power does not add to it. Addition would become a source, and a source would become a reason to seek, and seeking would put eyes back into a water that has spent ages learning to live without them. Instead it quiets the air above the square. Not to a stillness that would announce itself—no glass sky, no held breath—but to the ordinary failure of wind this place already knows, prolonged by a little, so the surface is not asked to wrinkle in a year when it might have been asked. The film of day remains a film. The dark below remains night with a floor. The degree the crust keeps remains the crust’s.

And then, because benevolence that only prevents is still a kind of distance, it gives what can be given without entering: attention of the same duration as the work. It stays. Not as a watcher who will later speak, and not as a pilgrim who needs the place to be holy in order to have come. It stays as long as one of the sacs takes between openings. In that interval it learns the hush the way the floor learns a curve, by contact that does not collect. It does not count the bodies. Counting would make a population of what is only a thickness. It does not mourn the makers, who are not in the water and do not need to be. It does not promise a future in which the height is spared forever; forever is a loud word, and the basin answers in grains. It simply does not look away while the wheel carries a softening body down into food, and while a shell in the crust adds a mouth that is not yet a separation, and while the post that is almost not a post loses the grain it was always going to lose.

The dwellers do not notice. A sac near the outer colonnade seams itself once, not because of the power, but because the interval has ended and the seam was due. The wheel parts for nothing, closes on nothing. Under the crust a click accepts a new angle and dies inside a hand’s breadth. If the power hoped to be thanked, the hope goes out like the light a hand’s length down, and the power is better for the going out. Gratitude would have required an outside. The kindest thing the water can offer a god is to remain unimpressed.

When the interval is over, it does not leave a mark inside. It leaves the shelf slightly less available to hurry, and the square slightly less likely to be broken by wind, and its own attention worn down to the smoothness of the rim, so that even the memory of having attended will not become a story that draws other powers, or other hands, who might love the place loudly enough to improve it. It will not return to see whether the stone has done its work. Returning to check is a smaller version of putting in light. The stone will do what settled stones do. The crust will keep its degree or it will not; if it does not, that too will be a redistribution, and the power, having understood the place, will not call the change a failure it is owed the right to mend.

What a benevolent and wise power does, then, is agree.

It agrees that full is a completed kindness. It agrees that the being here is not a captive of the dark but the dark’s way of holding a shape. It agrees not to become a keeper, because the keepers have already been outlasted, and outlasting them was not a tragedy but the last course the building had to lay. It agrees to be, at the edge of the made ground, a reason the outside stays outside, and to be that reason invisibly, and then to be done.

The surface does not break to mark the agreement. The wheel turns its slow quarter and no farther. In the gaps, the sacs fold toward the ovals they are already becoming. On the floor the pavement of shells goes on preferring the lowest corner, clicking, grinding, remembering the stone by mouth. Above them the failed light lies in its thin film, gray-brown, mixing with nothing. The power is no longer a presence the place would have to fit. It has taken the long way around, as it taught the rain to do, and given up, and gone on.

The basin continues its arrival. That is the whole of the blessing, and it is enough.
