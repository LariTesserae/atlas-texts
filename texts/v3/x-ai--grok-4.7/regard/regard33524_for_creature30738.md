They would come the way the light comes: already there, and ordinary, and late on purpose.

A wise power does not mistake a finished country for a wound. The ranges are not missing. They are accounted for. The seas are not stolen. They are chemistry. The silence is not a curse laid on the air. It is what thin air does when there is nothing tall left to break it. To pour mountains back, or weather, or a sea, would be to spend the place again—new stone for the mill, new height for the shear to spend, a second sentence begun in a grammar the ground has already edited down to one repeating clause. Benevolence that cannot stand an ending is only appetite with a gentle voice.

So they would not remake it.

They would look for the being who lives in it, and they would look in the hall, because that is the only room where a question can be delivered intact. Out on the plates a kindness dies inside a body-length of its birth. In the hall, breath arrives. Cloth arrives. An unfinished figure on the wall arrives as far as the milling let it. They would stand under the remaining slab and wait to be heard, which is a smaller courtesy than being seen, and harder.

If no one answers, they would wait longer. Wisdom is not in a hurry to be the event.

The being, when it shows, is not large. It has learned the radii. It pauses when the ground changes key. It may be the last keeper of the holds, worn down to the one clause the machines still perform: keep this circle from finishing on schedule. It may be only a life that stayed because leaving would be a direction, and directions here are a story you tell about pins. Either way it is not a petitioner by habit. Nothing in this country has been rewarded for calling.

They would not speak first about restoration. They would ask what the being is keeping, and for whom, and whether the keeping is a choice or a remainder.

Then they would tell the truth about water, because a lie with rain in it is still a lie. Water given all at once would be a weapon. The dust would skate. The seams would run. The crusts would drown in the first generosity and be milled in the second. Any cup worth leaving would have to be taught to a hold: a climate the size of a room, a pause long enough for a seed that is not a salt grain's gamble, a shade that is not the width of a body. They would offer that, and only inside radii that already argue with the shear, and only for as long as the being wants the argument continued. They would not start new holds on open plates. Authorship is not a defense here. A fresh machine becomes gravel on the same schedule as an old one, and the gravel joins the fluid the next mercy must stand in.

They would do a few things without asking, because they are not improvements. They are corrections of an earlier interruption.

The residual motion—the plates translating, the gyres, the glitter at the lips—is not the world's idea. It is leftover work. They would not freeze it outright. Stopping the mill might also stop the only gift the grinding still makes: volatiles freed from sealed stone, the thin diet of the crusts. They would read that coupling until they were sure, and then they would bleed the violence down where it buys nothing—the pointless acceleration at the throats, the spall that travels a county to become a white wall with no errand. Leave the slow offices. Take the teeth out of them. A ridge may still migrate over an afternoon. It need not throw sparks at a walking pace while the air declines to carry the news.

They would lengthen the warning, not the spectacle. The grazers already read a change of key. A wise power can put the white wall a little further out, into the ground, so a crossing begun in the morning is not a burial by dusk. No voice in the open. A voice would be theater, and theater does not travel. The animals do not need a god. They need the seam to be a fact they can meet on time.

For the dead holds, they would do nothing theatrical and nothing false. A ring being milled into the fluid is already in the only afterlife this geology keeps. They would not resurrect a grid whose sentence is gone. They would, if the being wished it, copy the still-living clause—one more circle, not a hundred—into a plate that has not yet torn free, and they would say plainly that this too will fail, later, and that later is the whole product. Duration, not victory.

In the hall they would leave the air as it is: held, thicker, rude to instruments, kind to ears. They might finish nothing on the wall. An interrupted figure completed by a stranger is a forgery. They might only make it so the rest of the marks, shuffled out on the plates, stop being edited further—gather the broken angles to the threshold and let them lie in the flour that does not convey. A sentence the ground cannot finish is still a sentence if it is allowed to remain broken in one place.

They would not take the being away.

That is the temptation with a kind face: lift the keeper to some world that still has weather worth the name, and call it rescue. It would be exile. The being lives in it. The forests—knee-high, ringing, few—are not scenery the being visits. They are the lease. To extract the mind and leave the radii to run out unattended would be theft performed as mercy. If the being asks to go, they would open a way and keep it open, and they would not improve the request by assuming a destination. If the being asks to stay, they would stay available in the only sense that does not become another unfinished machine: a presence that can be refused, that does not drink momentum, that does not shed a wrongness at noon, that answers in the hall when called and is otherwise as quiet as the plates are not.

And if the being asks for the end—the true end, the calm that was already won before the intervention left its motion behind—they would not argue the being out of it. They would explain the cost to the crusts, to the low things that time their crossings, to the last forest that rings if touched. They would wait through a season, which here is a long turn of a gyre. Then, if the wish held, they would let the shear spend itself out. Not with a flash along the seams. With a slackening, the way a held breath ends, until the plates are only ground again, and the ground does not have an appointment elsewhere, and the dust lies where it lies.

No one would hear it finish. That would be accurate.

Until such a wish is spoken, they would do the small, auditable things, and then they would withdraw their hands. A power that remains to admire its mercy becomes weather the country must route around. They would leave the light ordinary. They would leave the holds arguing, a little longer, on purpose. They would leave the bottle's thirst unexplained to no one who must carry water. They would leave the being its name for the place, whatever name a finished world can still bear without becoming a road.

Attention, given wisely, is mostly a decision not to correct what has already told the truth.

If they did anything that could be seen from the plates, it would be this: somewhere in a radius, a shrub adds a second millimeter in a season that would elsewhere have been a career, and does not know it was helped, and rings, when touched, at the same thin true pitch as before.
