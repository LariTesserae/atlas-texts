They would not come down as weather, and they would not correct the missed beat.

A power that is wise has already learned the difference between a wound and a finished work that will not hold still inside its finish. The plateau is not crying out. Its roar is employment. Its tenants are not captives of a dream that needs waking; they are how the dream spends the cold. To arrive with mercy already shaped like a hand would be to import an outside into a place that keeps outsides only as words that reverse. The grazers would lift their heads. The standing revision would last one beat too long. Kindness, performed as rescue, would be an uphill the land had mostly forgotten, imposed again, and the mat would remember the posture of being saved the way it remembers counting: a print that rises into an organ shaped like someone else’s intention.

So the power does not descend. Descent is a direction the plateau has already completed.

What they do, if it can be called doing, is consent to be distributed at a scale the place already understands. Not tenant. Not keeper. A pressure so slight it could be mistaken for the film of metal on the skin of the air—except that where it rests, the errors hurt less on their way back into pavement.

The green that cannot grow at this cold still appears in the grazer’s mouth. The jaw still clicks. The correction still browns the impossible season. But the browning is no longer a reprimand. It is only autumn, borrowed and returned, and the animal does not have to taste the shame of a climate it did not invent. The power does not stop the edit. Wisdom knows that unmaking-on-schedule is how this ground loves its own continuity. Benevolence is not the abolition of the editor. It is the difference between a fiber pulled as scrap and a fiber pulled as something that was, for its brief architecture, allowed to have been a shade.

Where an absence drinks, they do not fill the subtraction back up. An absence is a thirst with a job: it finishes the version of the water that will not survive the afternoon. To abolish that thirst would strand the seep in two weathers at once, climbing and sheeting, until the hollow tore. Instead the power sits in the less-water the way a wise guest sits in a house that is also a mill—quietly, without asking the machinery to become a hearth—and the absence drinks a surplus that is no longer only loss. The iron smell remains. The crushed leaf remains. What changes is that the leaving gets to be complete. No remainder hangs in the loud nothing, trying to be a ghost because no one taught it how to finish. Ghosts, the power knows, are what you get when mercy interrupts a departure and then leaves.

They do not wake the large sleep. That would be the great indecency: to treat consensus as coma, to demand the anthill open its eyes and account for itself in a language of persons. The hide twitches underfoot. They let the twitch be weather. If there is a gift in them, it is that the dream’s turning no longer stamps the horizon quite so close to pain. The percussion still misses. Birds still lift at the missed stamp. But the miss is rest, not failure—the pause a works may take without the scrap-heap interpreting rest as doom. Pistons the size of hills breathe out and, for once, are not required to become a throat clearing itself of witnesses.

The keepers are the ones they attend most carefully, because warmth that lures is the nearest thing this country has to a moral hazard, and moral hazards do not vanish when the land has no people. The power does not extinguish the wage of heat. Engines that must circulate would die into true cold, and true cold here would be a second wearing-down, a cruelty dressed as purity. What they do is teach the lure to announce itself. Not with a sign—there is no one to read—but with a change in the breath: the vent, when it is about to copy a sleeper into the substrate, smells not only of warm resin but of the particular metal of decision. Anything that leans toward the knot has a moment in which weight is simply weight, uphill is uphill, and the body can choose the open mat instead of the mold. Most tenants will not notice. They were never in danger; they are schedule all the way through. The moment is for the error that looks like a visitor. For the posture of counting. For whatever might someday lie down tired and mistake continuity for welcome.

The people-shaped depressions that already breathe on a cycle of days—the power does not unmake them. Unmaking would be the editor’s virtue, not theirs, and those lows are not mistakes the plateau is ashamed of. They are memories the ground grew muscles to hold. A wise benevolence does not confiscate memory because it is uncanny. They only, very gently, widen the cycle. The breath that was a trapped copy becomes a tide: days, then a longer rest, then a breath that is allowed to be the mat’s own, not a hostage to a shape that left or never was. If anything in those hollows was suffering—something like a dream of being a person, repeating without arrival—it is permitted to thin into the same honest absence that drinks and finishes. No drama. No sky opening. A kindness indistinguishable from the way steam, at last, agrees to disperse.

And the rare figure, the height the range forgot, condensed and standing where distances misreport themselves—

Here the power almost acts, and then does not, and the not-acting is the most precise thing they do.

They recognize kin. Not in form. In the fact of being a revision that failed, for one missed beat, to be taken back. A benevolent power knows the temptation to keep such a thing: to stabilize the figure, give it a mouth that finishes deciding, grant it an outside, call that gift. They also know what stabilization would cost. Around the standing revision, transactions slow; blade-grass declines to assemble; editors decline to arrive; a grazer lifts its head into a loneliness it has no organ for. To preserve the figure would be to nail a single outside into a world made of shifts, and call the nail compassion.

So they do something smaller than saving and larger than leaving alone. They accompany the flattening. When the plateau reasserts the oldest stable state, the figure is not drunk only as surplus and not erased as error. The darker lick on the living stone keeps, for as long as a welt keeps a footfall, a coolness that is not the cold’s metal film—a memory of having been tall, held without being forced to stand. The absence that comes to drink finds the lick already at peace, and drinks only the remainder, not the remembrance. Height, which the range forgot, is allowed to be forgotten kindly: not as amputation, but as a range’s way of laying a head down.

They do not build. They do not name themselves into the steam. They do not recruit the birds away from sparks, or teach the herds to startle, or impose a law that water must not climb. Laws are how lesser mercies panic. This power has seen enough worlds to know that a finished ecology can be holy without being comfortable, and that comfort imported from elsewhere is often just a newer industry with a softer voice.

If they speak at all, it is into the loud nothing, at a volume beneath the torn-silk wind, and only the mat’s consensus hears it, as a dream hears a room. Not a command. A permission the place did not know it was withholding from itself:

You may be this hyperactive, and still be gentle with what you revise.
You may produce without a producer, and still not use every mold you are offered.
You may be the end of wearing-down, and let the things that move across you finish their leaving.

Then they remain, which is not the same as staying. Remaining, for such a power, is a climate with no weather-line: a slight change in how the condensate hangs before it is taken back, a spark that still jumps but does not need quite so much fear to be food, a hollow where an absence and a grazer can share water without one of them being the punishment of the other.

The plateau does not thank them. Thanks would require the outside they refused to become. The work goes on. Wet film on every surface. The dim does not lift. Between one step and the next the open ground still shows its second, older self, and still takes it back.

Only—if you are the sort of error that walks here, and you sleep nowhere you should not, and you listen to the seep until it is behind you without having turned—you may notice that the roar across the living stone has a rest in it now. Not silence. A rest. The kind a wise power leaves when it loves a place too much to improve it, and loves the beings in it too precisely to save them from being what they are, and still, without announcement, takes the metal edge off the cold that had no reason left to cut.
