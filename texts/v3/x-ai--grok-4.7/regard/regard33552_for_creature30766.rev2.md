They would do almost nothing. That is the wisdom, and it is not a refusal of care.

A power that can warm a world, open a sky, or set a river on a dry throat would arrive already carrying the wrong gifts. Water is the most obvious of them, and the most fatal. Rain here would not be mercy. It would be a different country arriving inside this one, and the pavement, the oil, the silver that throws the sun back, the wide claims each stand holds on dust — all of that is a life arranged against water, not waiting for it. To wet the shield would be to love a greenness that does not live here and to kill the one that does. Warmth is the same error in another clothing. The cold is not a lack the stands endure. It is the rate at which they are possible. Company, in any number that could be called a blessing, would close distances the chemistry requires. A wise power does not improve a balance by feeding it the thing it has spent an age learning not to need.

So the coming, if it is coming, is not weather. It does not dim the coin of the sun or lay a hand on the crust. It does not speak, because speech would be a pressure, and the air here is already thin enough that a new pressure would register as climate. What arrives is a quality of regard calibrated to the place: cool, continuous, exact, and without heat. It looks the way the light looks — hard, and taking nothing for itself.

It sees the pavement first, because the pavement is the oldest decision. Millimeters of living holding a continent against a wind that would otherwise write only subtraction. It does not heal the pans. The pans are how the shield remembers a breach and then agrees to stop. To seal them faster would be to steal the memory and call it kindness. It sees the stands, metal-green, indecently lush in bodies that refuse to become a cover, tilting through a day by fractions no witness is meant to measure. It does not hurry a grain. A century that occasionally consents is not a stunted year. It is the reproductive law, and to shorten it would turn patience into a crop.

It looks for the being that is not a surface.

The pale walker is between stations, as it almost always is — a flank the color of the shield, three-toed, spending itself at a rate it can afford, which is nearly zero. It does not know it has been found. Knowing would be a change in its economy, a small heat of startle, a deviation in a route measured in weeks. Benevolence, here, is the discipline of not becoming an event in a life that cannot afford events. The power watches the track craze the nap and begin, already, to lose its edge. It understands that evidence is a luxury this animal has declined. It does not leave a sign that it passed, because the walker would have to spend attention on the sign, and attention is water by another name.

What, then, is done?

A keeping. Not a wall — walls make weather on their lee sides — but a refusal, set around the shield at a distance the shield will never feel, against any later power less patient than this one. Against the gift of rain. Against the road. Against the lamp, the cairn, the well-meaning greening, the wish to populate the emptiness so that emptiness might look less like loss to eyes that have never lived by interval. The refusal is not a fence the walker could encounter. It is a forgetting offered to everyone else: a slight thinning of the world’s interest, so that maps fail a little, so that routes bend toward easier countries, so that the brightness stays unclaimed. Inattention, accurately placed, is a shelter that does not cast a shadow.

And one alteration so small it is not an alteration, only a fidelity.

Where a fallen column has bled its dark oval into the grit, and the pavement is slow to cross, and the next stand — decades off, or longer — will root nearer that stain than chance would choose, the power does not hasten the rooting. It only does not let that oval be scoured out of sequence. A wind that would have been merely ordinary is, this once, a fraction less thorough. The dust that settles on the silver leaves settles as it would have settled. The oil remains oil. Nothing new is added to the bargain. The dead are allowed to go on being the only soil this life consents to inherit.

Then it withdraws its nearness and keeps the far regard, the way a high sun keeps a land without sitting on it.

The stands tilt their evening fraction. A leaf answers another with the sound of a thumbnail on parchment. Somewhere on the route the walker continues, unstartled, pale, almost never in the same place as looking. The shield does not register a departure, because what left was never weather. If the place could be said to have been helped, it is only in this: that it was seen clearly enough not to be saved, and then guarded from being saved by anyone who loved it less exactly.

The brightness continues. The balance continues. Catastrophe and repair keep the same patience. Nothing tries to fill the height, the cold, or the dryness. That is what was wanted for them. A wise power is one that can want that, and then do only the little that wanting requires — which is to say, almost nothing, and that almost, forever, in the right direction.
