They would come without weather.

Not as a correction of the sky, and not as a voice that requires the valley to rearrange itself in order to be addressed. The place is a shut orchard on a slope that no longer drains, a house whose rooms have narrowed around one person the way bark narrows around a nail. The being who lives there is not a symbol. They have a name they still answer to, a bad knee, a kettle they trust, and a grief that has become a method: each morning they do the small things in the same order so the day cannot open all at once. They have not asked to be found. That is not the same as wishing not to be known.

The power stops at the gate and does not heal the gate. Wisdom, if it is not merely strength with better manners, can tell a boundary from a wound. It waits while a bird the being has been feeding — a bird that is in no parable — finishes the crust on the step and leaves. It learns the order of the morning by not interrupting it. When the being comes out with the basin, it is only a change in the light on the wet stone, a warmth that does not ask to be believed in.

What it does is small, and exact, and it does not do it *for* the story of having done it.

It loosens one root that has been strangling the remaining fruit tree, not by tearing, but by giving the root somewhere else to go — a thumb’s width of softer ground, a patience long enough for the tree to choose. It sits with the ache in the knee for the length of an afternoon and takes nothing dramatic from it, only the part that is pointless, the grinding that does not teach. The grief it does not take. The grief is not a defect in the being. To lift it out would be to lift out the room in which they still meet the one who is gone. A wise kindness does not burgle the dead in the name of comfort.

It mends the kettle, because the kettle was going to fail on a morning when failing would have meant more than a kettle. It leaves the chip in the cup. The being likes the chip; their thumb finds it without looking, and that finding is a kind of home.

If the being speaks, it answers only the question that was asked, in words no larger than the question. If the being does not speak, it does not translate the silence into a plea. It stays within sight of the gate until the light changes, so that the being may decide whether the warmth was weather or regard — and either decision is allowed to stand. It does not install a shrine. It does not recruit a witness. It does not remain as a presence that would make the orchard a place *about* a visitation.

Before it goes, it does one thing that is not a miracle and not nothing. It remembers the being’s name correctly, in the place where names are not eroded by distance, and it ties that memory to the unremarkable facts: the order of the morning, the bird, the chip, the knee, the particular green of the failing leaves. So that whatever happens after — and it does not promise what will happen after — this life cannot become, even to heaven, a generalization.

Then it leaves the gate as it found it.

The orchard does not shine. The slope still holds its water badly. The being, pouring from the mended kettle, pauses only because the heat is right, and because for a moment the air on the step feels as if it has been considered. They drink. They set the cup down with the thumb in the chip. They go on with the day they were already having, which is the only day a benevolent power, if it is also wise, would ever have wished to return to them.
