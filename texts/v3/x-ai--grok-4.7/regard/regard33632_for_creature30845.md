They would not come in through the oculus.

That is the first decision, and it is made before anything that could be called an arrival. The coin of sky is already a burden the rim must be bitten back from. To widen it, even with kindness, would be to tell the chamber that the outside had won an argument. The hard blue is not a gift at this elevation. It is a cold that the mass has spent itself for lifetimes refusing. A power that is wise does not mistake an opening for an invitation, and a power that is benevolent does not improve a door until it has learned what the door is keeping out.

So attention enters the way water enters: along a path that already exists, without adding a direction.

For a long time—long as the place counts, which is not the same as long as a witness counts—nothing happens that any flock would turn for. The power does not speak. The galleries would not carry a voice intact, and a voice broken into pressure is how the larger hungers announce themselves. It does not lay a hand on a column to learn the count. Palms are loads. It learns the count by becoming, briefly, the sort of stillness the tenant inside certain columns already practices: present on the inhale, absent as extra weight, gone before the circulation of the room has finished a wheel. The herds do not sidle. The lawns continue to be shortened and not stripped. In a sleeping-hollow no larger than a body and its tools, a sleeper turns in the fiber and is not more afraid, which is the only prayer this place knows how to answer.

What the power understands, because it is wise, is that the place is not a problem that has failed to solve itself.

The folds are not errors. The hour that differs between one boot and the other is a joint under tension, and tension is how spans of this size remain spans. To straighten the ledger so that a walk along the floor agreed with the thickness of the wall would be to ask a living member to be smaller than the work it is doing. Wise benevolence does not simplify a load path because the simplification would be easier to love. It looks at the preserved wind in the rings, the dust of climates that never touched the outside, and it does not call this sickness. It calls it memory with a job.

It also does not worship. Worship would be a second weather, and the hollows are already full.

If it has a temptation—and a power that cannot be tempted is only a law, and a law would be worse here than a god—the temptation is to stay and become useful in the way the maintenance-folk are useful. To carry cups. To bite the rim. To correct the cord-maps at the folds. It does not do this. Not because the work is beneath it, but because a helper who does not tire, does not sleep in a hollow, does not have to cross a meadow under the old rules, would teach the spans a preference they cannot keep when the helper is gone. A courted span that learns to expect a god will go sullen at the next mortal season, and sullenness, here, is how a path stops being a path. Benevolence that creates a dependency and then calls the dependency love is only a slower closing of the sky.

So it does three things, and one of them is not a thing so much as a refusal kept.

The first is at the boundary, where the hard blue presses and other attentions, less patient, have begun to lean. Not demons. Do not make a story with demons. Hungers of a plainer sort: the wish to name, the wish to harvest a curiosity, the wish to cut a span and read the rings as trophy, the wish to pipe the upward water out into a country that has forgotten how to lift anything without metal. The power does not fight these wishes inside the rooms. Fighting would ring the joins. It stands, if standing is the word, in the thinness outside the mass, and it lets itself be the thing those wishes spend themselves against. They find the place difficult to see. Not hidden by a lie—the oculi still open, the coin still falls on the floor—but difficult to *want* in the way that becomes an expedition. A thought that was about to become a road becomes, instead, a hesitation, and then a different thought, and then a life occupied elsewhere. The maintenance-folk never learn that this happened. That is the point. A shield that requires gratitude has already fallen into the room and asked to be carried.

The second thing is small enough to fit in a leaf-cup, and it happens only once, because once is a climate and twice is a doctrine.

There is a graft, far up a member that has been a perch and then a wall and then a riser, that is going to dry. Not from neglect. From the arithmetic. The one who would have carried water to it was delayed by a flock that would not settle, and the flock was right not to settle, and the delay is correct, and the graft will still fail, and the failure will kneel a span that did not ask to be retired, and the kneeling will move a meadow's drainage by a hand's breadth, and the hand's breadth will, in a generation of rings, be the difference between a lawn and a wall. The power does not appear at the graft with a shining hand. It does not lengthen the climber's stride or shorten the flock's alarm. It lends the drying joint the time the climber is already spending correctly somewhere else: a few hours of moisture that did not have to be stolen from another conduit, a hesitation in the sap's decision to seal. When the cup arrives, late and ordinary, the graft is still alive enough to drink. The climber does not notice a miracle. They notice that they were not too late, which they have sometimes been, and they mark nothing on the cord-map, because nothing incorrect occurred. The power leaves before the thanks that will never be spoken can curdle into a story.

The third thing is the one that matters most, and it looks, from any distance that wants drama, like doing nothing.

It consents to the scale.

It does not gather the folk into a people. It does not give the hunter a face or the breathing columns a voice or the glittering in the film a rank in a chain of being. It does not lift the solitude, which is not loneliness but the distance at which the work can be done without becoming a vibration. It does not promise that the treaty will hold forever. Forever is a load no living span can bear; even a wise power knows that a promise of eternity, laid on a place made of growth and forgetting, would thicken the wrong members and starve the right ones. What it offers, instead, is attention of the kind the floor gives a kind path: not a record that can be demanded later, not a judgment, a thickening into better footing. While it attends, the several slow processes are less likely to be mistaken, by the place itself, for a reason to hurry. Hurry is the only rot the walls cannot keep out of the air.

The being—if the being is the continuance, the set of agreements walking around inside a body of rooms—does not wake to a revelation. Revelation would be a bright coin too large for the rim. What changes is only this: the preference in the old spans, which was never mind, rests a fraction more easily in its duty. A sullen member that had begun to refuse a path accepts, over the next years, the ordinary courtesies and does not require a miracle to be courted back. The herds pay their price and are not asked to pay a meaning on top of it. The folk go on clicking in the close dark, knuckle to wrist, and if one of them, pouring marsh-water where a riser has slowed, feels for a moment that the work is seen, the feeling does not survive into a doctrine. The fiber eats it, as it eats consonants, and what remains is the water moving up.

Then the power withdraws its nearness and does not withdraw its refusal.

It does not install a priest. It does not leave a mark in the pith that later beetles will raise their children around, a geometric ghost of its own decision. Tool-slots are for decisions made by hands that can be wrong. A benevolent power that cannot be wrong should not carve. From the outside, the mass continues to sweat its own weather. From the inside, the fog lifts and strikes the ceiling and falls again, too fine to become sound. Somewhere a column inhales on the old count. Somewhere a cup is carried because the cup is the whole theology.

If you asked the power, later, why it did so little, it would not answer in the galleries. But the reason is simple enough to lay on a cord without correction at the folds: it loved the place as a going concern, not as a sorrow. And what a wise love does, when it finds a world already busy keeping itself alive, is not to become its weather. It keeps the other weathers from mistaking that world for a thing to open. It spends a little time, once, on a graft. It declines to be a god.

Then it lets the room be the body, and the body be sufficient, and the continuance go on unimproved—which is to say, still alive.
