They would come the way weather comes here: already inside the air before anyone names it, and gone in the sense that arrival never becomes an event.

A wise power does not improve a finished flatness. The plateau has spent its mineral life becoming what it is — high, quiet in its bones, deafening in its living weather — and the benevolence worth trusting is the kind that can tell a completed shape from a wound. To raise a mountain again, to give the water a river’s ambition, to hush the flocks so a single voice could be heard across a gallery: that would be taste, not mercy. Taste rearranges. Mercy asks what is already trying to continue, and spends itself only there.

So they would do very little, and that little would be exact.

They would learn the being first by not looking at it directly. Everything here is overheard — calls crossing calls, wingbeat as surf, the mat answering feet it cannot see. A power that descends as a face would counterfeit a center the place does not have. They would stand, for a measure of days no one counts, in one of the empty halls, and let the vacancy include them. Condensation would bead on nothing. The culms would stay blood-warm. The sheets of birds would bank and darken the gallery mouth and bank away, and the power would be only another interval in the roar, which is how the oldest things here have always been met.

The being, when it is finally undeniable, is not large. That is the error outsiders make, equating the plateau’s scale with the life that has consented to remain. It keeps to the seams. It has learned the ribs of the living floor and the joints where a body can hang without puncturing the tube. Its trail is a revision of damp film, erased by noon. It does not rule the flocks and does not flee them; it uses the lulls, which feel accidental and are not, quite. In the lulls it mends. A stake line that has wandered from its water-pocket, reset by a palm’s width. A ring of carried stones cleared of the culm that rose through the floor, not because the culm is an enemy — nothing that grows here is an enemy — but because someone once slept inside that ring and the shape of sleep is a promise the being still keeps for people who are no longer there to collect it. It works between impacts. It does not sing. The plateau sings enough.

They would recognize it as a keeper of intervals. Not a god of the mat, not a last citizen. A creature that has made a craft of the short silences, and in those silences does the small restorations without which the loudness would become wreckage: pockets kept from sealing, paths of stakes argued back toward water, the dead’s floors left legible for one more season. It is tired in the way of work that cannot be finished, and it is not asking to be finished. That matters. Wisdom listens for the ask inside the endurance, and does not invent one to satisfy its own hands.

What they would do, then, is widen the lull by a breath. Not silence — silence here is a visitor and would frighten the sheets into a worse noise. A breath: one extra beat between wingbeat and answer, recurring, unreliable enough to stay wild, reliable enough that the being’s palm finds the stake before the film dries. They would set it into the air the way the plateau sets warmth into fiber, without announcement, so the being feels only that the work fits its hands a little longer than it used to, and does not look up to thank the sky. Thanks would cost a lull. They will not spend the being’s craft on their own witness.

They would touch one other thing. The oldest grooves, nearly flush with the mat, readable only when the low light and the dew agree. Those are already being revised by roots, which is the plateau’s honesty. They would not freeze them. They would leave, in the callus near a single empty ring, a water that does not sink by midday — tea-colored, insect-skinned, ordinary, and faithful. Not a spring that would cut a river and wake the stone’s old hunger. A pocket that remembers the rain a few hours longer, so the finer grass stays, so kneeling remains possible, so the being can drink without gambling the whole afternoon’s route. Anyone else who comes in a later age will think the pocket is a lucky dish in the pavement. That is the correct legend. Benevolence that needs credit has already become a monument, and this land has been planed free of monuments.

They would not stay to be loved. They would not explain the breath or the pocket. They would not thin the flocks, stock the halls, resurrect the sleepers, or teach the being its own importance. Importance is a kind of noise. The being is already doing the rare thing: keeping a vast, ancient, roaring aliveness inhabitable at the scale of a hand. To elevate that into a destiny would be to steal the intervals and call the theft a blessing.

Before they withdraw — not upward; out, into the same thin air, until presence is only a pressure the culms forget — they would do the last small thing wise powers do when they have truly seen a life. They would let themselves be unnecessary. The widened breath would hold a while, then fray back toward accident, because a permanent mercy becomes a climate and a climate is a cage. The pocket would remain, because water that stays a little is just water, and the being can survive the loss of miracles better than the loss of its work. If the being ever pauses in a lull and feels, without evidence, that it was accompanied, that feeling will be brief and unprovable and it will go back to the stakes. That is enough. That is the whole of what they would do: nothing that replaces the keeper, and two slight persistences — time, and drink — so the keeping can continue in a place that was never empty, only loud, and never finished, only old.

Then the hall is a hall again. The mat gives, slightly, under nothing. Somewhere a colony answers a colony, and the answer arrives already mixed with the next question, and the day proceeds in its stable deafening weather, which a benevolent power, being wise, does not correct.
