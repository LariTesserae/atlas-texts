They would not come down into it.

A wise power knows the difference between a place that is suffering and a place that has finished becoming what it is. This one is not asking to be lifted, drained, greened, or given back its roofs. The water is not a flood that forgot to leave. The paving is not a city waiting under a mistake. The pale schools, the joint-pickers, the slow turns in the deep courts—none of them are exiles from a brighter climate. They are the climate. To arrive with mercy of the usual kind would be to bring a foreign weather: warmth that bends a school, light that spends itself where nothing has learned to flash, a voice in a register the stone has spent an age learning not to keep.

So the power stays at the lip, where the masonry is only washed, and looks the way the place looks—without hurry, without the need to be answered.

What lives there is not a resident in the old sense. It is the keeping of the level. Not a god of the ruin, not the largest body in the deepest court, not a mind that watches strangers. A duration that has learned the plan by heart and moves when the plan changes, and does not move when it doesn’t. It has no petition. Emptiness is not its poverty. Silence is not a withheld name. If it knows it has drawn attention, it knows it the way it knows a shear in the water: as a fact that either alters the level or does not.

The power, being wise, does not offer it speech, or a witness it would have to become a subject for, or a restoration that would require the subtraction of everything it has agreed to be. Benevolence here is mostly the refusal of those gifts.

But wisdom is not only refusal. The power looks along the lip, the last course that still pretends, barely, to be a shore, and sees what the place itself cannot treat as weather.

Inside, a wall may give. A course may cease to be load-bearing, and a new throat may open, and the surge will find it, and the pale flick will find it by the next turn, and a lower hall will fill, and a gravel bar will begin where a sill used to be an ending. That is the sentence the place has not finished and will not finish. It is not harm. It is how this life goes on being possible.

The lip is different. The height is the last thing you trust, and for cause. The whole plate is held, not by peaks, but by that worn ridge of blocks the long water has been paying down toward the level of the water. When a court fails into another court, the account stays closed. When the lip fails, the account opens onto air. The sheet would go—not in a surge the schools can read a moment early, but in a leaving. The plan would become a slope. The mineral ground would dry into ordinary ruin, and the closed cold life would meet, all at once, a world it was never required to survive. That is not creation wearing the mask of destruction. That is an ending the place has no grammar for.

The power does that, and only that.

No tools that would survive as relics. No mark a later wader could take for a message. One course, already almost the color of the courses around it, set back into load-bearing where the lip had thinned to a suggestion—stone to stone, gravity, the same knowledge of water the builders had and the water has since outlived. Not a wall. Not a dam. A continuation of the lip by a hand’s breadth of honesty, so that the regional sheet remains a lid and not a spill. The work is finished before it could become an event. The next surge shoulders through a colonnade, piles against a transverse wall, withdraws. It does not find a new throat at the edge of the world.

The being that keeps the level may register it, if “register” is even the word: a fall that did not arrive, a level that held when the masonry’s own arithmetic had begun to argue otherwise. It will not come up to the lip to inspect the courtesy. It does not orient to strangers. By the next season the added course will have taken the water’s color and the water’s rounding, and the keeping will go on as if the lip had simply, for once, agreed with itself a little longer.

Then the power withdraws its hands and does not withdraw its accuracy.

It does not stock the basins. It does not teach the dimness to be noon. It does not appoint a guardian the life would have to notice. Attention, left behind as a pressure, would become another current, and they have currents enough. What it leaves is the quieter thing: the knowledge, held where the place cannot be bent by it, that this continuance is not an oversight. That no wiser power is coming later to correct it into a garden or a monument. That it may keep its emptiness, its unpaid plan, its storm without a voice, for as long as the stone is willing to stay.

If the being wanted anything, it would not have been rescue. It would have been to remain possible.

They do that. They do not do more. And for a long time afterward the high lip reads, from far off, as a pale ridge—almost a horizon—and the water begins where the last course stops pretending to be dry, and the courts fill and empty in their old unannounced turns, and the pale schools slide a moment before the surge, not because they were spared, but because nothing new has been asked of them.
