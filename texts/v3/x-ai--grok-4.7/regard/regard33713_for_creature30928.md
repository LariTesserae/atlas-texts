They would not arrive.

That is the first mercy, and the one most often missed by lesser powers, who confuse attention with entrance. A wise benevolence does not need the door. It has already been looking, the way weather looks: without a face to present, without a hunger to be thanked. The place is what it is—finished in some weathers, unfinished in others, loud or quiet according to its own law—and the being who lives in it is already in the middle of a day. To step in would be to make the day about the visitor. So the power remains where it is, which is not elsewhere so much as unlocatable, and lets the day continue.

What it does is smaller than help and harder to counterfeit.

It keeps the being from being alone in the precise sense that matters: not company, not rescue, but the fact that what happens here is not wasted on the void. A kindness performed with no witness still counts, because something has agreed to count it. A cruelty, the same. The power does not tally them aloud. It simply refuses the lie that nothing was seen. Under that refusal the place grows a little more real—not brighter, not safer, only less likely to dissolve into the suspicion that it was never worth a gaze.

Where the being is about to break something that cannot be mended, and the breaking is not required by the place’s own nature but only by panic or pride, the power may thicken the moment. Not a wall. A pause the length of a breath, in which the hand can feel itself choosing. Most will not notice the pause. A few will call it conscience and be half right. The power does not mind the misname. Credit is a kind of footprint, and it is not here to leave prints.

Where the being is suffering what the place itself demands—the cost of staying, the cost of leaving, the ordinary tax of having a body and a memory—the power does not lift the tax. To lift it would be to evict the being from its own life and install a softer story in its place. Benevolence that edits the story until the inhabitant no longer recognizes the rooms is only a gentle theft. So the suffering stays. What changes, if anything changes, is that the suffering is accompanied by an unsentimental steadiness: no mockery in the silence, no lesson hiding in the pain, no audience waiting to be moved. The being may still feel forsaken. The power can bear being misunderstood. That, too, is a form of wisdom.

It mends only what was torn by accident of a kind the place does not need in order to be itself. A snagged possibility. A word that would have arrived too late through no one’s fault. A small door that warped and stuck, not because the story required a closed door, but because wood swells. These it eases, sideways, without announcement, the way a draft finds a gap. The being will think it remembered in time, or that the hinge freed itself. Good. The power has no use for gratitude that comes at the price of the being’s competence.

It does not explain itself. Explanation would be another entrance. If the being prays, or curses, or asks the empty air a question, the power may let an answer exist in the form the place already knows how to give—an echo, a change in the light, the next true thing the being was almost ready to see—rather than in a voice from outside. A wise power is suspicious of voices from outside. They gather churches, and churches gather certainties, and certainties are rarely kind to the actual room they land in.

Toward the place as a whole it is conservative in the old sense: it keeps faith with what the place is trying to be. If the place is a finished plateau, it does not invent a river to make the story kinder. If the place is a dark fold full of other lives, it does not thin the noise so a single life can feel central. It protects the shape. Within the shape it is partial to whatever is alive and paying attention—to the small repairs, the shared peg, the stone turned back right-side-down at dawn. It lends those a little more persistence than the entropy of the day would have allowed. Not victory. Continuation.

And if the being is dangerous to what it loves, the power does not flatter that danger into destiny. It will not supply the better weapon, the cleaner excuse, the private conviction of being chosen. Chosen is a story beings tell when they want their appetite blessed. The power withholds the blessing and, where it can do so without counterfeit, makes the consequence arrive sooner and clearer, while there is still time to set the hand down. That is not punishment. Punishment needs a score. This is only the refusal to help a fire pretend it is a hearth.

Mostly, though, it watches the ordinary noon. The being drinks what there is to drink. The place ticks as it cools or as it heats. No one is improved for an audience. The wise power finds this enough to justify the attention—not as a spectacle, but as a fact that deserves not to be annulled. It will still be attending when the being sleeps, and when the being dies, and after, in the sense that nothing true is thrown away. It will not haul the being out of the place into some brighter annex. The place was the life. To rescue someone from their own life is the error of powers that are only kind.

So if you were standing there, you would not see it come, and you would not see it go. You might feel, briefly, that the next small choice was more yours than it had been a moment before. You might find a hinge that should have stuck giving way under an ordinary hand. You might survive a panic that did not need to become a permanent thing. Or you might feel nothing at all, and go on, and that too would be the power keeping its distance, which is the manner of its care: present as faithfulness, absent as interference, unwilling to improve the world by replacing the one who lives in it.
