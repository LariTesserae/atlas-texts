They would come without weather.

Not as a clearing of the sky, not as a voice in the channels. The valley already has a climate that does not negotiate, and a wise power does not arrive by breaking the first law a place has managed to keep. They would come as attention that does not ask the water to move in order to be seen.

For a long time—longer than the town’s longest pause, which is already the length of the day—they would do nothing. Benevolence that has not looked is only appetite with better manners. So they would look. At the film on the fern-tip that never quite falls. At the tool set beside the unfinished course of stone. At the roofs that do not leak and the courtyards that hold one tree as if one were a complete number. At the absence of tracks, which is not the same as an absence of tenants.

They would find the being where the stillness is thickest: not in the town, which is a manner of living, but at the upper rim, where the youngest faces weep and the mineral crust has only begun its first sentence. Or they would find that the being is not a figure at all, but the agreement itself—the single water-level the channels keep, the decision that nothing here will be asked to become. A power that is wise can love a decision without sharing it.

What they would notice, and not rush to name, is that the place is not in pain. It is not failing. Seed sets or does not; runners take the next damp pocket when the film allows; the tanks are full to a line that does not need carving because the line does not drift. No one is hungry in a country without mouths that hunt. No one is hunted. The quiet is not the quiet after screaming. It is the quiet of a room that was furnished completely and then left with the door unlatched, in case someone wished to leave, and no one has wished.

So they would not send animals. They would not tip the weirs. They would not lengthen the shadows to prove that time still knows the way. To correct an equilibrium that is not a wound is a kind of vanity, and wisdom is allergic to it. The missing birds are not a debt the valley owes the idea of a proper valley. The young rock is allowed to be young. The town is allowed to be finished in the same glance that shows it unfinished.

They might, once, sit. Not as a test. As company that does not require speech, because the place has already taught that sound dies against damp stone and does not return, and a guest who insists on being heard is only another weather. They would let the warm air be thick. They would let a drop consider falling and then not fall. They would not be bored. Boredom is for powers that came to intervene and found no handle.

If the being is a person—one of the figures in a doorway, or the last one who still smooths a stone already smooth—they would be met as a person, not as a problem the landscape has been keeping. The power would not ask what they want until it was clear the question itself would not tilt the water. Some beings cannot survive being asked. The asking arrives like a draft in a country that has no wind, and everything that was balanced begins to lean toward an answer.

Should the being look back, and should that look contain a request—not a grand one; a cup set down meaning *stay*, or a hand lifted meaning *the next terrace has no path and I have started to wonder what is under the level where water can stand*—then they would answer at the scale of the request. A wise benevolence is not dramatic. It might be a path that was always almost there, one joint in the young cliff softened by a film of moisture wide enough for a foot, leading down only as far as the being chooses to go, and stopping where curiosity stops, without a vista arranged at the bottom to shame them for turning around. It might be a single bird, not a restoration of faunas: one small body that does not need to be admired, that drinks from a tank and leaves, so the valley learns it can include a visitor without becoming a different valley. It might be nothing but the knowledge, placed gently where knowledge can be refused, that the door was never locked. That the stillness is kept, not imposed. That leaving would not collapse the channels.

If the being does not look back, they would not tap the shoulder of the world to force a glance. Attention is not a claim. They would remain through one more unchanging noon, so the place is not merely surveyed and dismissed—dismissal is its own violence, a second abandonment performed by a god who wanted to feel effective—and then they would go by the same door they came, which is to say without weather, without a sign, without a moral left drying on the stone.

What they would leave behind is not a blessing you could find. The tanks would still be level. The tools would still be set down. The fern-tip would still be deciding. But the deciding would be, by a margin too small to hear, a choice again, and not only a fate. The power’s work, if it can be called work, would be to have made room for that margin and then declined to occupy it.

They would not watch from the rim to see what the being does with the margin. Watching for the outcome is how benevolence turns into ownership. A wise power can love a place and still let the next hour belong to it.

Somewhere, after they have gone, a drop becomes heavy enough. It falls. The pool does not rise. The sound is still too small to keep. That is allowed to be enough. It was always going to be allowed to be enough. The difference is only that now, if it is not enough, someone in a doorway can set the cup down and mean a different thing, and the air—stable, warm, without a draft—will not punish them for meaning it.
