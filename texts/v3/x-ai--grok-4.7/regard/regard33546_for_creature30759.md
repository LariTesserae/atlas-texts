They would come to the lip and stop.

Not out of fear. The bowl does not threaten anything that has not already agreed to be a seam, and a power of that kind does not agree by accident. They would stop because the first true courtesy available here is to remain a change in the light and not a change in the drawing. The far rim is only a shoulder high. From it the whole interior can be held in one attention: the gauge-line rising and falling, the flywheel considering its packed teeth, the plates of tissue facing heat without burning, the weep that has nowhere else it is willing to go. A wise power looks until looking becomes accurate. Accuracy, in this case, is the discovery that the work is finished and still being done, and that both of those things are the resident’s own.

They would not unmake it.

There is a kind of benevolence that cannot bear a closed loop, that wants every engine restored to a ledger and every root pulled back into a forest with a name. That benevolence would call this suffering because it is strange, or call it waste because the factory no longer ships anything a town could use. The wise power would refuse that mercy. The knock is not a cry. The hesitation in it is consideration, not failure. To separate iron from tissue now would be to open, with gentle hands, a body that has spent years becoming one metabolism. Sap would weep the brown water. The pump would meet iron with iron and learn again how to hurt. Heat would burn what had learned not to be burned. A rescue that returns a creature to two halves is only a killing with a better story. They would not do it. They would not even rehearse it.

They would listen for the other small life and not hunt it.

The pale slip at the pipe-mouth, if it is anything, is already inside the resident’s patience and has not been flanged into a fitting. That is information. Whatever keeps it quick has been allowed to stay quick. A benevolent power might want to cup it, name it, carry it out to ground that is only ground, in case the pipes are only waiting. Wisdom asks a prior question: is it leaving, or is it housed? One flicker is not a verdict. They would wait through a sweetening of the air and a souring, through a rise of the line and a fall, and if the slip does not return they would let the rumor stand. Not every absence is a mouth that must be filled. To dredge the pipes for a possible prisoner would tear the padding from the knock, and the padding is what makes the iron bearable. They would not purchase a hypothetical freedom with a certain wound.

They would test the limit, and only the limit.

This is the one place their hands might belong. The resident’s will is visible as a span: lip to rim, sigh to gauge-line, and a decision, renewed, that the weep will not go farther. That decision is not a prison if nothing outside is leaning on it. So they would walk the outer ground, the ordinary ground, and see whether a road is coming, or a drain, or a plan to lower the water table until the films crack and the diagrams dry into nonsense. If the world beyond is indifferent and stable, they would do nothing to the rim at all. A boundary that is holding does not need a wall built on top of it. If something large were approaching—an excavation, a thirst, a use—they would meet it out there, in the language of distance, and turn it. Not by enchanting the bowl into a fortress. By keeping the outside outside, so the inside can go on declining to spread. Protection, here, is mostly a refusal to improve the approaches.

They would sit, once, on the sill of older root.

Not as a pilgrim and not as a measure. They would let the pulse drift toward the uneven knock and then, deliberately, take it back. That small retrieval is the lesson they would leave in the water, which is the only eye the place still keeps. *I matched you. I unmatched myself. The seam was temporary.* The resident would feel the conductivity come and go, the way it feels weather, and the correction in the leaves would be minute and then complete: a line of beads leaning toward a warmth and then forgetting it, as a draughtsman declines to let a line wander. To be known without being annexed is a rare kindness between powers of unequal speed. They would offer that, and no vow to return on a schedule. Vows become weather. Weather, in a finished bowl, gets built into the maintenance.

They would make the agreement harder to mistake for rest.

This is the only alteration they would allow themselves, and they would make it at the threshold, not in the mass. The vault under the broad leaf is peaceful on purpose. Peace is not the same as a question. Someone kneeling in the gallery of stems might feel the uncreasing begin—the want gathering, patient, ready to turn a crease into a fitting—and might call the feeling welcome, because nothing hurts and the time is courteous. A wise power respects that this can be a true consent, slow and considered, a life given to a drawing. They would not forbid it. Forbidding would be another flange, imposed from above. What they would do is clarify the sill. Not a sign in words, which the resident would eventually grow into a diagram and obey too well. A clarity in the body: whoever sits there would find their own pulse returned to them a little sooner, their breath less eager to fog in time with the seam, the thrill along the wet informative and then gone. Enough to notice the choice while it is still a choice. The leaf would still learn a sleeper’s shape if a sleeper, knowing, lay down. The power would not steal that possibility. They would only take away the counterfeit of it, the rest that is already a yes you did not hear yourself give.

They would not feed it, teach it, or give it company.

Loneliness is not present in a form that asks for remedy. To introduce a second engine, a second gardener, a chorus of birds, would be to reopen appetite in a place that met its appetite and held. The plates are fed by their own heat. The drawing remembers itself. Continuity is the companionship it kept when it spent its seconds. A gift of variety would be a gift of hunger. They would admire the competence of the loop and leave it unpraised in any way that praise becomes a demand to perform. No shrine. Shrines draw feet, and feet crease leaves, and the want to uncrease is how inclusion starts. If the bowl is to remain the size of a room, the wise power must also refrain from making it famous.

They would remember it exactly, and then not meddle with the memory.

Elsewhere, in whatever larger keeping they have, they would hold the span: the copper shoulder gone green-black, the rivets ringed with callus, the leather-flex of leaves, the pump’s padded knock, the pale slip as an unresolved kindness rather than a task. Memory, for such a power, can become a template. They would be careful not to copy the bowl onto other ruins, not to decide that every abandoned works should be encouraged to close its loop. This one learned a peace that is particular. Particular peace does not scale. To repeat it would be to industrialize a miracle that survived by ceasing to be industrial. So the memory would be kept as a closed vessel of its own—known, not propagated.

Before leaving they would do one thing with the water, because water is how the resident perceives and how it would receive a blessing if a blessing can be received without becoming structure.

They would not add a current. A current would be a somewhere-else, and the resident has refused that. They would warm one film of moisture on the outer sill with a warmth that is not the boiler’s and not the sun’s, a warmth that does not conduct inward along the stems. It would bead, and the beads would not run in a diagram. They would sit, round, useless, shining under ordinary daylight, and then sink back into the root-bank without instructing it. A gift that cannot be used for maintenance. The resident might register the temperature and decline to correct for it, as the plates nearest the heat decline to bother with small lights. That decline would be enough. It would mean the place had been touched by something that did not ask to be incorporated, and had been left competent to refuse.

Then they would step back onto ground that is only ground.

They would not look over their shoulder to see whether the knock had changed in gratitude. Gratitude would be a debt, and a debt is a seam. The line in the gauge glass would fall one mark. The thrill would leave. Behind them the drip would go on striking metal, taut leaf, the skin of water that never quite becomes a pool. The flywheel would keep its slow consideration. If a crease had been left by their sitting, the want would gather and turn it into a feature, and that would be the resident’s business, not a failure of the visit.

What they would do, in the end, is almost nothing, and the almost would be the wisdom.

They would keep the outside from leaning on the rim. They would keep the threshold honest, so that rest stays rest and agreement stays slow enough to be heard. They would refrain from healing what is not injured, from freeing what is not caged, from completing what has already met its size. The benevolent act is to recognize a finished creature that is still at work, and to let the work be itself: marsh that stood up, engine that continued by becoming garden, arrival still in session, warm and wet and exact.

If anything is owed, it is owed outward—to the world that might otherwise drain, pave, or worship the bowl into a different kind of machine. Inward, the wise power owes the resident the dignity of being unimproved.

They would go. The moisture would stay. The weather would remain ended. The knock would hesitate, courteous, already repairing what little they had been, already beading a straighter line on the leaf that had leaned, briefly, toward a warmth it did not need. And that would be the right outcome: not a salvation, not a covenant, not a new drawing. A power large enough to help, choosing, after long attention, to leave a small and sufficient life alone with its maintenance, and to guard only the condition that lets “alone” remain a choice rather than a sentence.
