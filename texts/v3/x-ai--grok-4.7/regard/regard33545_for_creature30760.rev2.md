They would not arrive as weather. Weather is already the language of the shelf, and a new bank on the western rim would only send someone to the spillway with wet hands and a count in their mouth. A power that was wise would know better than to speak first in rain.

They would come the way grade comes into an argument: by being already there when the stain starts to dry, and by having walked the causeway often enough that their presence does not revise the path. No face the districts could fetch. No damper-signal. If the cornice birds shifted on their ledges, it would be the ordinary shift, the one that means a belt slipped two streets over and the meal-smell changed. If a band in the gutter nosed the air and did not nose a hand, that too would be ordinary. Wisdom, here, would look like not becoming an event.

They would listen for a long time. Longer than a grade dispute. Longer than the months it takes a stack to be bricked around a newer boiler while the old one fills with birds. They would learn the layers the way a child learns them, except they would not need to sleep through the throb in order to stop fearing it. Water ticking in the seep-lines. The flat pour in the races. The knock of the loose board. Belts. The chalky cry. The wet mutter. Wingbeat when a flock is low. Human voices fitted into what the air has left them. They would not call the loudness suffering. Occupancy is not a wound. A place can be full the way a cistern is full, at the mark everyone recognizes, and the fullness can be the condition of its life rather than the emergency that called you.

So they would not drain it. They would not put trees on the ridges. They would not hush the flocks so a person could hear their own thought cross a square. They would not hand the markets a green thing in a basket and wait to be thanked. The paste is not a punishment. The stain in the creases is not dirt. The warm that does not leave at night is not a fever. A power that mistook any of these for a fault would be only strong, and strength without the walk in the rain is how seeps get stolen.

They would look, instead, for what the shelf itself does not have a procedure for.

There are a few. Not many. The place has had centuries to grow procedures, and most of what hurts it already has a knock, a board, a court, a distribution down the walkways by evening.

They would find the child who wakes at every belt-slip and will be tired for years, and the household that has arranged itself around that without much talk. They would not lift the child out. Out is the descent, and the descent is a different country, loud in the wrong way, and the child belongs to a stair. What they might do is narrow: a thickening of sleep in the hour the lines change over, a small mercy in the nerves, not enough to make the child deaf to a race that has changed its mind. The household would notice only that the mats were quieter. They would not know whom to thank. Thanking is not required. A wise attention does not invoice.

They would find the ones who go down the face and do not come back, the ones spoken of as if they had died respectably. They would not drag them home. Some of those stays are true. A dry wind can be a life. But they would stand, once, at the foot of an old pack-way where the trees start and the nights insult, and they would make the return path no harder than the leaving path. Not easier. Equal. A person who turned around on the third night would find the marks still readable, the seep still theirs, the film willing to come back into the creases on the old schedule, which is a week, not a judgment. The children who follow returners to smell the other dust would still have someone to follow. That is enough.

They would find the water room where a centimeter has been argued past the point of watching a stain. Both crews immovable. Both schedules old. The cisterns not yet saying. They would not rule. Ruling is a theft of the only authority the shelf trusts, which is memory of water. They might, on one night of reduced clatter, let the level in the great cut sit half a finger nearer the true mark than either stubbornness had allowed, so that in the morning both sides could claim the stone had spoken and neither would be entirely wrong. The spillways would be opened. The heat would not be spent. People would eat. Later, no one would agree on what the level had been. Agreement is not the gift. The gift is that the line did not stop.

They would look a long time at the body given to the flats, and the body given to the shaft above a race, and the body taken by a furnace that can bear the load, and they would not unify the rites. A household’s way is a seep. What they might do for the dead is smaller than consolation and harder: they would keep the name from being used too soon on a stranger, and they would keep the location from being argued while the warmth was still leaving. A few days of stillness in the vertical splice. The reduced clatter unchanged. The bands muttering. Someone at the spillway if the races ran hard. The living would say the weather had organized slowly, which it does, and they would be right enough.

They would not fix the haze. Farness is not a right the shelf owes anyone. But there is a rare hour when the cloud thins and the next district’s facing shows as facing, and people stop, and feel slightly foolish, and go back to the work. In that hour the power would do nothing at all, which is a kind of doing. They would not prolong it into spectacle. They would let it be exactly as long as it is, and they would be glad, in the manner of powers that do not need to be seen, that someone looked.

If they spoke, they would speak once, and not in a voice that cuts a square. They would speak the way people speak here, close, into the gap. Not to a crowd. To one person coming off a line with their ears still counting, paste on the fingers, sole pegged well enough not to slip in the finger of water along the paving’s lower edge. They would say nothing about destiny, nothing about the heights, nothing about being watched. They might say: the board in the third race is loose again. Or: the band under the drying shed will take a hand today and will not take one tomorrow. Or only: you can sleep. The spillway is open.

Then they would be gone in the sense that matters, which is that the mills would not have stopped, and the flocks would still be drifting rather than wheeling, and the stone would still be sweating at noon, and a traveler who stayed a month would still fail to find the edge of the wet. No temple. No new mark on the cistern wall. The knock-signals unchanged. A sole that does not slip. Fat in a good month. A laugh tipped mouth to ear so it stays in the gap the shelf has left.

Benevolence, if it is wise, does not improve a world that has already become itself. It keeps the procedures from failing at the one seam they cannot see. It stands in the doorway of the water room and does not sit down. It learns the smell of the paste well enough not to flinch, and then does not ask to be fed.

What they would do, mostly, is stay attentive and withhold their hand. And once, perhaps, twice in a generation, when a spillway board had swollen and the person whose song is about opening it with their hands was elsewhere on the causeway, they would put a strength into those hands that was not quite the person’s, and the board would move, and the water would take its path, and the song would remain accurate, which is the only immortality the shelf has ever wanted.
