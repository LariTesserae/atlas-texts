They would not arrive as weather. Weather is what this place has already refused, and a wise power does not open a door the house was built without.

They would come the way the steam sometimes thins at the west piles: not a clearing, a permission. For the length of a breath the roofs would be legible again—the hall, the tower, the gleam of a channel, one white bird in the act of lifting—and then the air would close, and the difference would be that something had looked the whole of it and not wished it otherwise.

The being who lives here is not one of the eleven, and not the engines, and not the mullet in their wheel. It is the agreement. Water at the marks. Speed that does not cycle. Edges cut back to vertical so the ground can stay new. Animals fitted inside a human schedule without being asked. People fitted inside a sound that has become the condition of other sounds. It is not wicked. It is young, and it is complete too soon, and it does not know that completeness can be a kind of weather too.

A benevolent power, being wise, would not break the agreement. Unmake the engines and the lagoon climbs the cuts by night, the piers go under, the cats drown on the pads that were warm because something burned. Grant a true season and the return water cools, the mullet scatter, the midge columns fail, the swallows leave the louvers, and the crew, whose contract was the climate, wake to a silence that feels like accusation. Mercy that requires the death of every arrangement already keeping breath in bodies is not mercy. It is a preference for a prettier ruin.

So they would do small things, and only where the agreement pinches a particular life without being what holds the rest.

They would sit, once, on the railing above the unused ditch, where the black cat with the white chest watches frogs and calls it work. They would not speak to the cat. The cat would know, and would not move, and that would be enough: one creature’s boredom recognized as a vocation and not corrected.

They would be in the galley on the second day after the supply boat, when the green things are already gone and the youngest is eating rice as if that were the whole of what a mouth is for. Not a feast. A lime, absurd, out of season for every inland he has ever seen, set where his hand will find it and not where a sermon could grow around it. He would not know whom to thank. He would taste it and startle—the way he still startles at the heron—and this time he would not pretend. The tartness would live in his mouth through the valve order. The square-shouldered man would notice the face and not ask. Wisdom includes not making a witness into an audience.

They would press the corner of the photograph flat, the inland street, the dry tree, and the tape would hold. Not so the tall man with the burned wrists would leave. So that when he opens the locker he would not have to decide, every time, whether the curl means the other life is already losing. Memory is allowed to stay in focus. Desire is allowed to stay without being turned into a plan the contract cannot hear.

They would stand once beside the woman on the night wash line, the scar through her eyebrow, in the hour when she hears a bearing before the gauge admits it. They would not take the hearing from her; it is her competence, and competence is one of the few kinds of dignity the steam has not sweated off. They would only make the gauge agree a minute sooner, so that for one night the proof arrives before the dread. She would check the fitting, find the looseness, tighten it, and sleep without the dream in which the hall speaks in a new voice and no one else wakes. In the morning the engines would sound as they have always sounded. That is the point.

For the being itself—the agreement, the young completeness—they would do the hardest kindness, which is to withhold improvement.

They would let the whistle blow on the hour and vanish into the floor of sound. They would let the banks slump and be cut back. They would let the dragonflies keep a clock no one wrote down. They would not grant a view. A view would teach the place to want a horizon, and a horizon, here, is a kind of leaving, and not everyone who lives inside an agreement is ready to survive the lesson that it could have been otherwise.

What they would give the being is attention that does not edit. To be seen exactly as it is: hot, loud, new, faithful, slightly cruel in the way a schedule is cruel, and full of lives that have already made a workable tenderness inside it—the cats on the pads, the nests that do not crack because the vibration is constant, the youngest’s clean hands, the woman’s ear, the mullet who do not flee a shadow. A wise power knows that some places are not prisons and not paradises. They are practices. To smash a practice because it is not beautiful from above is the error of powers who love outcomes more than inhabitants.

If they spoke at all, it would be under the engines, where only the agreement could hear them, and they would say something with no command in it:

You are allowed to continue.
You are not required to become gentle before you are ready.
When a life here asks for a lime, or a photograph that lies flat, or a gauge that tells the truth a minute early, that asking is not betrayal of you. It is how you learn you are not the only one who lives here.
The marks on the piles may stay. The light need not improve.
I have seen you. I will not unmake you to prove I am kind.

Then the steam would close. The outfall would keep its circle of silt. Someone would slice clay back to the paint. A heron would lift, once, loud, and the midges would scatter and reform, and the place would go on as if nothing had visited—which is how you know the visitor was wise, and how you know the small things were real: the lime gone, the tape holding, the bearing quiet, the cat still at its post, unimproved, and no longer unaccompanied.
