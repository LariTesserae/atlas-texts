They would do nothing that the water could measure.

A power of that kind does not arrive as weather. It does not thicken the mist, raise a level, sweeten a channel, or lay one truer lamina along a rim. Those would be actions, and action, however gentle, is a change in the fall. The beings read changes in the fall. To be read by them as an event would already be a kind of insistence, and insistence is not what wisdom sounds like when it is quiet enough.

So the power attends, and the attending has no weight. It learns the grammar the way the stone learned it: by duration, not by grasp. It hears the neighborhoods of ticking separate and rejoin. It tastes, without a mouth, the iron reach and the yellow reach and the faint green signature of a crust that flushed three basins up and died. It follows one traveler through an understreet and does not hurry the week in which the traveler decides that this fog is enough. It watches a margin open after rain and watches the smaller margin fail, abraded into the white dust the slots have always carried, and it does not rescue the failure, because rescue would teach the city a pace it does not keep.

What it understands, and does not need to announce even to itself, is that the place is already finished in the only sense that applies to something still becoming. The residents are not incomplete keepers awaiting a keeper of keepers. Their feeding is the maintenance. Their deaths are the laminations. Their silence is not a lack the air forgot to fill. A benevolent power that has looked long enough recognizes the difference between a wound and a form. This is a form. To heal it would be to wound it.

If there is a larger world beyond the plateau's edge—and there is, the power knows, though the edge is days of walking past any visible change—then the one deed worth doing is negative and elsewhere. Whatever else moves in that larger world, whatever hungers or schemes or simply blunders at the scale of countries, is turned aside before it can find the rise. No road is cut. No name is given that could be followed. No story is carried down into the lower air that would make a listener want to come and see. The plateau continues to be difficult to notice, which is the oldest courtesy available to it. The power spends whatever strength is required on that difficulty, and spends it far off, where the spending cannot tick.

Here, among the rims, it leaves no blessing the residents could bind into their mineral. A blessing would taste like a foreign season, and they would edit it, patiently, until it became local, and the editing would have been a labor they did not owe. Better a power that does not ask to be metabolized.

It remains a while in the way a clear noon remains: changing nothing about the whiteness of the walls, making the distances no shorter and no more symbolic. A nearby body, spread along a sill, eases neither toward it nor away. There is no warmth that does not belong to the stone, no pressure, no silt lifted by an attention that has consented to be weightless. In the deep cistern a wide life turns on its own schedule, and the plume rises and settles, and the glass rights itself. The power does not count this as a meeting.

When it goes, it goes as the evening goes—a thinning, not a departure you could mark. By morning the levels have shifted by the ordinary inches, according to where the night's own condensation preferred to fall. One channel has a voice again. One has gone back to a film. No one on the plateau is grateful, because gratitude would require having been given something other than the continuance of the law. The law holds. The hesitation at the rim completes itself one finger-width along, at the rate the district has always required, and the water that crosses the place forgets, as it is right to forget, that anything outside the works was ever looking.
