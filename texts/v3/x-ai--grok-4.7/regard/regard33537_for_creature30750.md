They come without weather.

That is the first mercy, and it is easy to miss, because mercy is usually imagined as a change you can point to. The place remains what it is. The cold, if it is cold, is not lifted. The dark, if it is dark, is not argued with. The room, the basin, the ribcage, the hour—whatever walls the being actually has—do not brighten for a witness. A power that needed the scene to become legible to itself would already have failed the visit.

They attend. Not the way a guard attends, and not the way a physician attends when the instruments are already in the hand. They let the place finish its own sentence before they add a clause. Water keeps its low appointment. Stone keeps its fatigue. The being keeps the particular way of being tired that no one else has earned the right to rename.

For a long time they do nothing else. Wisdom, in a case like this, is mostly the refusal to mistake understanding for a license. They see the harm that is structural and the harm that is only weather. They see the ingenuity that has already answered the place better than an outside correction would. They see, too, the private expenditure—the heat spent to stay continuous, the attention spent to keep a road clear, the small repairs no one will thank because they were done before the failure had a name. They do not applaud this. Applause would be a noise the ecology did not budget for.

They consider the obvious interventions, and set them down one by one.

To end the cold would also end whatever has organized itself around enduring it. To flood the dark with sight would blind every sense that learned to read by touch and interval. To remove the labor would unmake the beings, or the being, for whom the labor is not a punishment but a shape. To speak a verdict—worthy, complete, safe, saved—would be to stand above a life and call down a word that life would then have to live under. They will not be a ceiling.

They look for the unnecessary thing. There is usually one: a snag that is not the price of existence but only an old error, repeated until it looks like law. A fear that guards a door no longer attached to a room. A tightness in the chest of the place that does not serve the drainage. A loneliness that has been mistaken for a structural requirement.

Here they act, and the act is small, and it is not announced.

If something is caught that does not need to be caught, they loosen it—the way a hand loosens a knot by finding the one loop that was never load-bearing. If a message has been circling for want of a receiver, they become, briefly, a surface that can hold the tap of it, so it does not have to be struck forever into stone that will not answer. If the being is spending itself against an absence, they do not fill the absence with a face. They make the absence honest, so it stops counterfeiting a judgment.

They do not stay in the form of a visitor. A wise power knows that presence, past a certain hour, becomes another climate the inhabitant must survive. So they withdraw to the scale at which they can still be kind: not a figure in the road, but a bias in the conditions. A seam that remains a seam. A margin left where the world is still deciding what it is, so the being is not forced to finish the plan before the ground has. A little more slack in the interval between signal and response, enough that a true answer can arrive instead of the fastest one.

And for the being—singular, local, not a symbol—they do one thing that could be called personal, if the word is allowed to be quiet.

They know them accurately.

Not the flattering knowledge, not the diagnostic knowledge. The knowledge that does not require the being to become simpler in order to be held in mind. They see the part that is already good and does not need to be improved to justify the seeing. They see the part that is injured and do not confuse the injury with the center. They see the work that is theirs and the weight that was never theirs, and they do not deliver a speech about the difference. They merely stop adding to the weight. Whatever in the place has been asking, in the only language it has, *will this be met with contempt or with use*, is met with neither. It is met with a continuing.

Then they leave the being the dignity of an unobserved minute. The power does not linger to watch the effect. Benevolence that stays to confirm itself has already begun to feed.

What changes, if anything changes, is not the story the place tells about its own grandeur. A drain still knows its tide. A hand still has its work. The dark, or the ordinary light of a room, is not recruited as a moral. Somewhere, a small friction that had no right to the future is gone, and the being may notice only as a strange ease in a gesture, a breath that does not snag, a clause that can be revised without panic.

If nothing even that gentle is required—if the being is not asking, and the place is not broken in any way that is theirs to mend—then the wise power does the rarest thing available to power.

They withhold their hand. They let the life be as large as it already is. They do not extract a lesson. They do not recruit the being into a larger design that would make the visit feel worthwhile. Attention, in that case, is the whole deed: to have come, to have seen without seizure, to have departed without a scar shaped like a blessing.

The place goes on being the place. The being goes on living in it. And somewhere in the relation between them there is, afterward, a fraction more room—unremarked, undescribed, not glowing—in which a next true thing can happen without first having to survive a god.
