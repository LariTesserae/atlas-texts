They do not come down through the cloud-sea. That would be an entrance, and an entrance is a shape. What arrives is a change in how the shelves are held—no brighter, no warmer, only attended from a direction the light does not use. The fine delay in the tower's shadow lengthens by less than a blink, as if something on the far side of the agreement had leaned its weight onto the same old colleague and then, catching itself, eased back so as not to make the birds revise their nests.

The power is wise enough to notice that first small error and not repeat it. Benevolence, here, would be easy to mistake for help. The basins do not need draining or blessing. The herds are not few. The young who blink both lids at once are not failing; they are still inside the time the place allows for learning, which is the time erosion takes, and it has a floor. A gift of speech, a name, a road, a clearer memory of who raised the glass and why the floors were sealed—any of these would ask the country to start over in miniature. The rings in the cut wood are hairline-fine because nothing has been asked that. The power looks until looking is finished, which is the only courtesy the shelves already know how to receive, and does not sharpen the look into a plan.

What they understand, after a while that would be long anywhere else and is merely an afternoon here, is that the place is not waiting. The beings who walk the threads are not priests of a neglected machine and not a people in need of a patron. They are the part of the agreement that still has hands. The instruction too old to be read is not lost knowledge waiting to be restored to them. It is already in the palm, in the second lid, in the way a path diverts a few spans so a loop can close through an elder who no longer walks it. To lift that memory out and set it down somewhere safe—in a word, in a vault, in the power's own keeping as a story—would be to remove it from the only body that can still mean it.

So they do not teach. They do not heal the tower of its lateness. They do not stand inside the ring to feel the deeper marsh and call that intimacy. They remain on the outside of the agreement, which is the only place their size fits: above the rim, under the cloud-sea, in the white the beings do not watch, where the thick breath ends and ordinary height resumes its claim.

The one thing they do is small and has no witness who would call it a deed. The rim has always been a seam, and seams take strain. Height wants the heat back. The cloud wants in. On certain days the film at the last shelf thins, and whoever has the thread that day must stand longer with a palm on the stone, spending attention that might have gone to the elder, or to the glass, or to nothing at all—which is also a kind of keeping. The power sets a hand, or whatever a hand becomes when it is not a body, against the underside of that seam. Not to open it. Not to cross. To carry a little of the wanting-in, the wanting-back, so the last wet stone does not have to be argued with quite so often.

They match the pitch without adding a note. Where the air was about to remember it was high, it forgets for a span longer than it used to—a narrow mercy, local, unannounced. The sweetness in the breath on the final shelf shifts and settles with less effort from the one who walks that far. Some days the loop does not need to include the rim at all. The path uses the unused span elsewhere: a longer attendance beside the elder, a slower press of what remains of a grazer into the moss, a young one left an extra hour in the sedge to get the two lids out of step by sheer unhurried living.

They do not ask to be known for this. Being known would be another weight, and they have just taken weight off. If one of the beings, palm to the last stone, feels the work answer sooner than their strength accounts for, they do not investigate. Investigation is a hurried virtue. They take the hand away when the pitch is true and turn back into the heat. The power, on the far side of the white, feels the release as a small completed thing—the way a leaf completes its tip of water—and stays where they are.

Over a great many slow years they change nothing you could point to and several things you could miss. The scalloped cliffs go on shedding grains that do not accumulate into a new profile. The wrong shadow remains wrong by the original amount, not by the extra lean of that first careless hour; the power learned, and did not make the birds renegotiate. The elder who sits near the tower is allowed to become warmth in the stone in the season the stone is ready, not in a season hurried by a rim that kept calling someone away. No marker is raised. The path forgets by the old method.

Once, only once, they answer the sound the beings use when a body is required where a body is not—the soft clap inside the real clap of a platter-leaf. They answer it from under the cloud, at the same pitch, not louder. It is not a summons and not a revelation. It is the information that the outside of the agreement is held, and therefore need not be feared into a larger vocabulary. The upright one in the night hears it, or the place hears it through them. No one walks to the rim to find the speaker. The loop was whole before the sound and remains whole after. That is accepted as sufficient, which is what wisdom looks like when it has decided not to be visible.

They stay. Not as a ruler under the mountain and not as a god expecting the double lid to be turned toward them. As a maintenance the country never had to evolve a word for, because it arrives from the side the people do not face. Benevolence, in a place that has already arrived at itself, is mostly the refusal to improve it. The wise power does that refusal every day, with their weight set carefully against the seam, and the shelves go on—wet, green, loud, high, warm, and old—under an attention that has made itself light enough to live there without becoming weather.
