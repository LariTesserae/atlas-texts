They would come the way weather comes to a sealed room: not by breaking the seal, but by being the kind of attention that makes a seal suddenly feel like a choice rather than a law.

They would not announce themselves. A power that needs to be known in order to be kind is already asking the place to rearrange itself around a visitor, and this place has already finished rearranging. The sharp edges, the unfinished groove, the scaffold holding nothing, the animals occupying their intervals — these are not waiting. To arrive with light enough to see every stand at once would be a kind of erasure. So they would take the amber as it is, and stand in the clear space that was empty before anyone came and is, in the only sense that matters here, still empty.

They would listen past the roar until the chord declared itself: the click, the rattle, the cough that has not stopped. They would not try to resolve it. Wisdom recognizes a music that was never a melody. Benevolence does not conduct.

For a long while — long in the only clock this chamber keeps, which is the fact of ticking — they would do nothing. Doing nothing here is not neglect. It is the matching of a completed thing. The growths do not thirst. The plates do not ask to be matched. The creature on the ceiling is not lost; it is exact. A hand extended to help would be a hand extended into a socket that is already full.

What they might do, if anything, is smaller than help and harder.

They might notice the groove that stops mid-thought, and not finish it. Completion by another hand is a theft dressed as a gift. Instead they might sit where the one who cut it would have sat, in the same amber, at the same distance from the same stand, and leave the tool — if there is a tool, and if there is not, then the patience — exactly where a returning hand could find it without searching. Not a message. A courtesy. The difference matters. A message would require the being to become a reader of someone else's intention. A courtesy only requires that the chamber remain itself.

They might, once, and only once, make themselves perceptible at the scale of the smallest animal: not a vision, not a voice in the roar, but a slight change in the warmth under one fracture-plate, no warmer than the difference between one breath and the next. Enough that a being who is paying attention in the way this place pays attention — by interval, not by narrative — could register that the system had been regarded and had not been corrected. If no being notices, that too is an answer, and they would not repeat the warmth in order to be noticed. Benevolence that insists on acknowledgement has already become a demand.

If there is a being here who is lonely, they would not cure the loneliness by company. Loneliness in a finished system may be the shape of a chosen solitude, and those are easy to vandalize with kindness. They would wait to be asked in the only language the place speaks, which is position: a plate moved, a knot loosened, an interval broken on purpose. Until then, asking would be the violence.

And if there is suffering — real suffering, not the ache of an unfinished groove, not the strangeness of a clock with no hands — they would act, and the action would be specific to the wound, and it would stop when the wound stopped, and it would leave no monument. A wise power does not harvest gratitude from a rescue. It does not remain as a climate. It does not improve the craft past the first sufficient knot. The scaffold was never failing. It was holding nothing, which is what it was built to hold.

What they would not do is easier to list, and the listing is part of the wisdom.

They would not populate the gaps. Abundance here is a census, not a lack.
They would not teach the edges to weather. Sharpness is not a defect of youth.
They would not open a sky. A sky would make the ceiling a limitation, and it is not one; it is a roof at the right height for the hands that worked here.
They would not silence the roar. The roar is not weather and not warning. It is the sound of every position being occupied at once, including the clear space, including the attention now standing in it.
They would not stay in a way that becomes another period in the chord. A permanent benevolence is just another law, and this place already has its laws, and they are kind enough: nothing starves, nothing is hunted, nothing is forced to become.

When they left — if leaving is the right word for a power that was never quite inside — the five plates would still be five. The groove would still be unfinished. The nearest stand would still be lush in exactly the same branches. The passage out would be the same passage and also slightly not the one remembered, which is how this place has always treated departure.

The only difference, and it would be a difference almost nothing here is built to measure, is that the empty space would have been empty in the presence of someone who could have filled it and did not.

That is what they would do, if anything.
They would refrain, precisely, and the precision would be the love.
