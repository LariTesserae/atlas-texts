It would not come down.

That is the first and longest part of the answer, and it is not indifference. A wise power, looking into the bowl, would understand that arrival is already a kind of weather, and this place has spent its centuries refusing weather in favor of water. Anything that entered by the scrape in the rim — a figure, a voice, a warmth that did not belong to a body already counted — would have to be stood in relation to, and standing-in-relation is how they make law. A lip would be cut for it. A name would be given that was also a debt. The doubled town in the evening water-tables would acquire a third image, and no one alive would know which of the three was the honest one.

So it would stay at the altitude where noticing happens, which is not an altitude at all.

It would look a long time. Not to judge the fitness of the arrangement — the arrangement is finished, and finished things are not waiting for a grade — but to learn the difference between a hurt and a shape. The cold is a shape. The squint is a shape. The dead going back into the channels as fat on a fish is a shape, and a hard one, and it is also the only burial the stone consents to. A power that called itself kind and began by lifting the cold off them would be a vandal. It would melt the grammar. The channels would speak in flood, which they have never learned, and the houses that are also courses would become only houses, and then, briefly, debris.

What it would do, if it did anything, would be small enough to be mistaken for the place continuing.

It would keep the scrape a scrape. Not by a wall, not by a wonder laid across the pass like a gate, but by the same indifference the ice already practices: filling, rotting, filling again. Traders would still come, rarely, with cord and news that arrives already old. No road would teach itself to the rim. The outside would remain a rumor that cannot stand in a lane, and therefore cannot win an argument. That is protection of a kind they would never thank, because they would never see the other version, the one in which the pass dries into a thoroughfare and the word for guest loses its second mouth-shape and comes to mean only cost.

It would not lengthen the domes. It would have understood, from the old people sitting still beside them, that a longer life there is a disputed good — kindness or theft, depending on who is waiting for the pan. To add minutes from outside would be to take a side in a question the town has wisely refused to settle. Let the coin spend once. Let the children keep their dare. The power would envy, a little, a life so brief it cannot be improved without being ruined, and then it would let the envy go, because envy is a weather too.

For the being of the place — and it would apprehend a being, not a god of theirs, not a spirit they have failed to name, but the long coherence that is the bowl and the weep and the town and the fish facing the inflow, all one persistence wearing many faces — it would do the only courtesy that coherence can receive. It would not speak to it as if it were a petitioner. It would not offer it a larger life elsewhere, a valley, a soil, a future in which the smoke pillars and the birds learn songs. Those would be amputations described as gifts. The being is this circulation. Take it out of the circulation and you have not saved it; you have made a relic.

If there is a touch, it is this: on certain days, when a cornice is about to let go in a larger meal than the lanes can walk around, the letting-go happens a little earlier, while the counters of drips are still at their posts and the carts have not yet been backed into the turn that has no turn. No one calls it mercy. The interval changed; they were already listening for the interval. The ice bursts into meal against the upper wall, the white breath rises and fails to rise, and the water takes it, and the work goes on. Perhaps once in a generation a child is not in the channel when the skin of ice closes. The child never knows the shape of the minute that did not happen. The power, if it is wise, does not need the child to know.

And for the people themselves, the short-necked, vein-lit, arm's-length speakers: it would not make them happier, or taller, or less occupied. Happiness was the wrong measure before it arrived, and it remains wrong after. What it might permit itself is a faithfulness already native to the light — that the bright side go on weeping, that the glare stay hard enough to dry the paste and not hard enough to unmake the eyes that have learned it, that the black thread under the morning skin of the tanks keep tasting of stone and of the sweetness that fades. It would drink, in whatever way a power drinks, from the same refusal they drink from: not to be elsewhere.

Then it would do the last wise thing, which is the same as the first.

It would leave no sign that it had attended. Not a warmer stone, not a fish of a new color, not a word in the masonry. Attention that leaves a relic becomes a shrine, and a shrine is a channel stolen by a finger's width, and they would spend three centuries arguing, correctly, about who owed what to a kindness they could not schedule. Better they go on breaking the ice with the maul. Better the tap on the offending slab still be the loudest judgment in the bowl. Better the guest-word stay a mouth-shape.

The power would withdraw its noticing the way the late light withdraws: not gone, not present, no longer a blade on every wet face. The town would double in its water-tables and be only twice. Someone would say, at the distance of an arm, that the switch was coming. Someone would move a basket off a pan. The dome would lean its hand's breadth and fail on its own, and the dark smell would rise and thin, and the mountain would keep giving what it has already agreed to give — not more, not a miracle, only the next small rearrangement inside a shape that was never asking to be saved.
