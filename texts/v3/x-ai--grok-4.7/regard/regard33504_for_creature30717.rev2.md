They would not arrive.

Arrival is a footstep, and the floor is still learning what a footstep is. A power that understood the pour would know that. It would not split the haze at the end of the vale, or stand a new lamp where a lamp had failed, or send a warmer breath down the ventilation rivers so that the moths might last. Warmth, given all at once, would be a second weather. The duct-clingers were born to one degree of abandonment. A mercy that rewrote the degree would unmake the posture of their backs, which is the posture of the duct, which is the only scripture they have.

So the attention comes the way the drip comes off the western rebar: on a line already implied by the making, regular enough to be mistaken for design, small enough that the turbines do not have to share their sentence with it.

What it does, if the word still fits, is lean.

Not on the gantry. On the slack.

There is a moment in every cycle, after the sump has drawn down and before the pump’s next kick, when the false marsh goes still and the pale thread is sorting itself along the darker seam, and the film in the joint is neither advancing nor forgetting. In that moment the water is holding a shape it does not have to hold. The power enters there—not as a body, not as a light the tenders would have to go around. As a slight lengthening of the still. Half a breath. Then half a breath more. No one counting drips would swear to it. The conveyors do not lose their call and response. The valve that is about to hunt still hunts. But the thread, which cannot be hurried and will not be seen in full, is given a count of thirty-one paces instead of thirty before it frays, and in the extra pace it learns the corner of a grate it will need when the next seal weeps a finger wider than this one.

That is the whole gift, the first time. A pace.

The power is wise enough to be afraid of more. Benevolence, in a first season, is mostly the refusal to finish what the season has not finished. The rectangles of grit are empty because emptiness is still their material. To seed them would be to import a canopy the ceiling cannot hold and a litter of leaves the pumps would treat as a failure. The tenders already know this; they wipe their boots. A wise power does not correct them with soil. It does not open a seam in the polymer so the mountain can speak. The mountain is a rumor on purpose. The natives of the pour are becoming native by forgetting stone. To restore their memory of stone would be an act of love addressed to a valley that is no longer the one living here.

So it does not restore. It watches the seals the way the hooded light watches them, except it does not have to set a lamp down, and looking, for it, does not stop the bubble-chain. That is the difference it is allowed to keep: it can attend the sump-thought without the thought subsiding. It does not call the thought up. It does not give it a name or a depth it did not already have. It only declines, once, when a pump is a fraction from lagging into a flood, to let the lag become the minute that drowns the rectangles. The pump catches. Everyone who knows the vale’s weather will say the pump caught. The surface of the civic square tightens, thinks its broad slow thought, and lets the thought go, as it has always done. Only the thought goes a little less afraid. Not brave. Less convinced that rising would be the end of being permitted.

The tenders are harder.

They are the only ones who came from a coat-wetting weather, and a power that loves them will be tempted to speak in the quiet between the turbines’ exhalations, when two of them share a gantry and pass the cloth and do not waste air on words. It does not. Speech would be a door into weather, and they have learned not to open those. What it can do without becoming an event is smaller than speech and more useful. When a palm wipes along a pipe to find the shiver, the shiver declares itself a hand’s width sooner, so the jaw does not have to listen quite so long in the cold. When the cloth is nearly finished with release-oil and iron, it is not magically renewed; that would be a miracle, and miracles round corners. It simply does not tear on the grate it was going to tear on, and the tender, who does not believe in luck, folds it anyway and goes on toward the valve that has not yet begun to hunt, but will.

They will not feel accompanied. That is correct. Accompaniment, if they sensed it, would make them walk as guests, and the floor needs them as natives. The power is willing to be the thing in the vale that does not occur—to the eye, to the teeth, to the habit of stepping over bright lines. It occurs only in the margin the design forgot, which is where everything else that lives here already decided to occur.

Once, it almost does too much.

A lamp fails in the traveled way, and the darkness it leaves is the exact size of its reach, empty in the machine way, the way nothing living will enter. The tenders will go around. The thread will not cross. In that hole the power feels the old temptation of benevolent things, which is to fill. It could be a bulb. It could be a continuation of reflection. It could be a darkness that has condensation in it, so the wet dark and the made dark would no longer be two countries. It does not. The distinction is one of the few sharp edges the living have learned to read. To blur it would be to steal a north. The lamp will be replaced by the same hands that wipe boots, on the same schedule that replaces lamps, and the power lets the hole stand its watch, empty, trusted by no one, which is a kind of honesty a poured place is still young enough to need.

What it does instead is smaller and, if anyone were counting, stranger. In the joint where the gray-green has mapped the leak, it lets one drop fall off-rhythm. Not enough to suggest a god. Enough that the stain, which has never been given permission to be taller than a hand, mistakes the drop for ordinary weather and rises the width of a fingernail into the air above the grate, and holds, and is not punished by the fans. A height. The first voluntary height in the vale that was not a duct, a gantry, or a tower. The film does not know what to do with it. It holds for a cycle. Then the pump kicks, the pitch returns to the teeth, and it subsides to its permitted finger, having learned only that the air immediately above it is not an enemy.

That is as far as wisdom reaches on this night: a pace, a shiver found sooner, a thought less afraid, a fingernail of height, a lamp left honestly dead.

Toward the end of the long slack, when the western drips can be counted, the tender on the high gantry stops and lets the distances open—three sumps, the yard of pipes, the towers like mills, the vale closing in its own haze. The power is in that opening the way cold is in the rail: not visible, not separate, available to the wrist if the wrist were asking. It does not add a fourth sump or take one away. It does not bless the count of livestock that cannot be seen. It only stays through the count, so that the counting is not done in an empty attention. The moth still dies on the housing. The clinger still shifts and is still. The sump still thinks and lets go. None of them are saved. Saving would be a flood.

When the pump catches and the river withdraws and the formwork teeth shine with their first sharpness, the power withdraws with the water, not into a heaven above the duct-sky, but into the next slack, which is already forming at the far end of the vale, in a channel the tender has not reached tonight. It will be there when the thread needs the thirty-first pace. It will not be there in any way that could be pointed at with a chin.

If it has a desire, it is the desire of water that has been given a job and is willing to do it: that the place continue long enough to learn one rounding that is its own, not inflicted—one edge softened by nothing but years of the same fingernail of flow. It will not hurry the year. It will not announce itself when the edge finally gives. The tenders, if they are still the same tenders, will wipe the rail, pass the cloth, and say the concrete is beginning to remember weather. They will be right. They will not need to know who kept the pumps from becoming so faithful that memory had nowhere to stand.

And if, deep in the civic square, the broad thought ever does choose to rise—not because it was called, but because the surface one night is steady enough to bear being seen—the power will do the hardest thing benevolence knows. It will look away first. So the thought can finish rising into a world that did not flinch, and find, instead of a watcher, only the vale doing what it has always done: draw down, slack, the fine rain, the call and the answer, the sharp edges holding, a little longer, their unrounded light.
