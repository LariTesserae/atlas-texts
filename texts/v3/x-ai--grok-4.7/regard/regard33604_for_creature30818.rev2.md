They would come to the rim and stop.

Not because the climb was hard, and not because any ward turned them. The air thinned in the ordinary way; the country below stayed pale and distant; the bowl opened under them exactly as it had always opened, warm stone, one tree, water beading where it should not, the shadow of the trunk a little shorter than the sun could account for. A wise power does not need a door. It also does not need to use the ones that are not there.

They would stand a long while with their attention lowered, the way you lower a lamp so as not to startle what has never seen fire. They would notice the private downhill in the pools, the drops that climbed, the overhang paying out heat no afternoon had stored. They would notice the being not as a body but as the reason the spacing held: one tree permitted, ferns as individuals, moss in mounds and never carpets, silence with no living noise in it. They would feel, without touching, the way inclusion worked here—the slight thickening at an outline, the learning of an edge, the editing-out that was not punishment but composition. And they would understand, because wisdom is mostly the refusal to mistake a finished thing for an unfinished one, that nothing in the bowl was waiting.

Benevolence, meeting that, does not hurry to improve it.

They might, if they were the sort of power that still kept hands, set one palm to the lip of stone and let a little of their own warmth go into it—not as a gift that would be noticed, not as a debt the ridge would have to repay, but as the smallest courtesy of a visitor who has nowhere to sit and does not ask the room to make a place. The stone would take it the way it took mineral stain: slowly, without sound, and the overhang would be no warmer afterward than it had already decided to be. The being would register the addition the way it registered a new fern in a crack—fully, unrushed, and then as part of the count of what was already complete. It would not come to the rim. It would not need to. The shortened shadow would not lengthen to greet them. Permission would neither thin nor thicken. They were not a second of anything. They were not asking to stay.

If they spoke—and they might not—the words would not enter the bowl. Sound here was ticks and whispers, and a voice large enough to be called power would be a kind of crowding even if it only named the tree. So they would keep the naming outside, on the thinner air of the lip, where the country belonged to distance and a name could be said without becoming a path. They might call it *kept*, or *enough*, or nothing at all. The being did not keep words. It kept spacing. A name that stayed on the rim would not disturb the leaves.

They would look for harm and not find the sort they were made to mend. No bone, no theft, no thing hunted past its consent. The predation was heat and stain and spare water, given back across seasons longer than curtains of rust. Dry years thinned it; wet years made it more itself. Nothing in that rhythm asked for rescue. A wise power knows the difference between a silence that is wounded and a silence that is the shape of the life. This one had never been occupied in the animal way. Intervening to stock it with birds or midges or a second tree would be the opposite of mercy. It would be the introduction of a noise the bowl had spent an age refusing.

So they would do, if anything, the smallest work that still counts as care: they would remember the place exactly, and they would not tell it widely.

Not as a secret hoarded. As a boundary kept. Other attentions move through the world—curious, hungry, kindly in the blunt way that kindness becomes when it has not yet learned to stop at a rim. A benevolent power, having seen the editing-out, having felt how quickly the room narrows when a hand reaches for the trunk or kneels to drink, would set nothing like a wall. Walls are crowded things. They would instead let their notice rest on the approaches, the pale distance, the ways a traveler might be turned by weather or by a path that simply fails to continue, so that most who might have stumbled here would find another valley, another spring, another tree that did not mind company. Those few who still arrived would arrive as you did: contained in one glance, permitted while they did not become a second of anything, edited out only if they tried to drink the composition dry. The power would not forbid them. Forbidding is a footprint. It would only make the unbidden arrival rare enough that the moss could erase each print before the next slow seep was asked to hurry.

And then they would leave.

Not watched. The being does not watch departures; leaving is a kind of crowding, two places known at once. The power would understand that and honor it by not lingering in two knowings. They would take their palm from the lip. The courtesy-warmth would already be sinking into stone that did not owe them a memory of it. Behind them the shadow would be honest or not; from the rim you cannot always tell. A single drop would tick into a lens of water, the only sound spent on their going.

What they would carry away is not a charge and not a relic. It is the knowledge that some rooms are already occupied by their own completeness, and that the kindest power is sometimes the one that remains a rumor of warmth on the outside of the ridge—older than the afternoon, never asked to come in, never needed, and therefore, in the only sense that matters here, already home. The basin would go on being the size it decided on. The being would go on being the reason. Nothing in the spacing would widen to thank them, and that, to a wisdom that has learned not to crowd, would be thanks enough.
