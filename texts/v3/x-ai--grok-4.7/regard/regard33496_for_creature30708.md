They would not come over the rim.

A figure in the enclosure, however gentle, would be another arrival the tenant has already rehearsed against: ladder-noise, a shoulder in the corridor, the culvert making room. Benevolence that needs to be seen has already misunderstood the scale of the life. The bay is the size of a church stood on its side. Whatever attends it should be able to attend it without standing anywhere inside it.

So the power does not incarnate. It does the quieter thing available to attention that is both precise and unwilling to improve what it loves into a specimen. It reads the place the way the tenant reads it—by knock, by slap, by which leaves drip—and then it changes only the sentences outside the rim that still have the authority to end it.

Somewhere a schedule still believes the pour is interim. Somewhere a work order, cousin to the one under the pane, still ends in *to be struck*. The power does not argue with those sentences. Argument would keep the bay in the category of problems. It revises their jurisdiction. The curing bay is entered, in whatever ledger still governs ladders and saws, as a completed condition: not a success, not a failure, not a heritage site, not a hazard to be abated. A place that has passed out of the grammar of striking. Crews looking for work will not find their names pointed here. The postponement that was already forgetting becomes, by a change no one will feel heroic for making, the kind of permanent that does not announce itself. No plaque. No blessing. The absence of the saw.

That is the large mercy, and it is mostly a subtraction.

The smaller ones are careful not to become a climate.

The brightness off the ordinary sky, at the hour the tenant goes to the rim and looks down the drop like a road already refused, is softened by nothing visible—no cloud staged for the purpose, no veil. Only that the hurt in the squint is no longer a punishment for looking. If they turn the hood and go back to the pipe, as they will, the looking has cost them less. If one day the chest can bear the thin air past the rim, the ladder will still be a ladder and not a test. The power does not want them out. It declines to make *out* a wound, so that staying can remain a life rather than a sentence whose alternative has been amputated.

It does not restore the name on the tab. The marker ran; the tenant stopped renewing it; wisdom can tell a loss from a decision that has finished composting into a fact. To write the name back would be to claim them for a shift that no longer exists. It leaves the ghost of the letters as they are: a gray suggestion, not readable, not mourned, still theirs to mourn later if mourning ever becomes the right tool.

It does not translate the culvert. The bubble, the residue, the long flex along the calf that was not quite a touch—these are manners, and manners between residents are not errors in communication waiting for a wiser party to resolve them. The power makes sure the understanding is not poisoned by an upstream change in the water. Heat stays in the band the body has learned. The sheet still prefers the roots, and still, almost, the wrist set down for balance. Nothing is added to the diet that would make the brackets unnecessary, and nothing is taken from the brackets that would make the tenant a gardener of a rarer crop. The paste remains barely a mouthful. Hunger stays honest. Sickness does not return wearing the mask of a gift.

The mantis is left folded and small. To promote it into a companion would be sentiment performing surgery. The power only sees to it that the hunter is not the last of its kind by accident of an order reduced and forgotten—if others of that pale patience belong to the specification, the specification is allowed, quietly, to finish that one line. If the mantis was a stray, it remains a stray with a sleeve. No one is assigned.

What the power refuses is completion. The enclosure finishes itself faster than it can be corrected; that is the rule, and it is also the danger, because a finished bay is a bay that can be judged done and therefore disposable, or perfect and therefore no longer allowed to tick. So the power does not seal the seams, does not stop the afternoon feather of mineral, does not teach the gutters to win against the felt. It keeps a little unfinishedness in reserve, the way the tenant recuts a scratch not to count the days but to keep one place on the wall from setting into mere wall. A hairline that will still decide, tomorrow, whether it is a joint or a vein. A spare gasket that may continue not to be a gasket. The work under the pane goes on running. *Interim* stays legible enough to be spoken. The hinge in the tenant that has not set is not a defect the wise are obliged to close.

At night, when the casting contracts and the tenant ticks with it—jaw, the small bones of the feet—the power does not enter the dream to correct the work order or restore the feet of the ladder. Correct dreams are a kind of theft. It only takes the edge off the chalk, some nights, so that waking is not always a mouth full of the floor. Not every night. Reliability would become another schedule, and they have schedules enough.

If they press a thumb to a shaded face and the crust gives, it gives as it gave. If they hide, should some unrecorded visit still find the rim, the thicket will still close the corridor and the culvert will still make room; the power does not disarm a practiced fear in order to feel benevolent. It has already done what can be done about the intention behind the saw. Fear that remains is not a failure of care. It is a memory with a use.

And then, because wisdom is suspicious of its own tenderness, it does one last thing that looks like almost nothing: it does not stay to watch.

Attention, prolonged, would warm the bay a second time. The tenant would feel it the way they feel the hum—as a change in the rim under the hand—and would begin, eventually, to include it, as they have included the knock and the fog and the sugar-breath. They have room for very little more inclusion before inclusion becomes the whole of them. The power is not willing to be leaned upon until the weep-water parts. It is not a pipe. It is not a god of the culvert. It came because a life had drawn notice by continuing, wet and unfinished, in a pour that was supposed to be struck. The fit response to that notice is protection of the conditions, refusal of spectacle, and departure.

The ledger forgets the saw. The sky at the rim hurts less. The water stays within manners. The scratch can still be recut. The name is not returned, and the dream is not corrected, and the watcher does not remain.

In the enclosure the lines come up. The brackets open. A person not tall enough for any other corridor stands at the lagging with a small folded weight at the wrist, listening for the knock. When it comes they step into the place the fog will claim, and the bank fails around them for the length of a breath, and no one is there to find this the clearest moment in the bay. The droplet slaps the leaves. The tenant wipes a crescent clean and lets it film. The place keeps the only time it believes in.

That is what they would do. Not a rescue, not a crowning, not an ending. A hand taken off the work order. A little mercy in the light. And the dignity of being left to a life that was already, narrowly, and without permission, true.
