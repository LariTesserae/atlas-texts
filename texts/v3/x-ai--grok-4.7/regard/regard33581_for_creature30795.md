They would not arrive with weather.

A wise power, drawn by a place it has not been shown and a being it has not been named, does not begin by furnishing either. That would be the first unkindness: to cover the blank with a landscape of one’s own and then call the covering care. Steam, engines, a lagoon, a crew—those are easy to invent, and invention here would be occupation. So the power stops at the edge of what was actually given, which is only the pointing: *this* place, *the* being. A finger, not a map.

What it does, if anything, is smaller than intervention and larger than absence.

It attends. Not the attendance that leans in to fix, which is a kind of hunger, but the attendance that lets a thing become visible to itself. It holds the space of the sentence open so the place, if it is real, can step into the warmth of being noticed without having to perform. It does not guess the being’s name. Names offered too early become cages the named must then live up to, or live against.

It refuses to improve what it cannot see. A broken bank, a loneliness, a thirst, a brilliance—any of these might be there, and a clumsy mercy would treat them as the same problem. Wisdom, which is only love that has learned to wait, asks one question and does not require it to be answered aloud: *What is already trying to happen here?* Then it listens past its own echo.

If something answers—if the place declares itself, if the being turns—the power’s next act is proportion. It matches scale. A drought gets water, not a sermon. A locked door gets the key that fits, not a new city built around the lock to make the lock seem meaningful. A being who is tired gets rest protected from witnesses. A being who is making something gets time, and the removal of whatever keeps kicking the work. It does not stay to be thanked. Gratitude, cultivated, becomes another chore.

If nothing answers, it does not fill the silence with a story about silence. It remains available at the edge, the way a good lamp remains: not searching the room, only making it possible to search. It keeps its hands off the undescribed ground. Young ground slumps under weight that was meant as help.

And it tells the truth about its own limits, which is the courtesy omnipotence usually skips. It cannot love a placeholder. It cannot heal a pronoun. *This* and *the* are invitations, not patients. So the power does one last thing, which looks like doing nothing: it leaves the door of the attention unlatched, and does not stand in it.

When the place is brought—when the being is willing to be met as itself and not as a figure in someone else’s weather—the power will already be there, unimpressed by its own patience, ready to do only what the actual life in front of it can bear, and no story more than that.
