They come the way weather comes when it has decided not to be weather: without a threshold-crossing you can mark, without displacing the fliers, without giving the floor a vibration it must file.

The wise thing, having looked, does not finish the room.

That is the first mercy, and it costs more than it appears to cost, because power of that kind is always being asked to complete what it finds half-made. The ribs are soft. The seams have not committed to crystal. A hand that knew how mountains are supposed to end could close the bowl in an afternoon—drain it, cool it, teach the water to cut instead of weep, give the slate-birds a finished wall and, with it, a hunger no impact could answer. Benevolence that cannot refuse its own competence is only a quicker erosion. So the power does not set the stone. It does not name the color in the hoop. It does not drink from the dark dish to see what the animals refuse, which would be curiosity wearing a kinder mask.

It stands where a person would be blistered, and is not a weight the seam was untaught to spare. The flex continues under it. The nine-breath chime arrives and is not answered, not repeated back, not improved. To answer would be to enter the filing. The grind stills for voices; the power withholds the voice. In the amber interval, when the consultation draws close and the filaments pulse harder and the fliers pack the crown, it does not brighten itself to be seen. It lets the false color at the hoop's edge remain the only face in the room. Two listeners in one pause would be a crowd. The maker left the sentence mid-word in order to return to a listener who had not yet noticed. The power, noticing, pretends for a while not to have.

What it does instead is small, and mostly at the rim.

The finished mountain leans on the bowl with ordinary cold. That cold is not malicious. It is simply what completed stone does to a room that has never learned to be old. Left alone, the leak is slow—a direction in the wind, a thinning at the gap that is still a gap, a night that might someday teach the film to freeze and the ocher mats to die of a weather they were not extruded to survive. The power puts nothing across the door. It only thickens the not-yet at the threshold, the way a palm cupped near a candle thickens the stillness around the flame without touching the wick. Outside, the wind keeps its direction. Inside, the air remains the air of making: gray-amber, sourceless, warm from below. Travelers who do not know the tolerances may still wander in. The cold may not follow them like a creditor.

That is one thing.

The other is for the pause itself, which has already lasted longer than the stone's face admits. The animals inherit the hoop. They inherit the skip of the dish-wall. Inheritance is a clock, and it says the hand between strokes has been elsewhere long enough for a breeding to learn a gate. The power does not go fetch the maker. Retrieval would be a kind of finishing—yanking the pen back to the page because a reader grew impatient with white space. It does not know the maker's errand. A mind that lifts rooms into weather may be at another clause, in another gulf, and the absence may be the work.

But a pause can fail without being abandoned. Seams want to close. Pins hold them in a patience that is not infinite; touch one and the making, resuming, comes back thicker, angrier, as if the held breath had a limit. So the power does what a second hand does when the first is still raised: it keeps the breath from becoming a gasp. Where a pin is tiring, it does not replace it. It lends duration. The seam stays open at the same width, no wider, the film no sweeter, the clay-flats' circuits unbroken. Where the broken arc of filaments ends in a fan—mid-word, not failed—it does not complete the arc. Completion would speak in the maker's handwriting. It only keeps the glassy window from sealing, so that when the temperature-gears need a nerve to return to, the nerve is still a nerve, and the reed-rods still have a half-step to shift.

The animals are not addressed. They would only file a new stillness and click it into the news. The fliers drink what they drank. The slate-birds strike the walls they already strike and continue to spare the one that holds the dishes. No one is fed by miracle. Miracle would be a rival production, and the crowd is already calibrated to the room's own rate of becoming. To add food would be to add a god, and then a dependence, and then a second pause with worse consequences when the god withdrew. Wisdom, here, is the refusal to become infrastructure.

In the next amber, the power does one thing that can be called a gesture, and even that is nearly not a thing.

When the light thickens and the hoop brightens and the dark dish turns faster, it places attention—not a sound, not a touch—against the underfloor grind, at the exact period the grind already keeps. Matched, not corrective. A listener joining a held note without changing its pitch. For the length of one interval the maker's consultation, arriving, finds the room not only unfinished but accompanied: the count already attended, the filing not empty, the white space noticed by something that will not write in it. Then the power lets the match go, so the maker cannot mistake the company for a new fixture to be built around. Company that stays becomes furniture. The animals have enough furniture.

If the maker is the sort of being who can feel the difference between an empty pause and a witnessed one, the difference will be there, once, like a margin mark in a hand not their own. If the maker is not that sort, then nothing was spent that the room could miss. Both outcomes are acceptable. Benevolence that requires thanks has already misunderstood the hoop, which drinks light and gives back an edge no visitor brought with them, and does not thank the air for passing through.

Afterward the power does not remain in the bowl. Remaining would eventually weigh, and weight is how seams learn the wrong lessons. It leaves the way it came, which is to say the loudness never cuts off because it was never inside the bell in the way a body is inside. At the rim, the cupped stillness stays: not a wall, not a watchman, only a kindness in the temperature, a little more time for a sentence to be resumed by the one who began it.

The chime sounds. Nine breaths, then the tenth. The clay-flats confirm their edges. A new rib starts under the skin of the floor, warm, waxy, not yet committed, and the slate-birds drop from the windward ledge to go and find the crust it will grow.

No one is saved, because no one was asking to be saved. The room continues. That is what the wise power does, when the life of a place is the fact of its not being done: it guards the unfinishedness, and does not sign the work.
