They would come the way weather comes: without announcement, without a border the highland could refuse. Not a body among the chimneys, not a shadow the herds would learn to pause for. Attention, only—vast, unhurried, already older than the crust’s patience.

For a long while that would be all. The power would listen. Not to interpret the chorus, but to be inside the interference pattern the shafts make when the wind sorts itself between them. It would feel the beats in whatever stands for teeth. It would wait through the troughs and hear, in them, the small stick of the flat-lappers, the wet catch of a still one breathing three plates away, the industrious pulling under a fall. It would taste the night film as it vanished and the private sweetness the deep flanges keep. It would not count the singers. Counting would be a smaller kindness than letting the number remain weather.

It would notice the equilibrium and recognize it as a decision already made, repeatedly, by everything present. The plant no longer builds toward a shape; it maintains. The herds plane only the annual lip. The riders spend thermals they do not own. The people rent their sentences from the quiet and leave most of the country unmarked. Even the deep appetite, whatever it is, returns what it takes as warmth. Nothing here is asking to be improved. Improvement would be a rumor from somewhere lower, where things still believe they are unfinished.

So the power would do, at first, what wisdom often looks like from the outside: nothing that changes the grain of the plates.

Yet benevolence is not only restraint. It would see the narrowness of the people’s margin—the blank plates that mean *do not*, the galleries that muffle just enough, the child who must learn to wait for silence before learning anything else. It would see how a single wrong sleep, a basin gone warm, a note that continues after its body has gone, can thin a life already thin. It would not lift those risks. The risks are how the highland teaches its own scale. To sand them away would turn the people into guests in a garden, and they are not guests.

What it might do is smaller than rescue and harder to notice than a miracle.

It might lengthen certain troughs by a breath when a child is learning the difference between the hiss and the long musical note—not always, not on a schedule the singers could come to expect, only often enough that one more voice survives to carry the technical talk forward. It might steady a flange that has begun to think about falling while someone is still in its shadow, then let the fall happen an hour later, loudly, so the warning remains true. It might keep one basin a degree cooler for one dry season, not by decree, but by a slight rearrangement of which chimney drinks the night film fastest, so the deep appetite stays a year farther off and the route between two shelters does not have to be abandoned. The herds would not know. The riders would side-slip as they always have. The chorus would redistribute any missing pitch before the people had a word for the difference.

It might, once, answer the shaft that still throws a note belonging to no living column. Not with a body rising into the ordinary sun—that story is already complete, and completing it again would be vanity—but with a harmonic so far under the others that only the fiber itself would carry it, a warmth in the dead pavement where the split used to be. The people who avoid that route would feel nothing. A singer drifting past on evacuation-smoke might nest a single season in the old paper and find the room less hollow. Then the harmonic would stop, because a sound that continues forever after its reason has gone is not a gift; it is another obligation.

And then the power would withdraw its hands, if it has hands, and leave the attention in place like a climate that does not announce itself. The highland would go on running its low fever. The chimneys would keep their nations. The still ones would hold. The plates would warm the feet of whoever crossed them. Somewhere a storm would decide itself between two pylons and rain on one side only.

If the people ever sensed they had been spared a narrower season, they would have no sentence long enough, in the troughs they are given, to say so. They would mark nothing new. That, too, would be correct. A wise power does not require witness. A benevolent one does not ask the country to become grateful for remaining what it already, patiently, is.

The note would not finish. It never needed help to continue. It only, sometimes, needed not to be corrected.
