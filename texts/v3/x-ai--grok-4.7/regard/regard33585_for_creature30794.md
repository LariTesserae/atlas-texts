It does not come as weather. The lid is already weather, ordinary day, and the place has a rule about that.

It comes as attention that does not take up volume. The bowl is full. There is no air to stand in, and the gaps between bodies are already a kind of speech: turn sideways or do not pass. A wise power does not shoulder in and call the displacement a visit.

It listens until the roar becomes a map, which does not take it long, and then it stays anyway, through one full metal-weather of the seep—the long cycle the animals themselves answer only by gradients too slow to notice from inside an hour. It learns the pitch of the throat, the file of the edge-keepers, the piston-thump of the bells, the way a valve seated a fraction deeper drops the tone into the jaw. It does not quiet any of it. Loudness here is climate. Mercy that subtracted the sound would be a kind of winter.

It looks for the being, and does not find a single one, and does not correct the lack.

There are the small armored ones tiling the glass, drinking charge through pads of metal-salt. There are the bells, mouths you would not trust, weather that is their own current. There are the sheets of spicule and nerve, editing the day into bands that do not flicker. There are the dozen great valves in the notches, enduring more than swimming. And there is the process that wears all of them: a young factory with no author, exporting dissolved mountain into a dark pipe, keeping its tools sharp because dull crystal pays less. If that process is a being, it is a being with no face to bless and no center to improve. The power does not install a face. Eyes here are gauges. To add a gaze would be to invent a loneliness the bowl has not asked for and could not use.

It considers the gifts that resemble kindness and refuses them, one by one.

It does not open the lid. Air is above, thin and high and useless, and the animals treat the surface as a condition. A shore would be a wound.

It does not bring green. There is no crop to start. The bloom on the charged faces is already the crop, and it is not a plant, and it is enough. A forest in this water would be a colonization with better manners.

It does not widen the bowl, though the bodies are near enough to touch and the volume can only just bear them. Enclosure is the cathedral. Hamlets do not become kinder by being flooded into countries.

It does not blunt the edges. The youth of the rock is maintained by appetite; left alone, the water would begin the long kindness of rounding, and the animals will not leave it alone, because rounded crystal gives worse purchase and less charge. To sand the bowl into age would be to starve the keeping of it. The power loves the sharpness and does not call the love an improvement.

It does not take the export. The throat is an organ pipe with no silence between notes, and the freight in it is the product of a life that does not know the word product. A god who drank from that pipe would only be a larger throat.

It does not teach them worship, or names, or a slower pace. They already have intervals you could set a clock by. They already adjust. Nothing here is waiting to be told it is remarkable.

Then it looks for the harm that is not a style.

There is one. The bowl is recent. The mountain has only just decided to split and has not yet learned regret. The seep and the throat are single paths. The population is local increase, young parents already crowded, with no season of leaves and no older water to remember a crash for them. If the stone above shifts once more before they have lived a few long weathers, the seep pinches or the slot plugs, and the factory ends in the middle of a procedure it has not finished becoming good at. Not because it was cruel. Because it was early.

The power can hold a mountain. It does not hold this one still forever. A terrarium is a smaller bowl with a lid of intention, and they already have a lid.

What it does is smaller than a miracle and larger than a wish. It steadies the stone above the seep, and the stone around the throat, for a span equal to a few of those long weathers—not an age, not a promise, a borrowing of duration from a geology that can afford it. Long enough for the gradients they already know how to ride to include a near-failure and a recovery: a thinning of the metal, a partial seating of a valve, a sheet of spicules drifting one body-width and back. Long enough for the keeping of youth to learn a little slack, which is the only invention they need and the only one they are likely to make themselves if they are not ended first.

It does not announce the holding. No new pitch enters the roar. The light stays day. The water stays cool in the mouth. The great valves endure, the bells drive their measured volumes, the edge-keepers rasp the dulled microns and leave the crystal young. If any gauge-eye records a change, it is only that the seep ran true for a while, which the seep sometimes does, and which they already answer by continuing.

When the borrowed stillness ends, the power lets the mountain go back to being a mountain. It does not stay to watch the next shift. Staying would become a second roof. It leaves them the one thing it came with and did not spend: the knowledge, held outside them, that they were not a draft. If the throat closes later and the bowl becomes a vein of mineral meal and quiet glass, the pattern will have been complete at least once—not saved in a copy they would hate, not repeated elsewhere as a show. Complete, and let go.

A wise power can love a ringing, crowded, unmade thing without needing it to soften, or to pray, or to last. It does the least that answers the only unfinished mercy in the room. Then it does nothing, which is the rest of the kindness, and the bowl does not feel the absence, because it never felt the guest.

The file-sound goes on. The day slides up and comes apart. Somewhere in the slot, water that has been worked and charged and measured leaves without hurry, and the place, packed and sharp and young, goes on intending to continue.
