They would not enter.

A wise power recognizes a room that is already full, and this one is full in the way a working heart is full: not of vacancy waiting to be comforted, but of a process that has found its measure. The lid is a ceiling, the throat a pipe, the rasp a livelihood, the roar a map. To bless it in the manner of weather—light poured in, silence granted, space opened like a gift—would be to unmake the instruments by which these lives know where they are. Mercy that changes the climate of a place is only another kind of weather, and they have just learned this weather.

So the power stays at the scale where attention is not yet a current. It learns the bowl the way the bowl learns itself: by gradients. Pitch for distance to the throat. Rasp for the richness of a face. The interval of a bell for how hard that animal is working the water it also breathes. It does not tidy the new edges. Sharpness here is bread. It does not dim the organ-pipe in the floor. Loudness here is not an injury; it is the light by which navigation happens when eyes are only gauges. It does not sort the crowding into a fairer geometry. The gaps are narrow because the volume is the volume, and the young have increased into it on purpose, recently, as geology counts, and are not asking to be fewer.

What it does notice, and does not rush to correct, is the particular fatigue of a mechanism that has no shift-change. The great valves do not suffer in any voice that would carry. They seat and unseat. The small armored ones do not pray; they file. The bells drink the charge they themselves keep fresh. If there is pain, it is the ordinary tax of being a body that must stay in time with a process larger than its hour. A benevolent power that has lived long enough to be wise does not abolish that tax on sight. It has seen what happens to worlds when the tax is lifted and the work continues unpaid: the edges dull, the charge fails, the young starve on kindness.

Still, wisdom is not only refusal to interfere. There is one thing it can give that the bowl will not have to metabolize as food or as threat.

It attends long enough to be accurate.

It holds, outside the lid, a memory of the place as it is on this day: the glass still cutting, the seep still bright with unoxidized metal, the dozen door-sized valves in their notches, the sheets of spicule editing ordinary daylight into bands that do not flicker, the dark freight leaving by the slot and not returning. It keeps that memory the way a mountain keeps a spring—not displayed, not improved, not offered back as a mirror the animals would have to answer. Some powers announce themselves so that the announced may feel chosen. This one has learned that chosenness is a pressure, and the bowl has pressure enough.

If it acts at all, the act is smaller than a ripple and slower than the slowest adjustment already native here. Somewhere a grip that has begun to fail—not from age, from a fracture of the crystal under the pad, a local unluck—finds the purchase one grain truer than it would have found. A bell that has drifted half out of its layer, and would have spent a long dim interval burning charge to recover station, meets a thread of current already turning the right way, as if the water had remembered its work a moment early. No one is saved from the enclosure that is their way of eating. No one is given a shore. The surface remains a condition. The air above remains useless and is not decorated into a promise.

The animals do not acquire a god. They acquire, at most, a day in which the inevitable costs slightly less than it might have, and they have no instrument for that difference. Their clocks are rasp and piston and the metal taste of the seep. None of these register a witness.

The power does not mind. Benevolence that needs to be felt has already become a second industry, and this place has one. It remains a while anyway, not to be thanked, but because accuracy takes time and the rock is still learning its own note. When it goes, it goes with the unhurriedness of something that intends to continue, which is the only tempo the bowl would recognize as kin.

The place keeps ringing. The edges stay young. The water stays cool. Nothing in the cathedral of it kneels, because nothing in it was waiting to be visited—only to go on, packed and legible, exporting a thin dissolved mountain into the dark, exact as before, and fractionally less alone in the record of what is, which they will never read, and which was never the point.
