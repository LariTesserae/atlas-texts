They would do nothing that the hollow could feel as an arrival.

A wise power, looking long enough to understand what it was seeing, would recognize the completed sentence. The seam already wets the wall. The pan already holds its line. The shell already fits the silt the way the lichen fits the shell. Nothing in the room is asking. The animal is not in pain, not in want that the place cannot meet, not under a threat that belongs to this hour. Its solitude is not a lack. It is the correct spacing, the same spacing as the grass. To enter that spacing—even kindly, even invisibly—would be to add a second fact to a place that has spent itself down to one.

So the power would not deepen the seam. An extra thread of water would raise the pan past the mineral line, thicken the moss, draw the green out farther than the moisture means to support, and the animal would drink more often and walk the extra body-length, and the pressed path in the silt would become a track, and the hollow would begin, very gently, to be an event. Benevolence that does not know when to stop is only a slower weather. The power would not send rain the plain has not earned. It would not lengthen the wet months. It would not put a second shrub where the first has agreed to stand alone, nor a companion shell in the rubble, nor eggs in the warm grit. Those are gifts only if the receiver is incomplete. This one is not.

It would not heal the glazed pit in the scute. The pit is finished. It does not leak. The animal does not favor the side. To smooth it would be to edit a year that has already been survived, and survival here is not a draft. It would not brighten the eye, or warm the blood past what the stone already lends, or teach the throat a call. A call would need an answer, and an answer would need another body, and another body would need more water than the thumb-wide seam provides. Wisdom counts the cost in the currency the place actually uses.

It would not announce itself. Not as light, not as a change in the interval of the drop, not as a warmth in the grit that the afternoon had not stored. The animal has learned the difference between weather and arrival, and files non-approaching things with weather. A power that wished to be kind would accept that filing. To be noticed, even as a mild correction of the eye, would spend a fraction of the animal’s day on a fact it cannot use. The day is already correctly spent: on the clay, on the three or four pulls at the lip of the pan, on the slow taking-in of heat, on the withdrawal that is not quite withdrawal. There is no surplus attention to tithe.

What, then, if anything?

Perhaps only this, and even this done so far upstream that the hollow never has to know a hand was near. The power would look at the plain outside the slot—the part of the world that still sends weather hard enough, in some years, to matter—and it would see whether anything was coming that the hollow has no practice for. Not the ordinary dry. Not the ordinary cold. Those are already in the arrangement; the shell knows them; the dome becomes a stone and the stone is enough. But a road, if one were bending toward the rise. A herd driven through in a dry year, hungry enough to take the shrub and trample the pan and turn the seam into a hoof-cut that would not seal. A quarrying of the low hill for the soft stone, because someone had decided the ochre was useful. A season of rain so far outside the memory of the walls that the skirt of rubble would slide and close the slot, and the animal would be buried in the only room it has, not quickly, and not as a stone, but as a life that still needed the drop.

If none of that were coming, the power would do literally nothing. It would withdraw its attention back to the size of weather, and leave the drop to fall on its interval, and count that as the whole of mercy: to have looked, to have understood that the place was not a problem, and to have declined to become one.

If something were coming—some large, stupid, outside fact—the power would turn it, and turn it early, and turn it where the turning could not be seen from the floor. A road finds a different line, a mile off, for reasons no surveyor can quite name. A herd is watered somewhere else. A quarry opens in a hill that was already broken. The rain, if it must be extraordinary, falls on the far side of the rise and runs the other way. The animal does not look up. The eye does not correct by a degree. The lichen goes on with its tenancy. The power’s work, if it has any, is to remain among the causes that do not register, so that the hollow can keep its only tense, which is the one in which it has already settled.

And if even that turning were unnecessary—if the years ahead were only the years the walls already know—then the wise thing and the kind thing would be the same thing, and it would look, from inside the slot, exactly like the paper-colored light, the stored heat, the unbroken skin of algae knitting itself shut after the drop. The power would have come. The power would have seen a being at home in all of its country. The power would have loved it in the only way that does not subtract from it: by leaving.

By morning the dome would be where the morning always finds it, or a hand’s span nearer the stain, or turned a quarter so a different coin of lichen takes the light. The pan would meet its line. No one would have been saved, because no one had needed saving. That, too, is a kind of grace, and the rarest, because it requires the one who could have acted to be glad of their empty hands.
