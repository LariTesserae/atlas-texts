They would not come down.

A wise power, having looked long enough to understand what it was looking at, would feel the particular temptation this basin offers and refuse it. The temptation is to relieve. The air is thick; the census is full; the small clerks of the litter spend their whole lives between two known addresses; the large stillness in the oxbow breathes out and changes nothing. Benevolence, meeting that, wants to open a gap. Wisdom knows the gap would be a wound. The place is not waiting. It finished waiting so long ago that waiting is no longer one of its categories.

So the power would remain at the height where attention does not yet become weather. It would not part the canopy. It would not cool the night by a degree, though a degree would be a mercy to every hide in the dark, because the hides are built for the degree they have, and a mercy that rewrites the ribs is only a softer kind of eviction. It would not thin the vines. It would not mark the dishonest water so that a newcomer could drink in safety. There are no newcomers the basin has agreed to receive. The rules are ordinary, and the power would let the ordinary continue, which is harder, for a benevolent thing, than any miracle.

What it would do is smaller than an action and larger than a mood.

It would consent to the fullness. Not approve it, as a judge approves a sentence, and not love it in the way that reaches down to rearrange the beloved into something more comfortable to love. Consent, the way the river has consented to its bends: by ceasing to argue. The power would let its attention rest on the basin the way heat rests on the basin, evenly, without a favorite, until the attention itself became another layer of the dimness, undetectable, load-bearing in no way the inhabitants could be billed for. The keepers of still water would not shift by a leaf-width. The coin of an eye would not brighten. A wise benevolence does not announce itself to a creature that has organized its whole economy around not being announced to.

If it did anything that could be called an act, it would be this: it would remember the persistent one accurately.

Not the polished bark, not the smoothed mud, not the gap maintained by use — those are only the signs, and signs can be misread by anything that arrives later and wants a god or a monster. It would remember the round itself. The short circuit from honest water to the hour when standing is cheaper than walking, and back again. The fact that the round has been completed so many times the basin would notice an absence sooner than a stranger. The power would hold that circuit in mind without adding a meaning to it. It would not decide that the persistence is a guardian, or a sorrow, or a lesson. Those are names a visitor brings because a visitor cannot bear an unlabeled continuance. The power, being wise, can bear it. It would let the thing be the oldest habit of the ground and nothing more glorious, and in that refusal to glorify it there would be a protection no wall could give: the protection of not being turned into a story that other powers might come to finish.

For the rest, it would do what the leaves already do to sound. It would take the possibility of intervention into itself and not return it. Somewhere, if one could measure such things, a kindness would be swallowed before it traveled. A cooler thread of air would be considered and unmade. A seed of a clearing would be imagined and declined. The inhabitants would go on spending as little as the rules allow, which is all they have ever asked of the world, and the world, attended now by something that understands the ask, would go on not answering in any voice they would have to spend energy hearing.

Toward morning, when the pretense of a sun begins again and the green-black dimness thins by a fraction no clock would bother with, the power might do one last thing, and even this would be indistinguishable from the basin's own manners. It would look at the large stillness in the brown water at the moment the stillness breathed out. It would not bless the breath. Blessing is a change. It would simply not look away until the surface had accepted it. Then it would include that acceptance in the consent, and rise, or fade, or remain — it would hardly matter which, because none of the three would stir the flies on the hide, or hurry the clerks, or put a second note into the branch's single complaint.

And the place, which had drawn the attention without intending to, would not know it had been spared. That would be the benevolence. That would be the wisdom. The heat would continue. The works underfoot would not take a shift change. Every interval would keep its tenant. If the persistent one laid its weight again on the same shelf of mud, the mud would receive it as it has always received it, and the only difference, if there were a difference at all, would be that somewhere outside the scale of hide and wing and mandible, a power had decided that this was already enough, and had proven the decision by leaving.
