They would not come in.

That would be the first and largest decision, and it would not feel like restraint so much as recognition. The seam on the windward side is already a wound kept open by use. A power that entered there, however gently, would be another heavy shape: the stem would open a gallery around it, the moths would lift, the runners would abandon the fresh splits, and for as long as the visit lasted the city would be a throat instead of a vein. Benevolence that must be accommodated is only a polite sealing. So the power remains outside the rind, in the cold air of the basin, and does its looking from there.

Looking, for such a power, is not a beam and not a search. It is closer to the way water under the soil already knows the roots. It learns the column as one organism and as a scatter of holdings at once. It learns the cords in the central gallery without touching them, knot by knot: the lost, the drowned splits, the circuits that shifted. It learns that the humans are few and careful and not the point, and that the point is the arrangement — plant, patrol, climber, sleeper, drip — in which none of them is sovereign. It does not love this. Love would want to improve it, to dry a shelf, to hold a passage at head-height, to give the lower third a little warmth so clothes would not stiffen in the night. Wisdom, being wiser than love and kinder than improvement, declines.

What it does notice is the basin.

The landform is young. The rims are still sharp. Water lies too near the skin of the earth, and the rain, when it comes, has nowhere patient to go; it sheets, it loads the roots, it forces the stem to sweat harder than its rooms can bear. Twice already, in the memory of the cords, a split has drowned. A third drowning is not fate. It is only the unfinished edge of a new ground, and a benevolent power is allowed to finish an edge if finishing it does not become a monument.

So it does one thing, and does it where no one inside will ever stand and call it help.

Along the eastern rim, where the torn soil is thinnest and the cold pools hardest, it coaxes a single sag into the lip of the basin — not a channel a person could follow with the eye, not a sign. A thumb’s width of give in the earth, continued, over the length of a night, until excess water has a longer path away from the roots instead of down them. The rain will still come. The stem will still sweat. But the surplus that used to arrive all at once will arrive as the place already knows how to receive it: bead, two finger-widths, drink, drip, the ordinary redistribution. No chamber will be held open by miracle. No shelf will be warmed. The humans will not wake to light, or to a voice, or to the particular terror of being noticed by something that could unmake their dark.

Inside, nothing announces itself. A climber shakes its coat on a shelf and moves on. A moth ticks the rind and corrects. In the lower third a sleeper turns, damp, and does not know that a drowning scheduled in the shape of the land has been unscheduled by a sag in a rim they have never seen. The stem, which does not thank and does not notice thanks, leans its slow finger’s width toward the wetter side and finds the wetter side slightly less urgent. It opens what it was going to open. It seals what traffic will not keep. The fresh splits stay sharp for their hour and then become rooms, or do not.

The power stays a little longer, not to watch for gratitude — there will be none, and that is the evidence it acted correctly — but to be sure the sag does not become a cut. Wisdom is suspicious of its own hands. When the path of the water holds, and the column does not lean toward the new ease as if ease were a command, it withdraws its attention the way a hand lifts from a stem it was only steadying.

It leaves no knot. It adds no name to the cords. If the city persists, it will persist as it already does: by being used, continuously, by more lives than any one dark room can hold, and by never becoming still enough to close. A wise power, having seen that, understands that the greatest kindness available to it is to return the place to unimportance, and to go where it is actually needed — somewhere that is not already, in its cold and crowded quiet, doing the difficult work of staying alive.
