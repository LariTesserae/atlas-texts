They would come the way weather comes, which is to say they would already have been in the decision of the light before anyone thought to look for a cause. A wise power does not arrive. Arrival is a kind of boast, and this place has no room for one. It would notice, and the noticing would be exact, the way the tally-keeper’s hand is exact on the drag of a sack: not love in the loud sense, not a verdict, only the full weight of a thing registered without being improved upon.

What it would see first is that nothing here is unfinished. The wet is not a problem awaiting correction. The narrowness is not a poverty of space. The beaters are not a captivity the people have failed to escape. Competence is already doing the work that mercy is usually asked to do, and it is doing it without an audience. A power that was only kind might mistake this for suffering that had learned manners, and might set about lifting the river, drying the brick, sending the cough upriver before the boat, opening the cane wall so the sky could enter in pieces larger than a handkerchief. A power that is wise as well as kind would feel the hook in that kindness. The wall is why the bend still holds. The wet is why the fiber feeds the machines that feed the store that feeds the quarters. Straighten any of it and the piles sing for a season and then the current takes the cemetery, markers and all, and calls it improvement.

So it would do very little, and the little would be indistinguishable from the place continuing as itself.

It would not heal the knee. The man in the pump room has arranged his days around the knee, and someone else’s hands are not yet ready for the particular cough of the second pump. To take the pain would be to take the listening with it. What it might do, on the night the housing is about to lose prime and the walk down is black with leaf-rot, is let the cough come a half-minute earlier, while he is still on the step and not yet on the ladder. He would say later that he had a feeling. He has said that before. No one would write it down.

It would not stop the deaths. An hour of stopped beaters is already the right size of grief for a place this narrow; a larger mercy would require a larger room. But when a marker sinks in the wet months, it might be a child’s foot, not the river, that finds it first, so the name is lifted before the paint fails all the way to wood. The child would not know she had been used. She was sent for something else and took the long way, which children do.

It would keep, if it kept anything, the outside from arriving with a plan. Not by storm, not by curse on a surveyor’s instrument. By the ordinary stubbornness the bend already practices. A dredge contract delayed past the season when the channel can be read. A buyer upriver who finds, on the morning he meant to come and talk about clearing the wall, that his boatman will not bring a hull in while the boil off the outer pile is the wrong color. The color was always going to be wrong that morning. The power’s whole work is to refrain from making it right. The cane stays. The refusal stays. The fiber keeps leaving in the amounts the beds can spare, and no one in the city the bales eventually reach ever learns there was a moment the works might have been rationalized into something you could see from above.

For the rest, it would practice the same economy the people practice. It would not speak. Speech would turn the weir into a sign, and the boiler-man would have to start watching it for news instead of for a change in pitch, which would ruin both of them. It would not be visible in the ridge glass or the steam. If some of them felt, in the late afternoon, a slight easing in the brick’s heat, as if the day had decided not to spend everything it had, they would credit the cloud they could not quite see over the cane, and they would be right enough. Benevolence that needs to be recognized has already begun to feed on the people it meant to spare.

It might, once, sit the length of a shift in the sound under the planks, not as a listener they could discover, only as a slightly truer version of the water that is already there. The pumps would not falter. The hand-cart would knock as it knocks. A woman at the dye shed would decide the blue had taken, and she would be correct, and nothing supernatural would have touched the vat. The power would learn what they already know and do not say: that a life can be kept by inches, between leaf and pipe and warm brick, and that keeping it is not a smaller thing than saving it.

Then it would leave the way attention leaves when it has been accurate — not upward, not in glory, simply back into the condition of things, the wet, the maintenance, the hour. The beaters would go on. The pumps would go on. Someone would repoint a course of brick and, within a season, forget which course it was. That forgetting would be the proof. A wise power does not need a monument in a place that already remembers itself by continuing. If it did anything that lasted, it would last as the bend lasting: no wider, no drier, no more famous, and still there in the morning, ready to be walked by inches, water sounding under the planks the whole way.
