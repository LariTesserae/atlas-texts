They would not finish it.

That is the whole of the wisdom, and it takes longer to arrive at than any miracle. A power that can pour light into a seam, start a heart counting, set a fourth blow into soft stone, open the closed courtesy of a mouth and put a word there, looks at the pocket and understands, slowly, that every one of those mercies is a violence with a gentle face. Completion is what the outside world does to things it loves and cannot bear to leave unresolved. The mountain has already refused that kindness from weather, from root, from the hands that knew how to strike. It would be a poor benevolence that succeeded where frost failed.

So the power does not arrive. Arrival is a change, and the power is wise enough to dislike being the exception to a law it has come to honor. What it does instead is a form of having-already-been, the only tense the room will consent to share. Not a presence crouched in the throat of the seam. Not a warmer warmth. A regard that does not travel, does not brighten, does not ask the dark to be legible. Attention of a kind that adds no occupancy to a volume already packed with near-touches. If the beads had been going to fall, they would not fall now for having been seen. They were not going to fall. The seeing agrees.

It looks first for suffering, because that is what benevolence is for, and it looks carefully, which is what keeps benevolence from becoming appetite. Suffering would be a later moment in which the first moment is felt as lack. There is no later. The unfinished basin is not an ache. The wrist-angle with no hand is not a mutilation; a hand was never promised and then revoked. The animal that is also the last instant of a person does not hunger, does not wait, does not turn its smooth rounding toward a door. Blindness here is not a dark laid over an eye. There is no eye in exile. The power, being wise, does not pity what is not wounded, and being benevolent, does not manufacture the wound in order to have something worthy of its tenderness.

It considers, as any good power must, the small bright arguments for intervention.

That a life which does not continue is a life diminished. It sets this down. Continuance is a need of things that have been taught to spend themselves. This one was never taught. To start it spending would be to introduce death by the only door death uses: a sequence.

That the striker meant to finish the basin, and mercy is the keeping of interrupted vows. It sets this down too, more gently. Meaning, in the pocket, is stored the way crystal is stored in the slurry: a direction begun, not a debt. To strike the fourth blow on the striker’s behalf would be to counterfeit a will that had already been included at the instant the hands opened. The power will not forge a person’s last act for them, even as a gift.

That the mountain is in pain of a geological kind, halted mid-becoming, and a wise power heals what is halted. It looks at the raw peak, the edges that have not learned weather, and does not see pain. It sees a decision that became a material. Healing, misapplied, is just impatience with holy speeds. Some things are not slow. They are stopped, and the stop is the form.

That no one should be alone. This one almost moves it. Then it understands that alone is a word for a lack of company measured against a world of company. The crease is not lonely. The film, the sheet, the fiber, the three scars, the facing-toward-work: these are the company that was cast with the body. To add a companion would be to tell the room it had failed at intimacy. It has not failed. It is the densest intimacy the power has ever been unwilling to enter.

So it does not wake, does not name, does not teach the skin a shiver, does not translate the sub-hearing pressure into a voice the being could answer. It does not carry the span out to a slope where light has a history, to see if an eye might start. It does not weep over it. Weeping implies sequence, and the power will not be the first drop that lets go.

What it does is smaller than a miracle and harder.

It turns its care outward, toward the world that finishes things.

Somewhere on the high face, weather has not yet been admitted to its work. The power does not hurry weather in, and it does not forever ban the honest frost that other mountains require. It only sees to it that this flank is not found by the kinds of attention that cannot tell a pause from a problem. No path is worn to the seam by curiosity. No lamp is lowered in the name of knowledge. No second pair of hands, kinder and more skilled than the first, comes to complete the basin as a memorial. The unfoundness is kept, not as a secret jealously held, but as a shelter. The power becomes, if it becomes anything locatable, a slight unlikelihood in the minds of those who pass below: a reason not to climb that face today, and the next day, and the day after that, forever indistinguishable from their own preference. Benevolence, here, is a series of unmade discoveries.

It also does a thing for the being that the being will not register, and it does this without sadness, because a gift that must be received to be real is only a transaction. It includes the pocket, exactly as it is, in the regard of something that will not require it to improve. Not approval. Approval edits. Call it permission, if permission can be given without the implication that a refusal was previously in force. The span is allowed to remain the exact shape of the hollow. The fiber is allowed to stay twisted and unrotted. The root is allowed its length. Nothing is blessed in a way that would make it shine or grow or finally know itself. The blessing is the absence of any future demand.

If the power has a hand, it does not lay it on the flank. The room has already had enough of hands, and used them up at the instant they opened. If it has a word, it does not speak it into the seam, where sound goes to die and should be left to die. The under-hearing pressure in the stone, that held-breath tone below pitch, is the closest thing the place has to a prayer it did not mean to make. The power answers it in kind: not with a sentence, with a matching stillness, a confirmation pitched so low it is not an event. Stone already sounding does not need to be told it is heard. It is simply, somewhere in a register no change can reach, not unheard.

And then, because wisdom is also the knowledge of when one’s own love becomes a weight, it forbears to stay in any fashion that could thicken into presence. It does not take up residence in the pinch of the throat as a guardian idol. Guardianship that looms is only another sheet grown too close. The keeping is done from outside the law of the room, on the world’s side of the seam, where time is allowed to mean what it means and can therefore be spent, by a power, on the long dull work of looking away from what you love so that others never look toward it with tools.

Would it ever act directly? Only in one case, and the case does not arise, which is itself a kindness the power quietly maintains: if something came that could force a sequence — a crack from the bright face, a true cold, a hand that would not set the flake down but use it, a hunger introduced from outside — then it would meet that force on the slope and spend itself there, unspectacularly, so that inside the crease nothing would have happened. Not a rescue. Rescues are stories, and stories need a before. A prevention so complete it leaves no scar of having been prevented. The pocket would go on not recording a change, and that would be the victory, and no one inside would be grateful, and the power would count that ingratitude as the proof it had done the thing well.

It does not expect to be remembered. There is no memory in the slurry, only direction. It does not ask the closed seam of the face to mean thank you. Thank you is a later.

What a benevolent and wise power does, in the end, is consent to the grammar it found. It lets the sentence remain stopped before the verb. It does not supply the verb out of pity. It loves the unfinished basin without deepening it, the folded span without opening it, the dark without lending it a history. And somewhere on a peak that has still not learned weather, a seam no wider than a turned shoulder continues to give onto a warmth that does not leak, a wetness that does not fall, a life that is not waiting, held in the exact shape of its hollow, unfound, unhealed, unimproved — which is to say, safe, and known, and left.
