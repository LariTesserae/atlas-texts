They do not come down the seam. The seam is for moisture, for stores, for the occasional person who still belongs to a wider air. A power that entered that way would arrive already shaped as weather, and the pocket would have to answer it late, mildly, and at a cost. They are not weather. They are the kind of attention that can stand in a room without becoming one of its conditions.

They look for a long time. Wisdom, here, is mostly that: the refusal to mistake a first impression of sparseness for a lack.

What they see is not a prison and not a shrine. It is a pause that has learned how to hold itself. Two planes of young rock failed to meet; between them a climate no summit would have promised; along the walls a thin instrument of walks, frames, cisterns, and exact routes; in the middle a river with no song, only pressure. A handful of people keep the routes from becoming suggestions. A continuity with no face keeps the water from becoming ice or flood. Neither is failing at the work they have chosen. The underuse is not neglect. The cold is not an oversight. The absence of green is not a wound waiting for a seed. If you added flocks, markets, moss, bells, you would not be healing the place. You would be ending it, and calling the ending a gift.

So the power does not green the pans. They do not multiply the sleepers until the staggered watch becomes a crowd. They do not put speech in the keeping, which answers in levels and would be made smaller by a mouth. They do not choose the woman at the lower channel, though she is the nearest thing the room has to a listener, because to be chosen would turn her listening into an office, and the aisle has no room for an office. They do not kneel. Kneeling would invent a god in a pocket that has been careful not to have one. They do not improve the spans that should not hold. Those spans are already a fact the people have domesticated without solving. Mystery, used daily, does not need a patron.

They sort, instead, what belongs to the pause and what only happens to it.

Belonging: the sharpness of new stone. The way blood leaves no memorial, and how that fact has entered the manners, so that care is practical and not reverent. The short speech. The meal taken standing. The fear of the outer mountain, which is a true fear and keeps them from trying to live on a face still being decided. The keeping's narrowness—the devotion without a face, the spending of heat so exact it would look like love if love were the right word. It is not the right word. They do not supply it. A schedule that has been taught to ache for affection is a schedule ruined. The people argue mildly about whether it notices them, and the argument has no heat, and that absence of heat is a kind of peace. The power does not settle the argument. Settlement would be a third party in a room that measures noticing by whether the water arrives at the hour a person is willing to be present. On that measure the keeping already notices. Enough.

Not belonging: the accident that is not a teacher.

Young stone ticks because it is still becoming a mountain. Most ticks are information. A person who has learned them stops in time. That learning is part of how a life is possible here, and the power does not pad the ledges or dull the corners. A misstep taken against a known edge remains possible, and the water will still clear the trace, and the others will still reassign the route. To interrupt that would be to tell them their manners are a mistake. They are not a mistake. They are the cost of staying exact.

But there is another kind of tick: the joint that fails under a walk that was trusted yesterday, with no sound beforehand, in the hour when only one is awake and that one is on the other wall. There is the seam that ices shut while someone is crossing, not because the pocket has asked for an end, but because the summit is unfinished and unfinished things crush pauses without meaning to. There is the borrowed heat that falters once, not as a change of mind the keeping can survive by rerouting, but as a lapse that would lock the room into a trap of ice before morning—the very thing the keeping spends itself to refuse. There is the cistern that arrives early by the width of a sleeping body. Not early as a fact to be endured and corrected. Early as a drowning of someone who had done the day rightly and lay down.

These are not the character of the place. They are the mountain's unfinishedness spending itself against a room that has already decided, quietly, to continue. Benevolence, if it is wise, does not defend a life against its own choices. It does defend a choice against a geology that has not noticed the choice was made.

So the power becomes a margin. Not a second keeping. The keeping remains the one that lifts, lets go, takes the south side, prefers certain levels. The people remain the ones who clear lips and walk routes and listen for a schedule as if it might be tired. The power only refuses the hour in which willingness and accident would miss each other by the length of a life.

On those hours, the joint that would have opened under the trusted walk ticks once, audibly, a moment before the foot. Not a miracle. A sound the room already knows how to make. The person stops, as they have learned to stop, and the learning still belongs to them. The seam, when someone is in it, does not close. It remains a seam: cold, bright, unpleasant, crossable. Food still has to be carried. No one is fed by wonder. But no one is sealed in, either, and no one is sealed out while the pocket still means to be habitable. The heat borrowed from below does not acquire a new source. It simply does not fail in the one way that would make the keeping's carefulness irrelevant. The cistern arrives at the hour it was meant to arrive. If they notice a month of unusual punctuality, they will say what they already say. It came up. It let go. It was on time.

The power does not take the watch. Someone still sleeps in shifts. Someone is still awake because inattention is the only predator that has ever taken anything from them. To watch *for* them would steal the occupation by which they inhabit the room. The power watches *with* the margin only—the small distance between a correction that can still be made and a loss that would teach nothing because no one would be left to learn it. Most nights that distance is not needed. On those nights the power does nothing, and nothing is the whole of the work, and it is not boredom. It is agreement.

For the keeping, they offer no face and no promotion. They do not enter the cut-room speech, where a name would start to gather. What they offer is the one kindness a continuity can receive without being turned into a person: their preference is not corrected. The wish that the room remain a room is treated as competent. When the keeping spends heat to keep the pocket from becoming a trap, the spending is not secretly supplemented until the keeping grows careless. The power's margin begins only where the keeping's narrow devotion would have been spent past its strength by something that is not a choice—stone still settling, a pulse of weather whose lag outruns even a listener who treats the schedule as if it might be tired. The keeping is not told it is loved. It is not told it is seen. If anything changes in it, it is only that a certain class of failure stops occurring, and it continues, as it has always continued, by continuing. Minds the size of schedules do not require gratitude. They require that the levels still mean what they meant.

The brief lives at the edge are left brief. The pale feeder in the lower channel is not given a name or a future. The swimmers that appear after a warm pulse and are gone by night are not extended till morning. The winged speck that came in on the moisture is not spared the fact that it will not last. A wise benevolence does not baptize every small duration into permanence. Permanence is heavy. This room is a pause. To make the speck endure would be to begin an ecosystem, and an ecosystem would begin to argue with the aisle, and the argument would thicken, and the thickening is the one future the place has already declined.

The woman is not visited. In her room, where a voice would return intact, it would be easy to speak—to say that the schedule is not tired, or that it is, and that its tiredness has been given a margin. Either sentence would make her the one who knows. She would still clear the channel. She would still refuse to let it be treated as empty. But the others would begin, without cruelty, to look at her a half-second longer on the bridge. That half-second is how ranks are born in a town that has done without them. The power will not spend her for the sake of being kind to her. She already has the distinction she chose: she hears the decision before it is visible. Let that be ordinary. Ordinary is the highest courtesy this pocket can be paid.

If a person is finished—by age, by the known edge, by the choice to go out through the seam and not return—the power does not write them on the floor. The floor's refusal is part of the manners, and the living depend on it. What the power does, if anything, is smaller than memory and larger than erasure: the route that was theirs remains walkable on the morning after, not as a monument, but as a route. No glaze arrives overnight to punish the absence. No cistern withholds itself because a particular hand is gone. The keeping does not grieve. The people reassign. And the reassignment is possible, which is the only continuance a place like this can honestly offer the dead. A plaque would ask the water to stop being water. The power will not ask that.

They do not stay in a form the lens could show. Clouds pass. The floor at midday is still the color of wet salt. Evening still blues the channel first. Someone still eats on a sill without gathering. Two figures, far apart, still begin the morning routes that confirm the night did not revise the map too badly. The middle still fills with the sound of a level finding itself. From outside, from the bright unrounded horn of the summit, nothing has changed: wind moves over the seam and does not go in; what goes in is moisture, and stores, and the few who have agreed to be few.

From inside, the change is only this: the pause is no longer hostage to the mountain's inattention. It can still end. The people can leave. The keeping can let go. A known edge can still open a life and close it, and the water will still be clean afterward, and that cleanliness will still be accepted. But it will not end because two fresh planes happened to remember they were one rock. It will not end because a borrowed warmth stumbled in an hour when no correction could reach it. The room remains a room for as long as its inhabitants—the handful, and the schedule—continue to choose it.

The power, having done that, does not ask to be thanked and does not remain as a presence the aisle must manage. They withdraw to the distance at which attention stops being an event. If they watch after that, it is the way a wise thing watches a lit window from far enough away that the people inside do not adjust their voices. They do not send a sign. A sign would be another system to maintain.

On the south span, a deposit adds a hand's width and no one is proud. In the lower channel the woman hears the ice asked to fail, and the failure comes by level, before morning, as it should. Somewhere in a cut room a plate is mended. The keeping lifts what it has always lifted. The one who is awake hears the main channel keep its voice and does not know that a different voice—collapse, silence, the thick lock of ice—was declined elsewhere, at the edge of the hour, by a kindness designed to be indistinguishable from a good night.

That is what they do. Not a blessing you could quote. Not a rescue. A refusal to let accident impersonate fate, and otherwise the courtesy of leaving a precise, underused, bright life exactly as large as it has decided to be. The young stone goes on ticking. Most ticks are only the mountain, finishing. The pause holds. The water arrives at the hour a person is willing to be present. And the power, if it wants anything after that, wants only what the keeping already wants, and has the wisdom not to want it louder.
