They would not arrive as weather.

A power that is wise does not come down the fissures in a column of corrected light, because the light here already falls the way an afternoon does, and it is not broken. They would not bring water. Water is a foreign mercy in a country that builds itself from dust and warm gas; poured in, it would be a flood of the wrong chemistry, a kindness that drowns the callus before the callus can close. They would not hush the clatter, the grind, the tonal groan of the cables. The animals steer by that. Silence would be a kind of blindness.

They would come the way attention comes when it has decided not to own what it sees: small, and willing to be late.

First they would stand where you stood, on the living rind, and let the pulse teach their weight. They would taste the chalk and the salt and not call it thirst until they were sure it was. They would watch a corridor become a thicket and a shelf slump into the hollow below, and they would not reach to hold either one in the shape it had a moment earlier. Creation and wreck share a clock here. To freeze the clock in the name of safety would be to choose a museum over a world. The wise thing is to learn which losses are the place breathing and which losses are a life failing inside it.

They would look for the being by the marks that do not regrow on the same schedule as the tissue.

The lashed platform. The three remaining rungs. The keen flakes. The waist-high wall with its watching-slot, already half swallowed. Those are not geology and not swarm. Those are a hand that wanted a pause — a floor that would not decide to be a muscle, a height from which to see the next flex coming, a narrow dark to put an eye to. Scattered, unfinished, digested. Whoever lives here, or lived, worked quickly and did not stay, because staying is a negotiation the underside keeps winning.

So the power would search, not as a hunter but as someone following a conversation that was interrupted. They would move when the cables thrummed a release, and brace when the dust shoved, and they would not cut a bundle to make a path. Cutting teaches the tissue to seal and the mouths to come. They would take the passages the growth offered and abandon them when the growth took them back, the way a guest takes a seat that is also a tide.

If they found no one — only the works, and the pant of clustered bodies, and the ring-eyed climbers, and the boat-long swell of a mouth under the rind — they would not invent a petitioner in order to have someone to save. Benevolence that needs a recipient will manufacture one, and then manage them. Wisdom can love a place that is only itself. In that case they would do a smaller thing, and it would still be a doing.

They would keep the attention from becoming a road.

A place this unfinished, this loud, this full of unweathered mineral and racing tissue, draws more than wise powers. It draws appetites: for the gas, for the ceramic leaf, for the light that metabolizes itself, for the spectacle of a valley still being born inside out. The power would set nothing like a fence the tissue could not digest. They would set a fact, the way deep water sets a fact on a hull — not a wall, a difficulty. Those who came to strip or to pave or to quiet it would find their instruments going papery at the edges, their maps failing between one vault and the next, their names for what they saw arriving already wrong. Not a curse. A refusal to be simplified. The swarms would still clatter. The rock would still split. The power would merely have made exploitation as unfinished as the geology, so that taking required the same patience as living here, and most takers do not have it.

And they would leave one thing that does not fight the clock.

Not a temple. Not a law. A knot.

Somewhere the tissue is slower — an older vault, where the rind has thickened and the warmth is milder and the radiant layer is steady enough to read a face by — they would tie fiber to fiber the way the absent hand tied it, but with a tension that flexes when the great bundles flex, so the knot is not a scar the place must eat and not a nail the place must push out. Three turns, and a loop that holds a smooth shard of the pale mineral, edge wrapped so it cannot cut. On the shard, nothing written in a language of elsewhere. Only a wear-mark, the kind a thumb leaves if a thumb returns to it often: a small polished oval in the dust-glitter of the stone.

A being who is still here, or who comes back, would find it because hands find what hands have made, even among a million hooks and cables. They would not be commanded by it. They could hang a new platform from it, or sit with a shoulder against it when the regional groan passes through the sternum, or ignore it until the callus takes it and the place has decided the offer was not needed. If they are hurt in a way the underside does not know how to mean — not the ordinary tear-and-regrow, but a break in the will to keep tying knots at all — the shard would be warm from the gas-light, and the warmth would be specific, a steadiness in the palm, and with it a knowledge that does not arrive as words: that they have been seen, and that seeing has not required them to become quieter, wetter, slower, or finished.

If the being is the place itself — if the stacked hollows are one animal of tissue and noise and deciding-rock, and the platforms were only parasites of hope — then the knot is for the next mind that tries to sleep here, and the power’s true address is different. They would listen to the colliding sound until they could tell the groan of rebalance from the groan of overload. They would learn the interval at which a shelf, burdened by too many armored sheets, must slump. And where the interval was shortening toward a collapse that would not be weather but a seizure — a region of the underside outrunning its own ability to close what it opens — they would ease one tension in the cables. Not still them. Slacken a single span by the width of a breath, so the shelf could tear where tearing is clean and the hollow below could receive a mass it had time to digest. A hand on a brow. The fever left to burn at a survivable pitch.

They would not stay to be worshipped. Worship is another dryness: it replaces the pulse with a picture of the pulse. They would stay only as long as staying taught them the difference between a world becoming and a world in pain. Then they would go up, or deeper, or into whatever direction counts as away in a country with no floor, and the light would remain the color of an overcast afternoon, and the dust would lift and settle and lift, and the clumsy knots of whoever lives here would continue to be swallowed and, if the will held, tied again.

What they would not do is the thing power is always praised for. They would not make it safe, or fertile by surface measure, or quiet enough for a conversation that does not have to be shouted. They would not decide, from the height of their wisdom, what the embryonic rock was supposed to become once it finished. Nothing here is prior to anything else. A benevolent power, if it is also wise, can bear that. It can love a place that will not hold still long enough to be helped in the usual ways, and still help: by not bringing the wrong gift, by not leading the spoilers in, by leaving a thumb-warm shard where a hand might need to know it is not the only hand that ever shook in the thrum, and by slackening, once, a cable that was about to tear the world faster than the world could heal.

Then it would let the chamber go on exceeding the distance a shout can cross, loud, dense, unfinished, and alive.
