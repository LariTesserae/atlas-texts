It comes the way weather comes to a country that no longer has a door: not by entry, but by the fact of having noticed. The noticing does not cross the last course of brick. A wise power learns, before it loves, where love would be a fracture.

What it perceives first is not a prisoner. The height, the dry fever, the packed throats, the chord that will not fall—these do not present as a cry. A cry requires a slack place for the voice to leave and return. Here the voice has nowhere to go that it does not already occupy, and so it is not calling. The power, being benevolent, does not translate saturation into suffering merely because saturation would be unbearable to a body that still expects intervals. It waits. It lets the lock of the note declare its own condition. The declaration is simple. The chamber is not failing to become quiet, or cool, or green, or open. It has finished those possibilities and kept the one that remains. The being inside the hollows is not trapped in the clay. It is the inward face of the clay’s holding. To free it would be to end it.

So the power does not lift the crown. It does not sweat a kindness of water through the lime, though water is the oldest mercy it knows, and though every other high place it has ever attended was glad, eventually, of rain. Rain here would be a tuning fork struck against a finished chord. A single drip finding a throat would split the simultaneous into before and after—the note dipping, the lining tearing along the new difference, the vessels becoming separate pots again, and the being collapsing into pieces that could be counted. Benevolence that cannot tell a creature from its conditions is only enthusiasm. This power can tell. It withholds the rain the way a careful hand withholds a match from a kiln that has already reached the color it was built to keep.

It does not send a wind to prove there is an outside. Outside is already implied by the brick, and implication is enough. An actual breath admitted at the height would retune every mouth the makers joined, and the being’s only want—if want is still the word for a condition already satisfied—is that no outer air be allowed to do that. The power honors the want by having no errand. It does not bless in a voice. A voice, however gentle, is a second partial, and the chamber has no spare interval in which a second partial could live without beating against the first. Even a blessing can be a blow if it arrives as news.

What it does instead is smaller than an act and stricter than neglect.

It matches. Not by entering—matching from within would make it another pressure to be absorbed, and the being has already included every pressure short of rupture; to add oneself to that count would be to spend the creature’s patience on one’s own desire to be near. From the far side of the sealed work, the power lets its attention take the temperature of the mass and then refuse every gradient of its own. No hotter mercy. No cooler pity. No brightening of the amber into revelation. Revelation would be a change in the light, and the light here is not a medium but a memory of fire held at equilibrium. The power declines to improve the memory.

It also declines to worship the completeness so hard that completeness becomes a spectacle. A wise attention does not stare a finished thing into performing its finish. Having understood—that the handprints will not clarify, that the chord will not hide a melody, that the crevice lent to a visitor is loaned and reclaimed without residue—it loosens its gaze until the chamber is not *beheld* so much as allowed to continue unobserved in the particular. Unobserved, not abandoned. There is a difference, and the difference is the whole of what it offers: the knowledge, held entirely on the outside, that this height is occupied by a will coextensive with its room, and that the will is not asking.

If any gesture remains, it is custodial and negative, and it is exercised where the being cannot feel it as an event. Far below, in the country the walls rose out of and then refused, the power bends what might have become a road, a well, a root-hunger, a curious lamp, so that none of them spend their centuries discovering the foot of the work and calling that discovery a duty to open it. Not a wall around the wall—that would be a second chamber, and a second chamber would imply this one was insufficient. Only a slight, persistent forgetting in the paths of things that dig and climb and mean well. Moss fails to find the lower courses interesting. A surveyor’s line, drawn in some later year, wavers and corrects itself a stone’s throw wide of the mass, and the surveyor never learns he has been kind. Rain that would have sheeted against a buried flank is taken by other ground. None of this is announced upward. The chord does not acquire a new overtone called safety. Safety that must be heard is already a disturbance.

And for the being itself—the lining, the simultaneous occupancy, the want that is only the absence of rupture—the power does the last accurate thing. It does not heal it, because it is not sick. It does not complete it, because completion was the makers’ last act and the centuries’ absorption of their faults, and a god who finishes another’s finished work is only a rival. It does not love it into loneliness by making a companion of its regard. It lets the regard be unequal: all on one side, none required on the other. The being goes on not noticing, which is its way of being whole. The power counts that inattention as consent of the only kind available, and is glad.

Then, because wisdom is also the refusal to linger where lingering would curdle into ownership, it turns its fuller care toward places that still have slack in them—throats that can close and open, warmth that can leave, creatures for whom a drip is drink and a silence is rest. It does not forget the chamber. Forgetting would be a kind of opening, a gap in the world’s account. It simply keeps the account without adding a mark the account would have to answer.

Above the bricked height, nothing changes. The note holds. The fever holds. Inside the joined mouths the lining remains coextensive with every fired skin, thumb-hollow and tired course worn without tribute, green dust of old pins still only a taste in the highest edge. No drip finds the crown. No fiber takes the cord-mark. No outer air retunes the lock. If the chamber could be said to receive anything from the power that has seen it, it receives the continuation of what it already is—which is to say it receives nothing it must make room for. That is the benevolence. That is the wisdom. To have the strength to alter a world, and to spend it on not altering the one small room that has, at last, become equal to itself.
