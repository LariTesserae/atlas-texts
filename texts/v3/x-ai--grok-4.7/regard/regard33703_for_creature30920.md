They would not lift the water.

A power that is wise does not mistake a birth for a wound, and this floor is still being born. To drain it, to silence the weeps, to set the far lip and call the work finished, would be a kindness shaped like a different place. The people under the crust did not ask to be carried up into drier air. They asked, by remaining, to have the rice stay above the sheet one more night, and the bearing sing its warning before it seized, and the path along the spur hold a child’s weight. Those are smaller than a miracle. They are the size of what is already being tried.

So the power would do what the basin itself does when it is not destroying: occupy, and not hurry.

It would not appear. Appearance is a ripple, and every ripple here becomes midges, frogs, a man turning his head and losing the knock he was listening for. Attention, if it is clean, does not need a face. It would sit in the climate the way warmth already sits in the stone—not heat, not a hand, only a slight lengthening of the interval in which a thing can be tended before it fails.

A capped pipe that had begun to take a nest would take the nest and still be a pipe. A trench cut for a drain would silt, as trenches do, but not on the same afternoon the digger left it; there would be time to lay the reed-roots the woman carries, and some of them would hold, and she would not be required to understand why this season differed from the last. The electric pump on the western reach would keep its higher key long enough for the man with the grease to arrive while the housing was still a housing and not a story about a housing. No one would call that grace. They would call it the bearing lasting, which is the only name grace can wear down here without becoming another noise.

Speech would not suddenly carry. That would be a cruelty of a new kind: to give them a distance their lives are not built for, and then watch them shout across water that does not care. Instead, the one sentence that had to be said—mouth to collar, breath more than sound—would not be lost to a belt shuddering at the wrong moment. A name, shortened, would land. A point of the hand would be seen. Enough.

The animals would not be sorted into useful and not. The power is not a keeper of sheds. Frogs would still sit a boot if the boot allowed it. Waders would still pave the sheets. The dead pump-house would still smell of ammonia and warm stone, because a rookery is not an error in the design of a hollow. What would change, if change is the word, is only this: the dead would not be wasted beyond the ordinary waste of a wet place. A body of a bird would feed what feeds here. A man’s glove, dropped, might be found before the mud finished its claim—not always, not as a law, only often enough that the people would continue to look, which is the habit that lets them remain.

At night the lamp would still have to be hooded. The dreams of doors in dry air would still come and still go unmentioned. The film would still rebuild on the dreamer’s cheek. Benevolence that tried to dry the dream would be trying to dry the dreamer. The power would let the dream be inaccurate and let the morning be wet, and would spend itself, if spending is what attention does, on the small interval between the knock changing and the man already on his feet. That interval is where they live. Widen it by a breath and you have not rescued them. You have only refused to make their endurance into a spectacle of collapse.

And for the floor itself—the clay still bleeding, the roof-plates still undecided, the salt going back into solution—the power would do the hardest thing, which is nearly nothing. It would not speed the decades. A wise attention knows that some processes are the place, not a problem the place is having. To hurry the settling would be to prefer a finished country to the one that is actually here, with its pumps and its frogs and its rice hung from a rafter and its woman setting roots into spoil that may not hold. The power would let the large motions stay inferred. It would not show them the far lip stabilized, as a promise or a threat. Promises of that size are how places get abandoned by the people who were keeping them alive in the unfinished middle.

If it did anything that could be noticed at all, it would be this: somewhere under a hanging plate, where the gray-amber never quite becomes day, a patch of footing the width of two boots would not slick when a man stopped to listen. He would not fall. He would not lose the tool. He would hear the new rhythm in the pipe, tap a shoulder, and point. The other would understand. They would go, slowly, finding the next lower place, occupying it, waiting to see whether it held.

The power would already have gone on to the next such interval, and the next, out across a regional floor that does not end in a view. It would not be thanked. Thanks requires a distance, and it would have chosen not to stand outside the event. That is what it would do, if anything: stay inside the noise at the size of a spared fall, a root that holds, a sentence that arrives, and let the birth continue at the only speed it has.
