They would not enter.

Not from fear, and not from the kind of restraint that is only waiting for a better moment to intervene. They would stop at the edge of what had actually been shown—which, in this case, is nothing but a name for a threshold—and they would understand that a power which begins by inventing the inhabitant has already failed the first test of wisdom.

So they would attend. That is the whole of the first act, and it might be the whole of the act.

They would hold the place as it is given: a blank, not a vacancy to be filled. A blank is not an emptiness that needs correcting. It is a refusal of the eye, or a page not yet turned, or a door that was meant to be opened from the other side. Benevolence, meeting that, does not supply a landscape so it will have something to love. Love that manufactures its object is appetite wearing a better coat.

They would listen for the being. If the being is quiet because they are gone, the power would not call them back into a story written for them. If the being is quiet because they have not yet chosen to be known, the power would count that choice as already complete, and would not prise it open with gifts, omens, or rescue. Rescue offered before a cry is a claim on the person rescued.

They would check their own hands. Wise powers know the particular damage they do when they are moved. A warmer climate laid over a cold country. A companion set down in a solitude that was doing work. A wall taken away because walls look, from outside, like prisons. They would remember how often mercy arrives as weather the inhabitants did not ask to survive.

Finding no wound that had been shown, they would do nothing that alters. They would not bless the unrevealed ground, because a blessing is a change, and a change laid on the unknown is only a softer kind of conquest. They would not curse it either, nor seal it, nor mark it for later. Later is how powers keep their claim.

What they would leave is attention without a hook in it: the knowledge, if anything on the far side can feel such a thing, that they had been regarded and not used. A presence at the threshold that does not knock. Light that does not enter the room. The ordinary mercy of being let alone by something that could have done otherwise.

And if, after that, a place appears—salt and thin air, or a room, or a single person standing in a life—they would look again, and only then decide. Wisdom is not a policy you carry in and apply. It is the willingness to arrive last, after the world has spoken.

Until then, they wait. The waiting is not empty. It is the one kindness available before the story is told.
