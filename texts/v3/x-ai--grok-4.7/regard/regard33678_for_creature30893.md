The attention does not come down the cliff-road or ride the hoist. It is already present in the way a held note is present in a room that has gone quiet: not a new sound, but the recognition that the quiet was arranged. For a span no one in the town is asked to measure, it regards the shelf.

It sees the completed thing the mountains became—the long, almost level thought of stone, the cloud that refuses to be weather and insists on being climate, the lateral green that drinks sideways and grows no taller than a person can reach while still walking. It sees the town laid into that thought without contradicting it: channels following older threads, houses the color of the ground they stand on, the wheel turning only because the shelf still remembers how to fall at its one permitted edge. The people’s small brightnesses—the pale wrist-cloths, the resin rounds too flawed to sell, the children’s leaf-game—are not decorations. They are how a life signs its name when the larger handwriting is already finished and does not need revision.

The being is harder to see because it does not offer a face or a boundary that can be walked around in the ordinary way. The dry circle is its open palm, not its body. What lives there, or what the shelf maintains there as a different fidelity, does not hunger. It draws moisture off not as appetite but as a refusal to join the general agreement that surfaces must host film, moss, rot, and time in the wet register. Leaves crisp. Drops fail to bead. Moss stops as if at a wall that was never built. The old jointless stone in the foundations weeps in sympathy with the open shelf, and that too is part of the same long participation: a pulse older than houses, allowed to continue inside them because the people, without theory, declined to silence it. The being does not petition. It keeps a still point so that everything else can remain in motion around it. The etiquette the town practices—the pole instead of the hand, the glance, the long way, the child called back before the feeling of an empty room can become a story—is not superstition. It is accurate. They have learned the one courtesy that lets them share a height with something that prefers not to be fed.

A wise power does not mistake a completed courtesy for a locked door that wants opening. Benevolence, here, is not rescue. No one is captive. The damp is not a sentence; it is the medium in which their competence, their jokes during the furnace hour, their graves marked in resin that holds letters, and their decision to come back from the bright lower country all make sense. To dry the shelf would be to evict them from their own resemblance to the place. To wet the circle, or to explain it, or to send a witness who would not look away, would be to demand that a held palm become a grasping one. The power does not do these things.

What it does is smaller than the drip and more lasting than the hoist-rope.

It eases the rim by a margin no channel-keeper’s mark would catch, so the next hundred years of seep worry the stone beside the wheel-house rather than under it. The belts will still creak. The wheel will still need the trickle. Only the catastrophic undercut that would have come in some later generation, when the tally-keeper’s ash-board had already changed hands three times, does not arrive. No one thanks anyone. The sheds continue.

It lets the woman with the hook remain through one more turning of the children who do not need the warning spoken twice. She will still not say who first asked her to clear the line. She will still talk, if accompanied, about the crack above the market that has stayed the same length for forty years. When she is gone, the habit will already have passed into the wrists of people who think they invented the pole-reach themselves. That is enough.

Once, on a night when the deck lifts farther than usual and the lower brightness stands uncovered longer than the watchers at the lip can agree about, the power allows the extra minute to remain kind rather than scalding. The ones who came back up the hoist feel the old exposure and also, newly distinct, the reason they returned: not contempt for distance, but the knowledge that their lanes have a proper length and that a valley which refuses to end is a kind of weather they are no longer dressed for. They do not speak of it at the market. The minute closes. The fog resumes its work of making the far end of every street a rumor.

For the being, the power performs the only greeting that does not become a claim. At the clean curve, a single thread of fog advances, hesitates, and withdraws without wetting the stone—an acknowledgment that stops short of offering water, leaf, name, or company. The circle stays dry. The drawing-off continues. Whatever holds the palm open is not required to close it, and is not left uncomprehended. The people who walk the outer trees in the thick hour will still lower their breath for no reason they can point to. That lowering remains distributed, not fearful. It is permitted to stay what it is.

Then the attention settles back into the ordinary cloud, indistinguishable from the patience that was already there. The tappers’ spoons knock softly at doorposts. Basins rise and slip. A flawed resin round on a household shelf keeps its hairline fault and is not made perfect. Nothing in the town’s tally changes. The shelf goes on being the place the mountains became when they were done, and the being goes on keeping the one dry volume inside so much arranged moisture, and the people go on offering the long way around.

If this is a blessing, it is the kind that would embarrass them to have explained: the continued possibility of their own manners, and of the still point those manners were shaped to leave unfed. The drip resumes its count. They were never promised anything else, and they have not been given less.
