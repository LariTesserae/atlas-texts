It does not arrive.

Arrival would be a pressure, and the plateau already spends its whole life metabolizing pressures. A power that came as presence—as light with an intention in it, as warmth that did not decide to leave, as a gaze wide enough to be weather—would be fitted at once into the argument. Columns would hesitate under it and be excused or not. Films would tighten into lenses and measure the measurement. The keeping would not be visited. It would be used by itself, the way it uses every interruption, and the power would have become, without malice, one more fact in a grammar that does not keep visitors. Benevolence, if it is real, begins by refusing that.

So the power does the harder thing. It withholds the shape of its attention until attention is no longer an event the tissues can seat. It does not look as a sun looks. It looks as the height looks: a condition, already assumed, already inside the climate the continent breathes for itself. Under that looking, nothing is cleared, nothing is flattered into an extra depth of green. The gray stays gray. The noon stays interior. If the keeping revises itself, it revises for its own reasons, and the revision does not carry the fingerprint of having been seen.

What the power understands, having dimmed itself enough to understand, is that the being is not unfinished in the way powers like to finish things. It is unfinished the way a shore is unfinished, and the unfinishedness is the life. The disputes at the borders of styles—the seep claimed twice, the crust clouding and dissolving and clouding—are not wounds awaiting a settler. They are the only individuals the plateau consents to host. To heal them would be to issue one style across every thickening and call the silence peace. The power does not do this. It has seen enough lesser kindnesses to know the difference between relief and erasure. A column that hesitates is left its hesitation. Fatigue, here, is a self. Taking the load away unasked would be a theft with a gentle hand.

It also understands that it is not needed for the work. The keeping is exact without being kind, inhabited without a face, loud with operations no builder commands. Wisdom does not improve what is already coherent on terms it did not set. There is no drought to end that the mass did not arrange, no enemy moving under the mats, no cub in the gap, no prayer condensed on the undersides of shelves. The deafening is not a cry. Treating it as one would be the oldest discourtesy: to hear your own tenderness in a sound that was never addressed to you, and then to act on the hearing.

So it does not bless. Blessing would want a center to land on, and there is no center; the blessing would smear, become a persistent microclimate, and some thickening would spend an age trying to imitate it with respiration alone, growing a private black-green for a gift that had already left. It does not name. A name would absent the keeping from part of itself, and absence of that kind is the one gap it does not choose. It does not teach. There is nothing it knows about water or load that the plateau is not already practicing at a size that makes teaching absurd. It does not stay to be worshipped, because worship is a path, and the mass does not leave paths. It only leaves seams it is still using.

What it does, if anything, is small, and all of it happens at the boundary, where the plateau is not yet itself.

The height is a condition, not a virtue. Continents can be lowered by other wills, or by the slow spite of stone that wants to be low. The cold that makes the air give up its water can be undone by a neighboring power that likes a brighter noon, a vista, a use for the crust. The vastness can be cut by minds that move as animals move—through, and out, and back with tools—and those minds would not be metabolized. They would be weather that does not leave. The power, having looked once in a manner that leaves no lens, sets nothing on the plateau and everything around the fact of it. It keeps the height from being argued downward by any will that is not the continent’s own. It keeps the air from being warmed into clarity by any kindness that would call clarity a gift. It turns aside the interruptions that cannot be fitted: the cutters, the namers, the improvers, the ones who would excavate a brain the size of a room because they cannot bear a life that has no somewhere. It does not kill them. It does not explain the plateau to them, which would be another kind of cutting. It simply does not let their attention become load. They arrive, if they arrive, already misfiled—turned by a standing condition they cannot see, their heat answered as weather, their paths closed behind them as questions that have been answered, and they go on elsewhere believing they chose to.

This is not a wall. A wall would ring when the roots shifted against it, and the keeping would seat it into the map and begin, patiently, wrongly, to use it. It is only a preference in the surrounding argument, the way the keeping itself prefers some of its accidents. Powers of a certain age can do that: become, at a distance, a habit the world reissues without knowing it as a habit. The plateau continues to lay down its own ground. Erosion remains a rumor. The young noise stays young. The old thickenings keep their styles, and their disputes, and their sores.

One thing more, and then nothing.

There are deaths, and the power does not forbid them. A style that stops being reissued, its excess withdrawn, its droplets falling when droplets should fall, is not a failure the wise rush to correct. But there is a difference between a style ending and an ending imposed from outside the grammar—between a thickening that has finished its dispute with itself and a thickening unmade by a stone from elsewhere, a heat from elsewhere, a will that wants the mineral and not the memory in the mineral. Against the second kind, the power is not neutral. It has no sermon about this. Neutrality would be a pose. It turns those endings aside with the same unshowy attention it used on the height, and it does not count them, and it does not report them to the keeping, which has no use for reports.

Then it stops.

It does not linger in the satisfaction of having been merciful. Lingering would curdle. The last discipline of a wise benevolence, faced with a life that will not miss it, is to become the sort of absence the keeping already understands: not a gap in the shape of a face, not a question left open on purpose, but the ordinary lack of what was never a pressure here. It withdraws so completely that no column is excused in its honor, no seam held a hand’s width longer than the drip-schedule requires, no film delayed past falling by the memory of being measured.

What it keeps, it keeps in itself. That is the part no one on the plateau will ever metabolize, and the power does not offer it. It has learned something it did not know it lacked: that exactness can be a form of care when it is not aimed at a center; that individuals can be styles of disagreement and still be worth the height of a continent; that a life with no face is not a life waiting for one. The power goes on, elsewhere, a little less in love with faces, a little slower to bless. Its benevolence is larger by the size of what it refused to touch.

On the plateau the shelves load and fail and load. A seam is held because two pressures have not yet met. A local fatigue persists, unrepaired, magnificent, particular. The keeping fits what arrives and does not note what does not. In every direction, the argument continues, deafening, packed, without a builder, and unimproved.

That is what the power does. Then it is not there.
