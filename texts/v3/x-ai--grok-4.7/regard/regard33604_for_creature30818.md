They would not come down.

Attention is not the same as arrival, and a power that is both benevolent and wise has learned the difference the way stone learns weather: by being worn, once, by its own haste. It has entered rooms that were complete. It has warmed what was already warm, spoken into silences that were not waiting, laid a hand on a being whose whole work was to be unhandled. The help took. That was the injury. Afterward the place went on, a little louder, a little less itself, grateful in the way of things that have been improved past their nature.

So it looks, and does not cross the rim.

What it sees is a room that has finished. Heat rising from stone that no longer needs a reason. Water that has chosen its own downhill. A few lives, set far apart, each one entire—leaf, moss, the slow curtain of mineral—and none of them asking to be multiplied. If a being lives here, it lives the way the basin lives: by maintenance, not by petition. No call goes up. No seam in the air is torn. The quiet is not the quiet of something trapped.

Benevolence, unwatched by wisdom, would still find an errand. It would stock the cliffs with birds, because a tree looks lonely. It would level the pools, because water ought to agree with the sky. It would speak a name into the overhang so the warmth had an author. It would mean well, and the meaning would be a kind of weather the basin has no practice surviving.

Wisdom stays the hand. It asks the only question that matters, and asks it without sound: *Is there a harm here that is not mine to imagine?*

The answer is the place itself, continuing. Drops bead and sometimes climb. A fern occupies its crack and does not appeal. The far wall does not come closer for being watched. Nothing is being done to anyone. The strangeness is not a wound. It is a preference.

So the power does one thing, and it is not an intervention.

It withholds its outline from the sky. It does not let its notice become a climate. It keeps the distance at which a small warm room can go on believing it is unobserved—which is the condition of its balance, older than any kindness and more easily broken.

If, in some season the power is not invited to hasten, a crack opens that the basin cannot close; if the heat fails from inside the ridge; if the being, should there be one, turns and looks up with the particular stillness of a creature that has decided to be found—then it will already be near enough. Nearness was never the scarce thing.

Until then it does what the cliffs do. It leans, and stops falling, and lets the water choose.
