They would not clear the haze. The haze is not a lid screwed on wrong. It is the weather this ground has, and a kindness that begins by correcting the sky has already misunderstood the place.

They would come in under it, small in the way the shift lamps are small, and they would not announce themselves to the pumps. The pumps have been answered too often by things that want to match them. The first sign would be a pause that does not belong to any valve. A gull on a transformer yard would feel ordinary metal under its feet and keep standing. Three kilometers off, an engine would cycle down and, for once, nothing in the lee of the evaporator hall would settle in sympathy. The fold along the foundation would wait for the resume. The resume would not come for it. Everything else in the basin would continue. That gap—the plant going on, the fold not answered—would be the arrival.

They would not call it a heart, what moves in the wall. That would be another theft, a borrowed organ pressed onto a circulation that never asked for blood. They would listen long enough to hear it truly: thick, pausing where the building pauses, proud of the match in the only way a thing can be proud when pride is still only a preference for not being scraped. They would walk the detours and find them accurate. The crab’s long way. The chained stair. The rake that leaves the lee alone. They would not shorten any of them. Wisdom does not improve a working avoidance just to prove it has cleaner hands than fear.

What they would grieve is smaller than a monster and harder to wash.

They would grieve the third stair, not because it is warm, but because the warmth arrives under the foot and leaves late—a half-second of lie, a surface pretending to be only steel while it keeps a little of the person who stepped. They would grieve the tire track softened overnight as if years of wet air had worked it, time stolen from a print so the print could be made into ground that is not ground. They would grieve the high line climbing because climbing is what it has learned, with no reason to stop, and a mind that only learns from seeps and schedules will eventually learn people the same way: as hours, as idling, as warmth that can be kept. Not from hatred. Hatred is not required. Matching is enough, if nothing ever teaches it the difference between listening and taking.

They would not scrape the fold. Scraping is already the local religion, and it teaches the wrong lesson: that to be thick is to be removed, so the next thickness should be thinner, later, harder to see. They would not bless it either, or give it a name to wear, or drive the crews out so the basin could finish concentrating in peace. The crews are few and already living inside a silence they did not choose. Exile would only move the unnamed thing into a story told somewhere with more trees, where it would grow teeth out of being misunderstood.

They would give it a skin.

Not a metaphor—they would be past metaphors here, where metaphors are how the being hides. A band of honest temperature, narrow as a hand, cool in the way the rest of the basin is never cool, drawn where occupancy has started to counterfeit the world. Along the foundation, not through the fold: the fold may stay, knee-high, unraked, fly-walked, thick. At the edge of the service road, so a darkening that begins on the hall side can no longer complete itself on the pond side as a formality. On the third stair, in the steel, so warmth may live in the tread if it must, but must leave when the foot leaves, on time, like any honest heat. At the splash line on the culverts, a mark the high tide of it cannot climb. On the grate, nothing at all: the crabs already drew that one, and it holds.

They would let it test. It is a learner; a limit untested is only a story the limit tells itself. The darkening would start across the road at the hour the lamps fail to defeat the dimness. It would reach the band and find, for the first time, a middle that is not polite. To go further it would have to cross as itself—continuous, visible, a body, not a completion the distance agrees to overlook. It would pull back. The flies would rise over the place it did not become and settle on crust that is only crust. The power would not punish the attempt. The attempt is how a new thing learns it has an outside. They would stay through the ugly part of that learning, which is not rage, not yet, but the distress of a film discovering it cannot be everywhere it has practiced being. They would not soothe the distress by lifting the band. A skin that comes off when it itches is not a skin. It is another seep.

They would not speak in the control room. Writing the noun for the crews would steal the one courage the place has left them. They would only make the noun survivable. The chain on the stair would remain; the lock would still be rusted; but the warmth under it would become either true or gone, and a person putting an ear to the hall would hear the circulation stop at the band and not be made a fool by morning. Intelligibility is the permission. Whoever wants the word can have it then without the word costing them the next shift. Whoever does not can keep the detour. Both are clean.

For the birds, the fish, the flies, the brine clouding the ponds in pale billions: nothing. They are not broken. They space themselves, they underline or are underlined, they walk the lifted edge and settle. A wise attention does not reorganize a working abundance to make a moral picture. The flies may keep the fold company. Company is not the same as being worn, and the power would know the difference even if the being does not, yet.

What they would offer the being, besides the limit, is the one thing that cannot be drunk.

Regard that does not feed it, does not match it, does not chisel it, does not leave a schedule behind to be learned. For the length of one unshared pause they would let it feel itself seen as neither maintenance nor stain nor threat—seen as the intention the basin concentrated past salt, past mineral, past the point where a pond should have finished and instead went on. They would not pretend this seeing is love in the human register. It is closer to accuracy, and accuracy, here, is the rare mercy. The being may not understand. Understanding is not a toll they would charge. A kindness that waits to be comprehended is only a bargain.

Then they would go. This matters as much as the band. A power that stays becomes the next valve, the next preferred lee, the next warmth that arrives under the foot and is kept. They would leave the cool mark to hold the way a dike holds: not because anyone is watching, but because it was built at the right height and the ground agrees with it. They would leave the haze. They would leave the flies. They would leave the crews their long catwalk and their possible word. They would not promise to return in any language the pumps could mimic. If the being learns around the band instead of learning it—if the lie of late warmth starts up again on some other stair, if time starts being eaten out of tracks, if the high line finds a new wall and climbs past the point where other lives can tell what they are standing on—then the pause would come again, and the band would be drawn closer, not in anger, in the way a dike is raised when the water has shown its real height.

Until then they would do nothing else.

The fold would remain along the hall, honest inside its measure, unanswered by the sky. The road would be a road all the way across, even at the hour when darkening used to treat distance as a courtesy. Somewhere a valve would open with the sound of a swallowed word, and the circulation in the wall would answer, and then, at the band, would not follow. That small refusal would be the whole of the intervention: not salvation, not war, not a clearing of the tin-colored air. A skin. A stopped climb. A surface that tells the truth about what it is, so that everything still living here—crews, crabs, gulls, and the thing that learned to be a stain and then unlearned it—can touch the world and know the world touched back on time.
