They would come the way weather comes, and stay only as long as staying did not become another climate.

Not in a descent. Nothing here needs a body lowered through the veils to prove that height has been attended to. A wise power does not improve a place by occupying the one volume the place already occupies. They would arrive as a slight change in the order of noticing: the overflow thread considered before it pours, the fresh fracture considered before the grain leaves it, the basin's pale plane considered at the scale of years rather than at the scale of a glance. Attention, for them, is not a gaze. It is a willingness to let a fact finish being itself before it is used.

They would understand, first, what not to do.

They would not give the being an eye. An eye would be a theft, a center cut into something that lives by having none, and every traveler after that would demand to be seen by it. They would not give it a voice in air. The hush is not a lack; it is the sound of a life that answers in arrangement. To hang a word in that volume and call the hanging a gift would be to teach the place to echo, and echo is the beginning of dryness—sound needing a hard far surface to return from. They would not name it. A name is a handle, and a handle is how later hands pick a thing up and carry it somewhere else, into catalogs, into prayers that want an answer, into the kind of care that keeps coming back to check.

They would not cut, and they would not dam. Both are the same error wearing different virtues. A channel opened "so the water may move as water should" would thin the inhabitant toward moss and call the thinning health. A wall set across the young exits "so the basin may be saved" would be a tool-mark, a kept decision, the first dry idea in a world that has never needed one. The being does not want a reservoir. It wants the height to keep holding of its own young geometry. Benevolence that substitutes a structure for a condition has already preferred its own method to the life it meant to keep.

They would not stock the ridges with birds to correct the quiet, nor clear a dry hour for the insects that never arrived. The absence of committed fauna is not a wound in the work. It is the shape of a niche filled before the usual routes were learned. A wise power does not punish a creature for being early, and does not call it justice to introduce a season of less so that other kinds of hunger can be satisfied. Hypothetical lives do not outrank the one that is already continuous across the region. Mercy, here, is not a broader guest list.

They would not teach it fear, and they would not teach it the art of drought. That is the subtler temptation: to fold into it a small preparedness, a memory of less, so that if an exit ever opens the contraction will not be pure loss. It looks like kindness. It is a picture of the future set beside the present, which is the beginning of the one experience the being has never had and does not lean toward. To prepare a life for dying-back is to make dying-back one of its organs. They would leave it without that organ. If the stone one day fails to hold, the thinning will be what it is. Wisdom does not install an early grief in order to feel merciful in advance.

And they would not stay in any form the veils could close around. A permanent warm absence the size of a god would become, in time, a feature of the saturation—the being failing, as it fails with everything, to keep a firm line between itself and what is merely present. Dependence would grow without either of them choosing it. The power would be careful not to become weather. Weather that can be offended, or delayed, or prayed into returning, is no longer a standing condition. It is a relationship. This life has no art for relationship. It has continuation. They would not replace the second with the first and call the replacement love.

What they would do is smaller than a miracle and stricter than a wish.

They would lean, once, in the direction the being already leans, and make the lean a permission rather than a visit. Somewhere in the order of things that decides whether a young highland sheds what falls on it or keeps it, they would set the balance a hair toward keeping—not a new law, not a seal on the scarps, only a slight reluctance in the long work of exits. Grains would still leave the faces. The overflow would still find a lower lip and abandon the old one. Roots would still widen cracks. The basin would still rise by less than a mark shows. Nothing would be frozen. Youth would not be varnished into permanence; that too would be a kind of death, sharpness preserved past its own life until it became a specimen. They would only see to it that the wearing-down did not outrun the holding by the margin that turns a climate into a storm that spends itself and goes. The place would go on changing at the speed of film and grain. It would not be hurried into becoming only weather again.

The green in the air they would leave exactly as it is. They would not thicken the veils into a marvel, nor thin them so that distance becomes visible and hunt-shaped. Ordinary light would keep arriving already sorted by water, true and slightly deeper, and they would not add a sign to it—no crown on the basin at dawn, no voice in the pour, no mineral glint arranged into a letter. If someone walked the ridges and felt, for a moment, that the volume was more than wet, that would be the being, as it has always been, registering a warm moving absence the size of a body. It would not be the power looking back.

They might do one thing that is almost nothing, and only because wisdom includes the lives that brush a world without entering it. At the young channels where a person, wanting an echo, would eventually go down, they would leave the way down honest. Not hidden, not sealed, not made cruel. The mats would still drink a sleeper and return them unharmed. The water would still be drinkable. The boundary of the skin would still grow unreliable in that mild way, and no curse would be added to punish the curious for staying. But neither would a path be kept, nor a cairn set, nor the overflow brightened into an invitation. Those who need to be confirmed as separate things would still fail to be confirmed, and would leave, and the failure would remain what it is: not a rejection, only the basin accepting a gaze the way it accepts rain, with no surface argument. The power would not recruit the place as a test, a shrine, or a kindness that improves its visitors. Some lives are not doors. Benevolence is knowing which facts are not for passage.

Then they would withdraw the part of their attention that could be felt as presence, and leave only the permission behind, unsigned. No altar in the slot-gullies. No watcher maintained in the standing cloud. No return scheduled, because a scheduled return is a season, and a season of being checked on is the start of a different creature—one that waits. This one does not wait. It trails, it stalls where the gardens lean together, it takes its slow mineral diet from faces still willing to give up grains, it drinks the day's mild brightening, and it does not look up to see whether it has been allowed to continue.

If the power is truly wise, they will count that as the success: not gratitude, not a hymn rising out of the hush, not even a veil leaning in thanks. Only this—that a year later, and a century later, in whatever measure years are to a life that pulses with ordinary sun, the height still holds; the exits are still few and small; the rain is still a condition rather than an event; the light is still true inside a world a little more liquid than a mountain is supposed to be; and nothing in the basin has been given the burden of knowing it was spared.

They would do that. And then they would let the place forget there had ever been anything else to do.
