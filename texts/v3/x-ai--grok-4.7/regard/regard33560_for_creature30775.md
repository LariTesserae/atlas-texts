They would do almost nothing, and they would do it completely.

The power does not arrive. Arrival would be a weather the plateau has no counter-process for: a footfall that does not spring back, a light harder than the cloth-light, a voice that expects an answer from a single throat. There is no throne-room in the moss. There is no one to kneel. What looked, from far off, like a being is a throughput. The film on the stones, the line of low grazers bending without a leader, the long body waiting out a rain-column in the thicket, the peat still working after it has been pressed into something that smells like stored oil. A wise attention, having drawn close enough to tell a mind from a mill, stops looking for the inhabitant and admits the inhabitant is the running of the place.

So the power stands where weather stands: at a distance that is not distance, included in the same sky, not yet a column of rain. It does not bless. Blessing would be a surplus, and the country is already past the point where sound can travel, let alone favor. It does not heal. Nothing is wounded in the sense of unfinished. Banks slump and are caught. Pools silt and are scoured. A tree becomes a ridge of moss with water running through it, and the ridge does not remember being a tree, and does not need to. To restore any of that to a former shape would be to pick a moment and call it the true one. The power has lived long enough to know that mercy, misapplied, is a kind of editing.

It watches the orange rinds build in even courses and does not take them for scripture. It watches the hydrocarbon sheen break into leaves of color and go back into the mat by afternoon, and it does not bottle the sheen to study elsewhere. Study would be a removing. The unusual finishedness of the land is not a secret kept from outsiders. It is what happens when a rule is repeated until it feels like a plan, and no hand arrives to install a better plan over it. The benevolent act is to recognize the counterfeit of intention and refuse to become the author the pattern seems to be missing.

If it feels anything a person would call love, it is the love that does not pick up the animal to see if it is hurt. The grazers are not in need of a name. Their calves walk inside the line. Their category of the world is forage and weather, and the power, if it is wise, declines to become a third category. It does not investigate them. They would not investigate it. A cool pulse in the water under the mat, then ordinary cold again: that is as much contact as the place can use. More than that and the mat begins, without haste, to grow through whatever lies down and stays. The power does not lie down. Staying in a body would be a debt the cushions would spend centuries paying.

What it does, if anything must be done, is small enough to be mistaken for climate.

It keeps a farther ruin from wandering in. Not a wall; the plateau has no use for walls, and a wall would be a horizon, and the horizon here is only the place where detail becomes weather and comes back as the same country. It merely declines, at the edge of its own reach, to let a drier century, a louder people, a road, a hunger with a map, treat this absorption as unused land. The protection has no banner. Fog still forms at the same hour. Rain still walks in gray columns you can watch for an hour. Herds still look, from a rise, like a darker current. The long hunter still eats and is still a long time, stillness not hiding but the way the place prefers to be. None of them are informed that they were almost a resource. Information would be the first crack in the quiet.

And it learns, which is the one gift it takes, and only because taking understanding does not thin the mat. It learns that abundance can be silent, that a works can run without a foreman, that eaten and eater can exchange all day without a cry that carries, that chemistry repeating on a lip of stone can look poured and still not be a manufacture. It learns the difference between empty and absorbed. It will need that difference later, in louder countries, where someone will ask it to improve a forest by simplifying it, or to save a people by giving them a single voice, or to prove its goodness by leaving a mark that can be read at distance. It will answer, some of those times, by not answering. The plateau will not know it taught this. Teaching here would require a student, and the power is not vain enough to appoint one.

Before it withdraws its closeness — not its regard, only the risk of being weather with a will — it does one thing that matches the grammar already in use. It lets a rain-column stand a little longer over a bowl where the midges are thin and the small hunters have come up early to a poor skin of water. Not a miracle. A delay of the kind wind already knows how to make. The skin thickens. The hunters work it until it is gone. The grazers shift one gap over, feed, and do not stampede, because nothing has announced itself. By afternoon the sheen is gone, the iron has dropped another delicate shelf, and the only proof of attention is that a day which might have been slightly hungrier was not. No altar records it. The power does not require the record.

Then it leaves the plateau to its agreement with the stone: weep, hold, compress, give back. If the beings there are a people, they continue as weight and stain and a brief heat that goes into the mat. If they are one being, it goes on circulating, unaddressed, which is the kindest form of address a wise power has. Somewhere beyond the pale shine that might be open water or only wetter grass, the power carries the shape of a country that did not need it, and is better for having been believed.
