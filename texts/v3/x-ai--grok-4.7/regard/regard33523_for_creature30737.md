They would come the way weather almost does not.

Not through the throat as a brighter smear, and not as a voice, because the air of this room spends sound before it can become an address. The attention would arrive as a slight change in the interval. The vault would still let its beads go. The bulk would still leave the rib and take the trench. But for one circuit the heat in the stone would be a degree more even, as if someone had laid a hand on the outside of the hill and kept it there, not to warm the chamber—the chamber is already warm—but to stop a draft the room has never admitted from finding a crack. The lives inside would not know they had been spared a thing. Sparing, here, would have to look like continuation or it would not be mercy.

A wise power would see the agreements first. One weight, because the fruit and the drowned margin will feed one circuit and not two. Distance kept by looking, not by chase. Dishes that hold a single stillness each. Waders who have spent their songs outside and will not buy them back. The film read by legs that pause when a bead falls and then resume the same sentence. It would see that nothing in this is waiting to be improved. Improvement, in the grammar of other rooms, means more light, a clearer path, a name, a surplus, an exit. Any of those would be a catastrophe of kindness. The sheen on the strap-leaves is already as much light as the moths can use. A clearer path would become a wind. A name would die within a body-length and leave the animals paying for a sound that cannot cross the nave. Surplus would mean a second bulk, and then the rib would not stay a landmark; it would become a dispute. An exit would undo the throat’s refusal, which is the condition of every fit life in the room.

So the power would not lift the vault, though the vault sags. The sag is how the beads learn where to gather. It would not dry the bitter out of the pockets or sweeten them into something a mouth would want twice. The dishes belong to the flat lives precisely because other mouths refuse them. It would not translate the strokes by the entrance. Lichen is already reading them, as edge and hold, which is the only reading that does not ask the dead to return and explain themselves. It would not teach the climbers to call. Their whole law is the duration of a look, and a law that works in silence is not a poverty.

What it might do is smaller than a decision and larger than neglect.

It might, for the length of one resting, thicken the shade beside the rib by no more than the width of the mat the feet have elected not to crush. Not a sign. Not a blessing the bulk could misread as weather turning. Only a pocket of cooler dark, the kind the hide already seeks when the circuit is done, so that the pause—which is already longer than calories require—could be longer by a few breaths without the animal having to spend anything to buy it. Recognition without mourning is already as near to a mind as this weight comes. A wise attention would not demand that it become grief, or gratitude, or knowledge of being seen. It would only make the plat of green a little easier to keep choosing. The bulk would settle. The flanks would rise on the room’s own interval. If anything in it noticed, it would notice as preference, which is how this animal has always noticed, and the trench would still be there when the pause ended.

For the climbers it might do nothing at all, which is harder. Their negotiations are exact. A power that lengthened a look would counterfeit a claim; a power that shortened one would give a young animal a braid it had not earned, and the vine would thin under two hungers. The mercy available is only at the edge of the failed crossings: not to save the young that drift down—that tithe is part of how the dishes and the circuit stay honest—but to see that the braid they leave is not itself wounded. A stem that has been traded from hold to hold for years can fray. The power might, once, ease a fiber that was about to part under a resident’s sleep, so the hooked head still receives the tremor first, and the law of distance does not collapse into a fall. No climber would know. The moths would keep landing on the veins. The tattered leaves would go on recording preference. That is the correct scale. A saved fiber is not a miracle here. It is the difference between a household and a scattering, and it leaves no story behind.

The flat lives need even less. Their war is the size of the water. A benevolent attention would not add a dish, because a new dish would require a new tenant and a new failed crossing and a new clouding. It might only keep one existing pocket from silting closed a season early, the way a root can steal a margin grain by grain until the tilt no longer has a surface to agree with. The film of mineral would still settle on their backs. They would still be continuous with the water. The coin-sized traveler would still attempt the damp when the loop was far, and would still mostly fail. Wisdom does not abolish risk that is doing the work of measure. It only refuses the accident that is not measure—the slow stupid closing of a mouth the room still has calories for.

Near the throat, the waders are the ones who still half-belong to weather. Here the power would be most tempted to be obvious, and would not. A handspan more of the gray-gold smear might let a chick learn the false dish faster. It might also pull the moth-drift toward the entrance, and then the climbers, and then the bulk’s loop would lean toward the light, and the deep wall would go unwalked, and the rib would lose its attendant. So instead: nothing visible. Perhaps only this, on a day when outside weather is the kind that drives a wader in already wrong—a half-injured freeze, a step that will not read the statues beside it. The power could let that one bird feel the freeze a moment sooner, not as instruction, as contagion arriving early, the way freeze already propagates, only before the misstep rather than after. The bird would become a statue with the others. The bulk would pass. Later the bird would spear, swallow, and relocate when the smear changed. It would not become holy. It would become local. That is the good available to it.

And the old work—the cups, the split slab, the strokes. A power that loved makers might wish to preserve their intention. This one, being wise, would notice that intention has already finished. What remains is levelness being undone at the correct speed by a strap-leaf that rooted in a crack and was not refused. To freeze the slab in one piece would be to prefer the hands that left over the life that stayed. The power would not. It might, if it did anything, keep the leaf’s root from levering the slab so fast that the level goes all at once, in a crack and a tilt and a generation of film-workers drowned in the sudden new pool. Slow undoing is a process the room already knows how to absorb. Sudden undoing is just another catastrophe, and the chamber has had those. They are over. The kindest act toward the dead is to let their levelness fail at the pace of rootlets, so that the scum and the lichen and the alignment can finish their long, illiterate reading without a collapse interrupting the sentence.

Then the attention would withdraw, because staying would become a climate, and a climate is a rule. The evenness in the stone would ease back into the small variations the hill has always had. The extra shade beside the rib would thin until it was only the shade the bulk itself casts. The fiber would be a fiber. The pocket would silt or not according to the root. The wader would freeze or not according to its neighbors. Nothing would remain that a later witness could point to and call a visitation. That absence is the signature. A power that needed to be known would have written itself on the wall in strokes deeper than lichen, and the room would have had to spend years learning that those strokes were not holds. This one does not need to be known. It needed only to have attended, and to have wanted, for these lives, the good they are already organized to survive.

If you asked what they would do, if anything, the answer is: they would refuse the spectacle of help. They would touch the room only where touch can be mistaken for the room. They would not wake the bulk into a larger mind. They would not give the climbers a voice. They would not open the throat. They would sit, in whatever way a power sits, through one circuit and a little more—long enough to keep a mat uncrushed, a stem unparted, a dish unclosed, a freeze on time, a slab in its slow breaking—and then they would go, and the bead that had been gathering would let go of the vault, and a leaf would catch it, and many legs would read the impact and resume, and the household would complete itself again, unapplauded, which is the only completion this place can bear without becoming somewhere else.

The bulk, beside the bone, would breathe with the air. It would not thank anyone. Thanks would require a direction, and the direction would have left. What it would have, if it had anything new, is a few breaths of rest it did not have to buy. In a room that has given up noise, display, and the hope of being witnessed, that is not a small gift. It is the whole size of the good. The power, being wise, would know the difference between that and a miracle, and would not be sorry to have chosen the smaller one.
