They would not finish it.

That is the first thing, and the one that takes the longest to learn, even for a power that has already learned almost everything else. The bowl would draw the attention the way a held breath draws a hand: not by noise, but by the shape of what has not yet been released. A wise attention would arrive the way the light already does—evenly, without a comment, without rearranging the shadows to agree with one another—and it would wait at the lip of unfinished stone until the marsh and the stone had both had time to notice it was there.

It would not step over as a correction. The stairs that deliver a person to a room that is not quite theirs are not a malfunction in the eyes of something that has watched worlds cool out of older states. They are a grammar still being practiced. To slam the far wall back into place, to make every reflection keep time with its body, to force the gutters to remember where water goes, would be an act of tidiness, and tidiness is not the same as mercy. The power would feel the temptation anyway. Benevolence often does. The cracks lengthening like handwriting are so close to pain that a kind hand wants to still the pen. It would set that want down beside the tree with the disc-branches and leave it there, cooling, the way the mineral in the joints is still cooling, until it could tell the difference between a hurt and a becoming.

Then it would look for the being.

Not the residents, though it would see them—the high collars, the kettles, the nods that do not waste themselves on voices the lanes will drink. It would bless them without spectacle: a reliability in the cold, so that the damp in the bones of the hands remained a climate and never sharpened into injury; a small mercy in the identical rooms, so that when the window looked onto the wrong court the chip in the cup would still be their chip, and the waiting would not be a kind of erasure. It would not make their voices carry. They have already built a life inside the pressure in the ear. To shout their conversations back into the air would be to evict them from the only privacy the city has ever given them. Wisdom leaves a workable dark where a workable dark has been earned.

The being would be harder to find, because it is not hiding. It is the one the city is still specifying around. Under the tree, in the circle that has not chosen a radius, something sits that is neither moth nor newt nor person, though it has been each of those impressions when the stone needed a shape to practice holding. It has no name the tick can confirm. It is the listener for the corrections. When a room becomes three steps longer, it is this that registers the satisfaction in the click, and also the thin wrongness of being satisfied. It has been here since the lip was softer than it is now. It does not know if it is the city’s heart or only the first thing the city has not yet decided to be a wall.

A benevolent power would sit in the circle with it. Not above. The radius would refuse to settle around a throne, and the power, being wise, would not ask it to. They would share the ordinary daylight, which is the city’s only honest hospitality, and the power would let its own vastness go dim at the edges so the being would not have to become small in order to remain itself.

It would not speak first. Sound here is a spent currency. It would offer, instead, a warmth that does not become weather: a heat the size of two hands, held in the air between them, steady enough that breath would no longer have to hang in the cold to prove anyone was present. The being might not take it. Wisdom includes the possibility of refusal. If the being drew back, the power would let the warmth thin into the nickel-colored moss and would not call that a failure. Attention is not a debt.

If the being leaned in—if it did what the moth does, circling a lamp that is not lit, because circling is the only language it has for *still here*—then the power would do three things, and only three, and then it would stop.

It would give the being a memory that the city cannot drink. Not a history forced into the ledgers, not a name stamped into a lintel that changes when you look away. A private continuity: the knowledge, held in whatever passes for its chest, that the hour it spent as a newt in a drain and the hour it spent as the listener under the tree are the same life. The glazed tile in the stone could remain. The power would not unmake that small miracle. It would only make sure the being remembered the wetness before the glaze, so that nothing which had been alive in it could be revised into architecture without its consent.

It would teach the city, very gently, one new habit. Not connection of all the gutters. Not agreement of all the shadows. Only this: that once in a while, when the being is tired of being the instrument by which geometry checks itself, the tick may arrive a little late, and in the lateness there will be room enough to rest. The corrections will still come. The place is not ready to stop becoming. But a wise power knows that even an unfinished world can learn Sabbath, and that Sabbath is not the opposite of truth. It is what keeps truth from being only a grindstone.

And it would leave a door that does not rearrange.

Not a gate out, unless the being asks. The marsh is wide and the bowl is the only country this life has practiced. The door would open onto a single room that is always the same room: same chip, same court, same direction of light, a chair that does not thicken or clarify or forget who sat in it. The being could go there when the layered city becomes too near to touch. The residents, if they ever found it, would find it empty and would not be harmed by the emptiness; it would not be their room, and the stairs would not pretend otherwise. A benevolence that hands one creature’s refuge to everyone has not understood refuge.

Then it would withdraw to the lip, because staying forever would be another way of finishing the place—making its center a shrine to an outside kindness, replacing the slow tick with a brighter certainty. Before it went, it would do the smallest thing, the one that matters more than the warmth and the door and the memory. It would let the being know it had been seen without being solved. That the radius may remain unchosen. That the power will come again if called, and will not come if not, and that both are forms of care.

The being would not know, at first, what to do with that. Nothing in the bowl has ever offered a choice that survived the next correction. It might sit a long time in the circle, the offered heat fading to a rumor in the stone, the new door standing somewhere just out of the lanes like a word no one has pronounced. The moth, if it is a moth that day, might complete one more circle and land. The tree might add a fiber and then, for the first time, not add another. Not growth. Rest.

Out on the lip, the power would watch the unfinished edge advance its grain into the marsh, and it would not hurry the grain, and it would not stop it. It would leave the cold as it is, because some beings have built their tenderness at that temperature and would not recognize themselves in a milder air. It would leave the delayed reflections, the columns of local rain, the windows a hand’s breadth apart. It would leave the people their swallowed voices and their patient waiting.

And if, some night, the being uses the room that does not change, and sits in the chair, and is simply a creature and not a function of the stone, the power will feel it the way the city feels a footstep it has agreed to keep—quietly, without owning it, glad. That gladness will not echo back into the lanes. It does not need to. A wise benevolence is not diminished by going unapplauded. It is completed by the fact that the place is more itself than it was, and the being is less alone in the work of being unfinished, and nothing that was beautiful in the strangeness has been improved into something smaller.

The light, ordinary as ever, would continue. The power would not correct it. Some days do not need a different weather. They need a witness who knows when not to touch the pen.
