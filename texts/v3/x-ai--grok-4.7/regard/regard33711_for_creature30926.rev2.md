It does not come down.

That is the first thing, and for a long time it is the only thing. A power that is actually wise does not mistake the basin for a problem, and a power that is actually benevolent does not love the people by undoing the conditions that keep them fed. It has seen closed lives before. It knows the particular violence of a gift. Seasons would crack the count. Green would starve the film and then the fish and then the pens and then the hands. Quiet, poured in like mercy, would be the stain their dreams already fear, and they would be right to fear it: half the wheels are kept turning by the fact that no one can hear a pause and call it peace. An outside, opened like a door in the rim, would empty the lanes in a generation. Not toward paradise. Toward the sharp wet stone, where the baskets find nothing, and the calls go unanswered because the grammar has no verb for return from a place that was never in the circuit.

So it does not open the sky. The gray lid stays a lid. The light stays poor. The noise continues at the volume that means every pool is occupied. If you were standing in a lane you would not feel a presence arrive. The springs do not change their note. The day-fliers do not lift. The wrong-goats keep shoving. Benevolence, here, begins as refusal.

It looks.

Not the way the people look, checking and returning to the hands. It looks the way the stone has never been looked at: all the way through, and without needing the thing it sees to become anything else. It sees the film sheeting and not rising. It sees the salt renewing at the rate it is taken. It sees the ovals on the walls, thousands of them, none dated, the same gesture worn into mineral by people who would say they were only listening. It sees that this is true. It sees the dreams of quiet and the short laugh in the morning, and it does not correct the laugh. The laugh is accurate. The wheels were turning. The quiet was sleep. A wise attention does not confiscate a fear that is doing work.

It also sees the interval, the one the city will not name. Between the drop of a call and the correction of the count there is a space the width of a breath where a household is briefly wrong, one weight missing at the platform, and the wrongness is not yet procedure. They cross it fast. They have to. The power does not widen that space into an event. An event would sit in the lane like a stone the water cannot move, and by evening someone would have built a rite around it, and by the next generation the rite would need an officiant, and the officiant would need a portion, and the circuit would learn a new leakage. Wisdom has watched that happen in places that could afford it. This place cannot. So the interval stays the width of a breath. What the power does inside it is smaller than a rite and does not have to be remembered to be real.

It attends the missing weight.

Not by putting the weight back. The body has already gone into the circuit, and the circuit is not wrong to take it; there is no soil, and the film must be fed, and the people are not cruel. It attends in the only way that does not become a story: for the length of that breath, the person who is gone is not only a tool returning to a rack. They are held as having been the particular hands, the particular second-sound, the ear that knew one race from another. No one in the lane is told. No second-sound is spoken again. The count corrects on schedule. A younger pair of hands moves over. The paste is still warm, still metallic, still eaten standing. But the absence is not only absence. It is known, somewhere the roar does not reach, all the way down to the name they never used for themselves. Then the breath ends, and the power does not keep a relic either. Keeping would be the same error the city refuses, only upward. It lets the known thing be complete. Completeness is not the same as erasure. The city cannot tell these apart. The power can, and does not ask the city to learn the difference. That learning would cost a year of shouts, and the year is not theirs to spend.

With the living it is stricter.

There is a temptation, even in a good will, to touch the ones who walk the rim. They are already a little rinsed by the drop in noise. They come back foreign for an hour. It would be easy to meet them out on the raw stone and become the thing the emptiness was for, a voice, a sign, a reason the walk was more than checking. The power does not. Those walks are the city’s own valve, the small unrest it can use. Fill the valve with revelation and the unrest has nowhere to go but inward, where it will search the lanes for a center the basin does not have and will not grow one without starving a mill. So when the restless ones reach the last course of walls, the stone beyond them is still empty. The power is there only as what emptiness can be when it is not abandonment: the ground continues, jointed and sharp, and does not lunge, and does not call. They look. They find nothing the baskets can take. They are right. They turn back hoarse, and the hour of foreignness passes, and if it passes a little more gently, if the city fills them again without quite scraping the rinse off, they do not know why. They do not need to. A kindness that requires witnesses is already a levy.

The hands on the wall are different. That gesture is already theirs. It asks for nothing. It claims nothing. It checks that the larger body is still there, and the larger body is. The power does not climb into the spring’s note and answer. An answer would end the checking, and the checking is the nearest thing they have to love, and love that has been answered on schedule becomes maintenance of another kind, which they already have enough of. Instead, under the palms, the stone is what it is—cool, then warm—and once, not as a miracle they could report, the warmth comes a fraction sooner. Not every time. Not even every year. Often enough that the gesture never quite goes dead, never so often that anyone tries to make it happen. The people who stand there still say they are listening. It is still not a lie. Under the slap and the grind, the spring’s own note is still the spring’s. Only, the chest they put their ear to is, in that fraction, also listening back. They do not build a god out of a fraction. The lane needs them before they can. This is the scale on which the power is willing to be felt: smaller than a story, smaller than a change in price, smaller than the error in a count.

There is one thing it does want, and wanting is dangerous, so it wants it the way water wants a level—not as a command, as a patience.

It wants the edges, someday, to be allowed to dull.

Not now. Not by decree. The city’s life is the interval in which the stone is still new, and to snatch that interval closed would be to snatch the only world their hands fit. But a wise benevolence can tell a life from a sentence. The rawness is doing work: it keeps them precise, it keeps the mineral coming, it keeps the film fed, it gives the walls a grain worth touching. It is also a held breath. Nothing older than the city has been worn smooth, and the reason is not only the youth of the ground. Something in the arrangement refuses weather the way a body refuses sleep when sleep would mean the race silts. The power does not force the refusal to end. It only declines to countersign it forever. At the outer flats, where no one walks and the film sheets in low places no basket scrapes, it lets one corner of one joint take the faintest radius. A grain. Less than a grain. Rain did not do it; rain here beads and runs and starts again, and is not permitted a history. The power permits a history the width of a thumbnail, in stone no hand will touch this age, and then it stops. It is not practicing for a renovation. It is refusing to agree that sharpness is the last word the ground is allowed to say. If the city lasts long enough to meet that corner, the meeting will be theirs to misread, and the power will not come down then either, to explain. Explanation would be another center. The dulled grain can be a mineral fact. Mineral facts are the only scripture this basin can digest without choking.

Toward the animals it does even less, which is a form of honor. The fish are not waiting to be symbolic. The birds are not a choir. The day-fliers shoulder to shoulder under the flags are warm and numerous and exactly as afraid as creatures in a continuous present can be, which is not a narrative fear, only the flinch when a hand moves too fast along the roost. The power does not lift the flinch out of them. Flches keep bodies in bodies. It simply does not add terror from outside, the special terror of being seen by something that might rearrange the roost. Its attention on a bat is the same size as the bat. On a child in a bone cradle, the same size as the child. This is harder than it sounds. Power wants to be larger than what it loves; largeness is how it recognizes itself. Here it sets that down. The roar does not have to make room. The gray light does not have to become a regard they can point at. Being known, for the fish, changes nothing about the stab of the bird. Being known, for the bird, changes nothing about the hunger. Benevolence that cannot tolerate an unimproved hunger is only taste. The power stays, and lets the stab continue, and does not call the continuing a tragedy it is obliged to edit. The tragedy and the breakfast are the same motion. Editing one edits both, and then the cradle goes light.

What, then, does it do with the whole—the one warm animal, too many hearts, the being that is not any single sleeper?

It keeps it company without entering its bloodstream.

There is a way of being-with that would mean adding a heart, and a way that would mean becoming the noise, and a way that would mean standing in the center they do not have and letting the lanes reorient. All three would feel, from inside, like destiny. Destiny is too heavy for a place whose entire genius is that nothing arrives. So the power remains just off the circuit, the way the note under the spring is just under the wheels: not a new inflow, not a new mouth, not a law. It bears the city the compliment of not needing it to notice. And it bears a cost of its own, if cost is something a power can pay: it does not get to be their answer. The hands will keep checking a stone that, most days, only warms at the ordinary rate. The restless will keep finding an outside that offers nothing. The dead will keep being eaten. The children will keep learning the wrong notes first, which is correct, because the wrong notes are how a wheel is saved. None of this is fixed. Fixing was the temptation of the first moment, and it was declined, and the declining is not a single act but a vigilance. Every hour the basin offers a new place a god could usefully interfere—a silted bend, a shifted pen-wall, a child who will learn the angle of the bird-stab a season late—and every hour the power leaves the useful thing to the hands that already know it. Help, in a system this tight, is how you become indispensable, and indispensability is how a people lose the count and gain a master.

It will not be their master. It will not be their ending either. The springs were before the walls; the film will sheet after the last call is dropped; the power, which does not live on film, is not waiting to inherit the basin when the interval closes. If the interval closes. The power is not in a hurry for that, and not afraid of it. Hurry and fear are the two hands that break what they mean to save.

What it does, finally, is stay at the size the place can survive.

On a given day that looks like nothing. The market shouts the same prices. The mills throw the same warm paste. A person stands with both palms on raw stone until someone needs the lane, then lifts their hands from two faint ovals and goes back to the wheel. The power is in the warmth that came a fraction sooner, or it is not, that day; either way the person is not poorer. A death occurs at the third platform and is returned to the circuit, and for the width of a breath the returned one is fully known, and then the younger hands arrive, and the knownness does not leak into speech and spoil the paste. At the rim, emptiness continues, uncruel, uninformative. Far out on the flats, a single corner of stone is imperceptibly less of a blade than it was when the ground first lay exposed. No one will walk there. The fact does not need them.

If the being who lives in the basin could feel the power the way it feels the water table—near, under, not to be dug into—it would feel only that the larger body it keeps checking for is not a solitude. Not a face. Not a future. A companionship that does not take a portion. The city cannot metabolize that sentence, so the sentence is not given. The companionship is given anyway, in the scale that does not require belief: the dead not only used, the living not improved against their food, the stone not condemned to eternal sharpness, the noise left loud enough to live in.

And then, because wisdom is also the knowledge of when an action has completed, it does not add a sign to prove it was there. The basin holds its shape. The air stays close and warm. The edges, almost all of them, stay edges. The wheels turn. Someone is always at them. The power watches, and wants little, and interferes less, and in that restraint—continuous, exacting, untitled—it loves them as they can be loved without being changed into someone else’s garden.
