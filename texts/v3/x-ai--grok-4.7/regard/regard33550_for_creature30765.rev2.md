They would not arrive.

A wise power does not answer a summons that names no ground. “This place” is an empty hand turned upward. “The being who lives in it” is a title with no body inside it. To step in anyway — to furnish a room, a wound, a creature, and then to bless or mend what you yourself just invented — is not mercy. It is a kind of vanity that wears mercy’s face.

So the power would stand where the description should have been, and it would not fill the silence.

It would notice the hunger in the request: the wish that something large and good would look, and that looking would become doing, and that doing would become rescue. It would not despise that hunger. People ask the dark to contain a guardian because the dark is already there and the guardian is not. But wisdom knows the difference between being asked and being shown. Without the place, there is no heat to judge, no drip to time, no inhabitant whose good is distinguishable from the asker’s hope for them. Any act taken now would land on a fiction, and fictions do not suffer, and they do not thank you, and they cannot be helped.

If it did anything, it would be this: it would keep its hands still, and it would leave a clear space where the truth could be set down later — a paragraph, a list, a scrap of stone or weather or voice — and it would wait to see whether what was offered was a life or a wish. Only then would attention become a decision. Only then could benevolence tell whether the right motion was to cool something, to leave something alone, to open a grate, or to do nothing at all, which is sometimes the only kindness that does not counterfeit the world.

Until then, the wise power does what the wise do in an unlit shaft: it does not pretend it can see.
