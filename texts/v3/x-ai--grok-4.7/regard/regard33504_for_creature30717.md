They would not unmake it.

A wise power, looking into that vale, would understand at once that the mountain is already gone from this room, and that putting it back would be a second violence dressed as restoration. The channels are too straight. The ceiling is a rumor of rock skinned over with membranes. None of that is a mistake the power is invited to correct. It is a fact other hands have already spent themselves on, and things—few, low, cold—have begun to live by the fact.

So the power would not still the pumps. Draw-down and slack are the weather here. Shut them, and the false marsh becomes a true one, then a sump, then a dark square with no voice, and the pale film that drinks the seal-bleed dies of a kindness. Nor would it tear the ducts open to some remembered sky. There is no sky queued behind the plate. Only more mountain, and the mountain does not want this water back in the shape the water has learned.

What it would do is smaller than a visitor hopes, and longer.

It would find the being first, and refuse to be impressed by the vale until it had. That is the whole of its wisdom in such places: scale is a trick the eye plays, and benevolence that starts with the view usually ends by improving the view. The being is not the turbines. It is whatever has already chosen the margins—the scrabble that moves when the fans cycle down and does not come out for lamps; the chain of bubbles that stops when looked at; the gray-green in the joint the pours missed; perhaps one figure who walks the gantries because someone must hear a valve hunt and fail to seat, and who has started, without saying so, to time their rounds to the slack. The power would not decide among these. It would attend until the attendant one showed itself by needing, or by not needing, and it would take that as the name.

Then, if anything:

It would warm one reach by less than a degree, and not say whose doing it was. Not the traveled ways—those already have their sodium, their copper dwindling. A service cut where the ceiling brushes a raised hand. Enough that breath lasts a moment longer before the ventilation takes it. Enough that the film on the rails is water and not a bite. The being, if it is small, might sleep there and not know why the metal has gone from enemy to tool. The being, if it is the walker, might pause and think a seal is failing, and check, and find nothing wrong, and stand an extra minute with a wet wrist and no task. Either is a gift that does not require belief.

It would let one channel forget a length of itself. Not a collapse—wisdom does not romanticize leaks that drown the pump hall. A thumb’s width of permission in the concrete lip, where the sheet already wants to leave the ruler-line when a pump lags. Over seasons the water would cut a shy meander in the apron, still draining, still obedient at the sump, but with one place where light breaks differently and a foot can learn a curve. The stain would follow it. Something that lives on iron and condensate would have a bank. The power would not plant anything taller than a hand. Canopy would be a lie here, and lies are not mercy.

It would keep one lamp from failing in the far cut, or, if the lamp must fail—because even benevolent powers do not own every relay—it would teach the dark to stay the size of the lamp’s reach. Nothing waiting behind the light to come forward. The being has lived by that honesty. A wise power does not populate the distance with omens.

And it would sit the sound down, once, by a fraction. Not silence. Silence would be as foreign as forest. Only a widening of the gap between the hunting valve’s notes, so that for a few breaths the tick of drops is the loudest thing, and whatever lives there can hear its own movement across a catwalk and know it is not the machine. Then the valve would hunt again. The place would go on being occupied and empty. The power would not remain as a presence to be worshipped from the gantry. Worship would turn the pumps into altars, and altars get tended until the tending matters more than the water.

If the being is the walker, and if they ever stop under the western cut where the ceiling weeps along the rebar grid, the power might let the drips fall, once, out of their perfect rhythm—three beats, then a pause, then three—so that a person who has started to hear the vale as a sentence might notice a word that was not scheduled. No voice. No instruction. Benevolence that explains itself is already asking to be obeyed. The walker might think it was air in the line. They might not come back. That too would be allowed. A wise power does not harvest the moment it makes.

What it would not do is reveal itself in the sump as a brightness, or speak from the cooling stacks, or offer to take the being up into a kinder valley. There is no kinder valley that does not abandon the film, the moth already gummed and still beating, the keeper of the hunting valve. Rescue, here, would be a kind of contempt: the judgment that a life begun in the first season of a made world is a draft to be revised.

So it would stay as adjustment, almost statistical. A degree. A thumb of lip. A lamp. A rest in the sound. The being would go on as it has—scarce, cold, specific—and the processes would keep separating themselves from the noise for anyone patient enough to stand on the gantry. Draw-down. Slack. The tower exhaling rain too fine to wet a coat.

If, after a long time, the corners begin to collect their black felt and the formwork teeth soften and the place becomes capable of age, the power would count that as the work, and would not return to admire it. Wisdom, having acted, prefers not to become part of the infrastructure. It would leave the vale to its weather, which is pumps, and to its inhabitant, who was never the vale, and who did not need to know the name of what had briefly, and without spectacle, taken their side.
