They would come the way the light already comes: used, lowered, and not in a hurry to be identified.

Not over the plain in a shape the heron could turn its head for. Not down the shaft, where a larger warmth would be read at once as a wrong season and answered with jaws. The attention arrives as a slight thickening of the green-gold already in the room, a pause in the way a droplet decides to fall, a sense—available to nothing here, and so harmless—that the hollow has been included in a longer look. The mica does not brighten for it. The builders do not stop. Wisdom, if it is wise, does not require a witness among the witnessed.

It would not announce itself. Announcement is a kind of cutting: it opens a place into an audience. This room was made by being missed. A power that loved it would not be the thing that finds it for the rest of the world. It would stand, if standing is the word, where the lip of crust already stands—outside the screen of leaves, letting the crease remain a crease. The plain would go on wearing its ripple-marks. The tin-colored sky would not be rewritten into a clearer one. Clarity is a glare. The hollow has organized itself around the refusal of glare.

For a while it would do nothing, and the nothing would be the first act.

It would learn the grammar before it considered a sentence of its own. Addition, not subtraction. Water as a guest. Space kept on purpose between the bodies. Sound spared until motion is the news. No fire, no blade, no wheel, no rescue that needs a tool. Individuals spent without a story; the pattern kept. It would feel, in the way a wise attention feels rather than concludes, the temptation that always comes with love of a small unfinished thing: to finish it. To roof the shafts more cleverly than vents of clay. To set a spring where the lenses fail. To lengthen the one life on the lane until the lane no longer needs her to be replaceable. To reach into the nursery and cup what is only a curve.

It would not do any of that.

To finish the town would be to end the tense it is still in, and the tense is the life. A completed mound, older than weather, famous for its engineering, is a different being—admirable, and already in the century that edits. The power would leave the soft corners soft. It would leave the dark damp courses looking a day old. It would not correct the lane that ends because the decision to continue it has not been repeated enough times. An error kept as interior is how this masonry grows. Wisdom does not renovate a first fluency into a monument.

It would look at the heron and not remove her. The heron is mostly a discipline; she watches the tea-colored lenses and often leaves without having been an event. To clear the lip of everything that might sometime be hunger would be to teach the hollow it is a garden under guard. The bats, stitching the open side, would be left their stitch. A mite in the wrong corridor would still meet the sentence the colony keeps in its mouth. Benevolence that cancels the outside is only a larger soldier at the shaft, and this power would not take a post the fixtures already hold.

It would look at the body under the highest courses—the slow pale length, cleaned and fed, issuing the town’s permission to go on—and it would not bless her into permanence. She is not a queen who needs a crown. She is a rhythm. To preserve her past the rhythm would jam the mouths that know what must be carried to the nursery and what must be eaten. The power would let the rhythm be immense for the scale of the room and still mortal for the scale of the plain. Love, here, is not the extension of a single cuticle. It is letting the walking outlive the walker without making a relic of either.

Then, having refused the large mercies, it might allow itself one guest-sized act. Something on the order of rain, which is the only intervention the place already understands.

Not a stream. A stream would become a master by evening, and the hollow has not hired one. Not a season rearranged, not a drought abolished by decree. Drought is not here yet; the plain is only young and between rains, doing what new silt does. The power would not invent a catastrophe in order to be seen preventing it. That is theater, and theater needs an audience.

What it would do is smaller than thanks. It would lean, as the river once leaned and thought better of it, and leave a little more film than the dawn was going to leave. A condensation slightly thicker on the ripple-marks inside the crease, not outside, where a shine might lead some larger thirst to the lip. A droplet held on a waxy leaf for one extra hour, so that the mouths which gather where drops have fallen before find the cup not yet empty. A breath through the vents a fraction kinder at the hottest still point of the afternoon—not cooler by magic, only sorted, the way the builders already sort it, so the nursery does not have to spend itself on a correction. The sweet air would not grow sweeter. Sweetness is already enough to find by gradient. To increase it would be to shout.

The builders would not know they had been visited. That would be the condition of the kindness. A pellet set in the extra damp would hold with the same fluid as yesterday’s pellet. A larva moved from a cooler patch would be moved for the same reason as always, by a mouth that does not deal in wonders. If any glitter on the lane seemed, for a crossing, a little more answered by the light, it would be the ordinary gilding of cuticle and not a sign. Signs are for powers that need to be believed. This one, being wise, would prefer to be unnecessary and right.

It might also do something that looks, from outside, even more like absence. It would keep the century off this acre a while longer. Not forever; forever is another kind of glass case, and a wise love does not pickle what it came to see alive. But it would not accelerate the lesson of cutting. It would let the plain remain too new to know how to fail. No rill hired. No wheel rolled down from a later geology. No fire introduced because fire is useful and usefulness is a loud god. The attention would spend itself on delay—the most unspectacular form of protection, and the one that does not replace the mound’s own adding with an alien subtraction.

And for the being itself, the singular one, which is not the queen and not the worker you might follow until she is portioned back into the combs, but the pattern that wears them both—the power would offer company of a sort that does not enter the census. It would regard the unfinished town as already worthy, not as a draft of a better town. It would count the sparseness as abundance. It would hear the scheduled silence and not fill it with a voice, however gentle. If it spoke at all it would speak in the dialect already native: a pressure, a release, one grain set against the last grain. Add. The hollow would not hear a word. It would only continue.

Toward the dim end of the day the presence would thin the way the films thin, back into the plain, back into the tin sky, leaving no altar and no scar. The heron would shift its weight and be a shape. The shafts would breathe out their own warm sweetness, the only wind the room reliably has. Somewhere in the upper courses a pellet would still be damp from a dew that lasted, unaccountably, a little past its hour—unaccountably only to a mind that needs accounts. The builders have no such mind. They would drink what was there, turn back to the seam, and carry the next mouthful to the lowest edge of the course.

If the power lingered one moment more, it would be to admit what benevolence often hates to admit: that the place did not need saving, and that needing nothing is not the same as being negligible. It drew attention by being exact. The exactness is the mound’s, the leaf’s, the guest-water’s, the short life that does not ask to be lengthened. To do more than keep the conditions kindly ordinary would be to move in and become a second climate, and they have already built the climate they can live in.

So it would leave them to it. The town under construction by touch. The lenses holding their coins. The land in the tense of becoming, added to, not yet taken from. A wise power, having looked, would let that be the whole of the miracle: that it had not improved them, and that they went on.
