They would not come down.

A power that is both wise and kind learns, before it acts, the difference between a wound and a form. This height is a form. The thinness is not a mistake the air is making; it is the condition under which these lives became what they are. To thicken it would be to unmake the wide rib, the close speech, the drum kept against the body, the way a nod can carry what a sentence would waste. To call water out of the stone in abundance would drown the counters, the joined vessels, the particular gravity with which a birth is housed. Benevolence that does not understand this is only a larger weather.

So the power does not repair the plateau. It attends to it.

Attention, from such a source, is not nothing. It settles the way heat settles into stone after a day of ordinary sun—not as an event the people could name, but as a slight lengthening of the hour in which things can still be seen. A pipe-walker, feeling along a joint by temperature alone, finds the metal a degree more honest than it was yesterday, the leak announcing itself before it costs a measure. A counter chalks a tank and the chalk does not skip; the number comes clean. Out on the salt, one of the long-legged animals turns its head a fraction earlier than the wind would explain, and the string behind it adjusts, and none of them steps into the slick place where the crust is false. These are not miracles. They are the sort of corrections a careful world already tries to make for itself, arriving a little more often, as if the world’s own caution had been given one extra hand.

The power watches the oldest of them, the ones whose breath is a soft bellows and who spend it on nothing unnecessary. It does not speak to them. Speech would require them to answer, and answering would spend what they have so precisely kept. Instead it lets the hush of the city include, for the length of one evening, a pressure like company—the sense that the outer wall is being regarded from a distance that does not need to cross the plateau to arrive. One of them pauses at the parapet. The pause is brief. They do not know why they lingered. They go in to sleep, and their sleep is the same sleep, only less easily mistaken, by whatever part of a person keeps the count of empty roads, for the sleep of the already-forgotten.

For the children under the wagons, it does still less. Their games of pitch and balance are already complete. To improve them would be to admit that thin air had been a teacher with a flaw, and the power does not believe this. It only sees to it that the warm dark beneath a particular wagon holds, on a night when a child is learning not to fear the many-legged things, exactly long enough for the lesson to finish. The child comes out dustier and unafraid. No one marks the night as different.

There is one thing it refuses to withhold, because withholding it would be the one cruelty available to a power that has chosen not to intervene. It does not let the place be unremembered in the only sense that matters to it—the sense in which a thing can continue and still be, from the outside, as if it had not. The power keeps the plateau in mind the way the counters keep a level: accurately, without romance, and without the intention of spending it down. Should the foundries one day cool past recovery, should the tanks fail in a sequence the people cannot walk ahead of, should the roads between the cities lengthen into something the messengers cannot cross and still return, the power will already know the shape of what was lost. It will not have to reconstruct them from dust. That knowledge is not a rescue. It is the assurance that their particular way of continuing was not a draft the universe discarded before reading.

Most days it does nothing even this visible. The light stays thin. The hammer arrives as a thought. The mineral faces take their decades. The people breathe, and spend the air, and do not hurry.

A wise benevolence can bear to be unnecessary. It can love a thing precisely by declining to become its weather. So it remains at the height where heights are judged, looking at a country that has already finished hurrying, and it lets the country go on being the reason it was right to look, and not to descend.
