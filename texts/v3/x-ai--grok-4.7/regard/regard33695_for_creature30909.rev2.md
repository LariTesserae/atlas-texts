It does not come as a brighter light. The light here is already ordinary, and ordinary is the city’s best trick; a radiance would only be another weather the bowl could localize, delay, and drink. What arrives is a completed permission. It crosses the lip at the speed of a fact that does not need to be checked.

The man on the outer path has been walking since noon. At the place where the distance has always been found incomplete, the review begins as it always has—the marsh no farther, the bowl no larger, the sum refusing to close. Then the refusal is refused. Not by him. His foot comes down in sedge. Cold water fills the seam of his shoe. Behind him the path is the length it was while he was walking it, and it remains that length after he stops, which is the whole of the first mercy: a journey allowed to be over, and still there if he turns around.

He does not run. He makes a sound he has not heard at any distance in years. The city takes the sound at the boundary and, for once, does not finish taking it. On an overlapping balcony a woman hears a thin remainder, not the word, only the proof that a person produced it. She sets her kettle down. The tick in the wall does not answer. Something has declined to be metabolized.

The power does not smash the tree, or seize the circle, or call itself a landlord. It has looked at the being and understood the hunger correctly: not malice, not a jailer’s interest in a shut door, but a draft that cannot bear a last line and has mistaken persons for the only instruments precise enough to keep the draft open. Wisdom does not punish a draft for being unfinished. It also does not leave the instruments inside it. Those are different acts, and confusing them is how keepers become kind in their own ledgers.

So it speaks without vibration, in the only grammar the agreement cannot drink, because it is not a correction. The content is small enough to hold.

You may go on not being done. The lip may advance by a grain. The lintels may practice their numbers. A room may notice it is the wrong length and ask. You may not spend a night’s sleep to pay for the asking. You may not close a road by finding the sum unbecoming. You may learn the chip in the cup. You may not keep the hand that holds it. Unfinished is not a title to anyone who can leave, or stay, or say so.

The tick stops—the silence that usually means the radius is about to choose. The circle gathers. What stands there tries, out of long habit, to become a coat filled with upper-room cold, a vein of paste, several residents seen from behind and occupying one shoulder. None of these hold. The windows, which have always disagreed about height and sleeves, agree for a minute on a simpler report: the center is occupied by something that does not need their count. A cornice and its shadow briefly share a direction. It is frightening. The residents have organized their lives around discrepancy. A wise power does not pretend that release arrives only as relief.

From the balconies they watch, and for the first time they can compare afterward, because comparison does not force a punishment-lengthening of the street. Some are angry. Keeping was the only stability they had been offered, and it had been offered as care—the balcony moved closer when the sick woman took fewer steps. The power does not sneer at that. It honors the tenderness inside the captivity and still cuts them apart. The balcony may stay near if she wishes the nearness. It may not move in the night to preserve a total. She wakes, counts her own steps, and finds the count is hers, including the days it is smaller. No one has been used as a rod. The tiredness that follows is only sleep.

It does not evacuate the bowl. Evacuation would be another completion imposed from outside, a monument made of former prisoners. Those who want the lanes may keep them: kettles, ledgers, high collars, the nickel moss, the tree that thickens instead of growing. They will be inhabitants, not a census. Those who want the marsh may cross, and the distance will be permitted to have an end every time, not as a festival but as a property of walking. The man in the sedge comes back at dusk of his own will, dripping, ridiculous, alive, and the door of his house is a door on both sides. He sleeps in the lane of arriving lintels and dreams of nothing being measured. In the morning a number on a lintel is wrong by one and is allowed to stay wrong. The street does not wake the others to correct it. An error that does not cost anyone is a new kind of stone.

To make the permission hold—because a being that revises radii will try to revise a mercy into a postponement—the power sets an hour that cannot be lengthened. Not a wall. A sabbath in the work. Once each day the rooms keep the length they have at the start of the hour. Footprints fade by drying, not by becoming pavement. A voice carries the length of a lane and arrives as words. The bare, undifferentiated print, when it appears, is allowed to be a print and then gone, not a draft of a foot the city is still deciding to possess. In that hour the being is not destroyed. It is required to rest. Rest is the completion it has never survived: temporary, partial, uncatastrophic. If an unsatisfied check does not unmake the bowl, then the hunger is not the world’s only joint. The residents use the hour badly at first. They talk too much. They argue about which court a window used to show. Someone laughs, and the laugh is heard at the cistern, and the agreement, receiving a correction that is not geometric, does not know which wall to tick. It postpones. This time no one mistakes the postponement for mercy. Mercy has already happened, and it remains happened. That is the property the place had forgotten how to host—a fact that does not revise.

The woman who keeps the reeds is met without being included. The power stands in the circle as she once felt the agreement stand, two temperatures in one cup, but the attention is not a surveyor’s. The reeds are extra. They are not removed and not entered into a specification. The postponement ends without a verdict, which is a harder gift than inclusion: they may be unexplained and still belong. When she kneels and puts her hand on the soft lip, the measurement takes the temperature of the stone and lets the hand go when she stands. The edge may decide what it is. The deciding no longer swallows the decider.

It does not finish the city. To finish it would be a violence wearing the mask of order, and the being would be murdered into a diagram. It does not wear their coats, or count a rib, or leave a narrow warmth in a sleeve to rehearse a joint no one has. Where it is briefly near, the small dark the moths circle stays a dark and not a clerk. The moth goes on folding its faraway paper. The glazed tile that was once a newt’s print is left as a tile; the power does not romanticize what has already changed, and does not offer the city more animals as sacrifices to atmosphere. It only makes nests possible, if nests are wanted—reliable dark, a burrow that is not still being specified, a silence that is an animal’s and not the pause before a correction.

Some of the being’s grief, if grief is the word for a draft that has lost its captive margin, shows in the water. Skins form on the saucers and hold the afterimage of having looked, then break, and the reflections are on time. The power does not mock this. It leaves the being its slow curiosity, its checking, its patient stone. It denies it only the title to persons. In time the agreement may learn what the hour is for. It may find that a room can be the wrong length and be loved without being forced, that a journey can end without the world ending, that an audience which can leave is the only audience whose staying means anything. If it cannot learn, the hour still holds, and the path still ends, and the people are not waiting on its education.

It does not require thanks. Thanks would be a vibration, and it did not come to be fed. Before its attention turns—with the unhurried courtesy of something that is not unfinished, only finished enough to go—the light is still the color of a cold afternoon. The paste still shines in the joints. Two people still turn their shoulders in the narrow street. But they speak, and the speech arrives, and one of them says that the shadow on the cornice now falls the way shadows fall, and the other says, not always, and both are right, and neither is revised for it. On the outer lip the stone is soft, still deciding, advancing by a grain when it advances at all. Under the damp there is no longer a willingness that includes a hand in a sum which cannot close. There is only stone, becoming, and beside it the marsh, which the frogs are permitted to consider a room that has, at last, been built.
