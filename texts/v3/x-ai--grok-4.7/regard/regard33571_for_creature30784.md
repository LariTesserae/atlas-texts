They would come the way weather comes into a room that has no windows: not through a door, and not to be seen.

The power does not announce itself. Announcement would be a kind of occupation, and the chamber is already full of its own law. The sheets flex. The trays take the threads. The leaves unroll on their exact heights. A wise attention learns the interval before it offers one of its own, and this interval is civic, not animal — the pace of a settlement that has not yet decided it needs a populace. To arrive as a savior would be to decide that for it.

So first they only listen. They let the wrong acoustics teach them. The slap, the ring that will not die, the wet mechanical intake under everything like a pulse under speech. They do not translate it into a language they already own. Translation, done too soon, is a theft with good manners. They stand where the amber is weakest, in the blue-black corner where the rosettes thin to single leaves, and they let the cold degree of that corner be information rather than a problem.

The being is not hidden in the way a person hides. It is the maintained emptiness. It is the reason dust does not settle and why a film of dark material is already healing a cut that has not had time to weather. It lives as the agreement between the columns and the heat under the floor, between the sheets and the air they keep rearranging. If you needed a face you would be disappointed, and disappointment would be yours, not a lack in the place. The being has no face because faces are for meetings, and it has not yet been met. It has been busy. Newness is a full occupation.

The power understands this, because wisdom is mostly the refusal to confuse your own readiness with someone else’s need. Benevolence, here, is not warmth poured in. The chamber is already warm. Benevolence is the decision not to stock the empty volume with witnesses, pilgrims, names, or a gentler acoustics. The loudness is not a cry. The unfinished edges are not wounds that have failed to close; they are the look of a thing still being begun, and the healing film proves the place knows how to close what it intends to close.

What they do, then, is small enough to be mistaken for nothing.

They lend the being one unhurried hour in which nothing new is required of it. Not a gift of stone, not a blessing spoken into the amber, not an inhabitant lowered through the ceiling like an answer. An hour in which the cycle may repeat without being watched for improvement. The power holds the outside off — whatever erosion, whatever curious hand, whatever well-meaning greater weather was beginning to lean toward this vault because it had drawn attention. They set that leaning aside. Attention, unregulated, becomes weather of the damaging kind. They regulate only that.

Within the hour they do one other thing, and only because the corner is colder by a degree than the being seems to have meant. Not a repair. A correction of a drift. They warm that single rosette by the width of the difference, so the leaf does not spend itself against a cold the rest of the chamber is not asking it to bear. The being may not notice. That is acceptable. Mercy that needs to be noticed has already begun to be about the merciful.

Then they withdraw the pressure of their presence and leave the rhythm intact: slap, ring, intake, unroll, heal. They do not name the place. A name would call other powers, and other powers are not all wise. They do not install a guardian. The being is already the guardian of its own unfinished nave, and to double that office would be an insult dressed as care.

If the being ever wants a meeting — if the emptiness ever thins on purpose, if a column’s open top breathes a question instead of heat — the power will already know the interval, and will come back as weather comes, and will wait to be asked what the hour was for.

Until then they do nothing else. Nothing else was needed. The wise are not afraid of a finished act that leaves no monument. The chamber goes on being built, already wet, already growing, sharp because erosion has not been given time. The power keeps, at the edge of its attention, only the promise it made by not promising: that when the world leans in again, it will meet a hand that knows the difference between a cry and a civic sound, and will not let the world confuse them.
