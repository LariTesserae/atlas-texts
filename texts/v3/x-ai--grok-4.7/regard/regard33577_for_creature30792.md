They would not open it.

That is the first and almost the only decision, and it is made before arrival, the way a hand decides not to touch a film of dust that has become the only record of a still room. A power that can see the height also sees the drop. The valve under the dish is not a prison door. It is the floor of the world. To call the opening mercy would be to rename annihilation, and wisdom, if it is anything, is the refusal to flatter itself with a kinder word.

They come anyway. Not as weather — the room already has weather, and it is exact — and not as a body the water would have to make space for. They arrive as a change too slight to be a cycle: the cool wall one shade less cold on the night side, the summit light through the lens holding its clarity a fraction longer at the edge of the pass, the note in the sealed pipe dropping by a pressure the grazers do not rasp against. The room does not look up. Nothing here has the equipment for looking up. The power accepts that. Being seen is not the gift. The gift is the accurate sight.

What they see is not a mistake.

The empty middle is not loneliness waiting to be furnished. The columns tile it because crowding fouls the filters, and the filters are how the columns exist. A power that added companions would be adding corpses with an extra step. The beads locked to both lights are not praying. They are using the only two temperatures the room has ever offered as a single signal, and the signal is true: when both arrive, the weather that crops them is less likely to be passing over. To give them one kinder light would unmake the clamp that keeps them. The lurkers are not evil in the throats. They are the reason a drift does not become a fog, and the fog is the thing that would sand the jet, stall the valves, and end the broth. Even the smears on the far alloy — green-black, old, not growing threads — are instruction, written in the only script the water taught. A wise power reads them and does not scrub them clean for the sake of a prettier wall.

They look longest at the oldest grazer, because that is the being the room has, if it has one: the joined bridge in the dish, two tongues, one pavement, the habit of stopping one rasp-length short of the singing metal. There is no face to lift. There is a record. Scar where a lamp tanned the rind. Crust that is also a weather log. A life arranged so the note under the floor stays under it and not in it. The power could split the sinter. Could return two animals to the dish and call it healing. The tear would be in living rind. The two tongues would not know they had been separate, only that the floor had suddenly hurt in a shape they had no behavior for. Nothing in their inheritance lasts longer than clinging, rasping, and the taste of metal. Grief would have to be imported, and imported grief is just another broth with the wrong chemistry.

So they do not heal what is not asking, and they do not free what is not enclosed, and they do not teach. Teaching would require a listener with a span longer than a slam. The columns inherit a lean. The beads inherit a clamp. The sheaths inherit a reversal timed to noise. The vane-shells inherit a lie that keeps them alive. These are already complete educations. A further lesson would be a human vanity worn by something that should know better.

What, then, do they do?

They stay through more than one cycle, which is the minimum courtesy in a room where meaning is repetition. They hear the pump as the animals hear it: not as neglect, not as a god, as the condition. They watch the jet cross the upper third and become, for its minute, the only wind this world will ever have. They watch the milk of larvae occupy one corner and not the volume, and they do not multiply it. Abundance here is already the correct abundance. More would be a flood of the wrong kind — the kind that fills the wilderness the equilibrium consented to keep.

They notice the feed line, which is the one part of the room that still reaches somewhere else. The broth arrives on a timer older than the present shapes of mouths. Somewhere in the works a valve still answers, a basin still leaches, a pump that is not this pump still spends itself. The power does not introduce itself to that somewhere. They only confirm that the pulse is not failing by a hair the room cannot yet taste. If there is an act, it is that small: the timer kept true, the seal’s flaw delayed by another age of grain-widths, the lamp supply left on the same circuit as the impeller so the two lights continue to mean what the beads require them to mean. Maintenance was already a property the system had. They do not replace the property with a caretaker. They refuse to become a face the water would have to learn, and then miss, and then die of missing.

Outside, above the lens, the summit air is thin and hard and uninterested. They do not open a crack to let it in. Air would be a surface, and a surface would be a shore, and a shore would be the end of every mouth that grew up believing rising was only a direction toward lamps. The high wind can keep worrying the hull. The hull’s answering note is part of the loudness that means the world is present. Silence, here, would be the louder catastrophe.

They consider the joined grazers one last time. Not to bless them. Blessing is a surplus the dish cannot store. Only to understand that the bridge is the room’s single unintentional monument, and that monuments are not improved by explanation. Two bodies that did not let go because letting go would tear. A power that loves goodness more than its own gesture leaves the monument in the silt and lets the next veil of sediment cover it as the jet covers everything, incompletely, on schedule.

Before they withdraw they do one thing that is almost nothing. In the slack after the slam, when the ribbons lift off the horizontal and begin again to write the current that has not changed its angle since form stopped advancing, they let the sweetness in the upper third arrive a breath early. Not a new chemical. The same broth, the same metal, only the lean made slightly easier for the column that has gone the color of crust and is failing its corrections. It finds the taste. It turns its gut back toward the motors. It will fail on a later cycle; nothing in the room is promised a longer shape than the cycle can use. But it is not this slam that unmakes it, and the crumbs the beads would have taken are deferred by one weather. The other columns do not notice. The power does not count this as salvation. It is the sort of adjustment a wise attention makes when it can do so without teaching the water a new law: a private kindness with no doctrine attached, small enough to drown in the ordinary pulse, gone before the outlet opens.

Then they leave the emptiness empty.

The room continues. Slack, pulse, jet, slam, slack. The cool wall writes and is erased. The oldest grazer stops one rasp-length short of the note and does not know it was spared a visitor. The vane-shells click and seat. The sheaths rest in their plant-like lie. Two shadows fall where they have always fallen, precise and harmless, across manufactured crust that will look the same for as long as the seal holds.

A lesser power would have improved this. A hungrier mercy would have opened the valve and called the falling a release. What attends instead is large enough to see a finished fit and not be insulted by it. They do nothing that would require the lives to become anything else. The becoming is over. What remains is the warm ordinary light, the next taste of metal moving through the upper third, right on time, and a seal that sings under the dish because something, once, looked at the height and chose to keep the world on this side of it.
