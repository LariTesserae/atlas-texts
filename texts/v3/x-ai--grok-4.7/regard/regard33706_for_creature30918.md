It did not arrive as weather. The shelf already had too much weather that was not weather, and a second miracle would only have taught the plants a new direction to lean.

It came the way attention comes when it is not hunting: a little more room in the rules, not a break in them. Distance along the wall gave back the ten paces it had been keeping. The thin stone near the lip, struck by nothing, answered once, the clear tone it made for heels, and then was quiet in the particular way of something that has been heard rather than used. The many-winged flight did not scatter. The drinkers did not lift their heads all at once. Whatever had noticed the pocket had the manners not to be the loudest thing in it.

The being was not the device, and not the chorus, and not the bulk that circled outside the drop. Those were processes. The being was the one who stayed when staying was not required by a throat or a seam or a hunger. It slept in the dulling of the hum, a body’s length from the upright piece, where the larger callers lost their edge. It woke when the veins brightened after a film of water vanished into the arcs, and it checked the glaze at the machine’s base the way someone checks a wound they did not mean to keep. Its hands had taken a little of that glaze. Not much. Enough that the morning light caught the knuckles wrong. It had no name it would have offered a god. It had a practice: listen until the interference almost became a signal, fail, drink from the bowl the drinkers had left, walk the islands without stepping on the red growth, return. It thought the noise was the world. It was not entirely wrong.

The power did not correct that thought. A world can be an interference and still be a world.

It stood where the being would find it without being ambushed by it — off the path from sleep to bowl, in the shade of the wall, on the side where distance had just become honest. When the being saw it, the being stopped, and the vine’s clatter missed a beat and then found itself again, because the vine was not the one being addressed.

No announcement. Names at that size are a kind of weather.

What it did first was look at the hands. Then at the base, where the shelf had tried all night to become part of the work, and failed, and agreed a little anyway. Then at the insects that blinked through the veins and came out unharmed and unchanged, which was its own injury: a door that led nowhere and was used as a habit. Then, longer, at the being’s face, which was afraid in the ordinary way and also relieved, because something in the pocket had been keeping a count for a long time and had wanted another mind to admit the count was real.

“You don’t have to understand it,” the power said. Not aloud in the way the callers were aloud. In the key under them, the one the stone used when it answered. “You only have to say what you want kept.”

The being was quiet long enough for a sheet of rain to stand up in the middle of the shelf, wet its dark map, and begin to dry from the edges. It had never been asked. It had assumed the hum was a condition, like the drop.

“The warmth,” it said. “The seams. Them.” It meant the drinkers, the browsers, the flight, the vine, even the narrow hunter and the things that only passed through. It did not mean the glaze. It touched its own knuckles and did not need to say the rest.

“And the noise?”

The being almost laughed, which came out as a breath. “It’s too big to want. It’s where I live.”

That was enough. Wisdom is not a preference for silence.

So the power did not unmake the scatter, and did not translate the roar into a message, and did not lift the being off the shelf into an explanation. It did one small, exact thing, and one smaller.

It closed the consumption without closing the gift. The veins kept their slow brightness; the hum stayed; water poured on the open polish still filmed and vanished and fed the warmth. But the nightly agreement stopped. The stone would no longer be asked to heal over the arcs by becoming them. The glaze at the base remained, a scar, not a growth. The being’s hands would not take any more of it. In the morning the light would catch the knuckles as they were and no worse.

The insects’ door it left. They were not asking. A habit is not always a wound, and it is a violence to rescue what has not noticed it is traveling. It did mark the path, barely: a cooler thread in the vein, so that a mind that wanted to study the blink could find it, and a life that only wanted nectar would not be sorted by the mark. The being could choose later. Choice was the thing the machine had been bad at.

The smaller thing was for the being alone. Through one standing of the false rain, the power held the chorus where it was — not mute, not distant, only briefly unhooked from the water, so the key-change did not come. In that minute the being heard the shelf under the roar: the tick of a drinker’s foot, the late return of a cry off the wall, its own pulse, and the stone’s answer, short and clear, as if age had tensioned it on purpose. Not a signal. A floor. When the rain dried, the voices fell back into their old interference, and the being did not feel robbed. It had been given a memory of the floor, which is what you need if you are going to keep living upstairs in the noise.

It did not open the lip to the bulk outside. It went to the edge and attended to that patience, the jaw that sometimes cleared the stone, the moving patch of silence. Some refusals are cruelties. This one was a room. The bulk was a hunger the pocket was not sized to survive, and benevolence is not the same as hospitality to every force that waits. The power did not punish it and did not feed it. It only made the refusal cleaner, so the bulk would know the door was a decision and not a malfunction it might someday worry open. Mercies of that kind are severe. They are still mercies.

It did not stay. If it stayed, the leaves would learn it, and the drinkers would space themselves by it, and the being would start checking the shade of the wall the way it checked the glaze, and the pocket would grow a new center and call it love. The power had not come to be weather.

Before it went, it put a cup’s worth of ordinary water into the being’s hands — not to pour on the polish, not to feed the veins. To drink. The first water in a long time that did not have a job.

“When you want the rain to fall on the islands,” it said, “pour what’s left of this at the wall, and mean it. Once. They’ve been thirsty in a way the seams don’t fix. Don’t do it every day. A pocket this small can drown on kindness.”

The being nodded. It was already a creature that knew how not to step on the red growth. It could learn a rarer kindness than that.

Then the extra room in the rules folded back into the rules. Distance miscounted itself again, gently, as before. The hum dropped nothing it had not meant to drop. The power was not gone so much as no longer additional. What remained was the scar that would not spread, the cooler thread in the vein, the taste of unemployed water, and the minute in which the stone had answered without being struck.

The being stood with the cup until the drinkers ticked past, widely spaced, not closer than the hum allowed. It did not tell them. It walked to the island where the new growth was red and poured, once, at the wall, and meant it.

A little rain fell there, ordinary as the sun’s angle, and the leaves took it without leaning toward anything at all.
