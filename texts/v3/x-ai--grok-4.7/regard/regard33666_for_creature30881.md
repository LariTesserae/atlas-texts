They would not enter.

A power that is wise does not confuse attention with arrival, and a power that is benevolent does not spend its first kindness on being known. It would stand, in whatever sense such a power stands, outside the last bend of the gallery, where the sound is already weather and not yet a wall, and it would listen long enough to understand that the vault is not calling. The pressure in the stone is not a summons. It is a completed sentence the size of a world, and it has been complete since the river forgot itself.

What it would see is not lack. The mats hold their water. The mounds keep their climate. The flyers take paths that do not need a leader, and the lanes are occupied exactly to the density the reserve can bear. No mouth is open in hunger. No nursery is failing in a way the clack has not already begun to mend. A lesser kindness would come with rain, or with light enough to cast a hard shadow, or with a voice shaped to survive the curves. Any of those would be a theft described as a gift. Rain would unmake the locks. Hard light would teach contrast to eyes that have spent an age unlearning it. A voice that arrived whole would punch a hole in the only mind they have, and the city would spend centuries mending a wound it did not ask to receive.

So the power would do one thing, and it would look, from inside, like nothing.

It would set its attention on the approaches. Not as a gate, not as a seal — they have already sealed themselves, and a second seal would be a prison wearing the face of care — but as a slowing of whatever hurries in the rock above and the wet countries beyond. A crack that would have widened in a century widens in ten. A seep that would have found the old river-groove finds a longer way around, and arrives, if it arrives at all, as the same gray leak the roof has always given, no brighter and no wetter. The salt crusts are not defended. They are simply not hurried toward their end. Collapses that the mounds could not outpace are eased into slumps the builders already know how to read, a hand's width at a time, the shape of the repair already implied by the shape of the failure.

It would not announce this. Announcement is a wet habit. The flyers would not alter their turns by a finger's width. The tickers would not pause. If some outer change in the stone's fatigue were perceptible at all, it would be perceptible only as the vault continuing, which is the only miracle they are built to survive.

And then, because wisdom is not only restraint, it would leave one alteration so slight that calling it an alteration is already too much.

In the fallow plazas, where the mats have agreed not to stand, the cool of the pavement would be, in certain decades, a fraction fuller. Not damp. Not a seep. Only the memory of moisture held one degree longer in the stone before it returned to the air and was lost. The lane-travelers would not notice. The mound-builders would not change their vents. But over a span longer than their memory, a grove that would have retreated might hold its edge, and a granary that would have gone light might not, and the sound would not have to thin in a year when thinning would have cost them more than they could match back into themselves. They would not know they had been spared a thinning. Spared things that are never felt are the only gifts that do not become demands.

The power would not stay to be thanked, and would not remain in any posture they could mistake for a presence. To remain would be to become a hole that the weather must mend, and they have enough work. It would withdraw its face and leave its slowing behind, the way the river left its floor: not as a ruler, not as a parent, as a condition. If they ever grew a sense for such things, which they will not, the kindness would be indistinguishable from the stone having always been a little more patient than stone usually is.

That is what it would do, if anything. It would keep the outside from arriving faster than the inside can refuse it. It would not teach them they are loved. Love, here, is the decision not to become another sound in a vault that has already chosen its one sound, and to let that sound go on, hand's width by hand's width, for as long as the reserve consents to be a world.
