It did not come down the seam. The seam is a door, and doors are for those who must change rooms. Whatever had turned its attention toward the pocket was already on the same side of every wall as the water was. It had been, in the way the interval is already in the chest before the click arrives. Attention, for such a power, is not a visitor. It is a change in how completely a thing is held.

It looked, and the looking was exact. It saw the left wall weeping faster, and the platforms raised a thumb’s height to answer it, and no one calling that a decision. It saw the shell-blade and the basket of itself, emptied into a dark that does not give grit back. It saw the hollow that had kept a brother’s shape after it had forgotten his heat, and the child who slept there and called the stone kind. It saw the half-beat, years gone, still known in her teeth, and the two fingers on a boy’s wrist returning the count through skin. It saw that she had gone up once, been struck by silence, and come back before a day could turn, and that she had not wanted the surface since. Wanting thinned here. Fit remained.

A lesser kindness would have misunderstood the roar as a burden and taken it. Would have dried the walls, or lit the ceiling, or widened the seam until shoulders no longer had to turn, and called that mercy. A lesser wisdom would have admired the balance and then improved it, which is how balances die: a lip sharpened back to its first edge, a click pinned so it could never arrive early, oil that never needed a visitor, death that no longer had to be given to the under-dark. The pocket would have become a model of itself. Models do not weep at one rate. Models do not scrape.

It did not do those things.

What it did was smaller than a drop landing off its bowl, and it was not done to be seen.

It confirmed the socket.

Not forever. Forever would have been a theft, the same theft as pinning the click. It confirmed it the way a hand, resting near a stone that has settled, does not push and does not withdraw: the stone remains the one that chose the socket, and the socket remains willing. The next time a half-beat tried to be born—and it would try, because stone and water and living attention are not a clockwork, only a fit—the wrongness would still arrive in the teeth. She would still sit up. A child would still meet her eyes in the gray. They would still cross water they had never tripped in. And the correction, when it came, would still be the room’s own correction, not a rescue. Only this: the socket would not fail them while they were crossing. The drop would find the bowl before the story could form. Mention would remain unnecessary. The interval would stay something that had not happened twice.

That was for the place.

For her, it did one thing more, and then it stopped, because more would have been a surface habit.

On a night when she lay in the hollow and made the small courtesy toward the ceiling, the count arrived in her a fraction before the click, not early, not wrong—simply known, as if the room had leaned down and told her the truth she already kept: that she was not the last tool-mark only, not only the way the pocket continued, but also the one who had noticed when it almost did not. The power did not speak this. Speech would have had to cross the roar and would have arrived already partly claimed. It let the courtesy be answered. For the length of one interval, the ceiling-click was not only a count she kept. It was a count that kept her.

She did not wake the others. She did not give it a name. In the morning she heard a bowl beginning to dull, took the blade, and walked the lip until the pitch came back. The scrapings went into the basket. The channels ran at one volume. The seam stayed narrow, polished by shoulders, the whole world’s door, unused.

If she thought of anything, it was only that the hollow fit, and that the fit was not colder than usual, and not warmer, and that this was right. Benevolence, being wise, had wanted nothing added to her life. It had wanted her life to remain the kind that can be lived without looking up when her name is said, and still be entirely seen.

The power did not stay in the manner of a lamp, which is rationed and set in a niche and does not travel. It did not leave in the manner of a visitor, who speaks too loudly and carries away water that will taste ordinary and is not. It remained as the pressure remains when you have stopped noticing water at your chest: not a presence you could turn and thank, not an absence you could mourn. The many-legged things kept their routes. The under-dark took what it was willing to take. Somewhere a child slept in an accurate hollow and was not corrected.

That was all it did. It was enough, and it was not a story, and it did not need to happen twice.
