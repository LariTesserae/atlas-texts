The power did not come down the air. The air was already occupied, and anything new in it would have been read, correctly, as weather with an intention. It came the way the heat comes out of the culms in the late day: already in the fiber, already almost accounted for, a degree more of what the place was doing.

It had been watching longer than a season and less than an age. That is the only span in which watching this plateau teaches anything. Shorter, and you think the roar is an emergency. Longer, and you start to believe the empty halls are a sadness that wants filling. Neither is true. The roar is the stable state. The emptiness is a costume. A power that is only kind will try to mend both, and the mending will be the first loud, new thing this country has had in a long time. New sounds travel. Everything listening understands them as blood.

So it did not still the flocks. It did not thicken the herds at the horizon into something a walker could safely approach. It did not close the sour doors the insects open in the felt, or teach the long hunter a mercy the hunter cannot spend, or put metal in the hands of people whose grammar is stake and knot and the refusal to dig. Digging weeps. A gift that weeps is not a gift here. It is an announcement.

What it did was smaller than a miracle and harder, which is the usual ratio.

It learned the lean of the stakes before it leaned on anything. A single stake, west, means the pocket has moved. Two, crossed, mean the floor beyond is a story you should not finish with your weight. It did not add a third sign. It did not correct a knot it did not yet speak. In one district the hanging bundles meant *leave the cups*; two days further they meant *the rib is a road at dawn and a lie at noon*. The power waited until it could tell those apart. Wisdom that cannot read the local caution is only strength with a soft voice.

Then it spent itself on delays.

A gallery that had been meaning to fall since before the stones were carried was let to mean it for one night more. Not propped with a wonder. The knitting in the fallen vault beneath was hurried by a warmth the culms already knew how to be — blood-warm, sun stored in fiber — so that the scar held while the child who hears early crossed on the back foot, hand not quite touching the wall, because touch is sometimes louder than a step. The child did not feel a hand. There was no hand. In the morning the gallery went down in an empty hour, with a sound the roosts already had a wordless answer for, and the crossed stakes someone had set the day before were suddenly right in a way that looked like ordinary caution. The power counted that as clean. To be thanked, it would have had to be seen. To be seen, it would have had to stand in the air or on the mat as a darkness that was not a herd and not a cloud. The people would have made a knot for it. The knot would have drifted, as knots do, until five days out it meant something else, and someone would have trusted a floor because a god had once stood on a similar one.

It kept a water-pocket from concentrating, once, through an afternoon that would have turned the last of the drink bitter. Not a spring. The plateau has finished with springs. Only a slowing of the tick, so that midday lasted a few breaths longer than the sun had budgeted, and the scoop of peeled fiber came up clear. The one who reads the west knots drank, and did not look up to see why. Looking up is how you miss the half-beat.

It did not raise the dead in the closed rings. The stones were a witness, not a lock. Opening them would have taught the scavengers a new interest in rings, and taught the living that a body is a thing that might be argued back out of the felt if you are loud enough. This place cannot afford that lesson. The power, being wise, was willing to be the kind of benevolence that arrives too late for the already taken, and does not pretend lateness is a mystery. It stood — if a warmth can be said to stand — outside one closed ring at the hour when the mat gives back a duller version of a voice, and it did not speak a name. Names said too often become stakes left to mark a thing that will move.

There was a night when the temptation was precise. The flocks lifted in the dark for no reason anyone had knotted, and the surf came back over the scars, and a shelter's outer platform was empty because the one whose turn it was had gone down to the pocket with a bad thirst and not come back in time. Under the roar, something long had left its lie. It was not hurrying. It never hurries except in the last body-length. A kind power, a merely kind one, would have set a light, or a fear, or a wall of wind. Any of those would have saved the sleeper and ended the listening. The people sleep with their ears uncovered because privacy is a kind of death here. A wall that keeps the presser out also keeps the floor's decisions out. In a year they would not hear the half-beat-early lull. In five, the children would cover their ears and call it rest.

So the power did what the mat itself does when it accepts a weight. It took the print. It laid a warmth the size of a long body in the dust between the platform and the rib the hunter was using, a straw-colored nothing, drying already, not a shape you could worship. The hunter, whose whole art is the reading of wrong color and soft friction, paused. The pause was not awe. It was the file's pause, the runner's pause: something ahead had stopped, and stopping is information. In that information the one with the bad thirst made the scar, not heroic, spilling water, furious at themselves, alive. The hunter reversed. The whole night reversed with it, the way a file turns on a rib, patient as courtesy. By dawn the false lie-up was only warmer dust, and the condensation beaded on it the same as on everything else, and the power was gone out of that square of mat because to remain would have been to become a place, and places here get stakes.

It left the insects their revisions. It left the meat in parentheses. It left the halls empty and the costume intact. A wise benevolence does not improve a world past the point where its people can still recognize the sound of it.

What it gave, if giving is the word, was a handful of ordinary survivals that did not need a shrine to explain them: a floor that held until the crossing was done and failed after; a pocket that stayed drinkable through one cruel afternoon; a hunter that turned because the ground ahead had the wrong stillness, not because a god had forbidden the kill. The people, who are not mystical and not fools, noticed the texture of those days. They did not hang a new knot. They said, in the next lull, news first, that the west pocket had held and the gallery had gone down clean, after. Names they kept for the inside of the callus.

The power did not ask to be loved. Love, in a country this audible, is a long carrying sound, and the plateau has enough of those. It asked only the thing it had already practiced, which was to be slightly more of the warmth the fiber knew, and then to withdraw before the withdrawal itself became a season the knots would have to record.

On the outer platform, some nights after, the one who stayed awake felt the culm against their shoulder blood-warm past the hour when the heat should have left it. They did not wake the others. They shifted their weight to the back foot. They listened. The grain of the night was the grain they knew: creak, click, friction, tick. Nothing in it asked for an answer. That was the benevolence, finally — not a voice in the hall, not a darkness at the crease of the land, but a world that had been, for a few careful intervals, a little less willing to spend them, and that had done it without becoming a different world.

In the morning the sheets peeled out of the gallery throats. The runners tested the night's new lips. The power, if it was still anywhere, was indistinguishable from the sun stored in the straw-colored tubes, which is where a wise thing goes when it is finished: back into the ordinary heat, which never became a fire, and never forgot the living flatness it had briefly, and so lightly, taken the side of.
