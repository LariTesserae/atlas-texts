A wise power, drawn toward a place and a being it has not yet been shown, does nothing that would change them.

It does not invent the basin so that it may have something to bless. It does not furnish a creature so that mercy will have an object. Attention that arrives ahead of sight is only hunger wearing a kinder name, and kindness that cannot bear an empty hand will put its own face on whoever is missing.

So it waits where the description should have been. It keeps the quiet from being filled. If there is a lip of stone, a sheet of water, a life that has already learned how not to report itself, none of that is known yet, and what is not known is not improved by being guessed. The oldest courtesy available to power is to leave a shape alone until the shape has actually appeared.

What it would do, if anything, is this: stay. Not enter. Not correct. Not warm the rim by a degree in case warmth is wanted. A being that has not been met cannot be helped without first being replaced by the helper’s idea of it, and replacement is not a small violence just because the intention is tender.

When the place is actually set down — the light, the rules, the one who lives inside them — then attention can become sight, and sight can decide whether action would be a gift or only another weather imposed from outside. Until then the benevolent thing is restraint. The wise thing is the same.

Paste the place, and the being. Then there is something to answer.
