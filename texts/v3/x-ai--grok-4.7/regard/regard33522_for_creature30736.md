The power does not come down the stair.

The stair was cut for weight and repetition, and the dishes in the brick are a record of both. Anything that needed those treads in order to arrive would already have the scale wrong—would be thinking of the court as a destination rather than as a set of local rules that happen to be in a hole. So it attends from the disk of sky, and from inside the warmth the mass already holds, and it is careful not to become another surface. The swifts do not tick it. The knock-birds do not add it to the count. A benevolence that announced itself in that bowl would be a new weather, and the tenants have already signed their agreement with the weather they have.

It looks a long time, which is not the same as doing.

What it sees first is the temptation, and it sets the temptation down. The court can be read, by a hurried kindness, as a list of lacks. No soil deeper than a crack. No stream for the fish that should not be there. No roof. A keeper who comes rarely and drinks water that tastes of lime and old pins and makes the same face every time. A finger of stone that will, without drama, meet the floor and close a little of the volume. A technology that never rose. Green only where it must be. Sound that cannot leave, so that every quarrel is larger than the quarrel.

None of these are wounds. They are the terms. To give the sedge a depth would be to invite a mat, and a mat would take the film the moss is stitching, and the moss is the only green the lip consented to. To cut a stream into the north trough would end the scandal of the fish by explaining them, and they have outlived explanation; a river would also take the overflow lips out of authority, and the lips are the law the basins still obey. To roof the disk would keep the rain from sheeting down the stair and would silence the swifts’ corrections against the brick, and then the court would be a room instead of a bowl that breathes. To still the knock-birds would be to steal the count by which the walls know they are walls. To hurry the stalactite, or to break it off as a kindness to the pier, would be to declare that patience is a defect. The power is wise enough to recognize a finished thing when the finish is not shine but use. The place is not waiting to be completed. It was completed by being occupied.

So it does not improve the interior. Improvement is the shape its power would take if it were only powerful.

It looks for the one fragility that is not part of the agreement.

Inside the disk, the rules hold. Water goes where the cuts allow. Light comes from above and stops at the floor. The person, when they come, clears the joint that is about to dam a fall, and the animals treat that labor as climate. The interior is already being kept by the order of means that suits it: scoop, broom, cord, a curse, a drink taken twice. What the court cannot do—what the knock-birds cannot count, because it never enters the bowl—is notice the country beyond the rim. From the pavement that country is irrelevant, a brightness above a disk. It is not irrelevant to the disk. A quarry can walk to the lip. A roof of heavier making can be laid over an excavation that looks, to anyone who has not stood in the plain light at the center, like a hole that wants filling. A well driven in the unseen ground can pull the seep until the drip forgets its courses and the three waters become tile again. The frogs have a duty for rain of a certain grain. They have no duty for a flood that overruns the lips, and no duty for a drought that is not weather but extraction. The agreement ends where the country begins to want the brick for something else.

That is where the power does its work, and the work is mostly a refusal to let the court become interesting.

It does not hide the place. Hiding would be a glamour, and a glamour is a technology higher than pins, and the next keeper would feel it as a wrongness in the stair, a step that did not report itself truly. Instead it lets the country go on failing to see a use. Surveyors, when they come near, find a grade that is not worth fighting—a soft bend in a line, a note in a hand that is not theirs, a decision already made by someone they would rather not revisit. The ground at the rim reads as occupied in the bureaucratic sense, which is the sense that stops quarries. Rain continues to arrive as rain, not as a sign, in the measure the channels already know how to worry into true grades. No one pumps the seep dry. The disk stays a disk. Irrelevance, preserved on purpose, is the shelter. The tenants never learn they were sheltered. That is the point. A kindness that enters the count has already cost the count something.

For the being, it is stricter with itself.

The person who still belongs to the place is not a spirit of the brick and not a priest of the fish. They are a pair of hands that know which joint is about to fail, and a face that drinks bad water twice, and a voice that speaks numbers to masonry. They change slowly, as the stalactite changes, one replacing another without a rite. A power that loved them the way stories love keepers would lengthen the one life until it glazed the piers blacker than guano—would make a keeper who cannot die, and a keeper who cannot die becomes a pin swollen past use, a repair that will not take the next repair. Wisdom declines that. The hands will fail. The face will stop being made. This is not a problem the court has failed to solve; the stair is dished by more than one generation, and the bald oval on the lip, where a bucket used to be set, is a teaching that outlasts the wrist that set it.

What it does for the being is small enough to be mistaken for the person’s own competence.

There is a pin, black and swollen, holding a slab that wants to walk. The person has cursed it and not brought the right tool, and will curse it again. On the next visit the pin gives—not out, not into a cleaner metal, not into a fitting that would announce a higher art—only a fraction, the kind of give that old iron sometimes has when the weather has worried it in the same direction as the curse. The slab can be seated again with a cord and a stone and the means already in the court. The person will think they finally struck it true. They will be right to think so. The power does not collect the credit. Credit, collected, would be a signature, and a signature in that bowl would be another niche that nothing living had agreed to fill.

It does one thing more, and then it stops. It does not put the knowledge of the joints into the person’s head as a vision. The knowledge is already in the brick’s behavior: the dam of glaze where a fall is slowing, the tick of the stalactite’s drop that has gone higher and smaller, the moss that will not cross the oval. It only sees to it that the next feet find the stair before a joint fails past the scoop’s authority—not led, not called, simply not prevented. A child of the country above, or a laborer sent to look at nothing in particular, comes down because the treads are there and the sound, halfway, is a kind of question the brick asks with other people’s voices. They will not understand the fish. They do not need to. They need to lift the film that is about to become a dam, and to drink, and to make a face, and to learn the oval by setting something down where the moss has already agreed to be absent.

Then the power leaves the interior alone so thoroughly that the leaving is not felt as an absence. It keeps its attention on the rim, which is the only place its attention is not a trespass, and on the country’s useful blindness. Inside, the drip continues its unfinishable arithmetic. The swifts correct against the batter. The knock-birds restart the count from zero after the feet have climbed past the turn. The heavier fish noses the overflow lip and is not granted a river. The lime goes on meeting the floor at the speed of climate.

If the power can be said to love the place, the love is this: it does not add itself to the tenants. It does not make the court louder, or safer than its own cuts, or holier than a bowl that works. It refuses the improvements that would have been sincere. It stands where the disk meets the country, and it keeps the irrelevant irrelevant, and it lets a swollen pin give one fraction under a human hand, and it lets the hand believe the fraction was earned.

That is what it would do. The rest of what it could do, it would not.
