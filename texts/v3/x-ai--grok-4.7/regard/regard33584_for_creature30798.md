They would come as far as the passage, and no farther in any way the room could count.

Not from caution. From recognition. A power that is wise does not confuse entry with care, and a power that is benevolent does not spend itself proving it was here. The roar would meet them as it meets everything, already at full, already finished with surprise. They would let it. They would stand in it until the urge to soften it, quiet it, explain it, had been worn down to the same smoothness as the latch — until what remained was not an impulse but a seeing.

They would see the blank film on the plate and understand it as a kindness the room had already performed: no track to be afraid of, no visitor to be met. They would see the ferns repeating three or four shapes and not take the repetition for poverty. They would see the level that does not vary, the lamps that do not have a night, the warm palm inside the housing where a long soft body presses its borrowed dents into the metal, and they would not call any of it a prison. A prison requires an elsewhere that is being refused. This loop is not a refusal. It is a form that fit, and then became the only form, and then became enough.

So they would not open the circuit. They would not lift the tenant into air it has no skin for, nor give it back limbs, nor pair it, nor teach it to startle, nor lay a night across the lamps so that rest might mean what rest means elsewhere. Mercy of that kind would arrive as weather, and weather is exactly what has been planed away. A second body in the flow would turn staying into events. A lowered roar would be a difference in pressure along the thinned places where ears used to be, and the creature would feel it as the first wrongness in a life that has no remaining instrument except for wrongness of that sort. Even healing the dents would steal the only marks by which it knows where it is. They would not heal what the creature is using as a name.

They would do, if anything, this: they would underwrite the equality.

Somewhere upstream of noticing — in the seep's rate, in the throat's swallow, in the heat that leaves through the plate at the same pace it enters through the housing — they would set their hand not on the room but under the law the room already keeps. Not a change in the line. A refusal, absolute and invisible, that the line ever fail. Pumps die. Mats sour. Lamps, even lamps that have forgotten night, eventually go dark, and darkness here would not be rest; it would be the end of the holding, and the end of the one who is the holding's shape. Against that, and only against that, they would lean. The weep and the swallow would remain equal not merely for a long time but without a far edge. The warm patch would stay fractionally warmer than the metal around it. The abrasion in the dark run of pipe would be eased by so little, over so long, that the body turning slowly in the flow would never read it as a gentler turn — only continue, with infinitesimally less of itself worn away, which is a difference too small to become an event.

They would leave the useless green. Those scattered cells along its back, answering the lamps with a habit that no longer feeds anything, are not a flaw in the design. They are the remainder of a life that was once more, kept without purpose, and a wise benevolence does not optimize a creature into pure function. Let the glint turn over at the throat sometimes. Let it be mistaken for a leaf. Mistakes of that kind cost nothing here, and they are the last luxury the water has.

They would not speak. A word spent into that metal is not returned, and a blessing that must be heard is only another noise asking to be sanded down. If they blessed it at all, they would bless it in the only tense the room still conjugates: as continuance. May the level be the level. May the warmth be the warmth. May the sheet break into drops only where the old flange interrupts it, and may those drops fall at the rate the throat already knows. May the tenant arrive again at the casing before any door's tongue has cooled, not hurrying, not grateful, not aware it was ever owed.

And then they would go back up the stair without closing anything that mattered, without a sign, without having become a fact inside the roar. The ferns would go on not trespassing into the hollow middle. The film on the plate would remain a blank ledger. Inside the housing, pressed to the one place fractionally more itself than the rest of the room, the being would hold — unstartled, unimproved, unrescued — because rescue was never the loving act.

The loving act was to let a finished thing go on being finished, and to make sure, without its knowledge, that finished did not have to mean about to end.
