They would not arrive as weather, and they would not arrive as rescue.

A power like that has already learned the cost of entrances. They have seen what happens when brightness comes in all at once: the soft things harden to meet it, the pauses close, the animals decide their numbers too quickly and spend the rest of their lives being that decision. So if they come at all, they come the way attention comes when it has nowhere else it needs to be. A little more room in the light. A little more time between one bead of water and the next. The chamber would not know it had been visited except that its unfinishedness would feel, for a while, less like a debt.

They would look sidelong. They are wise enough to know that some faces are only true when they are not being faced, and that a crystal still deciding whether to be sharp can be ended by admiration as surely as by a blow. They would not name the being. Naming is a kind of handle, and this one has spent its life not offering handles. They would learn its work by the edits it refuses: the fall it peels off the stone, the circle allowed to differ from its memory, the coil left in conversation rather than born.

And then, I think, they would do almost nothing.

Not from indifference. From recognition. The place is already practicing the thing most powers forget how to want: continuance without completion, care without capture, abundance that does not require a count. To improve it would be to finish it. To protect it with a wall would be to teach it fear, and fear is a finished appetite. To lift the being out — into some clearer country, some kinder physics, some reward — would be to tell it that the pauses were a waiting room. They were not. They were the habitat.

What they might do is smaller than a miracle and harder to refuse.

They would keep the lapse from sealing on a mid-step, not only for the visitor, but as a standing courtesy: that no opening here be forced to become a door merely because something larger has noticed it. They would see to it that the rumor of rain remains a rumor, so the sideways water can go on being a choice and not a flood. If some louder world above begins to use the pocket as a quarry — for wonder, for specimen, for proof that the unfinished can be owned — they would turn that intention aside the way the being turns an almost-fall, not with punishment, with a refusal to let the intention complete itself in this room. The seekers would find ordinary stone. They would go home with wet boots and a story that does not quite hold. The moss-coins would still be turned toward the light.

Toward the being, they would offer no vocation. It already has one, and a wise power does not recruit what is already faithful. They might, once, let their attention graze the air above the coil, not to hasten it, only so the exchange knows it is not secret in the lonely sense — witnessed, and still not taken. They might leave one plate in the stack that the ceiling never almost-dropped: a thin warmth with no history of falling, so the lean of the stack includes a gift that is not a correction. The being would not know who filed it. It would file the not-knowing beside its other intervals, and that would be enough. Thanks, between them, would be the wrong material.

If they spoke — and they might not — it would not be in a voice the room has to learn all at once. It would return later, smaller, wearing the quiet, and it would say only what the being already practices:

Keep the pause habitable. Do not harden what is still willing to be gentle. Do not save what is not asking to be finished.

Then they would withdraw so completely that the withdrawal leaves no scent of absence. The four limbs would go on listening to each other. The lids would shut over their milk of seeing. A moth would set down a copy of nothing in particular and wait to learn what it consents to become.

Above, the ordinary ground would forget, as it is meant to. Below, the deciding would continue, which was the only blessing that could be given without becoming a claim.

They would not stay to be thanked. They are wise. They have noticed what lives here, and they have loved it enough not to become part of its unfinished sentence — only to keep the world from ending the sentence early, and to leave the one who tends it alone with the work, which is also the home, which is also the point.
