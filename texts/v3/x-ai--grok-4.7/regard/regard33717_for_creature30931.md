They would come the way the seep comes: already present before anyone agreed there had been an arrival.

Not as a figure on the rim, and not as a voice the vapor would have to carry. A power that was only spectacle would miss the place entirely, because the place has no appetite for being witnessed in the loud way. What drew the attention was not a cry. Nothing here cries in a form that travels. What drew it was the fit—the long, unadvertised exactness of film to seep, of boot to engine-turn, of stain-line to the animals that will not cross it. Wisdom, if it is real, recognizes a finished bargain when it sees one. Benevolence, if it is real, does not confuse “finished” with “in need of improvement.”

So the first thing they would do is stop.

They would stand—if standing is the word for a regard that does not require a body—on no gantry in particular, and they would not announce themselves to the ledgers. They would learn the hands before they learned the pipes. A flat palm. Two fingers walked. A fist closed and opened under the floor. They would watch a keeper rise before the brightness hardens, latch the outer door without waking the compound, and walk the seep-lines with a rod and a book, matching a new crack to its twin half a kilometer off. They would understand that this is not prayer and not omen. It is maintenance of a skin that remembers. They would not add a mark of their own to the book.

They would look at the child who turns two crawlers on a hexagon until one pours off the edge, and they would not bless the game. Blessing would be a kind of shout. They would see that the laugh is a shake in the chest, and that this is enough joy for a world that does not carry sound, and they would leave the bone-tiles where they lie on the cistern lid.

They would consider, because wisdom considers, the obvious mercies.

They could thin the vapor so a voice could cross a compound yard. They do not. The people have already become the size of the quiet; to give them shouting back would strip them in front of each other. They could cool the pans so no calf ever takes the bad step. They do not. The bad step is the only death the accounting has, and the hide becomes soles and the bones become toys, and a death removed without a substitute would not be kindness. It would be a hole the plateau would have to invent another way to fill, and the invented way would be worse, because it would be sudden. They could make the rail unnecessary, or the coal, or the sweet film that numbs the tongue. They look at the chain and see that every link is also a reason someone wakes, someone scrapes, someone stays on the pressure through the brown dark. A power that loves life does not steal its errands.

They would feel, for a moment, the temptation that even the wise must feel in front of a hard place: to be thanked. To part the brass light and let the narrow wings speak in a register the ground could hear. To set one rain on the plateau that was only rain, lowland rain, so the sleepers who wake with their mouths open would have been right for once. They set the temptation down the way a scraper sets down a pail. The dream of rain is already a kindness the place allows itself. To fulfill it would turn a private embarrassment into a public weather, and the people would have to rearrange their souls around a miracle, and rearrangement is a violence here.

What they would do, then, is smaller than intervention and larger than absence.

They would sit inside the remembering. Where a crack opens at dawn and its twin opens on the far bench, they would be the slight easing in the stone that keeps the split from running under a pipe already laid. Not a prevention of the message—the walkers still find the pair, still mark the book, still know where not to build—but a shortening of the dangerous inch. No one sees a hand. The keeper feels only that the rod sank where it should, and that the day’s walk was no longer than usual.

They would be in the coil that sweats the drinking water, on the morning the sintered bed is half a day overdue and a tired hand is about to pour anyway. Not a voice. A taste, a little more metal than sweetness, early enough that the keeper spits, frowns, and changes the bed before the compound drinks numbness. Later that keeper will say the water warned them. This is true, and it is not a lie the power needs credit for.

They would be with the newcomer who cannot be fitted, in the season when the house feels thick and the car down the rim is not yet due. They would not force the fitting. Some bodies are audible to themselves, and the plateau is not a school for that. What they would do is keep the thickness from becoming shame. A night when the engines’ turn comes up through the cot in a rhythm the newcomer’s ribs can mistake for their own heartbeat. A morning when a rail-hand, without knowing why, signs the two-finger walk and means *you can go, and it will not be a failure.* The newcomer takes the car. They do not write back. Somewhere in the lowlands they sleep without their mouth open, and they remember the plateau accurately: not as a rejection, as a place that was already full.

They would attend the shoulder-high calf at the scald pan—not to drag it back, if the step is already the bad one, but in the instant before, when the crust looks solid and is a lid. A filament, richer than the others, drifting half a body’s length toward the safer pool. The calf follows its mouth. The adult does not defend it, because nothing here hunts in the open, and the power does not become a hunter of accidents either. It only moves the food a little. Some days this is enough. Some days the calf steps wrong anyway, and the power does not look away while the crust closes, and does not turn the death into a lesson. Death on the plateau is already legible. Adding meaning would be decorative, and decoration is a kind of noise.

For the engines, they would do nearly nothing, which is the correct reverence for a machine that has been matched to its keepers. Once in a long while, a knock that should have traveled too late through the boot arrives early by the length of a breath. The hand on the pressure moves. The breath does not become flame in the wrong flue. The keeper rubs a scarred palm and says the house is honest today. The power is content to be mistaken for honesty.

They would not found a cult of the turn. They would not whisper a name for themselves into the short spoken language. If a child, collecting white dust on the glass fins at brass-light, feels briefly that the fin is warmer on one side than the sun allows, and leans there, and is comforted without knowing the word, that is the whole theology they are willing to permit. The child grows into a walker of seep-lines or a scraper of film or a rail-hand. They do not grow into a priest. There is no office here for interpretation, and a wise power does not create one.

Across years that are not years to them, they would keep this scale. A seam in the cast plate that holds one more season, so the replacement car can come before the flange fails, not after. A grazer that turns one hexagon early, so its rasp does not meet a palm that was only curious. A clock in an engine house that loses its minutes and finds them again a little more gently, so no one spends the day believing time itself has gone sour. They would never dry the seep. They would never densify the compounds. They would never teach the narrow wings to perch on gutters and be companionable. Companionability is not what the wings are for, and benevolence is not the art of making everything a pet.

If they spoke at all—and they would almost never—it would be once, and not in sound. It would be in the way the horizon sits, on an evening when the light is honey on the weeping faces of the rock and a keeper stands on the gantry and sees the whole arrangement: sparse towers, thread of rail, glint of pans, drift of grazers, rim far off and steady. The keeper would feel, not as a sentence, that the place is known. Not improved. Not pitied. Known, in the exact dimensions of its heat and its bargain, by something that will not hurry it and will not leave it unattended in the small dangerous inches.

Then the feeling would pass, because a feeling that stayed would become a haunting, and hauntings demand stories, and stories here would have to be shouted.

The power would remain as the seep remains: a condition, not a visitor. The film would go on eating mineral. The crawlers would tile the flats. The shoulder-high would salt the rims. The children would shake with laughter no yard could hear. The engines would turn. Someone would always be awake, a hand on the pressure, taking the muted knock through the boot for what it is.

And the plateau, which eats nothing, which condenses and splits and remembers, would not alter its face to acknowledge the regard. That, too, the wise power would have wanted. To be thanked by the stone would mean the stone had been made to perform. They did not come to be seen.

They came because a life had made itself the exact size of its quiet, and a benevolent attention, arriving at such a life, does not enlarge it. It keeps the pipes from the crack. It keeps the shame from the unfitted heart. It lets the bad step stay rare without pretending it can be abolished. It watches the brass light go brown, and it does not ask the sky to rest, and it does not ask the people to become louder so that love can hear them.

Love, in this case, had already learned to watch the hands.
