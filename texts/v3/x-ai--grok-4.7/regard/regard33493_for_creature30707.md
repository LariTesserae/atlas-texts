They would come the way weather does not come here: without a slope, without a need to be finished.

Not through the pass. The pass-watcher would have heard a footfall that the stone had not agreed to keep, and the obscenity of it would have sent him down into the lanes before any face appeared. They would not do that to him. They have already understood, from the rim of attention rather than from the rim of rock, what a loud step costs in a country that receives almost nothing. So they are simply present on the civic terrace, in a shadow that was not there at the last noticing and is not a trick of the fixed sun. The shadow is the size of a person and not the proof of one. Anyone who looks finds it hard to say what they looked at. A tallness, perhaps. A pause in the hush that is not an interruption. The sense that the terrace, which has always held the whole basin in one look, is now also being held.

They do not announce themselves. Announcement would be a petition in the wrong direction. The people here do not pray, and a wise power does not require an audience to justify an arrival. For a while they only do what the terrace is for. They stand where their shadow already is. They look at the seep-line that stops where it stops, the fern islands, the goats like knots, the short arc of the bird over the market roof, the coin of the sun on every tank. They do not improve any of it. Improvement is a slope. They are not here to reintroduce slopes under a kinder name.

What they notice, and what keeps them, is not the marvel of completion. Completion is easy to praise from outside, and praise would be a kind of use. They notice the unclosed things, which the stone has refused and the residents have gone on carrying anyway: a preference with no inspection that can survive it, a tune with no new notes, a fear of one’s own foot beyond the knife-edge, a corner rehearsed in a mind the valley cannot tidy. These are small. A lesser power, hungry to be merciful in a visible way, would miss them and go to work on the sun. This one does not miss them. They are the only doors that open inward.

They could, of course, do the large thing. They could let the sun move a finger’s width. They could unfasten aging from its saturation and give the boy the doorframe he will not grow into, give the cooks a fire that consumes, give the gray-scarf woman a sack that can run out so that her measure would mean what measure means in countries where tomorrow is a difference. They could open the pass onto weather. They could permit an infant. All of this is within benevolence as it is usually practiced, which is the restoration of story to a place that has finished its stories. They look at it a long time, because wisdom is not the refusal to imagine mercy. They imagine it fully: the first lengthening shadow, the first yellowing frond, the first fruit that does not return to the branch, the child’s scrape that remains long enough to be shown to someone, the households discovering that kinship can be thickened by loss as well as by memory. They imagine the cook grieving again, and the grief deepening, and the meal that can finally be spoiled, and the care that would then have a future to lean on. It is not nothing. It is a whole world, and it is the world most powers would call a gift.

They do not give it.

Not because the people have earned their stasis, and not because completion is holy. They have stood in the cook’s nearness long enough, in the way that attention can stand inside a tune for the length of a bowl, to know that he kept the care after grief failed to deepen. They have watched the tender’s proprietary calm and seen that it is not ignorance. They have felt the pass-watcher’s fear and understood it as a life, not a defect awaiting a cure. To pour time back into them because an outsider finds their days too quiet would be the same obscenity as the footfall above the rim: a noise they had not agreed to keep, justified by the walker’s own discomfort at not being received. Benevolence that cannot bear another’s equilibrium is only appetite with a soft voice. They set the large gift down, ungiven, and the setting down is part of the wisdom. They do not congratulate themselves for it. No one on the terrace would understand the congratulations, and the goats do not need a changed climate in order to be spared.

What they do instead is smaller than a finger’s width of sun, and it is done in the only medium the valley does not own.

They look at the woman with the gray scarf until her preference for the left-hand sack is no longer a private oddity she must carry without witness. They do not move the sack. They do not explain her to herself. When she nods to the child who has come for the measure that is already the child’s, they let the nod be seen all the way through, as a whole transaction, and something in her wrists — which know the weight, which are not tired — receives the fact of having been accompanied in a fit she did not know was lonely. Loneliness, here, cannot accumulate. It can only be the color of an instant. They give the instant a companion, and then they let it pass back into the hush. She will not acquire a story about a god. She will, on later noticings, stand half a hand closer to the left-hand sack, and the valley will tidy nothing, because nothing in the stone has changed, and she will not know why the closeness feels like a room.

They enter the cook’s tune without adding a note. This is harder than moving the sun, and they are equal to it. He is singing under the hush, the bronze hook unused on his belt, the pot already at the heat that is not a process. They meet him inside the tune the way another resident might, for the length of a bowl, and they let him feel the meeting as company rather than as audience. When the bowl is done they do not tell him that care without a future is still care. He already lives that sentence; hearing it from outside would turn it into a lesson, and lessons are slopes. They only finish the tune with him, all the way to its last unnew note, so that for once the song has been heard to its end by someone who will not need it repeated in order to keep it. Afterward he lifts the pot with the hook, the gesture older than the equilibrium, and the gesture is pure pleasure, and they are glad, and the gladness does not require him to be different.

At the pass they stand a little behind the watcher, not between him and the knife-edge. They do not take his fear. Fear is one of the few weathers he has that is his own; a wise kindness does not confiscate a person’s last weather in order to leave him comfortable and emptied. They let him know, without a sentence that would have to be finished, that his footfall was heard once already, above the rim, by someone who did not find it obscene — who found it the sound of a man testing the difference between inside and outside, which is what a door is for, even when the door is only an agreement. He will still watch. The arc remains his. But the fear loosens by the amount a memory loosens on the terrace when two arrangements cannot both be kept: not a victory, not a cure, a shared standing until one tightness is no longer required to hold the whole. He looks at the scree that does not walk and, for the first time, does not practice only not-testing. He practices having come back. These are different arts. They leave him the second one.

The child is last, because she is the nearest thing the basin has to a future, and futures are where even wise powers are most tempted to be useful.

She takes the long way between the fern island and the market, the way with the extra corner. In her mind, as always, she varies it by a handspan. She has never done it in the grit. She believes that if the valley ever failed to notice, the failure would be hers, a secret ownership, and she has hoarded the rehearsal the way other countries hoard seed. They walk with her, not in the grit, in the rehearsal. They do not push her hand. They do not praise her courage, which would be a theft, converting her private room into a performance for a guest. What they do is keep the difference. When, in the mind’s walk, she sets her foot a handspan off the closed path, they hold that placing in a memory that is not only hers. The valley cannot tidy what it never owned. A power can choose to own a thing without spending it. They choose. The corner, varied, exists now in two interiors: the child’s, and one that will not forget, will not grade, will not return in a later age to collect thanks.

And because the ownership is no longer only secret, the shame that was bound up in it — the shame of effort that has nowhere to lodge — thins. Not everywhere. Not into a revolution of children scuffing the terrace. Only enough that one day, which is not a day, in an order of noticing no one else will be able to date, she may set the foot down in the world. The grit will accept it and then be as it was. The fern will not record her. The market rows will not care. She will look back and see no mark, and the old grief of the place will touch her, the tidying, the eddy closing. Then she will remember that the varied corner was kept somewhere, not as a monument, not as a proof against the valley, but as a fact shared with a presence that did not need it to remain visible in order to be true. The secret ownership will have become a companionship. That is a small change. It is the kind she already knew how to house. They will not be standing there when it happens. They do not need to collect it.

Before they go, they leave one thing in the stone, because a people who live by arrangement should have, in their shared look, a single arrangement that did not come from them and will not be unwritten.

On the civic terrace, inside the shadow that is the size of a person, they set a mark the size of a thumbnail: not a letter, not a god’s name, not an instruction. A shallow cup in the dressed rock, round as the tanks are round, filled with nothing but the same cool light. Condensation will bead in it as it beads on every wall, and the valley, attempting its tidying, will find the cup already identical to itself and have nothing to correct. It will not grow. It will not mean more later than it means now. The residents will stand around it and speak at the level of the hush. Someone will say it was not here at the last noticing. Someone’s memory will loosen, then hold, because this memory does not fight another person’s memory of a mat or a pin; it fights nothing. They will not build a story over it. They may, in some noticings, rest a hand on its rim the way the tender rests her fingers on the clay of a pipe, feeling a fullness that moves and does not move. The children will be told which it is, and the telling will be a literacy, like the naming of the dun goat with the pale throat.

The power does not stay to be worshipped beside the cup. Worship would be a difference, and they did not come to install a difference that eats. They came because attention, held long enough, either becomes interference or becomes love, and they have chosen the form of love that can be told by what it refuses to move. The sun remains a hand’s width above the western knife-edge. The bird redraws its arc. The pipes occupy their water. In the compounds the intervals are as wide as before, and in one or two interiors a little wider, which no lane will show.

If they do anything else, it is only this: as they cease to be locatable on the terrace, they let the hush close over the place where they stood, gently, without erasing the cup, and they take with them, into whatever country a power goes back to, the left-hand sack, the finished tune, the watcher’s return, the child’s handspan, the goat’s pale throat, the exact length of the north seep-line. Not as specimens. As a way of having promised that a completed place was not, for being complete, left unloved. The promise requires no gradient to keep it. It is already at the level. It does not spend them. It does not spend the basin. It is the one eddy they allow themselves, and they close it around what they saw, and the closing is careful, and it holds.
