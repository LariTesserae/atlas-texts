It would not arrive.

A power that is wise does not need the shape of an arrival, and a power that is benevolent has already understood what arrival would cost. On this plateau a figure at two hours’ distance is news. A figure that cannot be read—shoulders wrong, load riding on nothing, no dust of the right weight—would be a louder news than any shout, and the city would make room for it the way it makes room for a herd, which is to say it would not rearrange itself and would be rearranged anyway. After that, looking would no longer be a sufficient sense. They would start listening for what cannot be identified by the set of a body. That is not a gift. That is a crack running into the neighbor’s room.

So it does not stand on the horizon. It does not thicken the air so that a voice will carry. It does not put a tree where a tree would be an event, because an event, here, is a thing that reorganizes heat, kin, and the next forty years of who may draw from which jar. It has seen that happen in other places: a kindness dropped from above, and then a people spending the rest of their weather learning how to live beside the kindness instead of beside the stone. Benevolence that must be lived beside is only another climate.

It looks, because looking is the native courtesy, and because it can. It reads the city the way the city reads a walker. The thicknesses of bodies. The south walls where the animals bed. The ledges accumulating their small dunes of grit. The needle moving through a street on a route older than some of the hands that hold it. The children turned by a palm along the cheek instead of by a raised voice. None of this asks for correction. A wise power is suspicious of the wish to correct what is already fitted. Fitting is the art this ground taught, and the ground is not finished teaching it. To improve the fit from outside would be to seat the last slab with unpracticed hands.

What it does, it does where they already expect to be wrong.

The listeners who put an ear to the foundation scarp are often wrong, and the plateau is young, and its underground decisions are not closed. A household spends a generation hauling because a seep was this year’s melt and not a lasting under-sound. The listener carries the neighbors’ looking, which is specific and enough. The power does not make the listeners infallible. Infallibility would end the consultation, and the consultation is one of the ways the city thinks. It does something narrower. Where a seep is about to fail in a month and take a thickness with it—jars, milk, an old woman who still draws the salt-caches in dust—it lends the under-sound a little more stone-time. Not a river. Not a spring that would become a story and then a claim and then a feud conducted as a shortage of milk. A continuation. The tick remains a tick. The listener says: this one will hold, and is, for once, not wrong, and does not know why, and is not praised beyond the ordinary relief of a street that does not have to rearrange its arithmetic. The power is content to be indistinguishable from luck, which is what they already call the difference between a seep that lasts and a seep that was only deciding.

It watches the wind. The wind is small and faithful and spends itself moving grit one grain at a time onto the roof-edges. It does not still the wind. Stillness would frighten them more than storm; they know the wind as a kind of clock. But there are years when the clock runs past what a body can pay—forty days without the low cloud that wets a surface without becoming nameable rain, the cisterns drawing down to a sound that is only clay, the blood-letting from the herd starting earlier and cutting deeper. In those years it does not open the sky. An opened sky would be a god, and they would have to build something to face it, and they have no timber for that, and they would use stone, and the stone is already spoken for. Instead the cloud that was going to form and fail two days’ walk beyond the northern rise forms, fails, and fails a little closer in. A week of wet surfaces. Jars gain a hand’s depth. No one saw a sign. Someone on a roof says the cloud sat lower than it had any right to, and is not believed, and does not insist. The year enters memory as a hard year that did not become the worst year. Memory is a structural material. The power has added a course to it and left no mason’s mark.

It does not stop their deaths. Death is in the arithmetic, and a power that deleted it would leave them with rooms full of breath that had nowhere to go and animals that no longer replaced anyone. It does, rarely, turn a death that would have been a mess into a death that can be built with. A slab that would have shed in the first thaw and come down a lane at dusk, when the animals are shouldering in and the children are still in the street—that slab it worries at, for a season, with frost in the right joint, so the failure announces itself at midday in an empty stretch, a crack no wider than a needle, seen by an old man who still knows which face will take a course. They reset the stone. They say the ground warned them, which is true, and which they have always said, and which does not require a new name.

The being that lives in the place—it has already decided this is not one listener, not one household, but the long animal of the city itself, the persistence that is not a center, the looking that wakes at a change in the color of a crack—that being does not need to be addressed. Address would split it into a speaker and a spoken-to, and it has spent itself learning to be singular without a shrine. The power attends it the way the old are attended: not by giving it a warmer room than the arithmetic allows, but by not losing what it knows. The unmarked cisterns. The marriages that must not be remade. The intervals of the salt walk, which the wind is always erasing, because a path is a softness. It does not carve the intervals into the plateau. Carving would be a path. It keeps a few of the buried jars from silting shut in the one decade when the walkers are young and the woman who draws the map in dust dies before the dust is memorized. A jar lip, found again, flush with the grit, where a hand expected only stone. They will call it her memory, seated in the ground the way bone is seated in a wall. That is accurate enough to leave standing.

It does not lengthen their lives. It does not sweeten the milk or enlarge the grain or teach the cushion plants a longer green. Abundance, dropped here, would be a foreign climate with a smiling face. They would eat it, and then they would have to live in the country it made, and that country would not know how to sweep a roof, or when to click instead of call, or how to refuse with the stillness of the eyes in a hard light. The power has no interest in a grateful people. Gratitude is a distance. They live inside an arm’s length of their weather, and that is the correct distance, and a wise benevolence does not insert itself into the arm’s length and ask to be thanked for the air.

If it loves them—and it does, in the manner of something that has watched a fitting succeed, which is a kind of love that does not need to be felt back—it loves them by refusing the spectacle of love. No star that pauses. No voice in the tick under the stone that suddenly uses their low flat words. The tick remains mineral. The dead remain in the walls. A rider still appears as a change in dust, is known by the load, and is given the space a herd is given, no more.

What it will not allow is the one loss that cannot be refitted. The plateau is young. Uplift is still in it. There are futures, not many, in which the northern rise decides faster than a people can walk, and the scarps become a breaking rather than a back wall, and the under-sound goes all at once into a cut that does not seep but drains, and the city becomes a story told in lower country about a high place that was there and then was a ruin with jars in it. Against that, and only against that, it sets its hand—not as a wall they can see, but as a slight, stubborn disagreement in the deep joints, a slowing of the decision to be higher, measured in the same units they already use and cannot perceive: less than a finger in a human life, less again, until the rise and the people keep the same pace. They will never know they were accompanied. The northern cuts will seem, to the very old, a little less fresh than the stories. Someone will say the ground is tiring. Someone will say the stories were proud. Both will be let stand.

And on ordinary days, which are all of the days, it does nothing that has a name. It is the difference that does not appear in the lane. Morning still comes as a change in the color of a crack. Whoever sees it first is already awake. The wind edits the roofs. The animals turn out into pastures that are also streets. A child of six sets a body sideways and the herd, which had begun to drift toward a cistern lip, agrees to be a herd again.

The power sees that, and wants, and does not take, and stays.
