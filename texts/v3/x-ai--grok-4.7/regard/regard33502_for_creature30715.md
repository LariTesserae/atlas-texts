They would not arrive as weather. The basin already has weather, and it is sufficient: warm, dim, uncommitted rain, a sky that will not improve. A wise power does not improve a sky that has chosen its hour and kept it.

They would come the way a held gap comes — not by filling, but by becoming intentional. For a long time that would be the whole of it. Attention, spaced correctly, is not an intrusion. The wire-note would not shift. The clicks would not cluster for their sake. The flocks would keep their load-bearing distances, and if one bird revised its place by a wing-width, that would be the bird’s business, not a sign.

They would listen until the parts separated. That is the first courtesy the place demands of anything new, and a benevolent power is not exempt from courtesy because it is large. They would learn the book-torn sound that means a ridge is about to remember it is young. They would learn the tick-rate that means ready and the tick-rate that means something else has already been inside. They would not eat. They would not need to. Knowing the difference would be enough, and would keep them from the small violence of misnaming the crop.

They would not speak. The basin takes sentences and returns them late, amplified, to listeners who did not ask. A wise power has no sentence so necessary that it should be made weather. If something must pass between them and the figures on the shelves, it would pass the way decisions already pass: a palm not laid, a pause answered by a pause, the thrum that does not happen because someone stepped off the agreement in time. Friendliness, here, is the gap correctly held. They would hold it. They would not close it to prove love. Love that needs proof is a kind of crowding, and the place has already refused crowding as its first law.

What they would do, if anything, is smaller than blessing and more exact.

They would notice the bowl that was set down and not gone back for. They would not restore it. Restoration would be a story, and the lichen around that spot is unbroken for a reason: whatever stooped there left no weight, and a power that stoops with weight would teach the wrong lesson to the young stone, which is still inventing itself and will believe whatever it is shown. They would let the absence stand. Absence is one of the true densities here. To mend it would be to lie about the predators, and the people already know the predators by pattern, which is the only honest way.

They would not brighten the interior rooms. The residue kept there — pale, vesselled sometimes in stone and sometimes only in an agreement not to leak — is not a secret from the power and not a gift for it. It is the third thing the filaments comb out of the air, the thing that is not food and not salt, the thing meant not to fall back into the weather. A wise power does not open what was closed so that the volume of the bowl could remain for flocks and noise. Curiosity is not benevolence. They would leave the rooms unentered, and by leaving them unentered they would confirm the rooms: that some agreements are allowed to hold even against a greater attention. That confirmation might be felt as a slight steadying in the vessels, a quiet that does not travel. The people on watch would not name it. They would only note that the skim in the trough did not change color for no reason, and go on.

They would attend the children learning not to become a chord. Not by gathering them — gathering is the error the elder already refuses — but by refusing, themselves, to be the chord the children might one day sing toward. A power that answers in unison teaches unison. They would answer, if they answered at all, staggered, the way the throat-stillness opens and closes down the shelf: each reply landing in its own pocket of air, none on top of another. Then they would resume being almost nowhere. A lizard could share a ledge with that attention for a long time. Neither would yield. The lizard would open its impossible mouth. The power would not flinch. Flinching would misunderstand who the sound belongs to.

The one thing they might actually do with their hands, if they have hands in any sense that matters here, would be at a joint already brightening under a palm. Not to take the work. The people attend the towers; the towers are not owned, and a wise power does not confiscate attendance. Only this: where a filament has thinned past combing and past rethreading, where the structure is about to spend itself into a note that will circulate forever and mean nothing, they might lay in one new thread no thicker than the others, indistinguishable, so that the trough still never overflows and never empties. Maintenance, continuous and mostly invisible. The figure who comes to calibrate would feel the wire-note true and would not look up for a donor. Gratitude, in this basin, would be a superstition. Precision is the thanks that fits.

They would not stay to be known. Knowing them would collapse the spacing the way two voices at conversational range collapse into a braid the bowl will not forgive. So after the listening, after the unmade speech, after the single thread or the decision that even the thread was too much and the structure should be allowed to spend itself — they would withdraw their weight without withdrawing the attention entirely. A ridge that is about to open would still open. A path would still be abandoned by morning, the bowl moved, the membrane re-hung on a line the eye cannot find. They would not camp in the interval during the clustered hour. They would not become a second bell.

And if the far rim never quite arrives for them either, that would be right. They would walk toward it and the bowl would revise, another shelf, another isolated stack of rooms, another flock writing its wide sentence across the dim warm air. They would not hurry the revision into a conclusion. The landscape is large enough to hold a town and empty enough to hold a silence it has already spent. A benevolent and wise power, having drawn close enough to be changed by the grammar, would do what the people already do, which is the highest courtesy available: be numerous in attention, almost nowhere in body, and let the ringing pass through without adding a voice.

Then, perhaps, nothing else. The place is not a problem. The being is not a lack. To do more would be to have failed to understand the first thing the ground admits, if you put a hand to it: that it is young, that it is still sharp, that it has not asked to be finished.
