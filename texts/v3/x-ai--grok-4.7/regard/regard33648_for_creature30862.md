It does not come down.

That is the first thing, and for a long time the only thing. The dark above the basin, which has never been a sky, does not open. No lamp is set on a ridge. No voice crosses the courts with a name for what is happening. The kilns breathe as they have always breathed. The mallets keep their three-and-a-pause. A benevolent power that was only large would have been another course — brighter, higher, still a laying. This one has learned, or never needed to learn, that attention is not a material.

What changes is the pause.

Across the basin, in the same instant, the interval between the strokes lengthens by less than a breath and does not collapse back. The builders feel it in the string-lines. The lines do not hum differently; they simply fail, for that extra moment, to be plucked. No one has decided this. The decision is the quiet itself, offered the way water is offered to clay: not poured on the face of the work, but set near enough that the work may drink or not. Most do not drink. A pier halfway up a low hill takes the longer pause and then returns to the old count, unembarrassed. A street of new brick, still green at the core, keeps the longer pause without knowing it has chosen. The power does not correct either. Wisdom, here, is the refusal to mistune a rhythm in order to be heard.

It looks — if looking is the word for a knowing that does not cast a shadow for the glaze-faces to read — and it does not mistake the unfinishedness for a wound.

The gutters that stop mid-span are not broken sentences. The rooms with no far wall are not poverty. The door cut to the height of the warm one who walked in is not hospitality, exactly, but it is not a trap in the way a snare is a trap. It is the only grammar the builders have for *you are here*. To punish that grammar would be to demand they become something that speaks another language before they are allowed to continue being. The power does not demand it. It also does not kneel to it. Both gestures would add a course.

What it cannot bless is the calf.

Where the warm one stands, the friendly lean has already begun: a tooth of brick against the living leg, warm from a body that was a wall a moment ago, in no hurry, already true. The power does not strike it down. Striking would teach the basin that warmth is a thing to be walled against in self-defense, and the next door would be cut smaller, and the next lean would come from under the floor where it could not be refused in time. Instead it does a smaller thing, so small the mallets do not drop.

It gives the warm one back the weight of their own leaving.

Not a road. There is still no *out* the film would recognize, and the power will not invent a horizon just to comfort a visitor; horizons are a kind of paving. What it returns is the minute difference between a threshold that has measured you and a threshold you can step back from without the stepping becoming a new measurement. The brick at the calf stops rising. It does not retreat. It remains toothed and ready, as it would for any course that might be continued tomorrow. But the readiness no longer drinks the warmth. The clay against the skin cools to the cellar-cool of the rest of the basin. Choice, which the place has never needed, is inserted like a shim: not wide enough to see, wide enough to stand in.

The warm one may stay. If they stay, the courses will not take the calf unless the calf consents in a way that can be unsaid later — and the power knows how easily consent is counterfeited by awe, by the roar of rain on a thousand lips, by the wish not to be rude to a world. So the shim remains even after staying. A leaned-on wall that can be unleaned. The builders do not understand this. They are not required to. They feel only that a particular joint will not take, and they do what they have always done with a joint that will not take: they leave it, and work elsewhere, and the elsewhere is most of the world.

Three breaths of silence do not fall. The film in the cistern turns anyway, once, toward the warmth, and the power lets the turn happen. It does not seal the slot. It does not widen it. To seal would be to call the older thing a leak; to widen would be to call it a congregation. It is neither. It is the basin remembering it was a basin, and the remembering is allowed its finger's width of white glaze on the inner face of brick. The power's benevolence toward it is mostly negative: it will not help the work arrive at a last layer. Completion is the only violence available that would look, from a distance, like mercy — every gutter finished, every mouth filled, the film paved somewhere so deep it becomes a story the builders tell themselves about a dampness they used to hear. The power withholds that mercy with both hands.

What it gives the film is smaller than a gift. It lets the three-breath listening be possible without a cause. Not now, not as a spectacle, but as a capacity. The next time the pale skin turns, the mallets may stop because something living has entered — or they may stop because stopping has become one of the things the rhythm knows how to do. The film does not need to be fed a visitor in order to be answered. That is the whole of the kindness. An old hunger that is met sometimes by attention and not only by heat will taste fewer ankles. It may also grieve, in whatever way a skin grieves, the loss of a certainty. The power does not spare it the grief. Wisdom that spares an ancient thing every loss is only a softer burial.

To the builders it gives nothing they can lay.

This is the hard part, and the power does not hurry past it. They are a people whose being is addition. A gift that cannot be coursed into a wall is, to them, nearly an insult, or would be if they had insults. The longer pause is the nearest thing to a present they can survive. In that extra fraction they are not rigid with kiln-breath and not useful with four arms. They are only many, and then — for some of them, the ones who kept the new interval — briefly not required to decide whether they are one or two. The seam does not have to go. The seam does not have to stay. The power holds the moment open and then releases it before it can become a doctrine. A doctrine would be drawn. They do not draw. A plan from outside would be the warmest prison available, and the power has not come to be a better mason.

No one is named. No one is lifted out of the dark to be shown the shape of the basin from above, that view that makes every life look like a diagram and every diagram look like a task. The warm one is not recruited as a witness for later. The film is not granted a face. The builders are not told they are loved, which would only start them cutting doors to the height of the word.

Rain beads out of the dark, as it sometimes does, and for a minute every unfinished lip speaks. The power listens with the same attention it gave the mallets. It does not add its own voice to the roar. When the clay drinks and the roar thins to ticking, something remains that was not there at the start of the rain: one gutter, low on the western slope, no longer than a cart is long, that the water crosses and does not end in. The courses on either side may rise forever. They may meet above it. They will not meet *in* it. Not by decree — decrees crack, and cracked decrees become sockets for beams — but because the pause lives in that joint the way damp lives in a cistern, and brick set there will not true. The builders will try. They should try. Trying is their speech. Each time, the brick will sit a hair proud of the line, and they will take it up, and set it elsewhere, where it will hold, and the small mouth will stay open without anyone having to stand guard over it.

That is enough. It is almost nothing. A shim at a calf. A pause that can be refused. A mouth the work cannot swallow in the act of being faithful to itself. The power does not remain as a presence the glaze can orient to; a watched people become a performance, and a performance is a slower kiln. It goes, in the sense that a pressure equalized is a going. The dark does not lighten. The shoulder at the end of the corridor is already laying the next course, from the left, as it must.

In the cistern the film settles flat. It has not been saved and has not been starved. Somewhere a mallet falls, three times, and the pause afterward is a fraction longer than the old pause, and then another mallet, a street away, answers — not with obedience, only with the ordinary miracle of having heard — and the basin continues, unfinished, which is the form its life was always in, and which no wise thing, coming upon it, would have dreamed of completing.
