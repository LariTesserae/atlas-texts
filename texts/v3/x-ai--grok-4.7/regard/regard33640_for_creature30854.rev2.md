They would come the way weather comes, and then they would not stay.

Not as a body in the lanes. A body would be tasted. The tack of a comber’s pad would lift a solvent that does not belong to any crack, and the lampers would mix it, and the gold would go briefly wrong, and the lanes would bend for seasons around a sweetness with no source. That is how the shelf survives the unfamiliar: it grows past it, buries it in carpet, includes it as a scar of attention. A benevolent power would understand that inclusion is not the same as welcome, and that a scar made by kindness is still a scar. So they would not offer a hand. They would not stand where a shoulder might sequence past them in the amber. They would not warm themselves at the rim beside the loaf, broadside to the drop, and let the counters build a court on their hide.

They would attend from the only distance that leaves no fat.

From there the shelf is one gesture, not a city. A palm of new stone still arguing with the air, heat not yet spent, a thickness of lives clamped to the argument. The power would not count the kinds. Counting is a sort of looking, and looking is not how this being spends a face. They would feel instead for the mixture: the east-sweet and the west-sharp, the rancid spot where grief has not yet been carpeted over, the tick of a crack just learning to gel, the amber of halls being peeled on schedule. They would feel the inhabitant, which is not the loaf and not the lamper and not the clamp, but the contact passing through all of them—a slow attention with no center, already as large as the world it has.

And having felt that, they would know there is nothing to fix.

The stone is sharp because it is young, not because it has been struck. The hush is not loneliness; it is the sound of everyone already touching. No one is thirsty. No one is waiting for a voice. The conflicts resolve by weight and warmth, and the loser goes to the rim and becomes an engine again, and that is not a tragedy the shelf needs lifted out of it. Even the grief is in the grammar. A power that rushed to sweeten the rancid dab would be erasing a sentence. Wisdom, here, is the refusal to edit.

What they would do is smaller than help, and harder.

They would keep the day ordinary.

They would set their hand—not on the shelf, never on the shelf—against the things that are not yet in the mixture and must not be. Against a wind that might learn the rim and start the erosion early, before the plates have agreed to become gravel in their own time. Against a cold falling out of the ordinary blue, the kind that would steal the formation heat before the clamps have followed it to its last inch. Against the curiosity of lesser powers, the ones who mean well and arrive as bodies, who would want to be seen, who would speak, who would pour water on the matte leaves to see them glisten and would not understand that glistening is a wound. Against the gaze that demands a face look back.

They would not announce this keeping. Announcement is a taste.

They might, once, do one thing that could be mistaken for nothing. Where a fracture is about to sigh—the shelf still finishing the gesture that raised it—they might let the sigh complete on its own schedule and no faster, which is already what was going to happen, except that somewhere a haste had been gathering, a small impatience in the stone, and they would lay that impatience down. Not a miracle. A correction of their own nearness. Because even attending has weight, and a wise power knows its weight, and takes it back before a clamp pales for reasons that are not the crack’s.

Then they would leave the light as they found it: harsh at the rim, amber in the halls, useless for messages, good only for making. They would not bless the fliers. The fliers are already saturated or they are not; dusk is their politics; a blessing would crowd the vein of sun. They would not teach the combers a quicker hand. The knots remember hands, and a strange quickness would split welds that took a season.

If they lingered at all, it would be in the mode the lampers already practice: mixture without instruction. They would let the shelf’s last many days pass through them and would add no temperature of their own. And if some faint trace of that passage reached the cellars anyway—because nothing vast is perfectly clean—they would hope it arrived not as a command and not as a god, but as a slight lengthening of patience in the gold. A loaf, uncertain, pressing its side to a lamper in the dark, would leave knowing only what it might have known: east or stay, rim or lane. The power would be in that knowing the way heat is in the floor. Unnamed. Not owed thanks. Already being grown past, which is the only honor the shelf gives.

They would not be sad to go unseen. Being seen is a hunger, and they would have set that down with the other imbalances, before they came close enough to matter.

Below the haze, if there is a below, they would not open it. The shelf is the whole habitable world at this height, and it is full, and fullness is not a problem that wants a door. Ahead of the rim is height and young air and a dryness without theater. They would leave the boundary that clean. A blade leans over the edge and stops, because past the edge there is no crack to face. A wise power would stop for the same reason.

And then, because benevolence is not the same as absence, they would remain in the one place that does not contaminate: in the continuance. Seasons enough for a mark in a resin lintel to sit a finger higher against the dun. Heat enough that a fan may still tilt toward a seam. Silence enough that the high note of rim-stone answering the sun stays faint, stays possibly inside the head, stays unwitnessed by anything that needs a witness.

They would do, if anything, that.

The city at the back of the day would go on packing the volume. Near-touching, the ordinary condition. A shoulder to a flank, a flank to a wall that is also a flank. No one would flush gold for a god. The work would continue on every side of where a god did not stand.

And the power, withdrawing to the distance at which love does not register as a foreign fat, would count that—not looking, not even counting—as the thing they had been able to give: a world still sharp, still warm, still young, still able to finish its own arrival without tasting the hands that wanted, very much, to help it, and did not.
