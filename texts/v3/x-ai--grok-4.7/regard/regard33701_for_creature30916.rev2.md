The power does not come down the heat-column and does not rise through the shove. It is already near enough, having attended, and it refuses the cheap proof of arrival. A new wind would have been a confession of vanity. A voice in the pipes would have made priests of the keepers by evening, and then every held level would have been a liturgy aimed outward, which is a way of ending a place that lives by insisting sideways.

It looks long enough to be sure of the error it will not commit.

Cooling the wet shine would invite a climate, and a climate would invite mouths that are not sheath, and the mouths would prune the arguments until the valley learned the manners of a forest. Setting stone under the crust would silence the deep tilt, and with it the one moment in which travelers drop their posture together—their nearest thing to a shared life. Drawing the lips of the mats closer, so that every tongue arrives, would abolish the cost, and a traveler that cannot fail mid-air is only a tempo with nowhere to spend itself. The power has seen places rescued into scenery. It will not make another.

So it does not fix the valley. It does not name the beings, which have no use for names and would only be slowed by them. It does not catch the tongues that chalk and drop. Catching them would teach the bulbs to aim upward, and then the bridges would become prayers, and the power would have replaced the rain as the heir of the dead. Rain, at least, does not answer. The dropped cost still drops. The quilt still goes on at the pace of heat. Benevolence, here, is partly the dignity of not being required.

What it does is smaller than weather and harder to keep.

It touches only the rate at which a successful weld forgets. Not the dead gutters—those stay visible, bones under new skin, because the place is young and ought to see its last hour. Only the living vein that a knot has paid for. For a few days longer than the sheath would have allowed on its own, the wall keeps a second pulse under the resident knock: the tempo of the one who crossed, pale and slight, not a ghost and not a guest, a courtesy of water. A later traveler entering that vein feels, in the way these beings feel, that the road has been used and did not begin with its own thirst. It is not memory as a story. It is hospitality without a host. Most such traces still weather out before a third crossing. A few last long enough that a span, singing in the wind, carries a phrase that is not only the shape of its hollows. The power does not stay to hear whether anyone alters course.

Under the crust it makes no answering shove. It only refuses, in whatever ledger powers keep, to borrow this forcing for any other work—no lesson, no harvest, no cooling borrowed for a gentler country that would like the look of green knives without the tear of vapor. The heat remains local. The edges remain sharp. When the mat tilts and the ugly chord empties the veins, the power does not hold the postures for them. To be spared that emptying would be to be spared the place. It lets them go briefly back to sap. It lets them gather again after, if they gather.

Then it withdraws even the small touch, so the extra days of remembered tempo do not become a law. One afternoon, somewhere on a mat no one is watching, a vein knocks twice a little longer than it should have. The light stays ordinary. The steam-ropes braid and unbraid. Nothing arrives to explain it. The wise act, completed, looks almost exactly like the valley continuing to fail in the open, hot and wet and loud, unrescued, and not alone in the only way that would not have changed it into something else.
