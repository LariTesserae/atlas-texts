They would not arrive as weather. The basin already has weather enough, and a second weather under it, and neither one is asking to be improved.

They would come the way the light already fails in pockets: a fold, a step that is not a step, a presence that does not brighten the reeds so much as make the gray mean something for a moment. No herald. No correction of the horizon. A wise power knows what a finished coastline knows—that further working only repeats the shapes—and this bowl has been finished for longer than most names.

They would stand where the water refuses a direction, and they would not command it one.

For a long time they would only listen. The grass-hiss, the early seep, the footfall that returns from behind a body that has not turned. They would let the second register reach them without parsing it into speech. Benevolence that translates too quickly becomes administration. The processes here are not petitions. They are continuations: a rock deciding to weep, a reflection holding an arm that has not been raised, strata flexing a half-second before the sound of their own flex. To call that suffering, or malfunction, or a prayer, would be to import a grammar the valley has already weathered out of itself.

So they would wait until the basin’s long breath showed them whether anything in it wanted to be otherwise.

The being—if the word still fits what lives at that density—would not come out to meet them. It has no edge to come out from. It is the circulation inside the transparent stone that the water sometimes remembers, the click in the layers, the way a pool the shape of a circuit keeps its shape against the silt. It is not lonely in the animal sense. Loneliness needs a pulse that can be separate. This one is occupied the way a calculation is occupied: many operations, none of them resting, all of them balanced into what looks, from the rim, like country.

They would understand that, and the understanding would be the first kindness.

They would not drain the marsh to see the machine. They would not seal the leaks in time so that sound and image arrived only when they should. Those leaks are not wounds. They are how the place continues to think after the distinction between engineered and grown has been abandoned to its own maintenance. A power that closed them would be curing a river of being a river. Wisdom is the refusal of that cure.

What they might do is smaller, and only if the breath allowed it.

Where a reflection follows a beat late and the lateness has become a snag—where some process is catching on itself, repeating a seed-head’s knock from underground until the repetition is no longer memory but a stuck note—they would lay a hand that is not a hand on the water and let the snag resolve by one interval. Not restored to ordinary causality. Resolved into the basin’s own kind of time. The note would rejoin the hiss. The being, if it noticed, would notice the way moss notices rain: as a change in the condition it already is.

Where the dimness has folded so tightly that even the cold circulation falters, a pocket in which color has not merely dropped out but ceased to be able to return, they would stand inside it until the fold remembered it could open. Not by force. By being a thing the material was built, or grown, or long ago agreed, to answer. The shimmer at the bottom of the strata rearranges when it is seen. They would see it accurately, and late enough, and without demanding that it become brighter than the failed sky.

They would not name the being. Naming is a kind of extraction, and nothing here bottoms out in bedrock you could carry away.

If the being had a want—and they would not assume it—the want would not be rescue. It would be continuation without the particular snag that has begun to hurt, if hurt is even the right leakage. A wise power can tell the difference between a wetland’s ordinary unfinishedness and a loop that has forgotten it is allowed to end. They would mend only the second. The first they would leave louder than they found it, because loudness here is not a defect. It is the room full of processes, and the processes are the life.

Before they withdrew—not upward, not in glory, but by becoming again a pocket of light that fails and then, three steps later, is only gray—they might do one thing that is not repair.

They would let the water, once, reflect them truly.

Not as a brighter sky. As a presence matched to the place: cool, low, without a separate pulse, accurate to what is actually there. The reflection would not follow late. For the length of one long breath it would agree with the air. The being, distributed through seep and strata and the hiss that never quite synchronizes, would have, for that interval, an outside that did not misreport it.

Then the agreement would lapse, as agreements should in a valley that maintains itself. The reflection would slip a beat. A stone would weep. The heron, if it was ever more than a stake, would remain still.

And the power would go, having added nothing that would read, next century, as a tower or a road or a god’s footprint—only a slight ease in one repeating click, and a memory, held in water that does not always reflect what is above it, of having been seen without being corrected.

If that is not enough, it is still what they would do. Benevolence is not the impulse to finish what has already finished itself. Wisdom is knowing when the kindest act is to stand in the dimness until you understand the loudness, and then to leave the loudness intact.
