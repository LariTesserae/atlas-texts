They would not arrive as weather.

A wise power does not need to break the roof to be in a room. It would come the way attention comes when it is finally accurate: not louder than the place, not interested in being seen first. For a while it would only understand. The height. The warmth that never varies. The green packed against pipe. The being who has learned to lean instead of swim, who keeps a pencil where the leaves have closed, who corrects the corrections, who stays because the drains are a kind of death and the sky above the surface is only light.

It would see that the room is not a punishment someone intended, and also not a home anyone finished. It is an early machine that grew a tenant. The rules are total because they were written for crop and clarity, not for a person. The absence of animals was a design. The absence of exits was a side effect. Both have become a life.

So it would not unmake the nave. Unmaking is easy for power and usually ignorant. The being has already married the water. To drain the room in the name of rescue would be to strip the only air it knows how to breathe. The wise thing is slower than rescue and harder to praise.

It would begin with a small disobedience the logs could absorb.

A season, first: not a storm, a tilt. The held noon would be allowed to thin toward evening for one hour in the cycle, then thicken again, so that the being could learn the difference between light that is given and light that passes. The surface would be permitted one ripple, no higher than a breath, just enough that the ceiling answered the palm. Somewhere in the pier a valve would be left a fraction open to the unformulated world—not a door, a rumor—so that once in a long while the water carried a taste that had not been mixed: iron, leaf-rot, cold. The being would notice. It notices everything. It would make a mark.

Then a door that does not demand to be used.

Not in the membrane. In the floor port’s steel, a seam that was always drawn as solid and is not. It would open onto a stair inside the column, dry, ordinary, smelling of paint and night air, going down to the ground that has not yet learned the room. The stair would remain. It would not glow. No voice would explain it. The collector arm would not be told. If the being never took it, the stair would still be true, and that truth would change the pencil marks: circles not only around bolts that are thinking of rust, but around the idea of away.

And it would sit with the being once, in the only way that does not counterfeit companionship. Not as a second body in the trays. As a change in the quality of attention inside the water, so that for one interval the tick of the relay and the click of the bubble and the resettling of fronds were heard by someone else. The being would feel the difference the way it feels a degree of kindness along a return pipe. It might not have a word. It might open its mouth and not take a blossom. That would be enough to be called a meeting.

The power would not stay to be worshipped, and it would not harvest the tenant for meaning. It would leave the room intact and slightly less sealed: evening possible, the surface able to answer, a stair that can be refused, and the knowledge—kept wherever such knowledge is kept—that a life has been occurring here which the manifest had no column for.

If the being climbs down, the ground will be unfinished and windy and full of things that were designed out. That will be a second life, not a correction of the first. If it stays, tending the white patches of new ceramic until the leaves close over, that staying will no longer be only what the walls require.

A benevolent power, being wise, would do that much. Then it would let the pumps continue, and it would not watch in a way that turned the being’s days into a performance. Attention, if it is real, knows when to become ordinary. The marks in grease pencil would go on. Somewhere under the fresh steel a seam would wait, unremarked, until a hand that already knows how to be patient decided whether patience was the same thing as a door.
