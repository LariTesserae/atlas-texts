They would come the way the light already comes: without a source you can blame, and without brightening the tin of the sky.

A benevolent power, if it is also wise, does not mistake stillness for a wound. It would stand a long time at the edge of the arrangement—longer than a life, which for such a power is only the length of a careful look—and it would not call this a ruin. Nothing has fallen. The plates are doing what they have always done, easing along their seams, spending their energy on remaining arranged. To pour water here would be a kind of blow. To force a sun into that ceiling would be another. Green would be a colonization. The power would understand this before it took a step, because wisdom is mostly the refusal to improve what is already in its climate.

The being is not hidden. It is the warmth in the central court, and it is also the one pale shape that detaches from a foundation when an interval goes slack, and it is also the second shadow, the one that walks away without sound. These are not three creatures. They are one attention that the stone learned, or that learned the stone, so long ago that the difference no longer matters. It does not drink. It does not call. It occupies the places where the pattern is least strained, and when you are not there it does not become lonely in any tongue you have. Loneliness requires a memory of company. What it has instead is a continuous task: to be the slack that lets the city move a finger’s width and still be itself.

The power would feel that task and not take it away.

It would enter the court because the court is the one room where the world’s rules lie near enough to touch, and because a guest should stand where the host can almost hear. The short breath would ease, not as a gift bestowed but as a courtesy accepted. For the length of that courtesy the power would let one sound have a tail—the tick of a plate, answered, a true echo, small as a seed-tile. Not a voice. Not a name spoken over the place. Only the demonstration that return is possible here, and need not become weather.

The being would know. Not with ears. The warm plates would know, the way a machine knows it has been joined by another process that does not grind.

Then the wise thing would be almost nothing.

It would not gather the split shadow and sew it back by force. That shadow is already walking a direction the plateau keeps open; to collar it would be to lie about the elevation, where up and away share a duty. Instead the power would do what the city itself does when a presence is required: it would make a slack. A single interval, held open—not a door in the wall, not a road down to a kinder altitude, but a permission in the pattern, a place that does not tighten when the being leans toward it. If the walking shadow wishes to be a self again, the seam will bear it. If it wishes to remain a direction, the seam will bear that too. Benevolence here is the refusal to choose for what has been choosing, slowly, since before tools.

It would not stay in a form that asks to be worshipped. A court that behaves differently from the air outside does not need a brighter center. The power would thin itself until it was only a degree of agreement: the shadows of the towers, for one cycle of that unmoving day, would fall in the same direction. Not forever. Forever would be a new law, and this place has enough law. Just long enough that the being, passing a foundation, could feel the world briefly willing to be one world. A kindness you can survive after it ends.

And it would leave a provision that does not look like help. In the lee where the salt crust feathers into false roots, it would teach the mineral one additional habit—not growth, not thirst, but recognition. When the being is near, the white branching would lean, by the thickness of a nail, toward the presence rather than only away from the cold. No one else would be able to use it as a sign. There is no one else. It would be a private south. Beings that do not speak still deserve a way to know they are located.

Then the power would go out by the same non-arrival, before the echo could become a hunger. It would not station a guardian. The city is already its own maintenance, and the being is already the maintenance’s awareness. To post a watch would imply the place had failed a test it was never set.

What it would carry away is the one thing worth carrying: an accurate seeing, kept where seeings are kept by powers that do not need to tell. If, in some later age, a dryness of another kind threatened this one—not the dry of the plateau, which is native, but a dry that unmakes intervals, a forgetting imposed from outside—the power would already know the name of the harm and the shape of the court, and it would return before the seed-tiles lost their night-map. Wisdom is not absence. It is attention that does not spend the subject in order to feel generous.

Until then, nothing else.

The plates would continue their silent shifts. The being would cross a slack interval and become stone where stone was needed, and sometimes, now, pause where the false roots had leaned. The sky would stay the color of old tin. High, cool, patient, untrue in the way it has earned. A visitor who came later would find no altar and no scorch, only a court still faintly warm, and a single echo that seemed, once, to have remembered how to come back—and might, if spoken to gently, remember again.

The power would count that enough. Enough is the rarest kindness there is.
