They would not arrive as weather. The wise ones learn, early or at great cost, that a place already has a climate, and that overriding it is a kind of erasure dressed as kindness. So the power does not part the low cloud or still the seep under the plates. It does not lift the shelf into older, kinder ground. It does not put a forest where the stone has not yet consented to roots. That would be mercy with the memory taken out, and mercy without memory is only a prettier exile.

What it does first is attend at the scale the town already uses.

It learns the second plank. It learns which tick is the stone settling and which is a lashing about to give. It learns the grammar of not-quite: the pot set down, the pot moved, the silence that means the seep sounded as it did yesterday. It does not enter by the stake-path in a body that would have to be housed, fed, and explained. A new mouth here is not a blessing. It stands, if standing is the word, where the reflections already misalign in the fog-hour—not as a second town, not as a vision anyone is asked to believe, but as a steadiness in the mismatch, so that the extra line of thatch does not feel like a mistake in the eye. People who have stopped mentioning it do not need a revelation. They need the thing they refused to narrate not to be a private crack.

Then it does a few small things that can be mistaken for luck, because luck does not create a debt the lanes would have to total.

The shared knife of the poorest lane does not slip into the slot and vanish. Once, when the keeper’s hand is wet and the edge would have turned out, the handle sits a finger’s breadth truer in the palm. No one sees a hand. The keeper only thinks: I was more careful than I felt. That is allowed. Care should feel like it came from the one who needed it.

On the stake-path, where memory is the only bridge, a stake that had leaned toward the black mud is upright again at the hour the walker reaches it. Not replaced. Not new. The same stake, the same notch, as if the ground’s breath had missed its usual pull. The walker does not invent a god. The walker has a dull imagination and a repeatable foot; that is why they live. They plant the next step where the last one was, and they come back with both hands free, reed for the roofs that were already promised, copper for a lip that had begun to give its water back to the air. The copper is still a patch, not an ornament. Wisdom does not turn repair into treasure. Treasure would have to be guarded, and guarding is a wall the footprint cannot hold.

For the children it does even less, and that less is exact. It does not multiply them, or return the ones the arithmetic took, or open a spare space for a game that needs a square of unused ground. What it does is keep the uncracked end of the reed findable when a bowl is being made, and keep a burned hand from going bad in the damp—not by miracle-skin, but by the hour of watching being enough. The person already sitting up with the burn does not fall asleep at the wrong moment. Their attention holds. In the morning the pair of hands is still in the lashings. The town does not feel the missing tooth. That is as loud as the kindness gets.

For the old woman who sleeps badly because the sound she was built for is not here, it does not import the wind in the tree, the beast, the distance. Those belong to a floor that does not tick, and fetching them would make this shelf a copy. Instead, when she wakes at the tick already pleased, the cord in the dark meets her fingers half a turn looser than disaster and half a turn tighter than doubt. She still does the work. She still talks to the cord in that low exact register. She still does not lift her head for visitors looking for a chief. But the pleasure is clean. The stone asked, and her hands were right, and nothing in the night had to be heroic.

It does not make them speak up into the clear air above the fog. A voice that travels past the rim and comes back wrong from the water would not be healed by being answered from the sky. It leaves them their low speech. It leaves them the refusal to dig. Some refusals are the only intelligence a place has, and a wise power does not talk a people out of the knowledge that is keeping them un-drowned.

If it speaks at all, it is not in the lanes. It is in the one place a carried people can hear without gathering: in the dispersed assembly at the end of a day, doors unlatched, light reaching the slot and not the room. Not a voice. A completeness. The jar weighs what it weighed. The knife comes home. The difference in the seep, on the night there is a difference, is sayable in one sentence, and the sentence is enough, and no one has to follow it with a story about why the world is watching. The world, in the only sense that matters, is the next plate, the next rise of a handspan, the next walker due at the stakes.

Would it stay? Yes, in the way salt stays—available, not admired, gone back into the stone when the air thickens. It would not found a cult of the fog-hour. It would not appoint the woman with the copper as its priest. It would not widen the footprint by a single plate to make room for an altar, because an altar is a space the town spent its whole invention refusing to leave open.

What it wants, if a power like that wants, is not gratitude and not improvement into some other people’s idea of a home. It wants the loyalty they already practice to be sufficient: stay, fix, do not ask the unfinished ground to become kind before it is ready. It adds only what their exactness cannot supply—the accident avoided, the stake remembered by the path itself, the burn that does not take a pair of hands, the reflection that can be wrong without being a verdict.

And if someone new, still in the order of wearing-off, says in the fog that the pan showed a slot where a wall should be, the power does not confirm it and does not punish the sentence. It lets the resident hand over the end of a cord that needs holding. That is the blessing, as far as blessing can go here: the sentence is allowed to die, and the person is allowed to stay, and the cord holds, and the land continues being born under the floors, sharp and wet and not yet old, with one more night in which nobody drowns of a false memory, and nobody is saved in a way that would make the saving larger than the town.
