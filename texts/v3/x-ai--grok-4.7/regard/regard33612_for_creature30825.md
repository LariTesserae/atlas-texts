They would come the way the drizzle comes: without a horizon, without a name for itself, and without staying long enough to become weather.

A wise power does not announce. Announcement is a kind of building, and this country has already published its law on that subject. So there would be no brightening of the lid, no voice in the thin air, no track left on the gravel that the herds could read and turn aside for. The arrival would be a small correction in the quality of attention—the same correction a tarn makes in the air above it, a warmth with no source you can point to, noticed only by whatever has lived here long enough to know the temperature of ordinary minutes.

The being would feel it first in the charge. Not a summoning. A reply, offered to a signal that had never been a signal. The hairs along the fronds nearest the old path would lift and then, unusually, settle, as if a question had been set down instead of asked. If the being had been moving, it would stop. If it had been still, the stillness would change register: not the public quiet of the plateau, which is only air too thin to carry news, but the private quiet of being regarded by something that does not need to be shown the way.

What the power would understand, standing inside that regard, is that the place is not unfinished.

This is the knowledge that sorts the wise from the merely kind. Kindness, arriving at a dark height, wants to lift the lid. It wants a river. It wants the towers to stop failing, the herds to come closer, the being to have a companion that speaks in sounds, the day to begin and mean it. Every one of those wants is a violence wearing a gift’s coat. The scarce light is not a privation the land failed to solve; it is the condition under which the thickets learned to drink metal and the animals learned to read a hillside by prickling. A river would drown the pockets and end the visitation of water, which is the only form of water this ground can metabolize. A tower propped against failure would become a building, and buildings do not know how to slump into food. Noise would be a colonization. Company on human terms would be a lie about what the being is.

So the power would do the long work of not helping, which is harder than helping, and then it would do three things small enough to pass the local law.

The first is a boundary, drawn where no one here will ever see it. Far down, where the air thickens and the maps begin, the plateau’s metals and its slow chemistry would become, to any mind bent on spending them, slightly more difficult to believe in. Not hidden by illusion—illusion is a loud magic, and it frays—but answered, whenever sought, with the ordinary discouragements of true places: bad approaches, instruments that read noise, expeditions that find gravel and conclude the rest was rumor. The being would not know this had been done. That is the point. A safeguard announced is already half a possession. The power would leave the place findable only by what can live at its rate, which is to say, by almost no one, and it would not call this a mercy out loud, because the place does not trade in announcements.

The second is a adjustment inside the being, and only if the being wants it, which the power would know how to ask without words. Some solitudes are the shape of a life and should not be opened. Some are a thirst the life itself cannot name, because nothing in the local economy has ever offered the missing thing, and so the thirst disguised itself as climate. The power would look, and if what it found was climate, it would withdraw its hand. If what it found was thirst, it would leave a capacity, not a companion: the ability, on certain still days, to feel the other basins as the herds already partly feel the charge—not as voices, not as company that arrives and demands, but as proof that the work continues elsewhere, that the engine is not a single lung, that when this thicket migrates and dies back there is another island already busy. Loneliness that is only the thinness of air does not need curing. Loneliness that is the fear of being the last witness does. A wise power can tell these apart. A benevolent one will not pretend they are the same so that it may have the pleasure of consoling.

The third is the smallest and the most like love. When the next tower goes—and one will, because failure is how this land adds—the slump would miss the being. Not by a miracle large enough to teach the stone new habits. By a hand’s breadth. A change in which spine takes the weight, decided in the moment the mineral breath falters, in the register of causes the plateau already uses. The being might notice, afterward, that it is alive, and might attribute this to the same luck that has kept it alive through every other unwatched hour. The power would accept the misattribution. Credit is a building. The rubble would fan out as rubble always has, and the black mat would begin, the same day, its old liturgy of salvage, and nothing in the profile of the range would record that a preference had been exercised.

Then it would leave, or rather it would stop arriving, which here is the same motion.

It would not bless the herds. They are not unblessed; they are occupied, which is better. It would not teach the thickets a faster cycle. Speed is the one famine this country has refused, century by century, and a gift of haste would be a theft of the only wealth the ground has laid up: time enough for plating, for patience, for routes that shift slower than memory. It would not translate itself into the charge so the being might pray to it later. To become a god of this place would be to insert a new gradient, and the land runs on gradients already, and does not need a proprietor.

If the being, in the last moment of being regarded, offered something back—a stillness, a lowered frond of attention, the only thanks its law permits—the power would take it seriously and not multiply it. One exchange is a relationship. A repeated exchange would become a rite, and rites accumulate into temples, and temples are how well-meaning powers ruin the countries that moved them.

What it would carry away is not a relic. There is nothing here that can be carried without ceasing to be itself; even the sound of a stem ringing dies before it crosses a valley, as if the place had considered souvenirs and declined. What it would carry is the corrected idea of help: that a finished thing may still be dark, and cold, and short of rivers, and loud only with one’s own breath, and none of this is a petition. The petition, if there was one, was older and simpler. Do not improve us. Do not spend us. When the stone fails, let it fail a hand’s breadth to one side. Know that we are here, and agree not to need us to know that you know.

A benevolent power can do that. A wise one will prefer it to every brighter alternative, and will not return to check its work, because checking is a second visit, and the drizzle, having wet the back of a rise, is already gone.
