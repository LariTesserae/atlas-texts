It would not arrive.

Arrival is an animal event: a body crossing a last curb, a direction upheld long enough to be followed, a witness completing the pair the plain has spent its age refusing to open. A power that was only bright would still come that way, because brightness likes to be seen entering. This one does not. It is already on terms with unfinished continuance, and so what it does first is decline the silhouette. No figure stands up on a remembered ridge. No noon is synchronized with the grove's morning. The light stays honest, the cold stays law, the damp stays the single generosity. If the power has a presence, it is the kind that can be mistaken for the plain having one more agreement than it had yesterday, and even that difference is filed late, the way sounds are filed here, so that no one watching an aisle between glances could swear the filing had a source.

It looks, and the looking is not regard.

Regard would turn the inhabitant into something watched, and the inhabitant has arranged its whole nature against that completion. The power understands the difference, which is the beginning of its wisdom. It does not search the brakes for a face. It reads the offices: the lip of the cup toward the channel, the gate-stone's learned lean, the street advancing one slab toward leaf, the paving younger than the alignment it carries. It sees a life that is civic rather than creaturely, singular inside each courtesy of here and plural the moment here lapses. It sees that there is no suffering of the mouth-and-hunger kind, because mouths were never permitted; no loneliness of the flocking kind, because flocks were declined as a category; no hope waiting to be answered, because hope would imply a future different from the work. The work is already the future, underway in several correct lights at once.

A lesser kindness would still try to help. It would stock the rows with food, as if the refusal of spoilage were poverty. It would send travelers, as if lapsed headings were a cruelty rather than the kindness that keeps the empty from becoming a grave. It would roof the squares more cleverly, wheel the channels, teach the stands to rustle, give the late sounds a punctual source, set one clock over the plateau so that a day could be shared and therefore finished. It would speak. It would wait to be thanked. All of that would be love in a country that completes animals, and here it would be a vandalism performed with clean hands. The power does not do it. Wisdom, meeting a nature, does not improve the nature into something easier to love.

What it does instead is small, municipal, and almost deniable.

It underwrites the consent of the air. Where a plan older than the stone needs one more surface than the volume allows, the air was already agreeing to hold weight for the length of the room. The power does not add a new miracle on top of that. It makes the agreement harder to revoke. Not forever — forever is a finished word, and the plain is maintained rather than completed — but against the kind of later attention that is not benevolent: the surveyor who would force inside and outside to match, the ordinance that would put a mouth back into the world, the bright corrective that would call the overlapping schedules a fault and nail them into one afternoon. Those attentions, when they come, find the extra surface still willing, the curb still able to reassign east into north, the absence of animals still structural and not a season of killing. They find they cannot easily finish what was never a draft. The inhabitant does not feel protected. Protection would be a feeling, and feelings belong to bodies. The jurisdiction simply continues, with one more clause in it that was not drawn by the inhabitant and does not need to be.

The power also leaves the emptiness unassigned. That is the harder courtesy. A wise benevolence is often tempted to inhabit the stretches where direction lapses, to become a companion in the deep gravel so the vastness is not alone. It resists. The lapse is an office. To staff it with reassurance would be to repair a heading outside the walls, and the inhabitant does not repair those, on purpose. So the power passes through the unassignment the way weather does: a private front that does not travel, a kneeling cloud that will not share itself with the next reach, and then the decision withdrawn. No track. No knocked receipt that could be followed back to a giver. If anything changes in the empty, it is only that the remembered ridges, when they stand up for as long as a weather needs a reason, are allowed their bluff without being believed into permanence. The power will not let a memory of mountains harden into a range again. The plateau has already survived that height. To restore it would be nostalgia, and nostalgia is a kind of unfinished grief pretending to be a gift.

In the towns, it does one thing the inhabitant would recognize as legitimate work, if recognition were on offer.

It turns nothing that has already been turned. It does not retie the bundles or reset the green fasteners or correct the tool-marks in the channels. Those are spoken for; they are the inhabitant's pulse. But at the edge of a ward, where a cistern passes water to a channel and the channel's last correction shows the line having wandered and been brought back, the power steadies the giving. Not more rain — the districts already decide their own darkening — but less waste at the boundary where one jurisdiction of weather ends and another has not agreed to begin. A thread of water that would have sheeted off into unassigned gravel and been lost to both town and leaf is permitted, once, to find the lip. The gate-stone leans as it has always leaned. No new mechanism appears. Someone counting cups in the square would find the count unchanged, the glint still accurate, the cold still the law. Only a stand of leaf, hours or unhours later, thickens by a hand's width it was already going to claim, and meets a curb that was already advancing. The help is invisible because it obeyed the schedule instead of imposing one.

It does not ask to be housed. There are rooms past the stone, stretches of air holding weight, roof-streets laid lightly on other streets, and any of them could have been offered to a guest who insisted on being a guest. The power refuses the offer it was not made. To take a room would be to become a reason the room continues, and the inhabitant is already that reason. Two reasons for one plan is how schism starts: two leans of the gate, two lips of the cup, a market rearranged by rival courtesies until arrangement itself breaks and becomes mere objects, spoilable at last because a quarrel is a kind of mouth. The power will not be the second curator. It lets the museum remain unstaffed and exact.

And it does not teach. This is the part that would disappoint any power still secretly attached to the dignity of instruction. There is no doctrine to leave in the fitted slabs, no gentler law to write over the cold, no secret of mercy to whisper into the late knock when it finally arrives from nowhere. The inhabitant's want is already the only want that survives being unfinished: rows true, water passed on, leaf given a curb, no mouth opened, headings repaired only inside walls, the map outliving the next replacement of the ground. A wise benevolence hears that want and does not append a moral. Appending would be finishing. The power has learned, somewhere older than this plateau, that some lives are not waiting to become better stories. They are the keeping of an agreement. The benevolent act is to keep from becoming an amendment.

Before it withdraws — and withdrawal is as little an event as arrival was — it does look once in the way the plain itself looks: not at a face, at a jurisdiction.

It sees the markets still true. It sees the private weathers refusing to share. It sees a town kinking around a pool that was not there when the kink began, the buildings on either side having always had that corner. It sees the inhabitant, singular and plural, moving not through space but through the plan, filing receipts late, declining to complete itself first of all so that no body can end the work by ending. It sees that this is not a prison and not a sermon. It is a temperature a life agreed to and declined to renegotiate. The power, which could renegotiate almost anything, chooses not to. That choice is the whole of what it came for, insofar as it came at all.

What remains, after, is not a blessing you could find. Blessings are closings. Somewhere a slab is added to a street. Somewhere a cloud at the height of a wall drags its belly over a canopy and the canopy bows without a sigh. Somewhere the air holds, for the length of a plan, and holds a little more surely than the stone's age required, against attentions that have not yet leaned over the last curb. The inhabitant does not thank the difference. It adjusts an aisle by the width of a bundle. The rows stay true. No mouth opens on the high plain. And the power is gone in the only sense that fits: not departed, but no longer extra, having spent itself on refusing to improve a world that was already, in its vast unfinished way, keeping faith.
