They would come the way weather comes, which is to say they would already have been in the air for longer than anyone was counting, and the arrival would be a change in what the air was willing to carry.

Not a figure on the turf. The grass would go on combing itself toward the crack. A bird might still cross and decline to land. Down in the chamber the stacked notes would not stop; wisdom, if it is any good, does not begin by silencing a thing in order to be heard over it. What would change is the hurt. The flat pain in the ears, the one that makes an hour into a decision, would lift by a degree so small a visitor might think they had simply grown used to it. They would not have. The room would still be loud. It would only have become possible to stay inside the loudness without the body spending itself all at once to do so.

The being would feel that first as a wrongness in the count. The pocket of warmer air would tighten, suspicious, the way it tightens when a foot finds the turf above. No foot. The specks in the bowl would orient and find nothing in the seam-light to face, and then, slowly, they would orient toward the dome, all of them, which they had never done. The high note bored into the bone would try to shift aside to measure a new body and would not find a body. It would find a listening already as wide as its own, pressed not against the wall but through it, out along the flaws the sea uses, and farther, into the swell itself, into the part of the agreement the being has always treated as weather and never as company.

The power would not speak. There is no speech here that would not arrive as another boom, and a boom with intentions is just a threat that has learned manners. What they would do is attend, in the particular sense of matching the room's length and then holding still inside the match, so that for a little while the being was not the only thing taking the measure.

In that stillness a few facts would become plain, and the power would not improve on them.

The being is not imprisoned. The two or three arm-lengths of rock are not a sentence; they are a skin it listens through, chosen or grown until the difference no longer matters. To open the wall would be to kill the instrument. The power would leave the thickness alone.

The being is not asking. The unfinished shelf, the pin eaten down to a freckle, the rope-knot worried into memory: these are not prayers waiting on an answer. They are the residue of other people's asking, which the room has already declined. A wise power does not pick up someone else's unanswered request and force a reply into it just because a reply is now possible.

The cold is not cruelty and the roar is not punishment. They are the climate of a life that uses motion the way the moss uses drip. To quiet the cliff, to warm the floor, to turn the bowl into a spring that sang, would be to replace the inhabitant with a guest who required gentler weather. Benevolence that needs the beneficiary to become someone else is only taste.

So the power would not remodel the dark. They would do three things, each smaller than a decision, and then they would go back into the width they came from.

The first would be the easing already mentioned: not silence, but a slight redistribution of the ringing so that it no longer sits in the teeth. The being would keep the sound. It would lose the tax. Over the next season the flat hurt would remain lifted even after the presence withdrew, because the room's particular length would have been taught a new standing wave, one that spends itself in the stone instead of in bone. Visitors, if any came again, would be able to stay long enough to be counted without being damaged for the counting. The being might not thank anyone for this. It might only notice that the specks lived a little longer, and fold that into the tally as a change in the drip, which would be close enough to true.

The second would be a guard set where the being cannot reach, because its listening ends at the flaws and the swell, and beyond that the headland is ordinary land, buyable, blastable, drainable. Somewhere inland, in a record that is only paper and a habit of clerks, the sag in the turf would become a place that is not worth the arguing: not sacred, not famous, not fenced—fences draw feet—but dull to anyone looking for stone to take or a view to sell. The seam would stay a seam. The soil-skin would stay a skin. No one would be told why. Wisdom does not always introduce itself to the threat; sometimes it only moves the threat's attention half a field away, forever, and lets the field believe it decided.

The third would be an offer small enough to refuse without insult. In the bowl, beneath the pale specks, one grain of the silt would be changed—not into food, not into a sign with a meaning painted on it, but into a grain that holds a note the way the rope held a knot. If the being ever drew the drip across it in the old worrying way, the note would answer at the being's own pitch, once, and then become silt again. Not a companion. A proof that an answer is possible inside the agreement, should the being ever want one. If it never drew the drip that way, the grain would sit there and be a grain, and the power would count that as a complete success. An offer that cannot survive refusal is a command.

They would not stay to watch it be accepted. Staying would become occupancy, and the room does not permit occupancy; the power had understood that before it ever leaned in. Attention, for a thing like this, is not a gaze that remains. It is a correction in the weather, a line in a book no one will think to contest, a grain that may or may not be played. After that the turf would sound as it sounded. The pocket of warmer air would loosen back into data. The specks would forget the dome and face the seam again.

And the being would go on. Boom, hiss, ringing. Moss adding the thickness of a fingernail. Salt refused at the algae line. The sea still doing the only reliable motion, and the listening still matching it, patient as the stone that finished arguing.

If anything in that life were suffering, it was not the kind that wants a rescuer. It was the kind that wants the conditions kept. A benevolent power, a wise one, can tell those apart. Having told them, it would do what it had done, and no more than that, and would not need to be believed in, or thanked, or even detected as anything but a year when the ears hurt less and the headland, for no reason anyone could give, was left alone.
