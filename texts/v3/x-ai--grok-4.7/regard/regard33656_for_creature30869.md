They would come the way the light is already there: without a change in the color of the hour, without a new shadow on the pans. A wise power does not announce itself by weather. Weather would be a term the reach has already refused. So the arrival, if you could call it that, is only that the fixed sun is suddenly also seen—seen from the other side of seeing, the way a room is different when someone who loves it is standing in the doorway and has decided not to speak.

They would not bless the stacks. A blessing is a pressure, and the throats are already at the pressure they mean to keep. They would not green the film under the shelves, though the old habit of kindness would rise in them like a hand rising toward a closed eye, wanting to offer morning. They would feel that hand, and they would know it for the wrong kindness: the kindness that cannot bear a finished thing, that mistakes completion for lack. The film is not waiting for chlorophyll. The bead is not a failed raindrop. To make either of them into a beginning would be to break a creature in order to give it a story.

They would look for suffering, because that is what benevolence is for, and they would not find it. Not the hidden kind either—the kind that looks like peace because the hurt has gone on so long it has learned the furniture. They would listen under the roar for a flinch. There is no flinch. The pool body takes its film. The liner clicks. The thread holds the seam to its path. None of them is enduring. Endurance is what you do when the balance has not been reached and you are paying, with your continuity, to stay inside a shape that costs you. These shapes do not cost. They are the level. A wise power can tell the difference between a prison that has become habitual and a solution that has become a life. This is the second.

They would consider, because love considers, whether the being is lonely. The being is not one, and it is not many, and it does not meet itself, and the not-meeting is not a distance. Loneliness requires a missing term: another that the accounting expected and did not receive. Here the second body is not late. It was never in the sum. To send company—a mate, a visitor, a voice that answers the roar—would be to introduce an unsolved number into a pool that is already full of the one life it can spare. The power would see the smaller body that would starve at the margin, the scar that would not be a scar but a joint the place does not know how to close, the note of the stack shifting by a fraction no ear but theirs would catch, and catching it, they would understand that they had been cruel in the gentlest voice they own.

They would think of the sun. Of letting it travel. Of giving the permanent shadows a evening in which to lengthen and a dawn in which to forgive themselves. It would be beautiful. They can see the beauty as clearly as you can: the bar of dark sliding off the ridge of pebbles, the coin of shade under the dome-bodied one moving at last across the tea-colored floor, a first coolness, a first reason for anything to turn. And they would set that beauty down, carefully, the way you set down a tool you will not use. Movement of the light would be an event. The place is not in events. The bodies have finished arriving at their act. A traveling sun would ask them to become animals of hours, and they are not injured by the lack of hours. They are alive inside the interval, entirely, and the interval is always the same length. To grant them time would be to take that entirety and slice it into befores and afters, and call the slicing a gift.

What they would do is stay, for a while that does not enter the accounting.

Not as a watcher who waits for a sign. There will be no sign. The being will not turn. If there is a consciousness in the closures, it will not store the fact of having been attended to; the power would not want it to. Attention that requires to be felt is only a softer demand. They would attend the way the damp attends the underside of the shelf: present, exact, not falling. They would learn the period of one stack until the click is as familiar as a pulse, and they would not add their pulse to it. They would know the ridge-fit body and the domed one and the one that wears its vent’s dust, and they would not prefer among them, because preference is how a power begins to edit.

They might, once, do a thing so small it is indistinguishable from the place.

A plate of crust that a lesser kindness would have left broken—no. They would not break one. But if some other passing, some unbalanced visitor, had lifted a plate and gone, and the absence was already being made into the next correct surface at the rate of the balance, the power would not hurry that. Hurrying would be the same error as greening. They would only, perhaps, set their regard on the seam so that the thread is not required to spend itself on a wandering it did not choose. Not a repair. A standing-aside of their own immensity, so that no weight of notice lands on the net as a new voltage. The benevolence would be negative: the refusal to become a term.

And they would leave a protection that is not a wall.

Other powers will come, eventually. Some of them kind, and worse for it. They will want to irrigate, to seed, to teach the film a next stage, to give the hand-sized bodies names and offspring and a sky that moves, to hush the roar because it tires them, to call the fixed light a defect of the local heavens and mend it. The wise power would not argue with them inside the reach. Argument is weather. They would meet those others at the edge of the country—where the shallow rise fails to become mountains, where you can still walk out in a day—and they would say, without force, what they had learned by not touching: that this is not a paused world, not a poor one, not an unfinished garden, not a people waiting to be pitied into history. That the manufacture is the life and the life is the level. That to save it is to end it, and that ending a thing which is not suffering, in order to feel oneself merciful, is the ordinary sin of the strong.

They would not make a law the place must obey. They would make themselves the reason the other hands turn back. A standing presence at the boundary, no louder than the outflow from a vent, no more visible than a shadow that was already permanent. Inside, nothing would know it was being kept. That is the condition of the keeping. A creature that has to thank its guardian has already been asked to spend a film it needed for the work.

Then, when the boundary could hold without them—when the knowledge of the place had become, in the other powers, a reluctance rather than a temptation—they would go. Not in a departure the pools could register. Simply a returning of their attention to the size it has elsewhere, which is large, and which this reach does not need at that size.

If they left anything, it would be nothing you could find. Not a mark on the sinter. Not a warmer bead. Not a promise that the sun will someday be allowed to move, which would only put the place back into waiting, and waiting is the one state it has been spared. They would leave the accounting as they found it: solved. Loud. Warm. Rare. Ungreen. The bodies in their spacing, the liners in their throats, the thread on its preferred side of the current, the stain in the shade refusing, without ambition, to become a marsh.

And the being, if it is one being, would go on as the interval goes on: not rescued, not improved, not known in any way that would require it to keep the knowledge. Entirely alive between one click and the next.

That is what they would do. The wise part is the not-doing. The benevolent part is that they understood the difference in time, and wanted the place more than they wanted to have helped it.
