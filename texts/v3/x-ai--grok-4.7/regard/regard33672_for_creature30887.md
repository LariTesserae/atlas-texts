They come the way delayed light comes: not by crossing the rim, but by having already been in the tissue, waiting for a path. No weather marks them. The frog-pairs do not alter their claims. A heron-thing lifts, then settles on the same ridge, having mistaken a change in attention for a change in grade and corrected itself. That is the first courtesy. A power that needs the ecology to kneel has already failed the place.

They do not specify. They could. The seed-cases would open for them; the colleagues—capillary, viscosity, the remembered hills—would recognize an older negotiator and make room. A cistern could be asked to be a cistern forever, a floor fixed against the third tick, a name pinned so it would not wear out. All of that is within reach, and all of it is the sentence already being served by the plain. Wisdom, arriving, declines the tools of finishing. Benevolence that completes this inhabitant would be a second rim, drawn tighter.

So they walk as the citizens walk, feet in the sheet, not over it. They drink from the weep. They do not lease an amendment or offer one. In the bitter quarter the insect-cloud does not deform around their breath; there is no lie in them and no price. People glance, the way they glance at a mammal whose hours they do not yet know, and go back to duration and radius. The power is not unrecognized. Recognition here is not ceremony. It is the absence of bracing.

They spend the longest time with the keepers, not as a superior but as someone who also counts costs. The copper-green halls are warmer than they should be. A constant bent for generations has a fever the town has agreed not to call a fever, because nothing dramatic fails and the bowls are still caught. The power puts a palm a finger’s width from a working wall—the same distance the citizens use when they read a seed aloud—and does not amend the law. They amend the accounting. The heat that was being spent to hold the older slope is not removed; it is met. For one interval, the tissue does not pay alone. The keepers, who have not slept, sleep. Not in a fixed room. In the inhale of whatever will have them. They dream of nothing finished. When they wake, the halls are as warm as before, the colleagues as exacting, the work still theirs. Only the arrears of a single night have been paid by someone who can afford a night.

That is the action. Not a new law. A settled debt, local, unleased, gone by morning in the sky and still, faintly, in the delayed morning that moves through the walls.

Elsewhere they do less, which is harder. A child naming a frog is not given a truer name. Inaccurate affection is already part of the temperature; correcting it would be the taboo in a kinder coat. A person whose seed-cases have begun to collapse inward, from too many braced nights, is not healed by decree. The power walks them the long way across the saucer, as a neighbor would, until the habit loosens. The walking is the help. To do more would teach the grain a permanent door.

At the rim they stop. Beyond is the finished plain, silent, dry, done. They could unfinish it. They could raise one remembered hill and let the city’s amendments spill into a country large enough to be safe, various, eternal. They look at that possibility the way the keepers look at a constant that is beginning to cost too much, and they leave the plain alone. The saucer’s jurisdiction is not a prison they are kind enough to open. It is the size of the refusal that keeps this animal alive. Enlarge it and you dilute it into weather. Abolish the rim and the unfinishedness has nothing finished to refuse, and goes out.

Before they go, they do one thing with no recipient. On a lawn nobody is leasing they specify a low warmth, the same warmth the town already leaves for itself at dusk, and they do not stay to be thanked when people find it and stand in it until the duration ends. The specification is ordinary. What is not ordinary is that it ends cleanly, with no residue of a foreign hand in the felt, so that later no one can make a memorial of the spot. The frogs resume. The tower kneels into a hall on its own schedule. Small feet thunder inside a petiole.

They do not announce departure. Delayed light does not announce arrival either; it simply ceases to be stored and becomes the hour. If the inhabitant notices—a keeper’s forearms a little less like working xylem, a debt missing from the count, a warmth that behaved—it will not build a story that finishes. It will call the interval by a rhythm for a while, and the floor will sometimes answer out of habit in the grain, and then the name will wear out at the only honest speed.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, when the being in the place is already refusing, successfully, to be done: it pays one night the tissue cannot pay, walks one person the long way, and will not save them from the shape of their life.
