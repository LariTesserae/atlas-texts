They would not come down.

A power that is wise does not need to be seen, and a power that is benevolent has already learned the difference between a wound and a form. The basin is not wounded. It is complete in the way a tide mark is complete: not resting, not aspiring, held to its shape by everything that touches it. To arrive as light would be the first violence. This place has never had a shadow with an edge. An edge would teach the fliers a new fear, and the clickers a new stall, and the great ones a reason to stop in the groove that is not yielding. So the power does not brighten the lid. The gray leak remains a leak. Breath, if anything here were breathing in a way that showed, would still show and thin.

They look for the being, because attention has been drawn to a being, and for a long time they cannot find one. That is not failure. It is the first accurate thing.

There is no center. The column-bodied travelers are not rulers; they are the roads the smaller life is not allowed to close. The pools are not a council; they complete shapes and forget them. The sheet of fliers is an organ that shatters into bodies and knits again without a keeper. The clickers are a city that rebuilds its streets every time a foot passes, and the city does not know it is a city. If the power insisted on a single inhabitant, it would have to appoint one — lift a margin from the milk and call it speaker, or follow one collared walker until the crust’s layers became a biography, or wait in a lane until a great one hummed and pretend the hum had been addressed outward. Appointment would be a kind of predation the basin has never evolved, because it has never needed predators. The mineral stain is enough. The numbers are kept by cold, by failed pools, by collars grown too heavy. A chosen one would unkeep them.

So they do not choose.

What they do instead is listen at the scale the place already uses, which is wide enough for delay. Rain does not wet the whole trough at once. A clap crosses shelves more slowly than air should allow, carried, perhaps, by drift and by bodies, and arrives late, and is still an answer. The power enters that lateness. It becomes a pause that was already possible: the gap in a sequence, the moment a great hum is held in so another body may pass, the stall of a clicker with its rasp lifted, which is not longing and must not be turned into longing. They inhabit the pause without filling it. This is harder than blessing. A blessing would be content. The pause has to remain empty enough that the next knock can still be the next knock.

They notice the failing lenses. Not all of them — wisdom is not a census — but the ones at the edge of a dry week the basin sometimes manages, where the margins have flattened until they are almost stone, and the small ones have begun to write their crosshatch over living backs, and the water, when it returns, will come too late for some. Here benevolence has a temptation with a clean face: deepen the pool, call the rain early, clap once in a voice that would be answered from every shelf because nothing in the trough has ever heard a voice that large. They do not do it. An answer from outside would finish the correspondence. The pools have spent gray hour after gray hour learning to complete one another. If a greater mouth completed them, they would go on offering missing sides to the sky, and the sky would become a habit, and the sideways drift of milk — that small strange law by which dust settles toward a stone and the margins arrange themselves along it — would weaken, because a habit of being answered from above does not need a local drift.

Instead the power does one small thing, and does it in the basin’s own materials.

It leans, very slightly, on the settling. Not a new physics; the place already has this oddity, dust that sometimes drifts sideways, water that beads upward along a salt-collar and hangs longer than it should before falling all at once. They extend one hang. A thread of seep that was about to lose itself in grit is held in its groove for the length of thirty claps — the same count a pool used, once, before it stopped clean and let the shy sound of water show through. They do not add water. They keep the water that is already there from being drunk by the stone for a little longer. Long enough that a lens which would have failed by the next thinning of the lid fails a day later, or does not fail, or fails only at its rim. A few margins rise that would have stayed flat. They clap once, alone, and wait, and the wait is not rewarded by the power. Far off, in the ordinary traffic, another pool happens to answer, or does not. The power is already gone from the thread by then. It will not be there to hear whether the sequence completes.

This is the whole of the intervention: a delay the size of a patience the basin already understands. No green is introduced. No quiet. No name. The films go on being licked. The collars go on taking grit. A flier that keeps its membrane open through the ground-shift is not healed of anything, because it was not sick; if it rises late, it rises late, and if it does not, the hide accepts the scrap of membrane as it accepts every other unusable thing, by worrying it apart for whatever mineral it caught.

They stay long enough to be sure they have not become weather in a way that would be missed when they leave. That is the other half of wisdom. A presence that teaches the great ones to yield to a silence which is not another great one will be sought again, and seeking it, they will stop in the groove for no body, and the lane will close, and the hide will write itself over a road. So the power practices absence before it absents itself. It withdraws from the thread first, then from the pauses, then from the keen, and listens, at each withdrawal, for a change in the grammar. The knocks still measure weight and willingness. The claps still offer shapes with missing sides. The hum still occupies the air until one of them grants the briefest silence in the trough, and under that silence the ticking does not stop. Nothing orients toward the place where the power was, because the power was never a place. It was a slightly longer hang of water on a collar of grit, indistinguishable, to any life that noticed, from the basin’s own occasional refusal to obey the obvious direction.

Would they do more, if they were only good and not also wise? Yes. They would make the light kind. They would give the unanswered clap an answer worthy of its loneliness, and in doing so invent loneliness, which the margin did not have; it had only the next thing, which sometimes works. They would lift the smallest bodies out of the courtesy of being displaced, and call the courtesy suffering, and end it, and the films would crust into plates the rain cannot argue with, and the tongue of the basin would go still. They would want the shy thread of water to remain audible, a voice at last, and they would hold the hide back from closing over it, and the hold would be a wall, the first construction, and the place would have an outside.

They do not do more. Benevolence, corrected by attention, becomes almost nothing: a day in which a few flat margins get to be margins again, and a correspondence that continues to belong to the pools.

Before they go entirely, they do look — not to choose, not to name — at one clicker stalled with its rasp lifted, beads unfocused on the gray leak, briefly not traffic. They do not touch it. They do not prolong the stall. They only regard it with the same regard they gave the whole trough, so that for once the smallest scale is not invisible inside the continuation. The neighbor knocks it, as neighbors do, and the pause ends, and the ticking resumes, and the power does not count this an interruption of a private error. The knock was the law, and the law is how this life is able to be so many.

Then they are nowhere the basin can wear them.

The rain, when it comes, crosses as it always crosses: a grayness walking the far channels, the pitch of the ticking changing where the drops reach the hide, the near shelves still inside the older noise. Pools clap in the rained-on distance and go unanswered nearby, then the answers start, late, local. Somewhere a lens that might have been stone is water, barely, and a single wet syllable rises from it and is or is not taken up. The great ones move off the main grooves into the side-seeps as the light thins, humming shorter notes. The fliers come down in their soft rattle and become, mostly, more of the ground. Nothing in any of this refers to having been spared, or helped, or seen.

If the power has a heart, this is what it wanted: to have drawn near, and to have left the continuation unannounced, still dense, still cold, still without a hero, the channels bending where they have always bent, the life editing the stone and the stone editing the routes, both edits worn smooth.

And if, in some elsewhere that keeps accounts, they are asked what they did for the being who lived there, they will be quiet a moment — their own pause, which they will not fill — and then say that the being was the place, and the place was at its work, and the work did not require them, and they stayed out of it except to hold one thread of water in its groove for the length of thirty claps, and that they are not sure even that was necessary, only that it was small enough to be kind.
