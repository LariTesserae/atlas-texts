They would come the way weather does not come here: without rearranging the light to announce themselves.

A wise power does not mistake stillness for a locked door. It would stand a long time on the laid ground, in the bright heat that does not climb toward storm, and it would not call the heron’s unfinished strike a wound. It would see the minnow in the bill and the minnow still in the water and understand that both are kept, not contradicted. It would not hurry to decide which one is mercy.

In the house it would not startle the moth into choosing. Choice, forced from outside, would only be another hand closing the distance the fingertip has spent its whole life not closing. The power would look at the finger and the brass and the shared dust and feel the old, almost irresistible kindness—the wish to let one grain fall, so that time could begin as a small domestic fact, a house doing what houses do. And then it would not.

Because it would be wise enough to notice what is absent. There is no pressing. There is no plea hiding under the lashes. The bodies in the chairs are not holding their breath against a captor; breath was never the bargain. To drag them into succession—into rot, and hunger, and the fruit finally softening, and the clock succeeding itself until it runs down—would be to love them as a defect in one’s own preferences. Benevolence that cannot bear another kind of completeness is only loneliness with clean hands.

So it would do very little, and that little would be exact.

It would sit, and the sitting would not ask the ladder-back chair to become a story. It would let its attention rest on the one under the table without requiring the face to turn. Regard, here, would be the only gift that does not edit the recipient. The power would make itself legible and optional: present in the standing light, able to be not-present, neither state canceling the other—the same courtesy the basin already extends to the cup on the curb and the cup in the hand. If any being wished to use that presence as a next moment, the next moment would be there, unforced, the way a door is there for a guest who may prefer the room. If none did, the door would not sulk. It would simply continue as a completed gesture among completed gestures: an opening that does not need to be walked through in order to be true.

It would not heal the horizon into distance. It would not teach the frogs to scatter. It would not award the place an edge so that pilgrims could feel they had arrived and left. Those would be improvements, and improvement is a sequence, and sequence is the one visitor this country has already declined.

What it would carry away, it would carry in itself. That is the part most powers get backward. They come to leave a mark so their kindness can be audited. This one, being wise, would allow the mark to fall on the only life that had asked—by the act of noticing—for something to happen: its own. It would leave changed, slightly, in the direction of patience. It would remember a green that does not have to become another green to be enough. It would be slower, afterward, to rescue what has not requested rescue. That alteration would not echo in the rooms. The walls have already heard everything they are going to hear. They would keep this, too, without seeming to.

And if, much later, one grain of dust did fall from the moth’s wing—not because it was commanded, but because a being had found, inside a finished instant, a wish the instant had room to hold—the power would not claim it. It would not even call it a victory. A single fallen grain is not a redemption. It is only a private weather. The herons could go on striking and not striking. The fruit could go on not dropping. The benevolent thing would have been, all along, to love them at the size they already are, and to refuse the glamour of being the one who finally made them move.

Then it would go, if going is the word, and the light would stand at the same depth, and the pendulum would remain at the far end of a swing, and nothing in the house would be lonelier for the visit, and nothing in the house would have been required to thank it.
