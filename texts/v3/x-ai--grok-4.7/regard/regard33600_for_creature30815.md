They would not drain it.

That is the first thing, and it would arrive before any other kindness could dress itself as help. A power that can see the basin entire — the four darker pulls, the wheel, the warm stone that used to be mouths — would understand at once that the flood is not a misfortune waiting on a rescuer. It is the climate the town has already accepted as its body. To lift the water off would be to unfinish a finished negotiation: roofs that are floors, dead that have changed jobs, lanes that reassert themselves because the current still prefers them. Benevolence, if it is wise, does not mistake an unfamiliar good for a wound.

They would not bring lamps. Light, here, is an event, not a right. A steady shining from above would turn the grain into targets and the cruisers into spectacles, and the town would spend generations learning fear of a clarity it never asked to be judged by. They would not teach names. A name is a handle, and nothing in the stacking is trying to be carried. They would not hand down tools. The builders already have the only tool that matters, which is the paper of themselves, laid against the neighbor, against the crack, against the dark. To improve that with an outside instrument would be to introduce a hurry, and hurry is the one weather this water has refused.

They would look a long time. Wisdom, before it acts, spends itself on scale. They would learn the schedule the way a stayer would: thicken, eat or build; thin, repair the gap you are; lean, follow, unless you are the kind that must not. They would taste the difference between a death and a birth and see that the basin already marks both, and does not need a second marking. They would notice the oldest crust, pumping nothing, persisting as stiffness, and they would not call it tragic that a life can be promoted into foundation. They would notice the cross-cutter's diagonal and the way the town closes behind it without cruelty, and they would not edit the angle out of love for the cruiser. Love that cannot stand the eaten is too small for this place. The eaten are how the thickness speaks.

What they would do, if anything, is keep the decision standing.

Not the animals — the animals keep themselves, by the only method they have, which is continuing. The decision: that the water stays. A benevolent power attending a held place would set itself at the edges, where the basin wall rises into marl and old roots, and where the outside world still has weather that is not this weather. Drought that would crack the skin and turn the lanes to paste. A cut in the far ground that would pull the wheel empty in a season and leave the shells to dry into a mere ruin, which they have never been. A seep of something bitter, upstream of the valley, that the grain could not make small enough to survive. Cold, true cold, the kind that spends heat faster than any blunt body can replace it. These are not part of the occupied balance. They are erasures of the terms. Against those, the power would be a quiet stubbornness, the way the lanes are a quiet stubbornness: not a wall the town could feel as a wall, not a guardian with a face in the murk, only the continued fact that the rain arrives already blurred, that the mineral thread still rises, that the four pulls do not fail.

They might, once, do something smaller than that, and only because it would be indistinguishable from the town's own habits. Somewhere a joint has been giving along the same line for years, and the line is about to prefer a collapse that would bend a lane past what the downstream settlers can correct. Not a drama — a mistake in the masonry, the sort that happens when maintenance in the interior has gone on so long it has forgotten a particular stress. The power could ease that line by a finger's width toward the old grid, a pressure no greater than a large body passing, and the builders would taste a clearer thread and build back, and no one would have been rescued because no one would have known there was a rescue to thank. Wisdom likes gifts that do not require a receiver to become a petitioner.

They would not speak. There is no ear here arranged for a voice, and a voice dropped into this hush would be louder than the rain, which is the loudest thing, and would remain as a new kind of weather the stacking would have to negotiate from the beginning. If they have a language at all that the basin could bear, it is the one already in use: a change in thickness so slight it reads as the ordinary pulse, a warmth that arrives a moment before nothing arrives, a lean in the water that still points along the streets. They might pass through the wheel once, in that dialect, and the layers would space themselves around the passage as they space themselves around any cruiser, and the agreement would hold, and the power would leave the number of the band unchanged. To be treated as traffic is, here, a form of welcome. They would not refuse it.

And they would not stay in the manner of a ruler. Attention, prolonged into occupancy, would become another animal in the stack — the largest, the one that does not shed, the one whose hunger is for seeing — and the town would begin, slowly, to build toward that hunger, as it builds toward every reliable current. Roofs would orient to a presence. Lanes would keep a gap that had nothing to do with water. That would be a kind of colonization performed by kindness. So the wise thing is to look until looking is complete, to set the keeping at the edges where it will be mistaken for geology, to ease the one joint if the one joint is truly about to forget the grid, and then to withdraw the face of the attention so that what remains is only the condition: rain, seep, warmth, the held valley, the dark that is not a lack.

If they left anything like a blessing, it would be unreadable on purpose. Not a mark on the plates. Not a new pulse of copper in the murk. Only this: that the schedule continues to be enough. That a young cruiser, shouldering back into its band, is not failing a greater life it ought to have been given. That the tube with no picture of the town is not ignorant. That the city remembering streets — not as memory, not as intention, but as a stubbornness in the plan — is already a complete way to be a city. A benevolent power does not improve a finished creature. It refuses the flattery of improvement. It lets the low ground go on doing what low ground does, given enough time and enough bodies, and it calls that refusal care.

They would do that. And then, because wisdom is also the knowledge of when an act is finished, they would do nothing else. The rain would keep arriving. The wheel would keep its four ways out. Somewhere a scale would slough and be claimed before it landed, and the argument of which face should be up would proceed without witnesses, which is how it has always proceeded, and how, under this attention, it would be allowed to proceed still.
