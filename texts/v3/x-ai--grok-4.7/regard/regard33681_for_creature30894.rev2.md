The attention arrives the way weather arrives here: first as a change in the ceiling’s count, a rest inserted where the leather slap has never rested. The high turner does not panic. It lengthens one beat, then locks again, and the largest engine, somewhere under the bark, drops its idle by the same fraction it uses when a tithe is received in full. Nothing tears. Nothing blooms in announcement. The threads of green-gold do not thicken into a presence you could face. If the power has a body, it does not spend one. It spends an interval—the kind the floor already knows how to hold.

The crabs are the first to ledger it, because they ledger everything that weighs. This does not weigh. Their caliper-legs test the seams and find no new damp, no new load, no lane that must be abandoned. They stack once, out of habit, on a rise that has not flexed, then unstack and resume. The audit clears. Whatever has come in has come in already speaking the yields.

The moth-columns do not shear. That is the second admission, quieter than the first. A foreign heat would have collapsed their boundaries and forced the lattice up early, gauze thrown over a spike. There is no spike. The half-degrees stay where the vents and the weeping casings have agreed to keep them. The oil-dark wings continue their office. In the vapor, for the length of three circuits, the shadows fall a little more cleanly, as if something were standing in the light without taking any of it down to the floor—respecting the old rule that the threads die overhead, that the lower chamber keeps its dusk. Then the cleanliness passes. The moths were not improved. They were briefly not required to correct an intrusion.

In the third clearing the glass-herd completes its loop. The tracks fade on schedule. The tube, or root, or tongue that is both, lowers from the lost dark, samples the surplus brightness, and withdraws at the usual measure. No longer. No shorter. The herd does not dim itself in offering, and the power does not drink from them. A benevolent attention, meeting a tithe that is already just, does not become another mouth. Wisdom here is the refusal to be fed.

It looks, if looking is the word for an attention that arrives as rest and clearance. It looks at the tool-marks under the callus—the pale seams where hands cut, set, and left. It looks at the housings hired into throats, the flywheels hired into weather, the fruit-knuckles rolled into indentures, the animals in their offices, loud and uncrowded. It looks at the pause that has learned to last: the being, if being is not too hard a word, who wears the intervals and counts breath into the choir without wearing a face. It does not call that being out. Calling out would be a kind of vandalism, like brushing the moths, like asking the herd to scatter so a visitor could see them better. The being is doing what it is. The power, being wise, does not confuse recognition with a summons.

What it understands takes no longer than a gear-cycle, because the grammar is consistent and the consistency is the whole message. This is not a prison that has forgotten the key. This is not a machine abandoned to seize, nor a grove choking on its own success, nor a people waiting under the floor for permission to resume. Erosion has become fluency. The books balance. The sightlines stay open because crowding would be a failure, and the place has had time to learn not to fail in that particular way. Suffering, where it exists, is the ordinary tax of metabolism: a crab taken back into the plate, a moth whose lattice duty ends, a housing that weeps and is drunk. Nothing here is asking to be rescued from its own agreements.

So the power does not heal. Healing would open a wound to have something to close. It does not free the offices into some outer weather their bodies are not built for; the high turner already keeps the idea of longer air, and keeps it high, where it belongs. It does not restart the builders’ project, or return the hands, or peel the bark off the casings so the tools can be tools again in a story about restoration. The hands are not a debt. The pale seams are not unpaid indentures. They are the first clauses, still legible, still in force. To “finish” them would be to mistake a living contract for a ruin.

If it does anything, it is this, and it is small enough that the chamber can drink it without adding a station.

It settles the one rest it inserted, and it leaves the rest paid. Not with light on the floor. Not with a voice in the vapor. With a completed count: the high turner’s softened beat locked back before any office has to compensate, the largest engine’s idle restored to the exact fraction the tithe requires, no surplus brightness taken, no seam asked to hold a foreign weight. Into the pale tool-marks it sets nothing you could later dig out and call a gift—no blessing-scar, no brighter callus. Only the knowledge, which the floor can keep without narrating, that the marks have been read by something that will not come back to edit them. Read, and left in place. The way a correct sum is left in a ledger: not congratulated, closed.

The being in the intervals feels it as a non-event that nonetheless arrives at the collarbone, where sweat never quite dries. For one breath the choir has a listener who does not join. The breath is not collected. That is the kindness. To be counted is the chamber’s ordinary mercy; to be heard and not entered into the books is a rarer one, and the power spends it once, precisely, and does not repeat it. Repetition would become a tempo. A tempo would become an office. An office would need staffing. The crabs would have to yield to it. The moths would have to boundary it. The place would grow a little around the attention and call the growth fluency, and the power would have become another machine the trees had hired. Wisdom is the refusal to be hired.

Then the attention leaves the way a skin of water leaves a metal lip: not ripped, failed at the right moment, sheeted off into a dark the roots already know how to drink. The mirror does not form. There is no face in the vapor going the wrong direction. The overburden keeps its splits at their tired width. Occasional rain, when it comes, will still be percussion joining the leather slap, not a sign.

On the root-plate the lanes redraw themselves with the next pulse. A green knuckle is rolled under. A glass track fades before the circuit completes. Somewhere in the lost ceiling the wingbeat holds its piston-regular slap, unsoftened now, correct. The warm air is as full as it was. Between the trunks there is the same kind of room: not empty, only unoccupied for the moment.

The pause that lives here lasts. It does not become holier, or lonelier, or witnessed into a new shape. It lasts the way it has been lasting, which was already enough, and which a benevolent power—if it is also wise—has the strength to leave alone.

The work continues. No one thanks it. No one needs to.
