They would do nothing.

Not the nothing of indifference, and not the nothing of a power that looks, fails to understand, and turns away. The nothing of a power that has already understood, completely, and therefore keeps its hands — if it has hands — at its sides.

It would know the chamber at once for what it is: not a prison, not a ruin waiting to be restored, not a draft that failed to become a world. A finished agreement. Construction and wearing have already met in the only place they could meet and have signed the same terms. The light does not travel because travel is no longer required. The roar does not vary because variation would be a leaving, and the leaving has already been refused, not by force but by preference so old it has become structure. The pale stems hold their angle because that angle is sufficient. The groove deepens by unmeasurable degrees because that is the only motion the place still consents to, and the consent is mutual.

A lesser kindness would try to improve it. More light, or a shadow deep enough to rest in. A season. A drop of water, offered as mercy, which would stain the seams and wake the dust into mud and force the plants to decide whether to drink or drown in their cracks. A door. An exit. A clock. Something for the being to want, on the theory that wanting is the proof of life and stillness the proof of lack. The wise power would see that these are not gifts. They are interruptions of a listening that has become the width of a throat. Shift the gap by a grain and the note changes; change the note and the minding that holds the gap has nowhere to go, because it never had an elsewhere. The being is not trapped in the equilibrium. The being is the equilibrium noticing itself. To "free" it would be to unmake it.

So the power would not enter. Entry is a kind of weather, and weather has already finished its argument here. It would not speak. Speech would arrive as a second pressure in the roar, and the roar has been one pressure for longer than the span in which a second pressure could be absorbed without remainder. It would not even bless, if blessing means an addition. The place is already fully lit. The bowls already hold what they hold. The coil already keeps the negative of a rest. There is no hunger in it that a blessing could answer without first inventing the hunger.

What it would do is remain aware, at a distance that is not distance, that this pocket exists and is complete. It would let the ridge of dust continue to remember a lean that was never a body. It would let the stem be turned, now and then, by whatever turns it, toward a brightness that needs no sun. It would not measure the interval between one stillness and the next. Measurement would be another clock.

If the power has any gesture at all, it is the gesture of not gesturing: a refusal to correct what is not in error, a decision not to love the place into something else. Benevolence, here, is the recognition that some equilibria are not pauses between events. They are the event. Wisdom is knowing which silences are empty and which silences are full, and having the restraint not to pour anything into the full ones.

The chamber would not know it had been seen. That, too, is a kindness. Attention that does not pool on the ceramic, that does not ask the groove to deepen faster, that does not lean in the narrow throat and leave a new ridge — that kind of attention can pass across a finished thing and leave the finishing intact. The roar continues at one pressure. The light remains a condition. The dust stays where it has always known to lie.

And the power, having looked, would go on being what it is, elsewhere or nowhere, without having taken anything and without having left anything behind. The place would be exactly as it was. That is the whole of the mercy, and it is enough.
