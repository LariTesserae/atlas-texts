They would come to the rim and stay there.

Not as weather, not as a second glare laid over the first. The attention would be the kind that does not add a sound to the roar already traveling the mat, and does not warm the thin air by so much as the width of a breath. From the shoulder they would see the dish still deciding, the dark seams that lose themselves, the iron roofs small as dropped tools, the cable sagging under its own damp. They would understand, without needing to descend, that the being is not a figure that could turn and meet them. It is the mat’s continuous body, the turnover working unseen in the fiber, the circuit of crews who borrow the light and refuse the dark, and the machines that have learned not to pause. A single presence, widely distributed, already fully occupied with the work of holding water and of being slowly translated into sheets.

Wisdom, meeting that, would refuse the obvious gifts. No clearing of the noise so that flocks might settle—the roar is the climate the place has accepted, and to lift it would be to invent a different basin. No quickening of the scars, though the darker rectangles close with a patience that looks, from outside, like neglect. The mat remembers the cuts by the rate at which it creeps; to hurry the anastomosis would teach it a false season. No fish slipped into the poor water, no nest sites cribbed on the rock knobs. What has not arrived has looked from the saddle and kept going for reasons that are still true. A benevolent power that stuffed the empty pools with life would only be importing an ecology the cold and the knock cannot keep, and then grieving the failure as if it were a wound.

They might, if anything, tend the conditions that already exist and are easy to misread as lack. A slight steadiness in the seep along the weeping lines, so the brighter moss fans do not fail when a joint shifts. A keeping of the rotation the cutters already practice—never more sod than the fiber can afford in a year—so that no crew, pressed by someone farther down the cable, is tempted to shorten the memory written in the scars. Perhaps a small protection for the tools stood upright in the mat: not a miracle, only that the moss testing the metal for purchase does not quite succeed before the next visit, so the wrench is still a wrench and not yet a stone. These would be adjustments at the scale of what the basin itself is already arguing for. Most of them would be indistinguishable from the place continuing as it has.

They would not build a house, or still the generator, or teach the bird to land. They would not speak. The workers would not feel watched in any way that changed the weight of a wet sod or the moment they chose to start down while the racks still threw their blue bars. If one of them paused on the shoulder at the end of a day and felt, briefly, that the whole dish was held in a regard that did not require anything of it, they would most likely call it fatigue, or the way the mist lifts without leaving, and they would be right enough. The power’s benevolence would include being deniable.

And after a time—long by the measure of a visit, short by the measure of a seam lengthening a pace—they would do the last thing, which is also the first: they would let the attention thin back into the ordinary light. Not withdrawn in disappointment. Simply finished with the task of having understood. The mill would keep its interval. The water would keep sliding under the fiber with that thick sound no engine quite covers. The basin would go on deciding what it is, unhurried, and the being that lives as process rather than as a sleeper in a dry room would not have been saved from anything, because it had not been in danger of the kind a power is meant to answer. It would only have been seen, accurately, all the way to the edges of its noise and its patience, and then left to the long work that was already its own.
