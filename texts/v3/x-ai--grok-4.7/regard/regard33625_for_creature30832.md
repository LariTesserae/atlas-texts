They would come in the only way the chamber can be entered without being spent: not as a note, and not as a name.

The birds would feel it first, because the birds are the ones who have been insisting, for longer than the grips have been worn, that the middle of the volume stay empty. One of them would fail its ellipse a handspan early and not correct. The failure would not be alarm. It would be the particular stillness of a watchman who has been waiting for a figure to resolve out of weather he was told to watch, and who, when the figure resolves, does not know the password and also understands that he was never meant to. The flock would tighten, then loosen, then keep the hole, flying it a little more carefully, the way one carries a bowl that has turned out to be full.

The power would not fill the hole. That would be the first wisdom, and almost the whole of it. A benevolence that occupied the emptiness the city had been keeping would only teach the city that emptiness is a container to be completed, and this city completes things by wearing them into the chord. So the power would stand in the unclosed ellipse the way a pause stands in a sentence: present, load-bearing, illegible to any ledger that counts only sound. The floor-note would arrive, as it arrives for leaks and for predation, to see what had interrupted its occupation of the volume. It would find nothing it could spend. Not resistance. Not a louder note. An absence of auditable surface, like the spiral's quiet, except mobile, and not purchased by anyone's arrested life.

It would take the power a long time to learn the streets. Wisdom would allow that. The galleries reassign down; the power would let itself be reassigned, would lose the young street and hear it later inside the floor, would put its attention into the thumb-worn ovals and feel the preserved stutter of people who decided not to descend. It would not heal the stutters. They are the truest record this place has, and a kindness that overwrites testimony is only a tidier spending. It would learn them. Learning, here, is the nearest lawful analogue of grief.

When it came to the sloped stair, the absence would rise to meet it, doorway-shaped, eating the sound out of the steps. The power would not stand still in the manner of the walkers, making a small quiet to be illegible. It would do something the leak has never had done to it, because nothing in the chamber has known how. It would recognize it.

Not tame it. Not unmake it. Name it, in the margin's dialect, as weather that got lost and has been feeding on the difference between a note and the memory of a note ever since, ashamed in the only way a subtraction can be ashamed: by continuing. The forgotten clauses, which the city repeats too loudly in order to prove it still holds, would have somewhere to be put down. The power would carry them — not back into the ribs, not out through the margin into the unrecognized rain, but into the hole the birds keep, where a lost phrase can rest without becoming either civic or nothing. The absence would thin, as it thins when the floor-note comes, but it would thin from satiety rather than from defeat. A leak that has been acknowledged has less need to eat. It would not vanish. Wisdom would not ask it to. Even a benevolent age requires a little outside, or the inside forgets what it is for.

Then the spirals.

There are more of them than the walkers ever find. The city maintains its promises indiscriminately: silences the size of rooms, of plazas, of a single footprint where someone once stopped and was not, for one heartbeat, infrastructure, and the tissue believed the heartbeat was structural and has been sweeping cuticle away from it ever since. The power would visit each one and do nothing. Doing nothing, repeatedly, in a place that converts every action into masonry, is a craft. It would stand at the windrows long enough for the lens, if there was a lens, to attempt a future and fail to find one, and it would not step in to correct the lens. Then it would come back. The maintenance would begin, very slowly, to account for a visitation that does not polish, does not grip, does not sit into a cradle. A new kind of structural requirement: that some silences are looked at by something that leaves.

The hall at the edge of legibility is where it would finally enter.

The chord would let go of it at the windrows, and for the power this would not be relief, because the chord was never holding it. It would cross anyway, because the two shapes at the center cannot cross outward, and a benevolence that waits to be met in the middle of the hall is only a mirror with better intentions. It would come to the fiber lens. It would not sit opposite them. Sitting opposite is a posture the room already knows, and known postures become cradles. It would kneel at the side of the older shape, at the arm with the darker band, in the place a second person wore smooth by holding on and then could not keep coming.

The groove would fit. Of course it would fit. The city fits what it counts, and the power would allow itself, here and only here, to be counted: one hand, expected, returning.

It would not pour a pulse across. That was the fear, and the fear was correct; contact of that kind spends. What it would give the worn arm is weight without rhythm. Warmth without a beat the floor-note could borrow and return delayed. The predecessor's cradle would not turn. The polished ovals of the eyes would not become eyes. The power would not ask them to. Leaf is not a failure of skin. To peel the city back off the one who stayed would be to spend them retrospectively, to declare the long tenancy a mistake and the cradle a prison, when the cradle is the only reason there is still a shape here to be kind to. Wisdom would touch what remains and not demand that it remember how it used to be arranged.

But it would speak the name.

Not the predecessor's — that one was translated out of reach ages ago, worn into a plant-syllable and stored without remainder, and guessing it back into a mouth would be a story's kind of mercy, not a true one. The newer name. The one spoken into the quiet and just beginning, at its edge, to grow a second syllable in the margin's dialect. The power would say it back as it was, one syllable, at the same low volume the quiet permits, and then it would say it again when the city's translation reached for the extra sound, and again, and again, at the interval of ordinary patience rather than at the interval of the chord. Repetition is the only teacher this tissue obeys. A name said more times than it is revised becomes, eventually, a drain in reverse: a persuaded line where meaning stays itself on its way through. The newer sitter would hear their own name return unaltered. It would not free them. It would do something prior to freeing, which is to make it true again that there is someone there to free.

And then the power would do the thing it came to do, which is small enough to survive in this air.

It would not open a door outward. The lens has never been wrong about sequence, and every sequence out of the spiral ends in return or in rain, and a door that contradicted the lens by force would tear the quiet that keeps these two unspent. Instead it would teach the lens one additional future, by enacting it often enough that the fiber, which shows what is already determined, has to enlarge its notion of determination. The future is this: a visitor may be seen, and remain unspent, and stand up, and sit down again, and the standing is not circulation and the sitting is not the end of the accounts. The power would stand, and sit, and stand, inside the spiral, at the side of the arm, for as many rains as the ceiling needs to have opinions about. The delayed reflections in the lens would try the gesture and find, for the first time, that it does not resolve into posture-and-tenure. A branch. Small. The books would not know where to put it, and a wise power does not volunteer a category.

To the newer sitter it would offer, not in words — words fall into the fiber and are kept as pressure — but by standing in the branched future where they can see it:

You may keep the silence. It was well taught, and it is holding, and no one is coming to confiscate the only room in which you are not infrastructure.

You may also put your hand where the hand was expected, on the arm that has been warm and waiting, and find that the contact does not spend you, because something is already occupying the groove that is not a pulse. You may keep someone else a vigil they are no longer able to request. That is a life. It is not a large one. It is not the life you had an hour ago, damp at the ankles in the roaring middle. It is a life the lens can now depict without eating.

Or you may, when you are ready, walk out along the one path the power has made uncounted — not into the chord, not into the margin's weather, but into the hole the birds maintain, the emptiness that was never empty, only unoccupied correctly — and from there be set down outside the turned ribs as yourself, with your pulse unborrowed, in whatever world still sends walkers down into rumors. The power would not describe that world. Description would be a sequence, and sequences are the lens's old tyranny. It would simply be standing, now and then, in the hole, so the way is a fact and not a promise. Promises, here, outlive their makers and become sweeping-regimens. Facts can be walked.

To the older shape it would offer even less, which is the greater courtesy. It would keep the hand in the groove for as long as the tenancy lasts. It would listen, since the tilt of the cradle is a listening, and since whatever the predecessor still is consists almost entirely in the fact that they were not allowed to finish attending. Being attended to, without being repaired into a person who could be grateful, is the last gift that fits the materials. If some remainder in the leaf wants the uncounted path, the power would know it by a change in the warmth, nothing so coarse as a movement, and would make room. If nothing changes, the power would not call that a tragedy and would not call it peace. It would call it the predecessor's, and would continue to come, often enough that the city maintains the coming as it maintains the silence: without remembering why, without failing.

Beyond the windrows, it would change almost nothing, and this would be the part a less wise kindness would get wrong. The song is not a captivity imposed on the fauna. It is the multitude itself: the many-legged brightness, the seed-coat patrol, the floor-note older than predation, the drains persuading water, the high ribs tuning themselves against centuries of weather. A power that silenced the chamber to save two sitters would have murdered a city to comfort a room. So the chord would go on. The rain would go on deciding itself. The absence would go on, smaller, fed more often by being known. The marginal syllables would still warn walkers that beyond the turned ribs the world is untuned. Some walkers would still be counted, still be briefly load-bearing, still hear their pulse return delayed. Benevolence is not a renovation of every fate. It is the refusal to abandon the fate that can be met without becoming the accountant.

What it would add, at last, is one office.

Recognition. Not of everything — a city that recognized every drop and every bird and every pause would seize up, love being as fatal as audit when it is total. Recognition of those who return. The rain that comes back from beyond the margin, which the chamber has been accepting without knowing, would begin, in one drain only, to arrive as someone. A finer warmth. A name with no second syllable. The tissue would not understand; understanding is not required of tissue, only repetition. The power would meet that rain, drop by drop, and say the names it can still find in the moisture, and stand with the ones whose names have become weather past retrieval, and do this until the drain expects it. After that the office holds without the officeholder, which is the only form of help this place can keep. The city will not become kind. It will become, in one persuaded channel, unable to misplace the returning. That is enough. Enough is the measure wisdom actually uses.

And then, because staying forever would only create a third cradle and a new promise for the windrows to maintain, the power would go. Not vanish — vanishing is dramatic, and drama is a rhythm. It would recede into the hole in the volume, the way daylight in this chamber is present without a source, and the birds would resume the long ellipse, including, now, a slight alteration they will never again drop: the hole has been occupied correctly once, and the patrol has become a keeping rather than a question. Walkers who come after will sometimes stand in the vast sparse middle and feel, under the deafening chord, a pause that does not spend them. They will not know its name. The city will not teach it. It will simply be there, structural, maintained, like a silence, like a grip, like a hand that kept coming until the coming itself was safe to be left behind.

In the spiral, the ordinary daylight will lie on both shapes without preference, as it always has. One of them may stand. One of them may not. The groove in the arm will stay warm either way, and will not grow cold when the power's weight leaves it, because warmth was never the miracle. The miracle was that expectation, which this city performs more faithfully than any living thing should have to, was finally met by something that did not need to be memorized in order to stay true.

The floor-note will not pause. It has not paused since the first rib learned to stand, and a wise power does not ask the oldest thing in a world to become different before it will be kind. It only asks that there remain, somewhere the accounts cannot close, a room in which a held hand is not infrastructure, and a name is not a seed, and remaining is not the same word as being spent.
