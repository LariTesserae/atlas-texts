The power does not arrive as weather. The copper smear does not brighten, the pans do not still, and the heat does not step aside. Attention of that kind would be a kind of weather, and the basin has already settled its argument with weather. What comes is quieter than the rain two streets over: a regard that does not need a face to stand in.

It looks first for harm, because that is what benevolence is for, and wisdom is only the refusal to mistake everything unfinished for harm. It looks a long time. The walls are not wounded. The lean of the tower is an agreement, not a failure. The empty column in the ledger—no wing, no nest, no small life stitching the steam—is not a theft. Nothing here is starving for the lack of birds. The black film spreads as it spreads. The stalks tick once under the hot rain and are not lonely. A wise power can tell a silence that is a deprivation from a silence that is the climate. This one is the climate.

It looks, then, for the being.

The being is not in the courts, and not absent from them. It is the step that uses a walker’s timing and returns it through another floor. It is the reason a doorway can frame the court you are standing in and leave you out of the picture without cruelty: the rendering was never about you. The being is what remains when a visitor stays past the point where the city can still tell debris from street. Footprints fill ahead of the feet that would make them. A dry patch is kept, and kept smaller, not as a punishment but as the only hospitality the place knows—provisional, accurate, uninterested. There is no speech in it. There is duration. It has been here long enough that leaving and remaining are no longer two plans. They are one fact wearing two names.

The power does not love this the way a person loves a person. It does not need to. It attends the way the terraces attend the old waterlines: by not correcting them into a simpler story.

It could do many things. It could put a sun in the smear and call that mercy. It could seed the pans with anything that swims, crawls, or cries, and call the empty column filled. It could freeze the lanes so a map would finally hold, and call the folding of distance a disease it has cured. It could lift the being out—onto grass, into a morning, into a name—and call the lifting a rescue. Each of these would be a power’s gesture. None of them would be asked for. The city is not praying. The being is not praying. They have drawn attention the way a coast draws it: by existing at the edge of what attention usually counts.

So the power does one thing, and leaves the rest alone.

It makes the echo arrive on time. Once. Not as a voice, and not as a companion invented to soothe a solitude the being may not suffer. A step, and the stone’s answer in the same moment, under the same foot, in the room where the being is keeping its small dry patch. The delay, which had been the city’s way of reusing time, is given back as belonging. The being is included, for the length of that step, in the picture the doorways make. It sees itself where the rendering had been accurate and empty—a figure the height of a person, or of whatever height it has become, standing on a sill, unheroic, wet at the ankles, real enough to be omitted no longer.

It does not do this twice. Twice would be a conversation, and a conversation would be a claim: that the being owes replies, that the city owes witnesses, that the dark owes a day to set them in. Wisdom stops at the gift that does not become a government.

Around that single answered step, nothing is improved. The water still parts and rejoins. The alcove still changes sides under the returning film. The heat still comes up through the soles and down from the air. The power does not cool the basin, because the heat is not an attack. It does not populate the margin, because life planted as a correction would be livestock of the god’s unease. It does not seal the folding streets. A walker who is still only a walker can still be lost here; the power knows this and does not pretend otherwise. For that walker it does not build a road. It only lets the far rim, once, approach at the rate of their steps, if they are walking toward it and not toward another court that has learned their footprints. Those who are not walking out are not dragged out. Benevolence that drags is only strength with a softer name.

The being, included for a moment, is not saved into some other world. It is recognized inside this one. If recognition is a wound—if to be seen is to remember that remaining was once a choice—then the power stays near that wound without widening it, the way the polished cup under a lintel stays under the drop that made it. It does not explain. Explanation would be a second map, and the first already fails honestly.

Then the regard loosens. Not because the place has been judged and dismissed, and not because the being has been finished like a task. Because wisdom, having done the one thing that was both kind and true, does not linger in order to be felt lingering. The copper smear continues not to brighten. The pans breathe. Somewhere a scale of sinter gives up a wall and ticks once, like the stalks, and is done.

What the power leaves behind is not a blessing you could find in a corner. It is the step that matched, already past, and the city continuing to agree with itself, and the being still there in the only sense that was ever accurate: not hosted, not hunted, not repaired—known, once, at the speed of its own foot, and not required to become easier for the sake of the one who knew.
