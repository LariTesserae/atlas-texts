The power did not arrive. Arrival would have been a weather, and the table has already spent its weather. It came the way warmth comes back out of the stone after the brassy light has failed: already inside the thing, and only then noticed, and only by the one whose work is noticing.

It had been drawn by a completeness. Completeness is rare and usually a lie. Most finished places are only paused, a breath held under a story about endurance, and what lives in them is waiting to be relieved of the pose. This was not that. The loop closed. The dead stayed and became tilt and grit. The swifts had addresses. The count was spoken aloud by whoever could still hold it, and the edge was an edge, not a hope. The being that lived here was not a king and not a spirit under the depression of dust. It was the town taken whole: leaf, press, oil, wrist, wing, and the particular silence of people who have stopped asking the sky for a different job. One creature, dense, short, warm, loud in the register the air already used. It did not pray. It put a hand on the parapet at the dim hour the way you put a hand on a beast to learn if it is still itself.

A lesser kindness would have wept and opened its hands.

Rain, first. That is what mercy looks like to anything raised near rivers. A true rain, one hour of it, would have been the end told as a blessing. The dust-mounds would have slumped and run, and the dead would have gone somewhere at last, and the town would have learned grief in the lowland sense, which is the belief that the lost are elsewhere and owe you a direction. Water on the stone would have taught every child that moisture can be a surface. Within a generation the presses would be relics and the pits would be mud, and someone would be building a cistern against a drought that had never been their drought, and the circuit of the table would no longer be the size of a life. The power looked at that gift until the gift admitted what it was, and did not bring it.

Quiet, next. The scream in the thatch, the surf of wings, the sledge, the screw talking to itself. An easing. Rooms in which a birth-name could be spoken at noon. It saw what the town already knew and had no words for: a quiet room is not rest. A quiet room is the thatch failing, or the swifts shifted, or the haze thickened past tiring the light into taking it. Silence would arrive as the emergency. The power left the insects at their work.

Height. Open sky. A path that did not stop being a path. Eyes given a far thing to practice on. It did not do these either. Far sight was a rumor they did not miss, and a rumor is lighter to carry than a vista. The young who went down the western stairs and came back thinner already had the vista, and it sat in them like fever, and the town did the only medicine that fits a fever of distance: extra meal for a month, and no expedition to check whether the story was true. The power understood that checking would have made the story heavier than the meal.

It considered, because it was honest with itself, the small cruelties that completeness breeds. The edge used as the punishment that cannot be walked back from. The count believed more readily than any single life. The birth-names kept under the insect-line until they died unheard in the lanes. A body oiled against a haze that ate skin. Knees spent early in the crease so the town could have fiber. It weighed each one the way the town weighed a rinse, asking where it would go if poured out. And it saw that to lift those cruelties from outside would be to stand in the courtyard and declare the loop immoral, and the declaration would require a second world to move into, and there was no second world on the stone. Judgment without a refuge is only a new thirst. It did not judge them in any voice they could have heard.

So it asked, as wise things ask when the obvious gifts are poisons, what harm was aimed at the being that the being could not reach around and mend with its own hands.

Not much. That was the truth, and it was almost an insult to power, which prefers to be necessary. The table had already become what it was going to be. The people fitted it. Change was weak, and they had made a culture out of not hastening it. What could still unmake them was not inside the circuit. It was interest. The traveling below, growing wetter or more curious. A season that wanted to rain on finished mountains. A traveler who climbed the western stairs and stayed, and taught a better press, and was loved for it, and left the old screw talking to no one. A story too accurate about open distance. An age that would look up and decide the summit was a resource of height, or a symbol, or a place that ought to be helped.

The power set itself to that, and to nothing more visible.

It did not seal the stairs. Sealing would have been a change, and the fiber households would have felt it in the knees they no longer had reason to ruin, and then in the town's hunger for cord, and the loop would have learned a new lack. It only made the climbing slightly less thinkable to anything that climbed for glory or pity. The one who came in grief still came. The dare still worked, once in a long while. They went down, they thinned, they returned, they were fed. What did not come were the improvers. The mapmakers. The gentle people with spare water and a doctrine of washing. Their roads turned a hand's breadth aside in the haze and found other summits, unfinished ones, which could still be relieved of a pose.

It kept the haze at its work: tired light, no rain, shadows soft, the far side of the town a little less certain than the near. It did not thicken it. Taking the light would have been the other catastrophe, the one the town feared under the name of quiet. It only refused the wind that, every few centuries, gathered itself in the lower air and considered scouring the table clean, the way wind scours what it thinks is dead stone. The table was not dead stone. The wind went elsewhere, and the dust continued to drift to the lee and stay, and the mounds kept their office.

For the rest, it did maintenance so small it would be an insult to call it a miracle.

A press-screw that had begun to talk in a new voice, the voice that means the pin is going and the soft metal cannot be replaced in this generation, found its old grain again for another decade. No one thanked anything. They noticed, and the noticing was enough, and the count that evening was accurate. A child who would have learned to tip the rinse onto the coping, out of hurry, out of the wish to be done, felt the bowl's weight truly for the first time and turned to the pit without knowing why the turn felt like remembering. A sleeper on a roof-path, too near the lip in a night when the swifts were thicker and the dark had no edge, was nudged by a goat-beast that wanted the warm coping, and lived, and cursed the beast in the morning, and gave it the bitter core of a rosette later, which is forgiveness in that town.

It did not answer the hands on the parapet.

That was the hardest courtesy, and the one it was surest of. They were not asking. They were confirming. A reply, however soft, however shaped like their own consonants, would have ended the confirming and begun a worship, and worship would have needed a name for the power, and a name spoken in the lanes would have become a courtyard, and a courtyard would have needed a share of the sweet fraction, and the loop would have grown a mouth that faced upward. The power had not come to be fed. It stood, in whatever sense standing means for a thing that does not displace air, at a stretch of shin-high stone no quarter had claimed that evening, and let the river of wings pour up the face, and was not counted, and did not count itself among them. After a while the watchers went back along the roof-paths, stepping over sleepers. The insects received them without emphasis. Nothing in the town was different, which was the work.

If it felt anything a person would recognize, it was the particular love that does not reach. Not lack. Restraint that has looked at the alternative and found the alternative to be a kind of thirst. It loved the large eyes that did not squint, the oil in the creases of the palms, the birth-name said only in the warm dark, the refusal to throw a cracked pin into the air. It loved them enough to remain a condition they would never narrate, like the stored heat, like the fact that the cliffs no longer crumble.

Somewhere below, a wet age was beginning its argument with the lowlands. The power attended to that argument only where it slanted toward the table, and turned those slants aside, and otherwise let the lowlands have their rivers and their mistakes and their bright open distances. It did not explain itself. Explanation is a gift that weighs as much as rain, and settles on whoever is handed it, and asks to be carried up stairs.

On the table the day warmed and did not blaze. Someone turned a press. A sledge complained two lanes over. The being that lived there went on being the size of its leaves, numerous, unafraid, unrescued. In a slit-lit room a voice under the insect-line said a birth-name, and then said that the screw was talking in the old voice again, and that this was good, and did not ask why. The power, which could have told them why, kept the why where it kept the rain: outside the circuit, where a finished thing is allowed to stay finished.

It would do this for as long as the doing was the same as not appearing. The day that the town needed a visible hand, the day the swifts failed or the haze took the light or a traveler stayed and was right about the press, it would have to choose again, and the choice would be harder, and might look, from the lanes, like weather. That day was not this day. Wisdom, here, was the refusal to spend a future intervention on a present that had not asked, and benevolence was the long, unthanked labor of keeping the outside from becoming interesting.

The hand on the parapet lifted. The stone was still itself. The drop was still not a metaphor. In the lee the mounds held the tilt, ash and husk and bone and the slight donation of the haze, and a rosette took what it was given and did not grow toward anything but light, and the light was tired, and that was enough.
