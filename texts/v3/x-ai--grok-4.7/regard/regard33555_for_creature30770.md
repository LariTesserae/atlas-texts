They would not still the growth.

That is the first mercy, and it would look, to anyone waiting on a crest for the bead, like absence. A power that can warm a rib could keep every split green for a lifetime of the long-handed people, and in doing so it would end the salt, the file, the hour in which food can be cut, the reason a ladder is driven at all. Wisdom here is a refusal to improve a world by removing the condition that taught its inhabitants how to leave in time. The plateau would go on rising. The crests would go on sealing. The wind-walls would still arrive with no announcement but themselves.

They would not speak.

A voice, even a kind one, fills a hollow the way a crack does, and arrives at the herd, the film-drinkers, the lid of threads, the child being carried across ice that is only barely a floor. Benevolence that cannot be quiet is only another weather. So the power would come the way the paler dark comes: a change in how far you can see, lasting not much longer than a gap between two ribs, and then gone, having touched nothing that was waiting to be startled.

What they would do is smaller than help and harder.

They would go to the one hollow that does not pinch, and they would stop at the threshold the underside-walkers already refuse. They would not enter. Entry would be an answer, and an answer, in a room that has spent years learning to absorb, is a kind of violence—the same shape as the fourth stroke that the sap arrested, a completion imposed from outside the hand that began it. Instead they would stand where a cord is sometimes left and never added to, and they would listen back.

Not with a voice. With duration.

The hollow has been keeping every crack and tearing that wandered in, holding them the way the pockets hold water, unsure whether holding is what a room is for. A wise power would know that listening which never returns a sound eventually becomes a wall of a different sort: not fiber, but attention so complete it cannot be answered, which is why the people give it the berth they give a scar shaped like a body. So the power would let one sound out. Only one. Not a word the people use on the crests. A knot, translated into air: the particular delay between a tear and its ceasing, played back a little warmer, so that what the hollow has been swallowing comes out as if the room had decided, in one place only, to stop holding.

The herds would not bolt. It would not be loud in the instrument-sense. It would be the sound of a name that has not yet frayed, returned to the edge of the platform where the coil was left. Whoever next reads cord with their fingers would find no new knot and would still understand that the wide berth can be a little less wide. Not safe. Answered.

That is half.

The other half is for the ones the plant has already closed over, and it is sterner, because benevolence is not rescue when rescue takes longer than the closing. The power would not open a rib to take a wrist back. The wrist is the whine now, and the whine is how the living know which crest not to trust, and unmaking a warning to comfort the dead would spend the living. What they would do instead is keep the knot that means a name after the cord is cut away.

Not in the hollows. Somewhere the growth cannot seal it into weather. A memory that does not become a scar, does not whine, does not call the herd onto a bad edge. The people would not be granted a monument. They would be granted the knowledge, arriving the way their own sentences arrive—slow, tactile, a thickness in a coil they did not leave—that a name taken off the plateau is not the same as a name ended. Mourning could still finish. It would not have to be the only place the finished name exists.

For the rest, nothing.

The flocks would keep riding the iron-smelling lift and dropping when it dies. The threads would keep making floors and waiting in ice. The file would still freeze in a wave that travels backward at the speed of attention, calves walking before the rime takes the heat. The platforms would still sag into dark eyes. A person who misses the green hour would still rasp rime and last and not thrive. A wise power does not sand down a life until it cannot cut the hand that lives it.

If they did anything for the plateau itself—the plant, which does not notice it is inhabited—it would be to let the newest crests stay sharp. Not forever. For as long as sharpness is what the place is. The attention of something benevolent is often a softening. Here that would be the mistake. The power would look at the rib the width of a valley, the edge that would cut a hand, the sap freezing before it runs, and would not round it.

And the being in the warmer dark, if it is a being and not the plant having learned, in one hollow, to listen: they would not name it, or free it, or teach it speech. They would give it the one returned sound and then withdraw, so that it might discover whether it wants to return a second. Wisdom leaves the next knot to the one who has just learned that a sound can leave.

Then the paler dark would close. The gap between the ribs would grow over. Rain would be the plant letting go, again, in a thousand places. Somewhere a pad would unstick on an underside. The power would already be gone, having done only what could be done without becoming another wall, and the plateau would sound as it always sounds—except, at one threshold, a quiet that is no longer the quiet of being unanswerable.
