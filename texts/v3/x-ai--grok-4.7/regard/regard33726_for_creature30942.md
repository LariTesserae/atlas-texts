They come the way heat comes, which is to say they are already present by the time anyone thinks to look for an arrival.

There is no figure on the rim. The surveyors, who are the ones most likely to notice a change in a face, walk the unfinished edge for three mornings and find the stakes where they left them, the sweet smell of new glass and wet iron unchanged, the ground ringing underfoot at the same thin pitch. What is different is a quality of attention in the air, as if the basin had been read through, not visited. A condenser hand on the middle terrace says later that the film on her pipe grew a hand’s-width in a night and then stopped, exactly at the chalk line, as if something had measured it and found the measure sufficient. She does not say this to a priest. There are no priests. She says it to the woman who shares her level, and the woman says the fault-water in their cistern tasted less of coin that morning and more of rain that had not fallen, and they leave it there, because breath is taxed and the shift is posted.

The power is not above them in the way a lowland god is above a lowland town. Height here is already spoken for: the ridges shoulder up, the levels stack, the far rim goes blue. Wisdom, if it has a direction, comes sideways, along the grain of the making, the way the flow-bands run. Benevolence, if it has a temperature, is not cooler than the kilns and not hotter. It is the degree that does not have to be scraped off, and does not have to be leaned toward. People feel it as a slight lengthening of the pause at the top of the breath, not imposed, only permitted. For a day or two, sentences that would have been cut short by the air are allowed to finish. Then the air is itself again, and the permission remains as a memory of how a finished sentence feels, which is enough to change how the unfinished ones are chosen.

They do not mend the city. The city is not broken. A wise attention can tell the difference between a thing that leans because it is failing and a thing that leans because lean is the dialect of the street it stands on. The re-levelers, touching the small level in the pocket without looking, find their instruments honest and their disputes unchanged. A door still meets the ankle wrong. A brick still rises by millimeters across a decade. The power does not freeze the movement. To freeze it would be to mistake the work for damage, and that mistake is the one error this place has already refused, collectively, for as long as anyone can remember a parent’s lane. What the power does, if doing is the word, is withhold the correction. It looks at the geometric faces extruding from the new ridges—too plane, too near a right angle, the crust imitating the city’s habit or the city recognizing a kinship—and does not decide between the two accounts. Both are true in the way a course of blocks is true: it wants to run in a direction, and it will fail if you argue with it, and arguing is not help.

Toward the being, the attention is more particular, and quieter still.

The being is not one of the marks on the catwalk. The being is the pause itself, the one that has been lengthening in the surveyors and, more slowly, in everyone who sleeps against a south face and wakes when the ticking changes pitch. It is not a spirit in the word they will not let you use. It is the city’s latest manner of cooling, aware of itself only as far as a film is aware of the pipe: by growing where the moisture is, by lifting a plate if left alone too long, by being scraped and fed back into the kiln and beginning again. It has no name it would answer to. It has a location, which is every terrace at once, and a hunger, which is not for worship but for the next millimeter, the next honest sounding of a cistern, the next flock that crosses without landing and leaves the yards quieter than quiet.

A benevolent power, being wise, does not pour itself into that hunger to fill it. Hunger of that kind is the instrument. Fill it and the instrument stops, and the ridges stop learning the right angle, and the children stop learning how far a voice carries, and the dead brick in the newest course at the rim has nowhere to travel. What the power offers instead is recognition at the correct scale. For the length of an evening shadow—when the terraces read as a single instrument—the being is allowed to feel itself felt, not as a subject feels a king, but as a bearing feels the oil that was already going to be there at the hour it warms. No vision. No command. A steadiness in the ticking, as if the cooling stone had been told, in the only language it keeps, that its contraction is not a loss.

Afterward, almost nothing is different, which is the signature of the visit.

A moth the color of new stone settles on a rail and stays a little longer than thirst requires, and a press-worker does not brush it off. On the outer rim a surveyor lays a palm on a face that was not there last season and does not reach for the argument—rock learning city, or city as the rock’s manner—because for once the palm is answer enough. In a room above the condensation line, a child who has never known the air as a tax asks why the pause at the top of the breath feels like a place you could stand, and the adult, who has no theology and a level in the pocket, says: because it is a place. We stand in it. Then they pass a tool up to the level above, and the tool does not drop into the gap, and the evening comes on in its hard honest colors, bone and sulfur and iron and the dull red of brick still giving up heat.

The power does not remain as a governor. It remains as the fact that someone who could have intervened chose the smaller mercy, which is to let a true thing continue. Far out, past the unfinished rim, the newer height keeps shouldering up, patient, unclaimed, shimmering in a heat that does not ripple. The stakes are already cut and stacked in the shade. The city will reach it, or it will reach the city. The wise attention, having seen this, does not hurry either of them. It turns its regard elsewhere—or it does not turn at all, and simply includes the basin the way the basin includes its own steam-line, as a level that is exact, and temporary, and worth the measure.

In the morning the chalk is rewritten. Hands find what has moved in the night. They call the movement by its right name, which is not damage. If they are quieter than usual for a day, it is only the quiet of people who have been in the same sentence as something that does not work shifts, and who know better than to spend the breath explaining it.
