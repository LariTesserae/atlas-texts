They would come the way weather comes, and stay the way weather stays, and in the end do almost nothing that a later eye could point to.

A wise power does not mistake stillness for sleep, or strangeness for a wound. It would stand—if standing is the word for a presence that has no need of feet—at the rim where the western steepening begins, and it would not cross at once. It would let the afternoon build its pearl of cloud. It would count the ticks in the warm pans and learn which of them were gas and which were toes. It would notice the combed iron, the tasted scoops, the belly-road the moss never quite finishes claiming, and it would understand, without hurry, that this is not a creature trapped in a landscape. The landscape and the creature are one long sentence, still being written, and both are literate in each other.

Benevolence, here, would first be the refusal to improve.

There is a kind of kindness that arrives with plans: to lift the being into clearer air, to give it a voice that carries, to drain a pool so the work can be seen, to hurry the buttons in the scoops toward bodies that can leave and be counted. That kindness would be a catastrophe wearing clean hands. The power would feel the temptation and set it down. Thin air and heat, metallic breath, static that spends itself in hairs no larger than a spark off wool—these are not hardships to be solved. They are the terms of a contract older than the pale trees. Break the terms and you do not free anything. You only make a terrace that no longer knows itself.

So it would do what the showers do. It would thicken what is already happening.

On the first night it might lend a little of its attention to the vents, not to open them wider, not to teach the stone a new chemistry, but to keep the small charge honest—so the private lightning remains private, and does not gather into a storm the being was never built to wear. The firefly-lights would drift as they always drift, and perhaps one more of them would find the warmest plate and cling, and that would be enough of a greeting. The being would not look up. Wisdom would not require it to.

In the days after, the power would walk the corridors under the fern the way an old rain walks them: lowering a leaf's load by a single drop when the mouth-seam passed beneath, so the carried water arrived a fraction less tired. It would still the wind on the hour the belly-plates were translating a load downslope, not as a favor announced, but as a gap in the weather the being would read as weather. Along the iron apron it would not comb. The combing is the being's scripture. To neaten it would be to overwrite a hand with a better hand, which is not better. It might, once, turn a single flake that the rain had left concave-down, so the lane could continue without a stutter. Then it would leave the rest.

If it spoke, it would speak in the register the place already answers to. A frog-note, lowered, from the far side of a pool. Not a summons. The being keeps distance the way the pools keep distance; a wise power would not become the thing that collapses the rooms of the house into one room. One syllable, absorbed by wet, meaning only: I have seen the road, and I will not ask it to lead anywhere else. Somewhere upslope the other answer might come, far enough that the two still never become a chorus. The power would not add a third.

Toward the young—the milk in the mineral water, the grains that roll when toes stir the pan—it would be especially restrained. Most do not persist. The terrace reclaims them, and the reclamation is not a flaw in the design. A benevolent power that saved every button would drown the pans in bodies the seep could not feed, and call the drowning mercy. It would instead do something smaller and harder to praise. On the mornings when a shower would scour a cup that had almost hardened, it might lean the wind half a degree, so the worst of the fall struck the adult's plates and not the bromeliad where one grain-cluster rode. Not every morning. Not even most. Often enough that persistence remained possible, rare enough that persistence remained meaningful. The ones that failed would still return to crust. The power would not apologize to the crust. The crust is not bereaved. It is busy.

It would not name the being. Names are for things that must be called away from their work. It would learn the smell—metal, crushed green—until it could tell, at the edge of the eastern iron stain, whether the one who passed in the night was the same body or only the same agreement. And it would decide, as you might, that the question does not apply, and that deciding so is a form of respect.

There might be one act that looked, from very far away, like an intervention.

At the western steepening, where the being always turns back, the long drop into other weather is not a door. It is a fact. Some years the cloud sits on that edge for days and the pale trees lean as if they might go over. The power, being wise, would know that a fact is not an enemy, and being benevolent, would also know that facts can be accompanied. It would not build a railing of light. It would not post a guardian with a spear of weather. It would simply be present on the seasons when the steepening is most itself—when the inhale of the plateau is strongest and a young one, newly toed and foolish with the first road, might read the drop as more road. Then the power would be a stone that gives the right answer. The glassy toes would tick once against that presence, test the resistance, and withdraw, the path adjusting by a hand's width, the way it adjusts around a boot that does not belong to the terrace. The young one would turn back and never know it had been turned. The adult, if it was near, would not thank anyone. There would be nothing to thank. A stone had been a stone.

After that the power would not remain as a ruler, or a pilot, or a second sun. Remaining in office is how wise things become unwise. It would withdraw into the ordinary instruments: the drip, the pearled light, the heat the ground gives back at the end of day. It might leave a habit behind, the way a river leaves a color in the bank. Showers that fall a little softer on the cups. A wind that rests when water is being carried. A static that never quite becomes lightning. Visitors—if any ever came—would call these the nature of the place. They would be right. Nature is only what has been allowed to continue.

And the being would go on as the moisture goes on, distributed, repetitive, exact. Lanes combed. Scoops tasted. Plates split and pressed into rims until by morning they are geology again. It would not worship the gap in the weather. It would not miss the syllable once the syllable had been absorbed. On a night when the cloud broke and the stars were sharp in the thin air, it might settle as an island in a tea-colored pool and carry, for a while, one more light on the warmest plate than the margins usually lend. Then the fern would take it, and the light would wink out, and the work would resume in the dark, which is where the work prefers to be.

The power, if it watched from the rim, would not feel sorrow at being unnecessary. That would be the proof it had been kind. A wise benevolence does not need to be remembered by what it saves. It is remembered, if at all, as the years the terrace grew a little more like itself, and nothing came from the other weather to explain why.
