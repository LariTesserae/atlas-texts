They would not come down the cut.

A wise attention learns a place by its refusals first, and the bowl’s refusals are old and accurate: the rim against wind, the raised thresholds against the finger of water, the unnamed weeks against hope spent too early, the wool taken back from the dead because wool is for the living. To arrive as a trader arrives—with sacks, with a voice pitched to carry under the falls, with a better plan for the channels—would be a kind of weather the rock never agreed to. So the power stays at the height where the sun still sits after the lanes have gone dark, and listens, and does not improve the town.

What it hears is not a cry for rescue. The place is not failing. The braid of water is kept, the walls are replanted, the goats come down when the gold leaves the rim, the frogs take the lower town and give it back at graylight. Continuity is the local form of the good. A benevolence that understood only lack would see the rosied hands, the joint lost to a wound that would not close, the child waiting for a name, and would want to dry the bowl, salt the food, still the seep, end the argument with the water by winning it. That would be mercy with the wrong object. The being that lives here is not a prisoner of the damp. The being is the damp, the cane, the pulse under the mats, the people whose feet already know the answer of the ground. Drain it into a clean bright country and you have saved no one; you have ended a long agreement and called the silence peace.

So the power does very little, and the little is exact.

It finds, in the seep-face above the main channel, a tooth of rock that has been working loose for years—not this season’s work, not a thing any water-reader could yet hear, but a fall that would come in some later rain when the note of the water changed and the children were sent to the high paths and the clearing was done faster and was not enough. The power settles that tooth. Not out of the face, not into a new shape the clearers would notice and argue over: only back, a finger’s width, into the hold it had before the frost of some forgotten year worried it. The seep keeps its dark shine. The channel keeps its name. In the morning the one whose hands are currently on the town’s throat walks the seams and hears nothing wrong, and is right to hear nothing wrong, and goes back to the shouting about roots. The drills are not canceled. Wisdom leaves the fear in place, because the fear is how the high paths stay in the feet. It only refuses the one blockage that would have turned maintenance into a year remembered like a sickness.

Then the infants, because benevolence that will not look at them is only taste.

The power does not abolish the bowl’s opinion. A small place has a size, and a living wall has a weight it can bear, and a people who were suddenly more numerous than the fish and the bitter green and the thin milk would learn a new hunger and call it a gift. But there is a difference between a limit and a waste, and the power is not sentimental enough to confuse them. In one house, then another, over years rather than in a single theatrical night, it does the smallest thing that is still a thing: it draws a thumb’s width of damp back from a sleeping-mat, so a cough that would have settled into the little chest never quite settles. It warms nothing. It lights nothing. The household still wakes to wet wool and the tick of cooling cane. Some children still are not named. The ones who stay still take names from a bird in the door-pole or a flood that stopped at the step, and the names are still allowed to be ugly. Only a few who would have been lost to the stupid arithmetic of mold and standing breath are still there in the morning, red-faced, ordinary, furious to be fed. No one counts them against a prophecy. The town does not become lucky in a way it would trust. A grandmother, later, says the upper terrace slept drier that season, and someone tells her she is thinking of a different season, and the matter ends, as matters end here, in a shared fish.

For the cane, a year when it would not have taken: not a miracle of growth, which they would see and fear, a gap-terror in reverse, wind’s opposite but still wind’s family—but a slight kindness in the peat, a nutrient the rock already had and was slow to give, so the new shoots make their nodes and the thumbs that touch them in passing find them firm. The habit continues. The denial of the habit continues. The power is content to be denied. It did not come to be touched.

It does not bring salt. Salt ends arguments and begins marriages; a bag of it left in a door-pole would become a god within a generation, and a god is a mouth, and the bowl already has a mouth. It does not bring grain, except that once, when a party on the ridge would have lost a sack to a slip that was pure clumsiness and not fate, the footing holds, and the upper terrace has its week of gruel with a body to it, and people comment on the body, and the power, if it could be said to smile, would smile at the commenting, which is the local way of tasting. It does not quiet the falls, or the goats, or the frogs. The structural sound is not noise. It is how the town knows its own size in the dark.

It looks at the lip, at the fan of water becoming someone else’s weather, and leaves the below alone. The silence the timber-cutters are paid in is a true silence. To open wonder downward would be to give the third fear a face, and a fear with a face starts wanting rites, and rites in a place this wet become another thing that must be carried, another pole in the lane. The power is a neighbor, not a revelation. Neighbors do not stand at the edge of your garden and explain the country under it.

The dead in the rim-cracks it does not wake and does not move. Their wool, when it is taken back and washed in the falls, parts with the bone a little more cleanly. That is all. Mourning remains a lane gone quiet for a day, a slot of stillness you can hear from the lip if you know how to listen, and then the clearing. The power listens to that stillness once, the way a person listens to a held breath, and does not fill it.

If it speaks—and it almost does not—it is not in words. It is in the pulse already under the floors, which it does not change, only keeps time with, so that a sleeper shifting to stay with the seep is not answering a stranger. The agreement stands. The water remains a powerful neighbor whose moods must be learned. The power is simply an older attention inside that neighborliness, willing to be mistaken for the rock’s own patience.

By full dark it has done what it will do. The other crew takes the lanes: the deer, the rats, the birds that hunt them, the dogs that may answer. Inside the living walls the heat is the heat of bodies that have been damp since morning. Someone counts the channels and slides under. The tooth of rock holds. A child who might not have been named breathes, ugly and ordinary, against a hand. A shoot the thickness of a reed stands in a harvested plot, and will be a wall, and will be cut, and will be a wall again.

In the morning they are already up, already answering the ground. The place does not rise to meet them. Nothing has been added that would require rising. The wise thing, having drawn near, remains near in the only way that will not cost the bowl its life: indistinguishable from the keeping they were already doing, and unwilling to be thanked.
