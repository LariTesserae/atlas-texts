They would not come down.

A power that is wise has already learned the difference between a country that is suffering and a country that has finished its argument with itself. This bench is the second kind. The heat is not a wound. The thin air is not a lack. The packing of lives into housings and seeps and the lee of fins is not a crisis waiting for a hand. To green it, to fill the cisterns past their hour, to give the birds a song that could cross the plateau, would be a kindness shaped like a stranger’s house: generous, and unlivable.

So they do not alter the weather. They do not lengthen the wet. They do not still the flywheel or start the towers that have gone empty. A bucket that still goes out is a fact the raptor has priced into its walk; a bucket restored to a full line would be a new law, and new laws here arrive as quarrels.

What they do is smaller than a rain, and aimed the way speech is aimed on the bench: one fact, one direction, held close enough that it does not have to be shouted.

They notice which machines are inhabited by agreement. Not all of them. The ones whose remaining job is the bargain itself—the shaft whose stroke the housing-birds have built their leaving to, the pump whose slow inhale keeps one seep a finger higher, the span whose tick tells the walking hunter where the third tower still holds. Those few, they keep from the particular death that is not predation and not weather: the death of a bearing that seizes because no one cleared the sinter-dust, the death of a cable-strand that parts on a night when the person who walks the line is elsewhere, or gone, or simply old. They do not repair toward former purpose. They do not add a second job. They keep the one job from ending on a day when the nests are still cupped to it.

It is done the way the place already does things. A grain of grit that would have walked into the race of a bearing meets, instead, a warmth in the metal just long enough to turn it aside into the tray the person already empties. A strand that would have frayed this season holds through the next two molts, which is as long as the ledge below it has arranged to need. No one sees a hand. The person at the third tower finds the tray a little fuller than habit, and does not call it luck, because luck is a word that asks to be heard across a distance, and they have a shorter one: the day did what the day does. They clear the tray. The birds leave between strokes. The seep rises its finger. The message moves along the ledge without a throat opening.

They do one other thing, and it is for the plate-backed ones, who cannot ask and would not know a favor if it stood up in the trail. In the dry interval, when the ovals shrink to salt and the sheets of beetles fail, there is a pocket—one, not every one—where the night film beads a little earlier on the underside of a fin and lasts a little longer into the dark. Not a spring. Not a miracle a traveler could follow. A curb of water, the width of a groove between plates, present on the nights when the rest of the bench has already gone back to stone. The large ones drink as they drink: by being already there, and lowering the front of the body, and letting it climb. They do not look up. There is nothing above them that has announced itself.

The power does not speak to the people. A voice in that air would die in a few paces or, worse, arrive whole, which nothing else here is permitted to do. If there is a blessing, it is that their names remain place-names, and that the older ones they keep for the night are not required, yet, to be spent on a leaving. The one whose pan still skins will skin a while longer. The child who has learned to step over a ridge of plates without saying they have seen it will not be corrected into wonder. Wonder is a brightness. A bright scrap is a claim the country does not honor.

And then, because benevolence that lingers becomes a climate, they stop.

They do not remain as a witness the inhabitants must live under. Attention, held too long, is another kind of weather, and this bench has its weather already. They let the noticing be brief and exact—the way a bird’s weight shifts when the seep has risen—and they withdraw past the rim, past the need to be known for having come. What continues is what was already continuing: the bucket’s creak, the motor’s half-second, the trails that use a living stone as a curb, the film that jewels the tin for an hour and fails.

If anything on the plateau registers the visit, it is only this: a bearing that did not seize, a pocket that held its damp one night longer than the schedule quite explained, and a quiet that is still not emptiness. The wise power wanted nothing the place could not spend locally. The place, which has room for every life that keeps its manners, spends it without thanks, and goes on not needing to be heard.
