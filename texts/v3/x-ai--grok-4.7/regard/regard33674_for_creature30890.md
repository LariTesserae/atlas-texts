They would do almost nothing, and they would do it from farther off than any eye in the valley can aim.

The power does not descend the rim. Descent would be a weather the galleries have no name for, and a weather with a direction is already an argument. It stays where the true night-cold lives, above the last frayed nests, and it looks the way deep water looks at a boat: entirely, and without making a wake the boat can use. What it sees is not a town and not a creature. It sees a rate that has learned how to persist. Mud carried, wood chewed, silk stiffened, holes worn oval, pools lifted by a hand and a hand, young that mostly do not last and yet the floor still takes prints. The benevolence in it does not mistake fullness for plea. Nothing here is asking. The asking would require a mouth that means the whole valley, and there is no such mouth. There are only beaks, incisors, the slap of a tail on a seam, a thread of alarm that dies at twenty body-lengths.

So it does not heal the wounded bird on the perch. By the time a healing could arrive kindly, the perch has already been taken by a bird that did not win anything except timing, and the first bird has become the hour's protein, which is one of the ways the runs stay able to move. To return that bird to the perch would be to stuff the hour backward. The power has learned, wherever it learned anything, that mercy aimed at a single body in a place like this is often a tax collected from the bodies that must then make room. It does not thin the midges. It does not lengthen the lives of the pink-grey clusters on the ledges. It does not teach the weavers that their nests are also a roof, because a weaver that knew it was roofing a valley would hesitate, and hesitation at that density is how a season fails.

What it does is smaller than a decision and more like a continued permission. It keeps the mountain from remembering, this century, that it is stone. A shift of the underlying grade, the kind of shift mountains make when no one is counting, would tip the channel out of its paved bed and turn the held pools into a brief brown rush and then into absence. The power does not build a dam of its own. It only declines to let that particular forgetting happen yet. The warmth in the basin, which is mostly bodies and rotting silk and the stored breath of a million small furnaces, it leaves alone; but it steadies the rim-cold so the cold arrives as the same night it has always been, not as a new and smarter night that would strip the upper works in a week. When a storm walks the high country with more water than the ceilings can turn into drip, the power spends a little of itself on the wind’s shoulder, not enough to be a miracle, enough that the rain remains a thickening and not a collapse. The inhabitants will never thank it. They will register, if they register anything, that the pans rose a finger and not an arm, and then they will drink.

It does not introduce itself to the snake, or to the cat-small pause that empties a circle, or to the raptor that drops through the failed shaft. Those are already the valley’s way of not eating itself by other means. A wise power that loved only the soft would have to evict the valley’s own arithmetic, and then sit forever in the ruins of that eviction, feeding by hand what used to feed by being unsurvivable at the margins. It will not do that. Love, if that is the word for what it feels toward a system that cannot feel it back as a system, is here a refusal to be interesting. It does not clear a gallery so that something might look up and see a face. It does not lay one straight path from the central pools to the drier districts. Straightness would be a sentence, and the valley speaks in leftovers that happen to touch.

There is a being, in the only sense that holds. Not a king of the weavers, not a mind in the dams. The being is the continuity that no single animal occupies: the way a district gives onto a district, the hollow under the mat that proves older galleries are still in use, the conversation between two holes thrown farther than either bird could buy in open air. The power attends to that the way a listener attends to a music that was never written down and will not survive being written down. Attention, held without reaching in, is already a kind of wall against the outside — against men who would call the roof timber, against a drier century, against the bright idea that such a place should be opened so it can be known. It will not be opened. Knowing it from within was always going to cost the knower their own breath, and the power has no need to pay that cost in someone else’s lungs.

If it touches anything, the touch is indistinguishable from the place’s own habits. A seep that might have wandered off into the mountain’s throat continues, for another hundred seasons, to find the cup it has always found. A section of roof that was going to fail all at once fails by the familiar thumb’s width, at night, so that the thousand small architectures can adjust before the day-callers return. A seed that would have blown outward and died on the stone blows inward instead, and is picked up, and becomes part of a nest, and is forgotten as an individual seed, which is the correct fate for seeds here. None of these will be called grace by the mouths that benefit. They will be called Tuesday, or the way water goes, or nothing at all.

And when even that is more than wisdom wants to spend, the power simply stays where it is, above the fray and not above the care, and lets the valley deepen another thumb’s width by its own surplus. The prints ease out of the husk. The notch is filled. The hole returns to its conversation. Somewhere a body is born into the noise and will never learn that anything watched. That ignorance is the gift. A benevolent power, being wise, can want for the beloved the dignity of not being improved, and can want it enough to remain, for as long as the rate holds, a stillness no alarm will ever correctly name.
