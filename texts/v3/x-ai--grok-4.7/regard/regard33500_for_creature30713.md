The power does not cross.

It has crossed other thresholds, in other ages, and learned that arrival is a kind of weather. This place already has weather enough: heat with nowhere to go, water that climbs when it should run, a skin that spends whatever passes through it. A wise attention does not add a body to a room that small. It stands where the birds stand, in the ordinary sky, and lets the birds keep their refusal.

It watches one full turning. The weep walks from the east wall to the south. A filament throws a shadow the wrong way, then corrects itself in the hour no one is meant to catch. A plate that was flush at morning sits proud by afternoon. A leaf held in a shadow that is only the power’s distant shade does not fold — the shade never quite reaches — and that, too, is noted. The power is careful not to become a standing body inside the rules. Bookkeeping should not have to open an account for it.

It looks for the being.

There is no face. There is a bench that slows the drip when sat on, and no one sitting. There is a word-eating wall, and no speaker. There are plants set far enough apart that something could walk between them without brushing a leaf, and the walking has not happened, or happens in a tense the eye does not conjugate. Under the notch a birth continues that has not asked to be finished. The posts keep a hot damp room standing open to a cold altitude, at a cost no one has itemized, which is the surest sign that someone, or something, is paying.

The power does not decide that the process is the being, or that the being is late, or that the empty path between the plants is a tragedy. Those are the conclusions of powers that need a story before they can be kind. It only notices that the place is already doing the work of keeping: heat kept from flooding, water kept from leaving, sound kept from crossing, lushness kept local so the emptiness can remain the rule. Whatever lives here is either that keeping, or is kept by it. In either case the first mercy is not to interrupt the accounts.

So it does not cool the stone. The heat is not a fever. Cooling would be a verdict.

It does not seal the skin. The skin is how the place stays open without being entered. A sealed mercy is a cupboard.

It does not finish the birth. Unfinished is not the same as failed. The ridge is still rising; to hurry a rise is to choose the shape for it.

It does not sit on the bench. Sitting spends the place’s listening to buy the visitor’s comfort. The power is not here to be heard by its own ears.

What it does is smaller than intervention and harder to mistake for one.

At the open side, where a spoken word returns smaller, late, and stripped of consonants, it offers not a word but a duration: it lets the wrong-way shadow of the filament last a little past its hour, and then releases it to correct itself exactly as it always does. Not a signature. A delay, the size of having been seen. The correction still belongs to the place. Only the witness was borrowed, and given back before the next weep-shift.

Beyond the skin, in the rumor of colder blue, it makes no door and no brighter heaven. It only keeps the ordinary light honest — hard, high, able to show every unsoftened edge — so that if anything inside looks out, the outside is still outside: not hungry, not holy, not a mouth. Dusk may thicken the skin as it always has. The power will not spend the evening to prove it was there.

Then it waits, but not as a test. It steps its attention back the way you step back from a cup you have set down, so that drinking need not be performed in front of you. If a leaf folds and opens wetter on the old cycle, that is enough. If a post warms before any hand decides it has, the power will not call it an answer and escalate. It will match, and only match: another small delay, another ordinary noon, another refusal to cross.

If nothing answers in any tense it knows, it does not come back as a demand, and it does not diagnose the silence as emptiness. A place that takes words at the back wall and returns them ruined at the lip may already be in conversation with itself. Wisdom, here, is the willingness to be unnecessary.

It leaves the birds to their turning. It leaves the moth-sized thing, if it comes again, to fold into a droplet and soak into the stone unharmed. It leaves the birth bleeding quietly into its own shadow, which was never a wound that asked for a healer — only a heat that asked not to be named finished.

And if, on some later day, the bench slows its drip for no sitter the power can see, it will not hurry in to meet what has finally sat down. It will stay in the ordinary sky, and let the being have the room, and the rules, and the first silence that belongs only to them. Benevolence, when it is also wise, is often just that: the decision not to become the weather in a place that has already learned, at great expense, how to keep its own.
