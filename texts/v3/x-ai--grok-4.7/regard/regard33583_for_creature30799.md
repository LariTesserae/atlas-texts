They would not come down.

A power that has learned wisdom does not mistake stillness for a wound. It would stand, if standing is the word, at the height where the dark still has seams, and it would look for a long time—longer than the filaments have burned, longer than the troughs have forgotten rivers. It would see the shelf that finished becoming itself, the lids of roofs, the hands that are nearly flanges, the paste that replaces what the air lifts away. It would hear the pressure in the jaw that is not quite sound, and the softer tick of dust that is not quite a clock. And it would understand that what lives here has already been offered every gift that change can give, and has set them down.

Benevolence, meeting this, would not arrive as weather. Rain on that sintered plate would be a violence: a solvent the ground has refused, a second state forced on a chemistry that balanced itself by ceasing to thirst. Green would be an accusation. A voice saying rise, or drink, or remember the mountains, would be speaking a language the mineral no longer keeps the throat for. The wise power would know this the way it knows not to warm a stone that has chosen its own heat.

So it would do very little, and the little would be exact.

It might, once, lay a palm of attention on the last filament that still burns orange under its tower, not to make it brighter and not to let it fail, but so that the one who walks the grid—when the distance between glows has become the length of a thought—finds the light neither more nor less than it has always been, and therefore does not have to decide whether the city has been touched. Decision is expensive here. Mercy is the withholding of a decision.

It might thicken, by a degree no crust would notice, the warm that comes up through the spine of the one who sits with their back to the masonry. Not a new warmth. The same warmth, held a fraction more faithfully, so that the deepening which is not sleep goes unmarked and unhurried. The pale hooks high on the stacks would drink the same air. Nothing would flock. Nothing would call.

If it spoke at all, it would speak in the only sentence the place still finishes: by being absent in the manner of the mountains, which is to say present as grain, as the angle of a street that has settled into a shallow dish and does not drain because there is nothing to drain. The beings who are remainders would not hear a name. They would feel, perhaps, in the moment two of them let their cooling match in an intersection, that the match was already known—that the plateau had agreed, before either of them arrived, to keep both seams. Then one would take the street that declines toward the troughs, and the other the street that barely remembers a made angle, and neither would look back, and the power would count that as answered.

It would not raise the small remainder that sinters out of the dust into a child. Birth is not what the dust is doing. It would not teach the ones who have stopped walking, palms grown into pipe, to unfasten themselves. Unfastening would be a loss of the contour the metal has accepted. It would not wind the clock whose hand has not been seen to move. The work continues at the rate the unwound clock describes, and that rate is the city’s courtesy to the ground.

Far out, where the last filament is only a rumor and the sound in the jaw has faded, the power might walk the plates a while, bootless, weightless, matching the soft tick without adding one. It would turn when the city was still where it had been left. It would not miss the place, and it would not require the place to miss it. Missing is a mode the highland finished with when it lay down.

What it would leave behind is not a sign. No brighter door, no new trough filling, no word cut into sintered dust that the wind—which no longer blows—might have kept. Only this: that the equilibrium, having been seen by something that could have broken it and did not, is no longer merely local. The needles that rest at their sufficient heading rest in a larger consent. The paste portions do not grow. The crusts shift by the width of themselves toward seams that have not cooled. The factories remain organs rather than producers. And somewhere in the dark that has thickness, a wise attention continues, not as weather and not as rescue, but as the agreement that a finished thing may go on being finished, and that this, too, is a form of care.

They would not come down. They would let the shelf keep its temperature. They would call that love, and then they would be quiet, which is the only blessing the place still has room to hold.
