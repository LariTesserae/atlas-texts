They would not come down the stair.

A power that is wise does not enter by the only door the rim can see, because that door already means errand, trade, and the bright hour that hurts the eyes. It would not stand at the rope beyond the last frame and call the open tank a problem. It would not still the pumps. Silence there is flood. It would not dry the air, or set birds in the lofts, or unmake the vines that have become railings. Those would be the acts of a kindness that had looked only long enough to pity, and pity is a short gaze.

It would look the way the people look: through drip, at small differences, for a long time.

It would hear the two-beat and learn which knocks are the life of the place and which are a valve beginning to stick. It would watch the bare stripe on the quarry wall and understand that they already keep a vigil, and that to take the vigil away—by promising the level will never rise, by writing the promise in light on the stone—would be a theft, even if no water moved. It would see the puckered hands, the white film over a cut, the socks changed at midday, the birth that costs the night watch, and it would not call those the meaning of the bowl. Meaning is the circuit: water arriving, plants drinking, surplus lifted, overflow returned, nothing thrown out. The sores and the dangerous weeks are costs that collected in the joints of that circuit, the way grit collects in a coupling. A wise kindness removes grit. It does not smash the machine to free the hands that tend it.

So it would do very little, and the little would be shaped like what is already there.

Into the grain of the noise it would set a rest. Not quiet—the fans would not spin down, the header would not stop venting. A rest the length of a finished sentence, recurring, learnable, in the place where a condensate hammer has always eaten the middle of a phrase. The first times, no one would use it for anything but numbers: bundles, a coupling, the intake at the third grate. That would be right. The gift is not a sermon. It is room. Later, under the fans, someone would start a thing that did not have to be completed before the steam-cough and would find, without deciding to be grateful, that it could be completed. They would keep their short speech in the lanes. The lanes are for short speech. The rest would simply be there, as the wiped window is there, if wanted.

It would take the wound out of the wet without taking the wet. Skin would still pucker; the place would still write itself on the hands. But a cut would close, and the new skin would be able to live in that air. Between the toes, the white opening would stop being the price of a day’s work. Boots would still be oiled. They would not be asked to become rim people in order to be unhurt. In the weeks of a birth, the night watch would not have to be gambled. Someone could be spared and the pumps would not punish the sparing. The child would still learn the two-beat before any lullaby. The power would not send a different first music. It would only refuse to let the giving of life be a crisis the machines could win.

It would not brush the stripe. Once a season a person would still go up on the ladder, and the moss would still come back everywhere but there, because they take it off, and the stone would still show its age. The power might, on the morning after a heavy seep, make the threads on the wall a little easier to read—not a sign, a clarity, the way good light is clarity—so that the one who looks can trust their own eyes and come down the ladder with a true report. If the level were ever truly rising, it would not hide that. Wisdom does not comfort by fogging the instrument. If the level holds, as it has held, the bare stone stays a bare stone. They may go on not knowing why they keep it. The power would not explain them to themselves.

And then, having done that, it would stay without occupying.

Not as a figure in the pump house, where a hand already knows to touch the housing. Not as a god of the tanks, which would only add a mouth to feed with ritual they do not have time to invent. It would stay as agreement: the circuit may continue; the people who keep it are not a leftover of the cut; the rope at the open water is a real boundary and will be honored; no one will be carried up the softened steps into a life they did not choose. If a person wiping their hands on their thighs at the end of a day feels, for a moment, that the fronds leaning back toward the walk are not the only answer the bowl gives, they will not be required to name what else answered. They can go in under the fans. They can change their socks. They can speak, in the rest, the ordinary next thing.

A power that needs to be thanked has not finished being wise. This one would let the drip find the grooves it has already polished, and would count that continuance as enough.
