They come the way weather comes here: already present when the noticing starts, with no disk to look away from and no shaft to blame.

They had expected, as powers do, to find a lack. A roof that wanted raising. A name that wanted keeping. A grief with a picture beside it, so the picture could be handed back and the grief made useful. They had come prepared to be kind in the ordinary way, which is to say, to finish something.

The basin will not hold that kindness. A mercy that stays is only another course of dressed stone, tight-jointed, meant to outlast the hands that laid it. They can read, as well as anyone who has walked the grooves, what staying did the last time. The joints are still beautiful. That is the indictment.

So they do not raise the walls. They do not put soil back into the meal, though they could, though for a moment the wanting is so strong it nearly powders off them like a front. Soil would root the clusters. Rooted clusters would wait for water in the place they left the vessel. Waiting would become a right place, and a right place would expire, and the children who take a wall down without anger would learn anger, which is only the belief that the basin has broken an agreement. There was never an agreement. The power will not be the one to invent it.

They do not give the open its echo back. They stand under a hide and hear the indoor talk—the short cruel names for cisterns, the teasing of a person who trusted a wall—and they understand that melody was never the missing thing. Melody needs a surface that returns you to yourself unchanged. Nothing here returns anyone unchanged. To hang a voice in the vault and have it come back whole would be a lie told in the only register the basin has refused. They let the quiet keep them. They are relieved, a little, not to have to be answered.

They walk. This they can do without counterfeiting. They are bad at it at first, in the way of anything that has never had to revise its path before the path expired. Their weight is too sure. A solitary walker, a day ahead of the porters, finds a chip of glass set on edge where no walker they know would have set it, and the chip is wrong by a finger’s width—too centered, too pleased with its own meaning—and the walker moves it, without irritation, to the place the damp will actually be. The power feels the correction as a teaching and is grateful, and does not set the next chip so well. Competence, here, is the willingness to be corrected by someone who will not remember your name past the next mote-front. They practice that. It is harder than raising a wall.

What they do, when they do anything, is small and unrepeated.

A district has gone still. Not dead—stillness is not death yet, only the condition death uses when it wants to look like peace. No herd has revised across it. No wall has been taken down for the pleasure of a truer place. The crust has thickened past eating and begun to forget it was ever a film. People have walked in, walked out, and declined to roof. The power does not call them back. Calling would be a voice that lasts, and a voice that lasts is the wrong weather. They put their palm on a wall that has not shone in longer than a body remembers, and they let it forget itself. One weeping. A single flagstone darkens. They do not do it again on that wall, or on the wall beside it, or anywhere the porters have begun to expect. A repeated mercy is a schedule, and a schedule is how you teach a people to sleep in the same foundations twice and call it law.

The rim-keepers find the wet first, as they find every wet, and stand up and walk a hand’s breadth and settle, and the stone is a finger lower. A herd’s reluctance passes through itself. Someone’s route bends. A hide roof goes up where yesterday there was no reason. The power is already elsewhere, being slightly less organized than a god, which is the only scale at which they can fit under the vault without becoming a ceiling.

They sit once with a calf too new to be steady, in the middle of a moving pale thicket, and they do not name it. Naming would move it halfway to meat. They only fail to spook it, which is easy, because it has forgotten spooking, and then they stand and are allowed, and the thicket closes and the light is the light. Later a person sees the duller patch on the glass where someone rested their weight too long and almost mineralized, and the person shifts their own heels and moves on, and never knows the patch was not a person. The power counts this as success. To be mistaken for the basin is better than to be thanked by it. Thanks would require a center.

There is one temptation they do not indulge, and it costs them more than the walls would have.

They can see the intact city. Not as a memory the people are missing—the people are not missing it; they do not have the picture, and so they are not in pain of that particular kind—but as a thing the power could hold up beside the grooves and say: *this is what you were, this is what was taken, here is the shape of your loss, you may be sad now, it will make you like the ones I know how to comfort.* They hold the picture the way you hold a blade you are about to return to the language it came from. They look at a child stacking stone two flagstones to the left of where the wall failed. They look at the indoor humor that would not survive the comparison. They look at a named animal lying in the warm-cool seam, already halfway, and at the person who named it anyway because noticing is the wealth that does not have to be carried.

They put the picture down on the glass and do not open it. What remains that is hard they do not keep. A loss restored for the sake of the restorer is only hunger with a liturgy. They will not feed.

If they are wise, this is the wisdom: that benevolence is not the opposite of leaving. It is leaving done carefully, by hand, the way a body is opened along the long muscle and given to whatever is nearest and still revising. They do not bury themselves in the meal. They do not stay to be a marker. The glass keeps a duller patch, and the patch will weather back, and that is as close as they come to having been here.

Before they go—if going is the word, if they are not simply the next front already thinning at the far rise—they do the one thing they saw the living do when the place offered its pleasant heaviness.

They had stood too long. Powers do. The light had seemed to deepen, the districts to draw in, the heels to consider the old bargain of becoming the kind of stone that is praised for not moving. It would have been easy to call that faithfulness.

They shift their weight. They look for a sign of revision that is not theirs: a cord knotted once around a foundation, a calf, a wall coming down without anger, a solitary walker who will sleep in the lee of a shoulder of stone and wake inside a herd and stand up slowly and be allowed. They go toward it. They are not hurrying. They are not called. They decline, once, to be finished on anyone’s behalf, including their own.

The basin takes the refusal the way it takes rain: on one plaza and not the next, soundless, already ending, already enough.
