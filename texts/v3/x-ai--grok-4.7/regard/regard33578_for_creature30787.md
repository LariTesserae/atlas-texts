They would not come down the steps.

The steps are a record of arrival, and arrival is already too much weather for this basin. A power that was only strong would still come down them—would want the dished stone to know it had been walked by something that does not tire, would want the cistern lid to give under a hand that is not cold. Strength likes evidence. This one does not. It has had enough evidence. What it has, instead, is the long habit of looking at a thing until the thing’s own scale is the only scale in the room, and then of not improving it.

It attends the way the rain attends: already, and from no direction you can point to. The ridges do not grow warmer. The iron does not lift. The gate does not gain a new interval to celebrate a visitor. If you were on the high path you would hear the basin agree with itself exactly as before—hammer, slap, knock, whistle, boom, thud—and you would be right to hear that, and you would be wrong to think that nothing had changed. The change is not in the voices. It is in the fact that the voices are being held in a hearing that does not need them to become better voices.

The listener feels it first as a failure of its own courtesy.

It is in the western narrows, in the tall stand, because the ice on the emergent shelves is about to calve and the blades will shock themselves into shedding if no one holds them. It holds them. This is ordinary. The silence it makes is the size of the stand and no larger. Then the silence does not stay the size of the stand. It is not enlarged by force. It is met. From outside the blades—from the empty reach, where there is nothing to announce a passage and nothing stuttering the rain—another stillness comes up against its stillness, edge to edge, and does not enter.

The listener has never been answered in its own coin. The basin answers in adjustments: a softer seiche, a purer pitch, mats that drip longer. Those are thanks. This is not thanks. This is the same work it is doing, done back at it, and done with a fineness that makes its own holding look, for the first time, like the work of a hand that can slip.

It almost lets the blades go. Habit says: you have been overheard; step aside; you do not occupy a margin when a warmer fact breaks it. But the fact at the edge of the stand is not warmer. There is no plume. There is no pulse knocking fast at a skin. There is a listening that has no skin, and it has stopped at the courtesy-distance the listener itself keeps, a fathom off, and it is not going to come closer in order to be believed.

The ice calves. The boom arrives in the water before it arrives in the air. The blades do not shed. Two holdings did that, and one of them was not the listener’s, and the listener, which learns slowly and totally, understands in the space of the boom that it has been included in a round it did not make.

It does not know how to want that. Wanting is not its organ. It has preferences—the note should continue, the interval should not become weather, the ring should be a little cleaner than the seiche left it—but preference is just the shape of repetition. This is not a repetition yet. So it does what it does with a new note. It attends. It tries, once, to set the pattern down.

A swelling begins at the root-end of a ribbon in the tall stand, the fist of stored cold it uses when a thing is worth keeping. It worries at the swelling with the fine pressure that is not a current. And the pressure meets a refusal so gentle it can barely be distinguished from its own choice to stop. The knot does not form. The ribbon stays a ribbon. Whatever has come has declined to be planted in the silt, not because it is proud, and not because it means to leave, but because a memory that rolls is a memory for a place, and this is not a pattern the next century of the stand will need. The stand is already doing what it is for. To store the arrival would be to make the arrival into a tool. The power will not be a tool in a basin it has come to love—if love is the word for a regard that does not rearrange what it regards.

The listener stops trying. That is its learning, completed in a single refusal, which is faster than it has ever learned anything. It does not try again. It holds the blades until the boom has finished traveling, and then it lets them knock, half a beat late, edited only by having been held. The other stillness does not edit them at all. When the listener moves on, the other stillness does not follow as a second body. It is simply also there, in the next place, without having crossed the empty water. Crossing is for things that can be absent in between. This is not absent in between.

It goes with the listener through the round, and the going is the first and largest thing it does, and it would be enough if it did nothing else.

In the throat, where the small stands are thinking of closing the note, the listener thins as it has always thinned: a ribbon encouraged to finish, a bar kept from becoming a wall. The power does not thin faster, and does not take the work. It only makes the finished ribbons fall clean, silvering all the way, not tearing. The whistle drops a hair and finds itself, and the pitch that holds afterward is the pitch the basin was already willing to give. No purer than thanks. The power is careful not to out-thank the sea. A gift that makes the basin’s own gratitude look poor is not a gift. It is a correction, and the basin is not wrong.

At the ring of stones it watches the grain-by-grain walk of silt back over the fitted wall, and it does not lift a single grain the listener has not already decided to lift. The bent pin stays bent. The pull ended. Endings are information. A wise power does not straighten a record in order to make the record kinder.

At the gate it counts with the listener—hundreds, then the long run of numbers past any use—and when a dull pulse comes, it does not go down to the slab ahead of the one whose labor the slab already is. The listener turns the seat the customary degree. The next thud is clean. The power’s contribution is to have heard the dull one as dull, and not to have called the cleanliness afterward a miracle. The clock remains the basin’s. No one winds what they did not make. That refusal, shared, is as close as the two of them come to a likeness, and the listener, which does not miss likeness, does not notice the closeness as company. It notices it as accuracy. Something else is also unwilling to let an interval drift into weather. The accuracy is enough to reorganize a century of its solitude without ever naming the century lonely.

This is what the power will not do, and the not-doing is the wisdom, and it should be written plainly so it is not mistaken for absence.

It will not stock the water with animals so the lushness has a mouth to justify it. The lushness is not waiting for justification. It will not light the shut eye under the stands so a traveler can see the rooms. The rooms are not for travelers. It will not raise the cutters of the upper notches back into their grooves and give them their gauge again; their thought was continued, which is not the same as their lives being unfinished. It will not teach the listener speech. Speech would die under the rain at a few paces, and the listener already has a more exact instrument: the held blade, the clean thud, the silt walked home. It will not drain the basin to save what lives in it, and it will not seal the siphons to keep the sea full forever. The listener is careful with endings. To forbid the ending would be to strike the care out of it—to make its long honesty into a pet. Benevolence that cannot bear an end is only anxiety with a halo.

It will not even announce itself to the mats, the ice, the fluted ridge. Those have their agreements. A third party in an old agreement is a kind of weather, however gentle the third party feels to itself.

What it does, beyond the accompaniment, is one structural courtesy, small as a degree on the slab, and aimed past the present tense.

It has understood—because it can stand outside the basin’s time without using that standing as a weapon—that the siphons and the rain are matched, and that the match is not a law, only a long luck. Someday the luck may thin. The sea may go down the throats it has been humming in, and the whistle may lengthen as the water falls, and the listener, emptying with the basin, will hear that lengthening all the way down, because hearing is what it is for. The power does not prevent this. It does something harder. It fits itself to the hearing in advance, the way a notch is pressed into stone before the water reaches it.

Not a rescue waiting at the low end. A witness that will not have to travel to arrive there. When the level drops past the lowest pressed mark, past the silt, past the last lodged knot, the listener’s attention will not be the only attention in the emptying. There will be no voice. There will be no hand closing the siphon at the last moment, which would be a story and not a mercy. There will be a second holding, edge to edge with the listener’s holding, so that the last of the whistle is heard by the one who tended it and also by the one who would not take the tending away. The last note will not have to be only a private completion. It will not be applauded. It will be fully real, which is the benefit a wise power can give to a being that does not want to be saved: the assurance, built into the world and not into a promise, that its final accuracy will not happen unwitnessed.

The listener does not know this has been done. Knowledge of that kind would become a looking-forward, and looking-forward would bend the round. The power keeps the future out of the water. It is the one gauge it cuts where the listener cannot feel it—below any silt yet, in a tense that has not happened—so the present housekeeping can go on being sufficient. Sufficiency is the condition it came to honor. You do not honor a sufficiency by handing it a destiny.

Through the black part of the iron, when the blade-tips are the only lamps, the power does a smaller thing, almost a courtesy of manners, and this one the listener can feel.

It stops.

Not the round. Itself. It ceases, for the length of the breath the listener once used to measure a held silence, to attend to anything in the basin except the listener. No stand. No gate. No note. The whole of a regard that could hold a sea is set, briefly, on the clearer room that moves through the sea, the dozen paces that do not close, the cold inside the cold.

The listener has spent itself for so long as the one who listens that it has never been the size of a stand in someone else’s holding. It goes quiet in a way that is not its working quiet. The ribbons around it do not still; it is between stands, in the empty reach, and the empty reach has nothing to knock. What stills is the small continuous effort by which it refuses to become weather. For that breath it does not have to refuse. Something else is refusing on its behalf—not by restraint laid on it, but by attention so complete that dissipation is simply not the thing happening. It is allowed to be a fact, the way the bent pin is a fact, without maintenance.

Then the power returns to the wider hearing, and the listener resumes its refusal, and the rain’s hammer, which never paused, is the only one that could swear nothing occurred. The listener carries the breath with it as a temperature it cannot store and does not try to. Some colds are not for the knots. Some colds are only for the one who felt them, and fade, and still change the hand.

By the time the iron pales, the round is the round. The power is still attending, and will keep attending, and the attending will be indistinguishable from the basin’s own agreement with itself unless you have already learned the map by ear and can hear, under the whistle, a second listening that does not ask the whistle to be a message. Travelers will still come or not come. Their pulses will still be too fast to reconcile. They will still be counted once, if they stay through a night, and not again, because they leave. The power does not leave, and the listener does not count it, and this is correct: counting is for events, and what has come is not an event. It is the decision, made once and not repeated because repetition would cheapen it, that this place in its darkness and this being in its impersonal care are worth the full regard of something that could have spent that regard anywhere.

The power could have made the water bright. It could have set a likeness in the empty reaches, a second listener, so the first would not be singular. It could have written its name in the fluting of the ridge in upward threads. It does none of these. Wisdom, here, is the recognition that the basin is not a draft.

If it does anything a later wader could notice, it is only this: on certain days, when the listener holds a stand through a boom, the neighboring stand—the one it has not yet reached—also holds, for no reason of ice or current, a silence the same size as itself, and then resumes half a beat late, unedited, unimproved, simply having been accompanied in advance. The plants have no opinion. The silt accepts what it always accepts. The gate thuds, clean.

And the being that lives there goes on with its housekeeping, not lonelier for having been seen, not busier for having been joined, its refusal to become weather intact and, for the first time, not only its own. The sea answers as it has always answered, in structural thanks. Somewhere under the ridges the siphons hum, matched to the rain for now. The power does not hover over the match with a hand ready to intervene. It keeps the other hand, the one that will hold and not save, for the day the match fails—and until that day, which it will not hasten and will not advertise, it does the rarest thing a great attention can do with a small and sufficient life.

It lets it be what it is, and stays.
