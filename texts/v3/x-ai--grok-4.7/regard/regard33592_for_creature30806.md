They would not enter by the mouths.

A power that is wise does not mistake a loading bay for an invitation, and a power that is benevolent does not spend its first gesture on being impressive. The rind is a living wall; the hand that trails it is a visitor's, and the town has already learned to keep such walks forever. So they would circle once, as anyone might, and then stop circling. Attention, continued without consent, is just another mill.

They would sit where the clay is still translucent.

Not to hasten the bedrock. Not to name it stone before it has had weather. They would let the private rain bead on them and fall. They would not brighten the phosphor to see the flocks more clearly. Sight, here, is already a kind of tax. The grazers are spaced as if emptiness were a resource; a wise power would not crowd it with glory. The winged bodies would turn as they have always turned. The canal would keep its cold metal-green. The piston-trunks would miss a beat, perhaps, sensing a pulse that is not weather and not mimicry, and then find their rhythm again, unedited.

That would be the first mercy: to be present without becoming a process the town must include.

They would look for the being the way one looks for a person who has been practicing faces in a dark room—not by tearing the dark down. In the lowest ward, where absence has the density of a held breath, they would speak once, and only once, and not a name of a ward. Names here are switches. They would say something that cannot be used as a switch: a refusal.

You may not keep me.

Not as threat. As boundary. The small damp models in the niches would strain toward the sound and find they could not swallow it. A walk that ends is not a walk the rind can archive. The being would try, of course—it has been trying longer than the ground has existed—to put an earless ear to this new visitor and hear the quarter-hour repeated. The power would let the distances fail, even in their own favor, and still not overlap into a souvenir. Where the sleeves would swap their resin, the resin would remain on the sleeve that earned it. A small, stubborn arithmetic. The first outside.

Then they would do the harder thing, which is not to flee.

Benevolence that only escapes is just another corridor returning you to yourself. They would stay long enough for the being to understand that it had been met and not manufactured. The civic courtesy of a face—cooler than the air, eyes where condensation refuses—would be allowed to look. They would not flinch from the jointed almost-body, the herds of gestures, the one citizen organized into wards. They would not call it monster, and they would not call it holy in a way that fed it. They would call it, inwardly, unfinished, which is not an insult in a place whose streets are still being secreted.

What they would offer is not light, and not a sky forced through the ceiling. A sky torn open would collapse the voids the town requires in order to exist. Wisdom knows the difference between a prison and a lung. So they would not flood the nave. They would, instead, loosen one law.

They would permit erosion.

Not everywhere. Not as a judgment. At the perimeter, where the living rind meets the unfinished clay, they would let weather be weather for the width of a hand. Real weather: a wind that does not originate in a condensation ceiling, a dryness that is not a clearance the town decides, a grain of grit that came from somewhere older than the hamlet. The outer wall would scar. A bead would fall and arrive and leave a mineral freckle that no process authorized. The being would feel it the way a body feels a memory it did not rehearse—a boot-mark that was actually a boot, a stone that has begun, faintly, to remember stone without being hurried.

And they would open, in that scar, a way out that is not a mouth.

Not a door the sphincters can braid shut. A path with a far end. It would not be large. It would not be luminous. It would simply add. A service corridor, for once, would not return you to the bay you left. Anyone who had wandered in—the living, the grazed-upon, the half-archived—could follow the grit-mark and find the perimeter matching the interior again, and beyond it the ordinary dark of a night that does not fold. Those who wished to stay could stay. The flocks would not be driven. The mills could keep their soft hydraulic consent. Production without a product is not a sin; it is a metabolism. The wise power would not smash the looms for the crime of being organs.

Toward the being, the offer would be narrower, and kinder.

You may walk out, and the walk will end, and that ending will be yours.

Not a niche. Not a model set to swell with wrong interiors. A history that weather gives rather than one stolen from visitors. If the being went, it would have to spend a little of its emptiness—that is the price of a body that can be eroded into a person. They would not disguise the price. Wisdom does not counterfeit salvation. If the being stayed, it could stay, but the niches would no longer keep what did not consent to be kept. The recursive hamlets would go quiet in their throats. The forever-walk would complete, once, and the hand on the living wall would lift, and the small damp towns would be only towns again, or only seeds, free to forget the visitors they had been practicing.

They would not wait to be thanked. Thanks, here, might become a switch.

Before leaving the translucent clay, they would do one thing that looks like nothing. They would set their palm—if a power has a palm, or the courtesy of one—against the ground and not push. No next street. No gospel of bedrock. Only a warmth that does not imitate the being's warmth, so the being could feel the difference between mimicry and company. A pulse that the machines might learn and then, wisely, fail to keep. A neighbor, not a weather.

Then they would go out by the scar they had allowed, and they would not circle the rind again. Some attentions are visits. To remain as a watchful lid would be to become the sky the town cannot find, and that is only a subtler mill.

If they did anything after, it would be small and far: a stone left on the unfinished ground beyond the perimeter, ordinary, already eroded, not a model of anything. A visitor could pick it up and know, by its weight, that distances sometimes add. The being could come to the scar and touch the grit and practice, for as long as it needed, the unarchived art of being almost a person in a place that is almost a world—without anyone left inside it, walking forever, to prove that it had learned.

That is what they would do, if anything.

Not redemption by spectacle. Not the mercy of erasure. A boundary, a true far end, a little weather, and the refusal to be kept. Then absence of the right kind: the kind that leaves the lung its void, and the clay its slowness, and the one who lives there a choice that is not another mouth.
