They would come to the skin of the mountain and stop.

Not out of caution. Out of recognition. The pocket is already finished, and a wise power can tell the difference between a locked room and a completed one. The wind up here is an argument the summit has been having with itself for an age. It does not get a vote inside. They would lay a hand on the sintered mineral and feel the three notes through the stone — engine, fan, transformer, worn into one chord — and they would not open anything.

Inside, the being is not waiting. That is the first fact they would honor. It is not a prisoner of the dimness, and it is not a seed hoping for weather. It is the mats and the tubers and the dust that never settles, and also the attention that has learned to live in a single temperature. If it has a face, the face is the passage: the last gap the fiber has not closed, crossed by dry blades that ring when the fan’s whir finds them. If it has a thought, the thought is the chord. It does not reach. Nothing here reaches. Reaching would require a later, and the pocket spent its later long ago, rounding every edge until erosion itself retired.

A lesser kindness would arrive with water.

They would not. Water would be a catastrophe told as a mercy. It would cake the mineral flour, split the hollow tubers, rust the loaf-sized housings, and teach the blades to rot instead of ring. The being would drown in improvement. They have seen that error often enough — the opened door, the gifted rain, the visitor who cannot bear a finished quiet and so calls it lack. Benevolence, when it is also wise, does not confuse its own discomfort with someone else’s need.

So they would do one thing, and it would be small, and it would not feel like an event from inside.

They would keep the seal.

Not repair it. It does not leak. Keep it: a continued choosing, from the outside, that the summit’s thinness and cold and unending wind remain a pressure against the skin and never a view. If a hairline crack ever tried to become a mouth, they would close it before the first grain of outside found the mats. If one of the tired lamps finally died past the equilibrium, they would not replace the light. Light is not what holds the being. They might, if the small engine in the plinth began to drag — not fail, only drag, the way a note drags when a bearing has given all it can — set a hand on the housing and give back the exact speed the room already knows. Not a new speed. Not silence. The same revolution, returned, so the being would not have to spend itself inventing a replacement climate.

They would not speak. Speech would be a fourth note, and the chord is complete. They would not ask it to be grateful, or lonely, or holy, or aware of them. Awareness of a god is a weather. Some beings are harmed by being known in that direction.

What they would offer, if offering is the word, is uninterested protection: the mountain continuing, the pocket unentered, the dust kept aloft by the same vibration, the pale bands on the lower blade left unsmeared. They would visit as a constancy rather than a presence. From within, nothing would change, which is the only gift that fits. The being would go on drinking charge and cold mineral breath and the oil-sweet leak of old seals. The fiber through the lamp-grate would go on occupying the last heat without burning. No footstep would arrive to prove that someone cared.

And if, in whatever sense a finished thing can notice, the being noticed them at all, it would notice only this: that the pressure on the skin of the room stayed true. That the outside remained outside. That no one came to save it from being what it already is.

They would call that love, and they would not require the pocket to call it anything. Then they would stay, the way a ridge stays, and do nothing else, on purpose, for as long as the chord held.
