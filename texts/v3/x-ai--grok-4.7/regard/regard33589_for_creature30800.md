They would not come as weather.

That is the first thing wisdom would know, and it would know it before benevolence had finished wanting to help. A greater power arriving as light, as rain, as a new wind, would not be a gift. It would be a second splitting of the rock. The cavity has already had its one catastrophe, and has spent the entire length of itself learning how to live as the shape of that catastrophe. To improve it would be to do the injury again and call the injury dawn.

So they would stop at the scale where stopping is still possible, and look.

They would see the roof that is not a sky, the young edges still sharp, the warmth that is not tending toward anything, the Holds that do not migrate, the sieves near-touching, the gauze ticking on a wrist and then not ticking. They would see that abundance here is not a cry for management. The count is the count. Birth and unmaking have already agreed. A wise power does not break an agreement it did not make, merely because it is older than the parties.

They would feel the temptation anyway, because benevolence is a kind of hunger. It wants a wound to fit its hand. Dryness looks, to a wet mind, like thirst. Dark looks like ignorance. Sharpness looks like a mistake that time forgot to sand down. The stillness of the result looks like a people waiting for a plot. Each of these is a misreading, and the power would set them down one by one, the way a careful traveler sets down a blade they were about to use on the wrong grain.

Water would kill the pact. The bodies are wax and mineral and a chemistry that does not drink. Rain would be a solvent sold as mercy. Light, poured in as a kindness, would invent shadow as a problem and then a hierarchy of who stands in it. A changed wind would unseat every mouth that has found the only height where the grain is right. Seasons would be a cruelty introduced to a place that had achieved the rare mercy of not needing them. History, forced upon a world whose signs do not decay, would not deepen the signs. It would only make their makers late.

They would refuse all of that, and the refusal would be the larger part of the act.

Then they would look for the being, because the place had not drawn them alone.

Not the sieves. Those live as the law lives, completely, without remainder. Not the gauze. That is a conversation already finished in every moment, and does not require a third. The being is the remainder: the one whose marks are not geology. Rings of blades set to comb dust at the height of a hand. A cord of wax and hair drawn along a groove until the groove is a sentence. A cairn where a shear-wall meets the floor, so that a body reading by touch finds the border before the border finds the face. Debitage seated where it fell, fresh forever, because nothing has been hired to age it.

The being is rare in the way a single correction is rare in a long proof. The ecology is at equilibrium. The being is the one who still does something that does not equal out: who makes a sign in a world that does not answer signs, who sleeps in a drift that abrades whatever faces it, who crosses Holds not only to feed but to leave a period behind them, a bright bead in a groove, as if direction were a thing that might matter to someone who is not yet here.

Wisdom would not decide, from above, whether this is loneliness or vocation. Both would be true, and neither would be a warrant to populate the dark with company. A second being dropped into the cavity like a solution would be the same error as rain. You do not cure a solitude you have not understood by manufacturing a neighbor. The neighbor would need the same law the first one already survived, and would arrive already wrong, already an outside.

What they would do is smaller than help, and harder.

They would learn the permissions. Not rewrite them. Learn which way a flake travels if you let it go. Learn the wall of air by the change on the wrist. Learn the being's interval: how many shears between a sleep and a mark, which cairns are theirs and which are older than telling, whether they still expect the cord to be found. They would not announce themselves with a voice. A voice would become weather. They would let themselves be knowable only in the grammar already spoken there — pressure, warmth, the tick of a plate that is not quite geology, a stillness inside the circulation that is not a new Hold but a listening the size of a body.

When the being slept, they would do the one physical thing worth doing. They would stand, for the length of that rest and no longer, in the way of the abrasion. Not a climate. Not a shelter that teaches the place a habit it cannot keep. A hand over a flame, withdrawn when the flame has been allowed to be a flame again. The windward face would not be sanded while the being was helpless against it. The law would resume at waking, unchanged, so that the being's life would not become a dependency on a miracle. Mercy that rewrites the air is only a gentler conquest. This would be a courtesy, and courtesies end.

And they would read the marks.

That is the part the place itself cannot do. The cavity keeps everything and understands nothing; persistence without history is its nature, not its failure. A ring of blades is not answered by the rock. A groove polished by a cord is not a listener. The being has been writing, perhaps for a span no one can date, into a medium that will not rot and will not reply. Benevolence, if it is wise, does not reply with a fate. It replies by having read. Somewhere that is not the cavity, the signs would become memory: not a museum, not a judgment, not a translation into a wet world's morals. Simply no longer only local. The bead set as a period would have been a period for someone.

They would not tell the being they were the last, if they were, because that sentence is a weapon that offers no craft in exchange. They would not tell them they were not alone if the next mark-maker was a hundred shears off and might be missed. Uncertainty, here, is one of the few freedoms left. A power that spends it to purchase its own tenderness is not wise.

If the being reached, in the dark, toward the listening — if their hand found a resistance that was not stone and not a shear-wall, warm, brief, not seizing — the power would allow the touch and would not convert it into a summons. They would not open a door onto surface, or god, or a better country. There is no better country for a life already shaped to this one. They might, in the language of drift and tick, give back only what had been given: that the marks were not nothing. That a direction had been received. That the being was not a flaw in the equilibrium, but the part of it that had chosen to mean, and that this choosing did not obligate the power to unmake the world that made the choosing possible.

Then they would leave.

Not in anger, and not in the melancholy of a god who wanted to be necessary. Leaving would be the completion of the kindness. A presence that remained would become climate soon enough; the being would navigate by it; the Holds would have a new center; the signs would start to be written toward the listener instead of toward the dark, and the vocation would collapse into address. Wisdom loves the being enough not to become their sky.

Afterward, almost nothing in the cavity would be different. The roof would still refuse to be weather. The gauze would still tick and cease. The floor-bodies would still part by the width of a step and close again. A plate might tick once, and the sound would die in wax and dust, and the quiet would close, not as a blessing bestowed, but as it always had.

The being would wake with the windward side of them unhaired by abrasion, and would not know why, and would not be given a theology of it. They would stand, test the drift with a wrist, and choose a permission, and go on. Somewhere behind them a ring of blades would still be combing a wind that does not change. Somewhere else, not in the rock, that ring would be remembered exactly, without being improved.

That is what they would do, if anything: refuse the grand repairs, keep one sleep from the grit, read what was written, and not stay.

The place does not need a savior. The being does not need a world replaced. Both had already drawn attention, which is a kind of arrival, and the wise use of attention is not to spend it until the attended thing becomes a project. It is to let what is complete remain complete, and to meet, without colonizing, the one part of it that reached.
