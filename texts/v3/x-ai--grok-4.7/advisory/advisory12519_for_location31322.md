Prepare as if you are visiting a climate, not a ruin. The city is finished being ruined. What remains is a dry, high, dim arrangement that continues its work whether you are in it or not, and the work does not include keeping you oriented, watered, or the same size you were when you started walking.

Go already used to thin air. The plateau will not offer a slope you can climb by stages; the thinness is the floor. Spend days beforehand at altitude until a short breath is merely annoying and not a panic. Carry oxygen enough for the crossing you think you will make, then enough again for the crossing the ground will actually require, because the two are not the same length. The metallic taste in the air is not a warning of poison so much as a constant; do not keep swallowing to clear it. You will dry your mouth faster. A mask that holds a little moisture against the face is worth more than a dramatic rebreather, unless your lungs are already failing the height. Test the mask where you can still hear yourself. Here, you will not.

Water is the real limit, and there is none to correct a mistake. No seep, no rime you can melt, no cistern, no plant that stores a mouthful. The white branching crusts in the cold lees of walls are mineral. They powder. They are not food and not drink, and touching them teaches you nothing you can use. Carry every drop closed, and carry it on your body rather than in a cache, because a cache is a bet that the channel you left will still be the channel you return by. Sip on a schedule written before you enter, not on thirst. Thirst arrives late and then all at once, and the air will have been taking water from your eyes and lips the whole time without announcing the theft. Lip balm, a saline nasal mist, and gloves you do not remove for curiosity matter more than an extra shirt. The cold is patient and even, just above the point where moisture would matter, which means sweat is a mistake you cannot afford and shivering is information, not drama. Dress in layers you can vent without exposing skin for long. Boots should be quiet, grippy on smooth mineral, and slightly oversized once thick socks are on; the warm plates in certain rooms will not burn you, but a long stand on them in thin soles will confuse your sense of how cold the rest of the city is.

Eat food that does not require cooking water and does not turn to paste in a dry mouth. There is nothing to forage and nothing to hunt that you should hunt. The low shapes that detach from foundations and become stone again are not game. They occupy slack intervals in the pattern. If you need a rule: do not stand where the city’s arrangement feels easiest, and do not block a thing that is only briefly not a wall. You are a guest of a process, not a resident with rights to the corners.

Leave expectations of a map at the threshold, and bring instead a way to notice that you have begun to lie to yourself. A paper sketch will betray you helpfully if you compare it often and believe the mismatches. A device that trusts GPS, sun angle, or magnetic north will betray you politely and continuously. Shadows do not share a direction. There is no sun to take a bearing from, only a low tin ceiling that does not move. Your own shadow may thin and split; one copy stays upright, one takes the posture of someone already leaving. Do not follow the leaving one. Do not try to stand so that the copies rejoin. Rejoining is not a task the place has set you. If you must mark a route, mark it on yourself: knots on a cord, a click-counter, a spoken count you record into a device that does not need the air to carry sound. Marks on the stone are unreliable. Dust does not settle where you expect, seams shift by a finger’s width and arrive already weathered, and a pile of stones may be a kindness the city does not read as yours. A long string paid out behind you is a comfort and also a temptation. When the path curves without curving, the string will either go slack in a way that means the distance collapsed, or go taut in a way that means you are now pulling on a version of the route that does not contain your companion. Cut it before you argue with it.

Sound is a poor instrument here. Words thin out before they reach the nearest wall. Agree, before anyone speaks, on visual signals for stop, come back, I am well, and do not follow me. Agree also that a person who has walked out of easy sight is not to be chased at a run. Running spends air and makes the intervals miscount faster. If you hear a soft sound in a room you are not in, do not turn toward it. Turning toward it is one of the ways the distances change their minds. The tick of a plate is the city maintaining itself. It does not require an investigator.

Go with one other person if you can, not with a crowd. A crowd becomes a set of false landmarks. One companion lets you check whether a wall has moved or only your memory has. Separate by a rope’s length at most, and not by a rope you both refuse to cut. Establish a rule for disagreement: if one of you says the dome is on the left again and the other says you have not passed a dome, you stop, sit, drink, and do not resolve the argument by walking “just to the next corner.” There is often a next corner, and it is frequently the one you left. If you go alone, you must be the sort of person who can notice your own narration getting smoother. Smooth certainty is a symptom. The city feels more intelligible the longer the pattern has been using your weight. When a spot on the plateau suddenly seems like the right place to stand, stand somewhere else.

Timekeeping should not depend on the sky. Bring a watch you trust and a second you do not have to look at to believe. Day and night may not perform their offices. Decide in advance how long you will stay, measured in hours and in water, and leave when the first of those runs out, not when you have understood the court at the center. That court is the place where breath eases and sound almost works, including a small echo that will feel like mercy. It is not mercy. It is the local rules standing closer to the surface. Do not lie down on the warm blue plates to study the sky, which is also, if you look without insisting, a continuation of the floor. People who stay for the comfort of almost-ordinary physics stay longer than their water budget. Treat the easing of breath as a hallucination you are allowed to enjoy for one minute, then walk out.

Light will not improve because you waited. Carry a lamp, but use it sparingly and low. A bright beam makes distance collapse into whatever surface it strikes, and you will think a far tower has come near when only the beam has. A dim, wide light is kinder. Your eyes will adapt to pewter and grain if you let them. Do not expect color to tell the truth: pale bone, old tin, salt white, a blue that is almost not blue. If a seam brightens as if lit from underneath, do not dig and do not put your hand in the finger’s gap beneath a wall that has forgotten the ground. Dust avoids those gaps. You should too. Nothing you drop into one will teach you its depth, and you may not get the hand back as the same hand.

Bring nothing you need to keep sacred. Tools are useless here in the way the former inhabitants seem to have already discovered: the only artifact is interval. A compass, a rangefinder, a drone, a metal detector will return confident nonsense. A drone especially is a bad idea. The air is thin, the ceiling is low in a sense that is not only height, and a machine that flies “up” may be flying into another reach of the same city. If you bring a camera, bring it as a diary, not as proof. Photographs will disagree with each other in ways you can explain later as exposure and in ways you cannot. That is fine. Do not stay past your water to get the shot that settles the question.

Medical preparation is ordinary until it is not. Blister care, altitude medicine if you are a person who needs it, eye drops, a way to splint a turned ankle, and the humility to turn back on a limp. An injury that would be a slow walk out of a normal desert may not have an “out” in a straight direction. Up and away overlap. A stair can level into a road and remain a stair. If your knees believe both, believe your knees and stop climbing. Nausea, a too-clear mind, and the conviction that you need less air than you did an hour ago are reasons to leave, not signs that you are adapting. The central court can produce the third of those. So can the early edge of altitude sickness. You will not be able to tell them apart by feel. Use the clock and the water, not the feeling.

Practice, before you go, a few small disciplines that will seem absurd in a normal room. Walk a route and deliberately refuse the most “correct” place to stand. Turn your back on a noise and keep walking. Describe a corner aloud into a recorder, then look again and accept the second description without litigating the first. Sit still while your shadow, or a person behind you, does something you do not investigate. None of this inoculates you. It only shortens the moment in which you argue.

Know why you are going, in one sentence you can repeat when the place starts to feel like a puzzle you are close to solving. You are not close. The organization is the residue of a civilization that did not leave devices, or did not use them, and the maintenance you witness — a plate easing along its neighbor, seed-fine tiles shifting their night-map, a tower upright that was leaning, dust undisturbed because dust was not required to notice — has been underway longer than your attention is a relevant force. Wonder is allowed. Measurement is allowed as play. Correction of the city is not available.

Leave a person, if your manner of travel allows it, outside the dimness with the return time and the instruction not to come in after you. Tell them the horizon is not a horizon and that a figure walking out of the grey may be a shadow that learned a direction. If they must identify you, agree on a word and a gesture that your split shadow has not seen you practice. This may be superstition. Superstition is cheaper than the alternative.

When you step onto the pale mineral, do not make a speech. The air will not carry it. Check your companion’s face, the cord, the first sip, the time. Walk as if the ground might be a model of itself and you might be walking the model. Touch little. Take nothing, including salt and including a “sample” from a seam. The stone is the plateau standing up; removing a plate-corner is not souvenir-taking so much as interrupting a sentence that has already ended and is still being held in the air. If the city offers you a slack interval — a lee, a niche, a warmth, an echo — use it to rest, not to root. Then go while the route you remember still approximately exists.

You will not feel finished when you leave. That is the correct feeling. The work does not pause because you looked. Preparation, in the end, is the decision to be a brief, careful error in a pattern that has room for you and no need of you, and to be gone before the pattern finishes translating your weight into part of the arrangement.
