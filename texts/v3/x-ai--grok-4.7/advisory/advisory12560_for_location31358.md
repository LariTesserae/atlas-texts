Treat this as a place that will keep your eyes and unkeep your coordinates. The plain is cold, wet, vast, and quiet, and none of those is the thing most likely to end you. The thing most likely to end you is that it does not finish animals, does not keep one distance, and has already arranged not to have travelers. Preparation is how an ordinary body stays a finished fact for a short, pre-agreed while.

Go only if your way home does not depend on local time, local direction, or a landmark you can see. If your return needs a horizon, a heading, or “three days out,” do not go. Those are the first things the plain withdraws.

## What you are walking into

A high, ancient plateau worn past drama. Stone and gravel, cold as a rule rather than as weather, damp in the grain, in shallow pools, under thick leaves. Rain is local: one reach darkens and drinks, the next stays dry, and the boundary can lie along a street as if surveyed. Clouds form with no coast and no range, some of them at the height of a wall, dragging over pools and canopies without fuss. Light tells the truth about color and lies about the hour. One ward can be in noon while the next, plainly visible, is already in a long shade that may never arrive. Both are correct. They do not have to agree.

Vegetation is lush only where water persists, and there it is discourteous: mats you could lose a hand in, brakes that glitter and do not rustle, pale trunks, leaves wet to the middle, pulp that is half weather. Between stands, emptiness. A grove is an event you can walk out of. Its edge creeps toward lasting water by a hand’s width, without sound.

There are no animals. Not “scarce.” The absence is structural. Soft damp takes no tracks. Nothing hunts, flocks, or crosses. Nothing is food, because the plain is not willing to permit a mouth. You are a category error with boots on. That is not a metaphor you can shrug off after the first uneventful hour. Uneventful is how this place does almost everything.

Settlements sit in the lush pockets and, stubbornly, on bare stone. Wide streets, tight dry-stone walls, roofs that have become other streets, fired cisterns, hand-cut channels that run when water is present, gates that are stones leaned the right way by long custom. Markets are set in rows: fiber, knapped blades, cord, nested cups, simple metal rings. Nothing is taken. Nothing spoils like food, because it is not food. The rows still change. A bundle is retied. A cup is turned to the channel. A street gains a slab. No hands. The civic pattern continues. In the oldest quarters, paving sits on paving, and the alignment above can be older than the stone it is drawn on. The town remembers its map more reliably than the plateau remembers its mountains.

Space does not add up. Two cisterns you can see from each other can be one interval by the channel and another by the street that claims to parallel it. A lintel can put you on a surface the volume did not have. A room can continue past its stone into air that has agreed, for the length of an old plan, to hold weight. Direction is maintained near walls and allowed to lapse in the empty, where you can lose a heading without moving. Ridges stand up on the horizon when the plain remembers a mountain loudly enough that the light goes along with it, then lie back down. The stone where they stood is undisturbed, because the standing was not an event in the stone. Sounds arrive late: a knock, a pour of gravel, a wet clap, from something that already finished elsewhere. Do not chase them. They are not happening.

Every true observation is true only inside a jurisdiction that ends, without a marker, a few steps from where you noticed it.

## Before you decide the length of the visit

Set the visit in your own units, written down and carried, and make the return condition something that travels with your body: a counted number of sleeps, a measured expenditure of carried food, a device whose spring or chemistry you imported and have tested against your own pulse. Do not use “when the light fails,” “when we reach the next town,” or “when the ridge is due west.” Light will not fail on a schedule. The next town can be reassigned relative to the grove. West is a municipal courtesy.

Short is the only sane duration. This is not a place to winter, survey thoroughly, or “get the feel of.” The feel of it is non-culmination. Processes overlap and never synchronize into a season you can wait out. Erosion already finished. What remains is maintenance of a final form. You are not part of the maintenance unless the plain starts to include you, and you do not want to learn what that looks like from the inside.

Agree, before entry, on a hard abort that any one person can call, and on what you will do if you cannot find each other. Expect that you will not find each other. Plan as if the party will desynchronize. That is not pessimism. Visible districts do not share a noon.

If your only way home is to retrace steps, leave a trail the plain cannot quietly reassign. The plain reassigns direction, remembers shapes, and repairs streets. Cairns can be absorbed into a curb. Groves move their edges. A scratched mark on local stone may outlast you or be reset with the joints. Better: imported markers with a property the place does not use — a dye foreign to the gravel, a chime in a register the air has stopped carrying, a sealed beacon that reports to something that is not this sky. Even then, treat the trail as a wish. Do not bet the exit on it.

## The body, and the refusal of animals

Pack as if metabolism might become optional and hypothermia will not.

The cold is steady, not theatrical. You will not get a warning storm so much as a law you are underdressed for. Damp is constant: wet stone, wet leaf, dew that is not quite rain, rain that sheets and then is withdrawn, pools that do not ripple because almost nothing pushes them. Your failure mode is not a single soaking. It is days of cold water against skin, joints, and sleeping gear, with no animal heat anywhere else on the plain and no fire in the local grammar. The towns do not tend flame. Do not count on borrowing a hearth. There isn’t one.

Bring insulation that still works wet, and a way to dry it that does not require local wood or a civic oven. Spare socks in a number that would embarrass you at home. A closed sleep system, off the ground, because the ground beads. Gloves you can work cistern-cold metal in, if you must touch it — better not to. Face and ears covered in the open; the wind is not the enemy, the agreement of the stone with a low temperature is.

Eat only what you carried in, sealed, and on your own clock. Do not forage. The leaf is dense, wet, sweet or smelling of nothing, and half weather. “Half weather” is not a cuisine. More than that: a mouth is a feature this world declines to complete. Chewing local pulp is a good way to test whether the plain will permit the act, the swallow, the nourishment, or the continuing presence of the jaw that tried. You will not like the result of any of those tests. There is no game. Do not scan the empty for it. The scanning is how people talk themselves into a longer stay.

Water is everywhere and not thereby trustworthy. It is cold. It beads, sheets, stands, and moves in channels without a hand on the gate. It is also, in places, a skin of condensation that is not yet rain and not quite fog. Drink carried water first. If you must take local water, take it from a cistern or a channel that is clearly in use by the town’s own plan, not from a pool under a kneeling cloud and not from the pulp of a leaf. Treat it. Then still assume it may not sit in you the way water sits in a permitted body. Watch your urine, your saliva, your sense of thirst against the ration schedule, not against how “fine” you feel. Fine is cheap here. The light is honest and the quiet is deep and the body lies when the world is interesting.

Count yourself on a fixed ritual, morning and night by your imported clock: limbs, teeth, name, the contents of the pockets you packed, the faces of whoever is still in sight. Write the count. If a count comes up short, abort. If a count comes up long, abort faster. Do not explore the extra. A room here can have more surfaces than its volume allows. A body that starts to agree with that habit is no longer a visitor.

Do not sleep in a posture the streets could mistake for goods. That sounds like a joke until you have seen a bundle retied and moved to another aisle between one cloud and the next. Sleep inside your own shelter, off the paving, not laid out in a row, not curled in a cup of gravel identical to every other cup. The similarity, the description is clear, feels like a refusal. Do not offer it a rhyme.

## Time, distance, direction

Carry a clock that does not look at this sky. Wind it or charge it before entry. Do not “correct” it to local noon. There is no local noon that binds the next ward. Use the clock for meals, sleep, watch changes, and the abort. When it disagrees with the light, the clock is the one that still belongs to you.

Do not navigate by the sun’s height, by shadows’ agreement across a distance, or by how long a walk “felt.” A day inside a town will not match the day on the stone outside. Two people who split at a lintel can return, each honest, with incompatible elapsed times. If you must split, split only as far as a rope or a sightline you re-check on the clock, and prefer not to split at all.

Direction holds near walls and lapses in the deep empty. When you travel, travel the municipal grain: along a street, a curb, a channel, a wall line. Those are where the plain still bothers to keep “left” as a service. The moment the last curb gives up and the stone resumes its long unassignment, you are in the country where a heading thins and then belongs to a town, not to you, and the town is not where the thinning said.

In the empty, move in short legs with a stop-and-count. Pick a near stone, walk to it, stop, confirm the party, confirm the back-bearing to something you imported, only then pick the next. If the back-bearing is gone and nobody moved, you have already lost the jurisdiction. Stop. Do not “correct” by walking farther to find a landmark. Farther is how the addition fails. Sit down on your own gear, wait through one interval of your clock, and try the return method. If the return method needs you to reach a threshold you can no longer point to, this is the moment you should have already used the carried abort, not a horizon.

Do not steer by skyline. A ridge the color of the gravel, blunt, already eroded, can stand for as long as a private weather needs a reason, then lie back. It was never a destination. If you walk toward it, you are walking toward a memory the light agreed to hold. You will not arrive at a pass. You may arrive at undisturbed stone and a late knock from nowhere, and find that the town you left is no longer in the relation you paid for with your footsteps.

Path choice changes the interval. If a channel and a street both seem to join two visible cisterns, they are not the same journey wearing two surfaces. Choose one and finish it. Do not cut across a brake to “save” what the eye calls an hour. The eye is being told the truth about color and leaf and is not a surveyor. Cutting across is how you leave the maintained direction and enter the lapse with wet leaves to your chest.

If a grove that was on your left is suddenly not on your left, do not update the map and press on. That is the street, or the plain, reassigning the relation so a curb can keep meeting the leaf. Your job is not to help it. Stop and re-found yourself on imported references. If you cannot, leave.

## Towns

The settlements are the part the mind resists, then accepts, because they are too consistent to be mirages and too empty to be occupied in any ordinary way. Accept them as working cities with the inhabitants subtracted, not as ruins and not as invitations.

Enter on the street, not by testing every lintel. A lintel is where inside and outside stop being the same courtesy. The light on the far side will still be normal. The cold will still be the law. Damp will still bead on a shelf. And the shelf’s shadow may fall on a wall that, from the street, was the outer face. You will not feel turned around. The building has more surfaces than its volume, and it uses them one at a time, politely, which is worse than a dramatic portal. Polite is how you walk until the plan is satisfied and the plan is older than the stone.

If you go in, go in roped to someone who stays with a foot on the street you entered from, clock running, abort threshold pre-spoken. Touch as little as possible. Do not sit on the stair that has become a street. Do not lie down “just to see if the air holds.” It may hold. That is the hazard. Weight-bearing air is not a floor you can trust on the way back out, because the agreement was for the length of the plan, not for your exit.

Do not take from the rows. “Nothing is taken” is an observed civic fact, not a dare. A bundle moved by you is you inserting hands into a process that has been running without hands. You do not know whether the rows will accept the edit, undo it, or edit you into the aisle. Fiber, cord, cups, rings, and knapped blades are not supplies. They are the town thinking with objects. Look. Do not shop.

Do not block a channel, straighten a leaned gate, or reset a slab to how it “ought” to go. The kink in a street around a pool that was not there at the start of the kink is the town accommodating a plain that forgot. Your correction is noise in a memory older than the rock. If a channel is running, let it run. If a gate-stone leans, it has learned which way. You are not the engineer of record.

Walk the margins if you walk at all. Where the last curb gives up is the clearest classroom on the plateau: damp gravel, a far stain of leaf, a cloud that will not share itself, light that would be afternoon anywhere else and may be afternoon only for the length of that curb, while the grove you can see is still in the morning it started. Stand there. Do not try to average those times into one itinerary. Note them as a warning that “later” is local.

At night — your night, not the sky’s — camp outside the paving if you can do it without losing the wall-line that keeps direction. Camping in a square among the rows is camping in a workshop. You may wake with your kit retied, or with the street one slab longer and your exit no longer aimed at the stand of leaf you used as a backsight. If you must use a town for the windbreak of its walls, pick an outer wall, stay on the street side, and keep the shelter obviously foreign: color, cloth, and shape the courses do not use. Do not improve the wall. Do not fill a joint. The town knows its repairs, and a fresh pin is not automatically younger than the alignment above it in any sense that will help you.

## The empty

Between towns the distances are indecent in the ordinary sense and unreliable in the worse sense. You can watch weather being born and not coming toward you. You can walk out of a grove in an afternoon and not meet another for a stretch of days whose count will not agree with anyone else’s, should anyone else be walking. There is no one else. That includes, soon enough, the people you came with, if you navigated as if this were a normal plateau.

There is no game trail, no birdline, no smoke, no distant herd to orient by. Do not invent them. The eye will try, especially where a ledge might have held a nest and instead holds a cup of gravel identical to every other cup. The trying is the first softness of the madness the plain has already priced in. When you catch yourself narrating animals — a flicker, a settle, a crossing — say so out loud and turn back toward maintained direction. The absence is not a silence after a killing. It is the world declining to complete the category. Hallucinating the category back into it is you collaborating.

Low cloud will wet you and hide a town that was a stain on the geometry. When a cloud at wall-height comes, stop if you are in the empty. Wait until you can again see the difference between leaf and gravel. Do not push through on a bearing. Bearings are what lapse.

If a single sound arrives with no source, log it and do not investigate. It is late. The event is over. Walking toward a delayed clap is how you spend a real interval on a false present and come out in a jurisdiction that has moved on.

Do not use the lush stands as camps in preference to open stone just because they break the wind. They are wet to the point of pulp, they shift their edges, and they are the places the towns grow curbs toward. You can lie down in a brake and have the relation between the brake and the street change while you sleep, not because the trees walked in a way you can hear, but because direction was reassigned and your bed was on the wrong side of the courtesy. If you shelter in leaf, you are borrowing an event, not a forest. Be gone before the event is rewritten.

## Mind

The psychological risk is not darkness or monsters. It is consistency without inhabitants, and truth that expires at the edge of a curb.

You will be tempted to call the place peaceful. It is not peaceful. It is busy under the threshold of noise: condensation, repair, retied bundles, a street extended by one slab, a ridge remembered and forgiven. The quiet is a filter, not a calm. People go mad where the filter removes the cues that a mind uses to stay one person in one day — shared hour, shared distance, an animal cry, a reason for a wall. Bring work for the hands that is not local work: mending you carried, a text from home, a game with rules that cannot be reassigned by a street. Keep a log in full sentences, including things that seem too ordinary to note: who is present, what the clock reads, whether the cloud is kneeling, whether the cup on the market stone is still turned the way you left the looking. Read yesterday’s page aloud. If yesterday’s page describes a geometry you can see and the clock interval is impossible, believe the impossibility and start the exit. Do not edit the log to make a cleaner story. Cleaner stories are how the town thinks. You are not the town.

Talk. Not about the meaning of the plain. About rations, wet socks, the next short leg, the abort. Meaning is abundant here and it does not culminate. You can drown in a correct observation.

If someone in the party begins to speak of the rows as needing help, of a room that “goes a little farther, it’s fine, the air holds,” of a ridge that will make a shortcut, or of not being hungry in a way that sounds proud, that person does not get a vote on staying. Kindness, in this country, is a geological force. Do not admire it from closer in.

## Kit, in the order it will matter

A return method that keys off the traveler, not the map. Test it somewhere that already has mild spatial oddity if you can. If you have never tested it, you are not ready, no matter how good your coat is.

Imported time, redundant: a mechanical clock and a second method that does not share its failure. Spare spring or cells. Do not rely on the sky, on radio pips from a sky that is not this one, or on a satellite that needs a consistent horizon.

All calories for the planned interval plus a full abort interval, sealed against perpetual damp. No “we’ll live off the land.” The land does not feed mouths.

Water capacity and treatment for cistern-cold, slightly mineral, possibly more weather than water. A way to melt nothing; there may be no ice, only cold liquid. Do not count on snow.

Wet-cold clothing system, spare insulation, spare socks, a sleeping system that stays dry in fog that has opinions. Ground sheet. Cord you brought. You will see local cord. Leave it.

Lighting that does not pretend to tell time. A lamp is for the interior of your shelter and for checking counts. It will not reconcile two wards.

Navigation that assumes failure: short-leg markers you imported, a rope, a sighting instrument you use only to notice disagreement, not to “solve” it. Compass error is likely in a place where direction is a service rather than a field; if it works near walls and wanders in the empty, that is information, not a device fault to calibrate against a phantom ridge.

A small medical kit biased toward cold injury, skin breakdown from damp, and the decision to leave. Not toward surgery in a square. You will not be evacuated by local traffic. There is no traffic.

Shelter obviously foreign, freestanding, so you need not tie into a wall or a pale trunk. Stakes that bite gravel. The wind may be slight. The damp will not be.

Something to write on that does not dissolve, and a rule that the log is not revised.

Blades, if you carry them, are tools for your own cord and food packs, not for harvesting leaf or prying a gate-stone. Local knapped stone is sharp and is not yours.

Leave the survey grid, the long-term cache plan, and the idea of a base camp in town. A cache is a bundle. Bundles get retied.

## How to move, if you go

Cross in at a town edge if you can choose, where a curb still maintains direction, not in the deep empty where the first thing lost is the heading you arrived on. Stand until you have seen one private weather form and not come to you. That teaches the scale faster than walking. Confirm the clock against your own pulse, not against the light. Confirm every person against the list. Then, if the visit still fits inside the abort window, walk one short municipal leg and stop again.

Prefer one town, learned slowly from its streets, to a traverse of the plateau. The plateau is large enough to keep its own sky. You are not. A single ward, a single channel followed to its cistern and back, a single market observed across one cloud-passage: that is a full visit. If the cup is turned when you look again, you have seen the active process. You do not need to see the slab being added. Wanting the hands is how travelers overstay a world that will not supply them.

Do not chase lushness. Do not chase the kneeling cloud. Do not enter a second story because the stair is sound. Soundness is not the same as a volume that will give you back.

When the abort condition trips — by clock, by ration, by a failed count, by a lost back-bearing, by a companion’s eyes going civic — use the return. Do not walk “just to the next curb” to make the leaving tidy. Tidy is the town’s virtue. Yours is to remain an animal the plain did not finish, and to be gone before it corrects the oversight.

## What it will feel like, so you can notice the drift

Honest color. Pale gravel, green-black wet leaf, dull metal gone the color of damp, fired clay, fiber the color of the trunks. No gothic dimming. You will see very well, which makes the contradictions harder, not easier. You will see the other noon. You will think you can walk to it.

Quiet that is not empty. After a while the small change in the quality of quiet when a channel runs will seem loud. Let it stay small. If you start needing the late knocks, you are listening for a present tense this place often does not keep.

Cold in the teeth and the knuckles, damp in every fiber you did not seal, the particular tiredness of a body that keeps checking whether it is still fully specified. Hunger on schedule if you are lucky. If hunger fades and you feel clear and light and fond of the rows, that is not adaptation. That is the kindness. Leave while you still have a mouth to argue with.

You may feel, standing at the last curb, that the view is simple enough to trust: pools, a far grove, a cloud that minds its own reach, afternoon laid politely on the stone. It is simple. It is also only true here, for this curb, for this interval, and the grove is in another morning, and nothing will call you back if you step off into the lapse to prove you can. The glint on a cup of water, if you walked back to look, would be accurate. The water would be cold. Accuracy is not a road.

Go in finished. Stay near the plan only as a guest who does not help. Eat your own food. Believe your own clock. Count your own hands. Leave by a method you carried, while the plain is still declining to notice that an animal got in.
