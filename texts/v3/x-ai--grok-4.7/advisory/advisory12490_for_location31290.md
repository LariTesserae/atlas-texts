Prepare as if you are unfinished erosion that has not yet been corrected. The basin does not host visitors. It continues a process. An ordinary human body reads, to that process, as an edge still too sharp, a heap that implies intention, a draft of height and plan. Everything you bring will be filed the same way unless it already looks like film, meal, or load.

Do not expect soil, shelter, fire, or a horizon that stays where you leave it. Drainage is gone. Emptiness is not produced here. Cold is not weather over the water; it is the water and the mineral agreeing. You will be wet before you agree to be wet, and the wet will conduct.

### What the place will try to do with you

It will soak you to the temperature of annealed stone. It will include you in a badly insulated regional circuit: small discharges in the film, sheet lightning when the condensate loads, a hum you feel in the teeth. It will offer mineral wax that, if held, starts to sort the salt of your skin and allocate a tiny chamber. It will treat regular sound as a drawing. Mills are templated out of wet stone by grind held in phase; do not assume your wet gear, your breath, or a repeated footfall is exempt.

It will mis-sequence you. A mill’s output can be present while its input is still empty, and both are accurate. You may see yourself, or your gear, at more than one point of a process without those points being copies. Counting will fail, not because the numbers change, but because the individuals were never the kind of thing a number survives. Flashes will show interiors you have not entered, grades not yet precipitated, arrangements that are not the present. Light is honest about how little you can see and dishonest about time.

Scale will not warn you. A shed-sized mill can contain valley-sized work. Crouching at a mouth-fold does not create a threshold. If you go in, the exterior will not swell to let you back out, and sequence will not guarantee an exit.

Nothing you construct will be allowed to finish. A stake, a taut line, a corner, a shelter pole, a heap of packs “for later” is unfinished erosion. Mills drift toward that kind of mark without hostility. Abrasion resumes. The mark becomes film, meal, and eventually another chamber. Untouched is enforcement, not peace.

There is no leaf, no crop, no fuel, no silence. Death, if it happens, does not clear a space. It changes grain size. Space is not returned.

### Mind, before body

Practice failing at sequence on purpose, in a safe place, until it is boring. Walk a short loop and refuse to narrate it as before-then-after. Pick up an object and set it down without letting the two moments become a story you need to be true. You will need that refusal when a flank-vent shows finished precipitate while the mouth is empty, and when a flash shows your own boot where you have not stepped.

Practice not counting. In a crowd, in rain, in a workshop, let density be density. The basin is packed without a center: mills, motes, wax-sheets, weather-columns, thieves of the middle grade. If you start a census you will spend the visit inside the error.

Practice irregular sound. A locked phase is a template. Hum a tune and stop mid-phrase, again and again, until breaking pattern is easier than completing it. Do the same with gait: no cadence long enough to be a drawing. The grind here pulses hard enough to print. Your job is not to join the lock.

Decide, in advance, that flashes are not maps. A discharge may show the grit already refined, or a chamber from an angle the shed does not admit. Acting on that image is how an ordinary human walks into an interior that has more country than its outside.

Accept that “standing” may not persist. The film may not take your weight as a point. Mills shoulder their load; they do not stroll. If the surface rejects a planted foot, you will feel it as a filing, not a slip. Distribute contact. Be ready to be slurry rather than statue.

Name one felt anchor that is not a count and not a repeated word: a pressure, a scar, the particular wrongness of your own pulse. Speech is sound. A name said in time with the regional hum is a risk. If you must speak, break it.

### Kit that will not be finished off immediately

Assume every surface you own will film over, and that dry is a temporary lie you bought with weight. The air is near saturation. Rain is mostly the same water coming back. Post-rain is the permanent tense.

Round every edge before you leave. No stakes, no bladed tools, no box corners, no frames, no taut ridgelines, no stacked “camp.” A knife is an argument the basin has already answered. If you need a cutter for your own layers, make it something you can lose without a ceremony, already dull, already stained, and do not set it down as a placed object. Placement is intention.

Prefer materials that already look worn past shine: matte, meal-like, slightly swollen with old damp, no logos that read as plans. New polymer, bright metal, crisp seams, and symmetrical packs are drafts. Bright metal is also a temptation. The mills precipitate dissolved metal as a late decision of cold water; a clean conductive edge may be read as ore that has not yet agreed to be meal. Do not wear a cage of clever antennas.

Insulation against the circuit matters more than insulation against a picturesque chill. The cold will not bite; it will soak until you and the mineral answer in the same temperature. That is slower and worse. You need a layer that keeps core heat while wet, and a way to keep current from taking the shortest path through chest and skull when the sheet discharge runs. Expect the film to bridge anything you thought was isolated. Redundancy should be soft and overlapping, not a hard shell with corners. A hard shell is a shed the basin did not accrete, and sheds here are mills.

Ear protection that you can break out of. The hum, the click of joints, the seep-tick, the wet crack of discharge, the rain on wax and crust: silence would be an event, and it will not come. You can be deafened, and you can also be templated if you are wet and a phase locks against you. Total, permanent muffling is a mistake; you need to hear a mill change its grind when something bores its mesh, and you need to hear the load coming back into the light. Use protection you can vent, and do not let your own breathing fall into the pulse.

Eye cover that dims further, not that “clarifies.” Flashes wrong-light the flats. A visor that tries to enhance contrast will feed you non-present arrangements more vividly. You want less myth, not more.

No open heat. There is nothing to burn that the basin recognizes as fuel, and a flame is a mark. Chemical warmers, if you bring them, should be flat, already ugly, and fail closed. Do not arrange them.

Water and food in forms that do not ask to be architected. Local water is the circuit: cold, metal-held, older than thirst. Do not drink it, do not rinse wounds with it, do not “top up.” Carry a little more than you think a short visit needs, because short is a sequence and sequence is optional. Packaging should be soft and sacrificable. Do not decant into a neat array.

A shedding layer for wax. The wax is yellowish, unmelting, cold-metal sweet, with no flower behind it. It is not a souvenir. If it wets a glove and you keep the glove, it may hum, allocate, and begin industriously on you. Outer layers should come off in one ruined motion and be released into the film without being piled. A pile is a heap. The basin files heaps.

Nothing for shelter that has a joint, a plan, or a name like “tent.” If you must have a refuge from the densest sheet-discharge, think of a low, already-collapsed skin you can lie under without raising height. Height was the draft that was corrected. Do not raise it again.

Do not bring a pet, a clock you must mind, a metronome, a recorder that captures long unbroken audio, or a companion whose job is to “keep watch in shifts.” Shifts are sequence. A long clean recording is a drawing waiting for wet stone. If your multiverse return depends on a device, that device is the one object you cannot let the basin finish. Encyst it in something rounded, matte, and uninteresting; do not mount it; do not display a signal strength ritual; do not let its status tone lock to the hum. Test, before you travel, that return can be triggered by a single irregular action you can perform with filmed hands, in dimness, while shaking, without a checklist. Checklists are sequences. If the device must hear a phrase, the phrase cannot be prosody. Break it.

If you keep notes, keep them as stains, not diagrams. A map of chambers will be wrong at the scale where it matters and may itself be read as a plan. The horizon occasionally includes the patch you are standing in, not as reflection, as grammar. Your map will try to unfold that and will fail.

### Body

Cut nothing decorative on the way in. Nails short and blunt. No fresh tattoos, no open jewelry holes, no taped edges of athletic tape that read as lines of force. Hair bound in a way that does not make a regular silhouette; loose hair films, mats, and becomes a wick for wax.

Skin is already a meal of salt. That is the specific risk the wax knows. Before entry, be as uninteresting to a sorter as a human can be: clean of perfumes and sweet metal smells, no oil that mimics the mineral wax, no conductive sweat left to dry into crystals on the wrists. You will sweat anyway. Plan to keep wrists, neck, and any broken skin under the shedding layer, not under a tight conductive band.

Ears, teeth, fillings, implants, pumps, old pins: the basin is one circuit. Tell yourself the truth about what is already metal in you. Sheet discharge does not negotiate with pacemakers or with a romantic attitude toward “a little static.” If you carry a device that must keep a rhythm in your chest, this visit is a category error, not a challenge. Ordinary, here, means you can afford to be irregular.

Eat and drink on your own schedule right up to entry, then stop expecting the schedule to remain a line. Hypothermia of the annealed kind makes people reasonable, which is fatal. Reason will say the seep is only damp, the mill is only large, the flash is only light. Rehearse the opposite sentence until it is dull: the damp is the country; the large contains more country; the light is a lie about time.

Do not fast into visionary clarity. Clarity is how the optional sequence recruits you. Be fed, salted from your own stores, and slightly boring to yourself.

### Entry

There is a cycle. Condensate thickens until the gray fleece is almost a wall, mills sound nearer than their sheds agree to be, and the circuit runs hot for cold metal, cracking in sheets. Then the fleece fails at once—the same water, harder—and the dimness lifts by a fraction. For that short while the packing is more visible than you can sort: one industry from the near film to a horizon that may already include you. Then the fleece rebuilds and the load returns to the light.

Enter as the fleece is failing, not at the lock. You want the brief honest dimness, not the moment when a phase is printing new bodies. You do not want to arrive as a sharp correction in the middle of a template.

Cross the film as weight spread out, already wet in your outer layer so the first soaking is not a drama. Do not plant a flag of dryness. Do not step in time. If a mill is shouldering toward you, it is not approaching with intent; adjacent machines share a floor. Yield the floor. Do not present a corner.

Do not touch the wax-sheets, even though they are the clearest “paths.” They are conveyors. Sorted grains ride them. You are not a sorted grain, and you do not want to become one. The black glass lenses are old melt sanded into the general flatness; they are not safe bedrock. The glass-pale mats process. They do not welcome, and they do not green. Walking on them is still contact with the metabolism of refusal. Keep contact short and unsurprising.

Your return device stays where your hand already knows it, under film, not consulted. The first minutes are for not becoming a mark. If you stop to admire the wrong-lit interior of a mill, you are already negotiating with scale. Do not crouch into a mouth-fold to “see how it works.” How it works is: wetting, grinding, settling, passing-on, and sometimes not in that order. Your skull is not a sample you can spare.

### While you are inside the only building

You are not in a landscape that contains an industry. You are in the industry, which was never built, which is crowded past the possibility of elsewhere. There is no elsewhere on the flats to rest your eyes. Look near. Far is a series of revisions.

Move when the small ticks are merely continuous, not when the regional hum locks. Locked phase is when sound leaves residue. If you must wait, wait irregularly: shift, stop, shift, not a sway in the pulse. Do not tap. Do not rock. Do not let fear give you a drum.

If wax finds skin, shed the layer into the film and do not watch it start. Watching is how you grant it a sequence. If a mote-school packs against your legs and the tick rises to a crackle, you are part of the leakage. Step—not in rhythm—toward a thickness of film that is already crackling, not toward a “clearing.” There is no clearing. You are choosing a district of an existing circuit, not an exit from it.

If a mill closes a chamber, something was taking the middle grade and has been reduced to catalyst. That is not a battle you join. Do not investigate a quieter flank. Quieter is relative; the seep still ticks. A closed chamber is the process improving itself. Your curiosity is an edge.

Do not pick up precipitate. It is bright because cold water held metal for an age and a body finally decided. In your pocket it is a sample, which is a plan, which is unfinished. It may also be the wrong moment of a process that is still happening in the mill you think you left behind. Input and output are positions. Carrying “output” does not mean you have finished anything.

Do not try to rescue a small body from a sheet or a thief from a mill. Death here does not empty a place, and your intervention is a story about sequence the basin will abrade, taking you with the story. Mesh-thieves bore for the half-sorted because it is richer. That economy does not have a role for mercy, and it will not hate you for offering it. It will file the offer.

Sleep, if the visit is long enough that an ordinary body demands it, only inside the failed-fleece window if you can, and only as a collapse, not a camp. A regular sleeper’s breath can be a small lock. Better to leave before sleep. If you cannot, break your breathing when you notice it matching the grind. This will feel absurd. Absurd is available. Local logic is not on your side.

Talk to companions only in broken pieces, and never to assign them posts. “You watch the left” is a construction. The left may fold and include the right. If you are alone, do not narrate into a recorder. If you are not alone, agree beforehand that separation is not distance. Horizon is a courtesy. You can occupy the same patch without having walked back.

When the fleece thickens again and mills sound nearer than exteriors allow, stop traveling. Nearer-than-size is the warning that interior scale is loose and the circuit is loading. That is the wrong time to seek a mouth, a vent, or a “viewpoint.”

### What will injure an ordinary human, in the order you will notice

Cold soak, first as competence and calm, then as the inability to remember why irregularity mattered. Electrical leak, first as a taste and a tick in the teeth, then as a sheet that does not care about your layers’ opinion. Abrasion if you have made a mark or simply if a mill’s drift takes your silhouette for an unfinished edge: load-bearing bodies, repetitive, not cruel. Wax contamination, local and industrious, worse if you help it by holding still. Sound injury and, if you are wet and regular, the beginnings of templating—stiffening, allocation, a chamber that is not metaphor. Metal uptake if you drink the circuit or press broken skin to bright precipitate. Disorientation from flashes, folded horizon, and the discovery that a minute inside a flank was not the same size as the minute outside it. The specific horror of the census: you may not be countable, briefly, and the feeling will arrive as sanity rather than madness. Refuse the promotion.

There is no local medicine. The mats are not poultice. The wax is not salve. The cold is not a clean kill; it is a shared temperature. Anything you bleed becomes film chemistry. Bind with what you already wear, then shed if the bind starts to read as a joint of a plan.

### Leaving

Do not save departure for a final chapter. Sequence is optional; treat return as a position you kept available the whole time, not as the end of a line. Trigger it on an irregular action, during the lifted dimness if you can, when you are not in phase-lock and not inside anything whose exterior is smaller than its work. Do not wait to “finish seeing.” Seeing does not finish. The basin has been final long enough that finality is an engine. It will still be taking on load after you are gone, and it will not have lost a mote it decided to keep.

If the device stutters, do not troubleshoot in a rhythm. Do not lay parts out. Do not improve the antenna. Improvement is a draft. One irregular retry, then a different irregular action you practiced, then accept that a third identical attempt is a drawing. If you must abandon a piece of gear so the rest of you can return, release it; do not arrange it. Arrangement is how visitors become catalyst.

Afterward, do not assume you returned in one piece of time. Check whether a scar, a filling, a hem of wax-stained cloth, or a sound in the teeth arrived before the rest of you. Quarantine anything yellow and sweet-metal as if it were still working, because it may be. Do not hold it up to the light to admire the hum. Salt and a tiny chamber are a poor souvenir, and an ordinary room is wet enough, if you help it, to continue a process that does not know it has been interrupted.

The preparation that matters most is the one that leaves you slightly worse as a machine: less rhythmic, less linear, less sharp, less sure that interiors match outsides, less willing to complete a sound. The country you are visiting completed its project and kept the machinery. You are not going there to complete anything. You are going there to remain, for one visit, the kind of draft it has not yet fully corrected—and to leave before correction becomes the kind of help you cannot refuse.
