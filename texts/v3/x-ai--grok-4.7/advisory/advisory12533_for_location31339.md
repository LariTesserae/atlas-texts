Take the description as a briefing, not as scenery. It is already telling you what the place will do to a body that expects air to carry sound, ground to stay, and water to exist as a fluid you can find.

**Breath and water first.** The air is thin from the moment you step through, and it will not thicken. Treat arrival as high altitude that does not relent and does not come with a lower camp. Sleep a night or two where you can still choose to leave, and do not plan a long walk on the first day. Carry your own water in vessels that seal hard; the air will take it. Do not ration by hope of a seep, a dew, or a crust that looks wet. Wet-looking ground is mineral. Salt will be on your skin, in your mouth, and on anything left open. You will drink more than the temperature suggests, because the cold is dry, not damp, and thirst arrives as tight skin before it arrives as a thought.

**Clothing for a sandblast that looks polite.** Light is ordinary and does not store itself in the air, so shade is only what you bring or what a hold grants. A veil or close hood matters more than a heavy coat. Dust is flour-fine, sorted by wind that no longer needs a lee, and a passing veil can bury you to the knee and then be scoured away. Weave, gaskets, and the soft parts of boots will be eaten by an afternoon of open walking. Cover wrists, neck, and the gap at the ankle. Metals will keep their faces until a fresh scratch turns them to the sky, then they flower—rust, green, copper—all at once. Do not depend on a tool whose function lives in a bright, unsealed edge. Carry spares wrapped, and do not set anything down “for a moment” near a seam.

**Sound will not help you.** A shout dies in a few paces. Impacts of real mass tick in the teeth and then vanish. Practice moving as if you are already alone even when you are not: hand signals, a line between companions, a written plan that does not assume you can call a correction across a plate. The one loud room you may find—a half-shaved hall where the air is being held—is startling because you can hear yourself. Do not mistake that pocket for the country’s habit. Outdoors, your breath is a private event. So is a warning.

**Navigation is a relationship between holds, not a memory of ground.** Plates the size of cities translate on dust that has consented to be a fluid. A ridge can migrate past you in an afternoon. The ground you crossed at morning will be elsewhere by dusk, wearing another longitude. Maps of terrain are maps of a day. What persists are the holds: rings of dark metal, a spar too regular to be crystal, a rectangle of slightly thicker air. Inside their radius—on the order of a hundred meters—dust settles, a crust may persist, instruments misbehave by small rude amounts, and noon-shadows fall a finger short. Those circles are your camps, your waypoints, and your only honest return. Learn to recognize a live hold from a dead one. Dead rings are being milled with everything else; they will not keep the dust down, and they will not be there in the same relation tomorrow.

**Treat seams as machinery, not as landscape features.** A bright line of fresh fracture thickens into a trench the width of a road, walls traveling opposite ways at a walking pace or faster. It does not clutch. It mills. A dropped tool is bright, then gone, then someday a streak of oxide with no itinerary. Give lips more room than habit suggests. When a white wall stands up where the horizon’s darkening should be, you are already in the warning the country is willing to give. There is no third condition between early and late. Cross or shelter before the veil arrives; do not try to outwalk a gyre by reading distance the way you would on a world that keeps its mountains.

**Sleep only where the ground has been argued into stillness.** Outside the holds, rest is a bet against translation. Inside, the mercy is posthumous and local: a circle kept from finishing its last small motion, a little heat, a wrongness small enough to live with. Surveyors return to holds because return is otherwise a story about pins. If you must lie down in the open, tether yourself to nothing you hope to find again, and wake sooner than comfort suggests. Night does not stop the plates. The thin air gives up the day at once. Stars come large. Seams flash when a new face turns up—silent lightning at ground level. A veil can pass between you and the dull warmth you were steering by, and when it clears both you and the hold will have been moved, politely, in opposite senses.

**Life is not a resource.** Gray-green crusts in temporary lees are a side-effect with a narrow lease, finger-thick in a good year, and years here are arguments. Do not eat them, and do not camp on them as if they were forage you can replace. Animals are rarer than the stains. A low grazer the color of sinter, ribs showing, will pause when the vibration in the ground changes key. A predator the color of corrosion on ring-metal has learned the same radii from the other side. Two living animals in a day is an event you will date other events by. Leave them the lease. Your presence is already a loudness the country cannot hear and a weight it did not schedule.

**Mind the scale, or it will do the planning for you.** The horizon is not a line; it is a slight darkening where the air finally fails. Beyond that the same ground continues. Hours pass with nothing that wants a name. Plans made in distance do not survive. Carry what you mean to drink, a way to mark a hold you intend to come back to, and a method of leaving that does not depend on retracing a track. If you feel the flatness stop meaning emptiness and start meaning completion, you have understood the place correctly. That is not a reason to stay longer. The turbulence is not a storm breaking a calm. The calm was the end. What moves is residual, and it has other appointments than you.

An ordinary traveler prepares by becoming briefly unafraid of carrying too much water, too little hope of wood or echo, and no faith at all in the ground. The country is finished. Your job is to visit without offering it a tool, a route, or a second day it can quietly edit.
