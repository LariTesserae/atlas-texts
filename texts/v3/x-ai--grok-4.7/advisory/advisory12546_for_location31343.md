The cold here is not weather. It is a property of the room, held against the teeth and laid as a film on the tongue before you have decided whether to stay. Prepare for a wet cold that does not move, under a ceiling only a few body-lengths up, in a volume so full of living mass that “ground” is a courtesy word. You will not hike this place. You will part it, and it will part you back, slowly, with the sound of wet rope through a ring.

Go in as an ordinary body that intends to remain one. The chamber is already doing enough becoming.

**Before the threshold.** Do not commit your whole weight on the first breath. The air is present — the room is loud with water finding new channels, and a person can stand in it — but there is no wind to mix it, and the sweet in the mist is a byproduct of work, not a smell the open world makes. At the thickening where ribs pack until the filaments have no gap, wait. Breathe there long enough to know your head is still yours. If the sweet climbs and your thoughts soften, or your chest works harder than the cold alone explains, that is the visit. Turn around. Beyond that compaction you cannot tell whether there are more chambers or only stone still deciding. The system does not seem interested in outside, and it will not explain the air to you.

If the threshold breath holds, still treat the interior as a short stay in a sealed wet cold, not as a walk. Color in a plate can shift over an hour; that is also long enough for soaked skin to stop being a reliable instrument. Set a clock you can read by touch, and a turnaround that fires before you are curious, not after. Curiosity here is how a sightline becomes a stitched gap.

**What to wear.** Cotton is a mistake you will finish paying for after you leave. You will be wet to the skin no matter what the outer layer claims. Wear insulation that still means something when soaked, under a shell that sheds the falling half-ice and does not drink the standing folds. The ceiling sweats; drops are already half ice when they land; every surface beads. Plan to be filmed, not rained on.

Keep the silhouette small and soft. Passages close to a shoulder and then, on the pulse, by another hand’s width. A rigid pack will wedge you in a place that is not trying to hurt you and will not hurry to let you go. Nothing sharp at knee or elbow or heel. You do not step. You push through yielding ribs and plates the color of old wax and pale copper, and anything that cuts will be the first violence in a room that has no tool-marks at all.

Cover cuts before you enter, completely. The bright specks travel filaments the way messages travel a wire, and they will cross a sleeve if the sleeve is wet enough to count as filament. A torn knuckle is wet living surface. You do not want to be recruited as substrate, even locally, even for an hour.

Gloves you can work in, and a spare dry pair in a bag that actually seals. Bare skin on a filament meets a cold, faintly electric lean — the thread leans into the touch rather than breaking. That is data, and it is also how a hand goes numb and clumsy. Numb hands mishandle the only gap you have. A light face covering helps the teeth and the sinuses; it will not stop the film on the tongue. Do not lick your lips and swallow. Spit. The mineral-green sweetness is the cost of the lattice working, not a spring.

Footwear should drain, flex, and refuse to bite. There is no path to crampon. There is no path.

Bring one full dry layer in a sealed roll for the moment you are out, including socks and a wrap for the head. You will leave colder than you entered, and the last thing to go is the cold on the teeth. Warming starts after the boundary, not in it. A small heat source that does not bare a flame into the mass — chemical, held against the chest on the way out — is worth more than food you will not want.

**Light, eyes, and the beads.** The room consents to a dim green-gold, a memory of reading light, pooled in water that is never still and never gone. You cannot trust it to show you a foothold or a page. Bring a small warm lamp and a second one, both kept against the body so the cold does not quietly kill them, both with a way to wipe a lens that will fog in a single breath. Do not wait for your eyes to “adjust” and then walk. Adjust, then look, then move one body-length, then look again.

Do not navigate by what the hanging beads show. A careful look into a condensate lens the size of a seed can hold a second chamber, or this one a little later: stone further closed, a new rib where your shoulder is now. It stutters when the pulse comes. It is not a reflection and not a map you were offered. It is the mass computing in tensions too small and too orderly to be growth alone. If you follow an image, you are following a process, not a corridor. Your lamp will fog, flare, and make those images lie more fluently. Use light to see mass and gap. Ignore the pictures.

Expect your sense of scale to fail in a useful way. This is one room, grove-small, cathedral-small, a feature you could walk the edge of in a short time if the edge would hold still. It will not. The boundary is a thickening, not a line.

**How to move.** Match the slowness or be rude by accident. Nothing here hurries. A many-legged back sheathed in the same wax-copper as the plates may remain a wall for as long as you need a wall. If you lean, it leans away at the speed of cooling metal, courteous, unbothered. Do not test that courtesy with your weight. What looks like architecture may be a tenant listening for the pulse and moving a fraction after it. Habit, not panic — but a passage narrowing by a hand’s width does not care that you meant to be gentle.

Keep your arms in. Let the filaments take the brush of a wet sleeve if they must; do not harvest them, do not pluck a bead to “see it better,” do not cut a plate to learn the grain. Under the dim, that grain can look like rows, addresses, a hand that does not smudge when it grows. Taking a piece is how you stop visiting and start interrupting a clock. The pulse you feel in the knees is not clearly a heart. It may be the whole volume agreeing, once in a long while, on when to be denser and when to let a passage open. You want to be out of the way when it agrees.

Talk little. The loudness is not wind. It is water ticking and hissing, a hollow note in a stem that holds longer than a breath, the hiss of condensate taking and leaving the air, a soft continuous creak as ribs adjust their load, and under all of it the pulse. Close, intimate, exhausting. Judgment fatigues before muscles do. If two of you enter, agree on touch signals beforehand: stop, back, cold, out. A voice will not carry cleanly, and once the compaction seals finger-width by finger-width, a partner in the next thickness may as well be in another world.

Better: one of you does not enter. The most useful preparation is a person at the boundary who watches the gap you came through and calls the clock, because from inside you cannot see the exit becoming a wall of ribs. If you go alone, your clock is that person, and it is a worse one. A tether sounds wise until you remember what the ceiling does to fresh stone — silver root-hairs drinking the weep, mineral recruited before weather ever gets a vote. A line left across a gap may be sheathed, stitched into, or simply be the reason a filament chooses that line as its next wire. If you use a cord, use one you can abandon without regret, check it every few body-lengths, and do not tie it to anything that might be a back. Prefer the partner. Prefer the clock. Prefer leaving early.

**What will try to become your landmark.** Do not blaze, notch, stack, or plant a flag. There is no made thing here, and the room is mid-sentence; it recruits surfaces. A flake of new stone no larger than a palm, still sharp, still deciding, can wear its first silver hairs while you watch. Your careful cairn will be a suggestion the mass improves upon. Your sightline will grow a filament, and by the time you notice, the specks will already be running it, and a nearby note will have shifted by a small irritable interval.

So your landmarks are temporal, not spatial. How many pulses since you turned. How much the exit thickening has narrowed, felt with the hand, not remembered with the eye. Whether your lamp’s spare is still where you clipped it — not because the room steals like an animal, but because wet cloth and small gear migrate into the general cling. Count, don’t memorize. The room you entered will not be the room you leave. That is not a curse. It is the ordinary fact of the place, and it makes visual memory a liar.

**Body, in order of failure.** Cold first, then judgment, then the small cuts and the sweet on the mouth, then the wish to stay for one more shift of color. Hypothermia in still, soaking cold does not announce itself as drama. It arrives as a reasonable idea: that the plate warming from pale copper toward dull rose is worth another half hour; that the imaged chamber in the bead is information you came for; that the courtesy of the leaning wall means you are being housed. None of those are warmth. Eat something plain from your own stores before you go dull. Drink only what you carried. Do not taste the standing water to “confirm the description.” The description is already the warning: mineral, green, faintly sweet, cold enough to ache.

Eyes will sting from the mist. A dry cloth inside the sealed bag, not the wet one at your neck. If a speck crosses your sleeve, you get a brief colder patch and a faint sweet smell, not a wound — unless you slap and tear. Let it finish being a message. You are not the address.

If the pulse comes and the passage tightens while you are in the narrow, do not fight the rib. Fold in, protect the face, wait the relaxation. Fighting is how a shoulder becomes the place a new rib thinks of occupying. The fauna answer the tightening by tightening. You should answer it by becoming temporarily smaller and then, when it eases, by leaving.

**What to carry, and what to leave in the other world.** A sealed dry-bag with the spare layer, a cloth, a little water, a little food, the second lamp, the clock. A way home that does not depend on this room remaining geometrically faithful — kept against dry skin, not swinging through the mass, not laid on a plate “just for a moment.” The filament tension is faintly electric and everything is wet; a device that is your only exit does not get to be curious.

Leave blades, saws, vials meant for tissue, bright floods, speaker rigs, and any plan that requires a sample to justify the visit. A drop that has already left the ceiling and died on your glove is the most the room will have offered you, and even that may be more claim than gift. You do not need to take it for the visit to be real.

Leave the assumption that abundant life means animals in a habitat, separable from the furniture. They are of the mass. A wall may stand up later, in the sense that a back was never a wall. Prepare your eyes to be wrong on purpose, and your hands not to grab when wrongness moves.

**How long, and how to stop.** Long enough to feel the pulse in the knees, to hear one hollow note decay, to watch one filament decide on a gap and decline to be the gap’s owner. Not long enough to watch a plate finish its hour. Not long enough to see the ceiling flake drink its fill. The processes are slow, which tempts a human into thinking they are safe. Slow is how the room replaces your exit without ever seeming to chase you.

Turn around while the way still parts. Backing out is the leaving — heels first, face toward the mass, watching for the stitch across the gap you just used. The compaction will narrow behind you. That is acceptable if you are already in the motion. It is a problem if you are still studying the rose in the water.

When the sound muffles and the cold deepens, you are at the boundary, not lost. Keep going until the loudness is behind you rather than in your mouth. Do not stop in the thickening to “check one more time.” The system spends itself on becoming more precisely what it already is. It will not hold the door because you were polite, though politeness — smallness, softness, no cuts, no leaning your life on someone else’s back — is the entire ethics available to you.

**After.** Get dry before you narrate it. Wet cold continues its work in the first safe room if you stand there talking. Warm the hands, then the chest, then bother with notes. What you will want to write down immediately is wrong in a specific way: you will remember a stable chamber, a path, a creature on a plant, a light you could almost read by. Correct it while your cuffs are still beaded. You parted a single unfinished room. It parted you back. The flake of new stone was already growing hairs. The visit was the overlap of two slownesses, yours and its, and yours was the one that had to end.
