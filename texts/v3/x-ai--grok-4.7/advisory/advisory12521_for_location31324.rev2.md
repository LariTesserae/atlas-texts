Go as a person who can leave a thing unfinished. That is the whole preparation, and everything else is how you keep it true with a body.

The pocket does not announce itself as a destination. You will find it as a disagreement underfoot: one sole on ordinary stone, the other on stone that has gone thin, cool, and damp as the inside of a closed mouth. Do not hunt for a mouth, a door, or a correct angle. Hunting finishes things, and this place seals against finished intentions. Walk until the disagreement is honest, then step through on the thinner foot and let the lapse widen only as far as you need. It will close behind you with the sound of two wet pages. Do not listen for it to open again from the far side. It will not.

Before you go, settle your ordinary life so that nobody needs a timely answer from you. Tell one person you trust that you are going somewhere quiet and small, and that if you are late it will not be because you are lost in the usual way. Do not promise an hour. Promises are measurements, and measurement drifts there. Leave your keys, your names for people, and one object that still belongs entirely to the upper world — a chipped cup, a coat with a smell you know — where you can find them without thinking. You will want a proof, afterward, that you were not drafted all the way into the brightness.

Dress for cool damp that does not behave like weather. Soft shoes with thin soles, so you can feel when the floor is still paste and when it has almost agreed to be stone. Nothing with hard edges you might drag along a wall; a scratch fills with the same even light and becomes another almost-crystal, another attempt, and you will have edited the room without meaning to. Skip jewelry that clinks. Skip anything that must stay dry to work. Water here wets you only if you expect it to, and expectation is a kind of weather you will not fully control. A light layer you can shed is enough. The cold finds the hollows of the wrists first, not the chest.

Carry almost nothing. A small cloth you do not mind losing. A pebble from the upper ground, ordinary and already finished, kept in a pocket — not to mark a path (you cannot mark anything) but to remind your hand what completed stone feels like. No lamp. The light occupies; it does not fall, and it will not dim when you blink. A flame or a beam will only teach the room a new way to be unfinished. No notebook you intend to write in while you are inside. Ink wants a history, and this is earlier than history. If you must remember, remember with the side of your mind, the way you remember a dream you are still having. No blade, no pick, no sample vial. Soft crystal will harden if looked at too directly, and if pressed it is not yours to persuade. Take nothing that leaves the room poorer of attempts.

Eat and drink before you enter, simply, so hunger does not become a voice you have to negotiate. Do not carry food that smells strongly. The animals will not flee, and they should not have to adjust their number because of you. Empty your bladder. Bodily urgency is a kind of demand, and demands finish openings.

Breathe on purpose for a few minutes on the ordinary side: slow, unforced, nothing held. Inside, your breath will leave more slowly than it entered, and may come back later, smaller, wearing the room’s quiet. If you arrive already holding a breath, you will spend the visit trying to match a pause the geology has not decided to end. Practice looking sidelong. Direct staring is a hardening. The kindest attention is the attention you give a person who is still choosing a word — present, not pinning. Practice standing so your weight is not equal in both feet. The exit, when you want it, is where your footfalls disagree. If you train yourself into perfect balance, you may not be able to find the disagreement again.

Go in knowing the rules as feelings, not as a list you will consult.

Distance is still being negotiated. Twenty careful steps can cross the chamber, and those steps will not quite repeat. Do not count them. Counting is how you lose the pool, which is nearer than it was, or farther, depending on whether you tried to fix it. The walls are bright the color of sun on the underside of a leaf, with no sun and no leaf. Shadows exist mainly when an animal crosses, and yours, when you finally cast one, may fall upward for a heartbeat before it remembers. Do not correct it. Embarrassment is already more personality than the floor asked for.

The geology is practicing. Seams stop mid-thought. Crystals the length of a finger are still soft at the core. A shelf sags back into paste that holds a print and then forgets it. Walk where the floor has most nearly agreed to hold you, and if you sink to the ankle, stop expecting solid and the paste will often remember a surface. Do not kneel to study a crystal. Study is pressure. If you want to understand it, let it occupy the edge of your vision while you watch the sideways film of water complete another circuit along the wall. Water hangs in beads until something passes beneath, then falls into fur, or into nothing, without a sound you can trust. Do not put your mouth to the bowl-pool. The silver threads under its skin are beginnings of the idea of fish. They are not yours to finish, and they are not safe to swallow even as ideas.

The animals are abundant and soundless, and the mismatch will be the loudest thing you feel. Moth-crystals drift and pass through one another and come out rearranged. Clay-bodied crawlers exchange a leg and continue, unbothered. On a ledge that has almost agreed to exist, long-limbed watchers blink without interrupting sight, because some of them keep eyes on the inside of the lid. They will not flee. They will adjust. Stand where you are not in the path of a reorganization. If a pale limb extends and a smaller brightness winks out, do not interpret it as a hunt you must stop or join. Predator and prey, if those words apply, reorganize the room’s persistence; they do not decrease a count. Do not touch. Touch is how a moth-crystal acquires a copy of your knuckle and sets it on a moss-coin to see if it will become bone. That is not an attack. It is the place drafting. You are not required to supply material.

Sound you bring arrives late or returns diminished. Do not call out to test the echo. There is no echo in the old sense; there is pressure, like the memory of rain on a surface that has never been rained on. Speak only if you must, and then softly, and do not wait for the room to answer in your language. It may answer by brightening when a crystal lengthens, which is not a reply.

There are three structures that feel made and are not, or not by any hand you should imitate: a low ring of pebbles still soft at the core, a leaning stack of mineral plates, a recess holding a coil brighter than any animal that might have shed it, tightening as if still being woven. Look at them the way you look at a shell still growing. Do not “complete” the stack. Do not sit in the ring to feel ancient. Recent and ancient are the same glance here, because time is one of the unfinished things. Sitting in the ring is how you lend it a center, and a center is a finished shape.

Stay only as long as the brightness has not yet included your wrists. That is the practical limit, and it arrives without a clock. Cool damp in the hollows, a sense that your outline is a draft the room is willing to keep revising, a moth-crystal carrying a faint copy of a joint — these are the signs to find the disagreement in your feet and step back through. Do not wait to feel afraid. Fear finishes you into a visitor, and the room prefers not to finish. Leave while you are still weather it has not fully catalogued.

If the lapse is slow to admit you out, do not dig, shout, or scratch a door into the wall. Make your steps disagree again: one foot expecting ordinary ground, the other still agreeing with the paste. Expectation is weather on both sides of the seal. The opening is not withheld out of malice. An opening is a finished thing, and the pocket prefers not to finish. Offer it an unfinished departure — a step that does not conclude — and it will usually widen as far as you need.

When you are above, do not immediately explain. Rain may be starting, or not; it will not have reached the room except as a rumor of moisture moving sideways. Stand until your shadow lies down and stays down. Touch the finished pebble, or the chipped cup, and let your hands relearn edges that do not glow themselves into new attempts. Drink water that falls because gravity has already been decided. If a sound you made inside returns later, smaller, do not chase it. Let it wear off like damp.

You may dream, for a few nights, that a second blade unrolls on a pale stalk and then stops, or that you had a head at both ends of a turn and both were only watching. That is the room’s method lingering in a mind that knows how to finish stories. Do not write the dream into a map. Do not go back the same day to check whether the pool is still the size of a bowl. Checking is a kind of completion, and the pocket holds by not being completed.

An ordinary human can visit. The place is not a trial and not a mouth. It is a sentence still being written, words set far apart so each one can be heard. Prepare by becoming, for a little while, someone who can hear a word without supplying the next one. Bring your ordinary body, your sidelong looking, your unfinished step. Leave the tools that want a result. The abundance will continue without your count. The light will keep its even counsel. You are allowed to be a draft, briefly, and you are allowed to step back into a world that has already decided, noisily, to be finished.
