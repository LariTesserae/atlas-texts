Treat the column as a room that is also a body, and assume your ordinary habits of travel will be the first things revised.

Do not dress for a sea, a summit, or a cave. Dress for a warm, close, animal darkness that will sometimes be water and sometimes be a stack of rooms of rain, and that will not keep the distinction long enough for you to correct a mistake. The temperature coming off the skin of the column is not weather. It is the heat of something that has been moving. Inside, that warmth thickens. A cold-water suit will cook you. A light climbing kit will leave your skin to the young edges. What you want is a thin, close shell that keeps your own temperature and your own blood from negotiating with the liquid, with palms and knees that can meet a fresh fracture without opening. The rock is new. It throws flakes the size of fingernails. They do not settle. They turn in the dark and are sometimes put back as a step that was not offered. Cover the eyes. Cover the mouth even though you will not be breathing the place. A cut inside this water is a sentence the place can finish for you.

You cannot breathe it, and you cannot trust it to remain the thing you refused to breathe. There are moments when the water is not water, and there is nothing to gasp. Carry air the way you carry a name: on the body, short, simple, not dependent on a machine that needs a stable up, a stable pressure, or a stable medium to keep working. Two small supplies, not one clever one. Test that you can find them with your eyes shut and your hands shaking, because the dark is not quieter and your hands will not be curious; they will be busy staying whole. Do not plan a long draw on that air. The column is no wider than a hamlet and no taller than a church raised on a church, but it will not stay the size it is. A visit here is a crossing, not a residence. Go in with the breath already counted, and leave before the count becomes a hope.

The sound will arrive before the skin does, and it will not have a quiet version. It comes through the ears and the chest at once: stone fretting stone, the inner face striking itself and coming back wrong, a low note that might be the fracture widening, a clatter as of many small hard things being sorted and never finished. Ordinary plugs will not be enough, because the roar is also pressure, and pressure here does not obey height. You are not descending an abyss so much as standing inside a decision about whether an ocean belongs in this room. Protect the ears, and also expect the ribs to take it. Practice, before you go, staying kind to your own pulse while a loudness sits on you and does not pass. If you need silence in order to think, you will not think in there. Learn a few plain thoughts that do not require quiet: out is the thinner light; the shell is not a door; I am one body even if the rooms disagree.

Do not navigate by sight, and do not navigate by the ruins. Light dies at about the length of a hall. Beyond that, forms suggest themselves and withdraw. A spar that seems an arm’s reach away may take a long time to arrive, or may arrive at you. Bring a small light if it comforts the hands, but do not ask it to map anything. The fitted ring of stones, the leaning post, the line of cups pecked into the face: these are memories of interruption, not waymarks. They do not align with any level the column keeps, because the column keeps none. A ledge can complete itself under you with a crack like a decision and then withdraw the decision. If you brace, brace lightly. If you grab, grab as if the hold might become a ceiling, or a rumor, or a cutting edge turned the other way. Rigid faith in a surface is how an ordinary body gets revised along with the draft.

Leave metal, glass, wire, and wheels outside. Whatever touched this summit did it with weight and patience and then stopped before it learned a second method. The column does not contain the memory of machines, and it is not obliged to host yours. A device that must keep a clock, a signal, a straight line, or a sealed certainty will fail in a way you cannot diagnose, because the failure will look like the place. Carry what a hand can understand in the dark: the shell, the air, a short cord you can afford to lose, a blade only if you already know you will not use it to pry at the built things. There is nothing here worth prying. The cups are not a code you should finish. The ring is not a shelter. Curiosity that needs a tool is the wrong curiosity.

Distance will lie, so do not trust a tether to behave like a tether. The column is small enough that a line to the outside seems like wisdom, and it is, until the skin turns its thinner face away, or outside steps in to hear the noise and cannot remember the door. If you use a line, use it as a timer and a promise, not as a measurement. Agree with whoever waits beyond the skin: if the line goes slack, or tight, or strange, or simply old, they do not follow it in. They wait at the place where the warmth begins, the hand’s breadth before the wall, and they count. You come to the brighter bruise when you can. If the bruise is gone, you do not search the dark for a better one. You stop moving and let the smallness of the place bring the skin back around, if it will. Someone who loves you should be told that following you inside is not loyalty. It is a second disappearance.

There is life, and it is not for you. A pale shape the size of a hand may cross a seam and be gone. Something larger may hold still until you understand it is being unmade and made in the same place. Do not follow it. Do not corner it. Do not decide it is injured, or holy, or a guide. The ecology is a series of near-misses. Stay in the emptiness between presences. The emptiness is most of the column, and it is the only hospitality on offer. A fan of weed streaming sideways in no current is a fact, not an invitation. Let it be wrong about direction without correcting it.

Before you step through the skin, stand in the true air and learn the last reliable things. The warmth. The clean cut of the edge. The way the outside day looks when it is still outside. Eat simply. Empty the body of anything you would hate to lose in a place with no level. Tell your joints what young stone feels like by touching, outside, one flake the water has not yet taken back. Then agree with yourself on a single rule that does not depend on up: when the place becomes a stack of rooms and you are in all of them and also in one, you protect the skull, the air, and the habit of a small body. You do not try to stand in the correct room. There is no correct room. Down is a courtesy. Up is a direction the water is currently interested in.

Go in already leaving. The liturgy of the place is the furious sorting of stone, at full volume, without pause, as if a world had begun an ocean in the wrong room and could not decide whether to finish it or take it back. You are not there to help it decide. You are an ordinary human, which means you can cross a short, impossible room if you do not ask it to be a world. Touch as little as a guest. Believe as little as a guest. When the skin offers you the bruised glow again, take it even if you have not understood the post, the cups, the pale shape, the note under the roar. Understanding is what the column does to itself, draft after draft. You are only visiting. Come out while you are still one clause, and do not look back to see whether the step you used has been discarded. It has.
