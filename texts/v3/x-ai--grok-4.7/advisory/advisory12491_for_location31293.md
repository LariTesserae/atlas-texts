Go down as someone who can be still. The bowl does not punish noise so much as refuse to keep it, and it will not rearrange itself to explain you. An ordinary body can make the descent. What fails people here is the expectation that a place this full must be about to do something.

The stair is short and older than any reason you will be given for it. The stone treads are polished to a matte the width of a foot; between them the moss is cold and springy and never muddy. It will take a boot print and give it back as darkness in the grain, not as mud on your heel. Step where the wear already is. Do not cut a new line in the lip. By the time your breath has gone white once, the country above is gone, and you should have already decided that you are not going back up for anything you forgot. What is carried down comes down rarely. Arrive with less than a basket.

Dress for a cold that lives in surfaces, not in weather. There is no wind to cut, and no sun to turn your face toward. Stone takes heat out of a palm at once. Wood gives it back more slowly. Fiber, grain, the rims of your eyes, and your lips are what the damp prefers; the centers of the lanes stay merely cold. Wear layers you can stand to have darkened at the hem, because the damp climbs and is not dirt, and fighting it will only wet you in the wrong places. Plant fiber, beaten and stitched, is what the town already knows how to read. Leather that cannot breathe will sour. Anything that sheds—loose weave, down, a coat that loses threads—will be a mess the place has no schedule for. A leaf that is finished stays until a hand takes it. Your lint will not be granted that courtesy. Bare face. Covered hands when you touch walls. A hood if you need one, but nothing wide: the slots are the width of shoulders and a basket, and eaves touch eaves.

Bring no lamp, no wheel, no fuel, no edge better than you truly need. The day does not fail. The white sky is hard, without a disk and without cloud-shadow, and it does not travel. Shadows, where they exist, are the color of the things that cast them and do not lengthen. Lamps of cold-worked copper hang in the town and are never lit, because a plant burned would have been better eaten or plaited. A flame would be an event, and events are what this continuance has replaced. If you need a knife, make it small, stone-ground in spirit if not in fact, and hang it where a peg can darken to its shape. An empty peg is an event. Do not be one.

The light will be the hardest thing your body does here. It finds every threshold. The pale rim throws the day down into the slots until even a deep cell has a bright band on its inner wall. Colors do not bleach; the cold fixes them. People stop working by custom, not by dusk, and they sleep while the sky goes on lighting the mouths of their cells. You will not be given night. Before you go, practice lying still in a bright room until sleep comes from custom rather than from darkness. Take a cloth you can lay over your eyes that does not smell of anywhere else, and accept that at the seams the white will still be there. Do not wait for golden hour, or blue hour, or a cue to be tired. The hour you stop is a decision you carry in with you. If you cannot keep it, you will walk the slots through other people’s sleep and become the only thing moving at the wrong size.

Eat before you understand the mats, and then eat almost nothing from them. The platforms are dense, dark, glossy, and exact. Food is taken at the rate the plants allow, and the plants allow a rate that keeps every platform full—not a surplus you can interpret as hospitality. A handful that seems small to you is a hole in a count. The hanging blades, the basin glaze, the horsetail screens, the little stiff leaves: few kinds, all occupied. Do not graze. If you must be fed by the town, wait until a bundle is placed where you can see that it was placed for a stranger, and take only what the placement says. Better to bring a compact food that will not crumb, will not scent the moss, and will not teach anyone a taste the bowl cannot replace when you leave. Seeds are a kind of promise the rim does not want. Do not plant them. Do not scatter husks for the birds in the peg-holes. Those birds already have their number.

Water is everywhere and almost none of it is a drink. It lives as a film. Drops on the undersides of eaves swell to a size and stop. Joints darken and go no further. Every threshold has a basin cut from the same pale stone, a still lens clear down to a living green glaze, and in it fish no longer than a hand, holding station. Touch the lens and it seals over your finger without a ripple worth the name. That is not an invitation. Those basins are rooms, clocks, and mouths. Do not dip a cup, do not wash, do not empty one to see it fill. Pipes exist, short and socketed, and most of the time they carry nothing; a tipped basin is rare and not yours to perform. Carry your own water in a single flask you can refill only if someone shows you a source that is not a lens. You will be thirstier than the air suggests, because the cold is dry everywhere the damp is not, and your lips will chap while your hems darken. A plain fat—unscented, little—on the mouth is worth more than another sweater. Blink more than you think you need to. The damp likes the rims of eyes, and the light does not rest them.

Speak as if the moss were a wall, because it is. A voice comes out small and stops. A body’s length away, people are entitled not to have heard you. The language you can actually use is placement. A bundle shifted from the left of a threshold to the right is a sentence the lane can read and the next lane cannot. You do not yet know the grammar. Until you do, move nothing. Do not “tidy.” Do not return an object to where it seems to belong. Do not set your pack on the left of a door because the right was occupied. Your ignorance will be fluent and wrong. If you must ask for help, use the smallest voice you have, once, and then set an object down with both hands and wait. Children learn placements before they learn to balance a jar. You are later than they are. Accept correction that arrives as a thing quietly moved back, and do not narrate your apology.

Walk the way the slots were agreed. Shoulders square to the gap, basket-width imagined even if you carry none, no brushing. The town turns; you will not see far. There is no square, no landmark except the rim of pale rock, no higher than a warehouse, continuous as a bowl. Navigation is memory of turns and of thresholds, not of views. Stoop for the stone cells. The rubble mouths were reduced for people who already know how to enter. Test a woven platform with your weight at the bracket side before you lean out; they are floors, but they are also gardens, and your boots are not knees and heels that have kept the same appointments for generations. Ladders are walls. Cisterns are steps. If you cannot tell what a surface is for, do not stand on it.

The animals are at their count. Moths with wings too heavy to flick cling to the blades. Fish hold their stations. Fig-sized birds occupy holes that may or may not have been made for them; the distinction was given up. Along the moss-runs, small bare-tailed animals follow paths so habitual the moss is two tones. Nothing is displayed for you. Do not feed, do not follow, do not block a run, do not lift a blade to see the moth better. You will not see a kill or a birth. Believing both happen beside the brightness, in the same quantity, is the correct natural history. A photograph, if you are the sort who needs one, is a theft of a stillness that does not know it is being kept. The light is bright enough. Your memory will not lack detail. It will lack change, which is what you were hunting.

What you can take back is attention, and even that you should spend slowly. Do not collect fiber, glaze, copper hooks, a finished leaf, a bead of water that has stopped at its lawful size. The bowl does not give anything back to the flat country, and does not take from it either. A souvenir is a small grave marker, and you will not find markers here because death, when it happens, returns fiber, copper, and a place in the slot to the count. You are not in that count. Do not leave a token “so they remember.” They will remember a wrong object longer than they will remember you, and the lane will have to speak around it until someone is willing to be the hand that resolves it.

The smell will not come find you. Cold leaf, wet stone, fiber curing in its own time, the clean animal smell of fur that is never wet enough to sour: each stays where it is made, as breath stays, as drops stay at their size. Do not expect a street of bakeries or a windward message. If you want to know a room, you have to be in it. Your own smell—soap, smoke, the oil of a road—will also stay where you stand. Wash it off before the stair, with nothing floral. Eat nothing onioned. Let the last fire you sit beside be a day behind you, so you do not walk in as a lamp that has already gone out.

Company is a kindness you should mostly refuse. Two strangers in a slot are a crowd the agreement did not include. If someone must come with you, agree in advance who is allowed to touch nothing, and that the answer is both of you. Do not hold hands across a lane. Do not talk your impressions into the moss while you are still inside them. The town can absorb a voice and still be altered by the need to have had one.

There is a danger, and it is not collapse. The packedness is not pressure. A crowded place usually implies a future collision; here the collision was resolved so long ago that the resolution is the architecture. You are the only unresolved thing on the stair. Prepare by practicing, in the days before, the arts the town assumes: stopping without a signal, sleeping in light, letting a silence be complete, keeping your hands in sight of your own intentions, wanting less than a mat can spare. Learn the names of the few plants you might be shown, and then do not say them often. Decide what you will do when nothing happens—not as a disappointment, but as the rule you will feel in your hands before you understand it.

When you have looked enough, go up the same polish you came down. Do not linger on the rim to compare the horsetails with the smaller cousins used as screens. The people already do that, and come back, because the horsetails are only the edge of what they have. Above, the turf will seem loud. Wind will feel like a mistake. Your breath will travel, and that will seem indecent for an hour. Let it. Do not try to keep the hush by refusing to speak in the country you return to. The bowl’s continuance is not a lesson you can wear home without turning it into a style. You were a missed step that climbed back out. Leave the counts full, the pegs occupied, the lenses unbroken, the bundles where the lanes can read them. If you did it properly, the stair will not be shinier for your foot, and no one will have had to become an event in order to survive you.
