Prepare as if you are going somewhere that has already finished, and as if your body has not.

The height will reach you before the view does. This is not a climb you can romance into thinness. The air is old, metallic, and insufficient, and it will not thicken because you stand still or because you are careful. Acclimate the way you would for a plateau that has no descending trail and no afternoon relief: staged ascent if your crossing allows it, otherwise oxygen you can wear for the whole visit, with a second supply you do not treat as optional. Move slowly on purpose. Speech costs more than it is worth. If your lips tingle, if the pavement begins to look kindly, if the crowd seems to make room, those are your signs, not the place’s. Sit if you must, but sit on something you are still holding. The ground here accepts what is set upon it, and acceptance is not the same as rest.

Carry every drink you will need, sealed, and do not bargain with the film. It is not dew. It will not become water if you wait, warm it, filter it, or name it. It wets the idea of a surface and then stops. Taste is a last experiment, not a first. Your mouth will want a ruling the chemistry will not give. The dark stains are former seas, not promises. Shade is not a cistern. A plant is not a well. Budget water for the confusion as well as the walking: people drink when a horizon refuses to arrive, and this horizon is skilled at that.

Dress for a cool that does not update. Not a blizzard, not a night that falls — a finished temperature, steady as the grade of the ground. Layers you can keep on. A hat and eye protection against honest light and a white pavement that gives nothing back but glare. Boots with soles that expect stone and will be wrong anyway: the surface has no yield, and then, under the same step, too many. You will hear yourself. That rasp is not a warning from the plain. It is the only sound with a source you can trust.

Do not go in order to meet them. They are alive in the completed sense, which is not the sense that answers. There is no blink to wait out, no breath to time, no path through the density that the density has agreed to. Bodies overlap in ways touch does not explain. Count them and you will keep a remainder. Look away and the remainder was already included. If you must pass among them, go as if through a thicket that is also a congregation: small steps, no grabbing, no “clearing a space,” no lifting to see what the far side weighs. A lifted body may weigh a handful or a district. Either result is the place being consistent. Protest will not come. That absence is not consent.

Your hands are the hazard you brought. Anything you set down becomes, at once and without ceremony, one of the old things: flaked, weathered, already part of the ring, the bowl, the hatch-marks, the unfinished course of stones. A dropped bottle is not a litter problem. It is a donation to a record that does not increase. Strap what you need to your body. Clip the oxygen where a faint means it stays with you. If you carry a tool, carry it until you leave, or accept that the plain has been expecting that gesture longer than you have been a traveler. Do not scratch a mark to prove you came. The pavement already has marks, grouped like tallies of things that no longer increase. Yours would join them and stop meaning you.

Navigation is the part ordinary habit will betray. You can see a wrinkle, a darker mass, a single upright figure, at a distance that ought to be a walk, and walking exchanges one identical square for the next while the mark stays the size it had when you named it. Regional, and already in the hand. Do not steer by “toward.” Do not expect the extra shadows to shorten and tell you the hour; some of them lie in directions the light is not using, and none of them are clocks. Bring a timepiece that does not ask the sky. Bring a line, a count of your own paces that you do not reconcile with the view, and a rule for turning back that is measured in your air and your water, not in landmarks. If you travel with someone, agree beforehand how you will know you are still the same number of people. This place does not conserve number. A headcount that comes out clean may be the one to distrust.

Record if you need to remember, but do not expect the recording to argue with what you saw. Stillness here is not a paused frame. Cameras like events. There may be none. Write, if you write, while the page is still in your hand. A notebook set on the salt-crust will be an old notebook, empty in the way the bowl is empty — made empty, and kept.

Before you cross, decide what you are willing not to finish. You will want a center, a summit, a speaker, a night, a wind, a drink of whatever beads on the gray-green wire of stem. None of those are stored here. The maker crouched by the bowl is not waiting for you to take the tool and complete the task. The plants will owe you no news if you watch them for the length of a life. Speech will not return because you found an ear. Water will not return because you were patient. Your arrival cannot begin the plateau again. That is the condition, not a failure of nerve.

Go fed, watered, oxygenated, unburdened of anything you would grieve to see become ancient. Go with an exit that does not depend on the horizon getting closer. Go ready to be the only wet sound in a sentence that has already ended — and ready, when the air in the tank and the water in the flask say so, to be the change that leaves. The plain will not watch you go. It has already included the afterward.
