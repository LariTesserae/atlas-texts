The chamber is not large. You could cross it in a slow swim if the water would let you, if anything in it would yield a path. It does not. From the first moment the eye adjusts, the place is full: pipes, fronds, housings, cables, the pale ribs of old bulkheads, all occupying the same water at once, so that distance exists only as a rumor between obstacles.

It sits below a ceiling of poured stone that has gone soft at the edges. Seams weep. The weep has been weeping so long that the ceiling’s underside is a low country of stalactite and verdigris, and from every drip-point something has learned to hang. Not rain. A continuous, almost invisible sift of warm water, thick as broth near the vents, clearer where it has traveled. The whole volume is liquid. There is no surface inside the chamber. Whatever sky this place once had is a story told by the stone above, and the stone has stopped telling it.

Warmth comes from below and from the machines. Not a blaze. A factory warmth, the kind that lives in jackets of lagging and in the slow opinion of boilers that have not been allowed to go cold. The water holds it. Skin would not shiver here. The cold of depth is a rumor from outside the boundary walls, which are close, mortared, and older than the industry they contain. You feel the room before you see its far side. Cathedral-small: a nave you could almost span with your arms if your arms were not already full of weed and conduit.

The industry did not leave. It settled. Winches the size of wardrobes turn on intervals too long to watch. A gear train the color of old tea advances one tooth and rests. Somewhere in the pack a piston completes a stroke that began before you arrived and will not finish while you are still thinking about it. The sound of all this is not sound so much as pressure changing its mind. A click arrives late, padded, already half-dissolved. Chains describe slow circles inside their own rust and do not clank. If there is an engine, it is the polite kind, shrouded, its exhaust a ribbon of warmer water that the fronds have organized themselves around.

Vegetation has taken the machinery as trellis and the trellis as law. It is lush in the way of a room that has been watered for a century without anyone cutting back. Ribbon-kelp in olive and bottle-green threads every gap a body might have used. Mats of finer weed felt the flanges of valves. A climbing growth, almost citrus where the dim light finds it, jackets the catwalks until the walk is a suggestion under leaf. Roots — or what pass for roots, pale and tubular — have entered the mortar and come out again through bolt-holes, so that the built and the grown are no longer a sequence. They are one fabric. Established. Weathered into balance. Nothing here is collapsing quickly. Nothing here is new.

Light is a ration. A few lamps still burn inside glass that has clouded to the color of onion skin, and their light does not travel. It stops on the first leaf, the first housing, and leaves the rest of the chamber in a brown-green dusk. Farther in, where the lamps have died, there is only the faint mineral glow of something growing on the iron — not bright enough to read by, bright enough to know that iron is still there. Shadows do not fall. They pool, and stay.

Animals are almost an absence, which in a place this packed is its own presence. No schools. No drift of small bodies. Now and then a single thing: a armored slug the length of a hand, pale as the inside of a pipe, crossing a flange with the patience of rust. Or a lantern-eyed creature no bigger than a thumb, holding still inside a coil of cable as if the cable were a burrow it invented. They do not call. They do not school. The water is too occupied for that kind of life, or the life has learned that motion is expensive and mostly unnecessary. What eats here eats slowly, or does not eat in any way a surface creature would recognize. The chemistry of the vents may be doing the feeding. The leaves look fed.

The strangeness is not a monster at the center. It is the grammar. Gauge-faces have been colonized until their needles are leaf-stems, yet the needles still point, and what they point at is not a human unit. A row of boilers wears epiphytes like ceremonial dress and continues, very quietly, to be boilers. In one corner the water is fractionally wrong — heavier, or sweeter, or slow to accept a hand — and the plants there grow in tighter spirals, as if listening to a different instruction. The boundary walls are close enough to touch in three directions and, in the fourth, give way not to open sea but to a throat of pipe large enough to admit a person, rifled inside with old flow-marks, going somewhere the chamber does not explain. Warm water moves through that throat the way breath moves through a sleeping house: out, then back, on a cycle longer than attention.

Nothing hurries. Silt, where silt can find a ledge, lies in laminations fine as pages. A bubble, if one rises, rises as if apologizing, and is usually caught by a leaf and made into a small silver room until it decides, much later, to leave. The drip from the ceiling arrives, is absorbed, is not dramatic. Erosion here is not an attack. It is the long handwriting of a place that has already become what it is: a drowned works, a grove of iron, a town the size of one building, fully occupied, warm, dim, and quiet enough that the next click of the gear train, when it comes, feels like speech.
