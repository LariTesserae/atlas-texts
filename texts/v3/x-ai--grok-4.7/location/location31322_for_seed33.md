The ground begins as a color before it becomes a surface. A pale mineral grey, the grey of cooled ash that never was ash, laid out farther than the eye trusts. There is no horizon in the ordinary sense. The plateau simply continues until the dimness eats the distance, and the dimness is not weather. The sky is a low, grainy ceiling the color of old tin, and it does not move. Nothing falls from it. Nothing has fallen from it in so long that the dust on the stone is not sediment but the stone’s own breath, ground fine and left where it settled.

It is high. The body knows this before the eye does. The air is thin enough that sound has nowhere to live; a foot set down arrives as pressure in the ankle and almost nothing in the ear. Breath comes short and cold and tastes of metal that has never been smelted. Temperature sits just above the point where moisture would matter, and there is no moisture. The tongue dries. The lips do not crack so much as forget they were soft. Whatever weather once worked this place finished its argument ages ago and left the verdict standing: a dry cold, even, patient, with no wind you can hear and a pressure against the face that might be wind or might be the altitude insisting on itself.

Across this openness the city is distributed rather than gathered. You understand it as a city only after you have walked long enough to see that the distances repeat. A low wall of fitted plates, no mortar, each plate a hand-span thick and pale as bone, runs for a stretch and then stops as if the sentence ended. Far off, another wall takes up a parallel thought. Between them the ground is empty enough that a person could lie down and not be found by anything but the light, which is poor. Towers stand singly. They are not tall in the way of ambition. They are tall in the way of a remaining tooth. Some lean a degree that erosion should have finished, and has not. Their surfaces are scoured into a softness that still holds edges where edges were wanted: sills, sockets, the ghost of a stair that climbs three turns and opens onto air that was always air.

No tool marks. That is the first wrongness that stays. The joints are too exact for stacking by hand and too irregular for any machine you can picture. Plates meet plates along seams that wander like cracks and then, if you look back, have straightened. A lintel the length of a room rests on nothing you can credit, and when you pass under it the shade is colder by a measure that shade should not manage. There are streets in the sense that the ground has been persuaded into corridors: shallow channels, packed mineral darker than the plateau, running between isolated structures with the confidence of a plan. They do not connect into a map that survives being walked. A channel that seemed to lead toward a cluster of low domes, after a long quiet approach, arrives at the same wall you left, or at a wall that remembers being that wall. You are not lost. The arrangement is doing something slower than travel.

What the city is built from is the plateau itself, lifted and set. You can see this where a foundation has been undercut by the old erosion: the stone of the wall continues down into the stone of the ground without a change of grain, as if the earth had been taught to stand up. In places the standing has gone on too long and the wall has forgotten the ground and hangs with a finger’s gap beneath it, a darkness there that is not shadow. Dust does not collect in the gap. Dust avoids it. The erosion is finished and unfinished at once. Every edge is rounded to the degree a thousand years of grit would round it, and no grit moves. The forms have reached a balance with a force that is no longer present, or that is present in a key the ear cannot hear. Corners hold. Floors remain level in rooms whose outer walls are half gone, the missing halves not fallen but absent, the cut edges smooth as if the stone had been poured away.

Inside, the rooms are larger than their outsides permit, and then, on a second look, they are not. A chamber entered through a doorway a shoulder wide opens into a square of dimness broad enough for a gathering that never gathered. The floor is a single plate, unbroken, faintly warm under the cool of the air, as if some process still spends itself downward. There are niches at regular heights. They hold nothing. Some of them hold the shape of having held something, a darker cleanliness. No tools, no vessels, no fiber, no ash of a fire. The civilization, if the word fits, did not use devices that survive, or did not use devices. The organization is the only artifact: alignments, intervals, the way three towers and a sunken court describe a triangle whose fourth point is somewhere under your feet and also somewhere you have not reached. You can feel the interval when you stop. The body wants to stand where the pattern is least strained. People who lived here, if people is the right category, would have known where to put their weight.

Light does not brighten. It changes its mind. For a stretch of walking the plateau is a flat pewter, detail available at the range of a thrown stone and no farther. Then a structure you had taken for distant proves near, its courses of plates readable, a seam brightening along one edge as if a thinner light had found it from underneath. There is no sun to blame. The ceiling of the sky stays the same tin color, and yet the towers throw shadows that do not agree with one another. One shadow lies east. The next, a bowshot off, lies in a direction the plateau does not have a name for. Stand between them and your own shadow thins, then splits into two faint copies that do not share your posture. One of them is upright. One of them is the shape of someone already walking away. Neither copy makes a sound when it moves, and both move when you hold still.

The activity of the place is entirely of this kind. Nothing runs. Nothing calls. The quiet is not emptiness of event but a failure of the air to carry event. While you watch, a wall completes a shift it must have been mid-way through for longer than a life: one plate slides a finger’s width along its neighbor, silent, and the seam it leaves is already weathered, already old, already the color of the rest. A channel in the ground deepens by the thickness of a nail between one glance and the next. Far out, a tower that was leaning is upright, and the dust at its foot has not been disturbed because the dust is not required to notice. Processes continue. They have the pace of weather and the privacy of thought. A dome you passed on the left is, when the path has curved without curving, on the left again, nearer, its surface drinking the dimness until it is barely a suggestion of curve. The city is maintaining itself. Or the plateau is maintaining the idea of a city, and the maintenance does not need witnesses, noise, or water.

There is almost no life, and what there is does not announce itself as life. No green. No stain of lichen, no mat of anything that drinks and grows. In the lee of certain walls, where the thin cold pools, a crust of white mineral feathers out in branching forms that mimic roots and are only salt, or the cousin of salt this chemistry allows. Touch one and it powders. It does not regrow while you watch, and yet similar crusts appear in rooms you are certain were bare. They are not plants. They are the dry world’s way of repeating a gesture it learned before there was anything to learn from. Lower, in the sunken courts, the floors are patterned with a dark reticulation, fine as cracked glaze. If you kneel, the cracks are not cracks. They are seams between tiles the size of seeds, each tile fitted, each one shifting, over the length of an afternoon, by a distance too small to see and large enough that the pattern, remembered, does not match the pattern under your hand. Something organizes at that scale. It does not breathe. It does not need to.

Fauna, when it occurs, occurs as exception. Twice in a long crossing you may see a motion that is not the city’s own. A low shape the color of the plates detaches from a foundation and relocates, never more than a body’s length, then becomes foundation again. It has limbs or it has the suggestion that limbs could be invented for it. It makes no track. It does not drink, because there is nothing to drink; the salt-crusts and the dry channels and the sealed smoothness of the plates offer no seep, no cistern, no rime of ice. The thing, if it is an animal, lives on interval: it occupies the places where the pattern is slack, and when the pattern tightens it is elsewhere, or it is stone. You will not get a count of them. Counting requires objects that remain objects between the numbers. Here a presence can be a presence and then be the corner you were already looking at.

Sound stays at the edge of occurring. The loudest thing is the blood in your ears, and after a while even that seems loaned. Now and then a plate speaks: a tick, single, as of cooling, though nothing is cooling faster than the rest. The tick does not echo. Echo would require a world that returns what it is given, and this one spends what it is given on staying arranged. If you speak, the words leave the mouth and thin out before they reach the nearest wall. The wall does not answer. Somewhere, rarely, a soft gravitational sound, not quite a sigh, happens in a room you are not in. You learn not to turn toward it. Turning toward it is how the distances miscount.

The weirdness is not a spectacle laid over a normal high desert. It is the floor the rest stands on. Scale fails politely. A cluster of structures that reads as a neighborhood, roofs at walking height, reveals itself, as the light reconsiders, to be a single course of masonry at your feet, the “roofs” the tops of plates, the streets the joints, and you have been a giant for a hundred paces without noticing the change in your size, or the world changed and you did not. Both descriptions remain available. Neither wins. Farther out, where the plateau should end, it does not end; it stands up. The vertical is another reach of the same city, towers continuing into a wall of towers, the wall near enough to read and far enough that walking toward it does not change its size. Elevation here is not a number of feet above a sea. There is no sea and no memory of one, no salt pan that was a shore, only salt that crystallized because crystallization is a habit of this mineral. Elevation is a direction the stone has taken seriously. Up and away have overlapping duties. You can climb a stair that levels out into a road, and the road is still the stair, and your knees believe both.

At the center of the arrangement, if center is allowed, there is a court wider than the others and more carefully empty. The plates underfoot are darker, almost blue in the tin light, and they are warm. Not hot. Warm the way a machine is warm when it has been thinking for a long time and has no moving parts left to show for it. No device sits in the court. No altar, no engine, no well. The emptiness is built. Four walls, none of them complete, frame a volume of air that behaves differently from the air outside. Step in and the shortness of breath eases, not because there is more air but because the body is briefly convinced it does not need as much. Sound, for the length of that conviction, almost works. You hear your own clothing. You hear, once, the tick of a plate inside the wall, and the tick has a small tail of resonance, the first echo of the day, and it frightens you more than the silence did, because it means the court is a place where the world’s rules are nearer the surface and therefore easier to break.

Above the court the sky is the same. It is also, if you lie on the warm plates and look without insisting, a continuation of the floor: the same joints, the same coursing, rendered in dimness so deep it passes for atmosphere. The city is not under the sky. The city is a fold in something that uses sky as one of its rooms. This does not announce itself with thunder. It is already the case. It has been the case long enough for every edge to weather into agreement with it. The breakdown is not a catastrophe in progress. It is the settled climate. Things proceed. Walls ease along their seams. The seed-tiles reorganize their night-map. A pale shape detaches from a foundation, crosses a slack interval, and becomes stone where stone was needed. Your split shadow walks a direction you will not take, soundless, and does not return, and the place is no less itself for the loss.

No one comes with water. No green thing argues with the mineral. No lamp, no wheel, no wire. The infrastructure is the persuasion of stone into intervals, and the intervals are occupied by process instead of by crowds. From any tower that will bear weight, the view is the same lesson at a wider reach: walls, courts, single towers, dark channels, salt-white crusts in the cold lees, all of it spaced with a generosity that would read as ruin if anything had fallen. Nothing has fallen. The city is in its state. It extends past the dimness and, past the dimness, continues the work of being arranged. The work is quiet. The work does not pause because you are looking. When you climb down, the stair may be a road, and the road may have moved its loyalty by a degree, and the plateau will still be there, cool, dry, high, and patiently untrue.
