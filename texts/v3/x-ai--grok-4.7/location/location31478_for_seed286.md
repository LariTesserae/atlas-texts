The water does not arrive and does not leave. It is simply the condition of the height.

You are high — high enough that the lower world, if it still exists, is a rumor under the roots — and you are under. The two facts do not argue. The sea is held up here, not pooled in a basin but kept, the way a lung keeps breath, inside a body that has grown to the size of weather. There is no shore. There is no surface in the ordinary sense, only a brightness overhead that does not ripple, a lid of fused leaf the breadth of provinces, so thin in places that daylight comes through as daylight: cool, even, without hour. Nothing in the light suggests that it has ever been otherwise, or that it intends to become night.

Between that lid and the dark under-root, the medium is water, cool to the bone, clear enough to insult distance. You can see farther than you can swim in a day, and what you see is mostly nothing. The place is sparse the way a cathedral is sparse: immense volume, few presences, each presence doing the work of a wall. The trunks stand at intervals that would be called landscape if there were land. One is a gray-green column, smooth where the current has been polishing it since before polishing had a beginning, flanged lower down into buttresses that branch, rejoin, and branch again until the idea of a single plant gives up. The next stands where a horizon would be, if horizons were allowed to mean edge rather than merely far. Between them the water is empty in a way that still feels occupied — occupied by pressure, by a permanent drift, by sound.

The sound does not start. It has no attack and no decay. It is the voice of transport. Inside the trunks, volumes of water and dissolved mineral move in conduits wider than roads, and the conduits are not carved; they are the plant, grown to the duty and worn to the fit. At every scale you can hear the same work: a low industrial murmur in the bones of the larger columns, a brighter hiss where a lesser vessel necks down and the flow cavitates, a crackle, dry and electrical, along the mineral skins where charge separates and returns. Nothing modulates it. There is no gust, no school turning as one, no valve that opens and then rests. The factories do not shift shift. They have no shifts. Production here is a property of the material, as color is.

What they produce is not for anyone. Metals come out of solution and plate the older wood in thin, uneven mirrors — not tools, not walls, only the exhaust of a metabolism that has been separating ore from water for so long that the separation and the wearing-away have signed the same treaty. A trunk shines along one flank and is dull along the other, and both flanks have shone and dulled in that exact proportion for as long as the current has had its direction, which is always. Lower, pale threads of salt extrude from seams and are taken again by the flow, so that the water a little way off is faintly milky and the water against the trunk is clear. Nothing accumulates. A filament that would, in another world, have become a reef, a bar, a beach, is here a standing quantity: made and unmade at one rate, visible, useless, permanent.

The current itself is the climate. This high sea has weather, and the weather is large, and the weather does not move. There are regions. Cross from one trunk toward the next and you pass through a standing front where the water is a degree colder and full of microbubbles that never rise far enough to matter; the roar goes up in pitch and stays there. Beyond it, a lens of stillness — not calm after storm, but a scheduled stillness, laminar, assigned to that volume forever — in which your own motion is the only disorder, and even that the place seems prepared to erase. Farther, an upwelling: a column of water rising inside the greater column of the sea, cold from the root-dark, bright with mineral, and around it a sleeve of descending water, the return leg of an engine that has never been switched off because it was never switched on. Fish hold in the shear between the two as if the shear were a reef. They do not travel. There is nowhere the conditions differ enough to repay the swim.

Life is abundant and hard to find, which is not a contradiction at this scale. Every trunk carries its nation. On the buttresses, gardens of finer plants — not separate species so much as the same will expressed at a size that can afford to be soft — filter the permanent drift with combs and nets of living tissue, and among the combs the animals thicken: crusts that are not crusts, wheeled colonies, pale blades that clip the flow and never have to hunt because the hunt is the current and the current does not fail. Schools hang in fixed lattices, each fish a replacement for a fish that occupied that coordinate, the lattice older than any body in it. In the open water, almost none of this shows. You can travel a long time through the loud clear nothing and believe the world empty, and then a trunk arrives out of the distance like a coast, and the coast is inhabited to the density of a city, and the city makes no sound of its own above the sound that was already there.

Underfoot — if the word can be used — the architecture continues down until architecture becomes grip. The lowest structures are roots only by courtesy. They are cables, spliced and respliced by growth that matches every fray, anchoring the held sea to whatever stone still exists inside the plant-mass, or perhaps only to more plant, deeper, older, mineralized past resemblance. You do not reach a bottom so much as a decision the world has already made: this far, and not farther, because farther would be down out of the water into air, and air is not part of the agreement. Some who have gone low enough tell of a thinning, a brightness from below that is not sky, a place where the lowest leaves open onto a drop and the drop is the rest of the planet, cloud, distance, a lower weather that this place does not share and does not watch. The stories do not change either. They cannot be checked against a season.

Nothing here is built. There is no joint that implies a maker, no repeated module that implies a plan executed by hands. And yet the work is industrial in the plain sense: scaled, continuous, electrified by its own separations, intolerant of waste, indifferent to audience. The trunks are mills. The clearings of empty water are the yards between mills. The roar is the roar of a works that covers a continent and has completed its construction phase so long ago that construction and ruin are the same smooth surface.

You can stay. The cold is honest and not lethal if you keep to the mid-depths, where the light is ordinary and the drift is only a lean. What you cannot do is wait for something. The place has already finished becoming itself, and the finishing did not produce an ending. No tide consults a moon. No leaf turns to track a sun. The lid holds its one brightness. The pumps hold their one note. A fish vacates a coordinate and another fish is that coordinate. If you leave and return, you will not know it by any difference in the water — only by the difference in you, which the current is already working, calmly, to close.
