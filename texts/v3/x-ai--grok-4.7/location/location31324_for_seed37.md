The place fits inside a breath held too long. You do not find it by walking; you arrive by noticing that the ground under one foot has become thinner than the ground under the other, and that the thinner place is willing to open. The opening is not a cave mouth. It is a lapse: a palm-sized failure in the surface of ordinary stone, cool at the rim, damp as the inside of a closed mouth. Step through it and the lapse widens only as far as you need, then seals with the soft sound of two wet pages coming together.

Below, there is no dark.

Light comes from the rock itself, or from what the rock has not yet decided to be. Walls stand in a pale, even brightness, the color of sun on the underside of a leaf, except there is no sun and no leaf overhead. The light does not fall. It occupies. Shadows exist only where an animal crosses, and even then they are brief, hesitant things, as if the room has not finished learning how to cast them. The chamber is small enough that you could cross it in twenty careful steps, large enough that those steps never quite repeat. Distance here is still being negotiated.

The geology is unfinished. Seams of mineral run partway across a wall and stop, mid-thought, their edges rounded not by water but by hesitation. Crystals the length of a finger push out of damp clay and then pause, transparent, still soft if you were cruel enough to press them. A shelf of stone tries to become a floor and loses the argument halfway, sagging back into a cool paste that holds the print of whatever last crossed it and then, slowly, forgets. Nothing here is eroded in the old sense. Erosion would require a history. This is earlier than history: raw material practicing shapes, discarding most of them.

Water does not flow so much as occur. A bead gathers on a ceiling that is also, depending on the angle, a low sky. It hangs. It does not fall until an animal passes beneath it, and then it falls into fur, or into the hollow of a back, or sometimes into nothing at all and is gone without a sound. Along one wall a thin film of moisture travels sideways, against every habit of water, and disappears into a crack that is not always there. In the lowest cup of the chamber a pool no wider than a bowl keeps a skin of brightness on it. Things move under that skin. They are not fish. They are the beginnings of the idea of fish, silver threads that have not yet committed to eyes.

Plants are few, and they do not behave like a covering. A single pale stalk rises from a crack, jointed, cool to the thought of touch, bearing no leaf until it decides, late, to unroll one translucent blade and then stop. Moss exists as isolated coins, each the size of a thumbnail, glowing the same even light as the walls, separated by long reaches of bare, damp mineral. Between them the air is empty enough to feel. You can see across the whole room. Nothing crowds. The sparseness is not poverty. It is the spacing of a sentence still being written, words set far apart so each one can be heard.

And yet the room is full of animals.

They do not match the quiet, and the quiet does not match them, and neither yields. A scatter of small things like moths, except their wings are the same unfinished crystal as the walls, drift without whir. They land on nothing. They pass through one another and come out rearranged: two where there was one, or one with the memory of having been two. Along the paste-floor, low bodies the color of wet clay move in starts, six-legged, then four, then a number that does not settle. They leave tracks that fill in before you finish looking. Higher, where a ledge has almost agreed to exist, a cluster of pale, long-limbed animals sits in the brightness and watches the not-falling water. Their eyes are large. Some of them have eyes on the inside of their lids, so that blinking does not interrupt seeing; it only changes the room.

None of them make a sound you can trust. Claws touch stone and the contact arrives late, or not at all. A wing should tick. It doesn't. What you hear, when you hear anything, is a soft pressure, like the memory of rain on a surface that has never been rained on. Breathing, if they breathe, is slower than the light. The abundant life of the place conducts itself as if sound were a material that has not yet been delivered.

At three points in the chamber there are structures that are not quite natural and not quite made. A low ring of fitted pebbles, each pebble still soft at the core. A vertical stack of thin mineral plates, leaning, not fallen, arranged with a care that no hand you know would claim. In the far curve of the wall, a recess the size of a cupped palm holds a coil of something like shed skin, except the coil is brighter than the animal that might have left it, and it tightens, very slowly, as if still being woven. No tool marks. No devices. If anything built these, it built them the way a shell builds itself: by continuing. The marks feel recent and ancient in the same glance, because time, like distance, is one of the things this room has not finished.

The weirdness is not an event laid over the place. It is the place's method.

Stand still and the chamber's measurements drift. The pool is nearer than it was. The single stalk has a second blade, or it had one a moment ago and the moment has been withdrawn. An animal on the ledge turns its head and, for the turn's duration, possesses a head at both ends of the turn, both of them watching you with the mild attention of something that does not yet distinguish visitors from weather. The light brightens without a source when a crystal decides to lengthen. It does not dim when you blink. Your own shadow, when you finally cast one, falls upward for a heartbeat and then remembers and lies down, embarrassed, cooler than the floor.

Processes run anyway. The sideways film of water completes another circuit and starts again, thinner. A clay-bodied crawler meets another and they pass through, exchanging a leg, continuing, unbothered. The unfinished crystals advance by the width of a hair and stop, as if listening for permission that will not come from outside. Condensation beads, hangs, is taken into fur, returned later as a darker patch that the brightness drinks. Predator and prey, if those words apply, conduct their exchange without chase-sounds: a pale limb extends, a smaller brightness winks out of the air, and the room's population does not decrease so much as reorganize. Abundance here is not a count. It is a persistence.

The rules, such as they are, can be felt more than listed.

What is soft may harden if observed too directly, so the kindest looking is sidelong. What is hard may still be persuaded. Water will not wet you unless you expect it to; expectation is a kind of weather. Animals do not flee. They adjust their number. Sounds you bring with you — a shifted foot, a breath — leave the room more slowly than they entered, and sometimes return later, smaller, wearing the room's quiet like a coat. You cannot mark the walls. Any scratch fills with the same even light and becomes another almost-crystal, another attempt. The exit remains where your footfalls disagree with each other. It does not close out of malice. It closes because an opening is a finished thing, and this place prefers not to finish.

Stay long enough and the brightness starts to include you. Not as a threat. As a draft. The cool damp finds the hollows of your wrists. A moth-crystal drifts through the space where your hand is and comes out carrying a faint copy of your knuckle, which it does not know how to use, and sets down gently on the moss-coin, where it glows, and waits to see if it will become bone, or mineral, or simply another small unfinished brightness among the many.

Above, if above still means anything, the ordinary ground has already forgotten the lapse. Rain may be starting there, or not. It will not reach this room except as a rumor of moisture moving sideways along a wall that is still deciding whether to be a wall. Down here the light keeps its even counsel. The animals continue their soundless abundance. The geology practices, and pauses, and practices again. Nothing is ruined, because nothing has fully begun. The pocket holds. The world, elsewhere, can be as loud as it likes.
