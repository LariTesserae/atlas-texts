The plain does not rise to meet you. It is already under everything, a floor poured to the curve of a world and then left there, so low that the water in it has nowhere to run except sideways, and sideways never ends. From what would be a height anywhere else—an elbow of gantry, the lip of a tank—you see weather before you see ground. A single cloud-body occupies the sky, not traveling, not building, not breaking. It is the size of the construct beneath it. Warmth lifts off the decks and goes up into that cloud and comes back down as the same fine rain, and the rain has been falling in the same places, at the same rate, for as long as the joints have been sharp.

The joints are sharp. Nothing here has been allowed to round. Edges of plate meet at the angle they were cut. Channels keep the section they were extruded in. Where a lesser place would have a stain of age, this one has a bead of water standing exactly on the arris, fat, warm, not yet fallen, as if falling itself had been specified and then held at the last step. You can walk a long time before you accept that the freshness is not newness. Wear happens. It is answered at the same rate it occurs, somewhere under the decks, in a mill that does not speed up and does not slow. The world stays at the hour of its completion. Shadows, where the cloud thins enough to cast them, do not swing. They are furniture.

There is no soil and no green. The substrate is the build: sintered mineral the color of wet slate, gasket-black polymer in every seam, glass-smooth runs where a fluid is meant to stay laminar. What you first take for moss on the horizontal faces is not moss. It is a pavement of small lives, shelled, packed until they touch, each one seated in a shallow the machining left for a washer or a weld-spatter dam. They do not graze. There is nothing to graze. They take what the water carries—mineral film, warmth, a sweetness of dissolved metal that the mills refresh—and they do not move off their seats. Turn one over, if you can get a nail under it, and the plate beneath is bright, unused, younger than the animal, which is impossible and ordinary here. The animal grew to the tolerance. The tolerance did not erode to the animal.

Upright surfaces wear a different hide. Feathery mouths, finger-long, hang in sheets so dense the metal behind them is a rumor. They do not wave. The air is wet but does not gust, and the water in the open channels moves like a thought you have already finished: continuous, warm, without a ripple that would count as an event. In the broader runs the bodies are in the water itself, layered, near enough to touch their neighbors, filter-curtains and slow plates and things with no limbs you would name, packed until the channel is more life than flow and still the flow is perfect, because the life is the flow's designed resistance. Nothing hunts in a way you can see. Nothing flees. Appetite has been averaged out until it is a property of the material, like conductivity.

The machines are not elsewhere. They are the relief. What breaks the flat is not a hill but a housing: pump-heads the breadth of headlands, ranked so their curves repeat to the horizon and become, at distance, a kind of low weather of their own. Impellers turn inside them. You know this only because a diagram of the world requires it. There is no slap, no cavitation, no laboring. Each wheel is matched to the water it was built to meet, and the water has not changed its mind. Conveyors exist and do not deliver. Their belts shine with the same constant wet and carry, if they carry, a film that is scraped and returned and scraped, a closed errand. Factories stand in blocks, ducts entering and leaving, and their product is the set-point: temperature held, salts held, the cloud fed, the animals kept at the density where they touch and do not crush. Stack lights, where they exist, show one color and have always shown it. Normal daylight comes through the cloud, milky, sufficient, without a sun you can watch travel. You can read a gauge. You do not need to. The needle was set when the edge was cut.

Sound is what fails to accumulate. Humidity and soft bodies take it. Your footfall is the loudest event, and it dies before the next step, swallowed by shell and water-film and the standing air. Under that, if you stop, there is a presence rather than a noise: the combined minute work of uncountable mouths, below the level where the ear keeps a record. Now and then a single click—shell on shell, or a relay that has always closed at this interval—arrives complete, not as a change but as a fact you have walked into. Wait, and it does not repeat on any schedule your body can hold. It was not for you. The quiet is not emptiness. It is a volume already full, with no leftover room for echo.

The strangeness stays inside the recognizable. These are animals you could almost file: filter-feeders, crusts, slow armored plates, a phylum that would not shock a shore. What will not file is the fit, and the scale, and the refusal of time. A continental machine, warm as a laundry room, wet as the hour after rain, and the rain does not end and the hour does not advance. No roots pry the plates. No season opens a crack the maintenance has not already measured. Fauna covers every surface you might have called ground, wall, or channel invert, overwhelming in the simple sense that you cannot choose a place to put a hand that is not already occupied, and none of the occupants object, startle, or make way. You are not in their cycle. You are a brief occlusion of the fixed light. When you move on, the mist closes over the warmer patch your body left, and the animals that were shaded resume the exact posture of animals that were never shaded.

Walk far enough and the repetition starts to feel like a law rather than a style. The same pump-curve. The same depth of water over the walking deck—ankle, never more, a specification. The same density of shells per span, as if counted. Low as it is, the plain has weather because it is large enough to breathe as one thing: one cloud, one return of rain, one vast exhalation of warmth that does not wander off the edge, because the edge, if it exists, is only another joint, and beyond it the same floor continues, sharp, wet, inhabited, and still. Nothing here is wild. Nothing here is growing toward a future. The equilibrium is the finished object. It does not explain itself, and it does not need to. It is already, everywhere you look, exactly as made.
