The chamber is not large. From the lip of the drop to the far wall is a walk you could finish before your breath settled, and the ceiling hangs low enough that heat pools against it and never leaves. It is below the skin of whatever world holds it—cut, or torn, or opened by something that did not bother to finish the work. The edges are still sharp. Fracture planes catch the dim light in clean planes, unrounded, as if the rock had split an hour ago and then decided that hour was enough. Nothing here has been given time to soften. Nothing here seems to be receiving time at all.

There is no water. The air is dry in a way that feels deliberate, a dryness with a temperature: warm, close, slightly sweet where the machines vent, mineral where they do not. Your mouth goes chalky. Dust does not drift. It stands in the air in fixed veils, layers you can step through, each one holding its place as if the chamber had been poured full of stalled weather and then sealed. The floor is a young pavement of plates—slag-colored, glassy along the breaks, still showing the grain of whatever cooled too fast. Salt-white rimes trace the joints, but they are not damp. They are the mineral breath of the place, precipitated and left.

Sound is the first true resident. It does not rise and fall. A low industrial chord occupies the volume completely: the even knock of a small piston somewhere under the left wall, the hiss of a blower that has no intake you can find, a higher ringing from a housing half-buried in the plates, as if a bearing were singing one note and had always sung it. The noises do not sync and do not fight. They have found a lock. Stand anywhere and the mix is the same. There is no echo that decays. The chamber returns what it is given at the same amplitude, forever.

Scattered through this are the works. Not a factory—too few, too incomplete—but industrial all the same. A squat engine block sits proud of the floor near the center, casing split along a weld that never failed further, flywheel turning at a speed you cannot quite believe is constant until you watch it long enough to distrust your own blinking. Cables leave it and vanish into sharp fissures without slack. Against the right-hand wall a press or a kiln—hard to say which—holds a single unfinished shape between its jaws, neither crushed nor released. A ladder of stamped metal climbs three rungs into the ceiling and stops, bolts bright, threads unstripped. Faint marks elsewhere: a chalked circle on a plate, a row of punched holes in a thin sheet leaned and forgotten, the ghost of a painted number whose paint has not peeled because peeling would be a change. The machines are not ruins. They are mid-task, and the task has become a property of the room.

Life is packed into what the machines do not occupy. The fauna is abundant to the point of interference. Things the size of hands and the size of seeds occupy every ledge, every cable shadow, every warm flank of housing. They do not scatter when you move. Most do not move in any way that counts as travel. A colony of jointed, translucent bodies clings to the piston housing in a fixed mosaic; each one flexes on the knock, riding the vibration the way a sleeper rides breath, and returns to the same angle. Between the plates, in the dust-veils, smaller motes beat wings or membranes at a rate that matches the ringing bearing, so the air itself seems grained with intention. Along the sharp new fractures, flat animals—more coin than creature—have seated themselves edge-to-edge, overlapping like scales, their mouths or pores open toward the heat. None of them drink. None of them seem to hunt. They persist by contact: heat, vibration, the mineral film, each other. Where two colonies meet they do not merge and do not yield. The boundary is as sharp as the rock’s.

Vegetation, if that is the word, is sparse and almost embarrassed by the crowding. A few crusts the color of dried blood hold to the upper fractures, fibrous, mineral more than green, putting out no new growth you can see because new growth would require a later moment. One taller thing—knee-high, branched like a burnt hand—stands beside the stopped ladder. Its tips are glassy. Sap, if it has sap, has gone to stone at the surface. It does not sway. There is no draft that changes.

Light comes from nowhere you would call a source. The dimness is warm-toned, as if the machines and the bodies were all faintly incandescent and the rock were only agreeing. Shadows do not lengthen. They are assigned. Under the press, a darkness the shape of the unfinished piece; under each flat animal, a thinner dark the width of its body. You can read the punched holes. You cannot read far.

What makes the place strange is not a monster in the corner. It is the completeness of the hold. Young stone, still sharp, should be the start of a history—collapse, cooling, colonization, the rounding of edges, the failure of bearings, the thirst that comes after the mineral film is licked away. None of that sequel is available. The flywheel does not slow. The dust-veils do not settle. The animals do not breed in any way that increases their number, and they do not die in any way that decreases it. Count them and the count is a fact, like the distance to the far wall. The loudness is not an event. It is climate. Warmth does not bleed upward into a night. There is no night adjacent to this room. The chamber is an equilibrium that happens to contain engines and a great many living weights, all of them balanced so precisely that motion continues without consequence.

If you stay, you begin to hear the chord as a single instrument. The piston, the blower, the bearing, the wing-rate, the small dry click some of the coin-animals make when their plates touch—the click also does not vary. Your own breathing is the only gradient, and the room does not answer it. Set a hand on the engine casing and the heat is exact, the same at the weld as at the far side. Set a hand near a colony and the bodies adjust by a fraction to keep their mosaic, then stop, as if correction were allowed only in service of sameness.

At the lip where you came down, the fracture that opened the chamber is a clean black angle, unweathered. Beyond it the world, if there is one, does not leak air or sound or moisture inward. The boundary holds. Inside, the small dense place goes on being exactly what it became when it became: a warm, dry, dim cavity under the surface, newly broken and already finished, loud with work that produces nothing but its own continuation, crowded with lives that do not need water because they do not need a future—only the next identical instant, which is already here, and which does not end.
