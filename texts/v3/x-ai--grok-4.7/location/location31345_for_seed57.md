The opening is not an opening. It is a seam in low ground, younger than the weather that found it, a split no wider than a wrist where the mineral still shows its break-face: glassy, unrounded, edges that have not yet learned to be stones. Cool air stops at the lip. Below the lip there is no air.

What fills the split is water, but not as a pool fills a basin. The water is the room. It does not have a surface you can trust; any skin you think you see is only a pause in the dark, a place where two darknesses failed to agree, and if you lower a hand through it the pause closes over the wrist without a ripple you can hear. Sound here is mostly the absence of arrival. Pressure leans. A faint tick, somewhere in the mass, might be a stem lengthening, or a grain of silt giving up and becoming part of a leaf. Then quiet again, the long quiet of a process that does not need witnesses.

The pocket should be small. From the seam it reads as a closet drowned on its first day, a single feature, embedded, the kind of hollow a root makes when it rots and the ground has not yet decided to forget the shape. Step—if stepping is the word, when there is no floor that stays a floor—and the smallness does not release you. It thickens. Distances refuse to match. An arm's reach ends in a wall of fronds that, followed by the fingers, continues past the place where the arm should have ended, into a further dark that is still the same dark, still the same room, only now the room is inside a leaf that is inside the water that is inside the split you have not left. The scale does not expand. It nests. You are never given a vista. You are given occupancy.

Everything is already occupied.

Vegetation does not grow here so much as coincide. Blades, filaments, pads, and the soft uncut flags of new growth pack the volume until there is no gap that is merely gap. Water occupies the same places the plants occupy, not by flowing between them but by being the medium their tissues have not finished distinguishing themselves from. A leaf-face presses to another leaf-face with a film between that is thinner than a decision. Stems cross stems and do not displace; they share the crossing, a knot of green-black that is two individuals and also a single thickened presence, boundaries smeared where the world's bookkeeping has slipped. Lushness, at this size, is not abundance spread across a marsh. It is saturation. You cannot turn without entering something that was already turning into the space you meant to use.

The plants are cool to the touch, and the touch comes back wrong. A pad that should bruise stays whole, and the bruise appears, minutes or what-feel-like-minutes later, on a different pad, identical, deeper in, as if damage were a rumor the pocket passes along rather than an event located in a body. New growth is sharp. Tips have not blunted. The mineral around them is the same: fracture-planes still bright, corners that catch what little there is to catch and give it back as a hard glint, gone as soon as the angle fails. This place has not been worn. It emerged—split, flooded, colonized—inside a duration too short for smoothness and too long for emptiness. Erosion has barely begun its patient subtracting. What you meet are beginnings that have already crowded.

Light does not enter. The darkness is not an absence waiting to be lifted; it is a material with weight and grain, cooler in the folds, slightly less cool where a leaf has been growing toward a direction that is no longer up. Up has become unreliable. The densest tissue pulls. You orient by pressure and by the way fronds comb one way, then, without a joint or a turn you can name, comb another, as if the pocket had folded its own downhill into a spiral and forgotten to tell the water. There is no current you would call a current. Only a slow exchange, a patience: minerals leaving a fresh face, entering a vein, leaving the vein again as a faint crust on a stem that did not have a crust when you last admitted it was the same stem. Cycles here are gradual enough to impersonate stillness. Stay, and the impersonation thins. A pad unrolls at the speed of cooling wax. A thread of root finds a crack, widens it by occupying it, and the crack's new edge gleams, younger than the root, which is impossible and local.

Fauna are almost a rumor. Once, between two pads that share a margin, something small holds still with such completeness that it might be a swelling in the plant—a bud, a gall, a trick of density. It has a sheen the plants do not quite have. It does not flee. Fleeing would require a gap. If it is alive it lives at the pocket's tempo, a life measured in the same units as mineral dulling, which is to say you will not see it complete an act. The rest of the animal absence is real. No schools, no skitter, no breath-bubbles seeking a surface that will not behave. What eats here, if anything eats, does it by becoming continuous with what is eaten, a merging so slow it reads as growth. The quiet is partly that: no mouths working, no wings, no feet. Only the tick, and then not even the tick.

Scattered through the pack are marks that are not quite the pocket's own. A curve of pale shell, bowl-hollowed, stuck between stems where no shell-animal has left any other sign, its lip already being written over by a film of green that treats it as substrate. A line scored in the fresh mineral, straight for the length of a finger, then continuing without a break into the midrib of a leaf and coming out the far side as a vein slightly paler than the others. A knot of fibers in a corner that is not a corner—the dark has few corners—tied, or grown into the likeness of a tie, holding nothing, the loose ends dissolving into root-hair. No tools made these, or if they did, the tools were not present as tools: no metal, no cut that remembers a blade's angle, no device humming or even having once hummed. Whatever intelligence passed through did not leave method. It left resemblance. The pocket accepts the resemblance and begins, patiently, to digest it into form. In another season of this slow time the shell will be a vein. The score will be a rib. The knot will be how a stem decides to branch. The built, here, is a fleeting misreading the world is already correcting, scattered, partial, faint as a word spoken into water and not repeated.

The breakdown is not spectacle. It is bookkeeping failing in a closed room.

You notice it first as count. There are more leaves than the volume can hold, and the volume does not increase, and the leaves are not crushed. They are concurrent. Two fronds occupy one gesture of space and both are entire; your hand, passing, passes through both and is wetted twice, the second wetting arriving from a direction your skin files under memory rather than touch. You notice it as containment. The seam you entered is still there—you can find it by the slight change in coolness, the one place where darkness thins toward the mere dark of the low ground outside—but the seam is also, somehow, on the inner face of a pad at the densest point, a slit the width of a wrist cut into living tissue, opening not onto the outside but onto the same water, repeating the entrance as a feature of the interior. Step through that second seam and you do not exit. You re-enter a pocket that contains the first, identical in pressure, slightly younger in its mineral, the shell-curve not yet filmed, the scored line still only in stone. Leave it again and the film has advanced. The processes are not synchronized across the nested copies. Time in here is a stack of slow versions, each patient, none of them the master copy.

You notice it as direction's resignation. Down pools toward whatever is most packed, so down moves when the plants do, a weak gradient sliding over hours you experience as a shift in which way your blood prefers to settle. Rain, or the idea of rain, sometimes stands in the water as a finer water, a wall of falling that does not fall, droplets arrested and stacked until they are a soft column, and the column is inside a stem, and the stem is raining inward, feeding itself a weather that never reaches the ground because the ground is a rumor the seam tells. The low elevation is real—the split is in low ground, flood-country, the sort of place that takes water and keeps it—but elevation inside the pocket is a courtesy the pocket is done extending. There is no high place to climb to. Climbing delivers you sideways into more pack, more cool, more of the dark's grain.

No one tends it. Nothing built to last has lasted in a form you could call a structure; the marks are already being recruited. No lamp has ever been lowered, or if it has, the dark took the lowering and did not record the light. What rules remain are biological and barely that: grow until concurrent, share the volume, do not hurry, do not smooth what is still new, accept the water as identity rather than surroundings. The weirdness is not an intruder on these rules. It is the way they are enforced once scale has collapsed and density has used up every between. A world this packed cannot afford singular location. A world this young cannot afford erosion's old compromises. A world this submerged cannot afford a surface. So location smears, newness stays sharp, and the surface becomes a story told at the seam for the benefit of the outside.

If you stay long enough to be incorrect about the length of your stay, the pocket begins to include you in its accounting. Not as prey—there is no appetite here you would recognize—but as another presence that must be made concurrent. The cool works inward from the skin. A film, not unpleasant, not even quite felt, considers the outline of your hand the way it considered the shell. You are not wounded. You are being offered a vein. Somewhere a tick sounds, closer to the tempo of your pulse than it was, and then the quiet corrects itself, and the quiet is larger than the pocket, and the pocket is still only a seam in low ground, wrist-wide, unremarkable, holding a drowned room that holds a forest that holds the seam, sharp-edged, lightless, packed to the point of shared body, changing so slowly that leaving and remaining are, for a little while longer, still two different things.
