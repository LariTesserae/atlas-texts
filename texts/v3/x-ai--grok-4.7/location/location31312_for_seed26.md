The floor is not ground. It was poured, in bays wide enough that the far joints vanish into the dim, and it has not yet learned to be old. Edges stay crisp: the lip of a drainage channel still shows the tooth-marks of the formwork, a pale seam of release-oil that the cold has not sweated away. Nothing here has been rounded. Water finds those edges and stops, a bright line, then spills in a sheet no thicker than a fingernail.

You are under. The ceiling is a made sky, low enough in the service cuts to brush a raised hand, high enough over the main vale that the lamps go small and copper before they reach it. It is all construction—ribbed plate, poured arches, a lattice of ducts that pretend at cloud. No rock shows. Where the excavators took the mountain out, the wound was skinned at once: membranes, shotcrete, a second skin of polymer that beads rather than drinks. The mountain is a rumor behind the walls. What you walk is the rumor’s replacement, valley-sized, still sharp, still loud with the work of being kept.

Water is everywhere and never deep. It condenses on the undersides of the ducts and lets go in a fine, constant weather—no storm, just the climate of cold metal meeting air that the machines have warmed by a degree and then abandoned. The drops tick on catwalks, on the crowns of pipes, on the empty aprons between them. In the low places the tick becomes a voice: channels cut ruler-straight across the vale, half full, moving with a muscular patience toward sumps the size of civic squares. At their margins the flow slows into a false marsh, a skin of water over grated beds, trembling when a pump kicks somewhere out of sight. The pumps do not rest. Their cycle is the weather here—draw down, slack, draw down—and the channels answer with a change in pitch you feel in the teeth before you name it.

Cold sits in the materials more than in the air. Steel rails sweat. Handrails leave a film on the palm. Breath shows, briefly, and is taken by the ventilation, which runs in long horizontal rivers overhead and never quite clears the damp. The light is industrial and insufficient: sodium in the traveled ways, colder white in the pump halls, and between them long reaches where only reflection works—a slick of channel, a wet plate wall, a ladder’s rungs repeating into nothing. Distances open anyway. From a gantry you can see three sumps, a switching yard of pipes, a row of cooling towers squatting like mills, and past them the vale continuing, lamp by lamp, until the haze of condensation closes it. The place is a town that refused buildings and kept only their infrastructure, scattered so that emptiness is the common material. You can walk ten minutes and meet no one and no machine that needs you, only the ongoing fact of flow, heat exchange, and the click of relays in boxes beaded with dew.

What grows, grows where the design forgot a margin. A pale film in the slack water—neither moss nor quite algae, a cold chemistry that likes the nutrient bleed from a seal. A fan of gray-green in a joint where two pours did not meet, living on condensate and the iron the walls give up. Nothing taller than a hand. No canopy, no litter of leaves. The beds that were meant for planting, if they were meant, stand as rectangles of manufactured grit, most of them empty, one or two colonized by the same low stain. The ecology is a rumor too: a maintenance schedule and a leak.

Animals are rarer than the stain. High in the duct forest, something small moves when the fans cycle down—a dry scrabble, then nothing. Once, a pale moth beaten against a lamp housing, wings already gummed with condensate. In a sump, far out, a chain of bubbles that is not the pump; it stops when you look. The loudness covers them. Turbines in the cooling stacks inhale and exhale on a period longer than breath. Conveyors somewhere in the eastern cut knock a steady metal sentence. Water slaps the lips of the channels. A valve hunts, fails to seat, hunts again, and the pipe beside you shivers with a held note. The sound does not come from one place. It is the vale’s way of being occupied while remaining empty.

The strangeness is not a monster. It is a mismatch of scales and intentions. The channels are too straight for water that wants to wander, so the water sheets sideways across the aprons whenever a pump lags, and for a minute the floor is a river with no banks, reflecting the duct-sky in broken panes. Then the pump catches and the river remembers its job and withdraws, leaving the concrete darker, the formwork teeth shining. In the western cut the ceiling weeps in lines that follow the rebar grid, a map of the making, and the drips fall in a rhythm so regular it feels designed—though no one designed the leak, only the steel that guides it. Fresh welds still throw a faint heat you can feel with the back of a wrist. Paint is unchalked. Corners have not yet collected the black felt of long damp. This is a landscape in its first season: an under-valley built all at once, already running, already wet, already forgetting—in the slow way metal forgets—that it was ever not here.

Stand long enough on the gantry and the processes separate themselves from the noise. Draw-down. Slack. A tower exhaling a column of barely warmer air that beads and falls back as rain too fine to wet a coat, only to gloss it. A conveyor answering a conveyor two cuts away, call and response. The false marsh trembling, going still, trembling. Far down the vale a lamp fails, and the darkness it leaves is the same size as the lamp’s reach—no larger, because nothing natural is waiting behind the light to come forward. Only more made distance, more sharp edges, more water finding them.
