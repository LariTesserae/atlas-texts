The pocket is smaller than a room and higher than weather. You do not climb to it. You arrive by noticing that a seam in the unfinished stone has a depth that refuses to match the stone’s thickness, and then you are already inside, standing on a floor that has not decided whether it is floor.

There is no water. There never was. What the air holds instead is a thin, hot, mineral breath — oxides, sulfur traces, a sweetness like heated resin that is not resin. It does not condense. It does not fall. It only thickens in the warmest corners and thins where the light is most ordinary, which is most of the place. The light itself is the first wrongness that feels polite: clear, even, the color of a high afternoon with no sky attached to it. Shadows fall as if a sun were overhead. There is no sun. The brightness is a property of the volume, distributed, uninterested in sources.

The rock is young. Not young as in recently broken — young as in not finished precipitating. Surfaces still have the look of something mid-thought: planes that grain into facets halfway, crystals half-extruded from a matrix that has not committed to being either granite or glass. Touch one and it gives, slightly, the way cooling slag gives, then firms under the hand as if embarrassed. Nothing here has been weathered. Edges are sharp because erosion has not been invented yet, not because anything cut them. In the lower reaches — and “lower” is a courtesy; the floor slopes in a direction that sometimes reverses if you turn around — the ground is a skin of unhardened mineral, warm enough to feel through a boot sole, printed with no tracks.

Between these raw plates the growth stands, and it is lush in the way a furnace is lush: dense where it commits, absent everywhere else. The plants are not plants in any watery sense. They drink the heat gradient and the metal vapor. Their stems are jointed tubes of a pale, fibrous silica, translucent enough that you can see the slow convection inside — not sap, a darker suspension of fines, climbing and falling on a cycle of hours. Leaves, where they occur, are thin plates, copper-green to furnace-orange, arranged sparsely along the stems so that each one has a private share of the even light. They do not flutter. There is almost no air movement. A colony might occupy a patch the size of a table, leaves overlapping until the patch reads as a thicket; then ten paces of bare, embryonic stone; then another colony, smaller, newer, its tallest stem still soft at the tip and beading with a dry, glassy exudate that hardens as you watch, not into ice, into a clear mineral cap.

Nothing eats them. Nothing walks. The lushness is unattended. Seeds, if that word applies, are not shed; they are pinched off at the stem-joint as little faceted nodules and simply rest where they fall, sometimes for what feels like a season of stillness, sometimes sinking a rootlet into the unfinished floor before the floor has agreed to be soil. The ecology is a patience. Succession here is geological. A thicket does not replace another thicket. It waits for the stone under it to finish a layer, and then it continues, or it doesn’t.

The industrial fact of the place is not built. There are no walls, no housings, no bolts, no one who assembled anything. The production is the formation itself, running at a scale that only looks mechanical because mechanics are what we have for processes with rhythm and product. Along one inner face — the face that sometimes is a wall and sometimes is the entire circumference, depending on whether you accept the pocket’s width — a seam extrudes wire. Not cable. Wire: hair-fine, continuous, a bright alloy sweating out of the rock the way a fault sweats quartz, coiling in loose, identical hoops that cool from white to straw to a dull industrial silver. The hoops do not pile into a heap so much as they settle into a grammar, each loop almost touching the last, a slow handwriting of metal with no hand. Elsewhere a vent no wider than a thumb sighs a hotter gas, and in that column a powder nucleates and falls as a dry snow of perfect microspheres, collecting in a shallow depression that is becoming a bin only because the powder keeps arriving and the stone keeps cupping. No chutes. No workers. The factory is the mountain learning a repeated gesture before the mountain has finished being born.

You can hear it if you stop. The quiet is real — no wind, no insects, no water — but under it a tick, spaced far apart, as a crystal face completes and locks. A lower, steadier note from the wire seam, more felt in the teeth than heard. Once, if you wait long enough, a stem-joint parts with a sound like a fingernail on glass, and a nodule drops and does not bounce. Then the quiet returns, larger for having been broken.

The rules fail gently, then all at once, then gently again. Count your steps across the bare stone and the count will not agree with the distance when you look back; the colonies have not moved, but the emptiness between them has been edited, a little more of it or a little less, as if the pocket inhales on a cycle longer than attention. Hold a leaf-plate to the light and for a moment you see through it into a larger version of the same place: the same even afternoon, the same unfinished plates, the same wire repeating itself across a slope that cannot fit inside the seam you entered. Let the leaf go and the larger place folds back into the plate, which is warm, and ordinary, and the size of your palm. High elevation is not a view. It is a pressure in the chest, a thinness, the sense that the ordinary light is the light of an altitude where air should not be this warm and this dry and this full of growing things. The heat does not come from above. It comes from the unfinished geology, still spending the energy of its own arrival.

Nothing here is in a hurry. A hoop of wire completes perhaps twice in an hour. A new colony takes, by any honest measure, longer than a visit. The microspheres deepen their depression by a dusting you would miss if you blinked through it. The place is emerging, and emergence is its weather, its industry, its only season: hot, high, sealed, waterless, loud only with the patience of matter deciding, again and again, to become a little more itself.
