The dark has a floor, and the floor is giving way under the idea of water without ever having been wet.

What meets a hand first is not stone and not soil. It is a packed, cool nap of fiber, laid in one direction and then another, the way basketry is laid when the maker has had centuries and no reason to stop. It drinks the warmth from skin and gives nothing back—no damp, no slick, no memory of rain. The mouth knows before the mind does: the air is cellar-cold and kiln-dry, and it tastes of old hay, of salt that dried out of a sap no longer moving, of a sweetness that went to dust so long ago the dust itself has a grain, sharp as ground glass and soft as pollen. There is no drip. There has not been a drip in the life of anything that could remember dripping. If water ever passed through this place, it passed the way a thought passes through a sleeper, and left only its mineral.

The volume is small. A few strides and a shoulder finds the other wall; a lifted arm finds, in places, the sag of the ceiling, in other places nothing, a pocket of empty headroom no taller than a room in a poor town. Embedded. The larger body this hollow sits inside does not offer itself. It announces only as weight: a slow, even pressure from above, the pressure of a world planed down to a final thickness and then left. Outside, if outside is a word that still applies, would be low, old, nearly flat—an exhaustion of land. In here the exhaustion has curled into a single interior and kept its shape.

And it is loud.

Not with weather. There is no weather. The sound is structural, a drone seated in the fiber the way a note sits in a hollow log when someone has stopped striking it and the log has not yet agreed. It does not rise and fall with any human patience. Over the length of a watch it thickens by a shade; over the length of a sleep it may drop a tone, or seem to, until the body learns that seeming is the cycle. The walls conduct it. A palm flat to the weave takes the sound into the wrist, up the bone, into the teeth. Speak, and the voice arrives already accompanied, already answered by a larger voice that is not speech. Two people in this pocket would have to put their mouths to each other’s ears. There is no one to do that.

Between the sounding surfaces there is almost nothing. The hollow is a held breath of vacancy. A fiber crosses it now and then, a bast cord left slack, thick as a wrist, gone gray in the way things go gray when color has had no light to be for longer than dye can mean. Touch one and it thrums at a different pitch than the wall, higher, meaner, and the difference maps the room better than eyes would. Most of the volume is interval. Presences are few and far, and the far is only a body’s length, which makes the emptiness feel ruder than a desert’s. A desert at least admits it is a distance. This admits it is a room that refused to fill.

What fills, entirely, is plant. There is no other substrate. The floor is rhizome compressed to a pavement, the grain still visible if a nail is dragged: bundles, anastomoses, the old decisions of growth. The walls are the same tissue turned upright and encouraged—by hands, once—into courses. Lanes run where the vascular bundles were cut back and the cut faces healed into curbs. Alleys are the gaps between fused stalks, some wide enough for a shoulder, some narrowed by later growth to a slot a hand can enter and a body cannot. At what would be a corner, a knot of tissue swells into a pier, and from the pier a rib springs to the far wall, load-bearing, alive in the only way left to it: not lengthening, not leafing, only maintaining a tension it settled into before metal was a habit anywhere near here. This is a town the size of a chapel precinct, built at the scale of a single organ, and then aged until town and organ became the same fact.

The making is primitive and careful. No nail, no fired brick, no glaze. Cuts are the cuts of stone flakes and ground edges; some flakes are still seated in the fiber where a worker set them and the tissue closed over the tang, a grip becoming part of the wall. Cordage of stripped bast lashes rib to rib in hitches a child could learn and a city could trust. There are thresholds worn not by feet—there is no track of feet, no scat, no shell, no chitin, nothing that hunted or was eaten—but by the slow shouldering of growth against growth, a polish of pressure. Niches at hand height hold objects that are also plant: cups lathed from hardened pith, combs of vascular needle, a stack of flat splints incised with counts. The counts are not pictures of animals. They are tallies of intervals, perhaps of the drone’s long periods, perhaps of the years the walls took to eat a lane and offer another. In one niche a bowl of salt stands to the rim with crystals the size of seeds. Lift it and it rings, a bright, dry strike that the room takes and lowers into the drone and does not return. The bowl was a cistern’s child. The cistern is the shallow basin at the middle of the hollow, paved in the same fiber, bloomed white with the last solute of a moisture that finished leaving. Stand in it and the sound is louder, focused, as if the town kept its well for acoustics after it lost every reason to keep a well.

Layers show where the civic plan was revised by the plant’s own patience. An older lane runs under the present floor, detectable as a hollow thump, a ghost street the new pavement roofed without removing. A stall-front has been grown shut, the counter still traceable as a ridge, the interior behind it a pocket of pockets, unlit, unentered, full of the same dry sweetness and a softer ticking—fibers shortening across hours, each tick a small surrender of length. Nothing here is in a hurry to fail. Failure already happened, in the sense that the lively phase ended. What remains is the stable afterward: a pure-plant mass that no longer spends itself on leaves or fruit or the theater of seasons, yet has not consented to rot, because rot would need water and a host of small lives, and both are absent. The tissue is cool, resinous at the breaks, fibrous where it has dried to a state past timber and not yet dust. Phytoliths grit under a thumbnail. Oxalate stars catch nothing; there is no light for them to catch. They only prick.

Darkness here is not an interruption. It is the medium the place was finished in. No spore-glow, no phosphor in the salts, no crack to a sky. If a flame were brought, the air might take it—there is no sign it would not—but the place does not produce flame, and the drone does not brighten or dim with any cycle of illumination, because there is no illumination to cycle. Direction is a matter of thicker and thinner, of where the sound piles and where it thins, of a draft so slight it is only the hollow breathing its own slow contraction. That contraction is the dynamic entire. Measure the span between two piers with a cord; leave the cord; return after a span of sleeps, and the cord has slackened by the width of a finger, or gone tight, according to a period longer than attention and shorter than geology. The town is still adjusting its final fit. It has been adjusting it since the plain above finished flattening. It will be adjusting it when the cord has mineralized into the wall.

Rules, then, as they offer themselves:

Nothing drinks. Moisture introduced from elsewhere beads, is taken into the salt-crust, and is gone, and the drone dips, offended, for a while that feels long and is not. The place does not grow toward water. It has outlived the appetite.

Nothing walks here that is not brought. No mite, no moth, no worm in the pith. The ecology is one organism continued into architecture, plus the salts and the silica it mined from itself, plus the tools it was cut with, which it has partly become. If there were gardeners, they were an episode. The episode closed. The beds they kept are the streets.

Sound is load. Pluck a crossing fiber too hard and a rib answers, and a course of wall shifts by a grain, and the tally-splints in their niche click once, as a clerk might acknowledge a transfer. The loudness is not waste. It is the audible form of forces still negotiating in a structure that has reached the shape it means to keep and has not yet been released from keeping it.

Scale is the rind. Beyond the inner bark—thick, cool, seamless unless a flaw is hunted with the fingers—the same plant continues into the compressed ages overhead and to every side, but that continuation is not a landscape you can enter. It is bedding. The world, for any purpose that still uses the word place, is this hollow: a hidden municipality of dried vascular will, urban in plan, primitive in joint and tool, empty in its air, total in its flesh, cool, low, older than the idea of a ruin, loud with the work of remaining, and dark in the way a seed is dark when the seed has decided, at last, against opening.
