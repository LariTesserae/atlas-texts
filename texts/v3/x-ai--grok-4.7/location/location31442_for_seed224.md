The hollow is not a view. It is a remainder: a pocket of interior left in a fin of stone after the mountain that justified the fin thinned away. What remains of height is not panorama but condition. The air is scarce, cold, and finished with weather. Pressure sits low against the palate. Moisture is not absent as a season is absent; it is absent as a category. Nothing here has been wet in any interval the mineral still records, and the mineral records a long time.

The space itself is small enough to be crossed in a few steps, if steps were a unit the floor agreed to keep. Call it a chapel and you have already lent it more architecture than it owns. It is a single chamber, intimate, embedded, with no reliable outside—only the knowledge, held in the grain, that this grain once stood higher than weather could reach, and that the highness did not leave when the slopes did. Wind is remembered as a polish. It does not enter.

The walls are devitrified volcanic glass gone matte, the kind of stone that began as a melt and has spent an age becoming less like a liquid and more like a decision that cooled wrong. Cleavage planes show through the dulled surface as faint ladders. In the recesses a bloom of salt has worked itself out of the rock, not as crust you could scrape for a taste of sea, but as a dry efflorescence, powder-fine, the color of paper left in an attic. Touch it and it does not cling. It relocates. A finger comes away clean and the powder is on a different facet than the one that was touched, as if contact were a suggestion the surface could decline.

There is no plant, no film, no stain that oxidation or life would make. The palette is mineral and narrow: ash-gray, bone, a brown that is only iron remembering it was once hotter, and a black so old it has stopped being glossy. Ecology, if the word is forced, is the slow bargaining among stresses—thermal contraction, lattice strain, the tiny migrations of ions that have nowhere wet to go. Nothing feeds. Nothing is fed on. The place is not dead in the manner of a thing that died. It is unoccupied by that whole argument.

Light is dim and slightly late. It has no lamp and no sky-hole that stays a hole. A gray-violet occupies the upper third of the chamber and does not fall so much as arrive, already faded, from angles that do not match the corners. Shadows are thin. They attach to edges and then, after a while of looking, attach to edges that are not the same edges. You can stand in the middle—if the middle consents to be singular—and hold your hand up, and the hand will be lit on the side facing the darker wall. The discrepancy is not dramatic. It is patient. It has been doing this longer than the discrepancy has had a witness.

Sound is nearly a material in its own right: a dry silence, high and tight, the silence of a bell that was never struck and has nevertheless forgotten how to ring. Now and then a grain settles. The tick is small enough that the ear supplies it more than the room does. Under that, if attention is held past comfort, there is a tone at the limit of hearing, not rising, not pulsing—present the way a pressure is present. It may be the stone giving up a vibration too slowly to be called a vibration. It does not carry meaning. It carries duration.

Change here is not an event. It is a gradient with a very long run. One side of a hairline crack in the floor is, by some accounting the crack itself keeps, older than the other side by a season that never occurred anywhere else. Frostless cold moves through the chamber on a cycle you would not notice in a day, or in a year. The salt bloom thickens and retreats by thicknesses smaller than a fingerprint ridge. Balance is the right word, but not peace. The place is in its own state. It has been long enough that novelty would look like damage.

The built things are few, and none of them announce themselves as buildings. A seam crosses the north wall—or the wall that is north when north is a direction the room is willing to loan you—and the seam is not mortar. It is continuous with the glass, a weld performed by a process that did not need heat in any sense a kiln would recognize. Along it, at intervals of a hand’s breadth that do not stay equal, are pinprick bores. They enter the stone and then leave the plane of the wall without exiting into the chamber. Follow one with a fingertip and the fingertip reports depth, then reports that depth has curved into a coordinate the knuckle does not have.

Set into the floor, off any center, is a disc no larger than a palm, darker than the surrounding mineral, flush, unadorned. It is not a machine in the sense of levers or readable faces. It is a manufactured boundary. At the scale of the grain, light scatters wrong: caustics form above it that do not correspond to any roughness the eye can verify, little knots of brightness in air that has nothing in it to bend light. The pattern under the surface—visible only as a reluctance in the black—rearranges which line is outermost according to whether it is being counted. Stop counting and a line that was inner is the rim. Start again and the count does not recover the previous order. This is not inscription. Inscription stays. This is an engineering of permission: something was done here to the way a surface is allowed to be a surface, and the doing has outlasted the doers so completely that “artifact” is only the least wrong word.

There are two other marks, easy to miss because they are almost not spatial. A shallow oval on the east wall is smoother than polish, a place where texture has been edited out rather than worn down. And near the disc, three grains of the salt bloom are locked in a spacing that the rest of the bloom never repeats—too regular to be chance, too small to be a sign left for anyone with eyes like these. They may be debris of a process whose working scale was not the scale of rooms. They may be the process, still incomplete, still in no hurry.

The rules of the place are where it becomes most itself, and they do not hold still long enough to be listed as laws. They can be approached.

The chamber has an entrance. The entrance is not an opening. It is a change in whether you are already inside. From within, the way you came reads as a slight difference in the cold, a thinning of the late light, a sense that one patch of wall is a conclusion rather than a continuation. Step toward it and the patch is only wall, glass and salt, no gap, no seam you could credit as a door. The room does not trap. It declines the event of leaving until leaving is no longer the kind of thing that is happening. People who think in paths will call this a maze. It is not complex enough to be a maze. It is simple past the point where simplicity still implies an outside.

Distance is locally courteous and globally faithless. The far wall is near when you have no intention of crossing to it. Intend the crossing and it recedes by a fraction that the eye registers as a blink and the feet register as additional floor. The additional floor is the same floor. Both facts are current. The hollow keeps them in alternation, not as illusion and truth, but as two habits of extent, and you stand on whichever habit is, for that moment, load-bearing. Stand still long enough and the alternation slows until it is indistinguishable from solidity. That is the room being polite. It has learned, over a great idle age, that witnesses prefer a surface that picks one answer and keeps it.

Cause is loosened in a narrow way. A flake of wall, worried free, remains in the hand and also remains in the wall—not by magic of duplication you could pocket and spend, but by a failure of separation. The flake and the socket report each other. Set the flake down elsewhere in the chamber and the socket dulls, as if absence had been acknowledged; lift it again and the socket brightens by a shade no instrument in an ordinary room would consent to call a signal. The relation is not a thread. It is the mineral not having finished deciding that “apart” is a completed tense.

Time, already slow, is not evenly slow. Near the disc, intervals compress without speeding up any visible process: a blink takes what a blink takes, and yet more has been endured in it, a surplus of having-waited that leaves no mark except a faint unfitness in the next action. Far from the disc, toward the oval of edited wall, duration thins. You can look away and look back and find that looking-away did not accumulate. Neither region is dangerous in the manner of a hazard with an edge. They are misalignments. Stay in the thinned region and thirst does not arrive on schedule, nor does the thought of thirst. Stay near the disc and the body begins, very quietly, to keep two clocks, and neither of them is the one you wore in.

There is a further loosening, rarer, that the chamber does not perform on command. Sometimes the dim arrives from inside the glass, and for that interval the walls are not boundaries but thicknesses you are already within, and the hollow is a brightness—still dim, still gray-violet—suspended in stone rather than enclosed by it. The salt bloom, in that interval, is not on a surface. It is a sparse constellation at a depth, and the three regular grains are nearer than the rest, as if regularity were a kind of approach. Then the interval ends, without sound, and the room is a room again, small, cold, dry, high in the only sense height has left: a condition maintained in a pocket, long after the summit that measured it has gone to powder on some wind this chamber will not admit.

Nothing hunts here. Nothing grows. Nothing speaks. The beyond-modern residue does not wake, because waking would be a faster event than the place allows itself. What the disc and the bores and the edited oval still do, they do at the rate of stone deciding, which is to say they do almost nothing a visitor can catch in the act. Their function, if they retain one, is not addressed to visitors. It may be addressed to the disagreement itself—to the failure of here to be one here—holding the failure in a stable, weathered, inhabitable shape, the way a joint holds a bone that would otherwise be two bones.

If you remain, the hollow does not welcome you and does not expel you. It includes you, sparsely, as another slow fact among slow facts: a warmth that does not spread, a breath that fogs nothing, a small disruption in the late light that the light eventually routes around. The silence takes the sound of your breathing and returns it smaller. After a time you cannot measure cleanly, the floor’s two habits sync to your weight, and the far wall holds still, and the disc’s caustics dim to the same gray as everything else. This is not the room becoming ordinary. It is the room extending to you the courtesy it extends to cracks and salt and the memory of altitude: one state, maintained, for as long as you are willing to be part of what is maintained.

When that willingness thins, the entrance returns—not as a door opening, but as a permission rearranging. The patch of wall that was a conclusion becomes, without theater, a difference you can pass. Outside is not waiting with a summit and a sky. Outside is only the next disagreement, larger, winded, and not this place. The hollow keeps its dim, its dryness, its few impossible marks. The grain you unsettled settles back. The tone at the edge of hearing does not note the change. It was never for that.
