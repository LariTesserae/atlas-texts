The first thing the place does is refuse a surface.

There is light, and it is ordinary light—daylight, the color of a high sun behind thin high cloud—but it does not arrive across an air you can stand in and then stop at a skin of water. It is already inside the medium when you notice it. Cool, clear, slightly green at distance, blue when you look up along the fall of it. You can see a long way. That is the second refusal: vastness here is not murk. The volume is almost empty, so vision goes until the eye gets tired of having nothing to land on.

What holds you is water, cool enough that heat leaves the body in a steady tax and never in a shock. Not ice. Not the laid cold of the deep trenches of ordinary worlds. A highland cold, the cold of tarns and late spring at altitude, extended without apology across a reach that has weather of its own. Pressure exists, but it does not pile the way a textbook says it should. You can be far under the bright zone and still not be crushed, still not be dark. Something in the equation of the fluid spends the weight before it arrives. No wall does this. No hull, no engine, no made thing. The spending is a property of the water, as salinity is a property of other seas. Current physics can name the outcome and not the term. The term is simply in force, everywhere, evenly, like a climate that forgot it was also a law.

There is a floor, or the idea of one, but it is not old enough to be geography.

It arrives upward. Far below the light, in a dim that is still only dim and not night, mineral weather condenses. Not sediment sifting down from a finished continent—there is no finished continent—but nucleation, points of stone deciding to be points, then sheets, then low ridges with edges still sharp because nothing has had time to round them. The erosion score of this world is nearly zero because erosion has not been invited. What you are looking at is pre-wear. Raw geology in the act of becoming solid inside a liquid that is already planetary. A ridge may not have existed yesterday. Tomorrow it may be a sill the size of a county, pale, glassy at the breaks, ringing if anything struck it, which almost nothing does. The turbulence is creative more than ruinous. Storms here are storms of settling-out: curtains of mineral snow, upwellings that are warmer by a degree and leave the water briefly less itself, internal fronts that travel for hundreds of kilometers and lay down a pavement as they pass. The pavement does not last in the way land lasts. It is interrupted, resumed, argued with. You can watch a plain thicken while you cross it, the way you can watch rime thicken on a wire, except the wire is a pressure-line and the rime is basalt still deciding its grain.

Above that unfinished floor the water goes on, and on, and is mostly nothing.

Density is the poverty of the place, and also its grandeur. Between presences there is only the medium: cool, loud, lit, moving. A drift can last a day and offer a single event—a far shimmer, a change in the pitch of the roar, a spine of new rock rising through the lower dark like a thought the sea has not finished. Vegetation is real and rare. It does not forest. It does not meadow. On the ridges that have stayed solid long enough to be trusted, a few anchors take: pale veils, not quite kelp, not quite mineral, photosynthetic in the ordinary way, sparing of leaf because the light is ample and the nutrients are not. They stand so far apart that each one is an occasion. You do not pass through growth. You sight it, the way a sailor sights a single tree on an otherwise empty coast, and then the coast is gone and there is water again. Nothing grazes them. There is no animal life—not a shoal, not a worm in the new stone, not a fleck that flicks and is a life. The veils shed no eggs into a food web. They shed spores or something quieter, and most of those never meet a ridge. The ecology is a scattering of patients.

The loudness is the shock, if you came expecting emptiness to be quiet.

Empty water carries. Every front, every collapse of a nucleating shelf, every internal breaker where two densities disagree, speaks, and the speech does not die at the edge of a bay because there are no bays, only fetch. The roar is continuous and high for a liquid world—less the muffled drum of a deep abyss than the sound of weather given a body large enough to have thunder inside it. You hear a ridge being born a long way off as a bright percussive stitch under the general noise. You hear the mineral snow as a hiss that moves. Sometimes the water organizes its own sound into long chords, standing waves of pressure that ring the volume the way a bell rings after the strike, except no one struck it. That is as weird as the place gets, and it is not very weird: a sea that prefers certain notes, and keeps them, until a new front arrives and retunes the room. No voice answers. No biosonar. The chords are the fluid’s, and they pass through you without regard.

If there is a surface, it is high, and it is not a shore.

Climb the light and the water thins toward a boundary that behaves like weather rather than like a beach. The upper reaches are a wall of rain that has forgotten which way is down—sheets, veils, rotating columns, a bright cold mixing zone where the sea rehearses becoming air and then changes its mind. Elevation is not a mountain you climb out of the water to stand on. Elevation is the fact that this entire ocean sits up in the world’s height, above older, lower atmospheres, above cloud decks that are not its clouds. From inside, you do not see the drop. You infer it from the light’s thinness, from the cold, from the way sound sometimes shears at the upper boundary as if part of the note has escaped into a sky that cannot hold it. The sea does not spill. It should. A body of water the size of a continent, perched, with an edge somewhere beyond sight, ought to pour off the highland into the dark below and boil away to weather. It does not. The same undescribed term that spends the pressure also spends the urge to fall. The rim, if it can be called a rim, is a standing argument: water arriving at the limit of its law and being turned, gently, loudly, back into itself. Spray goes up into ordinary light and comes down as the wall of rain. Nothing constructed keeps the argument. There is no lip of metal, no field-pylon, no ruin of the makers. If there were makers, they left the rule and took their hands away. The place reads as untouched because it is—no artifact, no road, no hull on the new ridges, no junk in the long empty fetches. The technology, if that is the word, is constitutional. It is how this water is allowed to be water.

Motion never settles into a picture you can keep.

A fetch that was clear at morning is by afternoon a lane of turbidity where a sill collapsed and gave its substance back to suspension, the particles bright in the normal light, loud as gravel in a rapid, and then gone, resettled farther on, sharper than before. A veil that stood alone on a ridge may be left in open water when the ridge un-decides, the holdfast closing on nothing, the organism—plant, barely—drifting until it finds stone again or doesn’t. Storms are regional and ignorant of any coast. They have centers. They have eyes of calmer, brighter water where the nucleation pauses and you can see, for a while, farther than is comfortable: down to a floor that is still sketching itself, out to a green horizon that is not land, only the optical end of emptiness. Then the eye fills, the chord shifts, and the work of becoming resumes.

Rules, such as they are:

Water is the country, the air, the distance, and the law. It is cool, loud, and mostly vacant. Light behaves. Life, if the word still fits, is a few patient anchors and no movers. Stone is young and does not erode so much as appear, fail, and appear again with cleaner edges. Nothing built stands, and nothing needs to: the engineering is the medium’s refusal to obey the book of pressures and the book of shores. You can cross for a long time and meet only sound, light, and the sense that the ground under the ground is still being written, in a hand that does not hurry, and does not stop.
