The place is smaller than the weather trying to use it. A slot, a pocket, a nave of stone no wider than a shout can cross and still come back as itself—roofed in places by a lip of new rock that has not yet learned to fall, walled on three sides by faces so fresh the edges still cut the hand that isn't there to touch them. You could walk the floor in a minute if the floor would hold still. It does not.

It is high. Not the high of a long approach and a view, but the high of air that has already thinned and has nothing left to give. The walls go up into a darkness that is not night exactly and not the inside of a cave, though it behaves like both. Cloud has seated itself on the rim and will not lift. What light arrives comes sideways, already used up, a gray that never quite becomes a color. In the thicker minutes it fails altogether, and the stone is known only by what it does to sound and to skin.

The rock is young in the way a broken tooth is young. Uplift has not finished arguing with the sky. Planes of fracture stand at angles no weather has had time to soften. Edges throw sparks of static when the drier gusts rake them—real discharge, blue and brief, the only electricity the place owns and enough of it to smell. Grit leaves the walls in sheets. A shelf calves, hesitates, and becomes a slope. Another shelf takes its place because the fault below has not stopped working. Nothing here has been rounded into the comfortable lie of age.

Water does not live here so much as interrupt. It beads on the undersides of the overhangs, gathers, lets go, and is gone into the mats before you can call it a stream. Then a gust shoulders a load of cloud in over the lip and the whole nave is briefly a vertical rain, cold and directionless, drumming on leaves and on bare planes alike, finding every fresh crack and widening it by a patience measured in hours rather than eras. Pockets hold. A basin no larger than a crouched body keeps a black inch of water that never settles; wind walks across it and the inch becomes a skin, a boil, a slap against stone. Elsewhere the damp is only a film, shining when the gray thins, invisible when it doesn't, cold enough that putting a hand to the wall is like touching the inside of a pipe.

And the pipe is full of green. The vegetation did not wait for soil. It took the damp, the mineral dust, the cool that never quite freezes, and it covered the young stone as if covering were a form of hunger. Moss in layers so dense the wall has a second wall. Ferns in the dripline, fronds interlaced until passage between them is a negotiation. Cushions of leaf and stem packed into every ledge, spilling, rooting into the spill. Where a face is too sheer for that, a crust of growth still dulls it, dark on dark, soft over sharp. There is no clearing. There is barely an inside. Stems touch stems. Wet blades touch the shoulder of the air. You do not look across this place; you look into a thickness.

Nothing moves in it that is not plant, water, stone, or wind. No call. No quickness along a branch. No eye. The spores and the seeds arrived on the same updrafts that bring the grit, and they did their work, and the rest of life has not yet found a purchase the turbulence will allow. If something small lived in the moss it would have to live without being known. The place, as it stands and thrashes, keeps its census at zero.

What it does not keep quiet is itself. The sound is industrial in the old sense of the word: an undertaking, a continuous making, too loud for talk. Wind in the throat of the slot has a pitch and a rhythm, a rise and a drop, as if a belt were turning somewhere out of sight. Water, when the cloud dumps, hammers the basin and the fern-mats with a mechanical insistence, then cuts, then hammers again as the gusts reload. Stone contributes the irregular part of the machine—the crack, the skitter of a fresh plate down a face, the grind of grit in a pothole that has only just begun to be a pothole and already knows the work. Under it all a lower note, not mystical, only the mountain still rising, the fault taking another useless millimeter while you stand in the product of the last one. Electrified air adds its own dry tick against the edges. None of this was built. There is no wall that was raised, no wheel that was set, no mark of a hand. The industry is the weather and the youth of the rock agreeing, at this one small address, to run without pause.

The agreement does not settle. A minute of near-dark and dripping. A minute in which the lip sheds a curtain and the ferns go flat and the basin overflows in a sheet down a groove that did not exist this morning. A minute of suction, as if the slot inhales, and the hanging moss lifts and the static walks the rim in brief blue stitches. Then a collapse of sound into a different sound, higher, meaner, the wind having found a new edge it sheared itself only an hour ago. Creation and undoing share the same tools here: water, grit, cold, dark, and a surface that has not yet decided which of its faces is the permanent one. There is no permanent one. That is the rule, and it is an ordinary rule. Mountains do this. They simply do not usually do it in a room this small, this green, this loud, with no animal present to flinch and no lamp present to explain the walls.

Stand in the thick of it and the boundaries remain obvious even when you cannot see them. Cliff. Lip. The far wall close enough that a falling plate would reach you. Beyond the lip, only the loaded dark of the cloud cap and the thin high air. Inside, the production line: leaf on leaf, drip on drip, stone reducing itself to the next sharp version of stone. Cool on the skin. Damp in the lungs. A factory with no owner, running the night shift because the night, here, does not end so much as thicken and thin and thicken again.
