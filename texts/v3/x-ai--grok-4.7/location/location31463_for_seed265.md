The first thing the place gives you is not an outline. It is a warmth held against the teeth, and a wetness that does not fall so much as already be there, beading on the inner lip, filming the wrists, finding the seam where a boot meets the ankle and deciding that seam was always a shore. There is no horizon to refuse. There is only the sense that refusal would be useless: the dark is not a lid on the day. It is the medium. You stand in it the way a stone stands in warm groundwater.

Underfoot the country is low and unfinished. It has the sharpness of something that has only just agreed to be land. Ridges come up in clean fins, edges still crisp enough to catch cloth, planes of mudstone and a paler mineral that has not yet learned a name from weather. Joints gape. From every joint the rock weeps—not a dramatic spring, a patient seepage, warm as the air, carrying a faint metal sweetness and a darker taste like wet iron left in a closed room. The water does not hurry. It sheets, pauses in thumb-wide basins on the young terraces, spills by a thickness, sheets again. In the lower reaches it loses even that intention and becomes a marsh that is barely a marsh: a warm film over silt, ankle-ended, endless in the lateral sense, broken by the new ridges the way a barely set skin is broken by a scar that has not softened.

You can walk a long time and not leave it. That is the scale. Not a pocket, not a room. A reach. One feature gives onto another without a gate between them: the weeping escarpment, the silt sheet, a pavement of fresh fracture-blocks still dusted with the powder of their own splitting, a shallow where the film deepens to the calf and the bottom is softer than it should be for ground this new. Distances announce themselves by effort and by the changing thickness of the air, not by sight. After a while the effort itself becomes unreliable. A walk that felt like a field begins, without a turn, to feel like the width of a town. You have not climbed. There is almost nothing to climb. Elevation here is a rumor kept by the occasional knuckle of rock, and even those knuckles are only high enough to put your hand on.

The air is crowded in a way the ground cover is not. Vegetation, such as it is, keeps to insults and omissions: a gray crust on the lee of a fin, a shelf of something fibrous and pale that is not quite fungus and not quite precipitate, a few stiff wands rising from the silt no taller than a knee, spaced so far apart that calling them a stand would be a kindness. Nothing leafy. Nothing that reaches. Whatever would have drunk light has declined the bargain, or never been offered it. The living thickness is elsewhere—in the film, in the warm seam of air just above the film, in the joints, in the hollows under the fracture-blocks. Life here is abundant and almost entirely uninterested in becoming a plant.

They move in layers. In the sheet, small bodies tick against the ankle and then sheer off, numerous enough that standing still is to be inventoried. On the fins, heavier things follow the weep-lines with a patience that matches the water’s, shells or hides clicking once when they adjust, then silent again. Higher in the air—still low, still within reach of a raised arm—there is a constant soft traffic, wingbeats too small and too wet to clap, more like cloth being folded in another room. Territories overlap until the idea of a territory thins out. You are never alone in a volume of this air. You are also rarely touched. They know the dimensions of you by heat and by the way you displace the film, and they revise around you without comment. Now and then a larger animal passes close enough that its warmth is a second climate on your ribs, and the smell of it is clean and tidal, and then it is only the ordinary warmth again. None of them call. If they signal, it is by some channel the dark keeps. The country is mostly quiet. What sound there is has to be earned: a single drip finding a longer drop, the minute collapse of a silt edge, the far slow turn of something built.

The built things are scattered, and they are not ruins in the sentimental sense. They are working, or they are in the long middle of a work so slow it counterfeits rest. A pipe run crosses a terrace and vanishes into a joint, flanged, warm to the palm, sweating along its underside in beads larger than the rock’s own weep. Farther on, a pump-house no bigger than a shed stands on piles driven into silt that should not yet hold piles, and does. Its door is shut. From it comes a pressure more than a noise, a cycled density in the sternum, as if the air were being counted and released on a period longer than a breath. Elsewhere a single stack, blind, unlit, and a short tram of rails that end without apology in the film, the metal already skinned with the same pale crust that passes for growth. No town. No street. The industry is a constellation with most of its stars missing, and the missingness is part of the design: each machine is far enough from the next that you could believe you had found the only one, until the next warmth in the pipe tells you the system is regional and does not require you to see its continuity.

They are electrified. You learn this without a spark. Close to a housing, the skin of the forearm stipples, and a taste of pennies arrives under the tongue, and the small traffic in the air revises its routes by a hand’s width, avoiding a column of influence that has no color. Whatever the current is doing, it is not becoming light. Lamps, where they exist—caged bulbs on a pump-house eave, a porcelain socket in a wall—are present as objects and absent as events. You can put a finger near the glass and feel the faint cooked smell of a filament that is spending itself. You cannot see it spend. The expenditure goes somewhere else. Into heat, partly. Into the dark, more than partly. The dark here accepts light the way deep water accepts a shout: locally, and then not at all. A struck match is a brief insult to the fingers and a smell of phosphorus, and then it is the same matched dark as before, the flame having occurred in a privacy so complete it might as well not have been flame. Animals that in another physics would have carried their own small lamps carry instead a chemical sharpness, a heat-speckle, a change in the way droplets bead on them. Their signaling is real. It simply refuses the one channel you arrived with.

That refusal is not the only place the world’s manners slip. It is only the plainest.

Stand on a fresh fin and face what your feet call outward. Walk until the pump-house warmth is gone from your back. The silt sheet continues. It also, without a seam you can find, continues as a sheet you have already crossed: the same ankle-film, the same spacing of stiff wands, the same tick of small bodies, but the pipe you left behind is now also ahead, sweating in the same grammar, and the piles of the shed are in two distances at once, both of them honest if you test them by walking, neither of them willing to be the copy. The country is not looping in the cheap way of a corridor. It is allowing a single installation to be coincident with itself across a reach, as if the industrial act had been to pin a location into the ground more than once and then forget to rank the pins. Water assists. A handful of the warm seep, lifted, runs out of the hand in two intervals—one immediately, down the wrist, and one a little later, from no higher place, as if the handful had included a delay and the delay had weight. People who stay long enough stop calling this a trick. They call it the way the basin keeps its accounts. Young rock, slow process, water near the surface: the land has not had time to decide how many of itself it is, and the water, older than the land in habit if not in this emergence, keeps drafting the books.

Change, when it comes, comes as a revision of those books rather than as weather. The film rises by a finger over what might be a day and might be the length of time it takes the far machine to complete a single stroke. Edges that were sharp in the morning of your attention are still sharp; a pebble you set on a crest is still there, but the crest has admitted, very politely, a new fracture, and the pebble now sits on two stones that have not yet agreed which of them owns it. Fauna shift their layers by increments. A species that kept to the joints begins, across a long stillness, to keep also to the pipe-shadows—not shadows, the cooled grooves under the pipes—and to treat the metal’s weep as a second hydrology, which it is. Nothing startles. Nothing stampedes. Abundance here is a saturated slowness, many bodies practicing a few motions with inexhaustible patience.

There are rules, and they are local, and they hold until they are beside another rule that also holds.

Light does not propagate. Producing it is allowed and irrelevant.

Water is continuous with itself across gaps it has not crossed.

Sound dies quickly, except for the drip and the subsonic cycle of the machines, which travel farther in the body than in the air.

The ground is new and will remain, for a long while yet, newer than the water that covers it. Erosion is present as wetness and absent as rounding. The fins keep their edges because the abrasion has not been scheduled, or because the particles that should do the cutting are occupied being alive.

Vegetation will not thicken to match the fauna. The pale crusts and the stiff wands are the concession, not the beginning of a greening. Anything that looks, at the edge of touch, like a young reed is usually a colony of the small shelled things standing in each other’s leaks, imitating a plant because the shape is cheap to hold in a current this weak.

The machines are not abandoned and not explained. They move on periods closer to geology than to shifts. Interfering with one does not darken a city somewhere else; there is no evidence of a city. It changes the taste of the seep downstream of the pipe, and the animals revise, and a coincident copy of the housing, elsewhere in the reach, may hesitate for one cycle, as if reminded that it is the same housing.

You can leave. The leaving is like the walking: a change in effort, a thinning of the pennies on the tongue, a moment when a droplet on the lash is only a droplet and not also a later droplet. Behind you the basin does not seal. It continues its slow manufacture of itself—warm, low, sharp, wet, unlit—densely occupied, scarcely grown, quiet enough that if it has a voice it is the voice of water deciding, again, not to fall.
