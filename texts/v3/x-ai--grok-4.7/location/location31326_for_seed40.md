The plateau does not rise so much as continue. From any edge you can still see the next edge, and beyond that a pale geometry of further edges, all of them too even to have been worn into being. The rock underfoot is not rock. It is a poured substrate, cool to the palm, the color of a dry riverbed that has never been a river, scored with ridges so new the grit still sits in the grooves. Nothing here has had time to round. Corners hold their angles. Seams show. The whole high country looks assembled last season and then left in the weather on purpose.

Moisture arrives without weather you can name. A film gathers on the metal walks and on the undersides of the great ribs that cross the open. Sometimes a brief rain needles down out of a sky that stays white and overbright, and the water finds the youngest cracks and stands there in thin mirrors, refusing to soak because there is nothing beneath to soak into. Between rains the air stays damp anyway. Condensation beads along cable trays and along the lips of vents. You can taste iron and cold mineral and, faintly, the sweet of machine oil that has not been hot in a long time.

The scale is a valley's scale, or a town's, except there is no town and no valley floor—only this constructed highland repeating outward until the bright distance smears the repetitions together. Close in, the density is almost rude. Gantries stand near enough to touch. Pipes run in bundles thicker than a body. Catwalks stack three and four deep, their railings casting hard ladders of shadow that do not move, because the light does not move. It is noon without a sun you can locate: a high industrial brightness, shadowless in the open lanes and knife-sharp wherever an overhang interrupts it. Nothing is dim. Even the pockets of water glare.

Vegetation is an afterthought the place barely permits. A few pale crusts cling where condensate collects longest—gray-green films, not leaves, not quite lichen, stuck to polymer and to the fresh edges of cut plate. No trees. No soil. What passes for growth is a staining, and even that is sparse, islands of it separated by long clean expanses of made ground.

The animals do not seem to mind the scarcity. They are everywhere, and they are quiet about it. Pale birds the size of fists roost in the angles of trusses, dozens to a joint, unmoving for so long you take them for fittings until one turns its head with a click. Things like lizards, jointed wrong, sun themselves on warm housings that are not warm. In the standing water, something pulses just under the surface film—many somethings, layered, near enough to one another that the water looks thicker than water. You hear almost none of them. A wing adjusts. A claw ticks once on metal. The rest of the abundance is a pressure, a crowded silence, bodies occupying the volume the way parts occupy a warehouse that has stopped shipping.

The industry is still here. It has not stopped so much as lengthened its cycle past the edge of attention. Housings the size of sheds tick as they cool, then, much later, tick as they warm by a degree you cannot feel. Conveyors exist, belts dull with a dust that is not dust, and if you watch one long enough a single carrier may advance the width of a hand. Valves sweat. A piston somewhere, very far, completes a stroke you register only because a shadow on a far rib shifts by a finger's breadth and then holds. Production, if that is the word, is a patient process. The engines are present, electrified, scaled for a region, and almost idle. Their sound is a low presence under the quiet, more felt in the railing than heard, and easily mistaken for the blood in your own ear.

Walk far enough and the rules begin to fail in small, repeatable ways. A catwalk that was ahead of you is also, when you look down through the grating, the catwalk you are standing on, occupied by birds that match the ones at your shoulder and do not react when you shift your weight. A pool of rain reflects a structure that is not overhead: an older version of the same rib, already eroded, which cannot be, because nothing here has eroded. The reflection stays when you move. In another place the light arrives twice—once as the flat noon, and once, a half-second late, as a second noon from a direction that has no lamp. Your shadow splits politely and then agrees again.

Distances disagree with steps. You cross what should be a short gantry and the next tower, which was near enough to read the stamped numbers on its plate, is suddenly a regional thing again, small with true distance, while the numbers remain readable if you squint, which they should not be. The stamped figures are not figures. They rearrange when you are not proving them. Fauna use these failures as corridors. A bird leaves a perch in the near stack and arrives, without a flight you can track, on a perch that the geometry says is a kilometer off and also adjacent. The lizards drink from reflections. Some of them drink from reflections of animals that are not otherwise present, and come away wet.

Nothing hurries to correct itself. The breakdown is as slow as the machines. A seam that was flush in the morning stands proud by evening, sharp as a new cut, as if the world were still being extruded and had briefly forgotten the order of operations. Water beads on a surface that, a moment earlier, was the sky. Or you thought it was the sky. Up here the bright has no reliable outside. The plateau continues. The plateau is also, in places, the interior of one of its own housings, walls of the same fresh substrate, lit the same unlocated way, crowded with the same quiet animals, damp along every young edge.

If there is a rule that still holds, it is patience. Change happens, but across durations that make a walk feel like a sample rather than an event. You can stand and watch a conveyor tooth emerge from a slot that was solid plate. You can watch a bird become, gradually, a fitting, and the fitting, more gradually, a bird again. The highland does not explain itself. It keeps its brightness, its cool, its industrial near-silence, its excess of living things on a ground that was never ground, and it lets the contradictions occupy the same volume without resolving, the way dense things do when the space they are given is both open and already full.
