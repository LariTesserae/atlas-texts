The roof is the first fact, and it does not behave like a sky.

It is close enough, in the first hours, that a raised hand can find it: a plane of stone still carrying the stretch of its own making, pulled into long lenses and arrested there. The break is young. Edges have not learned to be edges. They are still the moment of splitting, glassed over, keen enough to open skin if skin is dragged rather than set down. Nothing has rounded them. There is no drip to insist on a curve, no grain carried in a trickle, no film of weather worrying the lip of a fracture back toward bluntness. The rock looks unfinished because it is unfinished, and because unfinished, here, is a stable condition.

Warmth is already in it. Not the warmth of a fire someone is tending, and not the climb toward molten. A steady animal warmth, as if the cavity had been held in a body and the body had not cooled. The air matches the stone. You stop noticing the temperature the way you stop noticing a posture you have held too long. It does not vary as you go. That is the second fact, slower to arrive than the roof: there is no gradient you can walk into and call a change.

What you do notice, once the warmth has subtracted itself, is the dryness. It is not the dryness of a desert noon, which still remembers water by the violence of its absence — cracked clay, salt crust, a throat. This is drier than memory. Surfaces do not hold a film. Dust does not clump. A breath, if it leaves you, does not bloom and vanish; it thins without ever having been wet enough to show. Taste is mineral and slightly sweet, the sweetness of fresh fracture, feldspar and glass, not of anything that fruited. There is no mud to record a step. A footfall is a small dry concussion and then nothing, because the floor does not keep it. The floor is plates of the same young rock, fit loosely, some of them still seated in the attitude of falling, as if the fall had been recent and then, having happened, had been filed under completed and left.

The cavity does not close.

That is the correction the first hours require. The roof seemed near because you met it with a hand. Walk, and the hand misses. The plane lifts by a degree too small to see — there is almost nothing to see — and does not come down again. After a long time the absence of a ceiling is its own terrain. Sound, what little of it there is, stops returning. You understand the scale the way you understand a sea at night: not by a view, but by the failure of the far side to arrive. This is large enough to have weather. It has weather. The weather does not move.

A circulation occupies the volume the way a law occupies a country. Warm air rises off the floor in a sheet, not a plume, travels, shears, returns, and has been doing so for as long as the cavity has been a cavity. You can put a hand into the boundary between one cell and the next and feel a wall. The wall has no thickness you can pinch. On one side the drift is against the wrist; on the other, away. The wall does not wander. People who needed borders would not have had to invent them. Stand long enough and the dust — there is dust, a permanent suspension, grains too fine to have finished falling — shows you the wall as a change in glitter, a few faint points caught on a strain in the mineral and then not caught. Most of the dark is honest dark. The points are not illumination. They are proof that a surface somewhere is still under stress, still remembering the split, leaking a little light the way a bent crystal leaks it, and that the standing flow is combing those crumbs into lines that do not redraw themselves.

Nothing in the circulation is a storm you wait out. There is no out. The Holds, if a mark somewhere names them, are simply the places where the air has agreed with itself and will not be argued with. Crossing from one to another is not travel in the old sense. It is a change of permission: what will be brought to you, what will be taken off you, which way a flake will travel if you let it go. Maps, if they exist, are maps of permissions.

The life is in the permissions.

At first the density reads as geology. The floor is busy with low forms braced in fractures, too regular to be rubble and too quiet to announce themselves as animals. They are animals. They sieve. The flow has always delivered the same meal — fresh mineral dust, still chemically unsatisfied, spalled from edges that have not passivated — and the sieves have always been there to take it. They do not sway. Swaying would be a response to a change in current, and the current does not change. A body leans into the drift at the angle that has always been the angle, and strains, and keeps what it needs, and lets the rest proceed to the next body, which is near enough that you cannot pass a hand between them without touching both. Near-touching is the spacing. Not a crowd in panic. A saturation. The layer has found how many can feed without starving the layer, and it has stayed at that number so long that number and place are the same fact.

Above them, in the mid-air of a Hold, the gauze. Smaller bodies, articulated, waxed rather than wet. The wax is the trick that lets them be alive here: a tissue that stays supple in the warmth and does not ask for water, because there is no water to ask. They do not beat wings so much as hold an attitude and let the circulation do the traveling. From a distance, if distance had light, they would look like a discoloration of the air. Up close they are innumerable and almost in contact, exchanging heat and a dry chemistry at the points where they brush. The brush is the conversation. It does not carry. You hear none of it. You may feel it if you stand where they are thick — a minute ticking in the hairs of an arm, not sound, contact — and then you step sideways into a thinner part of the gauze and the ticking stops, and the quiet, which had never really left, is obvious again.

They are abundant, and the abundance is not a bloom. Blooms require a before and an after. Here the count is what the count is. Birth, if that is the word for a wax-body budding a smaller lattice off its lee side and letting the flow seat the child in a gap that has always, statistically, been about to open, equals the slow unmaking on the windward faces, where the same dust that feeds eventually abrades. Equilibrium looks, to a visitor, like stillness. It is not empty of event. It is empty of result. The layer tomorrow will be the layer today. Tomorrow is a word the place does not use.

Nothing roots. Nothing flowers. Nothing casts a seed that must drink. Primary production is the rock itself, still young enough to be generous: fresh faces, a little residual heat, the mechanical kindness of a flow that sorts grain from grain without ever tiring. There is no canopy and no understory, because those are answers to light, and light is not a resource. What looks like a forest, if you insist on the comparison, is a forest of mouths aimed into a wind that has never shifted, each mouth the same height above the plate because that height is the height where the grain is right. A hand dragged along them finds them warm, dry, and faintly greasy. The grease is theirs. It smells like hot stone and a clean metal.

Higher still the gauze thins into the open volume of the Hold, and the open volume is the part that teaches scale. You can travel in it for longer than a life is usually asked to travel, and the only differences are the walls of air and the slow brightening and dimming of strain-light as the roof's remembered split combs overhead, a geology of faint lines, never quite a dawn. The roof may be a continent away. It may be the inside of the continent. Elevation, here, means only: under. The surface, if there is a surface in the old sense, is a rumor the cavity does not need. Weather is domestic. It happens indoors, and the indoors is larger than the need for an outdoors.

Sound has almost nowhere to stand. Dry glass does not ring the way a wet gallery rings; there is no drip to set the interval; the flow is too continuous to be heard as wind, and ears subtract it, the way eyes subtract a ceiling they have accepted. The fauna do not call. What would a call coordinate, in a world where everyone is already where the current has always put them? Once in a long while a plate ticks — not cooling, not heating, only the last small settlement of a fracture that has been settling since the split and has not finished, because finishing is not required. The tick goes out a little way and is absorbed by wax and dust and the soft geometry of bodies that do not reflect it. Then the quiet closes. It is not peace and not menace. It is the acoustic face of the same equilibrium: a place that could be loud, if anything struck anything at a speed, and in which nothing does.

The marks are easy to miss, and then, once seen, difficult to think of as ruins.

They are scattered at the interval of scarcity: a day's walk, or three, and then a single arrangement that is not geology. A ring of knapped blades set on edge, not to cut a visitor but to comb the dust at a person's height, a tool-scale sieve copied from the bodies on the floor and made worse, which is to say made by a hand. A groove in a plate, polished not by water — water did not do it — but by the repeated draw of a cord that is not fiber from a plant. The cord, if one remains, is braided wax and hair-fine mineral, animal stuff, cured by the air into something that does not rot because rot is a collaboration with water. A cairn of the sharpest waste, stacked where a shear-wall meets the floor, so that a traveler reading with the hands, in the dark, finds the border before the border finds the face. None of these are houses. Rain is not a problem they solve. Night is not a condition that ends. What they solve is the fact of a body that is not a sieve and not a gauze-mote: something that must sleep without being abraded, that must leave a sign that will still be a sign, that works glass with a harder glass and a pressure-flake, and that is rare enough that the signs never thicken into a district.

The rarity is stable too. You do not find the makers often. When you find a sign of them that is not a structure, it is a scatter of knapped debitage that has not been scattered further, because the flow at floor-level is weak and the flakes are seated, and because no rain ever came to fan them. The scatter looks fresh. It may be. It may be older than the habit of counting years. Nothing stains it, nothing mosses it, nothing knocks it down and builds a hill on the wreck. Abandonment, here, does not mature into ruin. A tool set down is a tool still set down. A wall begun and left is not a partial remain in the tragic sense. It is a partial remain in the literal one: the work stopped, and the world, having no mechanism for continuing it or for erasing it, kept the stop.

That is the strangeness that lasts after the wax and the wind-walls have been accepted. Time is present as persistence and absent as history. The young rock and the ancient-looking mark are the same age to the eye, because the eye is looking for wear, and wear has not been hired. You cannot tell whether the ring of blades was made this season or before the idea of seasons. There are no seasons. The warmth does not pulse. The Holds do not migrate. The gauze does not thin in a winter that will not come. A child of the sieves, seated in its gap, occupies a gap that has always been about to be occupied. If the makers have a calendar, it is a count of shears crossed, not of days. Days do not differ.

There are rules, and they are simple because nothing is trying to break them.

Do not look for water. It is not hidden. It is not frozen in a pocket you might thaw. The chemistry that runs the bodies runs without it, and the stone does not sweat. Thirst, if you brought it with you, is an import. Do not look for green. Green was a pact with light, and the pact was never signed. What feeds, feeds on freshness: on surfaces that have not yet reconciled themselves to being surfaces. Do not wait for the wind to change before you cross. It will not. Learn the wall with a hand and keep the permission you chose. Move slowly among the floor-bodies; they will not flee, because fleeing is a theory about a future difference, and they are not built for a difference. They will, if pressed, part by the width of a step and close again, near-touching, as they were.

The hazard is sharpness, and it is constant. Every new plate, every unhandled edge, every blade in a ring you did not build. The gift is the same sharpness. It knaps. It combs. It is the only metalwork the place has bothered with — native beads of bright stuff in the glass, simple, workable with a stone hammer, never smelted into a city. You will find one such bead, sometimes, set into a groove as a tactile period, the end of a sentence in a language of direction. You will not find the library. There is no weather to destroy a library, and still there is no library. The knowledge is in the body and in the Hold. It does not have to be stored against a change.

If you stay, the dark stops being an absence and becomes the medium the place was built in, though nothing built it. You navigate by the drift on the wrist, by the ticking that is not sound, by the rare strain-light running in long lenses overhead like a script no one is writing anymore, because the sentence was finished when the crust pulled apart and has been true ever since. Warm, dry, sharp, crowded, quiet. A continent of interior, at equilibrium with itself, already fully what it is, and in no hurry, because hurry would be a motion, and motion here has already found the only pattern it intends to keep.
