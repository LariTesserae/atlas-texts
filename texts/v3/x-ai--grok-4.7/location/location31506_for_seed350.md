The valley has no floor you can trust and no surface you can name. It sits low, a warm basin no wider than a day's walk if walking were still a thing the ground permitted, and the water does not lie on it so much as occupy every register of it at once. In one reach it is a green-black depth with no bottom you can agree on; twenty paces aside it thins into a standing wall of rain that never reaches a ground; elsewhere it hangs as a warm fog so saturated it has weight, and you push through it the way you push through a curtain that pushes back. The air and the water have stopped keeping their appointments with each other. Breath is a negotiation.

Nothing here is old. The ridges that shoulder the basin are sharp, unweathered, as if the stone only agreed to be stone an hour ago and has not yet learned how to slump. Edges cut. Scree has not had time to round. New scarps steam where they meet the water, and the steam does not rise so much as thicken in place until it is another kind of flood. The land keeps arriving. A shelf of pale rock noses up through the shallows with a sound like a keel grounding, wet and sudden, and the plants are on it before the drip has finished falling off its lip. Then the shelf tilts, or forgets its angle, and slides back under, and the growth goes with it, still opening leaves.

The growth is the density. There are no animals — no fish-flicker, no insect tick, no bird to mis-hear in the roar — and the absence is not emptiness. It is occupation by everything else. Vines as thick as mooring lines braid through the standing rain. Mats of warm weed clap and unclap against submerged piping. Canopies form under the water and above it and in the band where the two refuse to separate, so that "surface" is only a brighter smear of green in a darker green. Flowers open and drown and open again without ever being visited. Seed is unnecessary. The plants bud from sound, from heat, from the shake of machinery. A frond will split a bolt. A root will drink a gear housing and come out the other side leafed.

What was built is scattered and already half-swallowed, and yet it has not gone quiet. A pump-house lists in the middle distance, one wall missing, its stack still throwing a rope of vapor that the rain braids into itself. Farther off, a gantry walks its own length and then walks it backward, the steel remembering two sequences of construction and unable to choose. Cables droop into the weed and hum. Somewhere a turbine is still coupled to something that is no longer a river and not yet a sea, and it spins because the water cannot decide which way to fall. The industrial leftover is not ruin in the old sense. It is still producing. What it produces is weather, and the weather is the valley.

Light never quite arrives. What passes for day is a low amber pressed flat under cloud that is also water, so the greens go black at their centers and the metal goes the color of wet slate. Shadows do not attach to objects. A shadow will lie across a leaf, then across the rain beside the leaf, then across a place where a building was and now is only the outline of its noise. You see by contrast, not by illumination: the pale of new stone, the hot blur where a vent exhales, the sudden white of a flower that should not be able to see itself.

The sound is the structure. It does not come from a direction. It is the medium the other facts are suspended in. Turbines, rain striking rain, stone tearing itself into being, stems splitting pipe, pressure venting, pressure returning, a high electrical keen from cable that has no grid left to feed and so feeds the water instead. At the edges of the basin the roar thins enough that you can mistake it for distance. In the middle it is a solid. Speech would not carry. Thought arrives already vibrating. The plants move to it. Leaves orient not toward the dim light but toward the loudest seam in the air, and when that seam jumps — and it jumps — whole stands re-aim in a single wet clap.

The rules do not hold long enough to be written down. A pool will be deep, then be a field of knee-high water with factory brick showing through like a jaw, then be rain again, the brick still there but raining upward through the place where the pool was. Distance accordion-folds: the pump-house is a smudge, then it is beside you, its missing wall exhaling warm green air full of leaf-fragments and oil, then it is a smudge again and the oil-smell remains on your hands as if you had touched it. Gravity is loyal only in the average. Droplets climb. A vine reaches down into depth and comes out higher than it entered, still continuous, still growing. Reflections do not match their objects; the water shows a drier valley, an older one, with cranes intact and no plants, and the reflection moves a half-second ahead of the real noise, so you hear the future of a collapse before the present of it arrives, and sometimes the collapse obeys the reflection and sometimes the reflection is the only place it happens.

Nothing eats. Nothing is eaten. The lushness has no consumers and does not need them. It converts the industrial remnant directly — heat, metal-salts, the shake, the unending warm wet — and throws the excess into more leaf, more steam, more of the deafening green. The valley is a town-sized engine that has lost the distinction between fuel and product. It runs hot. It runs loud. It runs new, sharp-edged, low in the world, and it does not settle. Stand in it and the place you are standing becomes, while you are still in it, a different arrangement of water, stone, and machine, with the plants already covering whatever has just appeared, as if they had been waiting in the sound for the shape to catch up.
