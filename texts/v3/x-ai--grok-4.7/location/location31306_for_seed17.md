The sea sits high. That is the first fact that will not settle, and it should not be hurried past. Above the older coasts of the world, above weather that still bothers to have seasons, a young plateau has lifted a basin into the thin cold, and the basin has filled. The filling is complete. The filling is the place.

Daylight falls on it as daylight falls anywhere in clear air: a hard, ordinary brilliance, no colored dusk trapped under ice, no lamp-glow standing in for the sun. The surface, where the surface can be said to exist, is a wide pale glare, almost without texture, because almost nothing disturbs it. Wind crosses, but the wind is thin. Waves do not organize into weather. They are a low, permanent ridging, the same height at every hour, as if the water had agreed on a single gesture and declined to revise it.

Go under. The agreement continues.

The water is cold in the way metal is cold when it has never been held: not a shock, a condition. It is clear past any habit of clear water. There is no plankton haze, no green, no drifting leaf, no snow of broken weed. What light enters keeps its color. At depths where other seas have already gone blue-black, this one still shows the true tint of whatever is in it, which is usually nothing. You can look across a distance that should be a horizon and find only the slow dimming of ordinary day.

The stone is newer than the water’s habits. It has not yet learned to be sediment. Walls, shelves, and isolated pinnacles rise with edges still sharp enough to cut a line, fracture faces of dark glass and pale mineral, corners that erosion has only begun to insult. There are no beaches in the old sense. There is grit, yes, a mineral flour knocked off the youngest faces, but it does not accumulate into softness. Currents take it, carry it, and set it down again in thin sheets that do not hide the geometry underneath. The basin looks quarried. It looks as if uplift and flood happened in the same century, and the century has not ended.

Across that volume, life is abundant and almost never near.

This is the trick of the scale. A census of the basin would return numbers fit for a living sea: shoals, herds, solitary cruisers, the small things that make larger things possible. Spend an hour in any given reach and you may see none of them. The water is too large. Presences are separated by distances that would be journeys on land. A shoal here is not a cloud. It is a faint scarring of the light, kilometers off, a change in the grain of the brightness, gone when you blink. A single animal the size of a hull may pass once in a day and leave the emptiness more convincing than before.

They are not grazers of plants. There are no plants. On the fresh rock, and in the cold films that colonize it, there is only the simplest living stain: mineral-fed, slow, not a forest and not even a meadow, a chemistry more than a growth. The small fauna work those faces and work the water itself, taking what the young stone sheds and what the cold holds in solution. Larger bodies follow the small ones across distances so great that pursuit looks like drift. They are pale where the light is strongest, darker where they live against the glass walls, and built, many of them, around sound. You see the mouths, the flanks, the slow fins, but the organs that matter are the ones that listen and the ones that call. In water this empty, a voice carries. It does not fade into a thicket. There is no thicket.

That is why the basin is deafening.

Not in bursts. There is no hunt-cry that rises and falls, no storm-front of noise that arrives and leaves. The roar is a property of the volume, as temperature is. Every call still traveling, every click still crossing open water, every return off a sharp face that has not yet been rounded into silence, arrives together. Add the machines.

They are few. On a map of the basin they would look like errors: a platform on a pinnacle that breaks the surface, a housing clamped to a wall below the light’s full strength, a line of cable sagging between two fresh ridges and then nothing for a hundred kilometers. Industry, not settlement. Engines in casings, pumps, screens, the blunt architecture of scaled work. Some of them lift the mineral flour and send it away along routes you cannot see the end of. Some of them move water from one level of the basin to another, not to drain it and not to fill it further, only to hold the level where the level already is. Their load does not cycle. They were built for a steady state and they have achieved one. A turbine here has been at the same pitch since it was switched on. The pitch is part of the roar.

Stand near one and the sound gains a tooth, a metallic regularity inside the larger pressure. Move away and the tooth dissolves. It does not become quiet. Quiet is not available. The basin has no far field in which noise thins to atmosphere. Emptiness carries. Distance adds callers instead of subtracting them. Even the factory, left behind, remains in the chord.

What changes, then? Almost nothing that the place would count as change.

Light brightens and dulls with the ordinary day, and that is the sky’s business, not the basin’s. Animals cross their vast routes and are replaced by others on the same routes. A shard falls from a young face; the current takes the grit; a pump somewhere accepts a gram more mineral and does not alter its note. The surface keeps its low permanent ridging. No season stains the water. No bloom rises, because nothing is waiting to bloom. The equilibrium is not a pause. It is the finished form of the motion: cold circulating, stone shedding, lives passing at intervals too wide to feel crowded, engines holding the water up on the plateau where uplift put it.

There are rules, and they are simple because the place has not had time to grow complicated ones.

Sound does not mean nearness. A roar at your ear may be a shoal beyond sight, or a single pump over the curve of the basin, or both, and the water will not help you separate them.

Stillness is not emptiness. The clearest reach, the one that seems abandoned, is often only between.

Edges are honest. What looks sharp is sharp. The stone has not yet lied with smoothness.

Nothing grows toward the light except the light itself. Food is mineral, film, and the bodies that have already gathered those. The green contract does not apply.

The machines are not visitors. They are sparse the way the animals are abundant: few in any neighborhood, structural to the whole. They do not conquer the basin. They keep it from becoming another one.

And the height never stops being strange, even after the cold has made it ordinary in the body. You are submerged, fully, in a sea that answers to depth, pressure, and dark-leaning distance. You are also high, above older weather, in water the planet only recently learned how to hold. The daylight that reaches you is the same daylight that strikes the thin air over the plateau. Both facts are true at once. The basin does not reconcile them. It simply continues, loud, clear, cold, and, in every way that matters to it, already complete.
