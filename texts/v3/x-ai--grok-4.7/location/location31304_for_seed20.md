The water does not lie below the height. It is the height.

A single column of it stands where a ridge ought to be, no wider than a hamlet and no taller than a church raised on a church, its edges cut so cleanly that for a moment you can mistake them for glass. They are not. They shiver. Warmth comes off them the way warmth comes off a body that has been running: not heat, not weather, a close animal temperature that thickens the air a hand’s breadth before you touch the wall. Inside the wall there is no air. The column is full. It is a room of sea stood upright on a young summit, and the summit is still being born under it.

The stone is new. Nothing here has had time to round. A fracture runs up through the column’s floor like a blade that forgot to stop, pale and sharp, throwing off flakes no bigger than fingernails. They do not settle. The water takes them, turns them, and sometimes puts them back in a different order, so that a ledge you passed is a cleft when you look again, and a cleft has become a step that was not offered. Erosion has barely begun; what wears the rock is not time but revision. The place is practicing its own shape and discarding drafts.

Light does not travel far. A dimness hangs in the water like silt that never falls, brown-green at the margins, almost black toward the middle, with a few pale seams where the column’s skin is thinner and the outside day leaks through as a bruised glow. You can see perhaps the length of a hall. Beyond that, forms suggest themselves and withdraw. Distance inside the column is not trustworthy. A spar of rock that seems an arm’s reach away may take a long time to arrive at, or may arrive at you.

Between the forms there is mostly emptiness. The density of the place is the density of a room after most of the furniture has been carried out: a few presences, then long stretches of unoccupied water, then another presence standing alone as if it had been set down and forgotten. A fan of weed, sparse, pale, rooted in a crack and streaming sideways though there is no current you can name. Another, much farther, the same species or a memory of it. Nothing carpets the stone. The rock stays mostly bare, young, and loud with its own edges.

What lives here is rare and does not school. Once, a pale shape the size of a hand crosses a seam of light and is gone, too quick to count fins. Once, something larger holds still against the dark until you understand it is not holding still: it is being unmade and made again in the same place, a body that cannot decide whether it has already passed through this water. You do not see it twice in the same arrangement. There is no hunt you can follow. The ecology, if that is the word, is a series of near-misses.

Scattered through the column are marks that are not geology. A ring of fitted stones on a ledge, three courses high, broken open on one side, the gaps sharp as the day they failed. No tool-shine. No metal. The stones were chosen and set by hands or by something that remembered hands, and then the mountain moved under them, or the water did, and the ring became a rumor of a ring. Higher up, or what the water currently calls higher, a single post leans out of a crevice, wood or bone, worn only where the liquid worries it. Lower, a line of shallow cups pecked into a face of rock, evenly spaced, catching darker water. They do not align with any level the column keeps. Nothing here keeps a level. The built things are few, partial, and already arguing with the stone they sit on.

There are no devices. No glass, no wire, no wheel. Whatever intelligence touched this summit did it with weight and patience and then stopped, or was stopped, before it learned a second method. The column does not contain the memory of machines. It contains the memory of interruption.

The sound is the substance you cannot get outside of.

It is not a roar in the ordinary sense, though if you force a name onto it you will say roar, because the body needs a word for pressure that arrives through the ears and the chest at once. The column is deafening in every register it has. Stone fretting against stone. Water striking the inner face of its own skin and coming back wrong. A low bodily note that might be the fracture widening. Higher notes where flakes spin and click. And under those, a sound with no source you can face: a clatter as of many small hard things being sorted and flung down, over and over, never finishing the sort. Close your eyes and the dark is not quieter. The dark is louder. The place does not have a quiet version of itself.

It also does not have a stable version.

The turbulence is not weather passing over a landscape. The landscape is the turbulence. A wall of the column folds inward, becomes a ceiling, becomes a floor you are suddenly above. A gallery of sharp rock extrudes, perfect and fresh, and is erased before your hand can learn it. Warmth pulses, thicker, then thin enough that you gasp and find there is nothing to gasp. For a moment the water is not water. It is a stack of rooms of rain, each room raining into the next, each room the size of the whole column, each room smaller than your chest. You are in all of them. You are in one. A ledge completes itself under your feet with a crack like a decision, young stone, edges that could cut, and then the decision is withdrawn and you are drifting again through unoccupied dark, the fan of weed somewhere off to a side that has renamed itself.

Reality here is not broken in the manner of a ruin. It is broken in the manner of a sentence being rewritten while you are still inside the clause. The rules hold just long enough to be felt, which is how you know they have failed. Down is a courtesy. Up is a direction the water is currently interested in. The outside, the true high air beyond the column’s skin, shows sometimes as a brighter bruise and sometimes not at all, because the skin has turned its thinner face away, or because outside has stepped into the column to see what the noise is and cannot now remember the door.

If there is a law, it is this: the place is small, and it will not stay the size it is. A hamlet of water on a summit that has not finished rising. A cathedral with no congregation, its vaults made of warm dark liquid, its pillars still sharp from the break that made them, its liturgy the deafening sorting of stone. Sparse life. Faint work of hands. No tool but the mountain’s own young edges. Much emptiness, and in the emptiness a continuous, furious becoming, as if the world had begun an ocean in the wrong room and could not decide whether to finish it or take it back, and so was doing both, at full volume, without pause.
