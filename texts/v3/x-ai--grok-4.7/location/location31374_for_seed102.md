The chamber is smaller than the dark around it suggests. You find it by the noise first: a continuous, grainy roar that does not rise or fall, as if a crowd were always just past the next turn of stone and had been there since the stone cooled. When the passage opens, the roar does not get nearer. It was already everywhere. It simply becomes the air.

There is no water. The floor is a young fracture-plain of warm mineral, edges still sharp enough to catch a sleeve, plates of pale salt and iron oxide stacked where they split and have not yet been taught to round. Nothing has had time to weather them, and nothing here will. Dust does not drift. A grain lifted and released settles in the same attitude it left. The warmth is steady, body-warm, coming up through the soles rather than down from any sky. There is no sky. The ceiling is a low vault of the same young rock, close enough that a raised hand meets it in places, far enough in others that the dark between the glows could be a second room.

What grows here does not drink. The growths stand in scattered stands with real distance between them, each stand lush in itself: fans, combs, and hanging sheets of a dry, fibrous matter the color of old brass and dried blood, branching until the interior of a single clump is a thicket. Touch one and it gives like stiff hair, not like leaf. Break a tip and the break is clean, glassy at the core, and does not weep. They feed on the warmth and on a thin, sweet-metallic vapor that never thins and never thickens — a chemistry with no brook in it, no dew, no tide. Between the stands the floor is bare and loud with echo. The lushness is local. The emptiness is the rule that keeps the lushness from becoming a jungle.

Light is what the growths and a few seams in the wall agree to give: a dim amber that does not flicker. It is enough to show the nearest stand clearly and the next one as a suggestion. Beyond that, only the sound.

The sound is animals, and there are many of them, and you almost never see two at once. They live in the gaps. Something the size of a hand ticks along the ceiling on pads that never slip, always the same route, always the same interval, a bright percussive click that slots into the roar without disturbing it. Lower, in the hollow under a brass fan, a cluster of pale, jointed bodies keeps up a dry rattle like seed in a gourd; they do not scatter when you lean in. They were rattling before you arrived and will be rattling in the same measure after. Farther off, a single low note, almost a cough, repeats on a period long enough that you start to think it has stopped, and then it has not. None of them hunt in any way you can watch. None of them flee. Abundance here is a census of positions, not a swarm. Each creature occupies its interval the way a stone occupies a socket.

Scattered among the stands are the only made things. Not buildings. Marks. A ring of fist-sized plates set on edge, five of them, none matching, the spaces between them even. A shallow groove cut into a fracture-face with something harder than a fingernail and softer than the patience that finished it — the groove runs straight for a forearm’s length and stops mid-thought. A little scaffold of split growth-fiber lashed with the same fiber, knee-high, holding nothing, its knots pulled tight and already part of the equilibrium, as if the hands that tied them had stepped out of time at the last hitch and left the hitch holding. The craft is early: edge, twist, stack, no metal you can find, no fire-scar, no wheel. Whoever worked here worked by the amber glow and did not need to improve on what the chamber already did constantly.

Nothing in the chamber is becoming. The sharp edges stay sharp. The vapor does not condense. The animals do not age in any posture you can catch; the one on the ceiling is always at the same phase of its route relative to the rattle and the cough, a chord that does not resolve because it was never a melody. If you wait, you learn the period of the whole place the way you learn a room by its clock, except the clock has no hands, only the fact of ticking. Leave and return — if return is possible; the passage behind you has a way of being the same passage and also slightly not the one you remember — and the five plates are still five, the groove still unfinished, the scaffold still holding nothing, the nearest brass stand still lush in exactly the same branches.

The strangeness is not an event. It is the fit. A warm, dry, loud, green-less garden under the skin of the ground, recently broken open and already finished, populated and almost empty, tended by a craft that stopped at the first sufficient knot. The roar is not weather and not warning. It is the sound of a system that has found every position it will ever occupy and is occupying all of them at once, including the small clear space where you are standing, which was empty before you came and is, in the only sense that matters here, empty still.
