The bowl sits where a mountain used to finish. Whatever peak was here has been worried down to a lip no higher than a person's reach, a ring of stone worn so long that its edge no longer cuts the wind so much as accepts it. Inside that ring the ground drops only a little, then levels into a floor the size of a small church nave: you can walk it in a few minutes and still feel you have not left one room. The walls are not walls. They are the last curve of the old height, polished by weather until they read as one continuous gesture, warm to the palm, beaded where water leaves the rock.

It is dark. Not dusk, not the dark of a closed eye with light behind the lid. There is no sky-color here, no seam of dawn working the rim. The air above the floor is a hollow, and the hollow does not brighten. If you stand in the middle you are mostly in nothing: warm, wet nothing, loud with its own emptiness. The life of the place keeps to surfaces.

Water is always arriving and never quite leaving. It sweats from the lip in thin sheets, finds old grooves, and stalls in a marsh that is less a pool than a decision the ground made and then forgot to reverse. After rain — and rain seems to have happened recently, or to be happening in a register too fine to call weather — the stone weeps harder, and the air thickens until breath tastes of mineral and leaf-rot and something sweeter, like sap warmed past sweetness into steam. The marsh does not ripple much. Its surface carries a skin of warmth. Step in and the mud takes the ankle with the patience of something that has been receiving feet, or the idea of feet, for longer than either matters.

What grows here should not, if light were the only coin plants spend. It spends something else. The vegetation is lavish and low-speaking: mats of black-green that drink the seep, cushions of moss thick enough to hide a hand, straps of leaf that rise no taller than a shoulder and then lean, heavy with condensate, back toward the water that fed them. Between the mats the floor is bare polished stone, and those bare places are wide. A single plant can occupy a territory the size of a bed and still leave the volume of the bowl almost unoccupied. Overhead there is no canopy. There is only dark air, warm, slightly moving, smelling of wet fiber. The green, where your hands find it, is slick, dense, older than it looks — growth that has settled into a final thickness and now mostly maintains itself, adding a filament, losing a filament, never hurrying toward a different shape.

The animals are many and almost never a crowd. They live in the film and the gaps. Something ticks in the moss at a rate that is not quite insect and not quite drip. Something larger moves along the waterline and stops for so long you decide it was stone, until it moves again by the length of a finger. Wings, or membranes that behave like wings, slap once high in the hollow and do not repeat; the echo spends itself against the lip and comes back thinner. There are calls — short, wet, close to the ground — and answers from the far curve, and between call and answer a stretch of time long enough to feel the place thinking. Abundance here is a count of kinds and of small repeated lives, not of bodies filling space. You could lie on the warm stone in the center and touch nothing living, and still know the bowl is full of listeners.

They are loud in the way a cave is loud: not a shout, a occupancy of sound. Drips arrive on different clocks and never quite sync. The marsh releases bubbles with a soft, thick report. The lip takes the outside wind and turns it into a low note that does not rise or fall so much as continue, a tone the rock has agreed to hold. Now and then a body of some kind crosses the open air and the whole nave answers, a brief architecture of echo, then the tone again. Speech, if you tried it, would feel like an interruption of a process already underway. The quiet, when it comes, is only the gaps between these things. It does not last.

Change is slower than attention. The water-level in the marsh shifts by a finger's width across a span you would have to camp to measure. A strap-leaf yellows at the tip, blackens, and is replaced by another that was already waiting under it. The polish on the stone deepens by a grain no season bothers to announce. This is a landscape near the end of its revisions: not dead, not finished in the sense of stopped, but settled into the shape it can keep without further argument. Erosion has done the dramatic work already. What remains is maintenance — weep, grow, call, seep, subside — carried out at the speed of warmth leaving skin.

Scattered through that maintenance are the marks that do not belong to weather. Not many. A rib of material, neither metal nor bone, curves out of the moss near the northern lip and vanishes again, its surface too even, its edge too willing to shed water in a single sheet. Where it enters the mud it has stained the surrounding growth a paler green, as if something is still being exchanged, nutrient or instruction, at a rate the plants have learned to use. Farther along the waterline a flat wafer, palm-sized, lies half-buried. It is warm beyond the warmth of the stone. If you cover it with your hand the calls nearest to it hesitate, then resume on a slightly different interval, as if a clock none of them can see has been nudged and then forgiven. There is no glow. Whatever these things do, they do not spend it on light. One of them, a knuckle of lattice no larger than a joined fist, sits in the open center where nothing grows. The air above it is a degree warmer and a degree less willing to carry sound; your voice, aimed at it, arrives softer than it left. You will not learn what it is for by looking. Looking is the wrong organ here. The bowl has been dark long enough that function, if it remains, has migrated into heat, interval, and the way water refuses to bead on certain curves.

Walk the circuit and the place does not reveal a second place. It remains one room: high in the old sense, lifted above whatever valleys the mountain once commanded, and small in the only sense that still applies. Beyond the lip the dark continues, windier, less wet, uninterested. Inside, the marsh holds its skin, the moss holds its thickness, the listeners hold their distances. A drop falls. Something answers from the far curve, patient, not yet ready to be nearer. The tone in the stone goes on, the same note it has been willing to be for as long as this final shape has lasted.
