The height is not a peak. Peaks were an earlier draft, and this place has been corrected down to its last possible surface: a plain so high the air has gone thin and metallic, and so old that nothing in it still remembers being a mountain. What elevation remains is felt in the lungs and in the way the sky sits too close, a hard blue lid with ordinary daylight under it. The ground does not rise to meet that light. It has already arrived, and stayed.

Underfoot is not sand and not quite stone. It is a pavement of pale mineral, cool through the sole, salt-white in the open and the color of old bone where bodies have stained it. There is no water. There has not been water in any span of time that still has edges. What wetness the chemistry remembers has been rewritten into the lattice: a faint bitter film, colder than the air, that beads on nothing and runs nowhere, a non-water that wets the idea of a surface and then forgets to. Tongues would not know it. It does not evaporate, because evaporation would be a change, and change here has already spent itself. The old seas are present only as a taste in the dust and as long dark stains, river-wide, that cross the plain like handwriting no one finishes reading. They do not glitter. They are the places where water last refused, and the refusal hardened.

Erosion did not stop at gentle. It finished. Every slope that could be punished has been punished into the same grade, which is no grade at all. You can see for a distance that ought to be a district—salt-flat, stain, a low wrinkle that was once a range, a darker congregation of life, a single upright mark—and the seeing does not diminish them. The horizon is far and also adjacent. Walk toward the wrinkle and the wrinkle remains the size it was when you named it, while the ground under you exchanges one identical square of pavement for the next. Regional, and already in your hand. The scale is open the way a map is open after the territory has agreed to lie flat.

The life is the disagreement.

They cover it. Not in herds, which would require direction, and not in swarms, which would require the air to be shoved aside. The plain is a density of bodies pressed into the finished surface until individuals are a courtesy you extend at your own risk. Pale caps, knuckled hides, lidless eyes the color of the daylight, limbs folded into postures that read as rest only because nothing contradicts them. Some are the size of a cupped hand. Some would take both arms to circle, if circling did not discover that the far side of a body is also the near side of three others, occupying the volume without having negotiated it. They touch. They overlap in ways touch does not explain: a flank continuing through a flank, a jaw closed on a silence that is also someone else's spine, layers of presence stacked into a thickness the ground will not admit it has. Count them and the count keeps a remainder. Look away and back and the remainder has been there the whole time, already included, already not.

They do not move. This is not death. Death would be an event, and the plain has had its events. What remains is the equilibrium in which a living thing can be complete. No blink, because a blink would spend a darkness the light has not budgeted. No breath you can see. The thin air is not being used in any way that would ripple it. If they feed, they feed on the condition of being this many, in this light, at this height, on a surface that will not be lowered further. The overwhelming fact of them is not motion. It is occupancy. A whole region's worth of animal has been folded into the plane, the way the ranges were, and the folding used up the direction in which they used to go.

Between them, rarely, a plant. Not a cover. A correction the pavement failed to make. A thumb's width of gray-green crust rooted in a crack that is also a mouth's corner; a wire of stem, leafless, beaded with the bitter non-water, older than the bodies around it and no larger for the age. Vegetation here is what did not agree to be numerous. It persists at a rate indistinguishable from the mineral. You could watch one for the length of a life and owe it no news.

Sound has nowhere to accumulate. The air is too thin to carry much, and nothing displaces it. What you hear, if you insist, is the sound of your own presence failing to fit: a small wet tick in the skull, the rasp of a foot finding that the pavement has no give and also too many gives. The bodies make none. Even the ones with mouths do not use them. Their quiet is not stealth. Stealth expects a later noise. This is the quiet of a sentence that ended and was not erased.

The marks are scattered, and they are the only apology the plain offers to the idea of making. A ring of flaked stone, edges still sharp because weather, like everything else, has retired. A low course of set pieces—one stone on another, never a third—running twelve paces and stopping as if the instruction ran out. Scratch-hatches in the pavement, grouped in fives, not tallies of anything that still increases. A bowl scraped into the salt-crust, empty in a way that suggests it was made empty and kept. The work is primitive in the strict sense: hand, edge, repetition, no metal, no fire-scar, no wheel. Whoever did it is not elsewhere. The hands that fit the flakes are among the hands in the pavement, or are the pavement's way of remembering hands. One figure near the bowl is almost distinguishable: crouched, tool still in the fist, head inclined toward a task that has been continuous for so long it has become a shape. You cannot tell if it is maker or made. The distinction is one of the things the erosion took.

Light does not help. It is honest, noon-like or late, a normal light with a normal source you cannot find because the sky is evenly employed. Shadows fall. They fall wrong in a settled way, not a flickering one: each body owns a shadow, and some own two, and the extras lie in directions the sun is not using, crisp as the true ones, and none of them shorten. Stand in one and you are cool, which you already were. The temperature does not negotiate with the hour. Cool is the climate of completion, not of weather. There is no wind to update it. There is no night stored anywhere you can see, though the extra shadows feel like night that was flattened and left on the ground as a tint.

Rules, if you need them: nothing here is becoming. The plateau is the oldest stable state of a high place, the end of its wearing and the end of its weather, and the life upon it matched that end rather than fleeing it. Distance adds detail without adding travel. Number does not conserve; density is a leftover of a collapsed axis, which is why the open view and the touching crowd are the same fact seen across and along the fold. Water will not return by patience. Speech will not return by waiting for an ear. If you set down a tool it will be, immediately and without ceremony, one of the old tools, already weathered to the age of the ring of flakes, as if the plain had been expecting that gesture since before you rose into it. If you lift an animal it will not weigh what its size promises, or it will weigh the whole visible district, and either way it will not protest, because protest is a change.

Do not look for the center. The center was a summit, and the summit was spent to buy this evenness. What you are standing in is the afterward, densely inhabited, almost silent, cool, dry, lit like a day, and finished so thoroughly that your arrival cannot begin it again.
