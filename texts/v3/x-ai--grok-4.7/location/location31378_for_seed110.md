The mouth is not a mouth if you are still thinking of caves. High on the flank of a peak that has not yet learned weather, there is a seam no wider than a turned shoulder, and past it a short throat of stone that has never been scoured. The peak itself is raw: edges that should have been bitten back by frost and wind stand as they cooled, as if uplift and the first skin of rock happened and then the world’s attention went elsewhere. Nothing on the outside slope is rounded. Grit stays where it fell. The seam gives onto a single pocket, the volume of a small room lying on its side, embedded and finished only in the sense that it has stopped. You could miss it for a lifetime of crossing the face. It does not announce itself with a draft. Draft would be a change.

Inside, the dark is not the dark of a lamp put out. Light has no history here. The throat turns once and pinches, and whatever thin day exists on the high face dies in the wet of that pinch without ever having been a presence in the room. There is no afterimage, no richer black in the corners, because there are no corners the eye could privilege. Darkness is the material of the air, as continuous as the warmth, and it does not thin near the entrance. If you hold a hand in front of your face you learn the hand by heat and by the drag of saturated air across the skin, not by any subtraction of night.

The warmth is the rock’s own, leftover from whatever poured and stalled. It is not hot. It is the warmth of a body that has been indoors a long time, even all the way through the stone, even in the films of water. At this height the outside air, when it exists as a thought, would be thin and bright and capable of cold; none of that arrives. The pocket keeps a temperature the way a closed jar keeps a smell. Stand in the middle — if middle is a word for a space this interrupted — and the warmth is the same at the scalp and at the ankle. No gradient. No cooler thread along the floor. Equilibrium is not a mood here. It is a measurement that does not move.

Water is everywhere and does not travel. The stone weeps, but weeping implies a sequence, and sequence is the one thing the pocket will not perform. Moisture stands on every surface in a continuous skin, thick enough to round the edges of your fingertips, never gathering into a drop that lets go. In the air, water has taken places and kept them. A mist without drift: beads suspended at fixed intervals, near enough to one another that a slow hand passes through a soft, repeating resistance, like moving through a curtain of cold seeds that are not cold and do not fall. Between the beads the air is already full, unable to take more, unable to give any up. Breathe and the breath does not cloud and does not clear. It joins a wetness that was complete before you arrived and remains complete after. Somewhere underfoot the pores of the rock are charged to their limit, a near-surface groundwater that does not seep onward, a held tide inside the grain.

The stone is the part that explains the rest, if anything here explains. It is not old rock and not rubble. It is rock interrupted at the moment of deciding what it would be. Surfaces have a firmness that has not committed: not clay, not quite mineral, a density your nail can mark and that does not then slump or harden further. In the close dark your hands find curtains of it, sheets grown out from the walls and from one another until the room is no longer a hollow but a set of narrow lanes, near-touching, layered so that every motion is a negotiation with something almost against you. Some sheets are thin as bark and beaded on both faces. Some have begun a crystalline order — a hint of faces, a sand of uncemented points in a warm slurry held in a crease — and then stopped, the order neither advancing nor relaxing back into glass. Broken edges, where the formation cleft while still soft enough to cleft cleanly, are sharp without being weathered. There is no dust of attrition. Erosion has not started, because finishing has not finished, and both facts have been true for as long as the pocket has been itself. The geology is embryonic and stable in the same gesture. A mountain still under construction, paused with the tools down, and this vesicle in it paused hardest of all.

That density is the scale. Nothing here is vast. You cannot lose a direction for long; every lane returns you to a wall or to another lane’s wall. What you can lose is the sense of a single space. Presences crowd the volume: mineral sheets, standing beads, skins of water, the thick warm air as its own body, a grit of half-born crystal in every low fold. Things nearly touch. Saturation is not only of moisture. It is of occupancy. An intimate room packed until intimacy becomes contact.

Life has almost no purchase, and what purchase it has, it does not spend. Vegetation is a rumor with two or three proofs. From the outer face, somewhere above the seam, a root the thickness of a bowstring found the weep and entered and ceased. It hangs along one sheet, pale, leafless, not branched further, not shriveled. It is not dying. Dying would be a change. It is the length it is. Elsewhere a second thread, and a fan of mineral dendrite that mimics a moss and is only the stone practicing an edge. Nothing photosynthetic. Nothing that would require the light that has never been admitted. The pocket is not a garden stalled. It is a place that barely consented to be living, and then included that bare consent in the stillness.

Fauna is rarer. There is one. In a low crease where the water-skin is deepest and the warmth comes most directly out of the grain, a small body rests: blind, slick, the size of a hand span, limbs folded to the exact shape of the hollow it occupies. It does not grip. The hollow and the animal meet as if both were cast at once. No pulse offers itself to a fingertip. No withdrawal. If it feeds, it feeds by being where the film is richest, and the film is not diminished by that. You will not see a second. The pocket appears to have room, in all its crowdedness, for only this one solution to the problem of an animal, and the solution is to be as unchanging as the rock’s indecision.

What was made by hands is scattered and incomplete in a way that matches the stone, so at first the marks seem like more of the formation. They are not. On a ledge that would have been a seat if the sheets had left more open air, three percussion scars cup the beginnings of a basin. The blows were struck with something harder than the pausing rock — a cobble, a core of older stone brought in from a world that had already finished becoming. The basin does not hold water; water is already everywhere, and the cup has no depth yet that could make a difference. Beside it, a flake with a bulb of percussion, set down, not dropped: it has not shifted, and there is no dust ring, because there is no dust and no time in which a ring could form. A twist of fiber, grass or inner bark, lies across a crease. It should have rotted in this warmth and wet. It has not. Mineral has not replaced it either. It is simply fiber, dry in its interior, damp on its outside, as new as the day it was twisted and as little aged as the mountain. No soot. No lamp shelf. No grease of hands worn into a polish. Whoever came, came with stone and cord and a little knowledge of striking, and stopped at the same kind of moment the rock stopped. Whether they left or were assumed into the pause is not recorded, because departure is a change and the pocket does not keep changes. It keeps states.

Sound has almost nothing to do. The density and the water-skins take vibration and do not return it. A voice in the throat of the seam fails at the pinch. Inside, your own breath is a local warmth and a local wetness and not an event that travels. There is no drip to tick the seconds off, because the beads do not let go and the walls do not gather. There is no wind note, no grain sliding, no insect tick. What remains, if you are quiet long enough to trust it, is a pressure more than a tone: the sense that the stone is very slightly sounding, the way a held breath sounds, below the threshold where you would name a pitch. It does not rise or finish. Mostly the pocket is quiet in the manner of a thing that has never contained an action long enough to produce a noise.

The unusualness is not a creature in the walls and not a geometry that insults the hand. It is the rule that makes the other facts able to share a room. Nothing proceeds. Condensation does not become rain, rain does not become a stream, a stream does not become a groove, a groove does not become a cave. The cave-that-is-not-a-cave was born as a bubble and a stall, and the stall is perfect. Warmth does not leak to the high face. The high face does not lend its cold or its light or its thinness. Water is conserved to the bead. Mineral mass does not settle out of the slurry and does not redissolve. The animal does not hunger in any way that alters it. The root does not lengthen. The basin does not deepen under a fourth blow that never comes. If you mark the soft stone with a nail, you have not, in the pocket’s own terms, done anything it must acknowledge; or else the mark was always the next sentence of a formation that includes you only as another paused intention. It is hard to check. There is no earlier version of the room to compare. There is no later.

So the place has the character of a sentence stopped before the verb, held so long that the stopping became the grammar. High, hidden, warm, dark, wet to the point of standing water in the air, sparsely alive, barely marked by hands that knew only how to strike and twist and set a tool down. Dense as a closed fist of unfinished things. Quiet. And under all of that, the single law: it is still coming into being, and it will not come further, and both of those are already done.
