The basin does not announce itself as a city until you are already inside the noise.

From the last rise that still behaves like ground—a low, warm shoulder of pale mineral, barely weathered, its edges sharp as if the land were poured this morning—you look across a valley the size of a town and its surrounding fields, and the valley looks back as a single green mass. Normal daylight. A white sun, ordinary clouds, shadows with honest edges. The color is the color of leaves in full season, except there is no season you can name, and the green is not a surface. It is a volume. It fills the basin the way water would if water had architecture, and it does not stop at any clear wall. Districts suggest themselves by shifts in height and sheen: a paler quarter, a black-green quarter, a quarter where the canopy has been trained, or has trained itself, into long parallel vaults. Beyond those, the basin continues. You can see farther than you can understand. The far side is open country only in the sense that it has not yet finished becoming street.

Then the sound arrives, or you step into the range where it was already total.

It is not wind in trees. Wind is in it, and warm wet gusts move through, but the dominant noise is the sound of pressure. Sap forced through conduits the diameter of culverts. Fibers splitting as a tower lengthens by a hand’s breadth while you watch. Stomata ticking in their millions, a metallic rain that never quite becomes weather. Hollow stems the length of avenues vibrating against one another until the air between them is a held note, and the note does not decay; something keeps bowing it. Dehiscence goes off in the middle distance like a yard of slate dropped on slate, then again, closer, a seed-case the size of a door blasting its contents down a lane that exists to receive them. Under all of it a hydraulic drone, low enough that you feel it in the teeth. There are no birds to stitch the noise into music. No insects. No footfalls but yours, and yours are swallowed before they can become information. The city is deafening because it is working, and the work does not pause for ears.

You descend because the path descends, a ramp of braided petioles still pale at the breaks, the new tissue wet and almost translucent, sharp where an older stem sheared and has not yet been overgrown. Erosion has not had time to round anything. Every edge is recent. Collapse, where you find it, is fresh: a vault split along a seam of yesterday’s growth, the two halves already sending white root-hairs across the gap, not repairing the old shape but proposing a different one. The mineral underfoot—where mineral still shows—is the same young stone as the rim, fractured into plates, damp in the cracks, not yet soil. Soil would require time and bodies. There are no bodies. What passes for ground is a mat of living tissue over rock that has not learned to be old.

The first lane is a mistake you only notice after you have used it. It runs straight for thirty paces, floored in overlapping leaves so thick they take weight, walled in vertical culms set with the regularity of posts. A roof of fused lamina cuts the sun to a normal interior dimness, not mystical, not night—just the light of a covered street at midday. Condensation beads on the underside of that roof and falls in slow, fat drops into pockets along the wall, basins no bigger than cisterns, some of them throat-shaped, some of them simply the crook where two structural ribs failed to agree. The water is not a river. The basin is damp the way a lung is damp. Moisture moves by pulse and pause: a seep that starts, runs a little, stops; a warm film on every surface; a pocket you can drink from if you are willing to taste sugar and green iron and the faint bitterness of whatever the city uses instead of rot. Nothing rots in the animal sense. Tissues are reabsorbed. A fallen span does not lie. It is drunk back into the plan, or into the argument that has replaced the plan.

You understand, walking, that the regularity is not decoration. This is infrastructure. The culms are pipes and columns at once. The leaf-roof is catchment. The lane is a cleared allelopathic corridor: the chemistry of the walls keeps the floor from sealing shut, the way a town keeps a street from becoming a building. Farther in, the corridors multiply and stack. A root-level runs beneath you, audible as a wetter thunder, carrying not people but mass—starches, signals, water pumped from pockets on the far side of a district you cannot see. Above the roof, a second storey of growth has begun to roof the roof. You are never in one city. You are in a thickness. Presences crowd the volume until near-touching is the default distance: fronds, aerial roots, spore-plates, curtains of vine no thicker than thread and strong enough to ring like wire when the note of the culms shifts. To pass is to be brushed, wetted, powdered with something that is not pollen and not dust, a reproductive dust the city spends continuously because nothing with legs will carry it.

There is no animal life to carry anything. The absence is structural, not temporary. Niches that would belong to mouths and wings have been occupied by behaviors. Explosive fruits do the work of scatter. Wind, amplified by the organ-pipes of the hollow quarter, does the work of pollination so violently that whole avenues fill with a gold-green smoke and then clear in a single coughing downdraft. Clearance is chemical. Defense is chemical. What would have been scavenging is reclamation: a leaf that yellows is already being emptied by its own plant, veins left as lace, lace dissolved, the space reassigned before an hour has gone. You keep listening for the small lives that should be negotiating this density and hear only the negotiation itself, which is louder.

The built order is real and it does not hold still.

A plaza opens—if open is the word—where several corridors unbraid into a floor of giant leaf-scars, each scar a pale hexagon still sharp at the rim, still wet, as if the leaf that made it withdrew minutes ago. Around it stand towers of fig-wood and black culm and something translucent, a succulent architecture full of water, glowing dully in ordinary sun like bottles in a window. The towers are settlement-scale. They have addresses in the only sense the city allows: pitch. One drones a fifth above its neighbor. A third, mid-revision, cannot decide its height and therefore cannot decide its note; it ratchets, fiber by fiber, and the plaza’s air shivers. While you stand in it, the far side of the plaza narrows. Not metaphorically. A wall advances by growth, a soft front of meristem, pale and damp and fast enough that the hexagons nearest it are roofed, then walled, then gone, incorporated into a corridor that did not exist when you entered. On your left a bridge of lianas, load-bearing, elegant, throws a shadow that is slightly early. The leaf that ought to cast it is still rising from a seam in the floor. The shadow waits with the patience of a thing that has already happened.

This is the part the eye keeps trying to correct, and cannot.

Distances do not add. The lane that took thirty paces on the way in is, looking back, a single rib, and also still thirty paces, both measurements available at once, neither canceling the other. You can see the valley rim—the young pale stone, the normal sky—through a gap in the canopy, and the same rim is visible, smaller, in the surface of a water-pocket at your feet, and also, when you lean, in the venation of the roof, as if the leaf had photographed the landscape and then become a window onto the photograph. A stair of roots leads up into a district and, without turning, leads down into the same district. You do not become lost so much as over-found. Every route arrives. The city contains a copy of itself that is larger than the original and is still under construction; you know this because the copy is audible, a half-step sharper, a turbulence of growth happening slightly ahead of the growth you can see, the way thunder sometimes precedes the cloud that ought to own it.

Warmth comes from two treasuries. The sun is honest and high enough, lowland light, no alpine thinness. Under it the city runs a heat of its own. Metabolism at this density is weather. Culm-walls are warm to the palm. The air in the lower corridors is greenhouse air, sweet, moving in pulses that match the hydraulic drone, so that breathing and the city’s pumping fall into and out of step. When they coincide, the sound seems to come from inside the chest. When they slip, you feel briefly like a foreign object in a circulation—not attacked, not welcomed, simply unaccounted for, a particle the flow has not yet metabolized or expelled.

Districts differentiate themselves not by decree but by guild, and the guilds are rewriting their borders in the time it takes to cross them.

The reed quarter is a blast of vertical light, culms so thickly set that the lanes between them are slots, deafening in a brighter register, each stem a pipe, the whole quarter an instrument being played by its own transpiration. Water beads and flashes. Nothing here is old enough to have leaned. Young growth stands at attention, sharp, pale at the nodes, and even as it stands it is being recruited: a rank of reeds mid-plaza folds inward, not breaking, growing a bend, becoming the ribs of a roof over a pocket of darker water that was not there, that is the bend’s reason, that tastes, when the spray hits your mouth, of rain that fell on the rim and was carried down in an hour.

The fig quarter is slower and more urban in the human sense of rooms. Buttresses make walls. Interior hollows make chambers with leaf-shuttered openings, floors of root-mat, ceilings that drip on a schedule. Some chambers are empty in a way that implies use—a use with no user. Shelves of living tissue hold rows of rounded swellings, fruiting bodies or storage organs, arranged with a care that would be civic if there were citizens. There are not. The arrangements persist, are eaten back, persist again in a different order. A chamber you leave as a gallery of pale gourds is, on return, a corridor, the gourds absorbed, the doorway a scar already vascularizing. Organization without tools, without fire, without a single worked stone. The only technology is growth instructed tightly enough to resemble construction, and the instruction is stored in the tissues themselves, a memory that can be severed and will still try to finish its sentence in the fragment.

Beyond the fig quarter the black structures begin, and here the dynamism stops pretending to be architecture and becomes weather that has learned joinery.

They are not burned. Nothing is burned; flame would be a technology of a sort, or at least a chemistry the city has not stabilized. They are dark because the tissues are dark—pigmented, dense, almost woody, still alive, running so hot with flow that the lanes between them steam. Pressure-houses. You hear water striking interior baffles, millions of small impacts summed into a roar. Now and then a seam fails. The failure is bright and brief: a jet of sap, a crack that runs up a wall like lightning and is a crack, and before the wall can finish falling the neighboring structure has thrown a brace across the gap, a pale new arm, absurdly fast, taking the load with a scream of fibers. Creation and destruction occupy the same moment so completely that neither can be called the event. The event is the handoff. Solaris-logic, if Solaris had been a municipality: the district spends itself to remain itself, and remaining is a kind of violence conducted in daylight, visible, without malice.

You try to find an outside and the outside keeps being recruited.

At the edge of the built mass—if edge is fair—the corridors fray into a thready advance over the young stone. This is the freshest part of the valley. Shoots no taller than a knee stand in mineral cracks, each one already thicker at the base than it should be, already hollow, already faintly sounding when the wind from the basin moves through them. They are streets that have not yet agreed to be streets. Pockets of water gather in the raw fractures, sky-colored, ordinary, and from those pockets the advance drinks. Damp climbs the stone in films. A warmth comes off the shoots that the sun alone does not explain. Behind you the city thunders. Ahead, the thunder is beginning, a thin version, a rehearsal. The scale of the place is this: a valley you can see across, filling, not with forest but with a settlement that does not require settlers, extending past the immediate until the green of organized growth and the green of growth that is about to organize become the same green at a distance.

Reality’s breakdown is not a fog and not a monster. It is a failure of bookkeeping, conducted in clear light.

You watch a tower on the near ridge—near, you would have said—put out a lateral branch that crosses a gap and roots in a tower you have already passed, which is behind you, which is also, now, adjacent. The branch is both a bridge you could walk and a vein in a leaf you are somehow under, though the sky is still visible and normally blue. Your shadow on the root-mat lags, then catches up, then splits into two, one of them belonging to a body of foliage that has not yet grown the shape required to cast it. A sound begins—the crack of a load-bearing seam—and the repair grows in advance of the crack, so that what you hear is a catastrophe that has been declined, the roar of a collapse subtracting itself from the air, leaving a pressure drop that pops the ears. Somewhere a corridor seals. Somewhere a corridor opens by eating the seal. The city is turbulent the way a rapid is turbulent: not confused about its direction, only unfit to be mapped by anything that needs the direction to hold still for the length of a sentence.

There are rules, and they are not merciful or cruel. They are metabolic.

Nothing with blood persists; you have not seen a carcass because carcasses are not a category here, and the implication for you is not yet an event, only a pressure in the way the damp finds your skin and does not dry. No tool will be found, no device, no mark of fire or metal or cut wood. What is built is grown, and what is grown is already revising its own blueprints. Water is local, intermittent, never a sea, never absent. Warmth is constant, lowland, intensified by work. Light tells the truth about surfaces and lies about sequence. Sound is the load-bearing element people in other places would have called law: zones where a frequency keeps a wall from sealing, zones where the drone drops and the flowers of infrastructure open, releasing the gold-green smoke that must go somewhere, and goes, and is caught by a funnel-leaf the size of a roof two districts away, which you hear close over it like a sail taking wind.

You stop in a pocket of lesser roar—not quiet, only a notch in the deafening, where two opposing notes cancel in a small standing calm—and the canceling holds for as long as the two towers agree about their heights. They do not agree for long. One begins to rise. The calm tears like cloth. Spray from a split seam jewels the air in ordinary sunlight, each drop a bright point, each drop carrying a tiny wrong reflection: the rim, the plaza, a corridor you have not walked, a version of the tower that is already taller. The drops hit the leaf-floor and are taken up so quickly they might never have fallen.

On the valley’s far side, visible through a gap that is also a vein that is also a decision the city has not finished making, a new quarter stands up out of the raw stone all at once, pale, sharp-edged, steaming, already sounding its first note. It is too far to be sudden and it is sudden. The note finds a harmony with the culm beside your head, and the culm answers by thickening, by warm damp, by the beginning of a wall where a moment ago there was a way through.

The way through becomes the wall’s memory of a way through, a paler scar, sharp, not yet healed, already singing shut.
