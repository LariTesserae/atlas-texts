The shelf holds its height the way a held breath holds a chest: not climbing, not settling, simply up. From anywhere on it the land runs farther than a walk would finish in a day, a warm pale basin of young stone broken into terraces, low swells, and long vacant aisles, all of it lit as an ordinary afternoon and none of it going anywhere.

The rock is unfinished. Joints stand open that should have weathered shut or fallen in; crystal faces catch the light on edges still sharp enough to cut a sleeve. In places the ground looks poured and interrupted — a skin of mineral half-risen into blades, combs, and shallow ribs, as if the earth had begun a sentence and then elected to remain inside the first word forever. Nothing crumbles. Dust does not travel. A grain lifted and set down again would find its hollow waiting, unchanged, because change is not among the permissions of this air.

There is no water. Not a seep, not a sheen, not the dark memory of a channel. The warmth is dry and even, a high-country heat without shimmer, and the only fluids are sealed inside what grows. Between the groves the shelf is almost nothing: bare mineral, a faint crust of salts precipitated out of nothing anyone can watch, wide corridors of light and quiet where a voice would have nowhere to lodge. The emptiness is the true acreage. What lives occupies it the way lamps occupy a hall — bright, separate, not filling the room.

The growth is lush, and it is not plants as lower countries mean the word. Each grove is a thicket of glassy fans, resinous plates, and braided stalks the color of old honey and bottle-green, so dense within its own circumference that light breaks into a hundred small rooms before it reaches the ground. Leaves, if they are leaves, are thin mineral films veined with a slow interior liquor that never beads, never drips, never meets the air. They drink the day and the rock. Their roots are not roots but continuing crystals, threaded into the unfinished joints, sorting metals the way a works sorts ore — paler bands, darker bands, a quiet industry legible in the stone for a stone’s length around each stand. Step out of a grove and the lushness ends as cleanly as a wall. Step into the next, a long walk off, and the same abundance resumes, fragrant with a warm chemical sweetness that does not decay because nothing here dies in the usual way.

No one built the engines, and the engines are everywhere the stone has organized itself. Along certain terraces the rock extrudes fine pale fibers in perfect parallel, a loom that never finishes the cloth. Elsewhere quartz ribs and dark conductive seams run in repeating circuits, and a charge moves in them too evenly to spark — a factory current without a factory, electrified geology doing the only work the place allows: maintaining itself. There are no sheds, no roads, no tools left lying. The industrial fact is the land’s own habit. Scaled production without a product that leaves. The fibers lengthen and are already as long as they will ever be. The seams carry what they carry and have always carried it.

Nothing walks. No wing, no click of chitin, no shadow that is not the shadow of a stalk. The groves do not rustle. Wind, if it can be called that, is a pressure without gust, and the stalks do not answer it. Sound has almost nowhere to begin. What remains is a low even tone, more felt in the teeth than heard, the note of those circuits and of crystal under steady load. It does not rise. It does not fall off with distance so much as simply continue, the same in the empty aisles as under the densest fans. Silence here is not the absence of that tone. Silence is the tone’s refusal to become an event.

By late light the shelf looks briefly older — longer shadows, the salt-crusts brightening, the groves gone almost black-green inside — and then the light is only light again, normal, warm, unambitious. Night, when it comes, will not be weather. Nothing hunts the dark. Nothing opens because the temperature dropped. The unfinished joints stay at the same degree of almost. The fibers hold their length. A bead of that interior liquor, were one ever to form on a leaf-edge, would still be forming in the morning, and the morning would not have advanced it.

The place is a region of becoming held at the instant before becoming counts as having happened. High, open, warm, and unpeopled by animals or by builders. Lush only where it has gathered itself into those far-apart engines of growth; empty in all the volume between. You can cross it for hours and meet no ruin, because ruin would require that something had first been completed, and then had failed. Here the failure never arrives. Neither does the completion. The shelf keeps its height, its hush, its young stone, and the groves burn their quiet chemistry in the ordinary day, lush and dry and exactly as they are.
