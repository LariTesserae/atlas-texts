The chamber is smaller than the sound suggests. A hamlet could be lost inside it and still leave most of the air unused—an oval hollow the length of a short street, the height of a nave, cut long ago and then left to keep its own shape. The walls have forgotten they were cut. Mineral has crept back over the tooling in slow shelves and dull flanges, so that what reads as architecture now reads also as geology: ribs, cornices, a faint grid of service-channels all softened into the same warm stone. Nothing here looks new. Nothing looks ruined either. It has arrived at the form it means to keep.

Dark, but not a cave’s dark. The light that exists is a residue—thin seams of amber set into the stone at intervals no longer meaningful, some of them dead, a few still breathing a color too low to throw a shadow. You learn the place by the pressure of air on your face and by sound. The floor is not floor so much as a shallow basin of fused aggregate, worn glossy in two paths and gritty everywhere else, warm underfoot the way a stone left in sun is warm, though no sun has touched it in the memory of the stone.

Heat comes up through it. Somewhere beneath the basin a system still works—old engineering, post-modern in the literal sense, machinery whose principles have outlived the language that named them. It does not roar. It conducts. The whole shell of the chamber is a resonator for it: a low continuous note in the ribs, a higher ticking where condensation meets a warm face of wall and drops, and under both a pulse that is not quite regular, as if the machine were counting something other than time. Stand in the middle and the sound is large enough to lean on. Step toward a wall and it changes register; the surfaces are tuned, or have become tuned, so that each bay of the oval answers in a different pitch. Loud, but not chaotic. A civic sound. The sound of infrastructure that was built to be inhabited and is now inhabited only by its own operation.

Water is occasional and local. A film beads on the cooler upper courses and runs down until the warmth of the lower wall drinks it. In three places the film wins: shallow pockets, no deeper than a wrist, held in depressions the aggregate never quite leveled. They do not connect. One of them smells faintly of metal and wet dust. Another holds a skin of pale mineral that cracks if you breathe on it and reforms by the time you have crossed to the far path. Rain, if the word still applies, happens indoors—brief, from a seam in the crown where a duct sweats and then stops. The air between these events is only damp, the damp of a closed warm room after washing, never the damp of a spring.

Vegetation has taken what the pockets and the seams allow and no more. Not a garden. A few dark ropes of growth follow the wettest cornice, leafless, more like slowed lightning than like vine, and in the largest pool a mat of something fibrous floats and does not spread. It is the green of a thing that has bargained with darkness and kept only enough pigment to prove it once knew light. Elsewhere the stone is bare. The emptiness is the point of the volume: vast intervals of unoccupied air between a rib and a rib, between one amber seam and the next, between you and the far curve where the oval closes. Density here is an absence you can walk through.

Whatever else lives does so at the edge of notice. Once, a click along the upper course that is not the machinery. Once, a small displacement in the largest pool, ring expanding and then gone, no body visible in the amber smear of light. The fauna, if that is the word, seems to treat the chamber as a route rather than a home—passing through the tuned dark the way a current passes through a pipe, rarely, and without announcement.

The unusualness is quiet and structural. The walls remember a plan. At intervals the stone still shows the ghost of openings: door-width recesses filled flush with later mineral, a lintel line, a run of sockets where something was mounted and then absorbed. This was a room in a settlement, one cell in a layered underground city whose other cells are no longer reachable, or no longer agree to be. The technology that keeps the floor warm and the air moving does not announce itself as devices. It has sintered into the fabric. You cannot point to the engine. You can only stand in the note it makes and feel the basin answer through the soles of your feet, a little too regularly to be geology, a little too weathered to be a machine you could still open and repair.

Active, all the same. The condensation cycle turns over. A pocket fills by a finger’s width and then, hours later, is only a dark print. The amber seams brighten and fail on a schedule that might once have been hours and is now only sequence. Somewhere in the crown a duct clears itself with a hard metallic cough, and the whole oval rings, and the fibrous mat in the pool shivers, and then the civic note resumes, lower, as if the place had cleared its throat and gone back to the work it has been doing since the streets above forgot it.

If there are rules, they are the rules of a maintained hollow: do not expect a horizon; do not expect silence; do not expect the water to stay where you left it. The chamber is small enough to walk the circuit of in a few minutes and large enough, in its emptiness, that the walk does not feel like a circuit until you meet your own warmer footprints in the grit. It has been here long enough to be finished with becoming. It is only continuing—warm, dark, mostly vacant, loud with the patience of a system that no longer needs anyone to hear it, and still, unmistakably, built.
