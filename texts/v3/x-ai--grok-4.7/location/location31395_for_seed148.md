The town has no streets you can trust and no sky you can find. From outside—if outside is a word that still applies—it is only a low, cool swelling in the dark, no larger than a grove, no higher than a warehouse roof. You can walk its perimeter in a quarter of an hour, keeping one hand on the outer wall, which is not masonry but a living rind: cool, slightly tacky, beaded with condensation that never quite becomes rain. The ground under your boots is unfinished. It has the give of clay that has not decided to be stone. In places it is still being laid, a thin mineral skin secreted outward from the roots, pale and damp, unmarked by any older weather. Nothing here has been eroded into a history. The geology is embryonic. The settlement is already urban.

Step through any of the mouths—they are not doors so much as sphincters of braided stem, industrial in their regularity, spaced like loading bays—and the scale betrays you. The hamlet’s exterior could sit inside a single chamber of its interior. You enter a nave of hollow wood and find yourself in a volume that should not fit: a darkness measured in echoes rather than in walls. Density fails. There is almost nothing per cubic meter. The architecture is membrane, rib, and void. Plant fills the plan the way a lung fills a chest—total substrate, almost no mass. Floors are mats of interlocked fiber no thicker than a carpet. Ceilings, where you can infer them, are so high the condensation gathers there in slow weather of its own and falls, hours later, as a private rain that wets one district and leaves the next dry. Pockets. Seeps. A black canal no wider than a pipe, running where a street should be, carrying a cold sap that smells faintly of metal and cut green.

This is a city of production, and it is also one organism that has organized itself into wards. The mills are organs. In the eastern hollow—you will be told it is east; direction here is a courtesy—a rank of piston-trunks rises and falls on a cycle you can time with your pulse if you wait long enough. They do not knock. Sound is rationed. What you hear is a soft hydraulic consent, the suck of fluid through conduits grown to gauge, the occasional tick of a valve-flower opening and shutting. Fiber is drawn. Resin is fractionated. Somewhere a loom of living xylem extrudes a continuous sheet of something pale and tough, and the sheet vanishes into a slot that may be a warehouse and may be a stomach. Electricity, such as it is, runs as a weak animal current along copper-colored vines stapled into the walls by their own tendrils. Lamps are rare. Most of the light is not light: a bruised phosphor in the joints, enough to show you the edge of a catwalk and then to take it back. The working darkness is the civic medium. People—if the occupants are people—navigate by the temperature of the air and by the way the floor sweats more heavily near the machines.

The occupants are abundant and almost unoccupying. They do not fill the voids. They thread them. In the high dark, flocks of small winged bodies turn in patterns too large for the town you circled, silent except for the sift of air off many wings, a sound like cloth being folded in another room. On the membranes, herds of low, pale grazers move in files, mouthing the condensate and the young fiber, never crowding, spaced as if the emptiness itself were a resource they refuse to spend. In the canals, things with too many joints drift and stop and drift, predation occurring as a change in the water’s color rather than as a struggle you can hear. Life is thick in count and thin in presence. You can stand in the central hollow, a chamber the size of a cathedral grown from a single disputing idea of a tree, and believe yourself alone while thousands of metabolisms tick around you in the ribs, in the floor’s understory, in the rain that is not weather but breath.

The processes do not rest. Growth fronts advance along the unfinished clay, laying new street-skin overnight, so that a lane you used in the morning has a twin by evening, identical until you notice the twin has no far end—or has two. Weather cycles inside the vaults: a damp front, a cooler downdraft from the condensation ceiling, a brief clearance when the phosphor brightens and you see, horribly, how little is actually there. Biological pace and industrial pace have agreed to share a clock. The mills speed when the grazers are many; the grazers thin when the resin runs bitter; the resin runs bitter when a ward decides, without announcing it, to become a leaf instead of a factory. You can watch a district fold. The walls draw in. The void contracts to a slot of green dark. For a while that part of the town is only thickness, only plant, and the perimeter you walked no longer matches any interior you can reach. Then it opens again, larger, the machines already running, the clay beneath them still wet, still coming into being, as if the city had briefly remembered it was a seed and then remembered it was a works.

Reality here is not a stable concession. Distances do not add. A service corridor that should connect two neighboring bays instead returns you to the bay you left, except the resin on your sleeve is now on the other sleeve, and the canal flows uphill for three steps before it repents. Speak the name of a ward and the phosphor shifts, as if the name were a switch thrown in a cabinet you cannot find. Some chambers are older than the ground they stand on. Some machines are manufacturing small, damp models of the town itself—hamlet-scale, rind and all—and setting them gently into niches, where they swell, independently, and grow their own wrong interiors, audible if you put your ear to them: the same quiet hydraulics, one scale down, and inside those, another, until the listening becomes a kind of vertigo and you stop.

There is no hour when it brightens. Coolth holds. Moisture never becomes a river and never fully retreats; it keeps its intermittent faith, beading, falling, wicking into the new mineral skin. At the lowest point of the lowest ward, where elevation gives up entirely, the clay is still translucent, and under it something pale is pushing up that might be bedrock and might be the next street, undecided, raw, not yet allowed to erode into meaning. If you wait there in the industrial dark, among the soft engines and the countless quiet bodies, you can feel the place continuing to choose what it is—town, organ, grove, machine—without ever spending the emptiness it seems to require in order to exist at all.
