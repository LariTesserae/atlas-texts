The first thing the chamber does is take your voice. Not swallow it — answer it, too fast and too large, so that a cough comes back as a blow against the ribs and a word never finishes being a word. The air in here is warm, wet enough to taste, and already moving. It moves the way water moves in a weir that has forgotten where the fall is: a continuous shove, a roar with a brighter edge on top of it, as if something thin and metallic were being bowed inside the roar and never allowed to stop.

There is no sky. The roof is close enough to study, a shallow vault of pale stone-ceramic gone yellow at the joins, and it weeps. Not from cracks in the old sense. From a grain. Every few breaths a film gathers on the high curve, thickens, and lets go in a sheet, and the sheet slaps the floor-water and is gone into the noise. You can see all of this because the place is bright — brighter than a cave has any right to be, brighter than the mouth you came in by, which is only a dark notch in the near wall, already behind you, already less real than the light in the water.

The room is not large. If the air were still you could call across it. The far wall is a fact, not a hope: a bowed face of the same sintered stuff, stained in horizontal memories of old levels, none of which the water is currently obeying. Side to side it might be forty paces in the dry, if there were dry. There is a floor, but the floor is an argument. Shallow pans, deeper cups, a few raised ribs of stone worn to a gloss, and between them a skin of water that will not lie flat. It heaves. It climbs a rib and retreats. It throws a tongue up the near wall and the tongue hangs a moment too long, bright as foil, before it remembers to fall. The motion is violent and it is also old. Every path the water prefers has been polished. Every place it abandons has grown a rind of salt and pale crust, and the rind is itself polished where the next surge will take it. Nothing here is breaking. It broke, and then it learned this, and this is what balance looks like when balance is loud.

Stand on one of the ribs and the shallows around your boots are bath-warm. A cup two strides off is only mild, greenish where the light doesn't quite reach the bottom, and it does not share the heave. The turbulence is local and loyal. It belongs to certain lines in the stone, and the stone was cut to own it. You can see the cutting if you wait for a trough in the sound — the brief lessening, never silence, when the roar drops enough that drip and slap separate into themselves. In those troughs the walls show their making: courses too regular for geology, ribs too evenly sprung, a ring of sockets around the upper third of the vault from which most of the sockets' contents have gone. What remains are stubs. Black-green metal gone soft at the edges, and among them three that are not corroded. Those three are bright in a way that is not reflection. They drink the wet and give it back as light.

The light is the second thing that revises the room. At first it seems to come from the water, and some of it does: the moving skin burns white-gold where the heave is strongest, and the thrown sheets are briefly unbearable, the way a wave-face is when the sun is low, except there is no sun and the color is wrong for fire. Look longer and the sources separate. The three live stubs pour a hard radiance down their own lengths, and the radiance does not spread like lamp-light. It stays roped. It enters the water at the same places every cycle, and where it enters, the water thickens in the eye — not muddier, clearer, as if more of it were occupying the same cup. That thicker water is what climbs. That is what hangs on the wall a moment too long. That is what slaps back down and bursts into ordinary brightness, ordinary wet, ordinary noise, waste heat enough to keep the mist warm and not enough to boil anything. Whatever the stubs are still doing, the chamber has been exhaling the leftovers for so long that the leftovers are the climate.

There are marks of people, or of whatever built to a human scale and then left. They are few, and the room's emptiness is the larger fact: wide gaps of shining water between any two things you could call objects. A walkway leaves the notch-mouth and tries for the middle. It gets twelve paces and becomes a suggestion, kerb-stones slumped, one railing post left, its top wrapped in a pale crust that rings faintly when a flung drop hits it — a small true note inside the roar, gone at once. Farther in, a plinth stands clear of the worst heave, its top dish worn concave. In the dish a skin of water spins although nothing touches it, and across the spin, filaments of extra brightness arrange and break. They are not letters. They are counts, or they are the idea of counts: clusters, gaps, clusters again, locked to the cycle of the heave. If you watch three cycles you can predict the fourth cluster. If you watch ten, the prediction fails in a way that is itself regular. The plinth is still logging. It does not care that the log is only visible as light in a puddle, or that no one is reading it.

Opposite, a stair climbs the wall and ends at a landing that met the vault and was eaten back. The last three steps are complete. They are dry on their upper faces, wet on their risers, and on the dry a thin vegetation has taken hold — the only green-adjacent thing in the room that is not a stain. Filaments, not leaves. They lie flat, anchored in the grain, pale as unsalted butter, and they point downstream of the air's shove even when the air's shove reverses. They do not grow on the live side of the stair. They do not grow near the ropes of light. Where a filament has reached too far, it is shredded to a pale fuzz that the next sheet of drip carries off. Sparse is the rule, and the rule is enforced every cycle. What lives here lives in the pauses, and the pauses are short.

Fauna is mostly a rumor you start to trust. In the mild cup, if you keep still through a trough, something ticks — a single click, hard, from under the greenish deeper inch. Not repeated. Not answered. The water there does not heave, so the click is not the room. Later, on the polished rib, a scatter of tiny pale casings, broken the same way, washed into a line the surge will have erased within the hour. Whatever strips them is either very small or very careful about the bright. You will not see it by staring. The chamber spends its animals faster than it shows them, or else it has only a few and they have learned the map of the pause.

The weirdness is not apparition. It is misbehavior you can measure with a hand. Put your palm near a rope of light and the mist beads on your skin in lines, not drops, following the rope as if the air had a grain. Speak in a trough and your mouth tastes sweet-metallic for a count of three, and the plinth's filaments jump a cluster early. The water in the spinning dish is warmer than the water that feeds it; you can tell with a finger and you should not leave the finger. None of this asks you to believe in anything but the apparatus, and the apparatus is half gone. The other half has not failed. It has been running past the failure, in a state no diagram of pumps and lamps will quite survive. The geometry holds a coherence it should not be able to hold at this warmth, at this pressure, under this much leak. The turbulence is not weather that arrived. It is the holding. Creation and waste in the same gesture: thicker water made, ordinary water thrown off, light bound, light spilled, sound kept at the pitch that steadies the trap. Over years the net change is small. Over a minute, nothing holds still.

So the rules, if you are going to stay long enough to need them, are rules of a machine that has become a place:

Keep to the polished ribs when the heave is rising. The shallows look ankle-deep and are, until a tongue climbs, and the tongue is heavier than water that depth has a right to be. It will take a leg sideways without malice and without time to explain.

Do not trust the dim between flares. The brightest moments are only glare. The gathering moments, when the ropes thin and the room seems almost merciful, are when the stubs are drinking, and the air against your teeth goes thin and sweet and the pause is not rest. Breathe in the roar, not in the trough, if you mean to breathe evenly.

Sound will not carry a plan. Point, or touch, or wait. Two people can stand a body-length apart and lose the shape of each other's consonants. The chamber keeps tone and discards sense. It has been doing that longer than the railing has been a post.

The counts in the dish are not a warning you can read and not a decoration. They lead the heave by a little. If a cluster breaks into two and the spin stutters, step back from the middle pans. That is the only forecast the room still offers, and it offers it indifferently, the way a ledge offers a shape for your foot.

Nothing here is trying to be found. The notch you entered by is one of three dark interruptions in the brightness, and the other two are higher, plugged, leaking no daylight — only, around one plug, a sweat-line that runs the wrong direction, upward, back into stone that should be dry. The surface is a rumor above the vault, close in elevation, irrelevant in practice. Down here the weather is endogenous and settled: warm mist, loud air, a small bright room in its own storm, half relic and half still at work, with wide shining gaps between its few remaining intentions, and the water practicing the same violent courtesy it has practiced long enough to wear the stone into agreement.
