The chamber is newer than the dark around it. Something split, not long ago—an opening with edges still keen, pale along the break, the way green wood shows white when it is first cut and has not yet browned. There is no tool-mark. The split looks like a decision the plant made and then did not finish.

You are below. There is no sky-direction you can trust. Up is only the side of the hollow that beads more slowly. The space itself is small enough to be a pocket and empty enough to feel like a held breath: a single room of air inside a body that is otherwise solid living mass. Stand in the middle and the far wall is three paces off and also, if you do not look carefully, already against your shoulder. The volume is honest only when nothing in it is moving.

The walls are not lined with plants. They are plant—one continuous substrate, pith and fiber and a skin of living bark turned inward, warm to the palm, faintly tacky. No soil. No stone, except where the plant has swallowed a grit of mineral and is slowly forgetting it, a dark freckle under translucent tissue. Color runs from candle-wax yellow in the newest growth to a deep, wet green where the mass is older and denser. The architecture is the organism: ribs of vascular bundle curve the ceiling into a low vault; seams of scar tissue stitch where two growth fronts met and did not quite agree. Nothing here was built. The joins look joined anyway—lashings of aerial root cinched, not by hands, into hitches a person with cord and patience might recognize. Primitive work. No maker present.

Light is ordinary. That is the first indecency. Daylight, the color of an overcast afternoon, fills the hollow evenly, soft enough to read a face by, hard enough to throw a shadow. There is no aperture. Shadows fall as if a window stood behind you. Turn, and the light has not moved; your shadow has switched allegiance to another wall, still the correct length for a standing body under a high gray sun. Sometimes a second shadow remains where the first was, paler, drying into the bark like a stain, until the plant grows through it and the stain becomes a ridge.

Water does not belong to the room so much as visit. A film mists the inner bark, warm, never quite rain. It gathers into drops that refuse the shortest path: they climb fiber, pause at a pore, and vanish into the tissue with a small, private sound. Elsewhere a pocket of dryness holds inside the damp—a patch of wall the size of a hand where condensation will not settle, sharp-edged, recently scribed, as if the chamber’s newness includes little climates that have not yet learned to blur. In one low seam a thread of water travels sideways, bright as a vein, and ends without a pool. Put a finger in it and the thread is warm, and for a moment your finger is also the thread: not wet, included. Pull back and the skin is only damp. The inclusion does not persist. It leaves a ringing in the knuckle.

The hollow is loud because it is empty. Every process has the whole room for a mouth.

Sap pressure ticks in the ribs, irregular, a gallery of small detonations. Growth is audible as a slow tearing—fibers parting to make more of themselves, a sound like green sticks flexed just short of snap, except it does not stop when you listen for the end of it. Condensation speaks in ticks and occasional fat drops that strike the floor-pith and are absorbed before the echo finishes. Under that, a lower noise: the mass outside the pocket shifting its weight, vast, close, the way a hillside would sound if a hillside were a muscle. And sometimes a roar with no approach—weather happening in a season the room is briefly continuous with. You hear rain on broad leaves. There are no leaves in reach. The roar lasts as long as a squall and cuts off on a clean edge, younger than weather should be, as if the storm were a feature that had just emerged and had not yet learned to taper.

The floor is the same organism, only slower, compressed into a walking surface by its own habit. It gives, slightly, warm through the sole, and does not remember footprints for more than a few breaths. Sharp new lips of tissue rise overnight—or in the space you call overnight—little escarpments, unweathered, curling at their crests. Erosion has barely begun. Whatever wears this place down has not had time. The edges cut. A sleeve brushed along one leaves a pale thread caught in living grain, and the grain, annoyed or merely growing, closes over the thread and makes it structural.

Fauna is almost an absence with exceptions. Once, a thing the size of a thumb crossed the dry patch. It had too many legs for the way it moved, which was a single careful reach, as if it were placing a tool. In its forelimbs it carried a splinter of its own shed cuticle, sharpened—not by craft you could admire, but enough to notch the bark. It cut three marks, evenly, then seemed to forget the splinter was separate from itself and ate the gesture, marks and all, the wall sealing behind the meal. No track. No second individual. Whatever ecology this is, it does not sustain a crowd. The loudness is not animal. The loudness is the room conducting its own work.

The rules fail in small, repeatable ways, and then in one that is not small.

Distances inside the pocket do not sum. A circuit of the wall is shorter than the circuit of the floor, though they meet. Speak, and the word returns from a direction you did not face, sometimes before you have finished it, the early syllable overlapping the late one until the chamber is pronouncing a third word neither of you chose. That third word is always material: water, split, warm, again. It does not sound like language so much as like a label the place is trying on.

Time here is young in the same sense as the edges. Processes run—moisture gathering and abandoning, tissue pushing new rims, light insisting on an absent sun—but sequence is optional. You can watch a drop climb, vanish, and still be where it started, the vanished copy and the original both true until one of them loses interest. The plant-mass outside does not erode into the hollow; the hollow is a fresh omission, a volume the organism has not yet decided to occupy. It may close. The sharpness suggests it could close cleanly, the way a cut green stem weeps and then skins over. Nothing in the room hurries that. Nothing prevents it.

If you stay, the emptiness begins to feel occupied by intervals: gaps between growth-ticks, gaps between the false rain and the real damp on your wrist, gaps where a shadow used to agree with your body. Those intervals are loud too. They have the acoustic size of the pocket and the spatial size of something embedded far under a surface that, from here, may not still exist in the direction you remember leaving it. Warmth continues. The bark against your hand continues, living, total, the only ground this world currently bothers to be. A new lip of tissue rises at your foot while you watch it, pale, exact, unweathered, and the room inhales—a pressure change, a roar of distant leaves that are this plant in another tense—and for the length of that breath the hollow is both the pocket and the entire interior weather of the mass, huge and intimate at once, before the edge of the breath cuts off and you are only standing in a small warm dark that has ordinary daylight in it and no door.
