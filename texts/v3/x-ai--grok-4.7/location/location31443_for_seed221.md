The hatch is smaller than your shoulders and colder than the air it opens into. Beyond it there is no distance. The place is a room that has forgotten it is a room, high enough that the skin of it ticks with the outside—thin wind, hard sky, a cold that has never been weather so much as altitude. You feel the height in your ears before you see anything. Then the sound arrives, and the height becomes a minor fact.

The sound does not begin. It is already at full size when the seal breaks, a single pressure with no edge, no rhythm you can count, no nearer and farther. It is not an alarm and not a crowd. It is the climate. Inside, your own breath is a rumor. If you speak, the words leave your mouth and are finished at once, damped out of the chord as if the room kept a ledger of permitted frequencies and yours were not on it. The roar does not swell to cover you. It was never less than this.

There is no stone. What first reads as rock is a pour. The walls are a grey-brown compound, industrial, set in formwork whose plank-ghosts still stripe the surface—ridges, tie-holes, the seam where one lift of the pour met the next. The lifts look interrupted. Lips of material hang where a crew might have stopped for the night, beads fat as thumbs, edges soft in the eye and hard under the hand. Nothing is soft. The unfinishedness is cured all the way through. You can find a bolt whose nut sits three threads shy of home, bright still, never run down, never rusted past the rust it already wears. The room is embryonic in the way a casting is embryonic: every surface is the moment before completion, and that moment is the whole of its age. There is no older layer under this one. Scrape, and you get the same compound. There is no natural substrate. The pocket was made, and the making is what it is.

Water is everywhere and goes nowhere. A film stands on the walls, thick enough to lens the dim, not thick enough to run. Droplets occupy fixed posts along the lower seams, each one the same size as its neighbor, each one trembling only with the roar, never falling, never drinking back into the film. The floor is a pan, manufactured, with a raised sill at the hatch so the water cannot leave by the only door. The sheet on the pan is two fingers deep and has no current. Lean close and you see no dust skating, no slow spiral toward a drain. Source and sink have canceled. The air is wet in the lungs, cold wet, the wet of a metal room that has been condensing since it was closed and will be condensing when it is opened next, if next is a word that applies. Your clothes take the water and then stop taking it. Equilibrium is polite. It lets you join and then refuses you any further change.

Light is a working dark. One lamp, caged, bolted to the short ceiling, throws a cone the color of old oil. It does not flicker. Flicker would be an event. Around the cone the room is a brown dim, and in that dim the population shows itself not as silhouettes but as a texture that moves only if you insist on the word. You should not insist. Nothing here moves in the way movement is usually spent—across, away, toward. Everything holds station so completely that motion has been refined into vibration, and vibration has been refined into sound.

They are on you before you have a category for them. The water is paved. Coin-flat bodies, pale as paraffin, lie edge to edge across the entire pan, gill-slits opening on a beat that is the beat of the room, which is to say not a beat. They do not swim. There is no gap to swim through. Set a boot down and they accept the boot as a new piece of floor, sealing around the sole, cold, slick, faintly electric against the skin of the ankle where the water finds you. They are not fish. The mouths are wrong—vertical, industrial, like slots milled for a tab. Along the walls the same species, or the mold that cast them, has climbed. A hide of bodies, each seated in a shallow cup that might have been formed by the pour or by the long fact of sitting. You cannot see the compound between them. Near-touching is too generous. They touch. They have always touched. A hand dragged across the wall comes back sequined with mucus and scale and a mineral dust that smells like a hot bearing quenched in brine.

The air is the third layer and the worst. Midges, if midges were the size of rice grains and had the patience of fixtures, pack the volume from the water-skin to the ceiling. Wings exist. You know because the roar has a grain, a high stitching inside the low plant-noise, and because the lamplight cones through them as through smoke that has agreed not to drift. They do not cloud and settle. They occupy. Breathe shallow and you still take them; they exit on the exhale, unharmed, unbothered, returned to their coordinates. Deeper in the press—because density here is layered, not merely crowded—are larger animals, few, immobilized by the logic of the pocket rather than by bars. One is a long pale loaf against the far wall, half in the water, half up the bodies of the wall-hiders, its hide the same paraffin, its eye open and unwet despite the wet, pupil a slot. It does not track you. Tracking would require a future. Another hangs in a sling of its own limbs from a rib of pipe, breathing at the room's rate, each breath a small bellows that neither adds nor subtracts from the water in the air. If there are young, they are not small versions arriving. They are already the count. The count does not rise.

Almost nothing grows. In the corner the lamp does not love, four tubes stand out of the water like reeds, hollow, glass-hard, beaded with the same permanent droplets. They are vents, or they were, or the compound extruded them and stopped. A black crust occupies a hand's breadth of wall above the loaf-animal, granular, not green. It might be oxide. It might be the only plant the cold and the made-world allowed, a lichen that feeds on metal and has reached the exact size it will always be. Nothing else attempts a leaf. The food is not vegetable. The food is the process: dissolved metal in the weep, an oil-sweet film that renews itself at the rate it is taken, aerosols from the plant in the walls. Mouths at every scale work without dining. Intake is continuous, waste is continuous, and the two are the same stream running in opposite directions through bodies that do not swell and do not thin. You will not find a scat, a shell, a corpse. Death, if it happens, is a substitution already complete, a face replaced by an identical face between one look and the next, except that you cannot prove the replacement because you cannot remember a difference. The room does not generate differences.

The industry is not a backdrop. It is the other animal. A ribbed exchanger occupies the long wall, fins packed until they read as a single grey lung, weeping between the fins where the cold brine inside meets the wet air outside. A motor housing, painted a red that has gone the color of liver, sits half-submerged at the pan's edge; its housing is warm—the only warm thing—and the coin-bodies crowd it in a tighter pavement, drinking the gradient without ever heating the room past cold. Belts run in a slot you cannot see the end of. Their contribution to the roar is a pure tone, almost kind, almost a note you could hum, except that humming would imply you could stop. Electricity is present as a smell, ozone tucked under the brine and the bearing-grease. No screens. No dials that travel. A gauge by the hatch has a needle. The needle is not pinned. It is simply at rest on a number, and the number is the true value, and the true value does not wander. Whoever built the governor built it for a single weather, and the weather complied so perfectly that maintenance became superstition. There is grease. There are drips of it, black, held on the underside of the exchanger like the water-drops are held, never lengthening.

Strange, here, is not a visitor. It is the fit. The animals and the castings share tolerances. Cups in the wall match the hips of the climbers. Slots in the mouths match tabs you can find on the underside of the lamp cage, unused, as if the cage were a spare part from the same catalog as the flesh. The loaf-animal's idle eye has a machining mark at the rim, a tiny circular witness, the kind a drill leaves when it kisses and withdraws. You can believe, for a while, that the room was built and then infested. Stay, and the other reading grows harder to refuse: that the infestation was the manufacturing method. Bodies as formwork. Formwork as brood. The pour rose around a living density and cured, and the density remained, and no one came back because coming back would be a change in the log, and the log is this sound, this level, this count.

The rules are simple because they have had no occasion to become otherwise.

Nothing finishes. The threads, the pour-lips, the unclosed story of the reeds—these are not delays. Delay requires a later.

Nothing starts. You did not arrive in any way the room consents to record. The hatch, shut again, is a seam. The waterline on your boot will dry to the same film-height as the wall, and then your boot is furniture.

Mass is constant: water, metal dissolved in water, grease, ozone, flesh. Exchange is allowed. Accumulation is not.

Number is constant. The midges replace the air the way the coin-bodies replace the floor. Remove one—if you can get your fingers around one—and the roar subtracts a thread so small you only notice because your teeth ache, and then the ache is gone, and your fingers are empty, and the station you emptied is full. You will not see the replacement cross the room. Crossing is a plot. This place does not plot.

Sound is a conservation law. It may not increase. It may not decay. A dropped tool does not clang. It joins, and the chord is the same chord, as a sea is the same sea after a cup is poured in. People who have stood here and tried to make a noise they could keep report the same small mercy: the room declines the noise without punishing the mouth. It is not hostile. Hostility is a gradient. This is a level.

Cold is maintained, not inflicted. The exchanger and the altitude and the wet agree. You will not freeze in an interesting way. You will approach the temperature of the coin-bodies and then stop approaching. Shivering is a transient. Transients are embarrassed here and put themselves away.

There is no day. The lamp is not a sun. Outside, high on whatever stack or tower or machine-mountain contains this gland, light might move. In the pocket, light is a material, stored in one cage, spent at one rate, balanced by the dark the way the brine is balanced by your skin.

Stand long enough and the first image you carried in—the cave, the marsh-edge, the nest—falls off like the excess water. What remains is a single feature, hidden, complete: a wet industrial organ at altitude, packed past touching with a fauna that is also its lining, its workforce, its weather, and its clock. The clock has one hand. The hand does not travel. The deafening is the silence of a system that has reached the only state it was built to hold, and held it, and is holding it now, in the perpetual just-before of its own completion, while the droplets refuse to fall and the needle refuses to lie and the animals breathe in place, overwhelming, exact, and still.
