The pocket has no horizon. It is a room the mountain made and then forgot, a fist-sized void by the standards of the ridge that holds it, though a person standing inside it would have to turn sideways and still brush both walls with the shoulders. The stone around it is the stone of the summit: cold, dry, ground down by an age of wind that never enters here. Whatever weather belongs to this elevation happens on the other side of a skin of sintered mineral no thicker than a wrist. Inside, the air does not move. It has not moved. It holds one temperature, a little above the ache of frost, and one pressure, and one smell — metal that has been warm for so long it has stopped smelling like heat and started smelling like itself.

There is no water. Not a seep, not a film, not the ghost of condensation on the cooler faces of the rock. The chemistry that keeps the place alive is dry. Fine pale dust, ground from the walls by a vibration older than the dust, hangs in the air at a density that never settles and never clears, and the growth drinks that. It drinks charge, too, and the faint oil-sweet vapor that leaks from old seals, and whatever cold mineral breath comes through hairline cracks from the summit stone. None of this arrives as weather. It arrives as a constant, metered by the same machines that have been metering it since the pocket was first cut.

The growth is complete. There is no bare place left to be lush toward. Mats of fiber, gray-green and the color of old copper, have sealed every vertical face and then grown out from them until the two walls nearly meet. Between them a single passage remains, not designed, only the last gap the mats have not yet closed, and even that gap is crossed by cords, runners, and stiff fans of blade that interleave without quite touching. The blades are dry. They ring, faintly, if a shoulder finds them. Some are translucent enough that the dimness behind them shows as a second dimness. Some are crusted with a salt that is not a salt of water — a flour of pale mineral that the blades extrude and never shed, because nothing here falls, and nothing here is rinsed.

Lower, where the floor should be, the growth has become a packed architecture of its own. Tubers like clenched fists, hollow and ringing, lock into one another. Between them run old pipes, not many, never a system you could map as a factory: a elbow of conduit here, a housing the size of a loaf, a pair of armatures bolted to a plinth that has itself been half swallowed. The machines are scattered the way a few tools are scattered in a room someone meant to finish and then did not. They are also the reason the room is not finished, and the reason it will not be. They run. They have always run, in the only sense this place has of always. A small engine in the plinth turns at one speed. A fan somewhere under the tubers turns at another. A transformer, no larger than a hand, holds a hum that does not cycle. Nothing starts. Nothing stops. The pocket has reached the only state it knows, and the machines are part of the state rather than an interruption of it.

Light comes from two tired sources and from the growth itself. A lamp behind a grated hood, industrial, glass crazed, throws a pool the color of a coin left in a drawer. Farther along the passage a second lamp has failed down to a filament that still glows and does not improve. Between them the mats give off a low mineral sheen, greener where the fiber is young — though young here means only less crusted, not recent. Nothing is recent. The dimness is even. Shadows do not travel. You can see the next arm's length and a little past it, and then the passage closes into fiber and you understand that seeing farther was never part of the arrangement.

The sound is the arrangement. It is loud in the way a millrace is loud when you have lived beside it: not an event, a medium. The engine's note sits in the sternum. The fan adds a higher, papery whir that the blades of the growth answer, each dry edge taking up a sliver of the tone and returning it, so the passage is full of small brightnesses of sound that do not resolve into echo because there is no open volume left to echo in. Under that, the transformer hums a third that does not beat against the others. They were worn into consonance long ago, or they were built already inside it; the pocket does not record which. There is no wind to vary them, no drip, no animal cry, no footstep unless you bring one. The loudness does not rise and does not rest. It is the climate.

Nothing alive moves through it except the growth, and the growth does not move. There are no insects in the mats, no moth at the lamp, no stain of guano, no track in the mineral flour. The ecology is the mats, the tubers, the dust, and the machines, and they have partitioned every function between them until the partition itself is invisible. The fiber fixes the vapor and the charge. The machines keep the vapor and the charge arriving at the rate the fiber can fix. The dust, shaken free by the same vibration that keeps the dust aloft, replaces what the fiber locks into its crust. A leaf that extrudes another grain of pale flour is answered, somewhere in the sealed arithmetic of the room, by a grain shaken loose from a wall already smoothed to the shape it will keep. Nothing accumulates past the shape. Nothing recedes from it. Erosion here is not a process you can watch. It is a finish. Edges are rounded because they finished rounding. Patina is complete because there is no weather left to add a layer or take one. The bolts on the plinth have the same worn brightness as the stone they were set into. The machines have been abraded by their own vibration into forms that no longer shed metal. Even the failed lamp has failed exactly as far as this equilibrium requires and then stopped failing.

The strangeness is quiet, once you have stood in the sound long enough to hear that it is not approaching anything. A cord of fiber has grown through the grating of the good lamp and flattened itself against the glass, not burned, not feeding in any way a plant feeds, only occupying the last unoccupied heat. A tuber has split around a pipe and healed into a collar that matches the pipe's diameter so exactly the joint looks machined. In the mineral flour on a lower blade, the constant whir has sorted grains by weight into faint bands, and the bands do not smear, because nothing walks, and the air does not gust. You can look at a band and know it will be that band when you look again. The pocket does not offer a later.

If there is a rule, it is that the room is already the whole of itself. The extreme of the summit — the thinness, the cold, the unending wind — exists as a pressure against the mineral skin and never as a view. You feel the elevation in the way your breath goes shallow near the seal, in the particular dryness at the back of the throat, in the fact that sound this loud should have somewhere to go and does not. Outside, if outside can be said to continue, the mountain keeps its weather. Inside, a handful of engines and a finished thicket hold one note, one dimness, one packed and waterless green, and do not become anything else.
