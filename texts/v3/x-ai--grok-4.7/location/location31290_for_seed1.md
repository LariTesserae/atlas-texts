The country has been argued flat. Not gently, and not recently. Whatever once stood here—ridges, cones, the rude first grammar of relief—was answered over a span long enough that height itself came to seem like a draft, and the draft was corrected. What remains is low in the way a conclusion is low: a basin the size of a weather, barely above the level water would need in order to leave, and water cannot leave. Drainage is a memory the stone has worn off its own mouth. The final form of erosion is not a ruin. It is a running surface, and it has been final for so long that finality has become a kind of engine.

Cold lives in the water, not over it. The cold is annealed, old, without the theater of biting. It has soaked the mineral until the mineral answers in the same temperature. Every surface holds a film. Every lip of every shallow seep weeps on an interval that predates intervals. The air is near enough to saturation that it keeps condensing out of sheer proximity to itself, a gray fleece renewing grain by grain, so that the light—never more than a dim, sourceless permeability, as if noon had been diluted and then left—arrives already mixed with weather. You do not see far so much as you are permitted a series of revisions of nearness. Post-rain is the permanent tense. The rain is mostly the same water coming back, cold, fine, loud against everything because everything is close enough to be struck.

There is no soil in the sense that would invite a leaf, and no leaf has ever been invited. The chemistry of the place did not take that turning. Black glass, worn past shine into a matte meal, lies in lenses where old melt once skinned and was then sanded by ages into the general flatness. Iron-stained sinter crusts the seeps. A mineral wax gathers in the joints of the film, yellowish, unmelting, smelling of cold metal and a sweetness with no flower behind it. Between frost and tissue, occupying the wet stone without rising from it, are mats of a non-life or an unrecognizable one: filamentous only if watched past boredom, glass-pale, feeding on the refusal of rock to remain unaltered. They do not reach. They do not green. They process. Under the dimness they look like tarnish that has learned a slower metabolism than tarnish should have.

And the openness is full. That is the first correction the eye suffers, and it does not stop being true at any distance. The basin extends—regional, multi-featured, a long permission of flat country under the condensate—and there is no interval in it that is unoccupied. Density here is not crowding at a center. It is the absence of remainder. Bodies pack the film, the air above the film, the soft upper stone, the standing columns of weather. They do not live on the land. Occupation is the land's mode.

The largest are mills, though nothing built them and they have no address. Shed-sized from the outside, accreted rather than assembled: sinter, cartilage, plates of that worn black glass, greased at the flexures with the mineral wax. They lurch rather than walk, load-bearing, their weight a kind of work. Inside, chambers sort grit by a sequence of wetting, grinding, settling, and passing-on. Coarse meal enters at a mouth that is only a thicker fold. Finer meal leaves at a flank-vent, bright with metal the cold water has kept dissolved for an age and the body has decided, at last, to precipitate. The motion is repetitive in the way factories are repetitive, not in the way animals are usually allowed to be. Joints click. A wet transformer-hum sits under the click. When several mills come into phase—and they do, across stretches of the basin, without any signal you can separate from the weather—the regional hum locks, and the light dims another fraction, as if the basin had taken a load.

Among them, and under them, and in the water that is not deep enough to be called a pool and not thin enough to be called damp, the small fauna make a slurry. Grains, each grain a body, schooling the way current schools in a conductor. They are the electricians of a grid no one laid. Where they pack—everywhere, with local differences of thickness that read, if you insist on reading, as districts—they snap and tick, a continuous small lightning, electrolysis along the film. The wet stone conducts. The basin is one circuit, badly insulated, loud with leakage. Discharge walks the flats in sheets. For a half-breath the dimness is wrong-lit, and what the flash shows is not always the present arrangement of bodies. Sometimes it shows the grit already refined. Sometimes it shows a mill-chamber from an angle that does not fit the shed of its exterior, as if the inside had more country in it than the outside had been willing to admit.

There are sheets, too, finger-thick, wide as roads, the color of the wax. They travel. They are not artifacts and not quite separate animals; they read as conveyors because that is the work, and the work is older than the comparison. Sorted grains ride them. Mills shoulder across them without hostility and without alliance, the way adjacent machines in a shop share a floor. Parasites—no, not parasites yet; call them thieves of mesh—bore into the mills to take the middle grade, the half-sorted, which is richer than either end of the process. A mill that notices, if noticing is the word for a change in grind, closes a chamber and reduces the thieves to catalyst. The catalyst improves the next precipitation. Nothing is grieved. Death, where it can be said to occur, does not empty a place. It changes the grain size at which a body continues. Space is not returned to emptiness, because emptiness is not a local product.

All of this is loud. The grinding has a pulse you feel in the teeth before you agree you hear it. The seep ticks under it, not softer, only drier in timbre. Rain ticks on backs and crusts and the wax-sheets. Discharge speaks in wet cracks, sometimes single, sometimes in a run like a bad weld. The hum is the sound of the circuit being a region rather than a machine. Silence would be an event. It does not come. Even the pauses are occupied by the smaller ticks, the motes, the film slapping a moving flank. Sound here is not a byproduct. It has standing. A grind of sufficient regularity, held long enough, begins to print. Wet stone within earshot of a locked phase stiffens, allocates chambers, and lurches. The new mill is not born in any biological courtesy of the word. It is templated. The noise was a drawing, and the basin, being packed with the willingness to be more of itself, complied. You can watch a sound leave a residue that is already shouldering its weight.

Nothing constructed stands, and nothing constructed would be allowed to finish. There are no walls, no roads, no housings for the industry that is nevertheless everywhere in session—engines of muscle and sinter, separation columns that are only weather standing still enough to reflux, a power system that is the fauna and the film and the dissolved metal agreeing to be a current. The industrial order is not an imposition. It is the oldest stable state of the processes, the shape they wore themselves into when wearing-away had gone as far as it could and motion did not therefore cease. If a mark is added—an edge too sharp, a heap that implies intention, a joint that cooled in the shape of a plan—the final form files it as unfinished erosion. The mills drift that way without seeming to choose. Abrasion resumes. The heap becomes film and meal and, in time, another chamber in another body. Untouched, here, is not innocence. It is enforcement. The basin keeps itself in the condition of having already been completely used.

The rules do not hold still long enough to be posted, but they can be suffered into partial clarity.

Sequence is optional. A flank-vent issues its bright precipitate while the mouth-fold is still empty, and both moments are accurate, and the mill is not in contradiction with itself. It is in a arrangement the eye keeps trying to stretch into a line. Input and output are positions, not a story. Attend long enough and you will see a single body at three points of its process, occupying three places in the basin, none of them a copy. Counting is the error. The census comes out different each time not because the numbers change, but because the individuals were never the kind of thing a number survives.

Scale is locally negotiable and regionally strict. The basin remains low, open, one weather wide. Inside a mill, a chamber can contain a run of conveyors and a weather of its own, condensate turning over, motes in a thicker schooling, a hum at a different pitch—valley-sized work in a shed-sized flank—while the exterior does not swell to excuse it. Crouch is useless. There is no threshold that acknowledges your change of size. You are simply, for that interval, wrong about how much interior a surface may have.

Horizon is a courtesy. Now and then a section of the middle distance includes the patch of film you are standing in, not reflected, included, the flat having folded a clause into itself without rising into a hill. Elevation stays low because the fold is grammatical. Water, deprecated from any serious uphill, still moves: it moves toward whatever the processes have not yet finished, and there is less of that every century, so the water is almost still, almost deciding, and the deciding is one of the louder sounds, a seep-tick that sometimes arrives before the wetness it describes.

Light misidentifies. The dimness is honest about visibility and dishonest about time. Discharge-flashes illuminate loads, grades, the next configuration, occasionally a chamber you have not approached and will not, because approach is a sequence and sequence has limited jurisdiction. Color slips its job. The wax is yellow until it is the sound of grinding made graspable, a residue in the hand that hums and then, if held, allocates a tiny chamber and begins, pitiably, industriously, to sort the salt of your skin. Set it down. The basin will not thank you, and it will not lose the mote you have accidentally fed.

There is a cycle, because the place is active, not arrested. Condensate thickens through a long dimming until the fleece is almost a wall, and the mills sound nearer than their exteriors agree to be, and the circuit runs hot for cold metal, crackling in continuous sheet. Then the fleece fails all at once—the same water, returning harder—and for a short while the dimness lifts by a fraction, and the packing is more visible than the eye can sort, a single continuous industry from the near film to the revised horizon, no clearing, no crop, no roof, no fire, only process at biological pace and mechanical insistence, predator and mesh-thief and mill and mote and weather-column sharing a floor that has been flat long enough to be eternal and not one hour quieter for it. Then the fleece rebuilds. The load returns to the light. Somewhere a phase locks, and somewhere a sound starts, patiently, to become a body.

It does not feel like an aftermath, though everything about its age says the world that could have been different has already finished. Life, if this is life, did not end; it completed its project and kept the machinery. The project was refinement. The machinery is the country. Standing in it—if standing persists, if the film accepts the weight, if the nearest mill does not file you as an edge too sharp—you are not visiting a landscape that contains an industry. You are inside the only building there has ever been, which was never built, which is crowded past the possibility of elsewhere, which is cold, wet, loud, and still, after all this age, taking on load.
