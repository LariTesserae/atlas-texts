The first thing the place insists on is the noise, and the noise is not an event. It has no onset. It occupies the volume the way a color occupies a wall: fully, evenly, with no place left for a quieter version of the room. A continuous industrial scream, pitched just above the range where a voice could cut it, locked to one note. If you stand long enough to stop flinching, you notice it does not swell or drop. The machines are not working toward something. They are holding a state.

The chamber that holds them is small enough to be a room and closed enough to be a world. Four walls, a floor, a ceiling low enough that the ducts in it read as sky only because there is nothing above them you are allowed to see. You could cross it in thirty steps if the floor were clear. It is not clear, but neither is it crowded. Between the housings there are lanes of empty air, wide as corridors, and the emptiness is part of the design. Density here is a budget. Each object has been given more space than it needs, and the surplus is cold.

Cold is the second constant, and it is dry. There is no moisture on the metal, no beading, no film, no darkening at the joints where water would have found a way. Breath, if a body were here to give it, would not fog; the air has already been stripped past the point where fog is possible. What moves through the pipes is not water and not steam. It is a working fluid that never condenses into anything you could drink or drown in: a dense, colorless gas, colder than the room, pushed in a closed loop that has no outlet and no weather. The pipes are thick, flanged, newly sharp at every weld. Their edges have not been rounded by time. Nothing here has.

The elevation announces itself without a view. The air is thin in the lungs and thin in the sound, which is strange, because the sound is also enormous. Pressure is low, as if the whole pocket had been lifted and sealed near the top of a stack whose lower stories you will not be shown. A narrow instrument glass in one wall looks out onto nothing navigable: a vertical run of cladding, floodlit the same white as the interior, dropping out of the frame. High, then, and hidden inside what holds it up. The outside, if it is an outside, is only a brighter version of the wall.

Light is ordinary. Banks of sealed lamps give a flat, workday white, neither dawn nor sodium dusk. Shadows are short and accountable. You can read the stamped plates on the housings without leaning in. What the light reveals is entirely made. There is no bedrock showing through, no soil in the corners, no seam where construction gave up and let the mountain continue. The floor is plate over plate. The walls are panel over frame. Even the grit in the joints is milled swarf, not sand. If the world has a natural substrate, it is outside this room and not admitted.

The machines are few and large. Two compressor housings, a manifold like a low metal hedgerow, a rank of valves whose handwheels have never been turned from the positions they were set to. Young metal: mill-scale still ghosting the less-handled faces, edges that would cut a glove, bolt heads with their machining marks unblurred. Erosion has not begun, or has been refused. The equilibrium is perfect enough that nothing abrades. Gas moves; surfaces do not wear; the scream does not vary. Time passes only as a concept you brought in with you.

Between the housings, in the budgeted emptiness, the vegetation keeps to itself. It is sparse the way a signature is sparse: present, deliberate, not a cover. Three stands, maybe four, none taller than a knee. They are not quite plants in the open-air sense. Pale, stiff, branching in abrupt rightish angles, they rise from shallow trays set flush with the floor, and the trays are plumbed into a line so thin it might be an afterthought or a sacrament. Whatever feeds them is not water. A clear, oily fraction, barely moving, darker than the gas in the mains. The growths drink it without glistening. Their surfaces are matte, slightly fibrous, the color of old paper under shop light. No leaves in the usual sense: plates, overlapping, that might be tissue or might be a kind of scale the process grows on purpose. They do not move. There is no draft that reaches them. If they photosynthesize, they do it indifferently under lamps that were installed for human eyes, not for them.

Nothing else lives here. No moth at the lamp, no film of biofilm in a corner the cleaners missed, no tracked-in spore that found a niche. The room has been held below the threshold where animal life can start. The growths in the trays may not count as animals, or even as the vegetation you meant; they read as a process given a shape, tolerated because they pull a contaminant out of the lubricant line and because someone, once, decided the chamber should not be only metal. They cast thin shadows. They make no sound the roar would allow you to hear.

The strangeness is not a creature and not a violation of the geometry. It is the fit. The room is too finished to be abandoned and too still to be in use, and yet every gauge sits at a living pressure. The sound implies labor, acceleration, a cycle with a before and an after, and the dials refuse it. Needles do not hunt. Indicator lamps do not sequence; the ones that are lit have always been lit. On the far wall a maintenance board lists tasks in a hand that has not been added to. The dates, if you bother to read them, are not old. They are simply not progressing. Young features, eternal operation: the contradiction the place has chosen instead of weather.

Stand in one of the empty lanes and the scale corrects you. What felt, at the threshold, like a pocket, develops a false distance. The roar has no near and far; it arrives from the metal itself, from the floor, from the trays, from the air. Without gradients you cannot locate the machines by ear, and without that location the room loosens. The ceiling ducts seem higher. The drop beyond the instrument glass seems longer. You remain in a single chamber, fully built, fully lit, fully known, and the knowing does not shrink it. High, sealed, cold, dry. A landscape with the dimensions of a workshop and the permanence of a climate.

Nothing will change while you are here. The gas will complete another circuit identical to the last. The growths will add, perhaps, a thickness too small to see, fed by a fluid that is not rain. The edges will stay sharp. When you leave, if leaving is possible, you will take the note of the scream with you longer than the image of the walls, and the place will not notice the difference. It was not performing. It was continuing, at the only volume it has.
