This is a high, cold, waterless mineral shield with no people and no structures — only sparse, self-sustaining growth and rare, barely seen animals. I'll describe what lives there as the place's own logic of persistence.What lives here has made a long bargain with absence, and has kept it.

It is not one body. It is a way of lasting that several bodies share, spaced so widely that each seems alone until you learn the intervals. The pavement is the oldest of them, a living nap a few millimeters deep, gray-green, continent-wide, holding the crust against the wind by the simple fact of covering it. It does not grow toward anything. It occupies. Where a breach opens, it returns at the speed of seasons stacked on seasons, sealing the pan once the pan has sheltered itself, and in that sealing it decides which forms the shield will keep. Kneel and it is lush in the only sense the place allows: continuous, damp-looking, dry to the touch, a chemistry that has learned to be a surface.

The stands rise out of that bargain at long intervals. A plant is a column of shields, outer faces silvered, inner folds nearly black, leaves thick as hide and full of a slow oil that does not freeze and does not ask for rain. They feed on what the wind brings — mineral dust settled on the silver, worked inward, turned into bulk — and they spend the day tilting, not toward a sun they cannot miss, but to manage how much of it they will accept. By afternoon the shields have turned a fraction; by evening they have turned it back. A stand of a dozen, or of a hundred, is a congregation of that same patience. They do not close overhead. They do not become a wood. Between them the shield stays empty at the scale of a walk, and the emptiness is not a failure of growth. It is the distance the chemistry requires: each stand drawing a wide, invisible claim on dust, on the richer nap left where an older column fell and bled its oil into the grit, on the rare breach where a hard bright grain can lodge and begin the decades of becoming a leaf.

Death, among them, is abrasion. A fallen column lies as itself for years, silver gone dull, a dark oval of oil beneath it that the pavement is slow to cross. When the pavement does cross, the stain remains as a denser living, and the next stand roots nearer that place than chance would choose. What they release travels farther than any animal — grains that ring on stone, lifted in a wind-week across a weather of their own. Most are polished to nothing on the intact nap. A few find a wound in it. That is reproduction here: not a season, a century that occasionally consents.

The animal is the exception that keeps the rule visible. It is large, spare, pale, and almost never in the same place as your looking. Three-toed, wide-set, it presses the pavement just deep enough to craze it and no deeper, running the empty distances between stands as if the stands were stations on a route measured in weeks. It cannot afford to sweat, and has arranged not to. Whatever it takes from the shields — a leaf-edge, the sweet oil, the waxed husks that are not seeds and tumble into the lee to be sanded thin — it takes rarely, and far apart, so the route itself is the feeding. You may see a flank at the limit of the glare, moving with an economy that looks like stillness until you notice the stands behind it have changed their order. Tracks that were not there at dawn lose their edges by the time you walk back. Smaller life is mostly that: suggestion, husk, a body already becoming grit. Nothing cries. If there are wings, the air will not carry their sound, or they are elsewhere, which on this shield is most of the world.

Nothing made lives here. No cairn, no cut, no lamp. The brightness is the sun, hard and continuous, heat taken off by thin air as fast as the ground receives it. Weather is the shield’s own: katabatic threads in the swales, lenticular veils of mineral dust that reorganize the light and then settle as a finer powder on the silver leaves, which is a kind of feeding. Sound is the crust answering or not answering, a leaf against a leaf like a thumbnail on parchment, a pan of grit slumping under its own weight and going quiet in the same breath.

So what lives in the place is a patience with addresses. The pavement holds the ground in balance, catastrophe and repair at the same speed. The stands keep a metal-green lushness that is a property of bodies, not of cover, and pass their dead forward as richer soil for a growth that will not hurry. The pale walker keeps the distances meaningful by crossing them, and leaves almost no evidence that it did. Stand long enough and a shield tilts a fraction you cannot measure. The dust-veil thins. Your own departure does not register as weather. The living continues, bright, cool, and spaced according to a rule older than any witness: that height, cold, and dryness can be persisted in, if nothing tries to fill them.
