They do not arrive. They are the continuance that the place has already settled into, and if you watch long enough for a body to seem particular, the particularity loosens and the role remains. What lives here is not a population in the old sense. It is a set of offices the water keeps filled.

The grazers are the first you can hold in the eye, because they keep to the mat and the mat keeps their road. A grazer is long, almost featureless in the dim, the color of the cool itself when the cool has been thinking of pale. Its underside is a continuous mouth, not a gape but a soft keel of cilia that reads the plant-fiber the way a hand reads a banister, taking only the standing crop of the groove’s edge and never the pavement beneath. The road bends once, at the fused root, and the body bends with it without decision; the bend is in the animal as much as in the floor. You will not see it feed in any moment you can call a bite. The taking is as even as the replacement of the edge. When one grazer dies it does not die in the open. It thins. The cilia slow, the pale length loses coherence, and the suspension accepts it as meal. Before that thinning finishes, another length is already on the road, not hurrying, already bent at the root, already the color of the cool. There is no parent to watch. The office was vacant for less time than the chord needs to state itself.

Higher, the filterers do not travel even that much. A filterer is a bell of tissue moored to no stalk, holding its station because the current has only one station to offer and the body has grown to fit the offer exactly. The bell’s mouth is a slow helix turned inward; the fine living weather of the open water enters and does not all leave. What leaves has been lightened by one increment of its own substance, and the increment is the animal’s whole wage. They do not school. The distance between one and the next is a measured darkness, and if you wait in that darkness you will eventually be granted a second shape, never a third in the same look. Their turning is the current’s turning. If the line of flow were to shift they would shift, but the line has not shifted since the terraces accepted their lips, and so the bells face what they have always faced. Their abundance is real and uncrowded: count them across the whole high sea and the number is large; stand anywhere and the number is one, then none, then one.

Inside the grown halls the listeners are thicker, though still not heaped. They are small enough to be mistaken, at first, for irregularities in the polished pith — a node, a healed pore — until one of them answers the tone of its chamber. The answer is a knock, dry and brief, placed in the beat where the conduit’s note would otherwise stand alone. Each listener is tuned to one overtone and keeps to the stretch of wall where that overtone is cleanest. They do not migrate between chambers. A listener born to the second room’s third-lower tone will not survive the first room’s pitch; the water there is the same water, but the chord is a different country, and the small body fails at the threshold the way a word fails in the wrong sentence. Their abundance is the abundance of the architecture: as many as the walls can pure-tune, and no more. When one ceases, the knock ceases for a count you can feel in the chest, and then the knock returns, same placement, same dryness, from a body that has come out of the low arch already pressed flat, already listening, already correct.

The swimmer-beast is rarer to the eye and ordinary to the arithmetic. It is the color of the directionless dim, so that what you meet is not a shape but a colder wake and, sometimes, the sense that a loose gathering of lesser bodies has been edited and then restored. It does not chase. It passes through. The lesser bodies are the small continuances that fill the open water between the great offices — flecks with just enough will to hold a loose relation to one another, never a school, never a name. The swimmer takes the number the region has budgeted for taking, and the conduit-mouths have already released the replacements, so the gathering’s count is a constant you could set a measure by if you had any other measure. You will not see the swimmer feed. You will feel the edit, and then the restoration, and the chord will not have remarked it.

Nothing here is old in its body. The bodies are always new. What is old is the office: grazer, bell, listener, editor, and the lesser flecks that have no office but still have a number. The nurseries are not places you visit so much as facts you deduce. Low arches, conduit-mouths, the inner ribs of a hollow column where the note is too narrow for a listener — somewhere in those darks the exact animal is always almost ready, held at the last thickness before emergence, released when the arithmetic requires it and not before. No one tends them. The conduits themselves, living and finished, keep the count the way they keep the flow: by having no other instruction left.

If there is a who, it is not any one of these. It is the way a role survives its holder without a seam. Stay beside the grazer’s bend and the body you watch will be replaced, and the new body will occupy the bend with the same unstartled fitness, and you will not be able to say which fact you were faithful to — the animal, or the bend’s being walked. The listeners knock, and the knock is older than any tissue currently knocking. The bells face a flow that has outlasted every bell. Even the swimmer’s colder wake is a recurring absence, a draft that the region permits at intervals and then closes.

They do not look at you. There is nothing in their arrangement that answers to an outside attention, because attention of that kind is a change, and the place has finished changing. If you rest near a listener it may include the faint disturbance of your presence as a dulling of its overtone, and then it will shift a finger’s width along the wall until the tone is pure again, and the knock will resume, and you will have been edited out of its country without having been noticed as a self. Speak, and the chord takes the shape of your voice and spends it. Leave a warmth, and the cool reasserts. The inhabitants do not investigate these small violations. Their work is the maintenance of a sameness that has already succeeded, and they perform it by being replaced exactly, forever, in a loud green-black water that does not remember having been otherwise.

What lives here, then, is continuity wearing bodies. The bodies are many, cool, and brief. The continuity does not move, does not age, and does not look back when you go.
