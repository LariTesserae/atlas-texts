They do not school as other waters teach schooling. A group of them is a spacing more than a mass: bodies held at distances that still allow a call to arrive as a separate event, not a blur. Up close, which is rarer than the roar suggests, one of the mid-sized kind is longer than a person is tall and narrower through the middle than habit expects, as if bulk had been refused and the refusal kept. The skin is not scaled. It is a taut, mineral-pale membrane, cooler to the idea of touch than the water, with faint seams where plates of denser tissue ride underneath. Light passes a little way into it. In the upper water they look unfinished, almost diagrammatic, the organs of hearing visible as paired swellings behind the head, larger than the eyes.

The eyes are small and do not lead. They register the hard daylight, the edge of a pinnacle, the brief dark of another body crossing. What leads is the low architecture of the skull: bone folded into chambers, a throat that can tighten to a needle of sound or open into a tone that travels the basin as weather travels a plain. They do not sing to announce territory in any sense a coast would recognize. Territory here is not a holding. It is a route worn into the volume by repetition, and the call is how a route stays true when sight fails across kilometers of empty clarity.

The small ones are easier to mistake for debris. Against a fracture face they lie in the film, not eating it so much as keeping its chemistry in motion, bodies no larger than a hand, flattened, with mouths that are less mouths than rasps. Lift one and the rock beneath is no cleaner and no dirtier. Their work is continuous and almost without product. They spawn in sheets when a face sheds, then thin again. Larger animals do not hunt them as hunters hunt. They pass a wall and take what has already gathered into drifting clots, the way a current takes grit. There is no chase worth the name. Speed, in this water, is mostly wasted.

The solitary cruisers are another order of the same idea. One may be the length of the platform that breaks the surface on the far pinnacle, and still look slight, because the basin does not furnish a scale. Their flanks darken where they favor the glass walls, a dusk laid into the membrane, not a pattern and not a warning. Along the belly run lines of harder tissue that answer vibration the way a string answers a finger. They listen with the whole underside. A pump three ridges off arrives in them as a fact, sorted from the living calls by a regularity no animal sustains. They do not avoid the machines. They use them. A housing on a wall is a cliff that does not shed, a sound-mark that does not migrate. Routes bend toward those marks and then away, the way older seas bend toward seamounts. The animals have incorporated the industry without granting it meaning. The turbines hold the level. The cruisers hold their intervals. Neither explains the other, and neither needs to.

Young are not carried. They are left in the films, brief and numerous, most of them taken back into the same chemistry that fed them before they ever grow a chamber that can call. The few that persist do it by learning the chord early: which tones are stone, which are kin, which are the tooth of a machine that has not changed pitch since it was switched on. Survival is an education in sources. An animal that cannot separate them spends its life crossing to nothing.

There is no face that watches you. Attention, when it occurs, is a slight alteration in course, a tightening of the throat, a call sent and answered or not. Some will circle a new object once, wide, the circle measured in hundreds of meters, and then resume the route as if the object had been a pinnacle they already knew. Others never alter. The basin is loud enough that novelty is a small event inside a larger pressure, and most lives are spent maintaining the spacing that keeps that pressure legible.

If anything could be said to dwell rather than pass, it is the film, and even that is only a persistence of process. The animals are traffic. Immense in aggregate, scarce in any hour, pale or dark according to the light they keep, built to be heard across a sea that has not yet learned how to be crowded. They do not fill the place. They articulate it. Between one body and the next the water stays clear, cold, and already complete, and the call goes on crossing it whether anyone is there to sort the sources or not.
