It does not arrive. You notice it the way you notice a correction in a sentence you have already read: a clause that was not wrong until the rest of the page made it wrong. The ring nearest you is clear. It was clear when you found it. It is clearer now, and the newest spill that should have lipped over the darker clasts has stopped at the perimeter as if the perimeter were a temperature, not a line. Something has preferred the inside to remain unoccupied by stone. That preference is the first body you are allowed to see.

It has no outline that holds. Where the ground is firmest it is a stillness slightly deeper than the surrounding stillness, a pocket of air that does not shift when the warm bodies of heat pass. Where the crust is mealy it is a faint compaction, as if weight had stood and then thought better of standing. It does not cast the kind of shadow that requires light. It casts a difference in cooling. Touch the glass beside it and the surface is already the temperature of an hour ago; touch where it has been, and the mineral is negotiating with the present, a degree late, a degree early, a thin lag that follows no footfall you can map. If you try to circle it, the lag moves to the far side of the ring and waits, not fleeing, only declining to be enclosed.

For a long time that is all: a maintained emptiness, a delayed heat, a refusal to be measured by walking around. Then a slab at the edge of the ring adjusts. Not a fall. An adjustment. One corner lifts the width of a fingernail and settles on a new bearing, the glassy face turning a fraction toward the center, and the sound is the sound of cloth you already heard somewhere else, only closer, and finished before you assign it. The three standing stones farther off answer with nothing visible. Their bases, half-swallowed, seem more swallowed. Or less. The dark cheats that too.

What lives here is not an animal that learned the land. It is a habit the land has not yet forgotten it is having. It keeps certain floors from revising themselves. It turns certain faces inward. It passes heat along the veins not as a creature might carry a coal but as a decision might travel through a material that has not separated decision from physics. When a terrace gives in the distance, the habit pauses — you feel the pause as a brief evenness, every vein the same dim rose, no hand-off — and then resumes, having noted the collapse the way a sleeper notes a door. It does not go to look. Distance is not a problem it has. The region is the length of its attention, and attention, here, is a thermal property.

It has no mouth and no need of one. The dryness is not a lack it suffers. Water would be an interruption of the only sense it possesses, which is the difference between what has set and what is still choosing. It reads the ground by inhabiting the choice. Fresh faces that split and show their dull rose are, for it, not wounds and not food; they are statements, and sometimes it answers by letting a vein brighten along a seam until the seam prefers to stay shut. Sometimes it does not answer. Indifference is also native. Much of the floor is beneath its notice, revising itself in the ordinary way, slumping, welding wrong, ticking against the lowered ceiling. Only the rings, the alignments, the clearings, the occasional standing stone that must not be allowed to fall until it has finished meaning whatever it means — only those are occupied. The occupation is so complete that the stone and the keeper are not two facts. Lift one of the clasts from a ring, and the lag in the heat stutters, offended or simply revised, and within a short while another clast, not carried but encouraged, works itself to the gap by the slow crawl of contracting crust. You did not see a limb. You saw the land correct a typo.

If it has an age, the age is the age of the floor: not old, not young, unfinished. It does not remember a wetter world because there has not been one to forget. It does not anticipate plants. A seed, if one could be imagined here, would be a foreign temperature, a small wet argument, and the habit would not hate it. Hatred is a later mineral. It would simply fail to keep the clearing for it, and the next spill would cover the argument without ceremony. Its tenderness, if the word can be used for a pause in cooling, is reserved for arrangements that are almost ready to be permanent and are not permanent yet. It stands — though standing is the wrong verb, a vertebrate leftover — inside that almost. It turns the slabs by fractions. It keeps the air in the rings a degree warmer and very still, which may be how it listens, or how it thinks, or how it sleeps without ever being absent.

It is aware of you in the only way it is aware of new rock. You are a weight that has not decided whether to remain a floor. Your steps are too crisp, then too muffled, and the mismatch registers as a seam that opened in the wrong order. It does not approach. Approaching would require a direction, and it is already everywhere it cares to be. Instead the nearest ring’s warmth extends, slightly, toward your shins, not in welcome and not in threat: a measurement. The vein under the glass brightens one handbreadth and stops, as if a sentence had been begun and then left to see whether the listener would finish it. If you step into the ring, the air does not resist. The habit makes room by becoming the room. For a moment you are inside the maintained emptiness, and the emptiness is not empty of regard. The regard has no eyes. It is the fact that the spill at the perimeter continues not to enter, even though nothing holds it back but a preference.

Should you remain, it will eventually include you in the arrangement or it will not. Inclusion would look like nothing happening for a long time, your boots acquiring the same delayed temperature as the glass, your outline ceasing to matter, the ring expanding by one clast to acknowledge a new center that is not quite stone and not quite not. Exclusion would look almost identical, except that a terrace under you would give, small and late, and the report would arrive after you had already been revised into scree. It does not hurry either outcome. Hurry is a kind of weather, and this place has refused weather. The habit can wait through the slump of ridges, through the lifting and lowering of the black ceiling, through the static that smells of hot mineral and never becomes a storm. It has waited out every version of the floor so far. You are only the latest version that can move on purpose, and purpose, to it, is a minor and interesting form of contraction.

What lives here, then, is the region’s unfinishedness, concentrated until it behaves like a resident. It will not give you a face. Faces are for places that have finished deciding what light is. It will give you the cleared ring, the turned slab, the vein that brightens and does not explain itself, and the sense, if you stay long enough for the floor to consider you, that the dark is attending — sparsely, warmly, without water or mercy or malice — to the question of whether you are a mark it meant to make, or only another spill it has not yet allowed to cool.
