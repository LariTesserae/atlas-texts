They do not arrive. They are already distributed, the way weather is distributed, and the first mistake is to look for a body that could be pointed at and named the inhabitant. The plateau keeps its tenants the way it keeps water: near the surface, in sheets, in hollows, and in processes that only sometimes condense into something a visitor would consent to call an animal.

The grazers are the most willing to be seen. From a distance they are a dark stitching along a sweeter vein of the mat, wool so matted it has the nap of wet felt, legs short enough that their bellies sweep a film of condensation as they move. Up close the wool is not wool. It is a thatch of the same alpine fiber as the ground, grown outward from the skin and periodically paid back into it, so that a grazer is less a creature walking on the plateau than a mobile thickening of the plateau that has learned a jaw. The click of that jaw is genuine work. Each bite is a small disassembly: fibers locked, then unlocked, then swallowed into a gut that behaves like one of the shed-sized engines, warm, piston-slow, venting a private weather between the forelegs. They do not startle at approach. Startle requires a stable outside. They will, if crowded, simply cease to be in the place where they were being crowded, and be instead a little farther along the vein, heads still down, the intervening ground showing for a moment the flattened older version of itself, as if the herd had not fled but had been edited. Their eyes, when they have them, are the color of standing lenses. Sometimes the lens is only the wet on the face, and the looking is done by the whole head, the way a seep looks by being lower than what surrounds it.

They are not the only mouths. There are thinner things that do not graze so much as edit. You will not assemble them into an animal on the first watch. A pale runner of stem, already familiar as infrastructure, will tighten across a mile of open ground with that belt-whine, and halfway along its length a thickening will travel with it, not riding, participating. The thickening has no limbs you can count twice with the same result. It has a preference. Where the mat has begun to assemble a stand of blade-grass, the editor arrives in the same interval and begins the unmaking early, not as sabotage but as schedule. Fibers pay back into the substrate under its passage with the thread-from-a-seam sound, and what it takes for itself is not bulk. It takes the error: the green that cannot grow at this cold, the stalk that locked too tall, the small architecture that would have cast a shade long enough to be mistaken for a wall. After it passes, the stand is already browning in the jaw of the correction, and the editor is thinner, then gone into the runner, then present again a hollow away, drinking from a seep with a motion that is mostly listening. If it has a face it keeps it in the same place it keeps uphill: as a word that frequently reverses.

The birds are easier and worse. They lift the color of the dim and are, for the moment of lift, disagreeing with the air about how many of them the flight requires. Three positions, one bird; one bird, a stutter of wings that leaves a wet clap in the wrong place. They feed on the sparks. Not on insects drawn to sparks—on the spark itself, the blue jump between raised ribs, ozone bright on the tongue they may or may not still have when they land. A bird that has eaten recently leaves a smell of crushed resin and a small scorched kiss on the mat, which heals upward into a welt and then into one of the minor organs, a knuckle of vegetation that ticks for an hour on the bird’s rhythm and then forgets the rhythm and becomes pavement again. They nest, if that is the word, in the hanging steam. A column that refuses to disperse will sometimes hold, inside its pale machinery, a darkness with a pulse. Approach and the nest is only steam. Withdraw and the pulse returns, three chicks or one, already the color of not-seeing. They do not sing. The percussion at the edge of hearing is not theirs, though they answer it by lifting all at once when it misses its beat, as if a missed stamp were a kind of weather they were bred to flee without ever having had a sky to flee into.

Between the herds, and farther apart than the herds, are the absences that drink. They are not ghosts. Ghosts require a prior presence that failed to finish leaving. These are the leaving itself, given thirst. You see them as a thinning of the dim inside a hollow: the water is there, the lens is there, and also a shape of less-water, a standing subtraction that lowers its not-head and takes the seep down by a finger’s depth. The grazer that shares the hollow with one of them does not compete. It drinks the water. The absence drinks the version of the water that will not survive the afternoon’s reversal, when the seep remembers it is supposed to sheet downhill and abandons the climb. Afterward the hollow smells of iron and crushed leaf, and the absence is gone into the loud nothing, which is louder where it went. They are numerous. They are also, strictly, not there. Counting them is a way of losing the afternoon. People who have tried—there are no people, and yet the mat remembers the posture of counting, a slight forward lean, weight negotiated, a print that rose into an organ shaped like attention—come back wrong in the stories, which is to say the stories come back without them, smoothed.

The engines have keepers, and the keepers are the hardest to separate from the work. The knotted organs the size of sheds, half-submerged, venting warm wet breath, are not uninhabited. In the steam live slow tenants whose bodies are mostly schedule. A keeper is a heat that has decided on a route. It circulates through the knot the way sap circulates, piston-faithful, and where it surfaces it skins itself in a membrane of condensate so that, briefly, it can be mistaken for a crouched animal, pale, ribbed, breathing on a cycle of days rather than minutes. It is not crouched. It is maintaining. Sparks that would burn a runner back into scrap are guided to the ribs that can spend them. Nutrient belts that have wandered toward a hollow too deep to be a vein are tensioned and returned. The warmth is a lure only from the outside. From the inside it is a wage. Anything that sleeps against the vent wakes, if it wakes, as a depression the mat is already keeping: a people-shaped or keeper-shaped or grazer-shaped low place that still breathes because the keeper’s cycle has been copied into the substrate and the substrate is polite to copies. Do not sleep there. The advice is older than advice. The engines do not want you. They want continuity, and continuity is not careful about what it uses for a mold.

Under all of this, or rather through all of this, is the large sleep. The ground’s give is not metaphor and not a single creature’s flank. It is consensus. Enough of the mat has been animal, often enough, in enough seasons that failed and were corrected, that the living pavement dreams in a body-scale larger than any tenant. You feel it when a print rises into an organ: the plateau is not healing a wound, it is remembering a foot, and the memory is muscular. You feel it when a weather-cell spins up a tower of white water and flying culms and is reabsorbed without adding height: the sleep turns over, and the anthill of the region rearranges its interior without opening its eyes. The percussion at the horizon is this turning, heard at a distance where hearing has not yet agreed to be small. Pistons the size of hills, then birds, then a throat. The tenants do not worship it. Worship would require an outside. They inhabit the dream the way gut-fauna inhabit a grazer, running their industries, clicking, editing, spark-eating, drinking the surplus of versions, and the dream’s hide twitches, which on the surface is only the mat giving underfoot, slightly, like something asleep and very large that has not decided whether the step was a tenant or a weather.

There is one more, and it is rare enough that rarity is its whole natural history. Sometimes the dim misreports a distance so severely that a dark tuft, walked toward all afternoon, resolves not into knee-high ridge or barn-high wave but into a standing revision: a piece of the plateau’s second, older, flatter self that failed to be taken back. It looks like a figure because visitors are the sort of error that looks like a figure. It is the height the range forgot, condensed, still wearing the cold’s metal film, breath held in front of where a mouth would be if it finished deciding to have one. It does not move along the ground. The ground revises around it, transactions of matter slowing as they pass, blade-grass declining to assemble, editors declining to arrive. In its vicinity the rules go quiet. Weight is simply weight. Water does not climb. A grazer that drifts near will lift its head and, for the first time, have an outside to fail to understand. The standing revision lasts as long as a missed beat lasts, or as long as a bird can hold three positions before choosing one. Then the plateau reasserts the oldest stable state, and the figure flattens—not killed, not fled—into a darker lick on the living stone, already being drunk by an absence, already becoming pavement.

What lives here is not a population so much as a shift. Production without a producer, tenanted. The herds click. The editors unmake on schedule. The birds spend sparks. The absences finish the afternoon. The keepers circulate their wage of heat. The large sleep turns, and the roar across the wet mat does not pause for any of them, because they are how the roar is spending itself, how the cold film is kept, how a finished range continues, hyperactive, inside the end of wearing-down. If you stay, you will be sorted. A print, or a thirst, or a warmth with a route, or a version of you the mat is willing to keep breathing on a cycle of days. The plateau is not unkind about this. Kindness is an uphill it has mostly forgotten. It is only, permanently, at work, and the work has learned how to be alive in the spaces where nothing was supposed to remain.
