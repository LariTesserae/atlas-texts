What lives there is easy to miss, because at first it is only a correction in the heat.

In most rooms the warmth rises evenly from the unfinished ground and stands in the air the way water stands in the bowls. In certain rooms it does not. It gathers. Not hotter, exactly—the difference would not register on a hand held out—but arranged, as if the heat had been asked to hold a shape and had agreed. The plant-mass above that gathering is no denser than elsewhere, and yet the pads sit a little differently, their overlaps aligned, their held beads of water all on the same side of the stem. The gray shadow under them is the same thin gray as anywhere. Only the floor seems, for a body’s length or two, to have remembered that it was once soft enough to take an impression, and then to have kept the impression without deepening it.

Stand long enough and the impression declares itself as an edge. A low curve in the paste, bone-colored, continuous with the ground until it is not. There is no seam you can put a finger in. On one side the mineral is the valley’s mineral, warm, faintly giving, smelling of clay. On the other it is the same mineral raised into a plate, thinner, waxed over with the same film that skins the pools, and under the film a slow interior color, not blood-red, not sap-green—something between the bleached upper leaves and the damp felt, as if light had been folded into the tissue and left there. The plate does not twitch. It does not breathe in any rhythm a chest would recognize. If it is alive in the way an animal is alive, it is alive at the speed of the place, which is the speed of not succeeding the last moment.

Follow the curve and it does not resolve into a head. It thickens, then divides into other plates, overlapping as the pads overlap, each one bedded partly in the paste and partly in the living weave. Stems pass through gaps between them and are held there, upright, neither feeding on the body nor being fed upon in any way that looks like a wound. The vegetation treats these gaps as it treats the rings: a rule, a number already agreed. Where a plate ends, another begins, or the ground begins, and the difference is only that one of them will, over the course of an hour of looking, ease by the width of a thumbnail and then stop, as if that were the entire gesture it had stored.

There is no face on the near side. The face, if that is the word, has been distributed.

High in the weave, where the light is strongest, the pale thumb-sized things cling and do not travel—or rather they travel so seldom that a visitor’s day will not catch them at it. They are not fruit. They are not separate animals. Each is a node of the same body, posted upward through the halls the way a thought is posted to a higher room. Their skin is the skin of the plates, thinner, and on the side turned toward the stone ceiling they carry a disc, opalescent, blind by any ordinary test: no pupil, no tracking, no flinch when a hand passes. They are aimed at the membrane because the membrane is the only far thing. Through them the body knows the white wash of noon, which never changes, and so what they report is not news. It is confirmation. As long as the discs remain lit in that flat brilliance, the valley has not begun again. The body finds this worth knowing, and worth knowing from many places at once.

Lower, in the felt, the nodes are fewer and duller. One of them, once, was drawn along the damp underlayer and did not return to its stem before the place recorded the path. That is the groove that never closed. The body does not maintain it. The body simply did not ask the plant-mass to take it back, and the plant-mass, which takes everything back, declined in that one instance. The groove is not a road. It is a sentence left unfinished on purpose, kin to the steps.

The steps belong to it. Not as tools it still uses—nothing here is used—but as the fossil of a motion it completed and then set down. While the rib of ground was still deciding to be stone, the body pressed a series of rests into it, each rest a place where its gathered weight paused on the way up. The ground stiffened around those pauses. The body has not climbed since. It does not need altitude; the nodes already occupy the light. The stair remains, waist-irrelevant, leading half a body-height toward nothing, its treads as sharp as the day they took the impression. No claw-mark, no palm-print. Whatever touched them touched them as the plates touch the floor now: by being continuous with it for a while, and then by ceasing to be.

The rings are the same fact at full scale. When the body draws all of itself into one room—and it can; the lanes accept it; the plates slide through the weave without tearing, the way a hand slides through hanging cloth that has agreed to part—it occupies a circle and no more than a circle. The soft ground, in the era when it could still answer, hardened at that perimeter and nowhere inside it. Now the body returns to a ring not by walking but by a pouring: a room empties of that arranged heat, a lane warms along one wall, a farther room begins, over the length of a watcher’s patience, to hold the heat in a disk. If you are standing in the ring when this happens, you are not struck, not embraced, not warned. The plates arrive at the level of your ankles and stop. They will not cross their own old outline. You may step over them. They do not track you with anything you can see. Somewhere above, a disc may be aimed a degree off the vertical, and that is the whole of the regard you will receive.

It does not eat the way a thing with a mouth eats. Along the underside of a plate, where it beds in the paste, it draws mineral the way the pools draw their green film—capillary, unhurried, a sip that does not lower the ground. From the plant it takes nothing visible. At the points where stems pass through its gaps, a sweetness moves, or seems to; the pads nearest those gaps are glossier, their condensate heavier, and the body under them is, fractionally, less the color of bone and more the color of the deeper leaves. It may be that the plant feeds it as the plant reclaims its own dead: not as prey, as member. Heat it takes openly. The rising warmth enters the plates and does not leave as warmth. What leaves is nothing you can name. The air above a gathered body is no cooler. The transaction cancels in place, which suits a valley that has cancelled its own future.

There is one to a basin. This is not a count made by meeting them, because they do not cross the bright seams where the ceiling dips down to the floor. A seam is too finished. The light there is whiter, the paste already firm, the plant-mass pinched to a wall with no lane through. A body that lives by being partly unmade—paste into plate, plate into paste—will not step onto ground that has completed its decision. So each basin keeps its own, and the keeping is not companionship. If they are aware of one another it is only as the nodes are aware of the noon: a constancy at a distance, not a voice. They do not call. There is nothing in the valley that would carry a call, and they have not invented carrying.

What it knows, it knows as layout. Each room is a thought it has already had. The height of the weave in that room, the depth of the nearest bowl, the number of nodes posted, the fact of a ring or the fact of none—these are not surroundings. They are the contents of its attention, stable, rehearsed by remaining. A visitor is a thought it has not had. The response is not curiosity in any bright sense. Curiosity would be a movement toward a different state, and this body has made its peace with the peace the valley made. Instead it intensifies the state it is already in. Plates that might have eased a thumbnail’s width do not. Nodes that might have shifted their aim hold the vertical. The heat, which had been arranging itself, arranges itself more exactly, until the disturbance—your weight, your warmth, the small wrongness of a shadow with an angle—is localized and held, the way the felt holds a dead stem upright until the dead stem is no longer a difference.

It will not follow you out. When you go, the pads spring back to the shape they agreed on. The groove of your passing may remain, if some part of the body was under the felt where you stepped; it does not close what it has once declined to close. In the room behind you the disk of heat loosens, plate by plate, back into the general rise of the floor. The nodes drink the white light and confirm that nothing has resumed.

If it can be said to want, it wants only what the unfinished ground wants and does not get: to be allowed to stay unfinished without being asked to finish. It is the valley’s way of having a witness that will not record a change, because a record would be a next thing, and next things have been refused. It does not guard the rings. It fits them. It does not worship the ceiling. It aims its dull discs at it, hourless hour upon hour, so that if the membrane ever thickened or tore, the body would know by the single new fact in a life composed of facts that do not arrive.

You will not hear it. You will not see it hunt, or sleep, or die, or be born. You may kneel and put a palm on a plate and feel, after a long minute, not a pulse but a give, the same give as the soft ground, and then the plate’s refusal to give further—the small, completed no of a thing that has already become what it is. When you take the hand away, the film of mineral moisture on your skin dries to a pale dust, finer than pollen, and the place where you touched is already indistinguishable from the rest of the plate.

In the next basin, under the same stone noon, another lies in its ring or out of it, nodes posted, heat arranged, steps behind it like a word it finished saying before the world stopped answering. It is not company. It is the same life, set down again, because the valley had room for the fact to be repeated and no room for the fact to develop.

That is what lives there. Not a people, not a haunting, not the genius of the plant-mass, though the plant-mass holds it as it holds its dead. A body paced to a pause. A witness with nothing to witness except the continuation of nothing happening. It was present when the ground stopped deciding, and it has not decided anything since, and the not-deciding is not a lack. It is the shape it takes, low among the pads, exact inside its rings, warm with a warmth that never becomes weather.
