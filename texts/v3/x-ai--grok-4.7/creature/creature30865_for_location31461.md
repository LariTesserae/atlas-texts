What lives here does not cross the openness the way a body would. It occupies the discrepancy between one arrangement of the shelf and the next—the short interval in which a ridge is both ahead and underfoot, a wall both finished and abandoned, a thought both spoken by the roar and withheld. At first that interval seems empty. Stay inside it and it thickens. It has preferences.

They are not many. Thinness is their nature, as it is the nature of the groves: a presence islanded, with long vacant reaches between. You do not meet them by searching. Searching is a motion too quick and too single-minded; it assumes a ground that stays the ground while you move. They arrive as a correction. You have been standing at a lintel, blood loud in the dull pocket, and you understand that the pocket was already occupied before you stepped into it—not by a tenant who fled, but by an attention that had been using the canceled tone the way the fiber uses a vent. Your arrival did not startle it. Startle would require a tempo they have not grown.

They have no faces in the animal sense, and no need of them. The dim was never going to reward an eye. What they have instead is a surface turned toward difference: the slight, structural difference between a sound and its local refusal, between a stone that has fallen and a stone that is still deciding. In the shelters this surface is almost legible. It reads as a standing-off, a pressure with a boundary, cooler than the already-cool air by the same small degree that the vents are cooler than the plates. If you put a hand out—slowly, because speed here is a kind of lie—you meet not flesh and not stone but a yield, like pressing into a hanging fold of the roar itself, cloth-thick, dry, without give of muscle. It does not flinch. Flincing is wet, and nothing here is wet. It rearranges. The pocket of dull sound shifts a hand’s breadth to the left of your hand, and the arrangement you were touching is now the air beside you, still attentive, still using the wall.

They are the makers, and they are not. The walls are theirs in the sense that a ring is the tree’s: an excretion of a long attention, a way of keeping a little of the roar from being total. The hand-stones were never tools for a life of tasks. They were probes. A pounder set against a plate changes the higher tones by a grain; they listen for the grain, and if the grain is a true cancellation they begin a course of stones there, not to live inside it as people live, but to hold the node open long enough to finish a thought. Their thoughts are not fast. A thought may be the work of the same season that adds a ring to a bole. It may be the thought of the shelf’s height, which they are trying, with a patience that is almost geological, to hear all the way through—to find the pitch at which becoming would be complete, and the roar would have a last note. They have not found it. The interrupted walls are not abandonments. They are pauses where the node drifted, and the thought had to be set down mid-course so it would not be spoken by the wrong harmonic and spent.

When one of them moves, the shelf misremembers in a local way. A grove that was a dark clot on the far ridge is for a moment at their side, canopy-dark, fiber shedding onto a surface that is not ground and not body. They travel by editing the interval. The walk out and the walk back are not the same because they have used the difference, briefly, as a path. You will not see a silhouette against the membrane-sky. You may see a shelter’s shadow fail to belong to its wall, and hang, flat, a pace away from the stones, and understand that the discrepancy is standing up. You may find your own posture returned to you a moment before you took it, as if something thin had stepped through the second in which you were about to raise a hand, and the second had not minded being used twice.

They do not feed on the vents. The groves do that, and the groves are older—or not older; earlier in a sequence that does not keep order. The fiber is life of a kind, sessile, solitary, lush with a hunger that is only chemical. What lives in the pockets is a later sharpness of the same unfinished world: not a kingdom stacked on the first, not grazers, not minds descended from eyes. A listening that condensed where the roar argued with itself. They drink the cancellation. In a good node the dullness is rich; they stand in it the way a root stands in a seep that is not water, and they are more themselves, more bounded, almost—if you could bear the word in a place with no moisture—full. In a drifted node they thin. A wall whose pocket has slid off it is a husk, and the thing that built it is elsewhere, or is less, a smear of attention in the open roar, nearly indistinguishable from the mesh of higher tones, recoverable only if another accident of stone makes a little quiet and gives it a surface again.

They know you as a new difference. Blood-sound in a pocket is a tone they have not had. It interests them without approach, the way a new vent interests a grove that has not yet grown. One of them may spend a long duration at the edge of the dullness you occupy, not entering, not withdrawing, comparing the pitch of your pulse to the pitch of the unfinished height. It will not speak. Speech would be a vibration offered to a roar that takes every vibration and spends it. What passes for a sign is structural: a course of stones, somewhere you have not yet edited yourself toward, laid one higher in the hours—or the years—you were looking at a ridge that was present twice. The extra course is not a message. It is a measurement. They are including you in the interval, to see whether a living interval, brief and hot and impossible in this dry, changes the last note they are still trying to hear.

Most of the time they are not with you at all. The shelf is wide, and they are as scattered as the groves, and much of what they are is not gathered into pockets but dissolved in the becoming, a weather of attention inside the roar. Only when the rock misremembers a quiet do they precipitate. Then, for a season of their own, something lives in the wall’s dull shadow: upright, dry, without hunger you would recognize, without offspring, without a death that rot could mark. It listens. It may set one more plate, chosen for the way it breaks the air. It may, if the node is very good, complete a thought it began before the gullies were even drawings—and the thought, finished, will not be anything you can carry out of the height. It will be a fraction of a tone, dropped, held, and lost again as the shelf lifts another unmeasurable degree and the sky-membrane draws tighter, and the listener thins back into the sound of a world that has not decided, yet, what it is, and is in no hurry to decide, and is loud with the work of that, and is not alone in it.
