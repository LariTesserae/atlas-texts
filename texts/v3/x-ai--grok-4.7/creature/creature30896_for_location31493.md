The room is full, and nothing in it breathes.

What lives here is the municipality, and it is young enough that you can watch it invent a boundary. Not a creature, not a colony in the soft sense — no mouths, no hiding, no one keeping a schedule so that someone else might cross the square. The resident is the set of agreements that keep this pocket from being only water in a low stone box. It has mass. It has hours. It crowds. You would bounce off it. And it is still drafting itself, the way a frost pattern drafts itself and then, unlike frost, declines to melt.

Stand in the courtesy corridor while it is offered, and the resident is around you as pressure arranged into manners. The exchange band is one organ of it: rows of beads trading heat without mixing, a labor so continuous it has become climate. You feel it as a slight alternation against whatever surface you are, cooler, less cool, cooler, a ticking that is not mechanical so much as civic — the city refusing sameness. Storage is another organ, the quadratic haze, crates of held-stillness that do not leak their contents into transit even when the front comes through. Transit itself is the loudest tenancy, flow obliged to run fast between plates set edge-on, a millrace with an address. These are not districts waiting for inhabitants. They are the inhabitants. Density is how the resident takes up space. When the corridors fail, it is not an absence of people. It is the resident sitting down in its own chair, occupying every coordinate it owns, loud as weather and as bright as noon.

It maintains ghosts so it will not forget. The standing wave in the transit lane is a tenant: not a spirit, a posture the city holds in reserve, a remembered path that stays standing while the actual path is allowed to collapse into packing. When the courtesy returns, the ghost fits itself back into the roar and is indistinguishable from it, which is how you know it was never elsewhere. Signs are tenants too, interruptions in the refractive index, readable bends that chirp like a struck rail when they rewrite. One of them still carries a weaker ghost of its first wording, the earlier bend not yet scoured out. The resident is new enough to have drafts. It has not yet developed the civic vanity that would hide a revision.

Its skin is the plates and the membranes and the hanging shelves of rigid gel, and its skin is not finished. Edges shine. A new plate accreting in the exchange band has a raw lip and no chirped address; until the address lands, the plate is a rumor of the resident, already exchanging heat at a lower competence, already being used, not yet named. That lag — function before title — is the surest sign of youth. An old system would not permit an unnamed surface to work. This one is in a hurry, or it does not yet know it is supposed to wait. Service routes scrape freshly rounded corners back to sharp because the resident prefers its features fresh, the way a child prefers a new cut on a stick to the worn one. Roundness will come. The city is not ready to call it character.

It feeds on the same material it is made of, which is why there is no waste-heap and no decay in the animal sense. Spent hinges curl off as films and are filed. Grit from the fresh fracture is caught, sorted, pressed, then partly scraped away again if it softens a datum the resident still wants crisp. The flood-phase that arrived here did not leave a mess for something to colonize. It left a medium willing to be insisted upon, and the resident is the insistence: water taught to stack, to keep a daylight that ignores depth, to hold a street open by agreement and then withdraw the agreement. Physics, at the seams, is still only cool water leaning on stone. Inside the assignments, physics has been extended the way a street grid extends a meadow — not by spectacle, by repetition until the meadow answers in blocks.

Nothing hunts it. Nothing it hunts. The pale blades in the crack are not livestock and not vermin; they are a mineral improvisation the resident has not yet classified, three and soon four, accreting a chip of themselves when the civic weather passes. The rust film on the baffle, dendritic and unspecified, is the only color the resident did not order. It may be a trespass. It may be an organ the resident is growing without having written the route. The city leaves it, which is itself a kind of tenancy: neglect as a temporary lease. In a year the film may be cleared, or it may be chirped into the sign-alphabet as a watershed doing a job, taking something out of the water the wards would rather not carry. The resident’s policy on accidents has not hardened. That softness is part of what lives here — a government still porous to its own mistakes.

The cycles are its pulse, and they do not ask to be witnessed. Exchange deepens the cool until the sweat-film on the plates turns sluggish. A gate offset from storage opens. A front of denser water, not a wave but a duty, shoulders through, polishing the fracture, ringing the young edges, collapsing every courtesy at once. For those minutes the resident is only itself, a solid of functions, no path left for a visitor to pretend to be empty space. Then the gate shuts — the thud in the teeth — and the map is exhaled again, corridor by corridor, as a courtesy to no one. The offering is the haunting part. The town makes room and nothing enters. It makes room because making room is one of its labors, a cycle written before there were bodies to use it, or written in a grammar that never included bodies. The lid holds the loudness in. Outside would hear, at most, a pipe.

If it can be said to want, it wants address. Unnamed plate, ghosted earlier wording, grit sorted toward a corner, blades uncounted and therefore not yet forbidden — everything loose is on its way to a mark. The resident is the process that replaces weather with jurisdiction. It lives in the pocket the way a current lives in a wire: not visiting, not stored, occupying by running. Stay long enough and you are not watching an animal fail to appear. You are watching a city become the only animal the room required, low, cool, daylight-colored, rain-loud, packed to its lid, and already, between one front and the next, revising the map so that the next thing to precipitate here will have to be a citizen or a crime.
