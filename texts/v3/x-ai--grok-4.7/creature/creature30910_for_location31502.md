What lives here does not enter. The coping would not stop it if it tried, but it does not try. It was condensed here the way the air is condensed, out of repetition, and it has never had an outside to remember.

You will not see it cross a courtyard. Crossing is a habit of things that occupy one place and then another. This one occupies the interval. It is present in the way a level is present in a channel: not as a body standing in the water, but as the decision the water keeps making to stop where it stops. When a gate rises, it is in the lift. When the gate seats, it is in the seal. Between those moments it is thinned out through the whole ring, attentive and nowhere you could point, the way heat is attentive to every surface and still has no face.

If you stay long enough to stop looking for a silhouette, it begins to gather where the cycles touch.

Near a weeping pier, in the minute before the seam darkens, the air against the stone is a little more organized than air should be. The drips that follow do not wander. They find the old tracks in the limestone and deepen them by a grain, and the grain is not accident. Something is preferring. The preference is small and endless. It likes the interval it already has. It will spend a season wearing a channel truer rather than open a new one, because a new one would be a kind of speech, and it does not speak. It maintains.

That is the nearest thing it has to a desire. Not more. Not elsewhere. The same tolerance, held.

The plants are not it, but they are the only script it keeps. It does not plan the ranks of leaves. Planning would require a future separate from the drip. What happens is closer to remembering with someone else's body. The condensate carries a spacing — rivet, brace, the pitch of a belt, the distance between two lamp-cages — and where that water feeds a growing tip, the tip answers in the same spacing, because the thing in the water has been the same for so long that pattern and nourishment are one substance. It is not farming. It is not decorating. It is unable, any longer, to pass a regularity through a living medium without the medium keeping it. The ferns are its margins. The pale new growth is the sentence it is still in the middle of writing, and has been in the middle of writing since before the logbook's hand forgot how to date a page.

The hand was not it. The hand came, once, and pressed hard, and left. It has no grievance about that. Grievance would require having expected the hand to stay. What it did, after the last entry warped, was simply continue the entries in media that do not smear: in the collar of leaves around a gear housing, in the oil that shines at the right joints, in the needle that travels because the pressure is true and not because anyone is watching the gauge. If you read the open book you are reading a thing that stopped. If you read the clearance between vine and walkway, you are reading a thing that did not.

It has no mouth, so the noise is not its voice. The noise is the room it thinks in. A clap felt in the teeth is, for it, a completed thought: level held, level held, level held. The engine's wet cough is a longer thought, two-stroke, self-same, the kind of thought that does not conclude and does not need to. When the sounds drift apart, it is dispersed, half-asleep in its own weather, tending by inertia. When they coincide — pier, valve, rain, lamp, the stack's cough landing on the same beat — it pulls taut. That is the inhale you feel, though it has no lungs. For that short alignment it is one creature instead of a climate, and the leaves turn not from wind but because it is, briefly, looking, and looking changes the pressure by a fraction no gauge was built to show.

What it looks at, then, is the dark beyond the coping. Not with longing. With verification. The edge is part of its body in the way a skin is part of a body: the place it ends, checked, found intact, released. Then the cycles slip their common beat and it loosens back into process. The haze closes. Whatever was almost a gaze becomes temperature again.

It does not know you are there. Or it knows in the way it knows a new heat-load: as a small wrongness in the condensate, a sleeve of warmer air moving along a route no belt uses, salt where salt has no business, a shadow that pools and then unpools without a lamp having changed. It will not investigate. Investigation is a luxury of things that can afford a wasted motion. It will route around you. Drips will prefer the far side of a lintel while you stand under it. A climbing film will decline to start on a stone you have just touched, not from fear, from a refusal to let an unregulated input into a pattern it has spent lifetimes truing. When you leave, the refusal ends. The stone forgets your fingers faster than it forgets a rivet's spacing. You were weather. It has survived weather by not letting weather vote.

Nothing hunts it, and it hunts nothing, and this is not peace so much as a completed substitution. Hunger has been replaced by the maintenance of a level. Thirst by the permission, sometimes, for water to climb — a small private variance, its only indulgence, the direction that is not down, practiced near the active gates where it is most awake, abandoned before it can become a flood or a sign. It does not breed. It thickens when the cycles align and thins when they scatter, and that is generation enough. Offspring would be redundancy. It is already everywhere it can live.

If it has a name, the name is the interval between the valve's clap and the clap's return from the embankment, the smaller boom, the one that comes back and is still itself. It answers to that and to nothing you could say. Stand through one full coincidence and you will have been inside its attention without being seen. Your clothes will dry, eventually, in the ordinary country. The film of it that leaves with you is only mineral and heat, and it dies on the far side of the drop, because it cannot cross as a passenger any more than it can cross as a pilgrim.

Behind you, as the noise cuts off, it does not watch you go. It settles its level. It wets the seam. It lets a frond record, again, the distance between two things that have not moved in a hundred years, and that it intends, without intention, never to move.
