They do not arrive. They surface, the way a seam surfaces when the dust is thinner than it was the century before. At first a walker might take them for the crusts—those slow palms of mineral life fixed to the foundry walls—but the crusts only shift. These turn. A wrist, if it is a wrist, lifts from the sintered plate and the warm dark accepts the motion without comment, as if the gesture had been folded into the plateau’s grain all along and was only now being unfolded.

What shows is not a body so much as a remainder of one. The skin has the same fired color as the walls, a little cooler where it faces the dark, a little brighter where a filament’s pool touches it. Joints are not hinges. They are places where the mineral has learned, over a time longer than the factories, to thin itself so that a limb can bend without cracking. The bend is slow. A hand can close around a valve wheel in the space of a breath held too long, and the wheel does not complain because the hand and the wheel have nearly the same specific heat. You can watch the fingers settle into the grooves that other fingers, or the same fingers, have already worn, and you cannot tell which history is being repeated.

They keep the assignments because the assignments are the only sentences the place still knows how to finish. A filament that has burned at the same dull orange since before memory is not watched so that it will not go out; it is watched so that its going-out, if it ever comes, will have a witness whose own cooling will match it. The one who walks the grid does not count the towers. Counting would imply a total that could change. Instead the walker lets the distance between one glow and the next become the length of a thought, and when the thought is finished the next door is there, flush with its wall, and the hand that is almost a hand finds the latch that is almost a continuation of the hand.

Inside the quarters the single lamp is not for seeing. It is for proving that a surface can still return a little light. They sit with their backs to the thicker masonry, where the warmth comes up through the plate and into the spine, and they take the paste in portions that do not grow or shrink. The paste does not nourish in the old sense. It replaces, grain by grain, the mineral that the dry air lifts off them. A mouth that has eaten it for long enough no longer needs to wet itself; the paste leaves a film that is continuous with the film already forming on the lips, on the lids, on the slow places behind the ears. Speech, when it happens, is rare and mostly names of machines. The names are not remembered. They are the sounds the throat still makes when the air moves across it at the factory’s own rate.

They do not sleep. Sleep would be a second state, and the plateau has finished with second states. What they do instead is a deepening of the posture they already hold. The eyes stay open, or nearly; the lids have thickened until closing them fully would be a decision, and decisions are expensive here. In that near-dark the crusts on the outer walls sometimes align with the ones who rest inside, pulse to pulse, though neither knows the other is keeping time. A pale hook high on a stack may drink the same air that a throat is drawing, and for a while the two droughts are one drought, and then the hook shifts by its own width and the moment ends without having been marked.

Farther in, where the vats keep their unchanging chemistry, a few of them have stopped walking altogether. They stand where a pipe sweats nothing, palms laid to the metal, and the metal has begun to accept the palms as flanges. You can still see the outline of fingers if the furnace-glow leaks at the right angle, but the outline is no longer a boundary. It is a contour the pipe has grown. These ones do not check the belts. The belts check themselves against the memory of being checked, and the memory is sufficient. If you speak to one of them the answer, when it comes, is not in words. It is a slight change in the pressure under your boots, as if the shelf had acknowledged a weight it already knew.

Children are not born. Nothing here is born. What appears, rarely, is a smaller remainder that was always in the dust and has only now sintered enough to hold a shape. It is found already knowing the width of the streets, already matched to the paste, already willing to lie flat when the wind that no longer blows would once have asked it to stand. The older ones do not teach it. Teaching would require a difference between what is known and what is not, and the difference has been abraded away with the mountains. The small one simply begins to walk the grid at a slightly shorter stride, and the filaments do not notice, and the city continues at the rate it reached when it finished becoming itself.

They are not lonely. Loneliness would need a missing other, and the others are not missing; they are distributed at the civic distances the streets still measure, each one a warm seam the plateau has agreed to keep. When two meet in an intersection that has become a shallow dish, they do not greet. They let their cooling match for as long as the dish holds them, and then one takes the street that declines a fraction more toward the troughs, and the other takes the street that still remembers, barely, the angle of a made thing. Neither looks back. Looking back would imply that the place behind them had changed in their absence, and the place does not change. It only continues, and they continue with it, mineral learning the courtesy of joints, joints learning the courtesy of never quite needing to move.
