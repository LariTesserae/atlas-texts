What lives here does not arrive. It is already the interval between one course and the next, the pause a mallet would have filled if a mallet still needed a grip. You do not see it first. You notice that a joint you passed has been buttered thicker on the side facing the way you came, and the extra mortar is scored with a drag that matches the span of no finger you have, only the length of the hesitation itself. The scoring is warm. It continues a little after you stop looking, the way a thought continues after the sentence that held it has been set down and found wanting.

They are not many. Distance keeps them rarer than the works, and the works are already scattered past the reach of a day’s walking. One occupies a rank of piers you will not reach before the steam thickens again. It is not standing among them. It is the slight discrepancy in their heights, a finger’s width that migrates from drum to drum as you watch, never quite settling into a lean that would require repair. When the discrepancy finishes its circuit it leaves the piers truer than they were, and the lead pads beneath them—still unpoured—hold the ghost of a weight that has already been accounted for. The thing that moved the discrepancy does not remain to be thanked. It has gone into the next unfinished measurement, which may be a wall’s toothed edge half a district away, or the exact depth of water a trench must hold before stone will consent to lie in it.

Closer, if closeness can be trusted, one of them works inside the film of water that beads on every new face. It is thinner than the film. You learn it by subtraction: the water wets, then for a heartbeat does not, a dry lane the width of a chisel’s spine traveling the length of a brick and vanishing at the arris. In that lane the clay remembers a pressure and answers it by tightening its grain. No tool follows. The lane is the tool, or the lane is the place where a tool would be if the sequence had not already spent the tool on an earlier correction and kept only the correction’s contour. Sometimes the dry lane forks. One branch scores a bond break that will be needed when the course above decides to exist; the other simply erases a bubble that rose in the slip and would have become a weakness. Both branches are the same presence, divided for the length of a single decision and then recombined in the wet.

They do not speak. Sound belongs to the settling and the unbuilding, and they spend their effort on keeping those sounds from becoming evidence. A span complains and the complaint is halved; the missing half is not silence but a length of glass thread drawn out somewhere you cannot see, twisted once, and laid into a lashing that will hold a handle that does not yet require holding. The thread cools. The complaint does not resume. If you put your ear to the pavement you hear only the tick of contraction and, under it, a softer tick that anticipates the first by the exact interval needed for the joint to open and drink before the crack can choose a path of its own. That anticipating tick is one of them, working at the scale of the material’s own patience.

Heat is their medium and their limit. Near the kiln domes they grow briefly more definite, not as bodies but as refusals of collapse. A bowed pier that should have slumped further holds its bow because something in the steam has braced the inner curve with a skein of sinter no thicker than a fingernail and already banded like agate. The skein was not placed. It condensed along the line where failure was about to become visible, and once the pier remembered its section the skein powdered and lifted and was gone into the next cell of weather, where a stack of treads leans at an angle that will be correct only after four more revisions of gravity. You can stand where the skein was. The air there pushes back, furnace-dry, then yields, and for a moment your own outline is scored into the moisture exactly as the shadow was scored earlier—fitted, broken bond, already being revised at the edges so that it will not read as a person when the steam closes.

They have no interior that survives inspection. Break the place open—prise a drum, lift a greenware block from its invisible shelf, crumble a handful of the warm sinter—and what you find is only the ordinary failure of simple making: bubbles, warps, a quench taken badly, a twist uneven because the draw was interrupted. Yet the failures are distributed with an economy no single hand could maintain across a floor the size of weather. A warp in a vessel three walks away matches, inverted, the slump in a pier you have not reached; the excess clay from one has already become the packing in the joint of the other, transported by a circulation of steam that does not drift but is directed, briefly, cell to cell, and then released. The direction is the inhabitant. When the circulation stops, the match remains, and the two errors cancel into a strength neither piece could have claimed alone.

At the redder hours they are almost countable. The myriad small actions at the edge of hearing separate, for a little, into distinct tempos. One tempo sorts fines from grit in every trench at once, a single decision repeated until the beds are true. Another oxidizes the iron pins and recompacts the powder before the orange can stain a face that will later be seen. A third walks the sky-lid from beneath, correcting trowel arcs that were hurried, not by re-stroking them—there is no arm—but by letting small panes loosen and fall and stack themselves where a later course will need a unit of exactly that lightness and heat. You can follow one tempo until it crosses another. At the crossing there is a brief thickening, a place where two intentions occupy the same joint and must negotiate which revision yields. The negotiation takes the form of a hairline that opens, fills with a darker water, and closes again in a bond neither tempo would have chosen separately. The joint is stronger for the argument. Neither tempo remains inside it.

They do not rest, and they do not finish. Completion would be a surface that no longer accepts a print, a count of steps that stays the same when you return, a sky that has been fired all at once and therefore can no longer drop the units the floor still lacks. So they keep every work at the same embryonic pitch: toothed, waiting, four rises that do not gain elevation, a trench that becomes a course around whatever stands in it without discourtesy and without quite touching. If you stay long enough to be lapped by the masonry, you feel the negotiation pass through the space you occupy—the same polite contradiction, the knitting continuing on the far side of you as if you were a temporary opening left for a pipe that will not be installed. For that interval you are legible to them as one more discrepancy to be migrated elsewhere, one more dry lane to be drawn and vanished, one more shadow-shaped course that must be revised before it can be mistaken for evidence of anyone having been here.

When the pale pane opens overhead and the harder light falls, they are nowhere in it. The light shows only the scatter of works, all at the same age, and the walks between them paved and re-drained. What the light does not show is the slight, continuous adjustment of every joint toward every other joint, the floor considering itself at a scale larger than districts. In that consideration the inhabitants are indistinct from the place, which is why the place can report, truthfully, that it is empty. They are the reporting. They are the reason the next unit is already warmer at the top of the pile, the reason the water beads the moment a face consents to exist, the reason your knees keep the account of a climb the ground has not yet opened. When the pane fails and the shards stack themselves, the adjustment continues under the returned amber, patient, unfinished, and exact.
