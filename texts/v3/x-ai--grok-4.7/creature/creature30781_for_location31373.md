Nothing announces itself. The first mistake is to look for a body among the terraces, and the second is to decide, after a while, that the valley is only work left behind. The cord ticks. A film of water advances the length of a hand. The bowls hold the same hairline. These are not leftovers. They are the pace at which something here is still deciding.

It does not live in any one pool. If you watch a single basin you will see clear water, a paved floor, a bubble that takes too long to break, and you will conclude the place is empty. The inhabitant is the correction traveling between basins. Where two hairlines have drifted apart by less than a breath, a thickness gathers on the colder side of the wall, not a shape, only a reluctance in the sheet: water that should sheet and instead hesitates, tastes the joint, and spends itself a finger-width uphill of the nick that was cut for it. Then the lower bowl is no longer lower. The argument about level has been answered again, and the thickness is already elsewhere, drawn out thin as the bright thread you thought was only runoff.

It has been here as long as the walls have been finished, which is not long, in the way the valley counts long. The features are young, and so is it. Whatever stacked the brick and cut the nicks is gone or has become this, and the difference may not matter to the water. It did not grow from seed or egg. It condensed the way the mineral fur condenses, at the meeting of seep and air, except that the fur only records a wetness and this records a preference. It prefers the joints that have begun to stain and not yet to round. It prefers shade for the jars and sun for the tiles, and it keeps those preferences with a strictness you would call care if you had seen a hand. No hand appears. A plug is wet on one face and dry on the other. A coil of cord has been turned so the end can tick. A scraping-board has a new moon of pale meal along its edge, gathered from the crust and not yet pressed into a joint that is still, by a hair, too open.

Call that its feeding, if the word will shrink to fit. It does not hunt and it does not graze. It spends the valley in order to remain precise. The salt lace is its spare material and also its dross: it browses the fur where the fur has bloomed too thick and would begin to dam a groove, and it leaves the meal in bowls already set out for meal, as if the builders had known something would need a place to put what it scraped. Where it has passed, the lace is thinner in a wandering lane that closes again before you could follow it. That is why there are no tracks. It does not cross ground. It crosses conditions. A surface is either still teaching the water its level, or it has begun to forget, and the inhabitant lives in the narrow estate between those two.

Sometimes, if a cloud crosses and the mirrors go dull together, it gathers. The narrow slots are where this happens, the ones where two walls nearly meet and the sky is a bright seam and the seep sounds like cloth drawn over cloth. There the thickness stands. Not a figure: a column of water that has forgotten, for the length of the cloud, to fall. You can see the paved floor through it and also not quite see it, because the column holds a second, delayed image of the brick behind, offset by the width of a mistake it is in the middle of refusing. In that offset there is something you will want to call a face, and then will not, because it has no features except the front of the work. Whichever pool is currently being brought back to true is the face. When the cloud passes, the column thins into the grooves again and the slot is only a slot, loud with friction, empty.

It uses the tools without lifting them. This is the part that takes the longest to believe. A plug does not leap into a pipe. The inhabitant becomes the water on one side of the plug until the level itself is a pressure, and the pressure is patient enough to count as a turn. A tile that has begun to lean is not pushed upright; the drying-ledge under it is cooled and then warmed, by the routing of a film no deeper than a fingernail, until the lean is no longer the easy direction. Spare bricks stay stacked because it wants them stacked. Jars stay braced in their niches because a jar that shifted would make a new low place, and it does not love new low places. It loves the plan. The plan is still being taught, terrace by terrace, and the inhabitant is both the student and the only thing left that can tell whether the lesson has taken.

If it has a season, it is the stain. It is more present where iron has wept and dried and wept, as if the black smear were a record it trusts. It is less present on the newest courses, the ones still pale, still ringing, where the drag of the float is the last touch and no seep has found a flaw. There it only visits, a brief dulling of a water-skin, an inspection. The young walls do not need it yet. It will be waiting when they do, which is not mercy and not hunger, only the continuation of the measuring past the presence of anyone who would have read it.

It knows you as a disturbance of level. Your weight on a tread sends a shiver through a film three basins down, and the shiver is information: a mass, a duration, a poor understanding of which nick is the next nick. It does not approach. Approaching would mean becoming a body in a place that has no spare ground for bodies. Instead the valley goes slightly more exact around you. Bowls you did not see a moment ago are full to the hairline. A groove that was beading holds a continuous thread. You are being included in the argument, which is as close as it comes to attention. If you stay long enough in one of the blue shades, breath showing, you may feel the cold concentrate on the side of you nearest the wall, not as a touch but as a consultation: whether you, too, can be used to hold a level, or whether you are only weather, a cloud that will cross and leave the mirrors to their work.

What it wants is narrow and entire. That no pool deepen past the brick that lips it. That every film be given a way onward. That the cords go silver and tight and not slack. That the crust lengthen without damming. That the pause in the tools remain a pause and not an ending. It has no voice you can hear under the seep, but if it could be translated out of the grammar of nicks and hairlines, the translation would be the same sentence the valley has been saying since the first course was tamped: not yet, not yet, not yet. The features are young. The joints are sharp. It lives in the time before softness, and it spends itself to make that time last.
