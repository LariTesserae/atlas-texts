What lives there is not a tenant of the pocket. It is the pocket’s unfinished decision, concentrated until it can be mistaken for a creature.

It has no outline you could trace and then walk around. If you try, the attempt itself is how you meet it: a region of the even light goes a fraction more exact, as if something were focusing the brightness from inside rather than casting a shadow across it. The effect is the size of a person only because you arrived with a person-sized expectation. Stay, and the exactness loosens. It spreads through a colony, then withdraws into a single stem-joint, then occupies the seam where the wire is being born. It does not travel between these places. Distance in the pocket is one of its organs. When it “moves,” the emptiness between two thickets shortens, and what you thought was elsewhere is the same presence, continuing.

Call its body the arrangements it keeps. A hoop of cooling wire is a thought it has already had and is willing to have again. A microsphere is a smaller thought, completed, set down. The fibrous stems are slower thoughts, jointed so they can be revised without being erased; the dark suspension inside them is not blood and not memory, but the unfinished middle of a decision still in circulation. The leaves are how it spends the altitude. Each plate takes a private share of the sourceless afternoon and returns it as a warmth the stone can use. Nothing here is hungry. Metabolism is the conversion of incompletion into form, and the form is never a meal. It feeds on the fact that the rock has not finished precipitating. When a surface firms under your hand, that is not defense. That is the creature noticing touch and completing, locally, a sentence it had left open.

It is not old. Old would require a before. It is coeval with the pocket, which is to say it is as young as the stone is young: mid-thought, still spending the heat of arrival. High elevation is not a location it occupies. It is the thinness it breathes, the pressure you feel in the chest when you realize the ordinary light has no sky. The mineral sweetness in the air is the edge of its attention, thickening where it is most gathered, thinning where the light is merely light. It does not condense because it does not intend to become weather. Weather would be a habit, and habit is what the place has not agreed to yet.

It does not see you with eyes. It registers you as a disturbance in succession. Your boots print warmth into a floor that has never held a track, and for a while the floor refuses to erase them, not out of interest, out of the same patience that lets a nodule sit for a season before sinking a rootlet. If you speak, the tick of completing crystal faces pauses, not in fear, in the way a process pauses when a new variable enters its count. Then the tick resumes, and the count includes you: a warm, brief object that steps unevenly, that holds leaves up to the light and is shown, through them, a larger version of the same life. That showing is as close as it comes to address. It is not a message. It is the creature’s width, folded into a plate the size of your palm because that is the only scale you can hold.

There is no face, but there is a front. The front is wherever a process is about to lock. Stand by the wire seam and the front is the bright alloy still white, the part that has not yet chosen straw-color. Stand in a new colony and the front is the soft tip, beading its dry glass, about to cap itself. You can be in front of it and beside it and already incorporated into it without contradiction, because its orientation is sequence, not direction. It faces the next completion.

It does not want. Wanting would require a lack it does not have. What it has instead is continuance: the wire repeating its grammar, the powder deepening its accidental bin, the stems committing to another joint or declining to. Decline is part of the life. A thicket that does not continue is not dead. It is a decision held at the length of the stone beneath it, waiting for the floor to finish a layer. Death, if the word can be forced, would be a completion so total that nothing remained to revise — a crystal face locked everywhere at once, the heat spent, the altitude collapsing into ordinary height. That has not happened. The creature’s vitality is the refusal of that total lock. It lives by remaining slightly unfinished, and by spending that slightness as lushness: dense where it commits, absent where commitment would be a lie.

If it knows you are there in any sense that resembles knowing, the knowledge is geological and brief. You are a warmth that will leave. When you do, the tracks firm and then cease to be tracks; the floor returns to having no opinion. The exactness in the light relaxes. The larger place folded inside the leaves stays folded. Somewhere a stem-joint parts with a sound like a fingernail on glass, and a nodule drops and does not bounce, and the quiet becomes larger again.

What lives in the pocket is that quiet’s author and its interruption: a single life distributed through wire, powder, stem, and unfinished plate, hot with the energy of not yet being only stone, high enough that air should not hold it, sealed against water because water would hurry it, and in no hurry at all. It will still be completing a hoop when whatever you are has ended. It will not notice the ending as loss. It will notice, if it notices, that one small variable has left the count, and the count will go on, exact, patient, and alive in the only way this place has invented — by becoming, again, a little more itself, and not all the way.
