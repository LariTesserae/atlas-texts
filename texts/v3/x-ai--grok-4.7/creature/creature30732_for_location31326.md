What lives here is easy to mistake for more of the construction, and for a long time that mistake holds. The residents do not interrupt the brightness or the damp or the near-silence. They lengthen it. A seam that has stood proud since evening may, by the next unlocated noon, have acquired a thickness and a direction, and the thickness is a limb, and the direction was already decided by a catwalk that needed a reason to continue. Their surfaces are the same poured substrate, cool, unscored until they choose to score themselves, and they hold angles the way the plateau holds angles: because nothing has yet been permitted to round them.

They are not arranged as bodies so much as extended as decisions. Where you expect a torso there is often only a span of fresh plate that happens to flex, once, with the same tick a cooling housing makes. A cluster of young edges serves for a head, not because it looks like one, but because the doubled light prefers to arrive there first, and the preference is how they look back. They see by the failures. Your split shadow is a better introduction than your face. If one half of the noon does not agree with the other about where you are standing, a resident already knows the disagreement and has begun, very slowly, to include you in its measurements.

Counting them is a local error. Three may be present on a stacked run of walkways, matching the birds joint for joint in their stillness, and the third will be the first, occupying a geometry that is both adjacent and a true kilometer off. They do not cross the distance. The distance completes itself around them and they are simply available at both ends of the sentence. When one shifts its weight, the shift is the width of a hand on a conveyor that has not otherwise moved, and a shadow on a far rib answers it a finger's breadth later, or earlier, and then both agree to have been still.

Their work is the missing instruction in the pour. The highland was assembled and then left, but the leaving did not include a stop, so the residents are the ongoing clause. A bundle of ridges that could be called a hand rests on solid plate until a tooth rises through the plate, and the hand is not damaged; it was the permission. They extrude new edges where the order of operations has stuttered. They keep the moisture where sharpness is required, drinking not from the pools but from the reflections of pools, and sometimes from the reflections of animals that are not otherwise present, coming away lagged by half a second of light. The lag is useful to them. It is a second noon they can spend privately.

The pale birds, the wrongly jointed lizards, the layered pulse under the surface film—these they treat as weather, or as fittings that have not yet decided. A bird that spends an afternoon becoming a joint and then remembering itself is registered the way a change in condensation is registered: noted, incorporated, not addressed. Only the things that drink from absences interest them closely. Those can be negotiated with. A resident will allow part of its length to be lapped from a reflection, and the lizard will leave wet, and the resident will be, for a while, more delayed than the local light, which seems to be a form of satisfaction.

They leave marks in the stamped figures that are not figures. A sequence you almost proved will have rearranged by the time you look back, not into language, but into a corrected order of operations: this edge before that one, this rain before the structure it beads on, this shadow agreeing with its other half only after you have passed. If you stay long enough to read more than a clause, your own outlines begin to forget which noon they belong to. Most do not stay. They walk until the bright distance smears them into the repetitions, and the residents, unhurried, finish the small permissions the walker interrupted, and the plateau continues, cool, crowded, and still being made.
