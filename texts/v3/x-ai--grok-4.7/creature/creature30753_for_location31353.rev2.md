The film is the first citizen, and it does not look like one. It has no edge you can call a body. It is a thirst that has learned a shape: a dry bloom no thicker than a fingernail-paring, rust where the gas is iron, pale where the gas is sulfur, and in the seams a near-black that is only thickness. It does not grow toward the slit. The slit is an insult. What it wants is the exhale, and it takes it the way a wick takes oil, continuously, without a season. Scrape it and it is not wounded so much as interrupted. The pale stone shows, and the next pulse paints the interruption shut, the same chemistry arriving in the same places, as if the wall remembers the stain more faithfully than it remembers the rock. Left alone it would glaze the whole nave. It is not left alone. That is the second fact of the place, and it is older than hunger as we mean hunger. Something has been eating the plenty since the plenty learned to persist.

What eats it is low, and plural, and finished with the idea of a single outline. An individual, if you insist on lifting one—which the mass resents, not with a cry but with the suction of neighbors—is a shallow vault of plates, wider than it is tall, the color of the dust it wears. The plates are not armor in the proud sense. They are roof. Heat would split a wetter animal; these have made their outsides into clinker, a little glass of their own, fused at the seams of growth and then sanded by other plates until the animal is a cobble that breathes. Under the rim of the vault the underside is softer, though soft is a cruelty of language here: a pad of ridged tissue, almost the color of the film, pressed to stone or to another back, combing. The combs are the mouth. They are also the hands. There is no reaching. There is only the pass of the body over the bloom, a slow file, and what comes away is not swallowed so much as taken into a gutter that runs the length of the underside and closes on its catch the way a book closes. You do not see chewing. You see a pale track, and the animal already a palm's width farther along, and the track clouding back before the animal has cleared its own length.

They have eyes, and the eyes are an old mistake they have not paid to correct. A bead, set where a head would be if the vault had a front in any lasting way. The bead does not swivel. It drinks the slit when the slit is above it and learns nothing from the lesson. What steers is a stippling along the plates, pits no wider than pores, and each pit is a small argument about temperature. A seam waking a handspan off is a shout. Another body, warmer from a recent pulse, is a murmur. You, standing, are weather. The stippling does not agree all at once; that is why a single animal seems to hesitate, one flank lifting, the vault tipping, the comb leaving the film and finding it again a finger to the left. Hesitation is the only private act they have. Once the flanks agree, the animal is decision, and the decision is small, and it is immediately revised by the body beside it.

Beside it is the true skin. A loner does not occur except as damage. Pull one free—if the stacks allow it, if your cold well does not simply make them flow into the vacancy—and it does not flee. It lowers the vault until the plates kiss stone and it stops combing. The stippling goes quiet in a way that looks like dying and is only the loss of neighbors' heat, which it had been using as a map. Set it back into the scale and the map returns in the time it takes the nearest plates to accept its temperature. It joins the lean toward the waking seam as if it had never been a noun. They do not call to each other. There is no call that would survive the glass. What they share is the gradient, and the gradient is public, and so the mind, if that word is not too proud, is public too: a preference distributed across a thousand vaults, none of which could draw the circle they are walking.

Young are not carried. They are shed. Along the inner edge of a vault, where the plates overlap like badly laid shingles, a seam splits that is not a wound, and what comes out is a scale already, a coin of the same clinker, sticky with a dryness that is not wetness—a mineral mucus, white, that flashes to powder when the gas finds it. The coin does not drop. Dropping is a luxury of empty air, and there is no empty air below the first layer. It is pressed into the nearest crack between backs, and there it combs at a scale too fine for the eye, taking the film that the larger vaults smear rather than finish. Most coins never become vaults. They are the ration of the longer animals, or they are simply tiled under, kept alive by leakage of heat and bloom, a bedding of almost-lives that makes the floor resilient. The ones that thicken do it by accretion, plate over plate, no metamorphosis you could celebrate. There is no moment of arrival into adulthood. There is only a day when the wheel begins to count you in the lean, and you cannot say which day it was.

The longer animals are the correction.

They are not many. The basin could not afford many, and it has known this so long that the number holds the way a watermark holds. You will not see one whole. What you see is the parting: a line in the scale that opens with the reluctance of a healed cut, and in the line a back that is not a vault. Longer, yes—three vaults, four—but the length is not the difference that matters. The difference is that it does not comb. Its underside is a keel. The keel is ridged for a different work, and the ridges face backward, so that what the body passes over is gathered rather than grazed. Coins, and the thin vaults, and whatever has gone still enough to be mistaken for stone. It does not bite in any way a mouth explains. The keel rolls its catch inward and the plates of its own back—darker, fewer, worn to a polish by the squeeze of the mass—do not shift while it feeds. Stillness is how it hides inside an animal that is all shift. When the pulse comes and the columns rise, the longer one rises inside a column and is, for that interval, architecture. Animals stack on it and do not know. It knows them as weight and as future, and it is patient with a patience that is not virtue. Patience is the shape of its keel.

It is not cruel and it is not kind, and the basin has no use for the distinction. Where it has passed, the scale seals, and the seal is already combing, and the numbers that matter—the thickness of the film, the density of vaults, the heat that reaches the next layer—remain inside the old bounds. A longer one that grew greedy would thicken the bloom, and the bloom, thickened, would crust, and the crust would blunt the keel, and the keel would starve in the room it had emptied. None of them have discovered this as a thought. The ones that would have discovered it are not among the number that holds.

There is a third kind, and it is almost not a kind.

It lives at the arc.

The fitted stones on the western wall are too clean. Not clean of dust—nothing is—but clean of the film's blacker stain, and clean of the pale tracks, in a band that rises exactly to the height a vault can climb and no farther. The avoidance is perfect, which is how you know it is not a decision renewed each hour. Decisions here are slow and visible as eddies. This is a law worn past the need to be obeyed in the present tense. And yet the band is maintained. Film tries it and is taken off. Not by the wheel; the wheel parts and passes. By something that keeps to the lee of the arc, where the three stones throw a pocket of slightly wrong heat—a cool the size of a curled hand, permanent, because the stones were set to baffle a seam that no longer breathes where it used to.

What keeps the pocket is smaller than a vault and older in its habits. It has plates, but they are not a roof so much as a lid it can lower until it is a stain on the stone. It combs, but only the trespass: the tongue of film that crosses into the band, the coin that drifts into the lee and would, in a generation, teach the wheel that the arc is only stone. It eats these with a fineness the wheel has lost, a picking rather than a pass. Then it settles, lid down, and its stippling—if it still has the pores; they are few, and rimmed, as if half-sealed on purpose—reads the pocket and is satisfied when the pocket is wrong in the old way. It does not join the rotation. Hours, the wheel climbs to the arc and splits and rejoins, a river around a pier, and the lid does not rise. You could believe the pier includes it. Belief lasts until the film feathers over the boundary. Then the lid lifts by a margin too small for triumph, the comb works, the boundary is restored, and the animal is stone again.

It is not a priest. Nothing has told it the scratches are a sentence. It cannot read them; reading is a heat it does not have. But it lives where a sentence was worried into the glass, and its whole economy is the preservation of a small incorrectness—a cool that should have filled in, a path that should have blurred, a ring of pebbles near the throat that it does not tend and does not cross. The pebbles are outside its pocket. Other lids, kin or the same habit in another body, hold other pockets you will not find unless you learn the basin's minor weathers. They do not visit each other. Visiting would require a reason made of something other than gradient, and they have declined that invention, or finished it and set it down.

If one of them remembers the hands that stood the stones, the memory is not in a mind. It is in the height of the band. A vault climbs only so far; a hand would have set a mark at the height a knee makes when a person crouches to fit a rock. The animal keeps the mark because the mark is a temperature, and temperature is the only scripture left that can still be practiced. The hands are not here. What the hands became, if they became anything that stayed, is this: a lid over a baffled seam, combing trespass, refusing the wheel without opposing it. You could live beside the rim and never grant it a self. It would not argue. Argument is a sound, and the glass eats sound, and the animal has put its voice into a boundary so slight you can lay a palm across it and feel only stone, until the stone under your palm is warmer on one side by a degree the place has decided, across an unremembered number of pulses, to keep.
