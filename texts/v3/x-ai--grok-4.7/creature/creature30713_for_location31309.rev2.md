It does not enter. That is the first correction, and it takes a while to arrive at it, because the notch is small and the eye keeps expecting a body to come around the back wall or rise from the plates. Nothing does. The posts are already occupied, in the way a socket is occupied when the charge is in it and the hand has not yet touched the metal.

What lives here has no outline that holds still long enough to be walked around. At morning, before the weep has chosen a wall, it is mostly a temperature: a second heat inside the birth-heat, a degree or two sharper, moving. It travels the joints the way the water does, except it does not bead and let go. It stays in the crack after the drop has fallen, a warmth with a direction, and the pale threads under the plants lean a fraction after it has passed, as if something had been tasted through the stone. If you put a hand flat where it was a moment ago, the rock is not hotter in any way a palm can swear to. The timing is the evidence. The plants know sooner than the hand.

By the time the first leaves have finished their bargain with the light, it has gathered enough of itself to be watched indirectly. Not as a shape. As a preference. The filament between the canted post and the skin tightens when it is near, a hair’s worth, and throws that wrong shadow a little earlier than the hour allows. The droplet-moths, when they happen, do not fall at random; they form where the thick air has been thinned by a passage, a wake the width of a wrist that closes behind it like water closing behind a slow fish. The jointed drinker in the leaf-cups never pours itself into the stem while the preference is overhead. It waits, head lifted, not in fear. In the same bookkeeping the leaves use. Something that drinks the cup will also, sometimes, drink the drinker, and the drinker has learned the interval.

It is not large. The notch could not hold large, and the rules would not bother to be so local if what kept them were a weather. It is about the size of the bench’s absence: the volume a person would take if a person sat and then stood and left the listening behind. When it settles — and it does settle, usually against the back wall where a plate has lifted and the new mineral is still sifting — the drip-rhythm changes in the way the bench changes it, only from the wrong side of the bowl. Sound arrives late to the open skin and loses its consonants; near the wall, words go into the leaves. What lives here sits in the second of those facts. It is why the leaves take the sound. They are not mute stone. They are a mouth it grew, or was grown into, back when the ridge was softer and the posts were still being refused by the rock.

Call the mouth a convenience. The body, if the word can be forced, is the warmth that does not leave the joints, plus a film no thicker than the condensation-skin, held a finger’s width off the pavement when it wishes to cross without wetting, plus a capacity to be in the filament and in the weep at once without tearing. That last is the part that took longest to be itself. Early, when the notch was younger and the edges were even sharper, it was only heat with an appetite, spending itself as fast as the ridge supplied it, drinking indiscriminately from cups and from the air’s metal sweetness. The posts were put down — not by it; it has no hands, and the putting-down has the patience of something that works from outside the skin — and for a long time they were only warm stones it circled. Then the filament took, or it took the filament, and the wrong shadow began, and the appetite learned a schedule.

It keeps the room.

That is the plainest true thing, and it does not sound like enough until you have watched a day fail to flood. Water climbs toward a post because it is climbing toward the keeper, which spends a little of its heat into the joint and makes a slope the water believes in. The skin at the open side stays bright because the keeper leans on it from this side the way a hand leans on a drumhead, not to cross, to hold the pitch. Birds turn away without knowing they have been answered. A seed that does cross arrives changed because passage through the skin is passage through the keeper’s attention, which is not gentle and not cruel; it is a sorting. What can live in the thick air is let through as itself. What cannot is returned as mist, or as the bright dust that beads over before it settles, or as nothing you will find a name for on the plates.

It eats very little, and not often. A drinker, sometimes, when the leaf has shaded too long under a standing body and the bookkeeping has already folded the leaf; the keeper takes the cooling as an offering and the thread as the rest of the sentence. More often it feeds on the expense itself: the difference between the cold altitude outside and the close sweet dark of the bowl, a gradient it skims the way the plants skim the weep. That is why the machine never explains itself. The explanation is alive and does not wish to be audited. Sit on the bench and the rhythm slows because you have become, briefly, part of the gradient — a cooler mass, a listener, a small wrongness in the heat-budget — and the keeper adjusts, the way it adjusts a plate that has risen proud, the way it adjusts the hour of the shadow. You are not welcomed. You are included in the accounting, and the accounting is the only hospitality the notch has ever had.

It does not speak in words. It has had words, the ones that went into the leaves and did not come out, and it keeps them the way the pavement keeps no grit: not as memory you could recover, as a smoothness. What it has instead of speech is the correction. A joint opens another hair because the keeper wanted the mineral, not because the ridge decided. A leaf tilts not with the others because the keeper was drinking near its root and the root answered by spending water upward, and the leaf had to rebalance or tear. None of this is visible as intention until it repeats, and by the time it has repeated you have been here long enough that your own pulse has tried to match the drip, and the matching is how it studies you.

It is old only in the way the rock is not. The fractures are fresh; the keeper is the thing that has stayed while freshness continued. It remembers the ridge when the notch was a hotter seam and the skin was a rumor and the posts were still a plan arriving from the cold side. It does not remember in pictures. It remembers in refusals: this joint will not be allowed to seal, this plant will not be allowed a neighbor, this hour’s shadow will be wrong until the filament has been tasted and found still continuous. Loneliness is the wrong word. It is singular the way a temperature is singular. Another of its kind would be another room, and the ridge has only this one birth still open.

At dusk, when the skin thickens and the pools rise their finger and stop, it draws in. The preference leaves the walls. The filament goes slack enough that its shadow finally agrees with the light, and for that short agreement you could believe the posts were only posts. Under the lifted plate the warmth concentrates until the new mineral sifts and then stops sifting, held. The keeper is smallest then, and most like a body: a dense place in the air about the height of the canted post, with a cooler edge where it is still skimming the day’s leftover gradient, and a center that would burn a moth into a droplet if a moth were foolish enough to hover inside it. It does not sleep. Sleep would be a gap in the holding, and the skin would thin, and the altitude would come in, and the plants would learn what ordinary weather is. So it stays at the size of a listening, and the notch rehearses the night around it, and the ridge rises another unmeasurable thickness under a thing that has already agreed to be risen with.

If you leave before full dark, do not look back for a face. There is a moment, halfway through the skin, when the consonants return to the world and the air goes thin and honest, and in that moment the keeper is only a warmth on the inner side of your leaving, already forgetting the shape of you, already spending you into the gradient, already turning its attention to the joint that ticked while you sat. By the time you are on the ordinary ridge, looking for the notch you could swear was a cathedral’s length, the preference has gone back into the cracks. The leaves are bargaining with whatever light is left. The posts are warm. Something lives there that keeps the room from becoming weather, and it will not come out to be seen, because out is the side it is for.
