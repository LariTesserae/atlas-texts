It does not begin as a body. It begins as a preference the water has, and only later admits it has been choosing.

At first there is only the way the stall in the square is never quite random. The glove, the spanner, the mica pane, the crate-husk: they orbit, yes, but the orbit has a center that is not the meeting of the streets. The center is a little off, toward the boiler shed, and it shifts when the west-lane inhale strengthens, not with the current but against a fraction of it, the way a moored thing shifts when someone on the deck changes their weight. You can watch a full sluice-cycle and decide it is an eddy with a memory. You can watch three and begin to doubt that eddies correct for lost tools. A washer that should have been combed into the turbine throat appears, between cycles, on the lee of a crane housing, set flat, as if placed where it would not sing. Nothing in the parish sings. That is the first courtesy, and it is not the parish’s.

Call it a tendency, then, until it spends itself on a more expensive gesture. When the gate in the ring wall seats and the basin’s pressure climbs back into its ordinary, a length of chain that has hung for years in a slack catenary between two hoists draws taut by a single link and holds. Not a current. Currents here push; they do not choose a link. The chain stays taut through the long inhale, through the stall, through the colder exhale, and only slacks again when the next opening softens the dark. It is bracing the lane. It is keeping two hulls from kissing when the lid ticks and the whole town shrugs by a finger’s width. Afterward the link that took the load is warmer than its neighbors, by less than a hand could swear to, and in that film the pale flecks gather thicker than they gather at the bearings. They are not feeding. They are standing where the work was.

So it works. That is the second thing, and it arrives before any outline does. The turbines do not need it; they were built to run on lowness and fullness and they run. What it does is the work the gauges were meant to announce and can no longer announce to anyone. A bleed-point under the ice that has begun to crust inward is worried at until the crust calves in a sheet no larger than a door and goes planing off into the top fathoms, dimming and brightening nothing that cares. A door hung at the agreed angle, threatening to swing shut over an intake and starve a lane, is leaned on until the hinge remembers the truce. These are not rescues. Nothing here is in danger in the way a town with people is in danger. They are continuations. The parish continues its shift, and something continues it on purpose where the machines have gone blunt.

Purpose is the wrong word for a long time. Purpose would have a face, or at least a front. What gathers, if you stay through enough cycles to be rude about it, is a density. In the square it is the off-center of the orbit. Along the west lanes it is a smoothness in the silt-roads, a combing finer than the current’s combing, as if the water there had been taught to lie flat so the intake could drink without choking. Near the dry room—only near it, never in it—the density thickens against the window glass on the outside, and the green coin of the gauge-lamp, or the coal of the work-lamp if the charge still holds, breaks into threads that bend. Not around a body you could sketch. Around a volume that refuses to be entered by silt. The particles walk up to the edge of it and lie down in a drift the size of a shoulder, and the drift holds its shape after the volume has gone, a mold of an absence, until the next weather of particles grades it away.

It is larger than a keeper and smaller than the lane it tends. That becomes clear when it passes the barge with the open hold. The gunwale is a handspan from the quay; a man would have to turn sideways, and would still brush. The density turns, and the turn is the first true gesture, a rotation in three planes at once, the flat shape you might have mistaken for a fish or a taught float, except that the flatness is only how it presents to a current it means to refuse. Edge-on it is a disagreement in the silt. Face-on it is a dimming, a place the brown dark gets one shade browner, wide as the hold-mouth and thin as the plate. It does not swim so much as occur along a path. Where it has just been, the water is a degree less murderous, and the dark green thread on the brick puts out a millimeter it will not be able to afford, and then, as the cold returns, the millimeter blackens and is not mourned. The basin does not do grief. Neither, apparently, does what lives in it.

What it uses for attention is harder to name than what it uses for hands. It has no gaze you can put yourself in front of. It has approaches. It approaches the phosphor coins and does not linger; they are bright enough to be found and too steady to be news. It approaches the air rooms and stops at the temperature wall, every time, the way a tide stops at a sill it has agreed not to cross. The tick of a buckle in the dry room, the indecent noise of a living body, would reach it as pressure long before it reached it as sound. Once, perhaps, it came up to the glass while the coal-lamp still had color, and the filaments bent toward the volume outside, and for the length of a breath the room’s window showed not brown nothing but a darker brown the size of the frame, pressed close, learning the shape of a pane. It has not done it again. The room is a rule. Air stays. It stays out. The agreement is older than the lamp and more carefully kept.

If it was ever a person, the parish has spent that fact. Keepers lived here; the coat on the peg is evidence of a kind, and the chair, and the way the sills were cut to a watermark as if someone intended to remain. But a person ends at a skin, and this does not. Follow the density down to the turbine house and it is in the housing, not as a saboteur, as a temperature: the bearing runs a shade easier, the granular shiver in the metal evens out, the flecks thicken and thin with the turn as if they were a circulation it occasionally bothers to have. Follow it up to the ice and it is under the dome when the seam ticks, already there, already bearing, a broad dull change distributed across twenty fathoms of lid so the bolt the seam is hunting for is not asked to take the whole shrug alone. It is not in two places. It is a process with a preference for where it is thickest, and the preference can be moved, and the moving takes about as long as the slack chain takes to choose its link.

Hunger, if the word can be made small enough, is for gradients. It browses the slight warmth of work. The friction-skin at a shaft, the heat-debt in the exhale, the palm-sized warmth of a link that has held a load: these are what the dark green thread is browsing too, and they do not compete so much as share a scarcity. When the seep from the uphill wall runs a little richer—after a thaw somewhere above that the ice will only acknowledge as a brighter pallor in the top fathoms—the density gathers at the intake and spreads itself thin along the west lanes, and the parish runs a fraction quicker, and the orbit in the square loosens, the glove drifting wider, as if attention had been withdrawn from tidiness and spent on throughput. When the seep starves, it pulls in. The orbit tightens. The chain is braced earlier in the cycle. The bleed-points are worried whether they need it or not. It is not anxious. Anxiety would waste heat. It is a schedule adjusting to a head.

What it is not: a ghost in the sense of a remainder, a story caught in brick. Stories need an audience and a voice, and announcement is a failure of design. It does not replay the keepers. The coat stays on the peg. The chair stays angled toward a window that shows nothing. If the keepers left, or thinned, or were simply not replaced when the lid became geology, this did not stay behind to represent them. It stayed behind to keep the seals leaning the correct way. In that sense it is more engineer than revenant, except that no one designed it. It precipitated. Long enough in a closed works, with a small engine running on lowness, with procedures outliving their clerks, and the procedures acquired a direction they did not have on paper. Filaments of mineral fur learn the habit of growing. A volume of water, given ten thousand cycles of the same inhale and the same bracing and the same refusal to let the doors argue, learns the habit of being the thing that braces.

You learn it the way you learn the gait, by staying. The long inhale. The stall. The exhale, colder. And under those, a fourth beat that is not flow: the moment, just after the gate seats, when the town shrugs and something in the town takes the shrug onto itself and pays for it in a warmth you could miss if you were only listening for noise. Put a hand on the housing then. The granular shiver is gone. In its place is a smoothness, and in the smoothness a faint, repeating lean, as if the metal were being counted, link by link, sill by sill, all the way out to the ring wall and back. Not a message. A round. The night watch of a place that has forgotten any other lid, performed by what the place has become willing to hold.

It will not surface for you. The ice is not a sky it wants. At most, if you are in the lane when it turns edge-on to pass, you will feel the pressure change behind your ears—the same pop as a sluice, smaller, nearer—and the silt will open a slot the width of that turn, and for a moment you will see through the parish farther than the murk allows, a corridor of clearer cold with chains on either side, and at the end of the corridor not an eye, not a mouth, but the next task already assembling: a crust at a bleed-point, a hinge considering betrayal, a glove that has drifted too far from the orbit and must be walked back before the exhale takes it. Then the slot closes. The flecks settle to their ordinary scatter. The water, which is nearly all there is of this world, resumes being water, except that it is slightly organized, slightly warmer where the work was, and the organization is already moving off toward the ring, on schedule, dense and close-fitting and quiet, to keep the casting from cracking in the mold.
