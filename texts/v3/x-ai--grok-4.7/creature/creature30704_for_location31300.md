The place is not a room with walls and not a wilderness with a tree-line. It is a reach of cold water over a floor that has already been finished by time, and what lives there lives inside the water’s load as much as on the stone.

Visibility is a rumor. A few tens of meters down the last red is gone; deeper, the last green. What remains of light is a blue so thin it dies in the suspended fines before it can make a shadow. Below that the dark is not empty. It is packed: silt, clay, flakes of stone no larger than dust, the ground bodies of things that died higher up and arrived already failing. At the top of the cloud the load is a haze. In the layer that never clears it is weather. A hand moved through it meets drag. An arm’s length of lamp-beam is a luxury, and past that the beam is only a brighter dirt. The medium is density. There is no clean gap between presences because the presences are what the water is holding, lifting, settling, lifting again.

The floor under that is old in a way hills are not. Ridges and stacks that once stood here have been planed. Currents older than the present climate worked the stone and then let the fines of that work settle back over what they had made. It is not flat like a table. It is flat like a life that has stopped arguing: long swales a walker would not notice, channels whose walls have slumped into themselves, the merged lobes of an ancient spill. Relief is a body’s height, sometimes less, across a walk of a day. Rock that still shows is rounded, dark, slick with clay. Edges were owed to time so long ago that time has collected.

Sound is the sound of a heavy cloth dragged once and left. Each grain striking each other grain is an event, and the events are uncountable, but each is too small and the cold water keeps the energy. Now and then a softer click — a stone the size of a fist finally persuaded off a slope, ticking once and going still. The basin does not roar. Its turbulence is sheets and billows, internal waves along boundaries a few meters thick, one cold sliding under another. Continuous, large, unadvertised.

Nothing farms. Light does not arrive in a form that grows a meadow. Fragments of weed and clots of plankton drift down already failing, and the silt takes them. What vegetation exists is a smear: bacterial film on a stone where reduced chemistry leaks from old rock, a pale scurf in a hollow that holds organic mush a day longer than the ridge beside it. No kelp. The color, when a lamp forces color onto the floor, is wet ash and old iron.

Animals are rare in the same proportion. Not absent — the basin is not dead — but a long crossing can meet nothing with a nerve. A worm-thick trail already softening. A pale crab the size of a palm, each leg negotiated with the cold. Higher than the floor and lower than the last light, a scatter of small brightnesses that are not stars: a jellyfish or a chain of them, pulsing, gone when a sediment billow turns. Fish exist, few and built for not spending. They hang where a density boundary offers a little less work and eat what the fall brings, which is not much and not regular. Nothing hunts in packs. Nothing sings. A life here is mostly waiting inside a moving cloud.

The plot is the motion of the load. Above, the real surface may be gray and windy or almost calm; that weather is another country. Down here the weather is lateral and basal. Internal waves travel the thermocline and, where they steepen against the old fan, break without air — cold rolled into colder, vortices that stand for minutes like ghosts of smoke and then are only silt. Along the subtle channels, the only roads and not roads, gravity still gathers. When a shelf at the rim fails, it does not thunder. A tongue of sediment-heavy water slides down a grade so gentle a standing person would call it level. Faster than any animal here. A river with no surface, a few meters thick or a few tens. It lifts mud that has lain since the last such tongue, holds it, drops it elsewhere. Ripples form and are erased inside an hour. A hollow fills. A rise is skinned. Then the tongue thins, lays a graded sheet — coarser grains first, fines drifting on — and the hush returns, identical, over a geography that is not the same.

Creation and destruction are one gesture: a grain leaves one rest and finds another. Over a human life the floor looks eternal. Over the life of the basin it is a slow boiling, a library continually reshelved by a librarian who does not read. The ancient flatness is not peace. It is the shape turbulence takes when it has had time enough to remove every other shape.

There is no structure. No wall, no post, no cut stone, no line drawn on purpose. A claw-mark, a mucus tube a worm lined and abandoned, a depression an animal made by sitting still until the silt mantled it — these last a while and then do not. Burrows collapse when the next tongue passes. The film on the rock is not a crop. It is a chemistry that happens to be alive, and it starves where the seepage pinches out. A walker, boots sinking to the calf, each step its own small turbidity, would find no artifact to triangulate by. Orientation is by the lean of the fines as they settle, by the faint persistent slope that only a long crossing reveals. Large enough that the far rim is a rumor — shallower water, a change in the pitch of the floor, a day’s travel or three — and regional rather than endless. The mid-features repeat: swale, skin of clay, a channel axis where the last flow scoured a palm’s depth of mud and left a pavement of small rounded stones, then swale again.

Cold sits in the body as weight. It is not a wind. It is the temperature of an argument the sea has already had with winter, stored and brought down. Metals would ache. Flesh would dull. There is no afternoon. A slight cycling of the internal waves, a pulse on the order of hours; a longer cycling of failures at the rim; the seasonal faintness of the plankton fall, which changes the silt’s smell if anything here could smell — a thin sweetness, then only mineral again.

At the margin of the dark, where the last usable blue still comes and goes with the surface day, the packed water thins enough that shapes almost resolve. That band is a lid, not the basin. Rain on the real surface is felt down here only as a trivial freshening, a slight destabilizing of the upper boundary days later. The wall of weather that matters is the one inside: sheets of silt standing and falling, billows with hard edges where one water refuses another, a slow rotational debris that in a lamp beam looks like smoke in a closed room after a door has been slammed and the sound has already left. The slam is missing. The smoke remains, and then a new sheet arrives crosswise and the pattern is lost without ceremony.

What lives rare keeps habits that waste nothing. The crab pauses half-buried when the boundary wave passes, because swimming is expensive and the wave will end. A fish turns into the flux and holds, mouth barely open, harvesting what the billow concentrates along its face. In the bacterial smear, chemistry proceeds at a scale that does not look like motion, warm only by comparison with the stone. Between these points the floor is only floor: graded mud, pebbles planed to ovals, a single larger cobble that has not moved since the current that delivered it lost its nerve. Around it the silt drops a small shadow-bar, a comet-tail of fines, pointing the way the last tongue went. It is the nearest thing to a sign. It will be rewritten.

Between events the basin seems finished, the end of topographic life. During an event it is all revision — busy at the scale of grains, perfectly silent at the scale of ears, hyperactive and ancient in the same minute. Both are true. They are the two speeds of one material.

Night does not arrive. It is the default, and the surface day is a faint modulation felt only at the lid. Stars do not participate. The only lights are carried: a rare animal’s brief signal, answered or not; the lamp of anything that comes down from the lit world, which immediately makes a halo in the fines and then a moving pocket of gold the silt begins, at once, to erase. Outside that pocket the basin continues its work without reference. Packed, cold, quiet, unbuilt, almost lifeless, and never still — an old floor under a moving mountain of dirty water, open enough to be a region, closed enough that the next swale stays hidden until the cloud in front of a face decides to thin.
