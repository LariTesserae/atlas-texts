It does not arrive from outside. Nothing in the passage could have walked it here in the time the stone has been stone, and the films are too new to have been carried. It condenses where the room has already decided the rules.

At first it is only a difference in the slime-shine on the basin lip. The film there is mineral, clear when a ripple passes, dull when the roof-panel holds still. Along one span of that lip the dullness does not lift with the ripple. It stays, a grease of gray no wider than a thread, and when the next drop lets go from the ridge and strikes, the thread tightens instead of washing. That is the first motion that is not water. It is not yet a body. It is a preference: this chemistry, not the chemistry a finger's width away, held against the current the drops keep making.

The preference thickens. By the time the third nipple of sinter has gained the height of a grain, the thread has a skin of its own, distinct from the mineral film, and the skin can fail. A section of it loosens, drifts a body's length — though there is not yet a body — and catches again on the same wet arris. What catches is already jointed. Not many joints. Three, then five, the way a cooling crust cracks into plates that still belong to one sheet. The color is wet ash, as the room already knew, because the iron in the weep is the only pigment available and the thing takes only as much as it needs to be opaque to itself.

It feeds by staying. The light through the panel is keeping something smaller than the film awake — a dust of mineral life, or the film's own edge, busy where the white noon arrives and quiet where the second light from the ridge does not reach. The jointed thing does not hunt that dust so much as occupy the line where it is richest. It crosses. It returns. The span never lengthens. A seed's worth of animal, if animal is the word, working a path that was drawn by nicks in the glass before any creature was present to use them.

Look longer and the path is not one creature's habit. The line frays at the basin end. Two threads, then a cluster, each the same ash, each keeping the same interval as if the spacing of the nicks had been copied into them. They do not greet. When two meet they pass, or one stalls and the other takes the stall as a wall and turns. There is no nest. The floor is too open, the heat too banded; anything that left the film would dry on the upper side the way the flake is drying, gloss going matte, and not return. So they remain a population of the lip, a living of the edge, numerous only in the sense that a crack is numerous.

The basin holds the other one, and it is not the same.

What turned under the surface was not a seed. It was a softness the size of a thumbnail, pale where the ash-things are dark, and it did not cross a line. It hung in the hottest band, the water just above the deepest part of the washtub pool, and turned as if the turn were how it breathed. The surface sealed over it. For a long while there is only the dimple of arriving drops, the skinning and breaking. Then the displacement comes again, smaller, and a thread of clearer water rises and dissolves. It is venting something the pool has too much of. Heat, or the gas that lives in the weep, or both.

It has no joints the eye can use. Pressed against the light — and the light does press, even through a palm's depth of water, because the panel is so near and so willing — it is a sac with a denser knot off-center, and the knot is what turns. Finer than that, the sac's skin is not smooth. It is shingled with the same mineral the sinter is building, taken on so quickly that the creature and the room's deposit are, at the boundary, the same substance. Older places grow such coats over years. Here the coat is hours, and already it is thick enough that when the sac drifts toward the cooler weep it stalls, heavier on one side, and rotates back into the hot band where the coat does not yet lock.

They do not know each other. The ash-threads on the lip never enter the pool far enough to meet the sac, and the sac never breaks the surface toward them. Between them the room continues its own work: drops, nipples, the oiling of light as the panel flexes, the low thread of pressure behind the ridge. The two kinds of life are solutions the pocket arrived at separately, one for the film that travels, one for the water that stays. Neither is old enough to have a hunger aimed at the other. Hunger, if it is here, is still only the refusal to wash away.

Higher, in the band where heat hangs and does not soak into the wall, something else is almost not alive and will not be argued out of the room. It is not a body. It is a persistence in the wet glass of the ridge: a run of beads that do not follow the nicks. They sit on the arris where charge should pull them toward the three drop-points, and they do not go. When the panel brightens they tighten. When the light oils away they loosen, nearly join the traffic, and then, as the brightness returns, draw back into the same wrong places. No joint. No sac. A habit in the water itself, or in a scum too thin to dull the glass, repeating a position as if position were a metabolism.

Call it the third. It does not feed in any way the other two would recognize. It spends the light. The beads that hold are warmer than the beads that run — measurable, if a hand could stand the room, by the way they refuse to lengthen and fall. They are doing, at the scale of a pinhead, what the whole ridge is doing at the scale of the wall: keeping what the panel gives, declining the equalizing the stone will eventually enforce. Whether that is an organism or only the young glass teaching water a trick, the room does not distinguish. The ash-threads avoid those beads. Their path kinks around them. So something in the film can tell the held water from the traveling water, and treats the held as occupied.

Nothing larger is coming. The passage is a failure of shelves, not a road, and the heat at the lid would turn back anything that had already learned, in some older dark, to fear a steady burn. The seed-sized things will not grow into walkers. The sac will not become a fish. The interval is too short, and the pocket too exact, for lineages. What lives here lives as the room lives: by being a sorting that has not yet been blurred.

Watch the ash-thread at the lip through one more circuit. It reaches the end of its span, where the film breaks into the basin, and does not fall. A joint locks. The body — and it is a body now, five plates, a wet-ash seed, unquestionably separate from the slime it crosses — compresses, takes a film of iron-orange onto its underside, and starts back. Midway it stalls. Not because another of its kind is there. Because the light has flexed, and for a moment the richest line is a hair to the left of the path, and the creature, which has never been anywhere but here, corrects.

That correction is the whole of its intelligence, and it is sufficient. It will not last past the crazing of the panel. When the second light goes and the bands mix and the nipple of sinter finally plugs the nick that feeds its line, the path will dry, and the plates will matte the way the flake is matting, and there will be no record but a grit on a lip. Until then it keeps the line. The sac turns in the heat it cannot leave. The beads on the ridge hold their wrong places and spend the noon the stone has agreed, for this short while, to let through.

The room is not empty. It is inhabited exactly to the degree that it is still new: three solutions, none of them finished, none of them in each other's way, all of them possible only while the edges stay sharp.
