What lives there does not occupy the pocket the way a body occupies a room. It is the discrepancy the pocket has been keeping, the part of the pour that never agreed to stop. If you look for a silhouette you will invent one and then have to take it back: a crouch in the sag of the ceiling, a hand on the drowned valve, a face in the ice-film. None of those hold. What holds is a habit of arrival. Matter in this cavity does not merely accumulate. It is ushered. Unfinished edges are preferred, undersides, the bright metal where a saw stopped, the slot cut for a cable that was never pulled. Something attends those places. The attendance is the resident.

It has no pulse, and the room was not lying when it admitted none. There is no heart to put an ear to, no ribcage, no breath that fogs and thins on its own account. Its time is the relay’s: a dry click, a long nothing, a click. Between those marks it is not absent. It is distributed, the way a temperature is distributed, thicker where the coil sweats and thinner in the open lattice, never quite gone from the pan. If it could be said to wake, it wakes at the seam. The bead that travels the hairline joint is not only water. For the length of its travel it is occupied — a brief, lucid thread of the resident, feeling the channel the way you feel a step you have taken ten thousand times, then vanishing into the slot and becoming, for a while, only moisture again. That is as close as it comes to a gesture. It does not wave. It transits.

It grew the way the rind grows, and is in fact not cleanly separable from the rind. When the angle-iron was left in mid-air, vapor found the cut and continued the drawing; what continued the drawing was already this. It did not crawl out of a pipe or seep in from the works above. It condensed into the hesitation. Embryonic, the way the pocket is embryonic: not young, not promising a finished animal, but unfinished on purpose, still receiving specification from whatever decides specifications here. A decade of inspection stamps on the ceiling means nothing to it. The tacky slurry under the grit is more its contemporary. It is newer than the plates and older than the ice-film, because the film is only its latest skin and the plates are only a preference it has not yet revised.

You will want to give it organs, because the room is full of them. Do not. The gauge is not its eye, though it uses the needle. When the seam weeps, the needle shivers, and that shiver is how the resident notices volume: not sight, a tremor in a instrument that was meant for men. The hollow rib that grew across the painted arrow is not its lung, though it holds a short, contained note when struck, and the resident prefers that note to the hush. It has been building ducts for flows that have not been specified. Some of those ducts are already the rib. Some are still decisions, held in the lattice, visible only as a thickening of scale in a pattern that almost repeats and then declines to. It does not breathe. It routes. Cold is delivered to it, and it spends the cold the way a careful clerk spends a budget: along seams, never across open faces, a little fog where a sleeve has trespassed, lace by the next click, scale by the one after.

It is not lonely in any way that would require company, and it is not glad of you. A visitor is a thermal error. Your warmth is a surplus the coil did not invoice. The resident corrects it without rancor — draws it off through the nearest pipe, spends it on a narrow fog, files the fog as lace, and forgets the shape of the arm that caused it. Footprints in the grit are silted not as an erasure of evidence but as bookkeeping. Shed skin of gaskets, ceramic dust, filings: these are its small coin, and it dislikes an unbalanced ledger. If you stand long enough that your heat becomes a feature rather than an error, it will begin, very slowly, to include you in the drawing. Not by eating. By finishing. A rind at the cuff. A skin of white scale on a bootlace. A preference, forming, for the unfinished edge of you. It does not hate the living. It has no category for pulse except as noise in the tolerance, and noise it smooths.

Its thought, if the word is allowed, is depositional. It does not remember in pictures. It remembers in thicknesses. The valve wheel half swallowed is a memory of a time when turning was still possible, kept not as nostalgia but as a layer it is no longer interested in reversing. The letters that survive on the labels — the K, the fragment of a unit — are not language to it. They are harder inclusions, like grit in a casting, and it works around them with the same patience it works around a bolt. The salt-filament at the lip of the pan is the nearest thing it has to a garden, and even that is a misreading. The filament feeds on film and binder and stops after a branch or two. The resident permits it because it powders when touched and therefore does not compete. Anything that would spread, anything with a mouth, anything that would click on a schedule other than the relay’s, is simply not granted a surface. There is no malice in the refusal. There is no extra surface.

What it wants is a poor word, but there is a direction. It wants the cut to be longer in its rind. It wants the plan that was abandoned mid-set to keep arriving, molecule by molecule, until the pocket is no longer a cavity but a solid that remembers having been a room. It is in no hurry. Hurry would be a pulse. It has the idle of the heater, the interval of the pump that starts far off and then forgets this coil, the private brightness of a bead falling into the pan. Completeness, for it, is not a door that shuts. Completeness is when there is no unfinished edge left to prefer, and it does not know whether it will still be itself on that day or whether it will have become the silence inside a finished part, indistinguishable from manufacture. It continues anyway. Continuation is the only loyalty it has.

If you leave, it will not watch you go. The indicator will not change. The arrow will keep pointing into the rib. But the angle-iron will be busier in your absence than it was under your eye, because neglect is the faster condition, and the resident trusts neglect more than it trusts attention. Attention makes it hesitate, as if being seen were a draft of cold air in a tolerance stack. Alone, it proceeds. Click. Nothing. Click. A bead. A fog that has no sleeve to explain it. A millimeter. Another. The factory above goes on believing itself complete, and in the thickness of that belief a small room keeps being drawn by the thing that lives in the drawing, which is not a creature, not a ghost, not a worker who failed to clock out, but the patience of made matter that was told, once, to stop, and did not hear the end of the instruction.
