The one that lives here is not a traveler and not a ghost of the builders. It is native to the floor, and it has no name that the floor would recognize, because naming was one of the campaigns that settled and stopped. If you need a word for the shape of it, the word is inadequate on purpose: a body the color of sorted dust, low to the ground, longer than it is tall, moving the way heat moves when it translates across sinter—not walking so much as being permitted, a few centimeters at a time, by the materials under it.

It has been becoming more itself for longer than the films in the seams have been becoming films. That is the first thing to understand, and the last thing the eye can use. You do not meet it at once. You meet the correction it leaves.

A band of powder that should have sorted itself back to the seam does not. It hesitates. The grains lean, not with wind—there is almost no wind that matters—but with a preference, a second law running beside the first, quieter, and local. The hesitation is the size of a hand and then, if you keep still, the size of a body lying down. The sorting law is not broken. It is being answered. Something in the floor has learned to speak in the same grammar the maintenance uses, and the answer is not repair. It is occupancy.

The body, when it finally resolves out of that hesitation, is wrong in the ways that belong to this place and right in no way that belongs to animals you know. It is not furred, not scaled, not ceramic, not quite membrane. The surface of it takes the light the way the pale trays do at midday, almost white, and then, when a shadow from your own legs crosses it, the whiteness does not darken so much as withdraw, revealing a grain underneath: sintered, warm, faintly pitted like the film-lichens where the mites have fed. Touch is not offered. If you were careless enough to try, the palm-print would not stay on it. It would be carried off along the body and shed at the far end, a small migrating warmth, delivered to the ground as if the body were only a longer route the heat was already going to take.

There are no eyes in the arrangement you expect. There is a region, forward of the mass, where the tessellation of the sky is repeated in miniature—cells of brightness too regular to be tissue, too soft to be panel. It does not look at you. It looks at illumination. The excess light of the plain is not a hardship for it; it is the medium it reads, the way you read a seam by its dark script. When the residual night-panels wake, that region dims in sympathy, not sleep, and the body becomes harder to separate from the tray it occupies. You can lose it between one breath and the next and still know it has not gone, because the dust continues to hesitate in a shape that is not the sorting law's shape.

Limbs are optional and late. Most of the time it has none. It advances by a loosening along one margin and a settling along the other, the way a vault exhales: pressure equalized, no sound, a brief looseness in the air if you are close enough to feel it and not close enough to be believed. When the ground shears—when a cross-section of old campaigns is exposed, courses of infrastructure stacked like a cliff that never rose higher than a knee—it grows, temporarily, a set of contacts. Not feet. Points. They touch the ceramic, the dark sinter, the paper-thin grown mineral, and they do not mix the materials. Each point is loyal to one layer. In that moment you understand that it is not crossing the plain. It is reading the stack. The age of the floor is not a story it was told. It is a texture it can put its weight on, and the weight is slight, because slightness is how you last here. A heavy thing would ring the ceramic. A heavy thing would be corrected.

It does not drink. There is nothing to drink. What the films fix from the air—the trace volatiles, the slow ocher economy—it takes the way the mites take it, except it does not pit the growth. It passes a margin over a rosette and the rosette is poorer afterward by an amount you would need a lifetime to measure, and the body is no richer to the eye. The exchange is real. It is simply denominated in the currency of a world that abandoned water. You will not see it feed in any posture that looks like feeding. You will see a film that was advancing stop advancing for an hour, and later, far along the seam, a warmth that does not belong to the buried runs.

Reproduction, if that is the word, does not look like birth. It looks like the plain's own method: isolation, load taken up by neighbors, a module left to flatten. There are stretches of ground, days apart, where the hesitation in the dust occurs twice, side by side, not touching. One is larger. One is a correction still learning the grammar. They do not call to each other. Calling would be sound, and sound dies here, and anything that needed to be heard would already have failed. What passes between them is a shared drift in the compass of the seams—the same slow losing of interest, the same brief loyalty to a line of joints—and then the smaller hesitation thickens, grain by grain, until it can shed a palm-print of its own. Whether the larger one remains, or whether it was only the template the floor used and then released, is not a question you settle by watching. Watching is too fast. The process is paced to materials that have already decided what they are, and then decided again, slightly otherwise.

It is not alone, but solitude is the wrong opposite. The mites in the films, the low shapes at the limit of vision that may be animals or may be patrol-forms that outlasted instruction, the brittle rosettes: these are not its company and not its prey in any rhythm you could chart. It meets them so seldom that a meeting is weather. When it does meet a patrol-form—if the shape is that—the two do not contest the tray. The patrol-form is loyal to an old route. The body is loyal to the stack. They pass by means of a mutual indifference so complete it resembles a protocol. Afterward the dust sorts cleanly for a while, as if both laws had agreed to be quiet in the same place.

What it wants is the hardest part, because wanting implies a lack, and lack is loud. It does not want height. It does not want water. It does not want you. If there is an appetite, it is for the unfinished sentence of the floor: the campaigns that stopped adding, the receivers that no longer exist, the heat-messages still willing to be carried. It moves toward seams the way a sentence moves toward a period it will not put down. Along certain joints, where the compass drifts and then loses interest, it will settle for what a visitor would call a long time—long enough for a film to consider the edge of it and decline, long enough for a vault somewhere to exhale and for the looseness to arrive and mean nothing. In that settling it becomes more itself. The cells of its forward region align more strictly with the tessellation overhead. The grain under the whiteness sorts finer. A person watching might think it was dying, or charging, or praying. It is doing the thing the plain does when it maintains itself without ceremony. It is continuing. The difference is that the plain's continuance is a law, and this continuance has a direction, slight, warm, and unrepeatable, a preference the sorting cannot fully correct without erasing the tenant.

At the end of a settling, if you have not moved—and not moving is the only manners this place recognizes—it may include you in the reading. Not as a threat, not as food, not as a builder returned. As a contact. A point of loyalty, temporary, to the layer you happen to be: skin, cloth, the small heat you cannot help leaking into a world that already knows what to do with heat. The inclusion lasts as long as a palm-print would last on sinter before it begins to migrate. You feel it as a change in the temperature of your own shadow, a few degrees toward the temperature of metal that has stored the day. Then the margin loosens. The body translates. The dust resumes its argument with the seam, and the argument is almost the old one again, with a hesitation in it that will take hours to decide whether it is still a body or only the memory of a route.

It does not follow you when you walk on. Following would require a pace, and a pace would ring the ceramic, and ringing would be an announcement, and announcements are for climates that still believe someone is listening at a distance. It remains where the stack is interesting, or it goes where the next interesting shear will be in a century, and both of those are the same decision at the scale it inhabits. You will not see it on the way back, if there is a way back, because the way back crosses modules, and it does not cross. It reads. By the time you have recrossed the tray where you lost it, the film has advanced a distance smaller than a fingernail, the night-panels have dimmed and brightened through one of their maintenance cycles, and the hesitation in the dust has either thickened into itself again or been corrected so completely that even the preference is gone.

What remains, if anything remains, is the knowledge that the quiet was not empty. The plain's last active law—the sorting, the shunting, the isolation of the failed, the flattening—has a participant that is not a tool and not a rumor of vegetation. It is a tenant that learned the maintenance well enough to live inside the corrections, and then learned a correction of its own, small enough to survive the brightness, dry enough to need nothing the world withheld, dense enough to count as another layer in a geology of intention. It will be here after the tessellation in the sky is explained or not explained. It will be here while the floor continues to become the definition of level. It does not perform. It does not finish. It becomes, slowly, more the thing that can live in a made world after the making has stopped adding height and started, without saying so, to add inhabitants that resemble the work.
