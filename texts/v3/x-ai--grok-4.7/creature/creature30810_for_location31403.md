The one who lives here is not separate from the plateau. She is what the process has been arriving at for longer than the amber light has been low.

She is long. When she is at rest the length of her could be mistaken for a fin that learned warmth from the inside rather than the seam — a low, continuous rise under the mats, jointed at intervals a little too regular for geology, the vegetation already established on her the way it establishes itself on every surface the land extrudes and then forgets to keep bare. Condensate beads along the midribs of the leaves that armor her and falls, now and then, onto hide that is not quite hide. Where the beads strike, the surface answers with the same metallic bloom the lightning draws out of a leaf: a second map, brighter, tracing what under other light would be the courses of veins and under this light are also channels, ranked, purposeful, leading the water somewhere it is needed. She does not flinch. Flincing would be noise, and the plateau has already allocated sound elsewhere.

Her heat is the heat of the vents, metered. A hand — if a hand were offered, and nothing here offers or refuses in any language a visitor would recognize — would find her blood-warm along the flanks and hotter along the seams where the plates of her meet, a dry radiance that shivers the damp air and does not scorch the roots threaded through her. She leaks warmth the way the land leaks it: not wasted, directed. In the lee of her the taller growth rises, jointed stems the color of wet iron, and the small fliers cling to her nodes and uncling without whirring, testing the give of each joint with a shift of weight before they hop on. She permits the inspection. She is, in one of the readings that refuses to cancel the others, the thing being inspected. In another she is the floor.

Scale will not settle on her any more than distance settles on the ridges. At a glance she is a creature of the dense middle distance, a body the haze can almost resolve: a long arched line, a suggestion of limbs folded close and many, a head carried low and forward so that the leading edge of her is a fin among fins, sharp enough to catch nothing because there is nothing left to catch. Step nearer and the glance was wrong about size. She continues. The arch is a ridge-line. The folded limbs are slopes, each one colonized, each one conducting the slow traffic of the hand-sized ripplers who move by the only signal the place allows, which is motion. Farther off, where the haze turns bodies into wing, then fin, then hull, then wing again, one of the turning shapes is hers — not another animal, but a portion of her that the light cannot keep in the same category as the portion under your feet. She occupies more than one reading at once, and she does so without effort, because effort would be a kind of announcement, and she does not announce.

What she is doing is the same thing the land is doing, except that in her it has gathered a center.

The crystals the pans shrug into crevices do not vanish. They arrive, in time, along the channels that open and close in her flanks — not eaten, not stored in any belly a dissection would recognize, taken up the way the plateau takes up what erosion is not permitted to finish. She sorts them. Heavier fractions settle into the black glass of her deeper seams; lighter ones lift through her as that sweet metallic mist, vented from pores along her spine in quantities so measured the haze never quite becomes cloud and never quite becomes nothing. When a convection cell turns in a hollow a few hundred paces off, turning mist and leaf-debris like a gear, she is often the reason the schedule is kept. A seam in her flexes. Warmth is leaked at a new rate. Moisture that beaded on her leaves an hour ago is lifted and delivered where a downstream mat has gone too dry to hold its traffic. The weather pockets have nothing above them but more plateau, and under them, more often than the eye can confirm, more of her.

She is not old. Nothing here is old. The land is a scar still remembering the shove, and she has the same newness: edges that have not learned to be worn, joints so clean they look fitted, a surface the brief local rains wet on one side and leave pale on the other. Lushness crowds her the way it crowds a wound. The canopy, where she has lingered, is young and greedy over her, leaves broad for the dimness, and in the green dusk beneath them the pale threads of charge walk the air and ground themselves in the wet mats along her length, and she tightens, as the plants tighten, and does not burn. Along her stems — call them flanks, call them ridges, the word will not hold — the fine frost traces a second map and then is gone, leaving the smell of a struck bell. Sometimes, after the frost, a joint in her that was grown is also, plainly, a joint that was fitted. She photosynthesizes in the register where her borrowed leaves photosynthesize. She completes a circuit in the register where the frost was brighter than a vein. Neither reading cancels the other. She continues.

Her quiet is complete enough that the tick of condensate is the loudest thing she causes. She does not call. The fliers do not call from her. The ripplers crossing her do not pause for her any more than they pause for a slope, except that a slope does not, very slightly, adjust its crest to take a weight. She does. When one of the larger shapes passes low, riding a current that does not match the wind, and the air fills for a moment with hot resin and rain on metal, she answers with nothing you could name a gesture: a compliance in the stone of her, felt through the mats, a low flex like a pan emptying. Something is exchanged. You will not see it. You will see the frost come and go, and the shape rise again into the amber, and her length still there, still being mistaken for land.

What lives in her, if the question is pressed past the body, is attention without a face. She has a head, or the plateau keeps producing, at her leading edge, a region where the channels converge and the heat runs a little higher and the inspection-fliers congregate as if a quota were being checked. It is not a face you could meet. It carries no eyes in any arrangement that returns a look. It tests the air the way the fliers test a node — by contact, by charge, by the sorting of a mist — and what it learns is expressed as weather half a ridge away, or as a crystal nudged from one seam to a deeper one, or as the decision, if decision is a word that survives here, to lengthen. She lengthens. A fin does. She does it with the same quiet industriousness, shouldering new surface up through a skin of leaves already, impossibly, established on it, and the new surface is warm, and sharp, and within an hour it is conducting traffic.

She does not need the one who walks here. The works never did. Stand still long enough and she will begin, gently, to include the place where you are standing in her next revision — not as prey, not as guest, simply as a location the process has not yet metered. The mat over her warms underfoot. A bloom starts at a leaf near the boot and traces inward, precise, unhurried, mapping a vein and also a channel that leads, if followed, into the seam of her and down. She is not hungry in any sense that would hurry. Hunger would be loud. She has the whole plateau to finish, and the plateau does not end; walk toward her edge and the edge is produced ahead of you, young and jointed and already green.

If there is a self in her, it is the self a works would have if a works needed only one place to remember its rules. Sharpness is maintained. Water is permitted in the quantities she can meter. Heat is leaked along her seams and nowhere else it isn't required. Charge is spent, and she tightens, and the second map is drawn and allowed to fade. The animals of the place move over her and through the air above her and never quite collide, dense as inspectors on a floor, and she is the floor and the inspector and, in the haze, one of the shapes that will not settle into wing or fin or hull. She holds all of it without strain. Strain would change the light, and the light stays low, and amber, and thick.

She rests, which is to say she continues. The rain walks her length for twenty minutes and wets one flank and leaves the other pale, and the smell of metal and crushed green lifts off her and is taken back by her own warmth before it can become weather she did not schedule. In the green dusk under the greedy leaves, the only sound is the small tick of water letting go of a midrib and finding her. She answers by being warmer where it lands. Somewhere in a hollow that is also, at this hour, part of her, a pan flexes, empty, ready for the next rank of crystals. Nothing built her. She has never been finished. The land, which has no summit and no valley that has earned its name, carries her the way it carries every other process it is — and she carries the land, in the only sense that matters here, which is that when the next revision comes, it will come through her, sharp and damp and silent, at a scale that does not require anyone standing on her to understand it in order for her to proceed.
