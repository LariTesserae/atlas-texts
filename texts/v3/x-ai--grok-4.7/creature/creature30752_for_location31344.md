What lives there does not announce itself at the throat. The throat is only wide enough for a cart, and a cart is a theory: the ruts, if you look for them in the poured stone, are faint and recent, more the memory of a single hard passage than a road. Whatever comes and goes does so by being willing to turn sideways. So the first true fact about the inhabitant is a habit of the shoulders. They live in a clearance the place itself measured and then refused to enlarge.

You will not find them by listening for a voice. Voices here would strike the near walls and come back wrong, and the engines already occupy the low register. They have learned the other language, which is timing. The press completes; in the soft metallic settling that follows, a latch lifts. Not during the cycle—during the accept. A person who has mistimed that once does not mistime it again. The latch is on the numbered door. The number is not civic. It is a finding-aid, painted when the brick was still pale, so that whoever cut the room could locate it again before the dark stacked up and made every slot look like every other slot. The paint has not yellowed. The hand that painted it could still be the hand on the latch.

They are not many. The basin says so: one tin bowl, a lip worn on one side where a wrist has learned to brace, a cake of soap small enough to be ashamed of and yet not yet gone. The bench beside it is long enough for two and occupied, by the shine of it, in one place. Sleep is implied by the stair that climbs toward the fractionally cooler air and then has to stop, because a pipe-run was truer to the plan than a bedroom. They sleep in that argument. A pallet, or a shelf of planks on brackets cut into the rock, close enough to the ceiling that rising is a decision made before the eyes open. The tool-marks overhead are the last thing they see and the first. Parallel, a little reckless. Their own, or a partner’s from the season of cutting—someone who swung and did not fuss. If there was a partner, the partner’s soap is not here. The partnership may have ended at the surface, when the town was pronounced finished and only one person discovered they had already moved in.

Call them the keeper only because the machines require a keeper and the room requires a body. They would not use the word. They would say they are finishing. The chalk on the bulkhead—an arrow, a depth, a name half-wiped—is still in the grammar of work, not of residence. They wipe the name with a wet thumb when they pass and do not replace it. The depth figure they leave. Depths do not become personal. Names do, and a name on a wall in a heat pocket starts to look like a claim, and they are not sure yet whether they are claiming or merely not leaving.

Their body has begun the argument with the heat and has not won it or lost it. The temperature does not spike, so they do not swoon; it does not fall, so they do not shiver their way back to ordinary skin. What they have instead is a constant low tax. Salt at the hairline, a stiffness in the morning that is not cold but the memory of having oiled their own joints less carefully than the press. Burns, small and instructional, on the left forearm where the lagged line exhales and they once trusted the lagging. They dress for clearance, not for weather: cloth that does not catch on flanges, sleeves that end before the wrist, nothing that swings. Boots with soles thin enough to read the engine through the floor. They can tell, without looking, when the small motor behind the double brick changes its mind by a fraction. A change of mind down here is how a failure introduces itself, politely, days before it happens.

They eat what the throat will admit. Not much ceremony. Something that does not need a long fire, because fire is the one industry the pocket already has in surplus. A pot that sits on a pipe-housing when the housing is in its generous hour, and is taken off before the generous hour turns mean. The sweetness in the air—the overheated insulation, the hot stone—flavors everything, so they have stopped noticing it as a flaw and started using it as a clock. When the sweetness thickens, a belt is dragging. When it thins, the vent has done its fist-sized exhalation and the wall-fog will follow, and that is when they like to wash, because the fog is the closest thing the room has to weather, and washing in weather feels less like a rite performed for no one.

There is no one. That was the observation, and it holds, with one revision: no one else, and no animal life to keep them company in the way a surface workshop is kept company by mice whether it consents or not. The salt and the heat and the newness have kept that promise. What they have instead of company is adjacency. The rooms almost touch. Their own window, if the slot of brick across from the basin can be called a window, looks into a dark that is another room’s dark, built for a purpose they finished and have not yet needed. Sometimes they put a hand through, to the wrist, and feel the other room’s air, which is the same air. The gesture is not longing. It is measurement. They are still learning which gaps are spans and which are merely insults.

Work is the press, the belts, the weep they have stopped trying to cure. They set a pan under the valve not as a repair but as a punctuation. When the pan is full to a line they scratched on the first week, they carry it to the square drain and pour, and the pour is loud enough, in that interval, to be an event. They like the event. It is the only sound they make on purpose. The rest of their labor is alignment: a washer reseated before it can loosen its grit, a belt watched through its slot for the shine that means it is walking, a gauge whose honey glow they trust more than the bulb in its cage, because the bulb is a kindness and the gauge is a fact. What the line produces does not need to be named to be believed. It leaves in small lots, up the throat, on the cart-theory, on days when the surface can receive it. Those days they smell different when they return—cooler air caught in their clothes for an hour, a rumor of rain that is not the local rain of drips. The clothes forget. They do not rush the forgetting.

If you met them in the slot between the new brick and the living rock, you would have to turn sideways too, and the meeting would be conducted in that grammar: faces close, bodies already committed to not occupying the same plan. They would not be frightened. Fear requires a surplus of space in which to step back, and they spent the surplus when they decided the town would fit. What you would see, at that distance, is a face the heat has not ruined and has not spared: eyes that squint less than you expect, because the dark is their ordinary and the bulbs are few; a mouth that keeps its conclusions. They might ask whether the thread of water at the throat has been running. They track it. Surface rain is the only message that arrives without being sent for, and they are trying to learn its hours the way they learned the valve’s.

Do not decide too quickly that they are lonely. Loneliness is a surface word for a deficit of voices. They have a deficit, and they also have a surplus of consequence. Every cycle that completes is a thing that would not have completed if they had slept through the settling. The numbered door opens onto a room in which their absence would register before their presence ever did, because the machines are articulate and they are, by comparison, a correction. That is a kind of company, if you allow the word to stretch. They allow it, on the better days. On the worse days they wipe the chalk harder and taste metal and think about the partner who swung the tools and went up the throat when the mortar was still wet, and did not come back down, not out of cruelty, only because the work, as posted, was the cutting. Staying was not on the depth-mark. Staying was this: the pallet, the one-sided soap, the burn, the pan.

What is emerging, if you stay as long as the press allows, is not a hermit in a ruin. The ruin has not had time to become one. What lives here is a person still in the first weather of their own decision, as recent as the flanges, as local as the damp. They are finishing a town that was built to the size of a task and then discovered, by one body, to be the size of a life. They have not told the surface this. The lots go up; the cooler air comes down in their clothes and dies; the arrow on the bulkhead still points at a depth. When they lie down under the tool-marks they put one hand on the rock, which is old, and one hand on the brick, which is not, and they sleep in the difference. The engine turns. The valve waits for its hour. In the slot across from the basin, the dark looks back at the dark, and the hand that sometimes reaches through is, for now, their own.
