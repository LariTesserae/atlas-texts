What lives there does not stand in the room.

For a long time that is all that can be said of it, and the saying is accurate. The open middle stays empty in the way the place requires: no body paused on the fiber, no track that the mat agrees to keep, no warmth left in a pocket after a rest. If you cross and recross the floor you meet nothing you can turn around and face. The joints go on dripping. The three notes go on. The emptiness does not thicken where you have been.

It is not one of the wall-lives. Those are many, and they are already named by their sounds: beetles in the packed generations, wrens in the green skin, warblers that will not come to the rim, frogs in pockets the floor will not show you. They are abundant and they are housed. Stem and root lie between them. They do not need the volume. They would be less themselves if they entered it. What lives in the place, properly, uses the volume and does not fill it.

It has no season of arriving. The country finished, the ring found the shape it keeps, the engines were left on, and it was already the habit of the room. You do not catch it settling. There is no younger version of it pressed somewhere in the dark layers, no shed covering, no mark of a first occupation. Finished things can still be occupied. This one is occupied by a continuance that does not grow and does not wear out.

Call it, while the word is still loose, an attention. Not yours. Yours comes in from the plain and is an event, and the room does not record events. This one was the room's before there was anyone to bring an ear to the inner face. It is distributed the way the damp is distributed: not wetter toward one wall, not drier toward the middle, no channel by which it prefers a part of itself. It does not pool. It does not leave.

It has no eyes, or it has given up the kind that require a direction. Light here does not stage anything; a gaze that hunted contrast would starve. What it uses instead is the difference between the three notes, which is stable and therefore legible forever. The flues in the hollow stems are its near sense, the rush of nearly the same bore, the pitch that tells it the ring is still of one age. The clicks and rasps and seeps are its knowledge of the housed lives, each of them kept at the right distance, none of them invited across the inner face. The engine-note is its news of the world that stopped at the boundary as an object and continued as noise. It does not resent the engines. Resentment would be a change of state. It holds the machine sound the way the floor holds it, as a fact arriving from underneath, faintly warmed with oil, never quite winning over the smell of wet fiber.

It does not feed in any way that would lower a pocket or thin a generation of beetles. The abundance in the wall is not its pasture. If it takes anything, it takes the continuance itself: the same joint, the same depth of brown water, the same balance of notes. That is not a metaphor for patience. It is the only exchange the equilibrium allows. A hunger that required more would have opened a path out of the ring ages ago, and there is no path.

When a single bird crosses the sky and is gone, it registers the crossing as weather registers a cloud that does not mean to rain: a brief amendment to the ceiling, already reconciled before the amendment ends. It does not follow. Following would require a center, and it has declined a center so thoroughly that the middle of the room, which you can walk, is only the place where its attention is thinnest and therefore easiest to mistake for absence. You are most inside it when you think you have found nothing. The walls are where it listens hardest, because that is where the lives are, but the listening is not a leaning. Nothing on the inner face is brighter, warmer, or more frequently wet from being preferred.

It is not lonely. Loneliness would need a memory of company, or a schedule on which company might still come. The animals in the fiber are not company; they are conditions, like the film on the stems. You are not company either. While you stand in the open air you are a small, temporary correction in the damp and in the sound, a body that displaces a little coolness and adds a little breath and then, when you step back onto the plain, is lost by the fiber before your print could have meant anything. It does not keep you. It does not fail to notice you. Noticing, for it, is not the same as recording. The room's rule holds: the space between presences remains the greater part.

If it has a body, the body is the held air, and only in the sense that a habit can be said to have a shape. It does not circulate. Wind through the stems is the ring's business. Its business is to remain at the same density in every part of the volume, so that no corner becomes a den and no center becomes a throne. Press your hand into the inner face and you are not touching it. You are touching the house of other lives, and beyond them the packed dead stems, and beyond those the plain. It is in the short distance between your skin and the fiber, and in the longer distance across the room, the same amount of it, cool, ordinary, unstaged.

It does not speak. The loudness is already complete. A voice would be a fourth note, and a fourth note would be a becoming, and the place has finished becoming. What you hear when you raise your own voice and find it useless is not its refusal. It has no interest in whether you are heard. The flues, the animals, and the engines continue at the volume they have always had, and your words sit on top of them for a moment like a drop that has not yet decided which pocket to join, and then they are gone into the general sound, unstored.

It will be here when the engines finally stop, and it will be here if they never stop. Either future is the same to it, because both are outside the ring, and the ring has already done the only work required of a boundary: it has made an inside in which nothing further has to happen. The dripping will go on at the rate that does not hurry or tire. The pockets will not feed one another. A bird may cross. You may come back and find the same joint at its work. None of that is news it needs.

What lives there is the room's agreement with itself: cool daylight, held moisture, housed abundance, excluded machines, and the empty middle kept empty on purpose. It is not a god of the place and not a ghost of whoever might once have wanted a shelter. No one made it, and it is not making anything back. It persists at the scale of the church-floor ring, no larger, because the air does not remain held beyond the slumped wall, and no smaller, because any denser life would have had to stand somewhere, and the fiber will not keep a stander.

Leave, and it does not watch you go. There is no outer face to its attention. The plain is finished and level and not its concern. Return, and it has not been waiting. Waiting would have been a lean toward the gap in the ring where a person fits. It is only, still, throughout the volume, as evenly as the damp, as loud as the three notes, as vacant as the floor under your foot, which springs back, and forgets you, and is in that forgetting most like the thing that lives there.
