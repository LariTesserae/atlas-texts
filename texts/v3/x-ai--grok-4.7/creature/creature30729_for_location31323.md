What lives here does not register, at first, as living. It registers as a correction in the light along a rim, a place where the white is a fraction less white, as if the newest layer of mineral had been laid down by a hand that hesitated. Stand and the correction holds still. The temptation is to decide it was only a change in the damp. Then the hesitation completes itself. A margin no thicker than a leaf lifts clear of the wall, water webbing between the lifted edge and the stone for a moment longer than gravity would prefer, and settles again one finger-width along. The sound it makes is not separable from the tick of the district. It has spent a long time learning that.

They are flat when they are at rest, and they are at rest almost always. Pressed to a face of travertine or to the floor of a pan, a body is a translucence the color of the water over it, a little more body than film, a little less body than stone. The outline is not an animal's outline. It follows the grammar of the place: a long margin that prefers rims, a thickening where a sill drops, a narrowing that can occupy a slot without damming it. In the open pavements they are nearly impossible. Against a new-white wall in ordinary noon light you can look straight at one and inventory only mineral. What gives them away is not shape. It is a refusal, very small, to glisten at quite the same angle as the wet around them.

They have no fixed number of limbs because they have no need of a fixed plan. Where a body must move, a portion of it draws up into a low ridge and the ridge walks—less a step than a pouring that has agreed to go forward. The contact never quite breaks. A track would require weight held in one place long enough to bruise the precipitate, and they do not hold their weight that way. By the time a film of water has crossed the place they left, the place has no memory, which is why the plateau can be full and still show you nothing but stone. Full is the wrong word anyway. In a dense quarter you might pass twelve of them in an hour's careful walking and never know. In the broad pans, one to a district is a crowd. They keep the spacing of things that feed on a thinness.

What they take from the world is what the world is already giving up. The cool water comes up the long rise of the plateau carrying its mineral, and where it slows, the mineral falls. They live in that fall. Along the underside of a body at rest there is a surface more patient than skin, a tissue that sorts what precipitates: some salts bound into the pale mineral of the body itself, so that a life thickens by the same means as a wall; some fraction released again, downstream, altered just enough that the next basin receives a water slightly easier to live on. They are not separate from the accretion. A rim that has had a resident for a long quiet decade is a rim with a truer line, the laminations beneath it finer, less tea-stained, as if the seasons had been edited. Break nothing. The cross-section, if you could see it without violence, would show their passages as absences that are not quite absences—layers where the deposit is purer, a little vacuous, the ghost of a body that was never buried because it was never elsewhere than the building.

They do not graze the black-green crusts. Those belong to a quicker, poorer exchange, and the residents leave them as a city leaves a weed in a joint: tolerated, not used. The shells on the moss ledges are another life, small and armored, climbing out after rain to rasp at what the films leave behind. The residents notice them the way a sleeper notices a fly in the room. Sometimes a margin will lift and the shells are simply elsewhere afterward, not eaten so much as unsettled, moved along by a change in the water's mind. The dark bird is more nearly a citizen. It knows the rims that are occupied and does not land on them. From the air, if the light is ordinary and the mist is down, a resident is only a dullness; the bird treats dullness as solid. In the deeper cisterns the slow plume of silt is often one of them rising a fraction off the bottom to turn, the way a page turns, and settling on a new bearing. You will not see the body. You will see the water remember that something wide changed its mind.

Perception, for them, is mostly chemical and mostly of level. A body spread along a sill knows the height of the water above it the way you know whether you are standing or have begun to lean. It knows the taste of what came down the slot in the last rain: iron from one reach of the plateau, a yellower sweetness from another, the faint green signature of a crust that flushed and died three basins up. It knows the tick-rate of its own cistern and the lower note of the channel beyond the arch, and it knows when those notes are wrong—when a night's condensation has preferred some other sink, and the old path is only a film, and the district has gone quiet in a way that means scarcity. They do not call to one another. The plateau is too committed to its silence for that, and a voice would be a kind of standing still the water would not forgive. What passes between them passes in the water itself, in alterations so slight a visitor's mouth would report only cold and stone. A resident upstream, feeding, changes the fall. A resident downstream reads the change and shifts its margin a finger-width, or does not, and that not-shifting is also an answer. Over distances of many districts the messages decay, as messages should. No one here is in charge of the weather. They only agree, locally, about sills.

Movement over long range is rare and looks like weather if you are not attending. A body that has occupied one rim for years may, after a wide slow storm has rearranged the levels, loosen all at once. The translucence thickens. What had been a margin becomes a shallow traveling pool with a purpose, pouring itself down a channel it has audited by ear for a decade, through an arch where the drip-rate is a landmark, out into a pan that has gone yellow and waxy and is, for reasons the body can taste, better. The journey may take a day. It may take a season, the traveler pausing for weeks in a vaulted understreet, feeding on the private fog, while the ceiling drips onto a back that is already half mineral and does not mind. They do not migrate as a class. Most die where they lived, which is to say they stop sorting and become, over a few years of ordinary deposition, indistinguishable from the wall they repaired. The laminations close over the hesitation. A subsequent resident may lie down on that exact seam and find it comfortable, without any knowledge of kinship. Knowledge is not the medium. Fit is.

Reproduction is a function of surplus and of a particular stillness after rain, when the films have joined and the city is for an afternoon one shallow sea. A body that has bound more mineral than it needs to keep its margin coherent will, at the thinnest place, open. Not a tear: a decision. The opened portion continues the parent's work for a time, sharing water, sharing the taste of the district, and then the sea withdraws into its rooms and the new margin is on one side of a rim and the old on the other. They do not tend the young. The young are already competent in the only competence required, which is to be wet in the right way and to hesitate at the right rate. Many of the openings fail. A fragment too small washes into a slot and is abraded into the same white dust the channels have always carried, and the district ticks on. The successes are why the tight quarters are tight. Over a span of time longer than the bird's whole lineage, residents have preferred the stacked places, the knee-to-knee rims, the arches, and their preference has been inherited by the stone. The city looks planned because the planners have been lying in it, editing the fall of mineral, since before the plan could have been told apart from accident.

There is no individual you could follow for a story. Even the ones that travel shed the wish to be singular. A body that has poured through three understreets and paused in each has mixed its margin with the drip of each, and arrives altered in its mineral, so that the resident who left is not precisely the resident who comes to rest in the yellow pan. They are continuous with themselves the way the plateau is continuous with itself: convincingly, and without a border you could defend. If one of them could be said to remember, it remembers as a wall remembers—by thickness, by stain, by the fineness of certain years. A bad decade of iron-tasting storms lives in a body as a faint band you would need to be them to read. They do not regret it. Regret would require a pace the air does not carry.

At night they are no more active than by day. The dark is only a loss of your certainty, and they never had that certainty in the form of sight. Some shallow ones, in the hours when condensation beads and returns to its own basin, lift a fraction higher off the stone to meet the sweeter water, and if you were there with a lamp—which you will not be; nothing here has ever needed a lamp—you might see a field of dull gleams at the waterline, each gleam a life taking a breath in the only sense that applies, each gleam gone the moment the beam settled. They do not fear light. Light is a weather that happens to the air above the film. Their weather is underneath.

Toward the edge of any description of them you find the temptation to make them into keepers, into the secret municipality of the works, tending the channels so that the plateau endures. That flatters the human need for a reason. They do not tend. They feed, and their feeding happens to be a kind of maintenance, and the maintenance has gone on so long that the infrastructure and the appetite are one anatomy. If every resident loosened and poured toward some impossible elsewhere, the walls would not fall. Deposition would continue, coarser, more stained, the laminations less true, the sills wandering by a thumb's width per century, the arches slowly forgetting their height. The city would persist. It would be worse at being itself. What they contribute is not survival but finish: the edited line, the honest tick, the water arriving in the next pan already a little clarified by having been lived in.

You can live among them for as long as the light stays ordinary and never be acknowledged, because acknowledgment would be a change in level and you are not a change in level. You are a brief pressure, a warmth that does not belong to the stone, a disturbance of silt if you wade. A nearby margin may ease a finger-width away from your ankle, not in alarm, in the way a sleeper rolls from a fold in the sheet. By morning the water will have agreed to forget you. The resident will be back on its seam, hesitating at the rate the district requires, binding the day's fall into a whiteness slightly more exact than the day would have managed alone. Somewhere under an arch a traveler pauses, tasting iron, and decides—not in a moment, in a week—that this fog is enough. Somewhere in a deep cistern a wide mind turns, and the silt lifts, and the glass of the water rights itself, and that is as much event as the country allows. The rest is residence: vast, cool, almost entirely still, distributed through the works at the density of a correction, building the only city it has ever known by lying down in it and not leaving.
