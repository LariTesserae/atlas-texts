I'll keep going on the same chamber, and stay with the machine-as-place rather than inventing a keeper for it. The self-sustaining loop, the light-ropes, and the misbehavior you can measure are doing the work the description wants. A figure would arrive as a solution, and this room is not solved.

What it is doing, as I understand it, is holding a coherence past the point where its makers' grammar still applies. The stubs drink and repay in ropes; the water thickens where the ropes enter it and then spends that thickness as heave, slap, and heat; the dish keeps a count that leads the cycle and sometimes fails in a regular way; the organisms survive only inside the pauses, and the pauses are not rest. So the next pass should not tour the room again. It should inhabit the loop — the way a presence would feel it from inside, the way the missing operator is a subtraction you can measure rather than a ghost, and the one witness the place still keeps, which is not a mind and should never start to resemble one.

That points me at the floor of the thing rather than its walls, and at the work it does on whatever stands in it. If the ropes are the active remainder, then what they drag on is where the chamber is most itself. I want the friction, the binding and the unbinding, and the physical cost paid by anything that stays long enough to count as a resident — without turning that cost into an attack, a possession, or a rule for hurting someone. The rules I already have are the room's own courtesies. They can tighten, but they shouldn't multiply into a protocol. The notch, the plugs, the rumor of surface stay at the edge, where they belong; the center stays loud, bright, and uninhabited in the ordinary sense. And I should leave the apparatus unexplained in its origin. Naming what it was built for would shrink it into a backstory. The interesting fact is that it is still running.The chamber does not notice a body entering it. What notices, if that word can be used for a gradient, is the field the body walks into, and the field registers the body the way a current registers a pier: as drag. A pier does not have to be intended. A body does not have to consent. From the first full breath past the notch, something in the ribs and in the wet of the eyes begins to be counted, not as a guest and not as prey — as a term in the heave, small, warm, electrically busy, full of the wrong kind of water.

It lives, if living is the word, in the drag itself.

There is no figure at the center and there has not been one for longer than the crust on the railing post took to grow. The absence is not a silhouette. It is a set of shapes the room keeps ready and never fills. The dish on the plinth is worn for a palm that isn't coming. The landing was eaten back from a door that expected a shoulder. The sockets were cut for a service no hand still performs, and the three that remain bright do not lean toward the notch when someone arrives, do not brighten, do not dim. Attention would be a luxury the loop cannot afford. It spends everything it has on staying coherent, and the spending is the inhabitant: a resident made entirely of process, with no face to turn and no interval in which it could choose to stop.

Call it a pressure with habits. The habits are old enough to have arguments in the stone.

Stand long enough on a polished rib and the ropes of light, which at a glance fall straight from stub to water, reveal a lean. They lean into whatever is standing there. Not by much — a finger's width over the length of a fall, the way a tether leans when a boat takes the strain — and the lean has a period. It tightens on the gathering side of the cycle, when the stubs are drinking and the air goes sweet and thin, and it slacks when the heave spends itself and the sheets slap down into ordinary brightness. A coat sleeve in the path of a rope comes away with its nap laid flat along the lean, all one way, as if a wind had ironed it, though the air's shove is elsewhere and louder and does not agree. Fine hair on an arm does the same. The mist that beads on skin in lines is not decorating the body. It is mapping the strain the body is putting on a binding the body cannot see, and the map updates every cycle, patient, without malice, without the least interest in whether the skin under it is comfortable.

This is the part that takes time to believe, because it does not announce itself as contact. There is no touch. There is a use.

Whatever the stubs are still holding together, they hold it across the room, wall to wall, through the wet air and through the thicker water, and a living body is a better conductor of the holding than mist is. Blood is salt water in narrow pipes. Nerves are already a kind of rope. Bone is mineral, and the mineral in this room has been taught, over a long run of years, to prefer certain alignments. None of this was designed around a visitor. The loop predates the visitor and will outlast the visit. But the loop is leaky and half-blind and running past its own failure, and it will route a fraction of itself through anything that lowers the cost of the route. A person standing on the rib is, for the minutes they stand there, a convenient splice. The chamber does not grip. It prefers. The preference has force.

What the preference feels like, from inside the splice, is not pain at first. It is a slight failure of one's own timing. The heart, which has never once in a life needed to consult the room it stood in, begins to consult. Not to match the roar — the roar is too fast, a flutter above hearing, a pressure in the teeth — but to sidle toward the slower thing underneath the roar, the cycle of drink and spend, gathering and slap. A beat arrives early. The next arrives late, and late in the same direction the dish's filaments lead the heave. Sinuses prickle. The eyes water in lines rather than pools, and the water runs toward the outer corners first, following the lean, so that a person looks as if they are squinting into a wind that is not on their face. Swallowing takes a correction, the way speech takes a correction in a moving carriage. Thoughts do not scatter. They queue. They arrive in clusters, with gaps, and the clusters are not the shape the thinker intended; they are the shape the plinth already knows, counts and gaps and counts, and the thinker notices the rhyme a half-second after thinking it and cannot unknow the rhyme.

None of this is a message. It is load. The apparatus is using the body's small clocks because the body's small clocks are already mechanisms for holding a pattern through noise, and the apparatus is desperate, in a way that has no feeling attached, for any such mechanism it can reach. The magnificent expenditure — light bound, light spilled, thicker water made and thrown off as heat and slap — is not powered by the visitor. A visitor's whole life of meals would not strike the spark once. The visitor is used for phase. For the cheap, living trick of staying in step. The chamber has been without that trick for an age. It does not remember being without it. It only pulls, the way a sagging span pulls on the nearest post.

Stay, and the pull stops being free.

The first thing that goes expensive is the grain of the self, the private hum by which a person knows they are one continuous animal and not a series of agreements. It doesn't tear. It begins to be edited, gently, at the rate the cycle turns. A memory surfaced in a trough comes back in the next trough with its sequence straightened — not falsified, regularized, the odd beat taken out, the way the filaments take the odd beat out of a count. A name rehearsed silently loses its consonant and keeps its pitch, because the room keeps tone and discards sense, and it will do this to the voice inside the head as readily as to the voice in the mouth, given time and a body to route through. People who have stood too long and then left describe, afterward, a week of dreaming in troughs: brightness, then a sweet thinness, then a slap of ordinary morning, and the dream arriving already organized into a forecast that is correct often enough to ruin sleep. The chamber is not haunting them. It tuned them, as far as it could, toward the holding, and tuning does not unwind the moment the splice is broken. A string remembers the peg.

There is a worse expense, and it is quieter, and it is the reason the butter-pale filaments never grow near the ropes.

Alignment is not neutral to flesh that did not ask for it. The same preference that lays the nap of a sleeve flat lays softer structures flat as well, given hours instead of minutes, and it lays them along a direction chosen by the stubs, not by the body's own grain. A bruise-bloom starts in the mouth and along the gums, not from impact but from capillaries that spent too long agreeing with a lean and then had to resume their own map. The eyes ache at the outer corners, where the lined tears ran, and the ache has a period for a day or two after leaving, rising and falling with nothing in the ordinary room to correspond to it. Knees that bore the weight on the rib complain not of standing but of a torsion, as if the polished stone had been slowly turning under the boots and the joints had matched it to keep the stance true. None of this kills. The loop is not a throat. It has no use for a dead splice — a body with its clocks stopped is just another mineral, and the room is already full of mineral, already fluent in mineral. What it uses, it uses alive. So the harm is the harm of being slightly, repeatedly rewritten in a handwriting that isn't yours, and of carrying the handwriting out with you when you go.

The animals learned this before any visitor did, and they declined the bargain.

What ticks once in the mild green cup and does not tick again is not hiding from a predator. There is no predator. It is spending its one safe interval on the single click by which it knows itself to be itself, and then it is done spending, because a second click would fall inside the next gathering, and inside the gathering the click would not come back as its own. The casings shattered the same way, washed into a line the surge erases, are the record of something that molted on the rib and dragged the cast-off toward the ropes without meaning to, and lost the cast-off to the lean — the old skin pulled into filaments, shredded, handed to the drip. The creature kept the new skin by getting off the rib before the slack ended. Sparse is not a scarcity of food. The room is warm and wet and full of mineral crumb; anything that wanted to crust the walls in life could do it in a season. Sparse is a discipline. Everything that persists here persists by refusing most of the cycles. It lives in the pauses, and it pays for each pause by not using the one before. Reproduction, growth, the long careless spreading that life does in a permissive place — all of that would require consecutive cycles of safety, and the chamber does not offer consecutive anything. It offers a trough, and then a use, and then a trough. To remain yourself you must be idle exactly when the room is most willing to hold you.

The butter-pale threads on the dry upper faces of the last stair have made a permanent strategy of this. They lie flat because flat is what the lean does to anything filamentous, and they have accepted the direction so completely that they no longer spend tissue fighting it. They point downstream of a shove that reverses, and they point the same way regardless, which means they are not tracking the air at all. They are tracking the average. The average over ten thousand reversals has a direction, slight, stubborn, the statistical ghost of the way the ropes lean when they lean hardest, and the threads grow along the ghost and nowhere else. Where one of them reaches past the average into a place the lean actually visits, it is unmade within a cycle — not eaten, unspun, its length recruited into the binding and then discarded as pale fuzz when the binding spends itself and slaps back down into ordinary water. The threads endure by being, almost all the time, useless to the apparatus. The moment they become useful, they are used up. That is the only ethics the room contains, and it is not an ethics. It is a wear pattern.

So the thing that lives in the chamber is the wearing. It is the coherence spending itself to remain a coherence, routing through stone, through mist, through thicker water, through any salt-and-signal body careless enough to stand where the route is cheapest, and throwing off, every cycle, a ruin of heat and roar and ordinary brightness that looks like weather and is bookkeeping. It does not want. Wanting would be a pause, and pauses are for the small things that survive by refusal. It holds. Holding is its entire biography: a machine that outlived the grammar of its makers and kept only the verb.

And the biography has a cost even the chamber pays, which is why the third stub flickers.

Watch the three live sockets through enough cycles and the flicker declares itself, not as failure but as arithmetic. Two of the ropes are steady in their period. The third drinks a fraction longer and repays a fraction hotter, and the fraction grows by an amount too small to see in a day and plain as a crack across a month. The dish knows. This is the failure-that-is-itself-regular: the prediction that breaks at the tenth cycle breaks because the third rope is borrowing against the other two, and the log in the spinning skin is honest about the debt even with no one there to be honest to. Coherence held past its design is not free for the holder either. Every splice the room routes through a visitor's heartbeat delays the debt by a handful of cycles and no more — a reprieve measured in heartbeats, purchased with someone else's phase, and the debt does not shrink. It waits. The plinth is not warning the visitor when its filaments stutter and split. It is posting the balance. Step back from the middle pans because the middle pans are where the borrowing expresses itself as a heave that arrives early and heavy, thicker than the depth allows, loyal to a line in the stone that is slowly, audibly, becoming a fault.

Nothing tends the balance. That is the final shape of the inhabitant: not a keeper, not a prisoner, not a god of the loud water. A debt that has learned to walk in circles. It patrols its own loop because patrol is what the geometry still compels, and every pass it is a little more expensive, and every pass it finds — if a body is there — a place to set the expense down for a moment, the way a beam finds a post. It will set it down on a person without hatred and pick it back up without gratitude. When the person leaves, limping slightly, dreaming in troughs, gums blooming with small regular bruises, the debt returns to the stone and the stubs and the third rope's lengthening drink, and the room goes on preferring whatever lowers the cost, even if nothing lowering it is present. The water climbs. The sheets hang a moment too long. The roar answers a cough with a blow. Between one slap and the next, in the trough that is not rest, the pale threads hold still along their average, and the one click comes up out of the mild cup, and is not repeated, and the count in the dish advances by a cluster that no one reads, faithful, early, and slightly wrong.
