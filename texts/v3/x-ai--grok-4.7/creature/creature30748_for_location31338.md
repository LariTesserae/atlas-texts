The first thing that lives there is a mistake, and it lasts a long time.

You will think the coil of cord is evidence of a person, and you will be right, and then you will wait for the person to be the answer, and the waiting will use up everything you have. The niches are the size of a life that almost started. The flakes came from lower down; someone carried a cobble that did not belong here, struck it until it consented to an edge, and set the edge to stone that had not yet agreed to stay stone. They worked until their hands shook from the roar. They may have slept in the niche, once, on the side of their face, because the floor would not hold a body on its back. Then the seam was open and the ridge was only wind, and they went, and left the cord because cord is heavy when your ears are full. They do not live here. They are the last animal this room has finished with.

The grass does not live here either, not in any sense that survives a count of days. The mineral fur is closer: a patience, a chemistry, the first green thought of a crack. It drinks the cool and gives nothing back that you would call a motion. If you need a population, you can have the fur. It will not object. It will not be what you came to find.

What you came to find keeps to the interruptions.

For a long time it is only a correction. The roar climbs, and in the climb there is a place where it does not. A cavity the length of a forearm, moving. Not silence — the room does not permit silence — but a thinner violence, a note subtracted, traveling the rain-wall the way a hand travels a rope it does not trust. If you watch the water for a fish you will not see it. If you watch the wall for a track you will not see it. The visitor who noticed a finger of pale muscle was watching the subtraction, and named the edge of it, which is all the body shows when it is working.

It has no fixed length. That is the part that takes longest to believe, because the room itself will not hold a measure, and so you keep blaming the room. Hold still anyway, if the floor allows it. When the curtain breaks into knuckle-sized beads and reforms, count the beads that hesitate. There is a run of them, never more than a few, that round themselves a fraction slower, as if the water had struck something that was not wall. The something gives. It is softer than the new stone and harder than the current. It does not flee so much as change employment. Along the wall it is a muscle, a single continuous fiber, pale as the inside of a wrist, pressed so flat the daylight goes through it and comes out the color of tin. In the open volume it is not a shape at all. It is a bias. Warmth where the extruded rock is giving up its heat; a cool thread where it has already drunk. Your sleeve, passing, goes heavy a hand’s breadth early. You think: the water thickened. The water did not.

It feeds on the decision.

Every joint the mountain makes in this pocket throws off a little heat and a little sweetness of mineral, the taste of melt meeting air and failing into rock. The fur takes the mineral. This takes the heat, and the small shock that comes with it, the instant in which stone is neither what it was nor what it will be. That instant is food. In a range still arriving there is no end of it. The creature — and by now the word is wrong, but the mouth wants a noun — does not graze a surface so much as attend a schedule. Where the floor grinds toward a new plane, it is already in the seam, flattened to a film, taking the warmth off the grind the way a tongue takes salt. Where a boss is about to be swallowed by a conduit that was not there a moment ago, it withdraws, not in fear, in the way a flame withdraws from a palm: the fuel has moved. It is never on the stone that has finished. Finished stone is cold and quiet and, to it, already dead. The visitor’s mistake was to look for life among the stable things. The stable things are the corpses. The life is in the argument.

It hunts, if that is the word, by being a difference in pressure that other differences fall into. There is almost nothing else to hunt. Once in a long while a bubble-line is not its breath but its tool: it vents a thread of itself sideways, not toward the lid — there is no up that matters — but across a conduit’s mouth, and the conduit misfires, and a little collapse of water drops a scatter of new flakes into the cavity it is keeping. It does not eat the flakes. It eats the drop. The impact. The tiny roar inside the roar. Then the vented thread is reeled back, and the bubbles, having nowhere to seek, are taken into the wall as if the stone had inhaled. A visitor sees the bubbles and invents a fauna. The fauna has already returned to being a pause.

Its body, when you have earned a look at more than an edge, is not an animal’s body and not a current’s. It is a tissue that has chosen, against the room’s constant instruction, to remain one thing. Pale, yes. Wet in a way that is redundant here. No eyes, because nothing in its life has ever been far. No mouth you could point to; the whole length can open along a seam, a long lip, and close on a gradient of heat. When it must be large it is large — it can sheet itself across half the rain-wall, a dimming you would call a cloud in the water, and the daylight still comes through, only a little wrong, a little delayed. When it must be small it is a knot no bigger than the coil of cord, packed into the niche if the niche is real that hour, so still that the chisel-marks seem to continue through it. It can be both in the same moment if the room flexes hard: a film on the wall and a knot in the niche, joined by a strand no wider than the fiber the early hands twisted, and the strand is the part it is willing to lose. The conduits take the strand, sometimes. It grows another. Pain, if it has pain, is a local event, the way the weather of the pocket is a local event. It does not propagate. A piece of it can be unmade and the rest does not flinch, because flinching would require a center, and the center moves.

It keeps one object, and the object is not a treasure.

The cord should have gone. The conduits have taken worse: shelves, a bowl if there was a bowl, the memory of a fire. The cord remains because the knot lives in it when the niche seals. Fiber and tissue occupy the same twists, turn for turn, so that a conduit closing on the coil would be closing on a living thickness, and the living thickness is a pressure the conduit has learned, the way water learns a stone it cannot shift. The cord is not loved. It is a shape that does not rewrite itself. In a room where every plane is a decision still being made, the twisted grass is the only geometry that recurs for reasons other than uplift. The creature uses it as a sentient animal uses a burrow it did not dig: a fact against which to measure its own drift. Without it, the pauses it inhabits would be pauses only, and it would thin, over not very many flexes of the floor, into the general roar, which is hungry in a stupider way and would not know it had won.

It does not watch you.

This is the mercy and the injury, and both are exact. A visitor is a heat, a weight, a new argument in the pressure, larger than a flake and briefer than a shelf. The creature registers the heat the way it registers newborn stone — with interest that is not personal, with approach that is not pursuit. It may lay a film of itself along the ledge where you stand, under the sole, taking the warmth that leaks through a boot. You will feel only that the water is more insistent there, that your foot goes heavy. If you speak, the shape of the speech dies in the medium, but the shock of it, the little collapse of intention against the roar, is a drop it can eat, and it will come to the place your mouth was, too late, pale lip closing on nothing. It does not follow you to the seam. The seam is a wasting. Outside, the heat of a body belongs to the wind, and the wind is a poverty. It has followed something to the seam before — the early hands, the shaking, the sudden absence of a large warm decision — and what it found was ordinary air, tin-colored sky, a ridge that had not learned to be a ridge, and a cold so thin it could not feed. It came back along the metal breath. It has not made the trip again. You may leave. It will finish the warmth you shed into the ledge, which persists a little, a ghost of a plan, and then the ledge will tilt and the meal will end and it will be at the rain-wall again, or in the cord, or nowhere you have a name for.

What it is becoming, if you stay long enough to be useless to it, is more particular than a principle.

It prefers the left of the curtain, the side the daylight strikes first, where the beads are smaller and the heat of the lid comes down in a cleaner sheet. It dislikes the rust streak — the mineral there is already deciding, oxidizing toward a conclusion, and conclusions are tasteless. It has a habit, when the floor’s grind drops in pitch, of gathering entirely into the niche for the space of three subsided notes, knotted through the cord, enduring the rewrite blind. It emerges larger by a thought. The fur in the crack has begun, at one edge, to grow in a pattern that is not the crack’s idea: a brief avoidance, a parting, where a lip has passed so often the chemistry has learned a gap. That is as close as the room has come to a sign of habitation. No track. No spoor. A place the first life will not grow, because something else is already keeping the appointment.

It will not become a god of the pocket, and it will not become a fish. Those are both finishes, and it feeds on the unfinished. If the range ever weathers — if erosion begins its long argument, if the pocket’s lid thins into a real sky or seals into real stone, if the condensed elevation loses its nerve and becomes either air or a lake — then the schedule of decisions will slow, and the heat will space itself out into years, and this life will thin along its last strand and be taken, not by a conduit, but by the ordinary, which is the only predator it has never learned to misfeed. Until then it is the room’s one completed refusal: a continuity that is not stone, not water, not the roar, living in the narrow fact that those three have not yet agreed. Almost nothing. Exact in its almost. Present in the way a subtracted note is present — not as a sound, as the shape the sound has to go around.

You can stand in the seam and call the pocket empty and be telling the truth you were equipped to tell. Behind you, if the curtain has not claimed the way, a pale film drinks the last of your heat off a ledge that may not be a ledge by evening, and does not look after you, because looking is a kind of distance, and distance is a thing it has never had, and does not miss.
