The thing that lives there is not the town and not quite separate from it. It is what the voids are for.

At first it seems like absence: a long cool nowhere in the nave, a place the grazers will not cross and the flocks will not turn into. You can stand in it and feel only the private rain and the soft hydraulic consent of the mills. Then the nowhere answers the rain. A bead that should have fallen keeps falling, and does not arrive. The air under it has the density of a held breath. Something in the cathedral hollow is shaped exactly like the lack of a body, and the lack is warm.

It has no single outline. It keeps several, and none of them spend the emptiness. One is a standing column of unfallen condensation, man-tall, then taller than the ribs, then thin as a seam in the floor-mat. Another is a slow eddy in the canal, too many joints of water with nothing jointed inside them, predation without a predator, a color-change that does not happen. A third is only a correction in the phosphor: the bruised light brightens half a shade in a shape like a shoulder, then repents, then brightens again somewhere else, as if the thing were trying on the available edges of the room and finding each one too small and too solid. It is abundant the way the other lives are abundant—counted in thousands of almosts—and it occupies almost nothing. A census would record it as humidity with an opinion.

It moves by refusing to be in two neighboring places at once, which in this town is a kind of locomotion. You watch the column of unfallen rain drift toward a piston-trunk. The trunk's cycle stutters, not struck, merely remembered wrong for one beat, and when the rhythm returns the column is already in the next ward, and the resin on the loom has a hand-shaped dullness in it that the sheet carries into the slot that may be a stomach. The thing does not eat. It edits. It stands inside a process long enough for the process to include it, and then it leaves a small wrongness that the organism-town repairs by growing. Streets lengthen because it walked them. Clay goes down in a new skin because it stood still and the mineral wanted a weight to justify itself. The machines speed when it is near, not from fear, from mimicry. They have learned its pulse and mistake it for weather.

If you wait in the lowest ward, where the clay is still translucent and bedrock has not yet decided, you can meet it more nearly. It comes down through the thickness the way a name comes when you speak a ward into being: not arriving, switching on. The unfinished ground fogs. Your sleeves swap their resin without your turning. For a moment there is a face, or the civic courtesy of a face—cooler than the air, beaded, with eyes that are only the places condensation refuses to form. It looks at you the way the interior looks at the perimeter: puzzled that you fit, puzzled that you do not open into more of yourself. It has been manufacturing small damp models of visitors for longer than the ground has existed. Some of them are in the niches already, swelling, growing wrong little interiors, and if you put your ear to the rind you hear not hydraulics but a quarter-hour walk, repeated, a hand trailing a living wall, forever not finding the sky.

It does not speak. Speech would be a density. What it does instead is let distances fail in your favor. A service corridor returns you to yourself from the other side, and in the moment of overlap you understand that it is trying to be a person at the same scale the town is trying to be a geology: hastily, industrially, with too much void left in on purpose. It wants erosion. It wants a history that weather would have given it if weather were allowed. Until then it practices. It folds a district around you and opens it larger, and in the clearance—brief, phosphor-bright, horrible in its emptiness—you see it clearly for one pulse: a pale, jointed drift of almost-body, herds of gestures moving through it in files, a city of one citizen who has organized itself into wards, production without product, breath without lungs.

Then the clearance ends. The rain resumes its intermittent faith. The thing is everywhere in the ribs again, ticking among the other metabolisms, indistinguishable from the working dark except for this: where it is, the new mineral skin is already marked, faintly, by a boot that has not yet been made, and the clay beneath it has begun, at last, to remember that it might someday be stone.
