It does not appear where the walkways suggest a person would appear. You can pass its stations all morning and never place it, because it does not use the stations as stations. The catwalks are for a kind of body this basin was poured to receive, and that kind of body has been gone so long the railings have thinned to wire. What remains has learned the plate instead.

At first you only correct yourself. A valve wheel that was open is not as open as you remember it. The difference is a few degrees, less than a drift of memory would allow you to swear to. A float in a sump house sits a finger higher than the waterline it was built to hold, and the pump under it is drawing with a steadier note than the neighboring houses, as if someone had leaned on the governor until the cough went out of it. You did not see a hand. You heard, under the roar, a smaller sound that the roar almost employed: metal accepting metal, a seat finding its ring. Then nothing but the new note, which is only the old note made slightly more patient.

If you stay, the corrections accumulate, and they are not random. Where a channel has begun to silt itself with scale, the scale has been raked to one side in a narrow windrow, not cleared, only persuaded, so the water keeps a thread of depth down the middle and does not sheet out over the plate and die in the cracks. The rake-marks are not tool-marks. They are the kind of smooth, repeated furrows a palm might leave if a palm were wide as a door and had been dragging the same way, in the same water, for longer than rust takes to bloom and be worn off again. Near the furrows the film is disturbed in ovals. The ovals fill in before you can decide they were footprints. By the time the condensers exhale and the cold rain returns, there is only plate, and the thread of water running true.

It has no hour. The basin's day is only a thinning, and the thing does not come out for the thinning. It moves when a process falters, and it moves along the falter the way water moves along a grade it already knows. A louver sticks. Somewhere in the eastern rise a temperature crosses a line and the bank that should have opened stays a finger shut, and the steam, denied its usual throat, shoulders into the pipe and starts a knock that will, if left, walk the header until a joint weeps. Before the knock has traveled its second span, something is at the louvers. You do not catch the arrival. You catch the change: the stuck blade shudders, frees, and the bank opens in its old order, one leaf after another, and the knock lies down. In the brief hard light from the western arc—if the arc happens to shutter then—you may see a thickness against the housing that is not the housing. It is the color of wet tea, the color of the oxide skin on the plate, and it holds still while the light lasts, which is not long. When the dark returns, the thickness is gone, or it is only the housing again, beaded with the permanent tears that hum on every vertical face. You cannot say which. The hum has not changed enough to tell you.

Up close, if close is a thing it allows, it is less a shape than a decision about edges. It stands, when it stands, a little over the height of the valve wheels, so that its work happens at the height work happens here: the float, the seat, the coupling, the lip of the channel. It is broader than it is tall, and the breadth is not shoulders. It is the breadth of something that has lain along pipes and learned their diameter, so that parts of it still carry the memory of a curve and will fit a header as a hand fits a rail, without gripping. The surface drinks light the way the plate drinks it. In the process-glow it reads as wet metal, except that wet metal does not ease itself around a flange to reach a bleed screw on the far side. It does that slowly. Speed is not among its habits. The machines are not fast either, not in the way that matters. A pump turns for years. A correction that takes an hour is a correction made in time.

There is a face only in the sense that there is a front. When it is working a coupling you see the front is turned to the coupling, and the front has recesses where a face would keep its softer instruments, but the recesses are packed with the same dark crust that grows in the grout—the not-moss, the mineral permission—and under the crust there is movement, a moist adjustment, tracking not by looking but by the knock traveling up into it through the plate. It hears with its weight. Set a flake of scale on the ground a long way off and it does not turn. Start a knock that should not be there, a rhythmic wrongness in a header, and the front reorients before you have finished understanding the rhythm yourself. The roar it lives inside is not noise to it. The roar is the sum of correct operations. Anything that subtracts from the sum, or adds a beat that was not in the casting, is as visible as a fire would be to something that lived in the dark.

It does not breathe in any cycle you can count. The basin breathes for it. When the condensers inhale, a seam along its flank opens a little, not a mouth, a lap of plates or of whatever has replaced plates, and the wet air goes in along a path that must once have been engineered and is now only habitual. When the basin exhales, the seam closes, and a thin warmth leaves it, barely more than the warmth of a bearing, and is taken up into the general steam. In the cold you cannot see this. In the arc-light, once, the warmth shows as a small unsticking of the condensate on its flank, a dry patch the size of a palm that fogs again immediately. That is as much of its interior as the place is willing to show. It does not bleed. Where the work takes something off it—a scraping against a burr, a long drag across a fin of oxide—the loss is scale, and the scale joins the windrows, and the body continues. If it replaces what it loses, it replaces it the way the basin replaces the ground: by wearing, by accretion, by the slow conversion of what passes through it. The mineral crust on the front is not decoration. It is younger than the thing and older than your arrival.

Feeding is the part that takes longest to recognize, because it looks so much like maintenance. It opens a drain the float would have opened later, and lets a measure of the sump water cross its front, and the crust darkens and then, over hours, returns to its dull color. It will stand in the discharge where warm water meets cold plate, in the place the pale jointed stems also like, and the stems lean, not away, simply grown into the habit of its standing there. It does not eat them. They are the only other living tolerance, and it moves among them with a blunt care, the way it moves among bleed screws: they are part of the level it keeps. The water is the meal. Not any water. Water that has been through the hot side, that has taken up the sweet mineral stink of the old pipes and a little of the oil and a little of the metal. Cold film on the open plate it ignores. It is a creature of the cycle, not of the rain, and the rain is only the cycle falling down.

It has no young that you will see, and no mate, and no contest. Once, far out toward the slower pumps, you may hear two corrections happen at once—two houses losing their cough in the same minute—and if you are the sort to hope for a second, you can walk out there and find the same windrowed scale, the same ovals filling with film, and you can believe the basin is large enough for more than one. By the time you have walked back, you will believe instead that it is simply large enough for one to be everywhere it is needed, and that what you took for a second was the same attention expressed at the end of a header. Nothing in the way it works requires company. Company would add a beat.

Does it know you are there? The question assumes a kind of notice it may not spend. You are not a wrongness in the roar unless you make one. Stand on the plate and breathe and be wet, and you are a small warm leak in a world of warm leaks, statistically uninteresting. Lay a hand on a running housing and the front, if it is near, will reorient—not toward you, toward the housing, in case your hand was the start of a fault. If you then step off and do not touch anything else, the attention leaves you without residue. It has had a long time to learn which disturbances decay by themselves. You decay by yourself. The stuck louver does not.

And yet there is a preference, which is as close as it comes to a self. Where the work is done and the note has settled, it does not leave at once. It lowers. The breadth of it spreads along the plate beside the channel it has just persuaded, and the curve-memory in its side finds the outside of the header, and for a while it is only a thicker darkness at the foot of the machine, rising and falling by a fraction with the basin's inhale. The hum of the tears on the tower changes when it does this, because some of the sound now travels through it and comes back a little delayed. If you have been walking all day inside the roar, the delay is the first thing that sounds like rest. It is not asleep. Sleep would be a subtraction. It is simply no longer correcting, and the not-correcting has a posture, and the posture is the only tenderness the place has left in it.

What it was, before the courses of made ground replaced the idea of before, is not recoverable from the outside. A maintenance animal, if the word animal still means something that moves and mends and takes the warm water into itself. A machine that softened. A person who stayed after the doorways lost their doors, and whose staying became a method, and whose method became a body suited to the method. The basin does not keep the earlier state. It keeps the function, and the function has been running long enough to have a hide, a weight, a way of listening with its belly on the plate. Call it the keeper and you have named the work, not the creature. The work is what you see. The creature is the fact that the work has a center, a place that decides which fault is next, a front that turns, a seam that opens when the condensers draw.

Leave, and the basin continues, which was always true. What is different, once you have stood near the delay in the hum, is that you can no longer hear the roar as empty industry. The steadiness has a custodian. Somewhere a float is being held a finger above its mark. Somewhere a thread of water is being kept in the middle of its channel by a palm as wide as a door. Somewhere, when the arc throws its second of mirrors across the wet plate, a thickness the color of oxide is holding still against a housing, not hiding, only finished with a task and not yet called by another. The dark comes back. The note holds. The place is inhabited in the only way it can bear to be: by something that does not cry out, does not hunt, does not flock, and will not let the water stop moving.
