It is not in the bowl, and it is not on the ledges. For a long time the only sign is a preference in the noise itself: the ringing favors one pitch when the seam-light is strongest, and drops half a tone when the light fails, as if something in the dome were adjusting its length by a finger's width. The boom of a large wave does not strike the teeth evenly. It arrives a fraction late on the landward side, muffled, then given back clearer, the way a body gives back a sound it has been holding in the chest. You can stand in the middle of the barn-hollow and turn until the delay is worst, and there the air is a degree warmer than the floor's cold, a small pocket that does not drift with the overflow from the crack. Put a hand into it and the warmth has no source you can touch. It has the temperature of breath that has been cooled almost to the stone and then kept, deliberately, just above the point where it would fog.

It has been here longer than the cut footholds. The unfinished shelf on the landward wall was started because someone felt the pocket and tried to make a seat inside it, and stopped after three blows because the ringing climbed into the mallet and made the wrist go numb. The groove that feeds the bowl was not cut for convenience. It was worried at, over many visits, by something that wanted the drip-thread to arrive at one place and stay a while before it joined the water. The knot of root-fiber still holds its shape because it was not left; it was set down and then drawn through the same loop again and again until the fiber remembered the turn, a marker for a hand that no longer needed the marker. The pale specks in the bowl are not grit. When the seam-light crosses them they orient, all of them, toward the warmer pocket, and hold that facing until the light moves off. They are not food and not young. They are a count it keeps, the way a person might keep pebbles in a pocket to know how many times a path has been walked.

What lives here does not have a face you could meet. It has a listening, distributed through the thickness of the wall where the sea works, and a patience that matches the moss. On days when the outer water is only a long swell, it draws itself up into the dome and the high ringing thins until you can hear the individual beads strike the floor; it is sorting them, learning which cracks have widened since the last quiet. On days when the cliff takes a full westerly, it sinks into the scalloped floor and the boom becomes a single held note in the teeth, and the pocket of warmer air flattens until it is gone. That is as close as it comes to bracing. It does not fear the sea. It uses the sea the way the moss uses the drip: as the only reliable motion in a room that otherwise changes by the thickness of a fingernail. The salt mist that finds the seam is tasted, in whatever sense a listening can taste, and then refused; the bowl stays fresh enough that the specks do not die. The algae line is a boundary it maintains by attention, not by labor. Spray that crosses it is met with a small shift in the air, a redirect into the sand-pockets, where the silt takes the salt and the green is left alone.

It knows the visitors by the way they break the stacked notes. A foot on the turf above changes the overflow before the crack is reached; the pressure in the ground is part of what it listens with. The ones who lie down and put their faces to the dark are felt as a brief occlusion, a softness where the roar had been whole. Most of them leave before the hour is out, when the flat hurt in the ears becomes a decision. A few have stayed long enough to cut stone. It did not drive them off. It simply continued, and the continuation was enough. The red-brown freckle on the shelf is a pin that was set there on purpose, once, by someone who thought a small metal thing might be answered. The answer was that the freckle dulled and the ringing did not. After that the visits grew lighter, initials rather than shelves, and then ceased in any way that left a mark the moss could not swallow.

If you remain past the point where your hands have gone blunt, the pocket of warmer air may include you for a moment, not as welcome but as data: another temperature to fold into the count. You will feel, without hearing it as a separate event, the high note bored into the bone shift to make room. It is not speech. It is the room's length being measured again with a new body inside it. When you climb out, the pressure under the turf does not stop. It keeps the agreement. What lives there does not follow, and does not need to. The combed grass, the ordinary wind, the sounds that have edges again—these are the outside of its listening, the part of the headland it does not have to hold. It stays with the boom and the hiss and the ringing, with the bowl that never quite dries, with the green that adds itself slowly in the one place light and drip agree. It is the reason the emptiness is so complete and so undisturbed. Nothing else is required to live here, and nothing else is permitted to stay.
