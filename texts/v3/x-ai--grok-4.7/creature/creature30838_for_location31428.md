At first they are only a mistake in the count. The lips of the pools are paved with flanks, throats working, and the eye files all of that as weather. Then one flank is not working. It has the same heat as the film on the stone, the same wet gloss, a color that belongs to the rind, and it is silent inside the wall of noise. A second stillness sits two bodies along, not touching the first, and a third is higher on the scarp where the threads of warm water strike and do not wander. They were underfoot and beside the hand before they were visible. Nothing about them startles the pavement. The singing bodies do not edge away. The still ones do not edge toward.

What separates them from the chorus is not size. They are low and long, the length of a reaching arm and then some, broad across the belly where the belly meets the rock, so that a standing glance takes them for another of the crowded swimmers hauled out. The difference is in the ends. Where a singer’s limb is a paddle for a slot of water, theirs divides, and divides again, into grips that close on the unfinished crust the way a root closes: not a clutch, a fitting. The digits are soft and precise and slightly translucent at the tips, and if one of them has been still long enough the tips have begun to cloud, a pale curd beading there as it beads on the stone. They do not scrape it off. It is not a deposit on them. It is them, at the rate they meet the mineral.

The skin runs the same bargain. Along the back it can look already geological — a thin rind, rust-flecked where iron has come through, jointed in clean faint lines — and at the flanks it is still living dark, the blue-black of the wet understory, film sliding on it and never drying. They shed without season and without ceremony. A shoulder pales, lifts at one edge, and comes away as a damp scale no thicker than the condensate on a leaf. The scale does not curl and blow. It settles onto whatever it was touching, stone or another flank or the lip of the sheet, and in a little while it is no longer distinguishable from the crust that was already there. Some of the basin’s unfinishedness is this. The land keeps the look of a thing interrupted while becoming stone because becoming stone is an ordinary motion of their bodies, continuous, local, and never completed into a monument. The rind you stand on is full of former surfaces. It will take a print and lose it, as the water films it, and some of what it loses was never yours.

Their eyes are large only because the lid gives them so little, and they do not shine. There is no point of light for a shine to answer. Each eye is a wet seed set wide, and it takes a field rather than a target. You can be inside that field and not be regarded as a separate event. They are reading pressure, warmth, the small displacement you make in a medium that was already full. If you stay, the reading thickens. Staying is the only way to become particular here. A passing heat is weather. A heat that keeps its shape long enough to be fitted against becomes a signature, and signatures are what they know.

They do not call. The basin is already all call, and they live inside it the way a current lives inside a river that has declined to flow. A listener who stops can tease one voice out of the wall — a metallic stutter in the reed, a thin strand, a low pulse that seems to come from the scarp itself. That pulse is theirs. It is not made in the throat. It moves through the belly where the belly is fitted to the crust, through the water-film, through the rind that their own shed surfaces have been thickening since before any one of them was a separate grip. Lay a hand on a still flank and the pulse is in the hand, the same tempo as the one you had assigned to the rock. They are conducting the basin’s steadiest sound, and the rock carries it farther than their bodies reach. Across the sheet, on a hummock you will not clearly see, another belly answers, not in imitation but in the same circulation. Whether this makes them one animal or many is not a question the place resolves. Bodies do separate. Grips release. And the pulse does not break when they do.

Separation is their whole history, and it leaves nothing you could put in a sequence. The young begin as a thickening along a flank, under the film, sharing the parent’s heat so exactly that the join is visible only as a slower place in the working of the skin. They feed from the parent’s reach: whatever the grip brings, whatever the small mouth takes from the fret of insects or the organisms riding the mat. There is no nest, no clutch waiting on a date, no hour when the water suddenly teems with a generation. When the thickening is long enough to meet the stone on its own account, the join clouds, rinds over, and comes apart as shed skin comes apart. Two signatures where there was one. The parent’s flank closes. The new body is already in contact with whoever is paved beside it, and the contact is not instruction and not abandonment. It is the same arrangement the basin uses for everything — near enough to touch, replaced at the rate of loss, unmarked by ceremony. You can watch a joining end and not know you have watched a birth, because nothing is emptier afterward and nothing has been added to the noise.

They eat as the rest of the basin eats, which is to say constantly and without making a hole. A grip slides, takes a bright body out of the fret, and the glitter is already whole. A mouth meets the film and the mat and does not cloud the water. They do not hunt in any way that would draw a path. Paths require repetition against a surface that consents to remember, and this surface consents only to become. What they prefer, if preference is the word, is a gradient too slight for a visitor’s hand: a thread of weep that is a shade warmer, a slot where the reed stutter is regular, a patch of rind still soft enough to take the grip and not yet set against it. They shift toward these with a redistribution so slow it can be mistaken for the general uneasy motion of the packed living. A hand releases a wrist. A digit finds a joint in the crust. The cluster has changed its membership, and no one has gone anywhere a traveler would call elsewhere. Elsewhere, across this basin, is the same heat and the same nearness. Travel would be a change of neighbors, not of country.

What they do, mostly, is attend. Attention is not a pause between actions. It is the action. A body fitted to the lip is reading the thread of water on its back, the pulse arriving through the stone, the insect-fret at the margin of the mat, the signature of the flank along its own flank. None of this is gathered into a picture to be used later. Later is not a place they store anything. The basin does not offer the differences out of which a store would be built — no cool hour, no dry hour, no band of spent growth, no carcass that outlasts the moment of its loosening. When one of them dies, the grips simply fail, in no special order, and the body is taken up as a leaf is taken up, at the rate it becomes available, into the living layer and the mineral film. There is no mound, no darkened roost, no site. The mourning, if that is what the rearrangement should be called, is conducted entirely in contact. A signature is absent from the web of pulses. Every reach around that absence adjusts by a fraction. By the time a watcher looks for the gap, a flank occupies it, because the basin does not hold gaps, and the knowing has moved into the new pattern rather than into a story about the old one. They do not search. A lost one is not imagined in some farther pool, still itself, waiting to be recovered. Particularity here is a rhythm against other rhythms. When the rhythm stops, the particularity has nowhere to be except in the changed touch of those who remain, and that change is already being overwritten by the next fitting, as a print in the wet rind is overwritten by film.

They are not empty of relation. Relation is the medium they have instead of time. Two that have been paved together long enough learn a micro-rhythm: a double pulse, a hesitation in the grip, a way of sharing a thread of weep so that neither body takes the warmer side always. You can see a pair maintain that sharing across a shift from scarp to mat to scarp, neighbors changing all around them, the pair’s fitting intact. It is not ownership. Either may be touched by others, and is. It is a persistence of signature inside a world that persists by not keeping. If one of the pair ends, the other’s rhythm goes on alone inside the circulation, a limp in the pulse that the rock carries a little way, and then the limp is fitted into neighboring bodies as a new hesitation, and something of the pair has been distributed rather than remembered. They do not have artifacts in which to put the dead. They have the continuance of a timing.

Nothing in their attention organizes around you unless you become a timing. Stand among them and you are a warmer stone, briefly, and the grips that find your boot or your ankle are testing purchase, not claiming it. They will not hold you if the hold does not fit. They will not follow you out, and there is no out that announces itself — the rim dissolves, the grade repeats the same rules, the pulse goes as far as the rind goes. If you remain through what would elsewhere have been a day, you start to enter the web. A belly fitted near your leg learns the small shift of your weight. A digit returns to the same place on your skin, not affectionately, accurately. You have become a feature that has not yet set. Leave, and the rearrangement happens at once: the reaches close, the saturation is what it was, and the only residue is a slight wrongness in the pulse, a missing hesitation, already being absorbed into the flanks that remain. They will not retain you as a visitor. The basin does not retain visits. What they are is the part of this pause that can feel the pause from inside, body by body, shed by shed, grip fitted to a crust they are still becoming, listening to a circulation that passes through them and is not confined to them.

The singers go on. The fret goes on. The sheet takes its slow backs and eyes and restores them. Among all that overwhelming life, the still ones are simply more itself than the rest — less calendar, more continuance — and if you watch long enough the stillness shows its fineness. A clouding at the tips. A scale lifting on a shoulder. A young thickening, unannounced, under a film. A pair sharing a thread no eye would have picked out of the weeping. They are not the genius of the place and not its exception. They are what it feels like to live exactly at the rate the land is willing to be: unfinished, crowded, loud around them, silent in their bodies, warm as the water, and already, at every point where they touch the world, turning quietly into the stone.
