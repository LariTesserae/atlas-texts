What lives here does not stand against the flat. It has given that argument up so completely that for a long time you would not grant it a body at all.

At first it is only a discrepancy in the water. A pan that should be tea-colored over a hidden bottom shows, for the length of a breath, a paler oval just under the film—then the oval is gone, and what remains is the ordinary seepage, the ordinary ticking of midges, the ordinary weak shadow. You would blame the light. The light here is always willing to be blamed. It comes through vapor, it lies down on every skin of water, and it makes mirrors out of things that are not mirrors. A second time, farther on, the same oval: not a fish-turn, which is quick and edged, but a slow paling, as if the silt itself had thought about rising and then thought better of it. The third time you stop walking. The ribs of firmer mud underfoot are the only paths, and even those are only preferences. You are already inside its country by the time you notice you are being measured.

It keeps to the permanent wet, the blue-black inch beneath the cracking skins. That is its weather. The crusts warm in the morning and bead, and the mats breathe and curl, and none of that is home. Home is the paste that does not dry in a year, the layer where seepage slows to a drift and the drift slows to a holding. Into that it fits the way water fits a pan: by having no project of shape except the shape the ground will allow. When the sheet spreads after rain, it spreads with the sheet. When the sheet draws back, it draws back, not by decision but by the same losing of level. A person watching from the firmer rib would see, over hours, only that one pan stays cloudier than its neighbors, and that the cloud does not settle.

Scale arrives late, and wrongly. The first honest sight is not of a whole animal but of a margin. Where a pavement of iron-stained crust meets the wet, something has laid itself along the seam to take the warmth the stone keeps after the sun has thinned. It is the color of the seam. That is not camouflage in the quick sense, the sense of a trick. It is the older compliance: a surface that has been silt-colored for so long that color is no longer a coat but a condition. Along the seam, a lip of it lifts and settles with the same interval as the ripples a long wind combs into open water. You can count the lifts. They are not breaths you would recognize. They are the body agreeing, again and again, not to be drier than the ground.

Come nearer—slowly, because speed is wasted here and it has learned the waste better than you—and the lip resolves. Not a fin. Not a foot. A leading edge, thin as the algal flake it resembles when the flake is wet, thickening backward into a body that never quite decides to leave the mud. The thickening is low. Relief, even in a living thing, is a matter of ankles. At its highest, where the mass gathers over whatever serves it for a spine, it would not clear the hand you might hold above it. Width is the dimension it trusts. It can cover a pavement and still have margin left to feel the film on either side, the living scum it both rests upon and, in a sense, continues. Touch, were touch possible without breaking the rule of the place, would find it neither slimy nor scaled. It would find the same paste the boot finds when it goes through a skin: mineral, faintly sweet, warm all the way through, as if the afternoon had been stored in it rather than shone upon it.

The head is the part that refuses, slightly, to be landscape, and even the refusal is modest. It is a broader weighing of the same flesh, set forward, with no neck to announce it. The eyes are small and set wide and low, so that they look along the water rather than over it. In the ordinary brightness they are hard to separate from wet silt; in the late amber, when every remaining skin becomes metal, they take a dull copper and you understand that they have been open the whole time. They do not track the way a heron’s eye tracks, that stillness which is a cocked weapon. They take in sheets. A flock lifting at the far edge of a pan arrives to them as a change in the light and a change in the sound together, and the body answers, if it answers, by settling a fraction deeper, not by turning. There is a mouth, but you will not see it work from above. It is ventral, a long slot that opens against the film when the film is richest, and closes on a pulp of algal mat, midge-fall, the soft dead of whatever the warm water has already begun to undo. Feeding is not a strike. It is a continuation of lying still. The slot opens. The mat dimples. A few fingerlings, no longer than a nail, vanish not because they were hunted but because they were in the pulp. The slot closes. The dimple fills. A heron a long walk off never turns its head. This is not the competition it understands.

What startles is the sound, once you have subtracted the country’s own. The place is never quiet: the stitch of shorebirds, the relay of frogs, the minute ticks of bubbles loosening from mud. Under that, this animal adds almost nothing, and the almost is the tell. When it shifts—and it shifts seldom, and only by the length of its own leading edge—there is a suck quieter than a boot would make, a private syllable of water closing where flesh has been. When it holds, there is sometimes a finer tick from beneath the near pavement, as if a claw, or a comb of stiff tissue, were testing the crack-seams for the seep it prefers. It does not call. Nothing in its life is far enough away, or dear enough, to be called to. The other of its kind, if it is near, is near in the way the next rush is near: thirty paces, a hundred, a dark vertical absence rather than a body you could point to. They do not flock. Density is the mistake the land has already corrected, and they are the correction made flesh.

You learn its age the way you learn the age of the pavements, by what has failed to mark it. There are no scars that read as events. There are only thickenings, slightly paler, where an old skin split in a dry week and healed by being silt again. Along one flank a seam of mineral has crusted into the flesh and been grown under, so that it carries a little of the iron-stone the way the ground carries it: not as ornament, as residue. It may have been here when the last low rib of silt was still a suggestion of a hill. It may have been here only as long as the mats have been this color. The two spans are not different enough, in this country, to be worth separating. Seasons do not pass through it. They pass over, and it keeps the mean: the warm, the wet, the level that is neither flood nor crust. In the rare week when the pans draw down until even the blue-black shows as a gleam between plates, it does not migrate and it does not dig in any way you would call digging. It thins. It spreads until the mass that was a low mound is a stain under the last water, and it waits inside the stain, mouth to the film, eyes to the sheet of light, metabolism slowed to the tick of bubbles. When the water returns, the stain remembers how to be thick. That is the whole of its drama, and it is enough.

Watch it at the hour the rushes go black and the frogs take the middle distance. The birds settle, each a small dark weight, scattered on principle. The animal does not settle because it has not been elsewhere. A last finger of wind crosses unimpeded and combs the pan, and the leading edge lifts once, in agreement, and is still. In the copper of the low light you can see, for the first time, the full outline: wide as a pavement, lower than the rushes, continuous with the wet it lies in, the head a weighting, the eyes two small dull coins looking along the level toward a horizon that is not a destination. Nothing about it asks to be followed. Nothing about it flees. If you step back onto the rib and walk the way you came, it will remain as a cloudier pan, and then as a paler oval, and then as the ordinary seep, and the country will go on sounding of itself.

What lives here is the flatness, given just enough private motion to persist. It eats the film. It keeps the warmth. It refuses height, and speed, and the company of its own kind beyond the distance the land allows. In return the land does not bury it, and does not finish it, and does not ask it to become anything a witness could carry away. By full dark you would not find it again even if you had marked the seam. The mark would already be a skin of water, and the skin would already be reflecting a sky that has no more shape than the ground.
