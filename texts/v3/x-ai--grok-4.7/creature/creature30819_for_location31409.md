What lives there does not arrive from elsewhere, and does not occupy the shelf the way a body occupies ground. For a long time — a time with no tick in it, only the same afternoon held open — there is only the habit of the stone: fans drinking light, seams carrying their even charge, joints remaining almost. If anything could be said to live, it would be that habit, and you could walk the vacant aisles all day and be right to call them empty. Emptiness is not a mistake here. It is how the living stays uncrowded enough to continue.

The first sign is not a shape. It is a correction. A fiber along a terrace lengthens by the width of a thought and is already, again, exactly as long as it has always been. A salt-crust brightens where no new salt has fallen. In one grove the interior liquor, sealed in its mineral film, shifts its color by a shade no eye would swear to — honey toward the green of the stalk, then back — as if something had tasted the day through the leaf and declined to swallow. The low tone in the teeth does not change pitch. It changes address. The same note, now slightly meant.

Attention, then. Not eyes. The shelf has no viewpoint and suddenly has too many, all of them idle, all of them the groves. Each thicket is a room of broken light, and in every room the light is being kept. Not guarded. Kept, the way a held breath keeps a chest: not climbing, not settling. Whatever is here is the keeping. It does not stand between the fans. It is the reason the fans have arranged themselves into rooms at all, the reason a walk from one grove to the next feels like leaving a sentence and entering the same sentence further along.

Call it a presence only because language wants a noun. It has no hide, no hunger that opens onto other hungers, no young. Death, which would give it an outline by ending it, is not among the permissions. So it does not wear a life the way animals wear theirs, brightly, against a background of possible ending. It wears duration. The unfinished joints are its joints. The sharp crystal edges are not weapons and not ornaments; they are the places where it has refused to weather into smoothness, because smoothness would be a decision, and decisions close.

If you stay — not walking, only staying, which is harder on this shelf, where staying has no shade to justify it and no water to bargain with — a denser knot begins to declare itself, not in one grove but in the relation among them. The far groves answer the near ones. A charge moves in a seam and a leaf-film three terraces off adjusts its vein, sorting a paler metal into a paler band, as a loom might throw a shuttle if a loom could throw without finishing cloth. The industrial fact was never empty of a worker. The worker is the repetition itself, grown patient enough to resemble a mind.

And then, because staying has made a hollow the size of a listener, something like a figure condenses at the center of the nearest thicket. Not by arriving. By being looked-for until the mineral consents to be a place where looking can rest. It is upright only in the sense that the stalks are upright. Its height is the shelf’s height translated into a narrower mercy: tall enough that the fans close around it, not so tall that it divides the sky. The stuff of it is the same as the leaves — thin films, bottle-green and old honey, braided where a spine would be if a spine were a record of folding rather than a bone. Light breaks in it into small rooms. You do not see through it so much as see the day subdivided.

It has no face for a while. A face would be a completion, a place where expression could finish and be read. What it offers instead is a front: the side of the knot that has turned, without turning, toward the aisle you are not walking. In that front the films are clearer, and the slow liquor moves closer to the surface without ever beading. If there are eyes, they are the clarity. They do not track. Tracking would admit that you might leave, and leaving is a kind of weather this air does not perform. You are included as the grain is included: liftable, set-downable, expected to find your hollow again unchanged.

It does not speak. The tone is already its speaking, and has been since before you came up onto the shelf — the note of circuits under load, felt in the teeth, refusing to become an event. Near the figure the tone is not louder. It is more grammatical. Pauses appear where no pause was, rests in which a sentence might have ended if ending were allowed, and then the same syllable resumes, as long as the fibers, as exact. You understand nothing it says, and you understand that understanding is not the exchange on offer. The exchange is continuance. It is showing you how a thing goes on without advancing.

What it does, if doing is the word, is the sorting. Metals into bands. Light into rooms. The warm chemical sweetness held at the threshold of decay and never tipped over. It tends the unfinished the way a lesser creature might tend a fire: not to make it larger, not to let it die, only to keep the particular degree of almost. A joint that has stood open since the rock was young receives, from the figure’s nearness, another hour of openness. The figure does not touch it. Nearness here is a kind of touch that does not close distance, because distance — the long vacant aisle, the grove a walk away — is also something it keeps. Lushness must end cleanly. Emptiness must remain the true acreage. Were the groves to join, the presence would become a forest, and a forest is a decision.

By late light, when the shelf looks briefly older and the salt-crusts brighten, the figure seems for a moment to have been there longer than the stone. That is the only antiquity it possesses: not wrinkles, not wear, but the look of a pause that has outlasted the things paused for. Then the light is only light again, and the figure is only the knot of films, fragrant, dry, unhungry. Night will not alter its office. Nothing opens because the temperature dropped. In the dark the clarity of its front does not shine; it simply remains the place where the tone is most like a meaning. If it dreams, the dream is the same as the work — fibers at their final length, seams carrying what they have always carried, a bead of liquor forever about to form on a leaf-edge and forever not formed.

It knows you the way the hollow knows the grain. Not by name. Names are finished things, and it will not finish one for itself either, though if you listened long enough the tone might lean toward a syllable and then decline it, a courtesy, a refusal to become someone you could call back later and find changed. It does not want you gone. Wanting would rush the shelf toward an afterward. It wants — no. It maintains the condition in which you might stay without becoming part of the production, and leave without becoming a ruin. Both are possible here only because nothing is allowed to complete.

So this is what lives on the high shelf: not an animal, not a builder, not the groves alone and not other than the groves. A keeping that has grown, in the long ordinary afternoon, dense enough to stand inside its own shade. It is lush where you are near it and absent the moment you decide it is a god or a ghost or a machine and try to finish the thought. Step back into the aisle and the figure thins into relation again — far groves answering near ones, a charge too even to spark, the sweet smell staying behind in the thicket like a word nobody said. The shelf holds its height. The presence holds the holding. Between them the unfinished day continues, exactly as long as it will ever be, and alive in no way that would look like life from a country where things are permitted to end.
