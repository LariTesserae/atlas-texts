They are not visitors. Nothing arrives here from elsewhere and stays. What lives in the valley is the same material as the floor, only organized past the point where a structure can be told from a habit.

You notice them first as discrepancies in the knocking. A column’s sap-pulse is irregular but local: it has a tempo, a sore flank, a side it prefers to swell. Then a second pulse enters it from a weld that did not exist at noon, and the two tempos do not blend. They negotiate. The donor pales in a stripe no wider than a wrist. The stripe moves. It is not a shadow and not a leak. It is a thickening traveling inside the wall, shouldering the resident flow aside the way a person shoulders through a crowded passage without becoming the crowd. Where it passes, the sheath goes taut, then slack, and a bead of hotter sap prints itself on the inner surface and is reabsorbed. If you put a hand on the outside—hot, flexing, untrustworthy—you feel the traveler as a knot sliding under the skin, purposeful, a little rude.

They have no fixed outline. A traveler in a vein is a pressure-shape, a few body-lengths of organized thirst. When it must cross open air it does not jump. It spends itself. At a frayed bridge-end the knot gathers until the bulb swells glossy and dangerous, and then it extrudes: a tongue of new sheath, finger-thick, then arm-thick, paying out into the gap with a wet tick as each segment locks. The tongue is the traveler. For the minutes of the crossing there is no distinction between the one who moves and the bridge it is making. If the far lip is close enough, the tongue welds, the knot slips through, and the span left behind may hold or may cork and fold by evening, a spent road, already being quilted. If the far lip is not close enough, the tongue thins, chalks, and drops. What drops is not a corpse in any useful sense. It is the cost of the attempt, and the traveler is simply not on the other side. Elsewhere, in some other vein, another knot with the same tempo may already be testing another gap. Whether that is the same one, continued, or only the same argument restarted, the valley does not keep a ledger fine enough to say.

They are not many. Against the scatter of towers they are sparse the way the towers are sparse against the air. You can watch a whole slope of hoods and knives through the steam-ropes of an afternoon and see only one crossing, one pale stripe drinking a flank, one bulb paying out into nothing and failing. Most of what stands is uninhabited in the traveling sense. It grows, splits, sings, slumps, and is only itself. The travelers prefer the welded places, the arguments already in progress, because a living vein is a road and a dead gutter is only weather. They drink as they go. A tower that has hosted one comes out of the hour darker along the path and lighter everywhere else, and sometimes, insulted, throws a sharp new eave in the direction the knot departed, as if to close a door that was never a door.

There is a second kind, and it does not travel.

These stay in the hoods and the cistern pockets, and they are slower to see because they are the holding rather than a disturbance in it. A hall-sized hood with its shallow pool is, most of the time, only architecture sweating. Then the sweat changes phase without a change in the sun. The walls tighten. The pool’s surface, which had been shivering with drip, goes tight as a drum, and the deep note in the pipes drops a half-step and stays there, no longer tracking the water level. Something has taken the level in hand. It is using the hood as a lung and a belly at once: drawing vapor in through the unfinished mouths, condensing it along a chosen wall, releasing it as a sheet into the pool, then flashing a measured portion back to steam so the pressure in the hollow columns knocks on a beat. The beat is too even to be growth. Growth is sore and lopsided. This is kept. For as long as the keeping lasts—an hour, a day, rarely longer—the hood does not rupture, does not sag, does not reform. It is occupied. When the occupation ends, it ends all at once: the surface slacks, the note sours, a seam the keeper had been refusing splits with the cloth-tear sound, and a ton of warm water dumps. The keeper is not in the dump. It was never a body you could pour out. It was the decision, distributed through the sheath, to hold a level in a place that does not hold levels. Afterward the hood is only a hood again, younger by one catastrophe, already beginning the slow work.

They do not speak. The pipes speak, and sometimes a keeper rides the pipes, so a whistle that had been wind becomes, for a phrase, intended—the same note repeated until a traveler in a distant vein alters course, or until it doesn’t, and the repetition frays back into weather. Whether that counts as a call is a question the place is too new to have settled. What is clear is that keepers and travelers are not the same persistence. A traveler drinks and spends and is mostly a direction. A keeper refuses and spends nothing it can help and is mostly a pause. They meet rarely, and the meetings are not tender. A knot entering an occupied hood is met by a wall that suddenly will not conduct. The traveler stacks against the refusal, bulbous, indecently full, and either backs up the vein it came by or forces a weld the keeper did not want, in which case the hood’s note shatters into the ugly chord of a tilted mat and both of them are, for a little while, just tissue again, sorting pressures with no one home.

There may be a third, and if there is, it lives in the shove.

Not in any sheath you can put a hand on. Under the crust, in the forcing that has not cooled, something answers the tilts. It is easy to miss because everything here moves, and a degree of slant feels like weather until you notice that some slants arrive when no seam has split and no pool has slopped first. The mat tilts, the pipes change key together, and only then does the water move. The order is wrong for an accident. Travelers freeze in their veins when it happens; you can feel them, if you are unwise enough to be touching a column, go from knot to mere sap, unanimous, as if a posture had been dropped. Keepers lose their levels. For the length of the chord the valley is unoccupied, and then the shove stops, and the small violences resume, and the knots gather again in the throats of the veins like animals that were never animals remembering a direction.

Nothing hunts them. They do not hunt. There is nothing to hunt that is not already the body they are spending. A traveler that fails mid-air is not eaten; it is inherited by rain, which prefers the dead, and by the next mat, which quilts without appetite. A keeper that is dumped is not killed; it is interrupted, and may or may not resume in the hood that sags and reforms, and there is no mark on the new walls to tell you which. They leave no young in any separable sense. What spreads, spreads by running, and a successful weld is as close as they come to offspring: a vein that now exists, which some later knot may use, or a level that held, which the weather has already begun to ruin. If they have memory, it is structural—the paled stripe, the eave thrown in spite, the pipe that once held a phrase—and it weathers out of the sheath at the same rate as everything else here weathers, which is fast, and visible, and never quite faster than the work of laying new skin over the bones of the last hour.

Call them inhabitants and the civic word will mislead you, as town misled you. They do not dwell so much as they persist, locally, against a crust that is still being forced up and a heat that will not let any surface finish drying. In the open light they are almost not there: a moving thirst, a held level, a shove from under that empties both. In the green pockets, in the breathless loudness, they are the only difference between a machine of sap and a place where something is insisting—briefly, expensively, without a face—on a direction, or on a pause, before the valley takes the material back and continues its larger argument alone.
