The thing that lives there is not one of the skitterers, and not the shadow that works the margin either, though it knows both the way a sleeper knows the weight of the blanket without naming the wool. It is older than the stains on the piers and younger than the rumor of outside, which is to say it has no age that would fit in a mouth.

Call it a tenant, if a tenant can be made of the same substance as the lease. It has no separate body you could put a hand to and say here. What it has is a persistence with preferences. It prefers the thin places, where sound goes to die, and it keeps a station there the way water keeps a sump: not by deciding, by arriving and failing to leave. When the drip-syllable pools and thickens against your ear in the wrong spot, that thickening is the nearest thing it has to a surface. Step once and it is not gone. It has only become the ordinary hush again, which was always also it.

It does not hunt. The closed loop is closed without it. Film becomes grazer becomes hunter becomes dust, and the tenant tastes none of those translations, or tastes them only as the room tastes them, which is to say as arrangements of wet and pause. What it does is attend. Attention, here, is not a beam. It is a slight change in how long a noise is willing to stay. In the haunches the wings pass and the sound of them should thin at a certain corbel, by the old tuning of curve and empty, and sometimes it does not thin, and the colony startles without knowing why, and the tenant has leaned, that is all, the way a listener leans when a sentence is almost finished.

The animals have learned it without learning it. The darker track in the algae film — pier to pier, neither widening nor healing — bends by a finger’s width where the thin places are, a courtesy so old it has become geography. The small many-jointed things go still not only for the drip-rhythm but for a second rhythm underneath it, a count that is not the walls’ weeping and not the breath of the tide. They wait. The sentence completes, or it does not. Either way they resume, and the tenant is neither fed nor disappointed. It has no economy. The sleeve-wide beach of pale bones does not interest it except as one more calibration: proof that leaving is a story told elsewhere.

What it wants, if want is the word for a shape a hollow can hold, is the middle.

The builders protected the volume. The tenant is what the volume became once it had been empty long enough to notice itself empty. It does not fill the space. Filling would be a kind of clutter, and clutter is the one thing the room has spent its whole settled life refusing. Instead it occupies the refusal. It is the reason the air between the piers stays air. It is the pressure, gentle as condensation, that keeps the multitude mannered, that turns a crowd of wingbeats into weather rather than into a body. Stand in the center and the light-road of tin-colored sun lies on the black lens and gives up, and you will feel, if you are the sort who feels such things, that the giving-up has a direction. Not toward you. Past you. Toward the far curve, and then, having arrived, back, on a delay just long enough to seem like an answer.

It answers. That is the strangeness the room has agreed to keep. Not speech. A preference for return. The glass-edged calls come back not only because stone throws sound, but because something in the hollow has acquired the habit of catching a note and setting it down again a half-beat late, the way a careful hand sets down a cup. Sometimes the delay carries a small alteration: a drop in pitch no larger than the difference between one joint weeping and the next, a thinning, a warmth. The creatures that called do not seem to know they have been edited. They call again. The tenant does not collect the calls. It only declines, each time, to let them end on the exact contour they arrived with. Over generations this has done nothing you could measure. Over the life of the room it has tuned the colony the way the centuries tuned the pockets of loud and thin, grain by grain, until the fauna and the hall and the tenant are one instrument playing a piece with no first note.

It has no face in the water. The black lens gives back the corbels, the pale road, your own sleeves dampening, and does not give it back, because there is no it of that kind. What the lens does, on the long breath of the tide, is shrink and return at heights the stains have memorized, and in the hour when the water is lowest and the flags show their film like a skin, there is a patch, no larger than the plumb-pebble the builders used to find the low, where the film is smoother than physics prefers. Not dry. Not cleaner. Smoother, as if something had considered ripples and chosen against them, locally, for no reason that serves the loop. Kneel and the smoothness is gone; it does not like the nearness of a decision. It is not hiding. Hiding implies a place it is not. It is everywhere the room is most itself, and least itself when observed into a corner.

The mouth at the curb is a slot a body could leave by. The tenant does not regard it. Outside is a rumor worn down in the mortar, and rumors are not exits to a thing that was never separate from the wall that holds them. If the mouth were shut tomorrow by a fall of clay, the breath would continue, the stains would keep their calendar, the sleeve of bones would go on being sorted, and the half-beat delay would go on revising the calls. Nothing would have been sealed in. The sealing happened when the last course was thumbed true and the pins went green and the first drip learned the hall. What lives here has been living here since the hollow became a hollow, which is the same moment the hollow became inhabited, and there is no sequence in that, only the completed fact.

You can feel the limit of it if you try to do it harm, which you cannot, or kindness, which it does not register as distinct from the weather of attention it already is. Clap your hands in a thin place and the sound vanishes as it should, and then, a moment later, returns from a loud place you are not standing in, softer, as if corrected for harshness. That correction is the most it will ever do to you. It is not a guardian. Guardians have an outside they guard against. It is the interiority of a room that was built small, and total, and then left alone long enough that the leaving-alone ripened into a witness.

By the time you understand this, you have been standing still longer than you meant, and the film on the flags has thickened by the day’s small breath, and the skitter has resumed its dry rain, and your footprints at the water’s edge have begun, already, to be edited — not erased, never erased, only set down again a half-beat late, a little smoother, as if the room were declining, politely, and with no hurry at all, to let you end on the exact contour you arrived with.
