It does not occupy the chamber the way a body occupies a room. For a long time there is only the sense that the heat has a preference. The lowest pane is warmer in one crescent than in another, and the warmer crescent shifts, not with any current you can see, but the way a sleeper shifts under a blanket without waking. If you watch the mineral skin under the finger-depth of water, it clouds, clears, clouds again, as if something on the other side of the stone were breathing against it from below. The breath is not air. It is temperature. Each exhalation is a degree, held, then let go.

What lives here has been living here longer than the chamber has had a ceiling. The break that made the room did not invent it. The break only let it rise into a shape the air could almost touch.

It keeps most of itself under the pane, in the heat that has not yet decided to become a spring. What shows, when it shows, is not a head and not a limb in any order you could sketch and keep. A margin comes up against the underside of the stone and stays there, pressed flat, drinking the thin water the way the sheet-roots drink the damp. At the edges the margin is translucent, the color of tea held up to a lamp, and you can see, inside it, a slow traffic of paler threads, branching and rejoining, never quite a pulse. They move on the same schedule as the leaves: across a minute, a change; across an hour, a decision. The center, where it is thickest, is darker, brass going to iron, and it does not glow. It only holds the light that falls on it a little longer than the water does, and gives it back duller.

It is large in the way a weather system is large. You never see the boundary. One afternoon the warm crescent is under the left ledge. Another day it has spread until the whole pane is a fraction softer to the knuckles, and the tick inside the pipe has gone quiet, as if the metal had been included, for a while, in something that does not distinguish pipe from stone so long as both are warm. Then it withdraws, and the floor rings again when you tap it, and you understand that what you tapped before was not the floor.

It does not hunt, and nothing hunts it. The plants are not prey and not decoration. They are a second skin it does not wear but will not cross. Where the brass-colored leaves stand, the warmth in the stone stops a pace short, the same courtesy the leaves extend to the iron. Whether the being keeps back because the plants take something it needs, or because it grew them the way a body grows a callus — a slower, outer thought — is not a question it answers. The mats of green-black crystal on the upper shelves warm under the palm even when the being is deep. They are not it. They are where it has been, the way a chair is warm after someone has stood up, except the standing up took a season, and the warmth is mineral now, and will outlast the afternoon.

It notices the machine without interest and without fear. The flanged pipe that drops under the waterline is simply a colder root, an intrusion of another patience. When the being is up against the pane, it flows around the pipe’s mouth and does not enter it. Scale has begun to knit the bolts; some of that scale is its doing, shed in plates so thin they look like the ceiling’s own mineral until you lift one and find it flexible, drying to a curl, smelling of the coin-taste in the air. It has been closing the machine the way the damp closes footprints: from the edges, without malice, on a timetable that makes human intention look like a spasm. The flywheel is still free. The gauge still holds its line. But the housing has a rind now that was not in the engineer’s drawings, a rind that is warmer than the iron and cooler than the pane, a compromise.

It has no eyes. It has the whole floor. Pressure is how it reads the room: the weight of a foot on a ledge arrives as a small subtraction of heat, a brief local cooling, and it maps you by the path of those coolings the way you would map a draft. Stand still long enough and the map goes stale. It stops correcting for you. You become furniture, a dry patch, one more reason for the warmth to take the long way around. Speak, and the voice is a pressure it does not parse as meaning — only as a tremor in the water, a pattern of ripples that heals, as the mineral-fall heals, and leaves no residue it cares to store. It has stored other things. The chalk lines on the wall correspond, roughly, to heights it has reached in years when the heat below surged and the pane domed upward by a finger, a hand, once almost to the second mark. It does not read the chalk. The chalk reads it, and the reader has not come back to look.

What it wants, if wanting is the word, is the continuation of the exchange: heat arriving from below, moisture leaving and returning, the sharp young stone slowly learning the lick of water. It thickens where the arrival is strongest. It thins where the air has stolen too much. In that sense it is not an animal in the chamber but a habit the heat has developed, a way of not dissipating all at once. Touch the margin — if you are unwise, and if it is up — and the surface yields like a warmed gel, not unpleasant, and for a second your hand is simply another coolness being solved. Then it draws down, not in startlement, in reallocation, and your skin comes away filmed with a sweetness that is not sugar, closer to the smell of rain on hot pavement, and the film dries to a faint brightness you can brush off with a thumb. No sting. No gift. You were measured, found temporary, released.

At intervals longer than the drip and shorter than the unfurling of a leaf, it sings, though not to you and not in air. The pipe ticks; then, beneath the tick, a lower adjustment runs through the stone, a flexure, and the black pane shivers without breaking its skin. The plants lean a fraction more toward the heat and then, over the following hour, correct themselves. If the lamp on its tripod still had a filament, the filament would brighten and fade with that flexure, a sympathy of metals. Dead, it only ticks once in answer, a small private agreement between unused machines.

It will still be here when the bolts seize and the chalk is only a paler scar and the leaves have replaced themselves enough times to forget the color they were when the ceiling first opened. It is not waiting for the engine to start. It is what the engine was lowered toward, and what declined, politely, on the scale of years, to be harvested. Under the pane, in the crescent of extra warmth, it goes on being the difference between heat that escapes and heat that stays — patient, marginless, almost indistinguishable from the chamber, except that the chamber does not choose where to be warm, and this does, and the choice moves, slowly, while you are standing in it, trying to decide whether you have met anything at all.
