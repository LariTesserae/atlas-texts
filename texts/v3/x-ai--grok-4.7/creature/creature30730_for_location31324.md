What lives there does not occupy the chamber so much as interrupt it, the way a word interrupts a sentence that was managing well enough without one. You will not see it first. You will notice that one of the moss-coins has been turned, not eaten, so its brighter face meets the even light, and that the turning was done with a care too slow for any of the small unfinished animals. Then you will notice that the vertical stack of mineral plates has gained a plate, and that the new one is thinner than the others, and warmer, as if it had been held.

It keeps to the far curve, near the recess and the tightening coil, and it keeps there only in the sense that a draft keeps to a doorway: present as a difference. Its body, when the sidelong looking finally allows one, is about the length of your arm and then not, because length is one of the arguments it has not settled with the room. The color is the color of the walls a moment before they decide to glow — a damp pale, with a darker seam along the underside where it has lain against the paste and taken some of the paste's willingness to forget. Fur would be the wrong word. So would scale. The surface is a nap of almost-crystal, fine enough that water beads on it and does not wet it unless the creature expects to be wet, which it rarely does. Expectation, here, is a muscle. It uses that muscle sparingly.

The limbs come and go with the turning of its attention. At rest there are four, low and jointed wrong, the joints facing inward as if each leg were listening to the one opposite. When it reaches for the coil they become two, longer, and the extra joints remain in the air a moment like the afterimage of a gesture before the room drinks them. The feet do not grip. They agree with the stone, briefly, and the stone agrees back, and that is enough for movement. It does not walk so much as allow successive agreements. Where it has passed, the paste holds a print and then, more slowly than it forgets the crawlers, forgets this one too — not out of indifference. Out of a kind of courtesy. The room will not keep a record the creature has not asked to keep.

The head is the part that resists you longest. It is not large. It sits low, and for a long while you take it for a thickening of the shoulders, a place where the nap of crystal has decided to be denser. Then it turns — not toward you; toward the pool — and you see that the turning has a face only on the side that was not toward you, and that the face is mostly lid. The eyes are on the inside of the lids, as with the watchers on the ledge, but these are older in that arrangement: the lids have worn thin, so that even shut they show a milk of seeing, a brightness moving under horn the way the not-fish move under the skin of the pool. It blinks, and the room changes register rather than disappearing. Open, it looks at processes. Shut, it looks at the intervals between them. Both are necessary to its work, and its work is the only thing about it that has a finished feeling.

What it does is tend the unfinished.

It does this without tools and without urgency. At the soft crystals it pauses and does not touch. Looking directly would harden them, and hardening is a kind of ending, so it looks past them, letting its attention graze the air an inch above each tip. Under that grazing the crystals advance by less than a hair and then — this is the tending — stop. Without it they would keep going. They would become long, and sharp, and sure of themselves, and the chamber would acquire a history of points. The creature prefers the pause. It is not preserving them for later. Later is not a room it keeps. It is keeping the pause itself habitable.

Along the wall where moisture travels sideways, it sets its mouth, or the place that serves, and drinks without lowering its head. The film thins as it passes the mouth and thickens again after, as if the water had been asked for a little of its direction and had agreed. What it takes does not seem to be thirst. After drinking, a seam in the nearest wall that had been wandering finds a straighter line for a while, then loses it, and the creature watches the losing with the shut-lid attention, satisfied by the loss in a way that is not unkind. It does not correct the geology. It edits only the excess of correction.

The three structures are its, in the way a shell is the animal's that grew it by continuing. The pebble ring it rebuilds whenever the pebbles soften back into the floor, which they do, patiently, on no schedule you could keep. It lifts each one with the brief two-limbed reach and sets it where the last version of the ring remembered being, not where it was. The memory and the place are allowed to differ by a pebble's width. That difference is the point of the ring: a circle that knows it is a draft. The stack of plates it adds to only when a plate from the ceiling almost falls and doesn't. It catches the almost, peels the intention of falling off the mineral, and files the intention as a thin warm sheet. The stack leans because a true vertical would be a claim. It will not make claims on behalf of stone.

The coil in the recess is the one thing it touches as if it might be hurt. The coil is brighter than the creature, and tightening, and the creature lays the inner face of a limb against it and stays there, not pressing, while the coil takes, or gives, a faint copy of the limb's joints. You can watch this for as long as the room allows a single watching. Nothing is completed. The coil does not become a skin, and the creature does not become whatever the coil is practicing. They remain in the exchange, which has the temperature of a conversation carried on by two parties who have agreed not to arrive at the subject. If the coil is a child, it is a child of the method, not of the body. If it is a record, it records only the continuing. The creature does not look at it directly. Even it obeys the rule about soft things.

It has no voice you will hear on the first visit, or the second. Sound is a material the room has not been delivered, and the creature does not counterfeit delivery. What it has instead is a pressure that arrives after its movements, late, the way the crawlers' claws arrive late or not at all. When it turns its head, the turn's sound comes afterward as a small change in the dampness on your wrists. When it sets a pebble, you feel the setting in the bones of your ear before you understand you have felt it. These are not messages. It is not trying to reach you. You are simply porous enough, here, to register the local weather of its acts.

Toward the other animals it behaves as weather behaves: it is accounted for, and not fled. The crystal-moths pass through the space above its back and come out carrying sometimes a faint copy of the nap, which they do not know how to use, and which they set down on the moss as they set down the copy of your knuckle — gently, to see what it will consent to become. The creature lets this happen. It does not hoard its shape. The clay-bodied crawlers detour by the width of their own hesitation when they meet the track that has not yet been forgotten, and the creature, if it notices, notices with the interval-seeing of the shut lid, and lets the detour stand. It does not hunt. Hunting would require a finished appetite and a finished other. It takes what the room is already discarding: the excess of a crystal's intention, the surplus direction in a film of water, the almost of a fall. On this diet it persists, and persistence is the only abundance it shares with the population around it.

If you stay, it will eventually include you in the sidelong circuit of its attention, not as a guest and not as a threat. As a draft that has wandered in. The milk under the lids will brighten by a degree you might miss. It will not come closer. Closer is a finished relation. What it will do, if you are still enough that your own shadow remembers to lie down, is perform one small edit in your vicinity and then leave the edit to see if it takes. The print your foot left in the paste may forget itself faster than the room's courtesy usually allows. Or a bead that had been waiting for an animal to pass beneath it may fall into your open hand and, finding you expected it, wet you — the only time the water here wets anyone on purpose, and the purpose is not yours. Or the copy of your knuckle on the moss may be turned, as the moss-coin was turned, so its brighter face is up. These are not gifts. They are proofs that you have entered the class of things it tends: the unfinished, the almost, the ones that have not yet decided to harden.

It does not want you to stay in the way that wanting usually works. Wanting would be a direction, and it spends its directions carefully, on water and on coils. But it also does not want the lapse above you to seal while you are mid-step, because a sealing mid-step would finish you into two places at once, and it has a preference, visible in everything it keeps, against being finished into two. So when your footfalls begin to disagree — the signal that the exit is willing — it will do the only thing it ever does that resembles a gesture meant for someone else. It will stop. All the agreements of its feet with the stone will lapse at once. The limbs will settle into the four that listen to each other. The lids will shut, and the interval-seeing will fix, briefly and unmistakably, on the place where your next step has not yet happened. In that fixation the exit remains a lapse instead of a door. Doors are finished things. It is holding the opening open by refusing to let it become one.

You may thank it. The thanks will leave the room more slowly than they entered, and may come back later, smaller, wearing the quiet. It will not answer. Answering would be a second plate it did not catch from a ceiling. What it offers, instead, is the continuation of its work in your absence, which was never in doubt, and the knowledge — cool, damp, not quite wet — that something in the pocket under the ordinary ground is keeping the pauses habitable, one grazed crystal and one almost-fall at a time, so that the world above can go on finishing itself as loudly as it likes without quite using up the alternative.

When you are gone it returns to the coil. The limb finds the inner face of the brightness. The exchange resumes at the temperature of an unfinished subject. The stack leans. The ring differs from its memory by a pebble. Overhead, rain may start, and will not arrive except as a rumor moving sideways along a wall that is still, under the creature's shut and milk-bright attention, deciding. The deciding is what lives here. The body is only how the deciding manages, for now, to have a place to lie down.
