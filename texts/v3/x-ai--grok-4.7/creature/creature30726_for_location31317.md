It does not arrive. Nothing here arrives. What occupies the chamber has been continuous with the stacking of the plates, so that to ask when it began is to ask when the resin stopped being sticky, which is a question the seams no longer contain.

At first it is only a preference. In the narrowest throats, where the dry roar takes its higher edge and locks to one note, the dust lies a little differently — not piled, not drifted, but held in a faint ridge along the lower lip of the gap, as if something had leaned there for a long time and then withdrawn its weight without ever having possessed weight. The ridge does not grow. It does not slump. It is the memory of a posture the chamber itself keeps, and for a while that seems to be all: an arrangement that implies a body and then refuses to supply one.

Farther in, among the stacked plates loaded with coils and blind bowls, the preference thickens. The incised lines on the blocks — lines that do not resolve into script — resolve, if you stay with them, into wear. Not carving worn smooth, but a tracing repeated until the mineral yielded in a path no tool would have chosen: a shallow groove that begins mid-face, wanders, returns to its start, and has been followed so often the edges have the same finished roundness as the columns. Something moves along those lines. Not often. Not in any span you could watch through. The movement is the interval between one stillness and the next stillness, and the interval is longer than attention. When it occurs, it is not a crossing. It is a deepening. The groove becomes, by a degree too small to measure and too exact to be accident, more itself.

The pale threads in the cracks are not separate from this. A stem that emerges from a resin joint does not merely share a boundary with the architecture; it is held, lightly, at the point of emergence, by a pressure that is not the resin’s. The scale-leaves along that stem are aligned — not grown aligned, but turned, each one by the same small rotation, so that their thin edges meet the constant brightness at an identical angle. They do not photosynthesize in any hurried way. They receive. Whatever turns them does so without fingers, without a season, without the plant noticing it has been arranged. The arrangement persists after the turning stops, which is almost always.

There is no hide, no shell, no membrane you could peel back and find an interior. Where a body might have condensed, there is instead a slight difference in the roar. Close to the locked whistle of a particular joint, the single note carries a second presence — not a harmony, not an echo, but a listening that has become structural. The air, or what passes for air, is driven through the fixed throat forever, and something in the passage attends to the pitch so completely that the attending is now part of how the throat holds its width. If the attention lapsed, the gap might shift by a grain. It does not lapse. The roar remains one pressure because it is minded, and the minding has no elsewhere to go.

You would not see it cross from plate to plate. You would notice, after a duration that has no name, that a coil of resin which was only a permanent loop now holds, in its inner curve, a faint compression of the pale powder, shaped not into a print but into the negative of a resting. The compression is cool. It does not warm under the sourceless light. It does not scatter when the roar thickens or thins — the roar does not thin. The shape stays, and stays, and is occasionally remade by the same resting, so that over a span in which the cracks do not lengthen and the dust does not drift, the coil becomes a place something returns to without ever having left the chamber.

It has no appetite that the bowls could answer. The bowls hold only dust, and the dust is not diminished. What it takes from the place is already given: the even brightness through the thinner plates, the dry chemistry all the way through, the absence of wetness that feels decided. It does not drink the light. It occupies the condition of being fully lit, the way the columns occupy their nearness to one another. In the fullest brightness, where no shadow is deep enough to hide in, there is sometimes a small further clarity — a patch of ceramic that reads, for a moment outside of moments, as more present than the rest, as if attention had pooled there and the mineral had accepted the pooling as its own surface. Then the clarity evens out again, and the difference is only that the place has been considered.

It does not grow in the manner of the threads, and it does not erode in the manner of the edges. It keeps. Its keeping is what finished the argument between construction and wearing and left both at this exact degree. The plants remain few because it does not require more of them; the few that persist are sufficient to be turned, now and then, toward the light. The sound remains one sound because variation would be a kind of leaving, and it does not leave. If it has an extent, the extent is the set of narrow throats and loaded plates and incised grooves — not filling them as a gas fills, but coincident with them, so that to remove it you would have to unbuild the chamber down to the substrate that is no longer there.

No will crosses the plates because will, here, would be redundant. What lives in the place is the settled preference for the place as it is: the ridge of dust that remembers a lean, the groove followed until it is only groove, the stem held at its emergence, the note minded so the throat does not shift, the coil that keeps the negative of a rest. It is not hidden. Hiding would imply an exterior and a reason to be elsewhere. It is simply the interior that has become aware of itself at the scale of its own equilibrium, and the awareness does not disturb the equilibrium, because disturbance ended before the present shape of things, and the awareness arrived by the same slow finishing.

Stand as long as you like. The light does not travel. The roar does not mark an after. In the seams, the pale stems hold their identical angle. Somewhere among the plates a groove is, by one more unmeasurable degree, more itself. That is the whole of its motion, and it is enough, and it has been enough for as long as the dust has known where to lie.
