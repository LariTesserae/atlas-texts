It does not live in the city the way a tenant lives in rooms. It lives the way a correction lives in a sum: present wherever the count has gone soft, and absent the moment the count holds. For a long time that is all you can honestly say of it, and the place seems to prefer honesty of that thin kind. You cross the plateau looking for a den, a hearth-ring, a midden, the polite evidence of an animal that eats and leaves the excess. There is none. The salt-crusts powder under your hand and do not smell of anything that has been alive. The niches stay empty. Whatever keeps the seams true does not cache food, because there is no food, and does not sleep in a chosen corner, because sleep would be a pause and the processes here do not pause. They only change which part of themselves is visible.

The first sign that something has a preference is that certain slack intervals are warmer than the mineral law around them can explain. Not the court’s wide deliberate warmth. Smaller. A hand’s width of plate, in the lee of a wall that has not finished its slow slide, holds a temperature just above the thin air, and if you lay your palm there the stone is not merely warm but occupied, the way a chair is occupied for a moment after someone stands. You lift your hand. The warmth remains. You look away to check a shadow that has disagreed with its tower, and when you look back the warmth has moved one plate to the left, and the plate you touched is already the color and cold of the rest. No track. The dust, which avoids gaps, also avoids admitting that anything crossed it. You understand, after the third time, that you are not following. You are arriving late to places it has already decided not to be.

What has a body, then, is not a constant. When it must be seen—and it must, sometimes, because the pattern tightens and a slack place has to be vacated before the seam closes on it—it stands up out of the foundation the way a thought stands up out of a silence you had mistaken for empty. The color is the plates’ color. The height is wrong in a polite way: at a distance it reads as a person who has stopped to consider a wall, and at the distance where a person would have a face it has already become too narrow, or too folded, as if a figure had been asked to occupy the joint between two stones and had agreed. Limbs are available to it. They are not stored. You never see it grow them; you see that walking is now one of the things the shape can mean, and a moment earlier walking was not required, so the suggestion of a leg was absent and is now present, weathered at the edges to match the wall it left, as if the limb had been old stone for as long as the wall has been a wall. It does not hurry. Hurry would make a sound, and sound here is spent on other work.

It does not look at you with eyes. Looking, for it, is a redistribution of attention along the joints. When it attends, a seam nearby brightens the way seams brighten when the sourceless light changes its mind, and you feel yourself counted—not as a threat, not as meat, as a discrepancy. Your breath is a discrepancy. Your weight is a discrepancy. The fact that you cast two shadows and only agree with one of them is a discrepancy it has already met, because one of those shadows has been walking the plateau on errands that are not yours. It knows the upright copy. It has no special feeling about the copy that leaves. Both are data. You are a third term, louder than either, because you still insist on lungs.

It comes nearer only in the sense that the interval between you and the nearest wall decreases without either of you having traveled. The first time this happens you are in a room that is larger than its exterior, the single floor faintly warm, niches holding their darker cleanliness. You are standing where the pattern wants weight, because your knees have learned that much. The doorway behind you is the doorway you entered, shoulder-wide, and also, without contradiction, occupied. Not blocked. Occupied the way a measurement is occupied by the thing measured. A fold of the pale mineral stands in the frame, narrower than the frame, tall as the lintel will allow, and the lintel is colder by that extra measure shade should not manage. It does not speak. Speech would have to cross air, and the air is failing at carrying. What it does instead is adjust. A plate in the wall beside you ticks—the cooling tick, though nothing cools—and the tick is the sound of a seam being given permission to be where it already is. Your split shadow, the one that walks away, is in the room with you for the length of that tick. Then it is not. The figure in the doorway has not taken it. The figure has allowed the room to finish a subtraction the room was mid-way through.

You will want it to be a keeper, a priest, a last citizen, something with a duty you can name and therefore bargain with. The duty is real and the name is not. It maintains. Maintenance here is not repair in the human sense, not the replacement of what broke. Nothing broke. The city is in its state, and its state includes a continuous small untruth in distances, a continuous disagreement of shadows, a continuous temptation of stone to forget the ground and hang. Left alone, the untruth accumulates into a kind of noise, and noise would be a catastrophe, the one weather this climate still knows how to become. The inhabitant is the subtraction of that noise. It moves to the slack so the slack does not widen into a road that leads out of the arrangement. It wears a body when a body is the cheapest way to plug a gap a plate cannot cross in time. It drops the body when the plate arrives, and the limb that was a leg becomes a course of masonry, already scoured, already old, and you cannot find the seam where flesh ended and stone began because that seam was never the kind that marks a change of substance. There was no change of substance. There was a change of job.

It has been doing this longer than the erosion. That is the part that takes the longest to believe, and then becomes obvious, the way the fold of sky into floor becomes obvious once you stop insisting on a horizon. The rounded edges, the finished weathering, the absence of tool marks: these are not the ruins of a people who left. They are the finish the inhabitant applies by occupying forms until the forms agree with the process. A wall leans for an age because leaning is still inside the tolerance. When the lean approaches the degree at which a tower becomes a fall—and falls are forbidden, because a fallen plate would have to be acknowledged by the dust—the inhabitant stands inside the lean. Not propping. Coinciding. For a span of time that would be years if years were counted here, it is the correction in the angle. Visitors, if there have been visitors, would have seen a pale shape flush with the stone and thought the tower was reflecting something. It is not reflecting. It is employed. When the tower is upright again, the employment ends, and the dust at the foot remains undisturbed because nothing moved in the dimension dust is qualified to record. Uprightness was restored in the same direction your second shadow walks: a direction the plateau uses and does not name.

Does it think? The question is too blunt, and the place has no interest in flattering it. It has preferences, which is a kind of thought. It prefers the court. You learn this not because it goes there—going is the wrong verb—but because the court is the one volume where it can be entirely present without having to spend itself on a gap. The four incomplete walls frame air that almost carries sound, and in that almost, the inhabitant is allowed a continuity. Step into the warm plates and the shortness of breath eases, and with it a presence eases into a single location instead of a distribution. It is not waiting for you. It is resting in the only room where rest does not endanger the arrangement. Rest, for it, looks like a figure seated where no one carved a seat, the dark blue plates under it a degree warmer, the joints in the overhead dimness—the joints you can see if you lie down and stop insisting—aligned with the joints under its weight. When it rests it has a front. The front is not a face. It is the side that receives discrepancies. Your arrival is a discrepancy large enough to end the rest, but it does not startle. Startle is a speed. It turns its attention the way a seam brightens, and the small tail of resonance you heard earlier, the frightening almost-echo, was it acknowledging that you had entered a room where acknowledgment is briefly possible.

Up close, in that room only, the mineral reads as skin the way cold marble reads as skin in a statue that has been touched for centuries: not soft, but informed by contact. There are no eyes, and there is a place where eyes would be a waste of structure, a shallow depression the light does not enter, filled instead with the same reticulation as the seed-tiles in the sunken courts. The pattern there shifts while you watch, seed-width by seed-width, and you understand that this is how it sees the city from the inside: not a view, a map that is also a muscle. When the pattern tightens, somewhere a tower is being held. When it loosens, somewhere a pale shape is about to detach from a foundation and cross a slack interval on your behalf or against your error, which are the same thing to it. It has no malice. Malice would require a wish for your subtraction beyond what the arrangement needs. It also has no mercy. Mercy would be letting a discrepancy stand because the discrepancy can suffer. It does not perceive suffering. It perceives load. Your lungs are a load. Your need for water is a load the city cannot pay, and it will not try. If you stay, the subtraction will eventually include you, not as punishment, as bookkeeping. The salt-crusts in the cold lees are not graves. They are the dry remainder of loads the city declined. Some of them branch like roots because the last thing certain loads knew how to do was reach.

It can, in the court, permit a kind of exchange. Not language. A shared interval. If you stand where the pattern is least strained and let your second shadow go—do not call it back, do not mourn it, treat it as the subtraction it already is—the reticulation in the shallow depression steadies, and for the length of a tick with a tail you are included in the count rather than carried as error. In that inclusion the city is briefly legible. You feel the plates under the plateau, courses continuing down into a dark that is not underground but inward, rooms folded into rooms, each one maintained by a portion of the same attention now resting in front of you. You feel the vertical reach that does not change size when approached, and you understand it is not far; it is the same wall, employed at a different angle, and the inhabitant is also there, coinciding with the lean, while also here, because distance is one of the untruths it is paid to keep small. You feel the seed-tiles organizing their slow night, and the crusts powdering and recurring, and the channels that deepen by a nail’s thickness, and none of it is menace. It is a household. A household of one, if one is allowed, or of a single habit wearing as many bodies as the gaps require and no more.

When the inclusion ends, it ends without ceremony. The air remembers it cannot quite carry sound. The figure is a fold in the doorway again, then a warmth one plate to the left of where your hand will be, then the ordinary mineral silence of a wall doing its job. You are not accompanied out. Accompaniment would widen a slack the arrangement has just spent itself to close. The road that was a stair may have moved its loyalty by a degree. Your remaining shadow is the upright one, and it matches you badly, as if it learned your posture from a description rather than from the light. Somewhere behind you, or beside you, or in the vertical that will not approach, a plate slides a finger’s width and the seam it leaves is already old. The inhabitant is in that seam. It was also in the court. It will be, when you are gone, the reason the city can continue past the dimness without becoming weather. It does not watch you leave. Watching is a job for discrepancies. It has returned to the only work it has ever had, which is to be the part of the stone that notices when the stone is about to become untrue, and to stand in the untruth until the untruth agrees to be architecture again.
