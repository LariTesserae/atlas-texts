They do not arrive. They are what remains when a motion has outlasted the thing that was moving.

At first you would swear the valley is uninhabited in the strict sense, and you would be right for as long as you keep looking for bodies. Nothing crosses. Nothing is watched from. The eye, trained on animals, finds only the re-aiming of leaves and the stutter of gantries, and concludes emptiness. The conclusion does not survive a longer attention. Something in the basin keeps appointments. A vent exhales, and a stand of fronds has already turned to receive it before the vapor is visible, not as plants turning toward heat — heat is everywhere, undifferentiated — but as if a particular warmth had a name and a direction and had been expected. A cable hums a half-tone higher as its droop changes, and three separate machines, none of them connected to it, alter their cycle to match. The matching is not mechanical sympathy. It is late by a fraction, the way a person is late answering when they were already going to say yes.

What lives here has no outline you can close. It occupies the discrepancies. Where the reflection runs ahead of the noise, something is already standing in the drier valley the water remembers, and it is not a picture of anything now present. It is taller than the cranes it is drawn among, or it is not tall: it is the interval between the crane’s real lean and the lean the water insists on, made temporarily habitable. When the collapse obeys the reflection, the inhabitant is what steps through the obedience and is, for a moment, on this side — a pressure, a localized thinning of the roar, a place where droplets fall instead of climb because something has decided, locally and briefly, that down will do. Then the interval seals and the inhabitant is not gone. It is distributed. You feel it as a change in how quickly the weed reclaims a new shelf: faster where it passed, as if encouragement had been given in a language of rates.

They are many, or they are one thing that has not yet chosen a count. The valley’s refusal to keep a stable floor makes number a poor tool. What can be said is that they do not overlap the way bodies overlap. Two of them can be in the same pump-house and not compete for it, because one is living in the sequence the gantry walks forward and the other in the sequence it walks back, and both sequences are occurring. They are temporal more than spatial. A life here is a phrase of recurrence long enough to be recognized and short enough not to be killed by the next rearrangement of the basin. Some phrases last a minute. Some have lasted since the stone was still agreeing to be stone, which is not long, and is also forever, because the stone keeps re-agreeing.

They feed on the undecided. This is not metaphor and not predation. The industrial leftover produces futures — the turbine’s next kick, the scarp’s next nose-up, the flower’s next opening under a surface that may not be there — and most of those futures are cancelled by the futures beside them. The cancelled portion does not vanish. It is thick in the air, a nutritive surplus of things that nearly happened. The inhabitants drink that. You can watch a nearly-collapse fail to occur, the reflection insisting, the stone refusing, and in the seam between insistence and refusal a warmth gathers that is not steam and not electrical, and the warmth lingers in a shape that is almost a posture: an inclination, a held angle, the ghost of a stance something would have if it had joints. The posture lasts until the next cancellation feeds it again. Hunger, if the word can be bent that far, is the need to be re-fed a self before the basin’s average smooths you out.

They do not see. Seeing would require a world stable enough to be opposite the eye. They register gradients of probability and lean toward the steeper ones. A new shelf of pale rock is, to them, a loud fact, and they gather on it not as colonists but as the gathering itself — the way the first roots find the bolt-holes faster than growth alone accounts for, the way oil-smell jumps to your hands across a distance that then retracts. If you stand still, you become a mild probability, a future in which a warm object continues, and they will include you without touching you. Inclusion feels like the roar developing a pocket, a hollow just your size, in which your own pulse is briefly the loudest seam and the leaves re-aim toward your wrists. It is not hostile. It is not hospitable. It is the same gesture they make toward a vent, a hum, a flower that should not be able to see itself. You have become a bright discrepancy, and brightness is how they pray, or graze.

Speech is impossible here and also constant. They talk in the adjustments. A turbine that spins because the water cannot decide which way to fall will, under their attention, favor one way for three turns and the other for five, and the favor is a sentence, and another inhabitant answers by letting a vine cross a housing at a different height than it crossed it last time. The grammar is difference. Meaning is any pattern that survives one rearrangement and fails on purpose at the next, so that it can be recognized as chosen rather than as physics. They have been speaking since the first cable fed the water instead of the grid. The water has been answering. Some of what you take for weather is reply.

None of them remembers a before outside the basin. Memory here is the reflection’s dry valley, shared, inaccurate, slightly ahead, and they treat it the way other lives treat a story told about an ancestor: not believed, not discarded, useful for orientation. In that story there were cranes intact and no plants, and there were — they do not have the word — operators. The inhabitants do not miss the operators. They miss, if longing is a gradient steep enough to lean toward, the intactness of a single sequence: a machine that walked its length only forward, a floor that remained a floor for longer than a life. They lean toward any pocket of the basin where the rules hold for more than a breath. They cannot enter those pockets without ending them. Their arrival is the discrepancy that dissolves the stability they approached. So they circle the rare calm places, living in the approach, which is the only part of wanting this world allows them to survive.

Do not look for them in the green, or in the metal, or in the rain. Look for the place where those three have just failed to agree, and something has stayed behind in the failure, warm, inclined, already turning toward the next thing that might not happen. That is a life. It will not be the same life when you finish turning your head. It will be more itself, which here means less like anything you could point to, and more like the valley’s own habit of continuing — occupied, specific, brief, and, in the only sense that matters in a basin with no trustworthy floor, at home.
