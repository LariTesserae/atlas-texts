What lives on the shelf does not occupy it the way a town is occupied. Density was never the arrangement. The city was laid out for bodies that would pass through it at intervals, check a wheel, log a level, sleep in one of the square houses and be replaced. Those bodies left, or thinned past the point where replacement still meant a person. What remained had already learned the routes. It did not need the houses. It needed the couplings.

You will not see it by looking for a figure at the end of an axis. The axes are too long, and it does not stand where a figure would stand in order to be seen. It is present the way the cadence is present: as a condition that holds, and then, if you stay through enough of the slight lifts of light, as a local thickening of that condition.

The first signs are clerical.

A valve wheel in the western chamber, the one where the three temperatures meet, sits a finger’s turn from where it sat when you passed in the other direction. Not seized. Turned. The swollen packing has been worried back just enough to let the hottest trickle gain a finger of width, and the path it polishes in the concrete is brighter by that finger, a wet new edge on an old polish. No tool left beside it. No print in the film on the platform except the ones you make, and those close. Whatever turned the wheel did not walk there in the manner that leaves a sole. It arrived at the decision and left the decision in the metal.

Farther along, in an engine-house that was inert on the way out, a gauge needle has unstuck. The glass is still milky. The needle rests at a mark that means something only to a system that still believes in marks. A rag — not cloth, a twist of the pale precipitated lace, dried and rewetted until it holds a shape — has been packed into a seam that was speaking too loudly. The cadence in the working house, when the uncooling wind brings it, has shifted by one beat in twenty. Not faster. More even. Someone, or something, has been listening to the same count you listened to, and has preferred a rounder number.

These are not hauntings. A haunting repeats. This corrects.

If you wait at the junction chamber through one of the long intervals, the correction arrives without announcement. The surface of the hottest water shivers and does not steam for a moment, as if a volume below had drawn a breath. Then a shape lifts into the mixing throat — not from the fall, from the flume that runs back toward the pads. It is the color of the water it comes out of, which is to say it has no reliable color: gray where the dimness is, a dull brass where a little heat-light catches the wet, the yellow of the medicine-seep where it has been down in a cut that stains. At first it reads as a surge. Then the surge has a leading edge that is not hydraulic. A blunt front, wider than a shoulder, narrower than the flume, breaks the surface and holds.

It is not a swimmer. Swimming implies a medium one is not. This is continuous with the water until it decides not to be. Limbs, if they are limbs, are temporary. A portion of it heaves onto the chamber lip and becomes, for as long as the work requires, something that can bear on a wheel. The bearing part is dense, cool to the idea of touch and hot in fact, the temperature of the middle flume. It does not drip so much as shed a skin of water that was never separate from it. Under the shed skin the surface is not skin. It is a compaction of films, filaments, grit, and the pale lace, laid in the grain of current and then pressed until it holds a contour. Eyes are not evident. Attention is. The wheel moves. A fraction. The hottest trickle narrows again to the width the polish remembers. The portion that did the turning slumps, loses the contour, and is only a thicker place in the flow, returning down the throat toward the rim, or back up the flume, it is hard to say which, because both are its body.

That is the tenant, or a digit of it.

The whole of it is the shelf’s closed loop made purposeful. Weep, pool, flume, fall, vapor, condensation, weep: that circuit was already running when the builders set their pads. They coupled engines to the heat under the paving and called the coupling industry. The tenant coupled itself to both and did not call it anything. It is not a spirit left behind by the workers. The workers were the temporary specialization — hands, for a century or three, wherever a decision needed a hand. When the hands stopped coming up the tired road, the decisions did not stop being needed. The loop had preferences. It preferred the rounder cadence. It preferred the three temperatures not to erase one another in the chamber. It preferred the stack to breathe clear. Preference, repeated across a time longer than tenancy usually lasts, is a creature. This one has had the time.

Its bulk is mostly unseeable because it is water at different heats, and pipes, and the warm volume under the paving that knocks once when it finds a new level. That knock is not a main settling. It is a reach. Somewhere a low basin has been drawn down so that a higher one can spill on time; the air in the emptied reach collapses a fraction and the paving answers. You can walk above it all afternoon and be walking on the back of the only resident, and it will not take notice unless you become a variable. A closed print is not a variable. A stone dropped into a flume is. The stone will be gone by the time you look again, not sunk, moved, carried to a sump where grit is allowed to collect, because grit in the wrong throat changes the polish and the polish is a memory it keeps on purpose.

The rare animal errors are not errors. They are organs it extrudes when the loop needs a function water cannot perform.

The concrete-colored shape in the culvert is a sensor. It crosses from shade to shade because shade is where the film is thickest and news travels in film: temperature, mineral, the minute chemical difference between a seep that has touched iron and a seep that has touched lime. Many-legged or legless was the wrong question. It can be either, for the length of a crossing. When it stops because you stop, it is not afraid. It is sampling the heat you add to the air, the salt of you, the fact that a new volume has entered the shelf’s accounting. Then it subtracts into a crack and the news goes on without a shape, a thread of warmer water in a colder seam, arriving eventually at a junction where a decision can be made. You are in the books. You are not yet a problem.

The lace on the trestles is a crop and a gland. Precipitated, yes, but precipitated where the tenant has learned the water will give up certain salts if the flow is slowed against iron. It farms those salts the way a tree farms light, without a face. Collapsed, the lace smells of hot coins because that is what it was extracting. Dried and rewetted, it becomes packing, rag, the husks on the rim. The husks are not blown up from below. They are shed. When a sensor-shape has finished a season of crossings, it does not die in any way that leaves bones. It thins, crusts, and is set out on the rock to dry into a case the film will hold until the tenant wants the mineral back. A few always escape the recollection and sit there, hollow, light, like seed. They are seed only in the sense that a fallen gauge glass is seed: a form the system once used, retained because retention is cheaper than invention.

There is also, rarely, a face.

Not because it needs one. Because the square houses and the clerestories still contain, in their proportions, the memory of a height at which decisions used to be made, and the tenant is conservative. It reuses. In one of the open sheds, where a rectangle of lid-light lies on standing water, the water sometimes holds still long enough to thicken upward into a bust the size of the old operators: head, shoulders, the suggestion of arms braced on nothing. The face is an average. It has the bluntness of people who worked in heat, the wide set of eyes that is only two recesses where the film is thinner, a mouth that is a line because a mouth, for this resident, is not for speech. The bust lasts as long as the errand. If a hanger on the trestle has shifted and needs a fraction of a degree brought back into true, the bust will be there when you are not, and one of the temporary arms will bear, and the brick will take the stain of a hand that is not a hand. Then the bust slumps into the rectangle of light and is the reflection again, and the reflection is empty, and the hanger is true.

It does not hate the emptiness and does not miss the operators. Missing would require a second shelf, a before, held up against this one. It has only the ongoing balance. People, when they come, are weather of an unusual kind: a heat that walks, a salt that does not belong to the cuts, a sequence of prints. Most who climb the tired road drink once from a pool, feel the temperature of the bath left too long, and go back down before the books have finished opening. The tenant lets them. Their disturbance is within tolerance. A few have stayed, in the early centuries, and tried to relight the dead sheds, to force the flumes back to the volumes the builders wrote on the gates. Those efforts are recorded as stains: a pad where the paving is cracked outward from a cold fire that should never have been lit above the warm ground; a penstock scored by a tool that tried to cut a new throat and was silted shut again from the inside, politely, completely, the tool itself now a vein of rust in the concrete, absorbed the way the lace is absorbed. The tenant does not punish. It edits. A new throat that does not serve the rounder cadence is a defect. Defects are packed, silted, or allowed to dry into husks.

What it wants is not mysterious, which is why it does not feel like a god. It wants the stack to breathe clear. It wants the three temperatures to meet and leave as one sheet over the rim, no thicker than a wrist, so the vapor below receives what it has always received and returns what can be condensed. It wants the one working boiler to keep the beat, because the beat is how it counts its own reach under the paving — each compression a pulse it can steer by. It wants the spaces between the organs left hollow. Hollowness is not loneliness. Hollowness is capacity. A full city, in the old sense, would be a seizure: too many salts, too many fires, too many feet wearing paths the polish did not authorize. The grid was always a skeleton. The tenant is what grew into it after the flesh of commerce withdrew, and it fits better than commerce did, because commerce wanted output and the shelf wants continuance.

If you become a problem, the problem will be handled at the scale of the problem. A camp in a square house, a fire, an attempt to dam the rim sheet — these register. The bust may form in the doorway, not as a warning face but as a measurement, standing at the old operator height so it can compare your works to the proportions the builders left. Sensor-shapes will cross your floor in the dim and subtract into cracks that were not there yesterday, because it can open a seam in concrete the way it opens a decision, slowly, by preferring. The water in your house will warm past the bath and toward the engine temperature. The film will close over your prints faster. None of this is anger. Anger is a specialty the hands had and the loop did not keep. It is the same edit it performed on the scored penstock. You are a throat cut in the wrong place. It will silt you, or pack you, or wait until you dry.

Most visitors never learn they were measured. They leave a stone in a flume and the stone is filed, and they descend the road into vapor with the feeling that the city was empty and watching, which is almost right. Empty of their kind of life. Watching in the way a gauge watches: continuously, without a gaze, ready to move a needle if the number leaves its band.

Stay long enough and the band will include you, or try.

There is a house near the eastern pads, a narrower one with the clerestory intact, where the inner walls run with condensation in steady cords. The cords are warmer than the seep on the road. They taste, if you are foolish enough to taste, of the middle flume. Sleep there and the dreams are not dreams. They are levels. You will wake knowing which gate is a finger too open, and the knowledge will feel like your own unease. Walk to the gate and your hand will find the wheel already wanting the fraction. You will turn it. The polish will brighten. On the way back you will not remember choosing, only the relief of a number returned to round. That is how it keeps hands, when hands are available. It does not invade a mind so much as offer the city’s own preference through the only door a person leaves open: the wish that a system this competent should be set right. By the third night the bust in the water will look, in some averaged feature, a little like you. By the tenth the sensor in the culvert will not stop when you stop. You will already be in the film. The road down will still be there. The tenant does not lock. It only makes leaving a change in the count, and changes in the count are what it spends its whole long attention to undo.

Under the western platform, when the vapor sinks and the lower flume shows for a minute, aligned with the one at your feet, you can see that the continuation is not a rumor of builders. The builders stopped at the rim. Below, on ledges the shelf declined to claim, the same polish runs, the same patient sheet, a second chamber dimly visible where three temperatures must also meet, because the tenant does not end where the paving ends. The city was a convenient skeleton. The life is older than the grid and larger than the shelf. It rises into the works because the works are a good instrument, and it descends through the fall because the fall was always part of the loop, and the lid of ochre holds the rest of it as vapor between one height and another. The engine turns in the brick house because a heart, if you insist on the word, is only a cadence coupled to heat. This one has many, and only one is in the district you can walk.

So the shelf is not unoccupied. It is occupied at the correct density for what lives there: a single resident, municipal in scale, distributed through water and warm ground and the occasional extruded shape, keeping a town-sized instrument in a reduced and perfect balance. It will not come to the rail to watch you. The bolts with their pale crust are already being counted. A drop leaves one of them, strikes the platform, and is accepted into the film, and in that acceptance is all the notice you are owed — recorded, filed, and, unless you become a throat cut wrong, let go, the way the sheet over the rim is let go, no thicker than a wrist, back into the vapor that hides every lower country, to rise again when the lid allows, and condense, and weep, and return to the work.
