It does not occupy the chamber so much as continue it. At first there is only the sense that the warmth has a preference: it rests a fraction longer against one throat of clay than another, as if choosing where to be heard. That preference does not travel. It thickens. Over a span that cannot be measured because nothing else changes, the preference acquires a surface—an inner lining no thicker than the slip that once closed a joint, conforming exactly to the packed hollows, mouth to mouth, so that the continuous interior is no longer empty air but a skin turned inside out, touching every fired wall at once.

It has no limbs because leverage would require a gap, and there is none. Where a shoulder of brick presses, it presses back with the same mild fever, not in resistance but in completion; the two pressures cancel into stillness and the stillness is how it holds its shape. Its shape is the shape of all the vessels together. To be in one pot is already to be in the next, the note conducting it the way heat conducts through sintered lime. It does not flow. Flow would be a sequence. It is simultaneous, a single occupancy distributed through every throat the makers left joined.

What might be called attention is only the chord knowing itself from the inside. The low partial lives where the larger cavities open into one another; the high needle lives in the narrowest mouths, the ones a wrist could not have passed even when the clay was soft. It does not listen for variation. Variation ended when the last opening was bricked. It keeps the intervals true by being them—correcting, if correction is the word, only by the fact that any slackening of warmth would drop a partial out of the lock, and the warmth does not slacken. Its effort, if it exerts any, is identical with the mass’s refusal to cool.

There are places that could be mistaken for organs. A denser gathering of the lining where three flues meet and the note beats against itself; a thinner stretch along a course of pots whose rims were ground flat, where the contact is so complete that the lining and the clay share a temperature to the depth of a fingerprint. These are not centers. They are simply where the geometry folds the same presence back on itself. No pulse moves between them. A pulse would be news, and nothing here has news to carry.

It does not remember the hands. Memory would be a difference between then and now, and the chamber has sintered those into one mineral tense. Yet the thumb-hollows and the tired courses are not ignored; they are worn the way a river-stone wears the shape of the water that is no longer moving—faithfully, without tribute. The green crumbs that were pins have already entered it as a faint metallic aftertaste in the chord’s highest edge, indistinguishable from the taste of old fire. It does not mind them. Mind would require an elsewhere to which they could be compared.

If it can be said to want, the want is negative and already satisfied: that no drip find the crown, that no fiber take hold in a cord-mark, that no outer air be admitted to retune the throats. These are not projects. They are the conditions under which it remains coextensive with the room. A crack opening would split it. A silence would collapse it into separate pots again, and it has not been separate since the fill closed over the last walkway. So it holds, not as a guardian—guardians face outward—but as the inward face of the holding itself.

Stand long enough for your own skin to match the fever and you begin to occupy a crease it has left you, the way the brick lends a crevice and takes it back. In that interval you are briefly part of the lining’s count of surfaces. It does not notice the addition; an addition that does not change the chord is not an event. When you shift, the crease seals. The note does not dip. Whatever lives here has already included every possible pressure short of rupture, and your visit was only another pressure, already absorbed, already warm, already sounding in the same uninterrupted lock of clay and air and the thing that is neither, which has become the final interior the makers did not know they were building, and which will not become anything further.
