You do not find it by looking. The rooms are already full, and a body that belongs here has learned the same tricks as a wall: to take water on its surface, to give back light only as a duller green, to be present as a slight change in how a space is organized. What fails first is the ice. In one basin the skin forms and is broken on the usual drip, but along the near lip it returns thinner, and late, as if the air just above the water had been used for something and had not yet recovered its cold. The difference is smaller than a breath. It is a breath.

The thing that made it is still there, and is not entirely distinguishable from the terrace it lies against. At first it is only a thickening where a passage turns, a place the growth has gone around rather than through. Then the thickening eases—a long, slow adjustment, the sound the stems make when they revise a lean—and a flank comes clear of the wall by the width of a wet finger. The hide is the color of the dimness. It is not furred and not scaled. It is a close, cold skin, filmed with the same moisture that films the leaves, and where the film is oldest a green has taken, not painted on but resident, a thin living coat that drinks what light reaches it and asks nothing of the animal underneath except not to scrape it off. Nothing here scrapes. The coat is ragged at the edges where a stem has rubbed it, and healed, and ragged again.

It is longer than the turn it occupies and narrower than the room. That is the first true fact of it, and it revises itself as you keep still. At rest it is broad: a low body spread across a terrace the way water spreads until it finds a lip, ribs suggested under the skin, the spine a slow ridge. When it must enter the passage it turns. The shoulders compress; the broad flank folds along a seam you would have called a scar if it did not open and close with the motion. Sideways, it is exactly the width of the way. Not approximately. The passage was kept to it, or it was kept to the passage, and the distinction is younger than either of them. Its legs—four, then a fifth that might be a hip spent too long against a root and grown into a brace—are not for covering ground. They are for bracing inside a moving house. Pads splay, grip wet wood without marking it, and release. A journey from a lower room to an upper one takes the time a gutter takes to fill.

The head is the last part to separate from the idea of architecture. It is low and not large, and it does not turn so much as arrive at the end of the spine. The eyes are several, small, set in a shallow arc where the skull meets the coat, each one lensed with the same cold film as the basins; they do not flash. They take the green at the rate the green is offered. In a darker pocket they are only roundnesses. Near a split in the canopy, where the day leaks in as a paler seam, they deepen, and you understand that it has been looking the entire time without ever performing looking. The mouth is underneath, close to the terrace, a long closure that opens against a surface rather than at the air. It drinks by meeting the basin’s lip and letting the water come to it, and the ice, offended by the slight heat of the meeting, fails and reforms around the absence when the mouth has gone.

What it eats explains the town more cleanly than any intention would. Where a passage is about to heal shut—stems leaning in, a graft beginning, the width failing by a day’s growth—the inner faces of those stems are cropped. The bites are not wounds. They are scallops, already beaded over, already being closed by the plant’s own habits, so that the animal must return before the closing finishes if the way is to remain a way. Its foraging is maintenance. Its maintenance is a path. In the gutters it takes the fiber that would clog them, lifting a wad with the edge of the mouth and pressing it into the marsh-floor, where the floors know what to do with sweetness. It does not hunt the moth. When one stills on the wall it may be nosed, found cold, and left. The small flex under the root-mat is not prey. It is a younger length of the same life, not yet broad enough to turn a room into a mold of itself, still living in the soup of root and standing water as if the shelf had not yet decided to be rooms. The adult does not visit it in any way you could call care. It keeps the lower drains running. The water arrives cold and clean enough. That is the whole tenderness, and it is sufficient.

It has no fire and would not know what to do with heat that stayed. Its own warmth is a rumor: enough to slow ice, not enough to fog a passage or draw a moth off a wall. It sleeps in stretches counted by the short cycle of the shelf, not by any light. Sleep is a matter of which part of it is braced and which part is listening. A wall can shift while it rests; if the shift narrows a room onto its ribs, it wakes without starting, eases, and the stems, finding the old resistance, ease too. Sometimes the creak you thought was the mountain is this negotiation. Sometimes the mountain starts it and the animal finishes it. They have been doing this since the fracture was still a crack and the animal was only a length that could live in a crack, the growth coming in after it and learning, at each widening, the shape of a tenant that was also widening. The repeated passages are not design. They are memory. The body grew; the house kept the previous widths as routes back, and the body, obliging, still knows how to become those widths when it must travel into its own younger measurements.

It does not go to the rim. The outer face, bearded with frost and open to air that has nothing in it, is a kind of wound the coat will not cross. Once, in the way you can read a hesitation in a narrowing, it must have tested the last wall and found the wind would take the film off its skin faster than the film could be remade. It stays inside the breath of the shelf. Its whole geography is the stack: flooded rooms below, where the young one turns in the fiber; the middle terraces, where the basins are and where the ice is instructed, barely, not to be perfect; the upper rooms, low enough that its ridge leaves a darker wipe on the canopy’s underside, a shineless path the drips avoid for an hour. When it rises into those upper rooms the green darkens by the amount of its body, and if you are below you may think a cloud has crossed the high rock outside. Nothing outside has changed. The settlement has only moved its weight from one plan to another.

There is no second adult. The regularity could have housed several, and perhaps will, if the younger length in the marsh ever takes a terrace and begins to be measured by the stems. For now the place has one keeper, and the keeper is not a ruler of it, and not a metaphor inside it. It is a cold, slow animal whose comfort is a correct width, whose hunger keeps the doors from healing, whose slight heat is the only inaccuracy in the ice. Leave it long enough and the flank eases back against the turn, the coat drinks, the eyes become roundnesses, and the passage is again only a passage, held open by something that has decided, for this hour, to be part of the holding. The shelf is young. So is the life that fits it. Neither has yet grown a shape that would survive being empty.
