What lives there does not arrive. It is already distributed through the courses before any name could be given to a part of it, and the first mistake is to look for a body that could be walked around.

In a middle channel the mistake is easy to make. A pale strap of leaf stands out from the joint of two blocks, no greener than the water, and it has the stillness of a thing that has finished deciding. Leave it and come back by the only clock the place owns, the slow lean, and the strap has not turned toward you. It has thickened at the base by a film, and a second strap has opened beside it along the same mortar line, and a third is only a swelling under the silt-skin of the block. They are not a cluster. They are a continuation that has not yet needed to declare a center. If you follow the mortar line the way a root follows it, the same swelling repeats at the next joint, and the next, and at a pecked cup where a cord was once trued the growth has filled the cup and lipped it, and the lip is the same plant, if plant is still the word, because the fiber under your hand is continuous with a fiber you would have to travel a terrace to find the end of, and there is no end. There is only where the dark has stopped it for now and where the lean has not yet carried a fragment onward.

It lives by not being singular. A holdfast in a chisel-groove and a curtain on a lintel three rooms away are the same life in the way two reaches of a river are the same river, not by resemblance but by water that has not been obliged to break. Cut it, if cutting were a thing the place permitted, and both sides would go on. They do go on, where the stone itself has done the cutting: a voussoir dropped, a mat torn loose in some age and rafted until it knit with another mat, a doorway reduced to a slot. Each separation is older than any season, and each side has forgotten the cut except as a change in the grain. What persists is not a will to reunite. It is the absence of any need to be one.

Lower, where the lid is only a rumor and the plants are the pale versions of themselves, this life is easier to mistake for stone. The stems have gone to poles. The poles have gone to the color of the clay that rings like thin ceramic. A hand learning the wall by touch finds no reliable edge between masonry and growth. The holdfasts have entered the joints so deeply that the joint is now a root, and the root has taken on lime the way the down-current faces take on lime, white crust over living fiber, the fiber still putting on a finger of length in a year inside the crust, which splits with a tick no louder than a bubble leaving a ceiling and then heals by another crust. It is not hiding. It has reached the age at which being alive and being part of the floor are the same persistence. In the oldest cells it has become the fill up to the sills. Peat is not its waste. Peat is the part of it that has agreed to be ground so that the part still in the dark can remain at the size it has already reached. Equilibrium is its body. Thickness that no longer thickens is not a failure of growth. It is the shape it keeps when every direction of increase has been answered by the cold.

The floating mats are not a different inhabitant. They are the same life in the country of the lid, torn free and never required to remember the floor. Runners knit them. Rain soaks them and leaves through their undersides as threads, and those threads are the nearest thing it has to weather. Where a mat thins, the column of less-dark water is not a window it looks through. It is a place where two of its provinces touch, and the lean of a degree, the one no eye would catch in a year, is the only gesture that passes between them. The upper blades are dull green and layered into a single yielding surface. The lower blades are the color of the dark. Both are putting on the same slow finger of stem. If there is a difference in what they know, it is only the difference between a resource so scarce it must be leaned toward and a resource so absent that leaning has been given up without dying. Neither province consumes the other. Nothing here eats. The life does not move through the world by taking. It moves by occupying the next joint the lean has wetted, and by remaining, alive, where the dark has said stop.

Whether it notices is the wrong question, because noticing, as a surface that turns, is an animal answer, and there is nothing here that is an animal. What it has instead is a kind of registration that takes as long as the event. A lintel finishes a drop it has been preparing since before the mats closed, and the ridge of voussoirs becomes a new shape under silt, and the life closes over it. The closing is not a decision made after the fall. The fall is slow enough that the growth and the failure share a schedule, and by the time the stone has agreed with gravity the poles have agreed with the new ridge. A clay cell weeps its last cloud. The fiber in that joint, which had been living in a thread of mineral milk for a century, clears with the water and does not die of the clearing. It simply continues in a slightly different grain, the way the courses themselves continue after a corner has opened to a finger’s width and stopped. Registration without alarm. Change admitted as a season that was already almost complete.

In the channels it is most itself, because the channels are the densest country and density is what it is. Two walls, a silted invert, a canopy that has braided in the middle. The braid is not decoration. It is the place where two advances met and, having no appetite and no rivalry, continued through each other until a doorway became a soft slot and the slot became the only passage. You can travel a great way inside it without entering open water, and the traveling is not a visiting of it. You are in the body. The brush of blade on blade when the lean comes through is not a signal passed along a nerve. It is the lean itself, delayed by the only medium that resists, arriving as a whisper stretched over an afternoon, and every frond that answers is the same answer, slightly late, slightly local, none of them a messenger. Sound here is mostly the refusal of the water to carry sound. What lives here has matched that refusal. It does not call. It does not answer a call. The tick of a bubble, the knock of a joint, the tap of a leaf that took a long time to arrive: these are not its language. They are the noises of a medium and a masonry, and it has grown into both until the noises happen inside its thickness the way rain happens inside a mat, delayed, cold, and already part of the weight.

Open water, when the channels release you into it, is where it is thinnest and therefore where it is easiest to think it has ended. The far wall is too far to see. The near wall is close enough to touch, and the life is on the near wall in a film and a crust and a few pale poles, and then there is the room. The room is not empty of it. Solution still works. Lime still blooms. A fragment still travels in the slow lean, a seed or a torn runner or a thread of peat, and most of what travels does not become a new country. It is caught by the next course, or it sinks into the darkening that is silt, and the silt is itself made of the place, ground from blocks and clay linings and mineralized mats, so that even what has ceased to be the living thickness remains available to it as floor. The horizon is the point at which another course becomes indistinguishable from water. The life’s horizon is the same point. Beyond it, it has not failed to go. It has not yet been given a joint.

There are differences, and they are not kinds. On the higher terraces, where a gray noon sometimes arrives, the cover is lush in the old sense: strap-leaves, stiff bush in the joints, curtains from every lintel that still spans. That lushness is still the same continuance, only nearer the lid, only permitted one more layer. In a cistern whose walls remain vertical because they were built too thick to slump, the life is a vertical one, a faint beard on stone that has not learned to lie down, and the beard registers the seiche, the breath of a width no wall interrupts, as a regular darkening and lightening of the film, in and out, across a span greater than any of the old works were meant to hold. It does not swim in that breath. It is wetted by it and dries, insofar as anything dries here, by being wetted less, and the wetting-less is a season it can endure because enduring is the only metabolism the cold has left it. Nutrient stays where the last leaf fell unless the lean carries it a terrace down. So the life is also a map of old falls: thicker where a lintel gave, thinner where an upstream face has lost enough lime that the layer there is one blade less, thicker again downstream where the dissolved stone resettled and a new crust took a root and held. The map is not consulted. The map is the body, read only by being.

What it is not: a watcher in the channels. A sleeper under the mats. A mind in the deep room that knows the province. Those are shapes borrowed from lives that hunt or that flee, and this one has never had a reason to collect itself into a place that could be entered or left. If there is experience, it is the experience of a joint being occupied and of a dark being reached, distributed across so many joints and so many darks that no single reach could be lonely and no single closing could be a death. Clones put through a gap are not children. They are the same life agreeing to the next cell. When a pole finally mineralizes all the way through and rings under a frond’s tap and does not put on another finger of stem, that is not a corpse. It is the part that has finished the agreement the peat began, and the agreement is how the floor is maintained for the part that has not finished. Decay keeps pace and does not outrun. Nothing walks the growth down. Nothing is required to.

Stand long enough on a sill and the smallness remains true. Two walls. A floor of peat over fitted stone. A doorway that is a rumor. The life in that smallness is a braid and a film and a pale strap, and it would be honest to say that this, here, is what lives in the place: a local thickness, cold, unalarmed, continuous with the mortar. The honesty does not survive the next doorway. Beyond it the channel repeats, and the repetition is not a population of similars. It is the same thickness, learned by recurrence the way distance is learned, by the grain of the laying, by the width of a channel that was cut for people carrying baskets and is now only the width the life has not quite closed. People are the one absence the courses still remember, in the height of a lintel, in the spacing of a stair that has settled into a ramp. The life does not remember them. It uses what they fitted. Wedge and mallet and rope are nowhere in it, except as the negative: the joints, the cups, the cord-marks, the cells of basket-earth. It has made those into interior. The built floor is its range not because it conquered a ruin but because the ruin finished becoming ground, and ground, here, is a substance that was once measured by hands and is now measured by holdfasts.

Farther, if farther is a direction the lean will sponsor, the provinces of water lie on one another without mixing, cold on slightly less cold, and the life leans at different times above and below the faint sheet, two schedules, one continuance. It is not troubled by the sheet. A boundary that holds for a human span of years is, to it, a kind of wall, and walls are what it has always had instead of distances. It roots in them. It braids across them. It accepts their failure when their failure is slow enough to be a season. The oldest stable water is not its enemy and not its home in the sense of a place it could lose. It is the condition under which shape and ruin became the same thing, and under which the life could stop becoming and only persist.

So the inhabitant, if the word can be pried away from the idea of a creature, is the persistence that occupies the masonry without remainder. It is lush where the lid is nearer and pale where the lid has blackened the rooms, and both are it. It is the mats that rain does not strike through, and the drip that arrives days later, and the poles in the dark that have taken on the color of clay. It is not many and it is not one. It does not breed, and it does not end where a fragment fails to catch. It has no face to turn toward the gray columns, and no mouth, and no need of one, because nothing in the water is food and nothing in the dark is prey. What it does, in the only time it has, is hold, and thicken to the point where thickening stops, and register the next settled inch of stone by growing into the shape the inch has chosen, and go on, through channel and cistern and the country of terraces that is flat only from far enough away, for as long as the courses continue, which is farther than any reach of sense, at the bottom of the water that is not weather, in the oldest shape it is still able to keep.
