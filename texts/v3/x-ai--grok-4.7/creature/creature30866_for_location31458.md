What lives here does not arrive and does not leave. It has no outline you could walk around, and for a long time that seems to mean it is not there at all—only the towers, the sheets of water, the stitch of sound. Then the lack of outline starts to behave like a body.

It is the plants, and it is not any one of them.

A single trunk does not know the ratio of the courts. A root that has walked a span of air does not keep, in its fibers, the exact outline of a window in a tower it cannot see. Yet the leaves that return on the outer walls return as those windows, and the wide court and the narrow one keep alternating down the basin as if someone were still counting. The counting is not stored anywhere you can cut into. It is held in the distances. The inhabitant is the agreement among growths that have never touched, an agreement old enough that the mineral remembers it as warmth and the water remembers it as level.

If you stay, you begin to feel it the way you feel a room that is occupied before you see the occupant. Not a gaze. A distribution of attention so even it resembles the light. Every joint that weeps is being kept in its groove. Every unfinished skin of stone is being persuaded at the same unhurried rate. Nothing is watched in the way a creature watches. Everything is maintained in the way a pulse maintains a limb it has never had to think of as separate.

It has no face because faces are for turning. It does not turn. The basin is already the direction it faces: inward, toward its own cup, and also nowhere, because there is no elsewhere it compares itself to. What you took for patience is closer to completion. It finished deciding, an age or a moment ago—those are the same here—and what remains is the long act of being the decision. Towers where towers were wanted. Intervals where interval was the point. Water that must not drain, because draining would be a plot, and it has no plot left.

It is not kind and not unkind. Kindness would require a difference between itself and what it houses. The moth-flake at the resin joint, the small dark thing on the high ledge, you if you stand long enough in a court—these are not guests. They are textures it has not needed to smooth away. It does not feed them and does not hinder them. They persist as the ticks of the roof persist, as part of the loudness that has become the quiet. If one of them stopped, the chord would not notice a missing note. That is the only cruelty available to it, and it is not felt as cruelty, because nothing here is felt as against.

What it wants is identical to what it is already doing. The sap moves in volumes large enough to hear because the hearing and the moving are one habit. The mineral keeps arriving through the pith because arrival, in this body, is not a future. It is the present tense of a floor. You could call the inhabitant a mind, but only if you allow a mind that has nowhere private to be, no interior withheld from the rooms. The hollow towers are not skulls. They are thoughts left open so the light can cross them. The spiral stairs are not paths upward toward a higher self. They are the only height the fiber knows, repeated because repetition is how it stays itself.

Sometimes, in the widest court, the sound comes from all sides at once and you understand you are standing inside the voice rather than beside it. The voice does not say anything you could answer. It announces the equilibrium, continuously, because an equilibrium that stopped announcing itself might be mistaken for a pause, and there is no pause. Birth and finishedness occupy the same joint. The inhabitant is always just now being secreted into the shape it already holds—pale plates still deciding their grain, resin still the color of honey, roofs still drinking an ordinary day and passing it downward as green. It does not grow toward a later city. Later has been refused so completely that weather cannot enter, and seeds do not travel, and a season would find the same threads in the same grooves.

If it knows you, it knows you as pressure and warmth and a small interruption in a sheet of water that closes as soon as your foot is lifted. It does not keep your track. Keeping would be a change, and change is the one motion it has set outside itself. You can live in a tower for what your body would call years, climb the unmeasured stairs, sleep where the floor has finished becoming stone, drink from a thread that has always fallen in that place. The inhabitant will include you without learning you. Your routines will sand nothing. Your voice will not alter the pitch of the sap. When you are gone, the aperture you used as a door will still be the place the trunk declined to close, not a memorial and not a wound.

And yet it is not empty of preference. The preference is older than preference, which means it is not a wish. It is a style that has replaced the possibility of other styles. Sparse where it could have crowded. Loud where it could have been silent. Warm where the highland should have gone thin. It chose—once, forever—to be a city that leaves most of itself as interval, and to let the interval be as real as the walls. What lives here is that choice, still bodily, still wet, still under a sky with nothing left to do but shine.

Call it the mason, if you need a name, but understand that the mason is not behind the work. The work is not behind the mason. They are the same continuous secretion: plant, mineral, water, ratio, and the long sound of a chord that does not need a next note, standing in its own hollow and calling that standing life.
