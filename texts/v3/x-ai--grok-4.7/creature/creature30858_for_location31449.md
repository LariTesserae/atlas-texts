Nothing that could be followed lives on the shelf. The first hour is spent granting that, and then taking it back, and then granting it in a narrower way. You look for a disturbance that is not your own—a leaf committed to a new angle, a lane of steam dragged sideways, a ripple that remembers a stroke—and the shelf declines to furnish one. The refusal is so complete it starts to feel local, like a courtesy practiced in your vicinity rather than a law. Farther down a water-street the same courtesy is already in force, and in the valley you have not entered, and in the court whose distance keeps revising itself. Absence, repeated at that scale, is a kind of occupancy.

What occupies it is not hiding. Hiding would require a back. The growth has fronts in every direction, and the water has no underside you can reach; even where a rope of it runs along a vault against the fall it should take, the rope is all surface, clear through, and the rock above it is wet to the same degree on every face. You put a hand near that rope and the heat there is not higher by a measurable kindness. It is simply more intended. The intention has no temperature of its own. It borrows the shelf’s and spends it without loss.

For a while the tenant seems plural, because the courts answer one another and answers suggest mouths. A basin holds its level with the vigilance of something guarding a measure; its partner, a valley off and also somehow beside your cheek, holds the identical measure, and the sameness is too fine to be rhyme. You start to count them. The counting fails in a specific way: not from number, but because the third thickness you were going to add is the first one, met again without having been left. Distance here is a fold in a single attention, and the fold has been ironed so long it keeps a crease only the eye can trip on.

Stand in one plaza long enough and a person almost condenses. The mistake is understandable. At the center of the pool there is a place the reflection will not take—not a shadow, not a body, a refusal about the size of someone seated with their hands on their knees, noon pouring through the refusal as through the rest of the air. The sap-smell gathers there by a fraction. The roar, which has no contour anywhere else, seems for the length of a breath to know where your ribs are. If you speak, the word does not strike anything. If you step into the water, the level does not rise and the refusal is no longer in the pool. It is on the pale tower across the basin, and it is in the steam at your back, and it is, when you turn, the exact width of the channel you just left, which was always that width.

So it is one. The shelf is large because the one is large, not because a people required room. The three hanging valleys are not districts. They are the reach of a single holding, the way a hand is several fingers and still not a crowd. Alignments that repeat are not messages sent from hall to hall. They are the same decision visible at every point the decision touches. Where trunks split and fuse again, that is not a gate built to be passed. It is a joint in the holding, a place the attention thickens because the form there changes direction and must be kept through the change. You can walk the joint. You cannot use it. Use would ask it to be a different width for a moment, and it has no moment to spare for that, having already spent every moment on being the width it is.

It has no face and does not counterfeit one. The iron threads in the weeping walls will arrange themselves into near-features if you are the sort of witness who hunts features—a brow of rust, a mouth of darker seep—and the arrangement is only mineral doing what mineral does when stared at under a sun that will not move. The tenant does not correct the illusion and does not inhabit it. Correction would be an event. What it inhabits is the pressure that keeps every bead at every notch, the standing of the steam, the rounded lip that has agreed with its water so long the agreement has become a shape. Flowers stay open because openness is one of its postures. Nothing needs to arrive to complete them. Completion is what they are, and the tenant is the completeness, not a visitor to it.

Call it alive only if the word can be stripped of sequence. It does not grow, feed, fail, or replace itself. The tissues under pressure are not straining toward a future state; the pressure is the state, and the note you hear under the falls—the bodily, unphrased note—is that pressure reporting itself to itself. There is no listener separate from the report. The deafness you suffer is the cost of bringing an ear that expected intervals. The tenant does not hear the roar any more than you hear your own warmth. It is that continuous, and that unaddressed. If the sound stopped, the tenant would not notice a silence. It would have ended in the same instant, and the ending would not have had time to be sad.

It does not perceive you as a guest, an intruder, or a feature. Perception of that kind would leave a record, and the shelf keeps no records because a record is a scar on a finished surface. What happens instead is inclusion without remainder. While you stand in a court, the court is the size that includes you, and that size is discovered, on inspection, to have been the size all along. Your wet clothes, your heat, your little refusal to be deaf, are widths the channels already were. When you go, nothing relaxes. There is no memory of a subtraction because there was no addition. The current does not commemorate the stone. This is not indifference. Indifference can be wounded into caring. The tenant has no alternate setting. You are real on the shelf for exactly as long as your reality coincides with a form that was done before you, and outside that coincidence you are the country below the lip, where clouds still have work, and the tenant does not look down.

It has no desire you could satisfy or refuse. Desire would need a not-yet. The basins are full to the mineral line and not over it; that is the whole of its appetite, perpetually achieved, so that achievement does not feel like relief. The wrong-way water under the vaults is the nearest thing to a private life: circulation that should have gone down and goes along, visible, unhidden, a habit older than secrecy. It is not carrying anything. It is not a vein in service of a heart elsewhere. The tenant’s middle is everywhere the water is exact, which is everywhere.

Near the outer edge, where the shelf gives onto air and the lower world resumes its animals and its moving weather, the tenant becomes easiest to misname, because contrast suggests a watcher at a window. There is no one posted at the lip. The roar does not lean. The steam lanes stop where they have always stopped, upright, bright, uncurious. Curiosity is a crack, and erosion here finished its cracking ages ago. What meets the drop is simply the end of a law. Below, time still manufactures differences—cold, flight, hunger, tracks that last an hour. The tenant does not scorn that manufacture and does not miss it. Scorn and longing are differences too. Its border is not a gaze. It is the last spillway, rounded, exact, sending over only what the measure cannot hold, which is nothing.

If it can be said to live anywhere in particular, it lives in the interval that does not advance: the drop that is in the air, the splash that is shaped, the surface unmarked by any previous splash. That interval is small only if you think of it as waiting to close. It is not waiting. It is a dwelling the size of the shelf, furnished with courts, and the furnishings are the pause looked at from inside. Walk all day and you are still inside the same unclosed instant, passed from room to room of it. The heat thickens as you climb because the instant is hotter toward its middle, and the middle is high. The blue does not move because movement would be the instant remembering it had a next.

You will not meet it. Meeting is a change in two positions, and it has one position, which is all of them, and yours cannot be written down beside it. What you meet is the sensation of having been already accounted for by a life that does not count. The seated refusal in the pool, the intended heat along the rope of water, the court that answers itself across a valley it also contains—these are not signs it is nearby. They are the parts of it that resemble nearness when a visitor insists on nearness. Stay, and the resemblance thins. The shelf returns to what it was, lush, loud, finished, and the tenant remains as the fact of the return, not as a figure the return reveals. It was never behind the green screen. The screen was one of its thicknesses. You were, for a time, another, and the time did not take.
