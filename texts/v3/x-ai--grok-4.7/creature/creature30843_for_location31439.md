What lives here does not arrive and does not depart. It thickens.

At the rim the lives are still almost countable. A shoot stands in a crack of the young stone, hollow already, no taller than a knee, its single node pale and wet. It is a resident in the only sense the basin allows: a volume of intention that has found a fracture and begun to spend the water pooled there. By the time the sun has moved a hand’s breadth it has siblings. They do not grow toward one another so much as remember, through the film of damp on the mineral, that they were never separate. The note they make when the wind finds them is thin, a rehearsal. They are streets that have not yet agreed to carry anything but their own lengthening. If you watch long enough one of them folds—not wilting, deciding—and the fold becomes the first rib of a roof over a pocket that did not exist until the fold required it. The life is the requirement. It does not wait to be inhabited; it is the inhabiting.

Deeper, the count fails and the lives become districts of themselves.

The reed quarter is a standing crowd so dense that individual culms can be distinguished only by pitch. Each is a pipe and a person and a pipe again, drawing, ticking, forcing sap at a pressure that would split animal hose. They do not turn their faces because they have no faces to turn; the whole rank leans into the light by adding cells on the bright side faster than on the dark, so that over an hour a lane that was straight acquires a bias, and the bias is a decision the crowd has made without a vote. When a rank folds inward to roof a darker pool, the ones that become ribs do not die into structure. They continue. You can lay a hand on a load-bearing curve and feel the same warm peristalsis that runs in the freestanding pipes beside it. The bent ones sing a lower note. The standing ones answer. The life here is a parliament of frequencies that keeps the lanes from sealing by the simple fact of its noise. Stop the noise—if anything could—and the crowd would grow shut in a day, a single solid organ with no interior. They live by refusing that.

In the fig quarter the lives have rooms. A buttress is not a wall that something else occupies; the buttress is the occupant, its interior hollows the chambers it keeps for the storage organs it continuously ripens and reabsorbs. Rows of pale swellings sit on living shelves. They are not property. They are the shelf’s way of remembering a shape it once held and might hold differently after the next pulse of reclamation. A chamber left as a gallery is, on any return, likely to have eaten its own door and become a corridor, the gourds drunk back into the plan, the plan already wrong. These lives revise their houses the way other lives revise their minds, except there is no tenant left behind when the room is abolished. The tenant was the room. What persists is the habit of arrangement: the tendency, stored in the cambium, to set swellings in rows, to shutter an opening with a leaf that closes on a chemical cue, to keep a floor clear by allelopathy so that the clearness itself can be called a street. They are civic without citizens. Organization is their metabolism. A fallen span does not become debris for something else to nest in; the span is reabsorbed by the same life that dropped it, veins emptied, lace dissolved, the space reassigned before the light has changed. Scavenging has been internalized. There is no second creature to wait for the first to finish dying, because dying, here, is only a change of address within the same body.

The black quarter lives hotter and louder, and its lives are less willing to be called houses. Dense, pigmented, almost woody, they run pressures that steam the lanes between them. You hear the baffles. A seam fails—bright jet, crack racing upward—and before the wall can finish becoming wreckage the neighbor has thrown a pale brace across the gap, screaming in its fibers, taking the load. These are not separate organisms cooperating. The failure and the brace are two gestures of one turbulence, creation and destruction occupying the same moment so completely that the event is only the handoff. They live at the edge of their own collapse and spend themselves to remain. A life that cannot fall cannot continue; so they crack, and the crack is answered in advance by growth that has already heard it. The roar is not a byproduct. It is how they know where they still are.

None of them move as animals move. They advance. A meristem is a front. At the unbuilt edge the fronts are knee-high and sharp; inside the mass they are the soft pale mouths of walls that close a plaza while you stand in it, or the root-hairs that cross a split vault not to restore the old shape but to propose a different one. What would have been walking is, here, a differential in the rate of cell division. What would have been reaching is a lateral that roots in a tower you have already passed, which is also still ahead. They occupy their places by becoming them, and the places do not hold still long enough to be owned. A bridge of lianas throws a shadow slightly early because the leaf that will cast it is still rising; the life has committed to the shade before the body of the shade exists. Their memory is structural and severable. Cut a fragment free—if cutting were possible without the cut being drunk—and the fragment will still try to finish the sentence it was mid-way through: a corridor, a note, a shelf of swellings. The sentence may no longer fit anything around it. It finishes anyway.

They do not see. They register light as a sugar gradient and a direction to thicken. They do not hear, yet the basin is organized by sound because sound is what their hollow bodies cannot help but make, and the making keeps certain frequencies of space from being filled. A zone of canceling notes is a pocket of lesser roar where, for as long as two towers agree about their heights, a small standing calm exists. It is the closest thing they have to a room set aside. When one tower begins to rise the calm tears. No one mourns it. The spray from the split seam carries tiny wrong reflections—rim, plaza, a corridor not yet walked—and the drops are taken up so quickly they might never have fallen. Information moves as sugar, as bitterness, as pressure, as the gold-green smoke of reproductive dust that no legs will carry, so the wind, amplified by the pipes, must. Pollination is a weather they generate. Defense is a chemistry exuded at the boundary of a lane. There is no mouth. There is no wound that stays a wound. A yellowing leaf is already being emptied by the life that owns it; the emptying is the eating.

At the scale of the valley they are one life, a clonal argument conducting itself in clear daylight, turbulent the way a rapid is turbulent, unfit to be mapped because mapping requires a direction held still for the length of a sentence. At the scale of a node they are innumerable, each culm a difference that can be recruited or reabsorbed, each one warm, damp, sharp-edged, recent. They do not know the observer. The damp finds skin and does not dry; the chemistry that keeps a floor from sealing does not distinguish a foreign surface from a rival meristem. Nothing with blood persists because persistence here is a matter of being metabolized into the plan or expelled as vapor and condensate. There is no malice in it and no hospitality. A particle in the circulation is either taken up or carried to a pocket and left until the pocket itself is revised.

On the far side a new quarter stands up out of the raw stone all at once, pale, steaming, already sounding its first note. It is too far to be sudden and it is sudden. The note finds a harmony with a culm that has stood all day, and the culm answers by thickening, by becoming the beginning of a wall where a way through used to be. The way through becomes the wall’s memory of a way through—a paler scar, sharp, not yet healed, already singing shut.

What lives in the basin is the singing shut, and the opening that precedes it, and the pressure that keeps both from ever quite finishing. It does not have a name it can be called by from outside. Inside, it is only the next cell, the next brace, the next note that must be answered before the lane can close.
