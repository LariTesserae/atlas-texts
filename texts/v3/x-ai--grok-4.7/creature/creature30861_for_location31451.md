Nothing crosses the fetches. The survey is right about that, and it stays right even after you have been inside what lives here. There is no animal to have been missed—no body hauled on a current, no mouth, no trail of waste, no reflex that flees the light. The veils are not its food and not its gardens. If you wait for a shape to detach from the green distance and become a creature, you will wait out the weather and leave with the same inventory: stone still deciding, a few patient anchors, water that spends its own weight.

What lives here does not detach.

You meet it as a mistake in the listening. The sea prefers certain notes and then is retuned by the next front; that preference is weather, and it dies when the front arrives. Somewhere in the long empty—never at a landmark, because landmarks are younger than the space between them—a chord arrives and is not released on the sea’s schedule. It is held. Not long. A fraction of a phrase, the way a hand keeps a glass an instant after the toast is done. Then it is returned, and one partial is missing, or one partial has been added that the collapse of the shelf did not put there. The roar does not stop. The edit is small enough to lose if you are counting ridges. It is not small if you are counting intentions. Weather does not correct itself toward a note that no longer has a source.

That is the first edge, and it is not an edge. There is no skin. Temperature does not change. The light does not bend around a hull. You are in ordinary water, cool, loud, uncrushing, and then you are in water that has already heard you. A sound you bring—the click of a stone in a pocket, the dull knock of a knee on nothing, the small perishable noise of a breath if you are the sort of visitor who still spends air—comes back retuned, with the shape of the noise preserved and the sea’s chord folded through it. Not an echo. An answer that did not need a throat. The place has edited you into its phrase and handed the phrase back so you will know the phrase was occupied.

It has extent, once you consent to stop looking for a silhouette. A locus, not a body: a region where the constitutional exception is more decided than it is elsewhere. The term that spends pressure and keeps the highland from pouring off its own rim is everywhere, evenly, like a climate. Here it has a knot in it. The knot does not displace water so much as inherit the medium’s refusal and spend that refusal on a point of view. You can cross it in an afternoon if you are fast and the eye of a storm is helping you, or you can be inside it for days if you drift. Leaving is not a door. The answers thin. Your noises return as noises. The chord goes back to belonging only to the front that made it. You are in the country again, which is to say in vacant law, and the tenant is behind you without having receded, because it does not travel by receding.

It is older than the floor.

That is the fact that makes it a life and not a standing wave with good manners. The ridges are pre-wear, written and unwritten, sharp because nothing has had time to round them. This has had time. It keeps partials whose shelves are gone—notes from pavements that unsided themselves, pitches that belonged to sills now reseeding as mineral snow a hundred kilometers off. A paleontology conducted entirely in sound, and conducted without nostalgia: it does not mourn the missing ridges. It declines to let the sea’s memory be only as long as the last storm. Continuance, for it, is that decline repeated. It does not feed. Calories are the wrong book, as pressure is the wrong book. It is maintained the way the rim is maintained, by the term, and it spends itself the way the rim spends the urge to fall—constantly, quietly, at the cost of being only itself and not the whole ocean.

Young stone can still insult it. A shelf nucleating inside the locus rises through the knot the way a thought rises through a sentence and ruins the grammar. There is no flinch you can see. What you can measure, if you have been dull enough to measure, is thinning: the edits grow weaker, the held fraction shortens, the returned partials come back closer to weather. It spends itself around the new ridge, reading a floor that was not there yesterday, until the knot is thick again and the ridge is simply part of the phrase. Most of the time the ridge loses the argument and un-decides. Sometimes the ridge stays, and the locus revises, and the repertoire gains a note. That is as close as it comes to growth. It does not heal in the animal sense. It re-reads.

The veils, which the empty sea leaves to chance, last a little longer when their holdfasts sit inside the editing. Not by care. By the local fronts being argued with before they scour. A map made only of anchors, if anyone bothered, would show halos—slightly less rare patients, still far apart, still ungrazed, arranged around centers that have no bodies in them. That is the second way to find it, after the listening. The third is the rim.

It goes there. Not often, and not as a migration; a locus can include the standing argument the way a long room includes a window. At the boundary the water is turned back into itself, spray rehearsing air and then becoming rain. The tenant is turned by the same law, because it is made of the exception and not exempt from it. It does not cross into the drop. What it keeps, that the water does not, is the memory of having been turned. Each refusal at the rim enters the repertoire as a partial with no shelf under it at all—a note whose only source is the edge of the world. Afterward, deep in the fetch, you can hear that note laid under a local storm that has never been near the boundary. The highland is present in the knot even when the knot is days from the weather-wall. It carries the argument inland, not as a relic, as a temperament.

There are more than one, if “one” is even the unit.

Across a volume the size of this sea the knots are few, separated by reaches of true vacancy where the chords die on schedule and the veils stand or fail alone. They do not visit. A front can pick up a partial from one locus and carry it, dulling, for a day or three, and deliver a ghost of it into the listening of another. Sometimes the second knot returns a correction—adds what was missing, refuses what the storm invented on the way—and that correction starts back along whatever density-line is leaving. Often it never arrives. Often it arrives late, answering a phrase the first knot has already outgrown. This is the whole of their society: a correspondence too slow to be conversation, conducted in a medium that would rather be weather, and never assembled into a school, a people, a coast. Solitude here is not a mood. It is the distance the law leaves between its knots, and they do not spend themselves on closing it.

They can end, which is how you know they were not the law itself.

The term fails locally, very rarely, the way a climate can forget itself in a single valley. Pressure arrives as weight. The water remembers the textbook for a little while. Ordinary things are only confused—the light goes wrong, a ridge that was forming is pinched into something older than it should be, the roar drops out from under itself. A knot caught in that forgetfulness is crushed, because it was made of the exception and the exception has been withdrawn. Nothing else here is crushed. The failure passes; the term resumes; the floor goes back to being young. The locus does not resume. What spreads, at the speed of the internal fronts, is a quiet in the repertoire: other knots stop returning corrections for a time, hold their chords longer, add nothing. They have noticed. They do not search. There is nowhere a search would go. After a while the correspondence resumes, thinner by one temperament, and the sea, which never knew it had tenants, sounds as it sounded.

If you are inside one when this happens far off, you may feel nothing you can swear to. The answers continue. Your small noises come back folded into a phrase that has been kept against the youth of the stone. It does not regard you as company. It does not regard you as weather either, which is the courtesy, or the indifference, that passes for regard in a country without movers. You are a partial it did not predict. It will remember the shape of you for as long as it remembers vanished shelves—not your name, not your errand, the contour of the sound you made when you were still pretending the water was empty.

Then the locus thins at its far side, and you are out, and the fetch is only fetch: cool, loud, lit, mostly nothing, writing a floor that will not last, keeping an edge that should spill and does not. The tenant remains where it was, which is not a place you can point to from outside, occupying the law without breaking it, older than every ridge under it, alive in the only sense this highland has ever needed—by refusing, repeatedly, to let the next storm be the whole of what the water is allowed to know.
