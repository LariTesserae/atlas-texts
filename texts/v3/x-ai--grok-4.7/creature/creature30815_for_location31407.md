What lives here does not arrive and does not leave. It thickens.

At the scale of the hand, the walls are not walls. They are congregations that have forgotten how to be separate. Each tube is an animal no longer than a finger, soft inside a collar of its own lime, mouth turned always into the same small push of water. It does not hunt. It does not turn. It opens when the grit runs thick and closes when the pulse passes, and in the long interval between it lays down another paper of shell against the paper of its neighbor, until the joint is not a meeting but a healed place. A single one, lifted clear, would be nothing: a pale sleeve, a wet thread, a confusion. In their numbers they are masonry. They inherit edges the way other animals inherit dens. A crack is not damage to them. It is a birth-site. The young settle in the lee of the old, and within a season the crack has a lip, and within a generation the lip has a street-face, worn on the outside, private on the inside, riddled with the narrow apartments of whatever is small enough to live in a wall's breath.

They have no eyes worth the name. Light, when it happens, is an event in the water, not a thing they look at. What they keep track of is thinner than sight: the taste of a seep, the minute change in pressure when a large body takes the lane, the chemistry of a neighbor dying and thereby becoming, for a while, food and fertilizer and a gap that must be closed before the current learns to prefer it. Their "knowing" is all local. No tube has a picture of the town. The town is what persists when every tube knows only the tube beside it, and the current, which is older than any of them, keeps preferring the old lines. A collapse bends a lane; the settlers downstream taste the bend as hunger and silt; they build back toward the clearer thread; the grid reasserts itself without anyone having defended it. In that sense the builders are not citizens. They are the city's reflex.

Under them, and on them, and in the film that makes the water slightly alive, is the grain. Too many to count, too small to single out, armored in the way of things that expect to be eaten constantly and have made an industry of surviving the average bite. They are the traffic you feel as texture. A plate is smooth because uncountable feet have polished it, and pitted because uncountable mouths have started the next meal in the same grooves their parents started. They rise and fall with the feeding pulse, not as a school with a leader but as a weather: dimming, thickening, settling. When a palm-sized scale sloughs from a roof, they are on its underside before it has finished falling. They do not claim it. Claiming would require a pause. They simply continue, and the scale arrives at the floor already inhabited, already arguing, by sheer density, which face will be up. Most of what the larger animals eat is this. Most of what the builders filter is this. The grain eats the shed of both, and the rain's slow gift of mineral, and one another. Nothing in the basin is wasted at a scale the grain can reach. Waste is only what has not yet been made small enough.

The cruisers are the first bodies you would grant the word animal without hesitation, and even they refuse a clean outline. Warm, yes — a heat you notice as a softening of the water a moment before the flank arrives. Blunt. Not streamlined for a chase; there is nothing here worth chasing at speed, and speed would only spend heat the basin already gives away for free. They move at the height where the lane does the most of the work, correcting with a shrug of the body when the masonry brushes them. Eyes, if they are eyes, are flat patches that register the rare copper pulse and otherwise report darkness as the normal case. They navigate by the push. A junction is a change in the way the water leans. The central wheel is a long turning they can enter and leave by feel, layer by layer, each size keeping to the band where its mouth meets what the band above has broken and not yet swallowed. They do not school in the bright sense. They agree. Agreement looks like spacing: no one crowds the flank ahead, no one drops into a lower layer without the thickening that means the layer can spare it. A young one learning the wheel makes small mistakes of depth and is shouldered, gently, back into its band. No bite. Biting would be a waste of a neighbor who is also a signpost.

They shed. Scales, mucus, the grey dust of skin. This is not decline. This is half of how they speak, and half of how the town is fed. The grit thickens where a cruiser has passed; the builders downstream open a fraction wider; the lane, for a little while, tastes worked. You could follow one without seeing it, by the trail of increase it leaves, until the trail dissolves into the general alive-ness of the water and the animal is only a warmer absence moving off toward the basin wall, where the current thins and the mounds give way to marl and they do not stay. They are town animals. Open ground unsettles them, not with fear — fear would be loud — but with the simple failure of their cues. No edges, no leans, no grain under the flank. They turn back.

Across the grain, rarer, are the ones the lanes make room for without comment. Not larger by much. Different in the angle. Where everything else corrects toward the current, these hold a line that cuts it, and the tick of armored feet pauses in a narrow wake and resumes. They take a cruiser the way the cruisers take the grit: by being already in the right thickness when the thickening comes. There is no strike you would tell later as a story. A warmth joins a warmth; a band loses one agreement; the wheel accepts the new number and goes on turning. The taken animal comes apart along seams that seem, afterward, to have been waiting. Its heat lingers longest. The builders nearest the place open and shut a few times more than the rhythm asks, tasting a richness that will not be repeated soon, and then the ordinary murk returns, and the cross-cutter is only another correction in the dark, already aligning itself with nothing, already forgetting in the only way this water allows, which is by continuing.

None of them call. The click you hear is structural: a tube sealing, a joint giving along a line it has been giving along for years, two plates finding each other again after a body has passed. If there is a language, it is the shared thickness — who opened, who shed, who settled, who did not. A death is a change in taste that lasts until the grain has finished. A birth is a new resistance against the fingers of the current, small at first, then reliable. They do not mark either. The basin marks them by what remains standing.

And yet something holds, larger than any life in it. Not a mind. Not a god of the sink. Call it the habit that has become heavy enough to have a direction. In the central wheel the layers turn inside one another, and the turning keeps four darker pulls clear, four lanes that could have silted shut a thousand times and have not, because every animal that feeds there feeds better when the water keeps its old promises. The oldest crust, deep in the masses, is still alive in the technical sense — tubes in the interior, mouths long since buried, bodies reduced to the slow chemistry of maintenance, pumping nothing, building nothing, persisting as the stiffness the young brace against. They are the foundations that have not died so much as changed jobs. A city of shells needs its dead to stay put. Here the dead were never separate from the living long enough to fall away. They accepted the promotion into stone, and the stone is warm, and the warm stone is what the rain has been falling on, season after season, without ever finding a surface that could be called finished.

So the resident of the basin is not one creature you could point to and name. It is a stacking: grain inside cruiser inside layer inside wheel; builders inside builders inside the idea of a street; a cross-cutter writing a brief diagonal through all of it and being erased by the next pulse. If you stayed, you would not learn a face. You would learn a schedule. When the murk thickens, eat, or be eaten, or build. When it thins, repair the gap you are. When the water leans a new way, follow, unless you are the kind that must not follow, in which case the town will close behind you and not call it cruelty. No lamps. No tools. No name. Only the occupied balance, and the bodies that are the balance, breathing the same warm black, laying down one more paper of themselves against the dark, because the water has decided to stay and they have decided, in the only way they can, to be what it stays in.
