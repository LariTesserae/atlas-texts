It does not arrive. Arrival would require a place that kept the shape it had when you started toward it, and the column does not do that. What lives here is already in the water with you, distributed through the unoccupied stretches the way a held note is distributed through a room: not everywhere, but not locatable either, until some edge of it crosses a seam of light and you understand you have been breathing beside it for longer than the word beside still means.

At first it is only a difference in the warmth. The column gives off a close animal temperature all along its skin; this is warmer still, a pocket the size of a doorway, then the size of a nave, then small again, as if the heat had remembered a body and then thought better of the memory. You put a hand into it and the hand comes back filmed with nothing. No slime. No scale-shed. Only the sense that the water there has been occupied recently by something that organizes liquid the way a lung organizes air: by taking it in and giving it back changed.

Then a flank.

It is not the flank you will keep. The first one is pale, almost the color of the new stone, and ridged in long low cords that might be muscle or might be the grain of the rock it has been lying against so long the grain transferred. It slides past at the length of a hall and does not end. You wait for the tail, for the narrowing, for the polite conclusion of an animal, and what comes instead is a second beginning: another shoulder, narrower, turned the other way, as if the creature had started itself twice and neglected to cancel the draft. Between the two beginnings the water goes briefly ordinary, empty, insultingly empty, and then a third mass bulbs into the dimness above you, soft-edged, the size of a chapel vault, and folds itself down until it is no thicker than a shawl drifting off a peg.

It is large. That much holds. Large the way the column is large: not a measurement, a jurisdiction. When it turns, rooms turn with it. A ledge that was completing itself under the idea of your feet shears sideways and becomes a rib of the thing, or the thing becomes the ledge, young stone-color running into flesh-color until you cannot say which revision belonged to the mountain and which to the tenant. It does not hunt the ledges. It wears them while they last. A fan of weed that has been streaming sideways for no reason streams now because a long plane of the creature is passing behind it, dragging the water into a temporary honesty. The weed bows. The creature does not notice the weed. Noticing, for it, seems reserved for interruptions sharper than plants.

There are eyes, eventually, and then there are not, and then there are too many, and the too many is the truer count. They are not clustered into a face. A face would be a decision, and decisions here get withdrawn. A pale disc opens in the flank, lidless, the pupil a vertical cut like the fracture in the floor, and watches you with the patience of something that has already watched the mountain fail to finish rising. Another opens farther along, higher, in what is currently a ceiling of flesh. A third scars over before it finishes forming, a milk-blind thumbprint, and the flesh around it keeps the habit of an eye anyway: a slight tension, a listening. You learn not to count them. Counting is a kind of stillness, and stillness is what the column edits out.

The mouth is harder, because for a long time you take the sound for the mouth.

The column is already deafening. Stone fretting, flakes clicking, that low bodily note of the fracture, the furious sorting of small hard things. The creature adds a register underneath, or perhaps it is the instrument the other registers are played on. When it draws water in, the clatter dips, not quieter exactly, but gathered, as if many small impacts had agreed to strike the same instant. When it lets the water go, the sorting scatters again, and with it comes a smell you should not be able to smell in a full column of sea: hot dust, split rock, the iron breath of a quarry at noon. It feeds on the revisions. Not on the fish-pale shapes that cross the light and vanish, though those vanish faster when it is near, sensible enough to refuse a jurisdiction. It feeds on the moment a wall becomes a ceiling, on the crack of a ledge deciding, on the flakes the water refuses to let settle. Each revision is a swallow. The place practices its shape; the tenant eats the discarded drafts.

That is why the built things are broken the way they are, and why so few of them remain.

The ring of fitted stones is not a house it smashed. Smashing would require a preference for ruins, and it has none. It passed through the ring while the ring was still a ring, and the mountain under it revised, and the creature, mid-passage, kept the curve of the stones in its flank for a while the way it keeps an eye-scar: a habit of having been round. When it shed that habit the ring was already open on one side, the gaps sharp, the hands that set the courses gone into the same revision or standing somewhere in the unoccupied water with their patience worn out. The leaning post remembers a grip. Not a hand, exactly. A tightening. Wood or bone worried where the creature once held still against it long enough for the liquid to learn the shape of a hold and keep worrying it after the hold let go. The cups pecked into the rock are older than that, or newer; sequence is a courtesy here. They are not a calendar. They are where it rested a row of some softer part, fingertip or feeder or the blunt ends of whatever it uses when it wants to be narrow, and the stone, young and loud with edges, took the impression because the creature asked it to stay still for the length of a placement. The stone tried. The column did not.

So it has used tools only in the sense that a river uses a throat of rock. Weight. Patience. The refusal to pick up a second method. There is no device in the column because anything small enough to carry gets revised into flake and sort and swallow. It learned this, if learned is fair. More accurate to say the column taught the lesson into its body until the body stopped offering handles.

What it wants is difficult, because want implies a future the place will honor.

Sometimes it wants the outside. You can tell by the way a thinner face of the column’s skin brightens, a bruised day leaking through, and a long plane of the creature rises toward it with all its eyes open on that side, pale discs drinking the glow, the flesh thinning as if it meant to become a window itself and step out into the high air as weather. It never completes the step. The skin turns its thin face away, or the creature’s nerve fails, or the air beyond is a revision it cannot swallow because it has not yet been discarded. It hangs under the brightness until the brightness is edited into brown-green dark, and then it is only heavy again, chapel-sized, shawl-sized, a doorway of extra warmth drifting down through halls that will not stay halls.

Sometimes it wants the fracture. It goes to the blade of pale stone in the floor and lays a beginning along it, one of its shoulders, and the low note of the crack deepening joins the note of its own body until you cannot separate mountain from tenant. In those hours it is almost geological. You could mistake it for a vein the summit grew, a hot inclusion, and walk the length of it naming minerals. Then a mouth opens somewhere along the vein, not a tear, a decision, and the quarry-breath comes out, and the mistake ends.

Sometimes it wants you, and this is the want that does not stabilize either, which is the only mercy.

It does not lunge. Lunging needs a distance that remains distance. Instead the empty water between you and the nearest plane of it shortens without either of you moving, the way a sentence shortens when a clause is struck. A pale disc considers your face with the same patience it gave the unfinished mountain. The warmth thickens. You feel the small hard sorting of the column gather, as if you had become a draft it might swallow: an interruption, a ledge that decided, a thing that could be revised into flake and taken in. For a moment your own edges go unreliable. A hand looks longer. A knee forgets which way it bends. The creature is not cruel about this. Cruelty needs a stable other. It is only feeding in the manner the place allows, tasting whether you are a revision yet.

If you stay unrevised, if you keep enough of a single arrangement to disappoint it, it loses interest with a vast, humiliating gentleness. The eyes scar. The plane thins into shawl and then into ordinary dark. The extra warmth departs into the general animal temperature of the column, which was always partly its temperature, which you understand now you have been walking inside of since the skin of the water first shivered under your approach. It does not depart. There is nowhere to depart to. It resumes being the difference you cannot point at, the held note, the reason the weed streams, the second beginning that never canceled the first.

What lives in the column is the column’s only continuous habit.

Not a god of it: gods require worship or at least a name that survives a rewrite, and nothing here keeps a level long enough for liturgy. Not a prisoner: the bars are made of the same substance as the breath. It is the appetite that formed when an ocean was begun in the wrong room and could not be finished or taken back. Sparse, because a second one would be a school, and schools need rules. Enormous, because the room is the ocean. Unfinished on purpose, or past the point where purpose still applies, its body a stack of abandoned drafts it has not yet eaten, eyes opening where the light is thinnest, mouth wherever the sound gathers. It will be here as long as the summit is still being born. When the summit finishes, if it finishes, the tenant will have to become weather or stone or nothing, and the column will finally have a quiet version of itself.

Until then it sorts. It swallows the discarded shapes. It holds still only long enough to be mistaken for a vein, a wall, a warmth you already felt. And if you listen under the deafening, under the fretting and the clicks and the fracture’s low note, there is a smaller sound that is not the mountain: a wet click, patient, repeated, like a creature trying to settle a single hard piece into a single cup pecked in living stone, and finding every time that the cup has moved, and beginning again, without anger, without pause, in the only room it has.
