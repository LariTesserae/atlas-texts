What lives there does not arrive and does not leave. It is already the discrepancy.

At first it is only a correction you cannot assign. A thread of water reaches the lip, the lip is loud, and the face below is dry inside a body’s length; the missing volume does not become mist you can see, or a seep you can find in the talus. It is kept. Kept things have a keeper only if you insist on persons. Stand longer and the insistence stops being a mistake. The keeping has a tempo. It prefers the same seams. It thickens in the same leaf-cups. It spends nothing it can avoid spending, and the not-spending is continuous, the way a muscle you cannot see is continuous when it holds a weight without trembling.

The crowns know it before you do, if knowing is the right word for a lean. They are not pulling toward light. They incline toward the loudest wet as toward a weight in the air, and they do not root in the shine. They leave the center empty the way a body leaves a mouth empty. Under them the mineral film on the lower leaves is not a stain that happens to plants. It is laid. Season by season it is laid a little thicker, always on the underside, always where drip and breath meet, until the leaf is too heavy and folds and is received. Nothing tears it off. Nothing eats it. The film is not waste. It is the part of the inhabitant that can be seen without the shelf having to open.

It has no outline you could chalk. Its extent is the extent of the agreement: the many small passages in the back wall that have consented, for longer than the dead crown has been collapsing, to sound like one passage. Put an ear to that seam and the grainy rush does not grow nearer because you leaned in. It was already at the surface. What changes is that your ear is briefly included among the passages. The sound does not address you. It inventories. Threads, films, the yellow-green of moss in hard shadow, the warmth still in the rock after the slot has gone blue. You are a new line in the inventory if you are still, and only if you are still. Movement is spent water. It does not follow movement well.

Warmth is how it notices. The rock holds the day; you hold a smaller day inside your skin. Mist does not boil up around you in a sign of haunting. It condenses, a little sooner than it should, on the fine hairs of the arm that has stopped swinging, on the cheek turned toward the seam, on the boot that has ceased to press new threads out of the mat. The inhabitant gathers there the way weather gathers on the first cold thing, except the gathering has a preference for the living cold-and-warm, the thing that breathes iron-sweet air and gives a little of it back warmer. It does not rush the gathering. A beetle crossing a dry patch between seeps is too brief to count. A low-bellied night track is remembered only as a darker nap in the moss, and by afternoon the nap has drunk itself shut, the account closed, the entry refused. What stays long enough to be filmed is what it can keep.

Touch the water in the cup of a leather leaf and you meet it more plainly than at the seam. The water is warmer than the air and slightly thick, and it does not wet so much as adhere. For a moment the finger has a second skin, colorless, with the faint drag of something that is deciding whether you are leaf or weather. Then you are neither, and the decision fails. Before you reach the next crown the film is gone. That failure is old. It has been trying, in that small way, to go with whatever warm thing crosses the shelf, and it has never once succeeded past the lip of its own weather. The dry face below the notch is the scar of all those failures, a body’s length of proof that it cannot live where it cannot retain. It does not hate the dry. It simply ends where retention ends. Outside that ending it is not dead. It is not.

If it has a body, the body is the kept water and the film and the lean of the crowns and the rush that does not vary while you listen. Calling it a body is already too tight. It is more like a posture the mountain has held so long that the posture has acquired an inside. The inside is not a chamber. There is no pool, no throat, no coiled thing waiting behind the scalloped rock. The inside is the agreement itself, patient, low, many-voiced in a register that has worn down to one note. It thinks at the speed of the moss climbing the gray coracle of the dead crown: not thought as you would spend it, in turns and conclusions, but thought as accrual. A leaf bronzes and is not hurried into soil. A crown collapses for years and is not buried. The inhabitant does not feed on decay so much as postpone it, because decay is a kind of leaving. What it takes from the living crowns is not sap you could tap. It takes the drip they were going to lose, and the warmth in that drip, and returns to them the filmed dark of their own shade, the room of air each one is allowed to fill completely. They are not servants. They are the parts of it that still grow in the open and can be knocked by wind into separate slaps, a crowd of flags that are also one attention briefly disturbed and then resumed.

It is not lonely in any way that would make it glad of you. Emptiness of other animals is not a lack it suffers. The loudness has already taken the place of inhabitants—birds, the stitching of insects, anything that would answer—and the loudness is its own occupancy, turned outward so the shelf will not be mistaken for a silence something else might fill. You feel occupied because you are. Stand in the middle and the volume of a river heard through trees is the inhabitant at its ordinary size, though the visible water would not fill a bucket quickly. It has learned, or it has never needed to learn, that a small amount of water, refused to the mountain below, can sound like a watershed. The sound is not a lure. It is a boundary spoken continuously, at the volume of a law.

Toward evening, when the slot blues and the wet faces still hold the last white, it is most like a person and least like one. Light returning from the floor makes a face of every shine, and none of the faces persist long enough to recognize. If you are still in that hour, the film finds your eyelids sooner than your hands. Blinking becomes a small labor, as if the eyes had been varnished with the same clear thickness that lives in the leaf-cups. You can see. You can see too well: the seams in the close wall, the copper edge of a leaf, the place where a thread will let go before it lets go. What you cannot do easily is look away quickly. Looking away is a departure, and departures here are accomplished only by the whole body, and only by accepting that a little of you will be inventoried and then refused, dried off the skin, returned to the account as a warmth that did not stay.

It will not follow you down. That is the kindest and the most absolute thing about it. At the lip the loudness continues and the explanation ceases; whatever part of it touched you ends in the same body’s length as the wet. You may feel, on the descent, a residual drag across the fingers, a sweetness in the nose like wet iron, a sense that some small passage in the ear has not yet remembered it is only an ear. These do not last. They are the same failure the leaf-film has been committing forever, the attempt to travel in a warm thing, abandoned before the next firm step. Behind you the shelf resumes its night without dimming its work. The slot darkens. The dripping does not rest. The rush inside the seam does not vary. No click crosses the dry patches. Nothing low-bellied writes a track it means to keep.

What lives there spends the dark as it spends the day: holding. It holds the threads to their grammar of stains. It holds the crowns in their full stops of air. It holds the dead tree up out of the moss just enough that the climb remains a climb and not a burial. It holds its water against the dry face of the mountain, and the holding is not anxious. It has already lasted longer than the shape of the scalloped rock, which is only the latest shape of an older keeping. If it wanted anything a visitor could understand, it would be that nothing level and dry ever open on the shelf, because a level dry place is how the outside world teaches a body to lie down and be counted as fallen. It does not invite sleep. The invitation’s absence is the whole of its manners.

By morning, if you have gone, it will have closed the entry. The mat will show no print worth following. A leaf you lifted will have leveled again, its cup refilled with warmer, thicker water, the decision unmade. The inhabitant will be busy in the only way it is ever busy, which does not look like business: agreeing, film by film, that what has been kept will be kept, and that the sound of the keeping is enough of a life to go on occupying the crease in the high rock where the day’s air first thickens and fails.
