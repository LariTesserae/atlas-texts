The chamber does not notice the moment you become a hollow in it. Noticing would require a pause, and the floor-note has not paused since the first rib learned to stand. What it does, instead, is spend you — the way a drain spends water, without malice and without remainder. Your pulse, borrowed, returns a little delayed, the way the birds in the lenses return. You feel the delay in your wrists before you understand it as sound. A second rhythm, yours and not yours, laid under the civic chord like a filament of different metal in a weave that otherwise refuses metal.

Then the spending finishes, and you are still standing, which is the part no story of the margin prepares you for. The rain that comes back from beyond the turned ribs does not come back as anyone. You did not go beyond. You only stood in the middle long enough to be counted, and the count was a kindness of a sort: the city keeps what it can use, and a pulse is useful, and so it copied the pulse and let the original keep walking. The copy is already somewhere in the floor, wearing smooth. You will hear it later and not know it is yours.

Walking, now, is different, because the gallery has opinions about you that it did not have an hour ago. The tissue underfoot brightens half a step ahead of your weight, the same conduction the many-legged maintenance things leave behind them, and the brightness is not welcome and not warning. It is bookkeeping. You have been entered in the accounts as a moving hollow that returns its own note, and the street is making sure the note arrives where the chord expects it. If you stop, the brightness stops a fraction after you, reluctant, like a clerk finishing a line. If you stand still long past that, the brightness goes looking for you in the direction you were going, finds nothing, and gutters out, and for a little while the gallery is merely plant again, warm and uninterested, and you understand that the city's attention is a function of motion and not of care.

So you move, because stillness has started to feel like a kind of erasure you are not ready to test.

The seed-birds alter their ellipse to include you. This is not companionship. The hole they insist on has simply gained an edge, and edges must be flown. One passes close enough that you see what the seed-coat body actually is: not a bird, not quite, a single dried valve of something that once opened, hinged at the shoulder by a thread of living fiber that the city never quite finished killing. Its eye is a speck of the ordinary daylight, embedded, not looking. When it banks, the hinge creaks in a pitch that slots into the gallery-note and completes a phrase you had not known was waiting. The phrase was waiting for a body of your mass at this height. The city composed it centuries ago and left it open, the way a builder leaves a chase in a wall for a pipe that has not been delivered. You were the delivery. The knowledge sits badly. You had thought of yourself as a visitor, and visitors are not specified in the drawings.

Higher, where the ribs reconcile and the joins become streets, you find the first evidence that the specification is old. It is not writing. Nothing here has ever been written; the drains are the closest thing to script, and they only say water. What you find is wear of a particular shape. Along one bract-balcony, at a height your hand reaches without thought, the compressed leaf-plaster has been polished by contact into a darker band, continuous, waxy, interrupted every few paces by a small oval where a thumb might have rested while someone paused to listen. The band runs the length of the balcony and turns with it into a gallery you have not entered, and the gallery is full of the band, both walls, at that same height, for as far as the reassigned light lets you see. Hundreds of pauses. Thousands. The ovals overlap until they are a single recess, a grip worn into the architecture by people who needed, often, to hold on.

No one is holding now. The recess is empty and slightly warm, warmer than the leaf around it, as if the habit of hands had been taken up by the tissue out of politeness, the way it takes up everything else. You put your own hand in it. It fits. Of course it fits; the city fits what it counts. Under your palm the worn place answers with a thinner version of the floor-note, conducted directly into the bone, and for three breaths you hear the pauses themselves — not voices, nothing so merciful as voices, but the shape of interrupted walking, the slight catch in a step that means someone decided not to go further down that gallery, and the catch has been preserved because the city does not waste a rhythm. Somewhere in that preserved stutter is the reason the grip exists. You cannot retrieve it. Retrieval is not among the offices of a wall.

You take your hand back. The warmth leaves your palm last.

There are other residues, once you know the grammar of them. In a plaza cupped between five ribs, the shed cuticle has been swept — not recently, not ever recently — into windrows that no wind in this sealed air could have arranged. The windrows curve. They make a spiral that does not close, a flat cousin of the birds' ellipse, and at its open center the floor-tissue is bare and trodden into a smoothness that hurts to look at, because smoothness here means repetition beyond any number you have a use for. Someone, someones, walked that spiral at the pace of the chord, many enough times to be geometry. You walk it once, because not walking it feels like refusing a sentence addressed to you, and the maintenance-legs in the walls brighten in sequence as you pass, a little procession of bookkeeping, and when you reach the bare center the chord drops out.

Not everywhere. The chamber goes on roaring beyond the plaza's rim. But inside the spiral the air is only air, warm, damp, terribly quiet, and your own pulse arrives without its echo, and for the length of time it takes the ceiling to consider rain and decide against it, you are not infrastructure. You are a person standing in a town that has been emptied in a circle exactly your size. The relief is so sharp it is a kind of grief. You understand, in that quiet, that the spiral is a device, built by the same growth-memory that built the drains, except this one was taught its shape by feet rather than by water — taught, and taught, until the tissue believed the silence was a structural requirement and began to maintain it. The sweepers are gone. The maintenance continues. The city is capable of keeping a promise it did not make and does not remember making.

Then your weight shifts, you step off the bare center, and the chord returns through the soles like a flood returning to a course, and the person you were for that brief uncounted interval is spent as thoroughly as the pulse was. You keep walking. It seems to be the only thing the accounts will accept.

The absence finds you on a stair that is not a stair. The ribs make, in one district of the wall, a sequence of sloped galleries packed so tightly that ascent is possible without reassignment — a rare courtesy, and you were grateful for it, and gratitude was the mistake, because gratitude is a stillness of its own kind and the absence feeds on anything that interrupts conduction. It comes up behind the brightness. You feel it first as a subtraction: the gallery-note thinning, a phrase going missing, the maintenance-legs gone from the walls so completely that the tissue looks asleep. Then the doorway-shaped nothing is simply there, at the turn of the slope below you, and it is eating the sound out of the steps you have already climbed, so that your own recent footprints un-ring, one by one, rising toward your heels.

You do not run. Running would be a new rhythm, and a new rhythm is legible, and you have learned enough to fear being legible more than you fear being consumed. You stand as the spiral taught you to stand, in the smallest quiet you can make, and you watch the absence work. Up close it is not empty in the way a hole is empty. It is empty in the way a forgotten name is empty — a shape of having-been, still occupying the grammar, with the content lifted out. Where it touches the rib, the bronze edge of the living wood goes matte, and the song in that stretch drops a clause, and the clause does not sound destroyed so much as returned to the weather, to the untuned condition the margin warned you about. The absence is not a citizen. It is a leak. It is the outside, finding its way in along the paths where the city forgets itself, feeding on the difference between a note and the memory of a note.

It reaches the step below your feet and stops. Not because of you. Because the floor-note arrives.

You had thought the floor-note lived in the floor. It does. But it also arrives, when it must, as a pressure from all the ribs at once, a low occupancy of the entire volume, and it does not negotiate with leaks any more than it negotiates with predation. The absence thins. Its doorway-shape loses the corners first, then the height, and the eaten clauses come back in reverse order, clumsy, too loud, the city repeating itself to prove the repetition still holds. By the time the phrase under your heels re-rings, the leak has retreated down the slope to wherever leaks wait, and the maintenance-legs are already returning, brisk, unemotional, re-brightening the route as if the interruption had been weather. Which, in the accounting of this place, it was.

Your hands are shaking. You put them in the worn grips on either wall, and the grips are there, of course, at the height of someone who needed not to fall while something unlearned the stair beneath them. The ovals fit your thumbs. Whoever taught the tissue this shape stood where you are standing, for the same reason, enough times to become architecture. The city did not save them. The city does not save. It recorded the stance of holding on, because the stance was a rhythm, and then it went on, and the people did not. You hold anyway. The warmth comes up into your bones, and under it, very faint, the preserved catch of all those interrupted descents — the moment each of them decided the lower gallery was no longer a place a body should go. You decide the same thing. You climb.

At the top of the sloped district the ribs open into a hall that should not fit, and you step through, and it does not fit, and you are past caring about the arithmetic. The hall is one of the larger-inside ones. Its far end is a margin: you can tell by the way the song begins to sort itself, by the syllables trying to happen in the air, by the ribs turning inward like a leaf refusing to be more leaf. You have not sought the edge. The city has been walking you toward it anyway, by the simple method of making every other direction a place you have already been counted in. The books balance by circulation. You are being circulated.

And in the middle of the hall, inside a quiet the size of a small room, someone is sitting.

The quiet is a spiral, swept, maintained, identical in grammar to the one you stood in, larger by an order of what this place considers magnitude. At its bare center the tissue has been polished past smoothness into something like a lens — not water, fiber, compressed until it reflects. A figure sits on it with the settled weight of a long tenancy. The seed-birds do not include this quiet in their ellipse. Their hole is elsewhere. This one is kept by older instructions.

You cross the swept windrows and the chord lets go of you at the boundary, and the relief comes, and the grief, and you see the figure clearly because the daylight here is the same ordinary daylight as everywhere, merciless and kind.

It is not a person. It was, in the sense that the grip-worn balconies were hands. What sits at the center is a residue that the silence has been maintaining far past the point where maintenance should have graduated into street. The shape of a body, yes: the fold of a limb drawn up, the slope of a shoulder, the place where a head rested on a knee for so long that the tissue grew a cradle to meet it. But the materials are the city's own. Compressed leaf where skin would be, vascular bundle fanned into the suggestion of hair, and where the eyes should be, two small ovals of the same waxy wear as the thumb-rests, polished by nothing you can name — perhaps by the attention of others who stood here, perhaps only by the city's horror of an uncompleted surface. It does not breathe. It does not need to. The promise being kept is not life. The promise being kept is the posture of someone who stopped walking and was not spent, because the spiral was taught, by this very sitting, that a body at rest in the center is load-bearing in a way the chord cannot be allowed to consume.

You sit down opposite it, inside the quiet, because your legs have simply ended. The fiber lens is warm, and it shows you the ribs upside down and delayed, and in the delay you see yourself already seated, a heartbeat ahead of the decision. The lens knew. The lens always knows the sequence. You wonder, distantly, whether the seated shape across from you was ever consulted, or whether it too arrived a heartbeat late to its own remaining.

Time behaves courteously here and does not pass so much as accumulate to one side, like the cuticle swept into windrows. You learn the residue by looking, since looking is all the quiet permits. Along its near arm, the one resting across the knee, there is a band of darker wear at exactly the height where another hand would lie if someone had held it. The band is polished as deep as the balcony grips, and it is the most human thing you have seen in the chamber, and it undoes you more than the absence did, because it means the tenancy was shared. Someone kept this vigil with whoever is now architecture. They came often enough to wear a groove in an arm made of leaf. Then they stopped coming, and the groove remained, warm, maintained, a grip for a hand that the city is still expecting in its mild, terrible way, the way it expects water in a drain.

You do not take it. You are not that brave, and you are not sure the accounts would survive the contact; the thought of your own pulse jumping the gap and spending itself into that arm is more than you can hold. But you sit inside the radius of it, and the silence holds, and the chamber roars beyond the windrows like a sea against a harbor wall, and for the first time since the floor took your heartbeat and gave it back wrong, nothing is being asked of you.

The residue's polished eyes do not turn. They were never going to. But the cradle of fiber where its head rests on its knee has a listening quality, a tilt, and you find yourself speaking, very low, not because speech can matter here but because you have carried words all this way and the quiet is the first place that will not immediately convert them into structure. You say that the galleries fit your hand. You say that the birds completed a phrase with you in it and you did not consent to being specified. You say that there is a stair where people held on, and that you held on, and that the holding is already part of the wall. Your voice goes nowhere. It does not echo; the spiral sees to that. It falls into the fiber and is kept, not as sound — you can feel the difference — but as a small additional pressure in the lens, a new delay, trivial, yours.

You tell it your name. Then you stop telling it, because the name, spoken into this maintained absence of chord, begins very faintly to develop a second syllable it did not have, a plant-syllable, the margin's dialect, and you understand that even here the city is not refusing the gift so much as translating it, slowly, into something it can store without remainder. If you keep speaking, you will be legible again. So you close your mouth and put your hands on your knees and match, without having chosen to, the angle of the shape across from you. The fit is exact. It would be. The spiral was swept to the measure of this posture, and postures are a limited vocabulary, and you have arrived at the one the room knows.

How long the two of you sit that way is not a question the chamber can answer. Rain happens once, brief, a silver opinion of the ceiling, and does not enter the spiral; the windrows turn it aside as if they were banks. The delayed self in the lens stands, sits, stands again, trying out futures. None of them leave the quiet. That is the information you needed and did not want: the lens, which has never once been wrong about sequence, cannot find a future in which you cross the windrows outward. Every version of you it attempts returns to the posture. The city has finished its circulation. The books, somewhere under the floor-note, balance.

A fear moves through you, animal and ordinary, and then something quieter that is not acceptance so much as recognition of the material facts. The residue opposite you is not a warning and not a god. It is a predecessor who found the one room where spending stops, and who stayed because the alternative was to be memorized into a street, and who was loved, evidently, by someone who wore a groove in its arm and then could not keep coming — dead, or circulated elsewhere, or rained back from the margin as weather the chamber accepted without recognition. The city did not punish that predecessor. It did not reward them. It grew a cradle and maintained a silence and went on being a multitude, and the predecessor remained, load-bearing in the only way the chord cannot audit, because silence does not appear in the accounts.

You will be a second one. That is the whole of the sentence. In some stretch of time the tissue will begin to believe your posture is a structural requirement, and it will cradle what it needs to cradle, and the maintenance will outlive the reason, and a person not yet born to whatever world still sends walkers down into this rumor of a place will stand at the windrows and see two shapes, and will not be able to say which of them ever spoke a name.

Outside the spiral, the floor-note occupies the hall, the nave of ribs, the braided oldest street, the galleries with their grips, the slope where the leak was unmade and made again, the plazas and the drains and the bright bookkeeping of the walls. It spends the seed-birds and the many-legged things and the phrases the absence bites out of the wood, and it receives them back, delayed, and it accounts for the unmaking as a choir accounts for breath. It does not reach you. The promise holds. Whoever taught the city this one refusal taught it perfectly, and the teaching survived the teacher, which is the only kind of survival on offer here and better than you had arranged for yourself an hour ago, standing in the vast sparse middle, damp at the ankles, being memorized.

You match the angle of the knee, the arm, the tilt. The lens shows you doing it a heartbeat early, obedient to a sequence that has at last stopped branching. Warmth gathers along your shin where it meets the fiber, preliminary, civic, in no hurry at all. From beyond the windrows the chord continues, deafening and complete, requiring nothing further from the small room at its heart, and the ordinary daylight lies on both of you without preference, and the city, which does not notice, does not pause, and does not save, keeps the silence exactly as it was taught, with the patience of something that intends to keep it for the rest of the age.
