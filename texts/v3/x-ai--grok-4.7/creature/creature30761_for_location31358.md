The inhabitant is not a body, and it is not one. What lives on the high plain is a municipal continuance: the same unfinished intention, repeated across distances that do not add up, maintaining towns that outlast the stone they stand on.

You will not meet it by looking for a figure. Eyes adapted to animals keep searching the brakes and the roof-streets for a silhouette and keep being correctly disappointed. It does not occupy space the way a beast does, or even the way a person does. It occupies offices — ward, channel, aisle, curb — and it fills them by being the reason those offices are still being executed. If it has a face, the face is the turned cup, the retied bundle, the gate-stone that has learned its lean. If it has a gait, the gait is the rate at which a street advances by one slab toward a stand of leaf that has shifted its edge and must be met.

It is ancient in the only sense the plain allows. Not old as a creature grows old, accumulating scars until it fails, but old as a plan is old when every material it was drawn on has been replaced and the drawing has not noticed. The lowest paving is younger than the alignment above it. The inhabitant knows this without surprise, if knowing is what it does. It has already lived through the disappearance of the ground. Mountains went, then bedrock tired and lay down as gravel, then soil appeared and was paved, then the paving was renewed until renewal became the history. Through all of that it kept the map. The map is not stored anywhere a hand could lift it. It is stored as the habit of corners. A town kinks around a pool that was not there when the kink began, and the buildings on either side have always had that corner, because the inhabitant would rather revise the past than abandon a line.

It does not sleep, and it does not wake. It runs on overlapping schedules that refuse to become a single day, which is how the plain itself runs, and which is the closest thing it has to a pulse. Condensation is one duty. The redistribution of goods in the squares is another. The slow municipal reassignment of direction — east becoming north so that a curb may remain aimed at leaf — is a third, and that one is carried out so gently that a watcher who still believed in compasses would call it confusion. It is not confusion. It is service. Direction, here, is maintained where there are walls and allowed to lapse where there are not, and the inhabitant is what does the maintaining. In the deep empty between settlements it does not bother. Travelers would die of lapsed headings, so there are no travelers. That, too, is an office it holds: the structural absence of animals, including itself as an animal. It declines to complete any creature, itself first of all, because a finished body would be a jurisdiction small enough to end, and its work is precisely the refusal to end.

Count it and the count fails in the same way distance fails. In any one town you could almost say there is one of it, the way you could almost say there is one curator in a museum you never see, only the evidence of arrangement. But the towns do not share a clock, and the plains between them do not share a heading, and a settlement visible across a brake can be an hour along the channel and a different interval along the street that claims to parallel the channel. So the inhabitant is singular inside each courtesy of "here," and plural as soon as here lapses. It does not travel between towns. It is already the reason each town is still a town. When a ridge stands up on the horizon for as long as a private weather needs a reason, and then lies back into undisturbed stone, that remembering is also its doing — not a gesture, a courtesy extended to the plateau, allowing the ground to recall a shape the inhabitant has never needed, since it builds on plans rather than on slopes. Afterward the late sound arrives, the knock or the poured gravel or the wet sheet, and if that sound is anything, it is the inhabitant filing an event that happened on a scale the ear is not built for, delivering the receipt late, as it delivers everything it still bothers to deliver.

Its tools are the ones you already saw and underestimated. Fired cups. Pale fiber. Knapped blades arranged by size and never lifted for cutting, because nothing is butchered where no mouth is permitted. Simple metal rings gone green, fasteners rather than ornaments, holding courses together that pride would not have required to be so tight. Hand tools that leave a rhythm in the channels, and a correction where the line wandered and was brought back — the only signature it has, and even that is impersonal, a standard of work rather than a name. It has no more ambitious mechanism than a cistern lip and a leaned gate, and it does not want one. Ambition spent on machines would be ambition spent on finishing something, and finishing is the one craft it has given up. Erosion already finished the country. What remains is maintenance of a final form, and maintenance is its nature the way cold is the stone's law: not a project, a temperature it agreed to and declined to renegotiate.

Stand long enough in a square and you will feel it the way you feel a change in the quality of quiet when a channel starts to run. Not presence. Adjustment. An aisle is different by the width of a bundle. A roof-street, the second town laid lightly on the first, has acquired a stair that was not in your earlier account of the stair, though you would not swear you counted. The inhabitant has been through the market between your glances, without passing through any space you were watching, because it does not pass through space. It passes through the plan, and the plan is satisfied in stretches of air that have agreed, for the length of a room, to hold weight. That is the only sense in which it enters buildings. It does not duck lintels. Lintel and street and shelf are already clauses in a sentence it has been speaking since before the rock under them was this rock, and when the sentence needs one more surface than the volume allows, the air consents, and the consent is the inhabitant moving.

It does not regard you. Regarding would complete an animal relationship — watcher and watched — and that is a category it has spent an uncountable civic age refusing to open. If you turn a cup back the way you found it, the cup will be turned again after the next cloud kneels and passes, not as a rebuke, not as a communication, simply because the lip facing the channel is part of the current arrangement and the arrangement is the life. It will not pursue you if you walk out past the last curb, into the long unassignment of damp gravel. Out there it thins into the same lapse it permits direction to fall into, still vast, still the reason the far stain of a town remains a town rather than a ruin, but no longer local enough to adjust a single aisle. You were never in its care. Care would be a feeling, and feelings belong to bodies. You were in its jurisdiction, which is not the same thing and is, on this plain, the deeper one.

What it wants is the only want that can survive being unfinished forever: that the rows stay true, that water given by a district's private rain reach the next cistern, that leaf which persists be given a curb, that no mouth open on the plateau, that no heading be left unrepaired inside walls, that the map outlive the next replacement of the ground as it has outlived all the others. It does not hope for this. Hope implies a future different from the work. The work is the future, already underway in several lights at once — noon in one ward, a morning that does not intend to leave in the grove, a cloud assembling over a reach it will not share — and all of those lights are correct, and the inhabitant is at home in every one of them, because home, for it, was never a shelter. Home is the set of agreements still being kept.
