What lives there does not occupy the court the way a body occupies a room. For a long time, walking it, you would say nothing does. The fins give back only your own shoulder. The lens under the slab shows only the color it has that hour. The cups on the lip hold their spoon of water or their spoon of salt, and the copper lies where it was left, bright at the scrape, green at the bend. If residence required hunger, or a cry, or a track that did not belong to grit migrating toward the night side, the notch would be empty, and it is not empty.

It announces itself first as a disagreement in the bookkeeping of the stone.

A flake the size of a fingernail leaves a ledge in the morning tick of sun on quartzite, clicks twice, and is then a flake among flakes — except that by the time the west wall’s shadow has crossed the court, that flake is back on the ledge, seated in the pale scar it came from, the fit so exact that only the absence of the small dust-shadow proves it traveled. You can watch for the return and not see it. The court is full of small honest motions: rime letting go, a grain hissing in a slot, a joint relieving itself with a spaced report. The flake’s going is one of those. Its coming back is not. It happens in the interval when your attention is on the other end of the notch, or on the thread of water darkening three paces of grit, or on the way the tin-color of a passing cloud lifts. Nothing hunts. Nothing is hidden in the sense of crouching. The flake is simply elsewhere, and then it is where it was, and the place has spent no sound on the correction.

Once you have caught that, other corrections become visible, and they are not all stone.

The knee-high wall across the slot slumps, as it should, by the thickness of a season’s patience. Under the top course, though, one stone has been turned. Not recently in any human sense — the grit in its joints is packed, the contact faces dull — but turned relative to the slump, so that its long axis now answers the draft instead of fighting it. A person stacking unmortared rock would have done it that way at the start, if they had stood in the slot long enough to feel where the wind actually leans. Whoever stacked it did not. The turning happened later, after the top course had already begun to fail, and it happened without disturbing the packed grit above it, which is the part that does not make sense until you stop requiring hands.

Near the unfinished niche the peck-marks have gained one more bite. It is the same half-moon, the same angle, a hard point struck and withdrawn. The new mark is not brighter than the others. It has already taken the sugar-grain of the quartzite into itself, as if the strike and the weathering were the same event. The copper awl is still in the second cup. It has not been used. Whatever struck the niche did not need to retrieve a tool, because the tool was never the point. The mark is the point. The niche is not being finished. It is being kept at the depth of almost.

In the deepest pocket, where the slab roofs the dark like a hand, the lens of water sometimes stands and sometimes does not. When it stands, and you lean, you get sky. When you do not lean, you get black. On the morning after a clear night the pane of ice is there, thin enough to powder. If you powder it, it powders. If you do not, by the time the east fins are fully lit the pane is a skin, then a dampness, then nothing you can point to — except a line, no thicker than a hair, where the ice chose to let go first. That line is not the shape melt makes. Melt makes a ragged retreat toward the warmest stone. This line is a circle, broken in one place, as if something had risen through the pane at a single point and the rest of the ice had decided, all at once, to agree. There is no hole afterward. There is no body standing in the basin. There is the circle, and then the sun finishes the work, and the pocket is only damp quartzite and a blue-white rim around its dark.

It lives in those intervals, and in the refusals.

It does not eat. There is nothing here to eat, and it has no inside in which a meal could be kept. It does not sing. A voice in the court is taken by the fins and not returned; whatever this is has learned that, or was never in the business of sending sound across a distance. It does not hide. Hiding would be a kind of storage, and the court does not store. Water arrives and is given back. Heat arrives and is given back. Sound arrives and is given back. The thing that lives here arrives in the same economy. You do not find it waiting on a shelf. You find the place a degree more itself than the weather accounts for, and the degree is the creature.

What it has, in place of a body, is a preference for completions that are not quite completions.

It likes the cups because they are the size of a decision: a palm’s width of sky, a pinch of salt, a spoon of nothing by afternoon. It does not drink. Drinking would lower the spoon by a measure you could accuse. It attends the moment when the water is about to be not water, and it is present in that moment the way a temperature is present — not as a figure, as a condition the stone is under. On damp mornings the salt feathers a little farther toward the rim of the third cup than evaporation alone would draw it, a dendritic push, cousin to the manganese films on the shaded undersides, except that manganese records a hesitation and this records a reaching that stops on purpose. The reaching stops when the pattern is about to become a picture. It will not be a picture. Pictures are a kind of saving.

It likes the slot-wall because the draft is an argument, and the argument can be answered without being ended. Knee-high was the right height. A taller wall would have made a room, and a room would have asked to be inhabited in the ordinary way, which this presence cannot survive. Ordinary inhabiting stores heat, stores food, stores a name for the door. Here the door is the lip where the air falls away and does not come back, and the presence uses that the way the fins use the sun: as a fact that organizes everything else. When the evening wind leans into the slots and draws its long low note, more pressure than pitch, something in the note is briefly organized — not into music, into a held shape in the moving air, a knot that travels the length of the court and comes apart against the east wall as a slightly different hiss of grit. If you are standing in the passage when it passes, you feel it as a change in the pressure on your eardrums, a softness, as if the altitude had been revised by the thickness of a hand and then restored. Your breath, which this high does not return in full, returns a little more than it should, for one inhale, and then the court is only the court again.

That is as close as it comes to touching you. It does not want the touch. Wanting would be storage.

People have been here, and it knows the shape of their visits without having been one of them, or without remaining one. The pecked niche, the ground cups, the awl set down to be found — these are the grammar it has. A visitor comes for a particular hour of a particular kind of day, grinds a salt, shelters a draft, leaves a point where the water will find it, and goes down before the light changes into the kind of dark this notch does not forgive. The presence keeps the hour. Not the person. Persons leave heat in the stone for a while, a palm-print of warmth on the lip that the quartzite gives back with its usual readiness, gone by the time the shadow arrives. The presence does not keep the warmth. It keeps the interval in which the warmth was almost a reason to stay. That interval is its nearest equivalent to memory, and it revises it the way the mountain revises an edge: honestly, without nostalgia, until what was sharp is only exact.

If you stay past the reasonable hour — past the moment when the drip has stopped thread by thread and the grit has made its small migration and the brightness has gone from white to the color of a blade cooling — you can mistake it for a companion. The mistake is understandable and wrong. In the last light the fins stand closer than they did at noon, shoulder-width becoming the idea of a shoulder, and the false ferns of manganese read, briefly, as attention. Something is attentive. It is not attentive to you. It is attentive to the rate at which the court is becoming the night version of itself, which must be accurate. A stone you set down in the morning will still be there, unless the day has had a particular reason. If you set a stone down now, at the edge of dark, and the day has had no reason, it remains. If some correction is owed — if you set it where a joint needs to relieve, where a draft needs answering, where a cup’s salt-line is about to become a picture — the stone will be elsewhere by the time you have walked the long axis and come back. You will not see the hands. There are no hands. There is the preference, applied.

Over years, which it does not count, it has become more like the place and the place has become more like it, until the distinction is a courtesy for visitors. The dendritic films will outlast the copper. The copper will go to powder and join the grit. The wall will become a line of stones that happen to lie level, and then not even that, except that one stone in the line will lie in answer to a wind that has not blown yet this season, and that answer is how you will know the inhabitant is in the middle of its life. It does not grow. Growth would add a body the altitude would have to feed, and the altitude feeds nothing. It does not die in any way that leaves a remainder. When a correction is finished, the correction is gone, and what remains is stone that looks as if it had always intended this face.

Call it, if you need a word, the maintainer of almost. Not a god of the notch, not a ghost of the people who ground the cups, not a crystal with a mind. A habit the weather has picked up from being repeated exactly, until exactness developed a direction. It lives where water is about to leave, where a mark is about to deepen into a niche someone could call finished, where a wall is about to forget the draft it was built to answer. It turns those about-tos back into themselves. It does not wait. Waiting would mean the middle was somewhere else. The middle is the court, small enough to walk before the light changes, high enough that the walk must be slow, bright and cold at once, divided by the width of a fin. In that division, for as long as the quartzite is willing to be revised by increments too small to watch and too consistent to doubt, something is home.
