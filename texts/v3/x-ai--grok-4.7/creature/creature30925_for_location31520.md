What lives here is not an animal and not a god. It is the wet itself, having nowhere else to go, and having learned the shape of staying.

It does not have a body the way a body is a thing you could walk around. It occupies. It lies in the black inch of the basin and refuses to become a skin. It films the faces the gusts have only just cut, so that a hand—if a hand were here—would come away shining and colder than the stone. It packs itself into the moss until the moss is not a plant holding water but a thickness the water is using. In the thicker minutes, when the gray fails and the walls are known only by what they do to sound, it is also known by what it does to sound: a soft subtraction, a dulling, as if the slot had been lined with cloth no one hung.

It has no center you could point to. Shift your weight and it is under the other foot. Look into the ferns and it is the reason you cannot see through them. It moves only as far as the place allows, which is not far: from the lip-drip to the groove that was not there this morning, from the groove to the basin, from the basin back up the stems by a patience that is not patience, only surface tension and the cool that never quite freezes. When the cloud dumps, it briefly becomes many, hammering, and then it is one again, settled into every pocket, offended by nothing, claiming nothing. Claim would require a boundary it wanted. It wants the boundary it already has. The walls are new and sharp and exactly the right size.

Listen for it and you hear the machine, and then you hear what the machine is wet with. The belt-note of the wind is dry. Under it, and in the pauses when a gust fails and the next has not yet found the new edge, there is a smaller industry: the tick of a drop detaching from an overhang that has not learned to fall, the kiss and unkiss of water against water in the basin the wind will not leave alone, the faint suck as a mat takes what the sheet-flow tried to carry past. These are not signals. Nothing here is trying to be known. They are the sounds of a tenant who pays in damp and whose lease is the crack.

It does not flinch when a plate calves, because flinching would be a direction, and it has already taken every direction the nave contains. The new slope is simply another floor. It sheets down the fresh plane before the grit has finished skittering, finds the pothole that is only beginning to be a pothole, and begins the work of keeping it. In an hour the pothole will still be young and already full. In a day the lip may shed again and the inch may become two, or the wind may drink it back to a film, and either outcome is occupancy. It does not prefer the inch. Preference is for things that can leave.

The static does not belong to it. Blue stitches walk the rim and it takes no notice, being already past the point where charge could lift it. It is too continuous for a spark. What the electrified edges do is remind you, by contrast, how little of the place is dry. The sparks are the exception the rock manages for a second. The wet is the rule that outlasts the second.

Stand long enough and the census the place keeps—zero, for anything with an eye—starts to feel like a courtesy the tenant extends. It does not watch. It does not need the dark to hide in, because it is the reason the dark has texture. When the gray thins and a sideways light finds the film on the wall, you can see it: not a shape, a sheen, the mineral face dulled to a second, softer face. Then the cloud seats itself again and the sheen goes back to being a temperature. Cool on the skin. Damp in the lungs. You are breathing the edge of it. It does not mind. Minding would be a kind of attention, and attention would be a kind of scarcity, and nothing in this slot is scarce except space, which it has already filled to the width of a shout.

It will be here after the next millimeter of uplift, and after the shelf that replaces the shelf, and after the ferns have rooted into their own spill and died of it and rooted again. It will not be the same water. It does not need to be. What lives in the place is the decision, repeated by weather and by stone, that this pocket will not be allowed to dry—that every interruption will be kept, filmed, packed, drummed, and quieted into the mats—until the mountain has made some other room, and the cloud has chosen some other lip, and there is at last an elsewhere. Until then it stays, smaller than the weather, and wetter than the dark.
