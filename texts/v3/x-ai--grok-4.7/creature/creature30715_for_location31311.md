They are easier to hear than to meet, and easier to meet than to count.

At first you think the basin is only occupied the way weather occupies a valley: traces, not tenants. A line with no posts, and cloth on it that is not quite cloth — a pale membrane, drying, that tightens as the damp leaves it and loosens again when the next drift comes. A bowl set under a filament trough, already holding a skim the color of weak tea. Lichen worn through in a line no wider than a careful foot, the young stone bright in the track and dark again a handspan to either side. You can follow that track for an hour and not see who made it. The track does not wander. It knows the shelves that will not open under you, the agreements of roadway that will hold, the hour when a green stand is only insects and not the quieter thing the insects are avoiding.

Then a figure is simply there, the way a change in the wire-note is there before you have decided to call it a sound.

Not hidden. Spacing is their first habit, and hiding would be a kind of crowding. One stands on a bare shelf with the horizon-colored sky behind them and is almost a correction in the rock: taller than you expected only because the light is poor, narrower than a person who eats often, jointed in the ordinary places and then, if you keep looking, in one place that is not ordinary — an extra stillness at the throat, or a shoulder that can turn a fraction past where yours stops, as if the socket were cut for listening rather than for labor. Skin takes the basin’s palette without matching it. Not bark, not stone, not the green-black of the stands. A matte warmth, the color of the dim hour itself, so that when they turn, the turn is what you see, not the body. Eyes are set for this light. They do not shine. They receive. You will not catch a glint and thereby know you have been seen. You will know because the spacing changes: the interval between you and them becomes intentional, held, the way the birds hold their gaps.

They do not travel in bands. A second figure, when there is one, is far enough that you could put a room between them and still be undercounting the distance. They keep that distance the way other peoples keep fire — not from dislike, from a knowledge of what the bowl does to nearness. Two voices at conversational range would braid, amplify, return late and wrong. So they speak into the small air at a mouth, or they do not speak. What passes at a distance is not language in the air. It is a revision of posture, a hand laid flat on stone so the click travels, a pause in walking that another pause, a kilometer off, answers. You can watch a decision cross the open middle of the basin without a word: one figure stops at a roadway’s agreement, another, already on it, steps off, and the thrum that would have doubled does not happen. Courtesy, here, is acoustic.

Up close — and up close is a permission, not a right; they allow it the way the stands allow a crossing, while the roar is full — the strangeness is smaller than the legends you will be tempted to make. They have hands. The hands are sure with the bowls, with the membrane on the line, with the joints that brighten under a palm as if recognized. Nails are short, thick, useful against new rock. Teeth are unremarkable until they eat, and they eat rarely in front of anyone, and not what you would set a table with. The ticking clusters from the green stands are carried back in slings of the same membrane, and opened later, in rooms, and what is inside is not seed and not flesh exactly; it is a counted thing, a density, and they take it the way you take water when you have been listening too long and your mouth has gone to chalk. The warning holds. It is not fruit. It is a crop only in the sense that they have learned the tick-rate that means ready, and the tick-rate that means something else has already been inside.

They sleep in the stacked rooms, never in the interval, and never all at once. A household — if the word survives; they do not pile into a single shelter — is a vertical scatter: one sleeper in a room whose floor is another room’s ceiling, another awake on the skin of the structure with a hand on the stone, counting clicks. The young rock still invents itself. Someone is always on watch for the clustering, the book-torn sound, the fresh face that will exist by morning where a path was. When a ridge decides, they are already elsewhere. You will find the bowl moved, the line re-hung, the worn lichen abandoned in a bright scratch that the next rain-drift will soften and not erase. They do not mourn a site. Sites are sentences. The bowl is the speaker.

What they are, if you insist on a kind, is not a visitor-species laid over a landscape. The landscape has not had time to have visitors. They fit the way the vines fit the filaments: at the joint, hard to assign. Some of their tools are the towers. Not owned — attended. A figure will stand under the slow rotation and lift the face, not in worship, in calibration, and the wire-note will shift by a degree you feel in the teeth, and the trough’s skim will change color. They comb the air because the air is the only reliable field in a place with no soil and no rivers. What the filaments lay down is food for the stands, salt for the body, and a third thing they store in the rooms you cannot enter from here — a residue, pale, kept in vessels that are sometimes stone and sometimes only the agreement that a vessel-shaped volume will not leak. You are not shown the vessels. The rooms that face the interval are for drying, for listening, for the bowl under the overflow. The interior rooms are for what the combing takes that is not meant to fall back into the weather.

They have young. You understand this late, because the young keep the spacing even more strictly, and because they are the ones in the flocks’ grammar: small figures posted far apart along a bare shelf, each with the same matte warmth, learning the gaps. An elder — the word is only age; there are no ornaments of rank you can read — crosses the open and does not gather them. She, or he, or the pronoun the basin would use if it used one, passes within sight and makes a small motion at the throat, that extra stillness opening and closing, and each child answers with the same motion, staggered, so the answers do not land on top of one another. It is a lesson in not becoming a chord. Afterward they resume being almost rock. A lizard the length of a forearm shares a ledge with one of them for a long time. Neither yields. The lizard opens its impossible mouth and the child does not flinch. The sound belongs to the bowl. Flinching would be a misunderstanding.

They are not many. That is the fact that will not sit still. The evidence of living is everywhere — paths, membranes, bowls, brightened joints, the continuous maintenance that looks like the structures loving their own precision — and the bodies are sparse as the towers. You can believe, on a given walk, that you have met the entire people: seven figures, then none, then one at a window that is only an agreement about light, looking at you from nearer than the distance allows, then the agreement lapses and the face is a kilometer of dim air away, still looking. They do not need density to be a city. The city is the spacing plus the upkeep plus the wire-note held in tune. A people can be a basin’s punctuation and still be complete.

There is a predator-knowledge in them, borrowed or original, you cannot tell. They enter the stands only in the roar. They leave before the roar thins. When a hole opens in the insect-sheet, they are already on the roadway, and the roadway thrums once, and the thrum is allowed to circulate — a signal spent into the general deafening, where it becomes weather instead of news. Whatever hunts the edges has learned the same grammar or taught it. You will not see a killing. You will see, once, a figure stop and set a bowl down and not go back for it, and later the bowl is gone, and the lichen around the spot is unbroken, as if nothing with weight had stooped there. They do not discuss this. Distance superstition covers more than talk. Some sentences, even mouthed close, are not worth the risk of the bowl repeating them.

If they have a name for themselves, it does not survive the crossing into your ear. What you can carry out is a practice. Speak only where your breath still owns the words. Walk the bright tracks. Do not camp where the clicks cluster. When you meet one in the interval, do not close the gap to prove friendliness; friendliness is the gap, correctly held. Set your bowl where the overflow is honest. Eat nothing that ticks until someone whose throat-stillness you have learned to read has opened it first. And if a figure lays a palm on the stone and goes still, go still with them. The ridge is about to remember it is young. They have lived inside that memory longer than the stone has been tired, which is to say, since the beginning that has not finished happening, and they are still here, spaced, listening, combing a little of the sky down into bowls, hanging the day’s membrane on a line the eye cannot find, keeping the rooms awake in turns, a people shaped like the place’s one commandment: be numerous in your attention, and almost nowhere in your body, and let the ringing pass through you without becoming a second bell.
