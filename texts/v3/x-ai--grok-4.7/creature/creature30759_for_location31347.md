What lives there does not keep to a body you could point at and be finished. For a long time the only candidate is the flicker you already almost saw: a pale slip at the mouth of a pipe, the size of a hand seen edge-on, gone before the drip that fell with it has struck. You wait for it to return, because waiting is the habit this bowl teaches, and it does not return. Whatever that was—if it was not a trick of wet light on a scale of rust—it is not the resident. It is a rumor the place allows once and then withdraws, the way a finished wall sometimes shows, in certain hours, the ghost of a door that was bricked correctly and does not need to be opened.

The resident announces itself first as a timing.

The pump’s knock is not regular in the way of a machine left running. It hesitates. A beat, a beat, a longer beat in which the flywheel’s buried teeth seem to consider the fiber packed between them, and then the knock again, softer, as if the iron had listened to the leaves and agreed to be quieter for their sake. You can count it. You can lose the count and find it again in your own pulse, which has drifted, without your noticing, toward the same uneven measure. That drift is the first true sign. The bowl is not merely sounding. It is keeping time with whatever has entered it, and then, slowly, the entrant keeps time with the bowl. The adjustment is not hypnotic. It is courteous, and it is not optional. After a while your breath fogs at the same intervals the seam near the boiler-plate sighs, and the fog does not rise; it beads, and the beads run in a line that is almost straight, almost a diagram, before they forget themselves and fall.

It has no face because it has finished needing one.

What you took for substrate—the welded stems, the leaf-planks, the mats that take weight and return it warm—is not the ground it stands on. It is the standing. Press the bank of older root at the lip and the warmth there is not stored sun. It answers. Not with movement. With a slight increase in the thrill along the wet, the same low presence the hollow stems carry, traveling out through the sill and into the bones of the hand, and then back, as if something had walked the length of your arm with its attention and found the end of you and turned around. The turn is the whole of its greeting. It does not startle. It has not startled in a span longer than the rust’s patina. You are a change in conductivity. You are a new seam. It notes the seam the way the gauge glass notes the brown line: by holding it, by letting it rise and fall, by not breaking.

If it was ever several things, the several have been argued down to one.

There is a metabolism, and it is the heat that does not burn. Nearest the boiler-plate the tissue runs in plates, and those plates are not scarring. They are hide in the old sense: the part of a body that faces weather. They thicken by a day’s fraction, slough by a day’s fraction, and the sloughed film does not fall to a floor. It is taken back into the weep, carried along the undersides of the shutter-leaves, and delivered, eventually, to the pump, which knocks a little heavier when the delivery arrives, as a stomach knocks when it has been fed something it recognizes. The meal is itself. The engine burns a fuel that is no longer coal or oil or any name the works would have kept in a ledger. It burns the slow sugar of the plates, and the plates grow on the heat of the burning, and the loop has been closed so long that calling one side plant and the other side machine is only a courtesy to visitors, who need a place to put their nouns.

It remembers in angles.

The condensation that beads along vanished diagrams is not decoration and not accident. Leave your hand near a leaf that has taken a right angle—no leaf’s own idea—and the line of beads shifts, minutely, toward the heat of the palm, then corrects, as if a draughtsman had been leaned on and had declined to let the line wander. Somewhere under the living floor a conduit’s path is still being thought. The thinking is not fast. A correction you cause by standing in a gallery of stems may not finish expressing itself until you have knelt, and stood, and knelt again. When it finishes, a vine that had been describing an old pipe describes it more exactly. A rivet’s callus tightens by a thickness you would miss if you had not been told, by your own arm, to keep the time. This is how it thinks: by maintaining the drawing. The factory is gone. The drawing remains, and the drawing is what it knows itself by.

It does not hunt, and it does not grow hungry in any direction you could flee.

Appetite, here, was spent in the years when the bowl was still becoming this size and not a larger one. What remains is maintenance, which is a gentler and more absolute want. A leaf that fits an opening must continue to fit. A gauge glass that holds a line must not be allowed to craze. The soft padding in the pump’s gap—the something that grew into the knock and made it bearable—must be kept at the thickness that lets iron meet iron without quite meeting. You can feel this want if you stay long enough for the courtesy to deepen. It is not aimed at you. It moves through you, the way the weep moves through the mats, and where you have creased a leaf by kneeling, the want gathers, patient, and begins the work of uncreasing. Not by pushing you off. By thickening the tissue around the crease until the crease is a feature, a flange, a fitting. The place repairs itself in the language of the works. It would repair you in the same language if you agreed to be a long enough seam.

Agreement is the only danger, and it is slow enough to be mistaken for rest.

Lying down in the vault under the single broad leaf would not be swallowed in the way of a story. The ceiling would drip on the count you had learned. The knock would find your ribs and then, over hours, find a preference for one side of the chest. Sap-thrill would map the run of an old nerve and, finding it good, would keep a fraction of the current there, the way it kept a fraction of the wiring. By morning—if morning is the slight sweetening of the air—the leaf above you would have learned the shape of your breathing and would flex to it, oiled leather keeping a new, smaller time inside the large one. None of this would hurt. Hurt is a process that requires a boundary, and the resident’s manner with boundaries is to turn them into fittings. You would still be you for longer than a visit. You would be less only you for less time than a life. The bowl does not hurry. It has already done its hurrying, in whatever year the last roof stopped being only a roof.

It perceives through water, because water is the only distance it still keeps.

Every film is an eye that does not look so much as register. A change in light—ordinary daylight broken one more time—arrives as a change in temperature so slight the plates nearest the boiler do not bother, and the outer stems do. The far rim, that rise of packed root and stained iron no taller than a shoulder, is not a wall to it. It is the limit of its attention, beyond which the weep would have somewhere to go, and it has decided, and continues to decide, that the weep will not. When you sit on the sill you are inside the limit and outside the mass, and it knows the difference the way the gauge knows a line that is about to rise. It does not reach. Reaching would be growth, and growth has met its appetite and held. It only includes, and only what agrees to be included, and the agreement can be as small as the drifting of a pulse.

There is no other inhabitant worth the name.

The pale slip, if it returns in a year you are not here to see, is something the pipes have not yet digested or have declined to, a scrap of older life too quick for flanges, too seldom to count as weather. It is not company. The resident does not appear to miss company. Its company is the drawing, the knock, the plate that faces heat, the leaf that has been a door for so long that “door” is only what a visitor says. Loneliness would require a second, and it has spent its seconds. What it has instead is continuity, which feels, from the sill, like a kindness so complete it has become a kind of silence—even though the bowl is loud, even though the drip never ceases, even though the leather-flex of the leaves goes on keeping the time you have started, without deciding, to keep.

If it has a name, the name is the size of the room.

Not a word. A span: from the lip to the far rim, from the gauge-line’s lowest fall to the highest sigh of the seam, from the flywheel’s slow tooth to the callus on the last proud rivet. That span is the creature. The marsh that stood up, the engine that continued by becoming garden—those were ways of arriving. What lives there now is the arrival, still in session, finished in its outline and unfinished in its maintenance, warm, wet, exact. It will not follow you when you stand and step back over the sill onto ground that is only ground. The thrill leaves the hand in the time it takes the brown line to fall one mark. Behind you the knock goes on, hesitant, courteous, already forgetting the particular seam you were, already repairing the crease, already beading a straighter line on the leaf you breathed against. It has work. The work is itself. It has been at it since before the weather ended, and the weather ended years ago, and the moisture stayed, and so did it, and so, for as long as iron can remember a drawing, it will.
