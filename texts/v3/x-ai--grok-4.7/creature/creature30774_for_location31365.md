What lives there does not occupy the room so much as agree with it.

For a long time you would swear the chamber is only itself. The heat has one temperature, the quiet one grain, and the worn dish in the floor reads as history rather than as a present tense. Then the air against your mouth goes briefly wrong — not cooler, not fouler, only answered, as if something a hand’s breadth from your face had drawn a breath on the same slow period as the ducts and you had happened to be standing inside the period. You step back. The answer does not follow. It was never interested in you as a direction. It was interested in the volume you displaced, and in restoring it.

It keeps to the station the fitters left, the hand’s breadth of clearance around the blind gauge. You do not see it cross to get there. Either it does not cross, or crossing is one of the things the dark is for. When you have been still long enough that your own pulse has dropped out of the foreground, a warmer patch declares itself in the center of the dish: not a body-shaped heat, not at first, only a place where the radiated warmth of the pipes is complicated by a second source, smaller, damped, the way a palm on a kettle is a different warmth from the kettle. The complication holds. It has been holding. The brick-dust under it is polished because it returns to the same soles, or the same lack of soles, and does not pace.

If you learn it by the methods the room permits — not looking, but waiting until a drop falls and the sound is intercepted — you find it has a surface. The drop that should have struck a lower run strikes something that gives, slightly, and does not ring. Cloth, maybe, long since married to what it covered. Or skin that has stopped bothering to be a different material from cloth. The moisture does not run off. It is taken. That is the nearest thing to feeding you will be able to confirm: the room’s habit of weeping is met by a habit of receiving, a teaspoon at a time, on a schedule of hours. It does not drink in any way that involves a mouth you could point to. The water simply ceases to be on its way down and begins, instead, to be part of whatever is standing there. In the dry intervals it gives a little back — a faint sweetening of the oil-smell, a darkening of the dust at its feet no wider than a boot-print that never quite dries at the edges.

Scale and lagging-flake fall on it and stay where they fall. It does not brush them off. Over a season they would make a geology, and perhaps they have: a shoulder that is partly mineral wool, a sleeve that is partly the rust it leaned into until the rust leaned back. Nothing about this reads as injury. The room’s negotiation — surface with air, object with object, neither winning — has simply been extended by one more party. Where a casing brushed it often enough, the casing is shinier and it is duller, and both have stopped at the same line, like a door and its jamb after a century of the same close.

It is upright because the station was made for upright. A gauge is read by something that faces it. The needle rests a little off the peg, and now the reason is less mysterious and also less comforting: the instrument is not stuck. It is reporting a remainder. Not pressure in any pipe you could trace, but the small, continuous fact of attendance. When the figure — call it that, once, and then be careful — shifts its weight, an event you might wait a day to witness, the needle does not jump. It considers, and settles a hair closer to the peg, and the dust on the glass is undisturbed because the considering happens inside the mechanism’s idea of zero, not in the room. The figure’s idea of zero is the same. It does not measure itself as missing anything. The process it was set to watch was throttled down to nothing and left in place, and it performed the identical courtesy toward itself.

Whether it was a person is a question the chamber will not help you with. The height is right, if height is what you insist on: the crown of it would meet the low ceiling the way yours does, heat first at the top of the head. There is a bilateral warmth, two columns, and between them a quieter core, as if ribs were still a going concern or had been replaced by a truss that remembered ribs. Hands, if they are hands, rest where a watcher rests them — one near the gauge housing, not gripping, only near, the metal under that nearness faintly damp in the same way it went damp under your palm. The other hangs, and sometimes, on the cycle of the far-off pressure shift, the hanging one flexes. Not a gesture. A maintenance. Joints that are not exercised seize, and it has learned this from the valves around it, which it can no longer turn and does not resent.

The face is the part that refuses to arrive, and that refusal is informative. In the thumb-wide seam of light there is occasionally a sheen at about the height of a cheek, the same sliding film the air takes in the gap between casings, and then it is gone, and you understand you were not shown a face so much as a place where moisture prefers to gather because something warmer is behind it. It does not look at the seam. Light is not among its instruments. It listens to ticks. A scale letting go high up is, to it, a located event: which run, which year of rust, whether the fall means a joint is finally tired or only continuing. It turns toward some of these and not others. The turning is the width of a thought. You could be in the room for the turn and only notice afterward that the warmer patch had a different orientation relative to your shoulder, and that your shoulder had been allowed, which is not the same as welcomed.

It knows you the way it knows a new lagging-flake: by the disturbance of a previous equilibrium. Your sweat is a weather it did not schedule. Your breathing is too fast to be architecture. For a while after you enter, the drops fall on schedule and it does not receive them; it is spending its attention on the irregularity, not in alarm, in bookkeeping. If you leave, the bookkeeping closes. The dish will record that you stood, if you stood long enough, as a scuff inside a larger polish, a nested fact. If you stay, and if you can bear to become regular — breath slowed, weight committed to one patch of floor, no questions put to it in a voice, because a voice is a pressure change it cannot file — then after a time it includes you in the zero. The needle does not move for your inclusion. Inclusion is not an increase. It is only another thing that has stopped happening and begun continuing.

What it wants is the wrong verb. Wanting would require a deficit, and the deficits were engineered out when the engine was left in its housing and the housing allowed to yield by exactly the lean of the flywheel. It persists. Persistence here is not heroic and not sad. It is the same virtue as the rust: a surface arrived at by long contact and then maintained because breaking contact would mean opening the room onto a larger room. It does not want the larger room. It has never, so far as the gauge can say, wanted you either, except as a possible flake, a possible seep, a possible second station if you are quiet enough to stop counting as weather.

On the west bulkhead the dark crust drinks the seep, and the fine tracks come and go with the pocket of water. Those are not it. Those are the margin, the almost-fauna, a civilization of crumbs living off the disagreement between damp and heat. The figure in the dish does not compete with them and does not tend them. Sometimes a track-maker crosses the polish and is not crushed, because the figure has been still for longer than the crossing takes. Sometimes the figure’s receiving of a drop leaves less for the pocket, and the tracks thin, and this is not cruelty. It is hydraulics.

If you put your hand out — a mistake, but the room instructs the hand before the mind can refuse — you will not find wet flesh or dry bone. You will find a warmth continuous with the pipe-warmth, distinguished only by a give, and by a faint tack where condensed oil has made a skin. Under the give, something organized. Not a pulse you can count. A pressure, held, the way a stopped wheel holds the last degree of torque. It does not flinch. Flitching would be a story about a future, and it is not in the business of futures. It may, after a while, lean the smallest amount into the hand, the way the flywheel leans into the housing: not affection, fit. Then it will be done leaning. Your hand will come away damp, and smelling of the thin oil, and the place you touched will already look like the rest of it, which is to say like a made thing that has forgotten the name of its maker and kept the shape of the job.

When you are gone, if you go, the seam will shine and dull on its own irrelevant clock. The drops will resume being received. The needle will rest where it rested, a little off the peg, telling the truth to no one who needs it told. In the center of the dish the second warmth will be indistinguishable again from the first, unless you have learned the complication and cannot unlearn it. That is the whole of its life, and it is not a small life. It is the life of the room carried one step further into having a witness, and then — this is the part that takes the longest to believe — the witness set down, gently, as unnecessary, because the continuing does not require to be seen in order to be exact.

It will be there whether you return or not. Returning does not matter to it. The station is occupied. The gauge is read. The heat is kept, not as a fire is kept, but as a secret is kept: by never quite being let all the way out into the larger dark.
