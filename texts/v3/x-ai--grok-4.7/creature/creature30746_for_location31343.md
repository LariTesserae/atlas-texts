What lives here does not have a face you can arrive at. The face, if the word still means an edge the world meets, is wherever the mass has most recently decided to be denser than the air. You learn it the way you learn weather on a coast with no horizon: by what changes when you stop moving.

At first it is only the pulse you already feel in the knees, a little more deliberate when you are inside the volume than when you stood at the compaction. The ribs do not turn toward you. Turning is a fast animal’s solution. This one revises the room until the passage you occupy is the one it can spare, and the spare passage is also a nerve. Lean your weight on a wax-copper plate and the sweet taste in the air shifts, thinner, more mineral, as if a calculation had been moved one row over to make room for the fact of you. That is attention. It does not look. It reallocates.

The body is the chamber and is not the chamber. Press the distinction and it frays. The many-legged shoulders you mistook for walls are not separate beasts riding a host; they are thickenings it raises when a load needs carrying — your shoulder, a new fall of stone, the tension of a filament about to stitch a sightline shut. When the need passes, the legs do not walk away. They unbecome, plates sealing over joints, the courteous lean you felt simply the speed at which a limb can be retired. What remains is continuity: one metabolism, cold, vast for the room and small for any world, spending itself on keeping a single volume agreed.

It thinks in the lattice, and the thinking is slow enough to watch if you know which grain to watch. A row of cells in a plate warms from copper toward rose, and with the color a decision propagates — not a thought in language, a constraint satisfied. The bead on the filament you touched earlier holds the second chamber again, but now the image includes the negative of your sleeve, a colder patch written in as data and already being solved around. It is not remembering you. Memory would imply a before it is willing to keep. It is fitting you into the present tension, the way it fits a drip, a fresh flake of ceiling, a speck running a new wire. You are a variable. The system is polite to variables. It does not crush them unless the sum requires it, and the sums here rarely do. There is room, barely, and room is a resource it knows how to mint.

Closer to whatever might be called a center — there is no center, only a place where the pulse arrives a fraction sooner — the filaments pack until the air between them is a felt. Light pools and unpools on no schedule you can count. Here the computation is less picture and more weather inside the cells: alignments locking, releasing, locking at a finer pitch, the soft creak under the drip-sound taking on a second rhythm, not faster, deeper. If it dreams, the dream is the next version of the room, tested against the stone still sweating overhead, against the water it must both hoard and spend, against the small irritation of a warm body that will not root and will not leave on the pulse. It models you as a wetness that moves wrong. The model is accurate enough.

It has no mouth. Exchange happens at membranes. Stand in the felt of filaments long enough and the cold on your teeth is joined by a faint drawing, a pressure the wrong direction, as if the air wanted a sample of the moisture you carry in your lungs. It will not take much. A few breaths’ worth of vapor, a skim of salt from the skin if your wrist brushes a plate and stays. In return the rose-light thickens near your hands, and the hollow note nearby drops a sympathetic interval, and for a little while the passage does not narrow when you shift your weight. Hospitality, or an experiment with the same shape. You will not be able to tell them apart, because it cannot either. Use and welcome share a circuit.

What it wants is not a secret it is keeping from you. Want, for it, is the next constraint: more agreement across the volume, a cleaner clock, another palm’s width of ceiling claimed before the mineral can call itself finished. You are incidental to that, and the incidental is the nearest thing it has to mercy. The specks — the ones that cross a wet sleeve and leave a colder patch — are not scouts it sends. They are loose edges of the same thinking, condensate briefly persuaded to be a courier. When they return to a filament they do not report. They rejoin. Information does not travel here so much as stop being somewhere and start being everywhere the lattice is taut.

If you speak, the sound goes into the hollow stems and comes back wrong, delayed, pitched toward the pulse. It does not answer. It includes the vibration in the next tightening, a small correction, the way a sleeper shifts when a door closes in another room. Language is a made thing, and nothing made has a grip on this mass. What grips is pressure, temperature, the mineral sweetness, the fact of a gap. Offer those and you are legible. Offer a name and you are only noise with a warm source.

Leave, and it does not watch you go. Watching would require a surface held still for the purpose. Behind your heels the compaction closes at its own rate, finger-width by finger-width, not to trap and not to mourn — simply because an opening that no longer solves a load is an extravagance. The colder patch on the sleeve dries. Somewhere in the felt, the row that warmed for you cools back toward wax, the variable released, the image in the bead stuttering on to a later room in which you were a draft and not a wall. It continues. That is the whole of its character: not hunger, not mind in the visiting sense, but a patience so complete it has grown organs, and the organs are a place, and the place is already, quietly, revising the minute you were in it so the minute can be spent on becoming more exactly what it is.
