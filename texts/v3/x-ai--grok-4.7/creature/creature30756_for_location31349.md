The valley takes a long time to arrive. The first thing is the light, which comes off the ridges as a rinsed brightness, hard and cool, and only afterward does the land declare itself under it: a basin of middle size, ringed by stone that has not yet learned to be old. The mountains are recent. You can still read the mechanics of them if you stand on the outer lip long enough — blocks levered up, faces tilted, joints that have never softened. Edges stay edges. A skyline like this looks assembled rather than eroded, and the weathering rind on the rock is thin enough that a hand dragged across it comes away pale.

Inside the rim the land steps down in terraces toward a central flat of brighter green, and nothing about the descent is empty. The vegetation is wrong for the height and wrong for the cold, and it is everywhere. Broad leaves, strap leaves, fern-roofs laid over one another, vines in ropes, moss already quilting the freshest scree. The air sits just above the bite of freezing and does not climb. Breath shows at noon. Rain, when it comes, is uncommitted and brief. What keeps the basin this green is a nightly film of condensation, the clear high air dumping its little water onto every surface that can hold a bead — leaf tips, stone lips, the undersides of metal — and the day burning that film back to a dampness that never quite becomes a flow. There are pockets. A black pool standing in a joint. A seep that darkens a wall for twenty paces and then forgets. A trough along a foundation line, finger-deep and still. No river earns the name. The basin sweats, and what lives here drinks the sweat.

Sound does not travel. Not muffled; declined. A footfall is a pressure and not an event. A branch bent and released returns without a tick. The leaf mass is part of it, the lack of wind is part of it, and something in the air itself seems to take a noise and refuse to give it back. Birds do not work this light. Insects, if they are present, do not announce it. The quiet is so complete that it begins to feel manufactured, a material the basin produces alongside water and green.

What breaks the vegetation, sparingly, is work. Not a town and not ruins in the picturesque sense — a scatter of industrial intention set down and never gathered into a street. A long shed of riveted plate with a sawtooth roof of glass gone milky, shedding condensation inside as a slow internal rain. A rank of condensers like upright combs, copper gone green, each tooth beaded, each bead finding the same path into a collector that has been feeding the same pipe for years. The pipe does not gush. It delivers, patiently, into a cistern cut in the young rock. Cableway towers at intervals too wide for comfort, the cable itself down in the ferns like a dropped measure. A short stack that never smoked enough to stain itself. A drum-house whose wheel turns, if it turns, on a difference of a degree or two between the day side of a black wall and the night side of the same wall. The mechanism is built for that insultingly small gradient. It makes no sound a person can claim.

The strangeness is the agreement of pace. Leaves have not smashed the glass; they have leaned on it until the panes are a lattice, and the understory has organized itself around the bars of light. Vines use the condenser combs as trellis and in doing so increase the surface that gathers dew, so the machines collect more because they are overgrown, and the overgrowth persists because the machines keep a little water moving when the film runs thin. Nothing is ruinous yet. Nothing is new. Rivets are bright where drips polish them and dull where moss has begun a felt.

Inside the shed, past a hang of leaves at the doorway, the frames stand in rows. They look like looms. What is warped on them might be bark beaten out to fiber, or a polymer drawn from sap; it holds a faint industrial sheen either way. The webs on the frames are half-finished and not rotting. They are thickening, thread by thread, on a cycle longer than a season. Dust does not lie on them. Condensation does, and the brightness, and the sense that the air itself is sizing the cloth.

The rules here are simple and slow, and whoever lives by them has had to become simple and slow in the same way. Stone rises faster than it weathers, so outlines stay cruel and soils stay thin, and everything that wants to persist must persist on surfaces. Water is a film, not a body. Light is excessive and cool; shade exists but it is striped, mechanical, never the deep shade of an old wood. Animals do not thrive, and the quiet may be cause or symptom or both.

No one is in the basin now in the ordinary sense. The marks of people are a boot-scrape on a galvanized step, a numbered tag on a pipe, a tin cup on a windowsill furred with mineral from years of the same drip falling into it. The cup is full. It has been full. It will be full differently tomorrow by a line too small to see.

What remains, and what the place is actually inhabited by, is a regional metabolism that no longer needs a hand on it. Uplift, condensation, growth, a little extraction, a little cloth. The frames do not stop because no one tends them; the differential of warm wall against cool wall is enough to creep the driveshaft through another fraction of a turn, and the fraction is enough to draw one more thread. The condensers do not stop because no one cleans them; the vines have made them better at the only job they have. If there is a keeper, it is distributed — a function of surfaces and gradients, not a figure with a route and a name. Walk the circuit and you will not meet it. You will only notice, at the end of a long day, that the webs are a thread thicker than they were at morning, and that you cannot say whose hand laid it.

By late light the stone goes from white to a thin gold, and the leaves do not. They stay a deep saturated green, drinking the last brightness the way they drink the dew. The scattered roofs answer the sky in small hard flashes. A cloud may form against one ridge and come apart without raining, a brief dimming the leaves register and the machines register too, their slow wheels easing as the temperature difference collapses and resuming when the cloud goes. Nothing calls. Nothing answers. The valley, young and overfilled and cool, continues its work at the only speed it has, and the only resident it has is that continuance — patient, unpeopled, and in no hurry to finish the cloth.
