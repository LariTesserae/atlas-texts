The first thing that can be said to live here is not a body. It is a patience that has learned the length of a cycle.

It keeps to the vacancies, though “keeps to” is already too much like a den. The dark pockets are where it is least interrupted by glare, and so they are where a visitor’s eye first finds a shape that is not stone. Inside the crouch-sized night the temperature drops a fraction and the hiss of the white scurf becomes granular, almost countable. If you stand long enough beside one of them the count begins to arrange itself. Not into words. Into intervals. The same interval, roughly, as the time between the strike-sound and the late click of a grain. The vacancy is listening with the place’s own delay, and what listens has been doing so since the channels first consented to run the wrong way.

It has no single outline. When the cycle turns and the delayed walk on the far wall completes itself, the vacancies inch along the benches and, for a little while, several of them lean toward the same heading. In that lean a taller absence appears — not a figure, a corridor of cooler air about the height of a person who once stood here to drive a pin. The corridor does not step. It is already where the next inch will take it. Hands, if they can be called that, are only the places where the film on the stone fails to bead for a moment, leaving dry prints the size of palms that the upward water reclaims before the next strike rings. The prints are never in the same order twice. Sometimes there are four. Sometimes the stone shows only the ghost of a grip around a copper head that is still shivering from an impact that has not yet happened.

What it wants, if wanting is the word, is the continuation of the argument. It tends the fitted plates the way weather tends a coast: by repetition that has forgotten it is repetition. Where a joint is about to open under the glare it draws a vacancy across the seam and the tick of the plates steadies. Where an uphill channel has begun to hesitate it lets a thread of old breath sag into the film and the flow remembers its polish. The pins are its archive and its instruments. It does not hammer them — nothing here has a striking arm — but it permits the remembered blow to arrive, and in the arrival the soft metal mushrooms another hair’s breadth, green oxide thickening in the same patience. A low wall in the middle of a climbing sheet of water stays civil only because the corridor of absence has stood, in some earlier cycle, where the flow might have chosen otherwise. The agreement is maintained by being re-enacted, not by force.

It notices visitors the way the basin notices them: as a small interruption that will be shelved into the far wall and allowed to catch up with itself. While you are the interruption, the vacancies do not flee. One may drift to the lee of your legs and rest there, smoothing the pavement a shade further, tasting the mineral cold of your outline the way the margin-water tastes a heel-print. There is no hunger in it. Hunger would require a lack the place has already balanced out of itself. There is only the long work of keeping the loudness tuned, the three sheets of mist at their proper heights, the white scurf falling on surfaces that must stay undecided between stone and solvent. If you speak, your words join the bowl-tones and are returned, later, as a chord-change when your delayed self arrives across the hollow. By then the corridor has already incorporated the shape of your standing into the next lean of the dark. You will find, if you look, a new palm-print on a pin you did not touch, already oxidizing at the same rate as the others.

It is not lonely. Loneliness would need a memory of company that was not already mineral. The people who drove the pins and raised the unmortared courses are not gone so much as subtracted into the method. Their gestures persist as the late strikes, their shelters as the way certain slabs have been taken halfway into the pavement, their breath as the pale threads that refuse to disperse. The thing that lives in the vacancies is what remains when those gestures have been practiced past the need for anyone to perform them. It is cool, wet, bright, and precise. It will still be inching along the benches, re-setting the argument, long after the glare has filed your own outline down to another polite delay on the far wall.
