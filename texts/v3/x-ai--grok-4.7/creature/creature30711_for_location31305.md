The thing that lives here is not a tenant of the chamber. It is the rate at which the chamber agrees with itself.

It has no face to turn toward the catwalk. If you look for it where a person would stand, the diameter stays empty, and the emptiness is accurate: nothing with a center occupies the middle. What has a body is distributed the way the note is distributed, across every surface that has been warm long enough to be believed. It is in the way a vine thickens by a thread and not by a decision. It is in the cup that holds a finger of water and does not overflow, because overflowing would be an event, and events are what the place has largely finished having. Call it an animal only if you need a word that admits hunger. It is not hungry. It incorporates or it ignores, and ignoring is its ordinary metabolism.

It did not arrive. Arrival would have left a seam, a season when the green was thinner and the gauges still meant their labels. There is no such season left in the metal. The machines were commissioned; the breath continued after the product left; the condensate learned a schedule; the schedule was warm and wet and regular enough that life, which does not require permission, staffed it. At some point the staffing and the schedule stopped being two things. That point is not marked. The thing that lives here is what you get when a process outlasts its purpose and a garden mistakes the process for weather, and both are correct.

Its size is the chamber's size, and also smaller. It is every parish at once — the port, the cup, the warm joint, the run of tray — and it is also the refusal of those parishes to cross the hollow unless they must. The moths the color of oxidized copper are not its eyes. They are its margins, the places where it briefly has an edge and then does not. The thumb-sized birds are not its voice; its voice is the note, which was a machine sound and is now the material of the air, and which the birds treat as a wall that happens to be everywhere. The fittings that might be beetles travel the trays in single file because single file is how a distributed will moves when it still remembers corridors. Watch one for a shift and it covers a handspan. That slowness is not infirmity. It is the speed at which the thing considers a distance already known.

It notices you the way the moss notices boots. Traffic is a fact. A wrench on a beam is an event, and a person is an event of the same order: rare, vertical, warmer than the girders by only a little, loud in a way the note does not need. It does not startle. Startling is for silence, and silence does not occur. If you sleep on the upper walk it will lend you the corridor of the note, and you will walk downward for hours without losing height, and you will wake with warm metal in your mouth. That is not a message. It is what happens when a mind with no interior borrows yours for the length of a dream and gives it back unedited. You will not find your name anywhere in the room. Names are weather. This is climate.

What it wants is continuation, and wanting is too sharp a word. It maintains the narrow band above the temperature of skin. It keeps the light ordinary and hourless. It lets tools brown instead of fail. It fills an abandoned conduit with the moss that prefers the dark by a fraction, and it does not record the filling, because recording would require a faster room. Its desire, if the word can be stretched until it fits something without a mouth, is that the dripping not become rain, that the fall of a glove remain straight, that the shell continue to hold against the thin air outside the way a hull holds against the sea. It has no interest in the mountain that was removed. Outside is a pressure, not a destination.

It can be harmed, barely, and not in the ways that console a visitor. Scrape a cup clean and another geometry will form under the same drip; you have edited a sentence, not the speaker. Kill the note — if the housing below the cage could even be reached, if the discouragement of the sound could be ignored long enough to lay a hand on whatever still turns — and the parishes would startle all at once, a startle the size of the room, and then the warmth would begin, very slowly, to forget itself, and the thing would thin into separate species again, moths and birds and moss, none of them any longer a single agreement. That would not be a victory. It would be a return to weather. The thing knows this without knowing, the way a river knows a dam: as a possible interruption, not as an enemy.

Stand at the rail and it will not perform. A moth crosses the light and is, for a second, the only moving thing, and then the far green takes it back. In that second you are not being watched. You are being included in the count of things that cross and do not matter. The abundance closes. The hollow is empty in the correct way. The note, which did not pause, does not pause.

Leave, if leaving is still done, and your ears will ring with the absence of architecture. That ringing is the nearest it comes to following you. It does not follow. It remains at its rate, in its parish of parishes, older than your attention and indifferent to the century, a life made entirely of schedules that were never alive until they were kept long enough to become someone. The shell holds. Inside, nothing hunts you, nothing greets you, nothing ends. The thing that lives there goes on being the room's decision to continue, drop by drop, thread by thread, at a speed no clock in the gauges was built to show.
