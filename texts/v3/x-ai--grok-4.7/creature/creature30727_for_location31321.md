They do not arrive. That is the first thing that becomes clear if you watch long enough for watching to turn into a kind of consent. Nothing in the chamber is traveling toward anywhere. The lives packed against the housings and into the dust-veils were not colonists that found a niche and multiplied into it. They are the niche, held at the density the room will bear, and the density does not ask to be explained by a before.

Closest to the engine, the jointed bodies are not a colony in the way a hive is a colony. Each one is complete. Translucent enough that the piston’s knock shows through them as a pulse of shadow in a pale interior, they grip the casing with hooked feet no larger than seeds, arranged so that no two share a line of stress. Their mosaics look ornamental until you notice the rule: every body is placed where the vibration is the same amplitude as its neighbor’s. They are tuned, not piled. When the flywheel’s constancy throws a slight harmonic—once, perhaps, though “once” is a word the room does not honor—they flex in a wave that starts at the weld and dies at the far flange, and then they are still again, each returned to the angle it already occupied. They have no eyes you can find. They have a seam along the belly that opens on the heat and closes on nothing. Whatever they take from the casing, they take without diminishing it. The metal does not pit under them. Their feet leave prints that are the same depth as the metal’s own grain, as if contact and surface had agreed on a single roughness and then stopped negotiating.

Lower, in the veils, the motes are harder to grant the status of animals. At first they are only the grain of the air, a brightness that beats. Stay, and the beating resolves into individuals: membranes the size of a fingernail paring, ribbed, dry, hinged to bodies too small to hold a story. They do not fly in the sense of crossing the chamber. Each one holds a station inside its veil and strokes the air at the bearing’s pitch, so that the ringing and the wing-rate are not accompaniment but the same fact heard twice. If you pass a hand through a veil the motes part around the heat of your skin and re-form the layer behind you, not hurriedly. Hurry would imply a disturbance they recognized as temporary. They treat your hand as a new fixed object, route around it, and resume. Their persistence is not courage. It is the absence of any other mode.

The coin-animals along the fractures are the ones that almost seem to know you are there, and that almost is the most unsettling courtesy in the room. Flat, overlapping, edge to edge, they face the heat with pores open. When you lean close, the nearest rank tilts by a degree—not toward you, not away, but so that their pores remain square to the warmth and their edges remain locked to their neighbors. The tilt is a correction in service of the mosaic, the same correction the jointed bodies make. Touch one and it is warm, dry, slightly flexible at the rim and glassy at the center, like a scale that cooled while still alive and then did not need to choose. They click when plates meet. The click is not a signal passed along the rank. It is the sound of contact maintained. Run your eye the length of a fracture and the clicks do not travel. They occur everywhere the edges touch, simultaneously, at the rate the room has chosen, which is the rate it has always had.

None of them feed on one another. That absence takes longer to believe than the stillness, because abundance usually means a ladder, something eating the dust, something eating that, a body at the top whose stillness is vigilance. Here the ladder is horizontal. Heat, vibration, mineral film, the precise distance to the next body: those are the foods, and they are not consumed. The film on the plates does not thin under the coins. The casing does not cool under the mosaic. The air’s sweetness near the vents does not sour. If there is metabolism, it is a loop so short it never becomes a debt. They live as a standing wave lives—by fitting a frequency that is already present, and by not introducing a new one.

The plant beside the ladder is the outlier only in height. Knee-high, branched like a hand that closed on fire and kept the shape, it is not reaching. Its glassy tips are not buds. They are the place where growth arrived at the same temperature as the air and could not tell itself from the room, so it vitrified at the boundary and called that a leaf. Fibers in the crusts higher up are the same argument made smaller: a tissue that began to organize and then found organization was a change, and change is the one resource the chamber does not stock. They persist as crust because crust is a stable word. Green would have been a season.

For a while it is possible to think the machines are the true residents and the bodies are only frost. The flywheel, the piston, the press holding its unfinished shape—these have the look of intention, and intention is a kind of life. But intention wants an end, and the press does not close. The cables do not deliver a load you can measure beyond the fact of their tension. The ladder stops because three rungs are the number of rungs, not because a worker was interrupted. The machines are mid-task in the way the animals are mid-breath: the middle has become the whole. What lives in them is not a ghost of an operator. It is the continuation itself, wearing iron so that continuation can have a temperature.

What lives among the bodies is quieter and more exact. There is no single creature large enough to be the genius of the place, no eye in the far wall, no voice in the chord. The inhabitant is the agreement. Every jointed body, every mote, every coin, the vitrified hand, the crusts, even the way your own breath is refused an echo—each is a local clause in a sentence that does not need a speaker because it is already true. They are not waiting. Waiting would open a gap between this instant and the next, and the gap is what the chamber has been built, or torn, or cooled, in order to refuse. They occupy. Occupation here is not a siege and not a home. It is the condition of being so precisely fitted to a warmth, a dryness, a knock, a ring, that leaving would not be death so much as a grammatical error, a word removed from a sentence that would then no longer parse.

If one of them could be said to notice you, it would be only as a pressure, a slight new constant to be routed around until you, too, either fit or go back through the black angle at the lip. They will not follow. The boundary holds for them as it holds for moisture and night: not as a fear, but as the edge of the frequency they are. Inside that edge they are many, crowded, loud only because the room is loud, dry, warm, and unfinished in the manner of a bell that was struck and discovered the strike could be made permanent. They do not breed toward a larger future. They do not dwindle toward a smaller one. They are the population of a single held instant, repeated without remainder, and they are, in that repetition, entirely alive—alive the way a chord is alive when every note has found the others and none of them intends to resolve.
