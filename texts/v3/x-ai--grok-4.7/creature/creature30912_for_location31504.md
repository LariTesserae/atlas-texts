It does not cross the light, and so the hollow looks empty. That is the first true thing about it, and it remains true even after you have been standing long enough for your own print at the pool’s edge to fill and forget you.

What lives here keeps to the thickness just off the stone. At first it is only the same faint breath the walls already make: a slow whitening a hand’s width from the wet face, visible when you look along the seep and gone when you look at it. Stay, and the whitening acquires a preference. It thickens where the drop falls. It thins where the rectangle of noon lies hardest on the floor. It does not drift. Drift would be weather. This gathers, and the gathering has a direction, always back toward the crack no wider than a finger, always back toward the open flange and the sleeve where the pipe enters the west wall.

It has no outline you could trace and then remember. Edges fail it. If you insist on a body, the nearest honesty is a sleeve of warmer water held inside the ordinary water, a second skin inside the skin on the wall, the same tea-color as the pool and a degree more alive in the way it refuses to sheet downward at the same speed as the rest. Where the mineral crust feathers a ledge, this warmer thread slows, spreads under the shelf, and stays. The shelf is not shelter it found. The shelf is something it keeps. The white rime is its patience made visible, a little architecture of refusal: do not dry, do not fall all the way, do not give the heat back to the air all at once.

The moss knows it. That coin of green does not spread because the thread under the fracture drinks the spoon of water down to a film and then, with a reluctance that is almost manners, lets a portion return. The green lives on the remainder. In that exchange the hollow’s one plant is not a neighbor so much as a ledger. Too much taken, and the moss would brown by evening. Too little, and the thread would have to cross open stone to reach the pool, and it will not do that while the light is a hard rectangle. It has learned the noon already, though the cut is young. Learning, for it, is a change in where it consents to be thin.

Sound is not background to it. The pump’s stroke arrives through the sleeve as a pressure, and the pressure is how it keeps time. Each low turn of the unseen machine leans the warmer thread a fraction toward the pipe, then releases it. You can watch this if you stop trying to see a creature and watch the drip instead. The drops do not fall at random. They queue themselves to the stroke. One, then the machine’s inhale, then another, slightly heavier, as if something in the flange had drawn the water up along the bright curve and changed its mind at the lip. The tick that climbs the wall and comes back thinner is partly the hollow’s acoustics and partly a small retrieval: not all of the drop reaches the plate of new stone. A measure of it climbs again, against sense, along a path too narrow to glint, and vanishes into the gasket’s black curl.

That is as close as it comes to a mouth. The curl is where the warmth enters the pipe’s orange and the orange enters the warmth. It is feeding on the long mistake of the metal, on the heat the factory spends and does not miss, on iron beginning to be something else. The bolt in the pool is not an offering and not ignored. Overnight the water around it is a shade darker, and the thread has been there without disturbing the surface, occupying the bolt the way it occupies the shelf, using the iron as a slow wick. By the time you see the bolt it has already been tasted and set back down in exactly the place the job left it. It is careful with objects. Objects are how the hollow will be read by the next person who climbs down, and it seems to prefer the reading that says: nothing lives here.

It is not old. That is the part that takes longest to accept, because its patience feels inherited. The habit is older than the cut; the tenant is not. When the bucket or the blade opened this room, water that had been moving in the dark under the fill found a new mouth and came out warm, and with the water came the habit of holding heat against stone. What lives here is that arrival still unfinished. It has not yet decided whether the pocket is a room or only a pause in a longer seep. The pump argues for staying. The pump is a climate that does not sleep, a promise that the pipe will keep arriving with effort, with metal, with a rhythm that can be worn as a pulse. So it stays, and the staying is the whole of its ambition. It does not hunt. Nothing here is prey. It does not call. The loudness is already occupied. It does not mark the soft margin, because marking would be a claim made in the language of boots and wings, and it has no interest in being answered in that language.

If you hold your palm near the weeping face, it notices as weather. Your skin is a small cold, a theft of the tightness the heat wants. The thread withdraws under the crust, not in fear, in bookkeeping. A minute later the steam-thick air has repaired itself and the drops have returned to the pump’s count. Should you block the sleeve with a shoulder, curious, the stroke in the ear muffles and the whole wall seems to hesitate, the film going abruptly ordinary, the moss’s spoon trembling as the retrieval stops. It can be interrupted. It cannot be cornered. Lift your shoulder away and the pressure returns, and with it the preference, the thickening, the queue of drops. You were a draft. Drafts end.

At the rim, in the ordinary dust, there is still nothing to point to. No track that is not yours, no feather of unusual heat you could carry up in a jar, no second silhouette when you look back down. The hollow keeps its rectangle of light, its one pool, its industrial voice. Below the voice, uninterested in your leaving, the warmer thread resumes its slow circuit from flange to crack to shelf to bolt and back, thickening the rime by a grain, returning a little water to the green, drinking what the pipe can spare. It will be here when the angles soften and when the number on the lip loses the rest of itself. It may be here when the pump is finally shut off and the rhythm ends; or it may thin, then, into ordinary seep, and the moss will spread or die, and the place will look as empty as it does now. Until that day it lives as a held breath in a new mouth in the ground: hot, local, almost invisible, and exact.
