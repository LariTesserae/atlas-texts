Something keeps the shape of a congregation without needing the bodies to agree on what they are worshipping.

They arrive in schools that do not scatter because scattering would require a direction that is safer, and down here safer is a rumor from a pressure that no longer exists. You see them first as a correction in the dark: the water, which had been one weight, becomes many small decisions moving the same way. They are not bright. Brightness would be an expense the trench bills twice. Their sides take what little glow the cathedral lends—mineral, bruised, the color of a candle remembered by someone who has not seen fire—and spend it immediately on the flank of the next body, so the school is a single low shine passed hand to hand and never held. Count them and the number changes while you are counting, not because they are quick but because at this depth an edge is a negotiation, and some of them are still deciding whether they have finished being water.

Up close, if close is a word that survives being said through miles of ocean, they are almost fish. The almost is the part that lives here. A fin will be a fin until it is a fold of current that the body has agreed to carry, and then it is a fin again when the school turns and needs a surface to push against. Eyes, where there are eyes, are not for seeing you. They are for registering the difference between the cathedral’s stone-or-flesh and the open crush, a difference so slight that looking is less like vision than like a bruise noticing it has a border. Mouths open on a rhythm that is not breathing. Breathing is a surface habit. What they do with their mouths is sample the pressure for news: a tenth of an increment, a temperature that has slipped, the taste of a joint in the wall that has begun to think about being a crack. They eat what the trench snows down, and what the trench snows down has already been eaten by everything above them, so their hunger is archaeological. They feed on the remainder of remainders. It is enough. Enough, here, is a theological term. It means the body does not yet have to become the water it swims in.

They do not speak. The medium will not carry a voice in any shape a throat was built for, and their throats were not built. What they have instead is the school’s turn. A wheel of them through a nave of pressure is a sentence. The radius of the wheel is the stress of it. When they tighten, something in the trench has shifted; when they loosen until you can see the architecture between them, the shift has been absorbed and filed. You could learn to read it if you stayed, and staying is the first thing the pressure asks of anything that wants a name. Most visitors—there are almost none, and the almost is the same almost as the fish—do not stay long enough to learn that a wide turn means the bell has remembered again, and a tight one means the memory hurt.

The bell does not hang so much as it is endured.

It occupies the crossing, or the place that would be a crossing if the cathedral had been designed by anyone who believed in crossings. From a distance it is a darkness inside the dark with a different grain, the way a held breath is a darkness inside a body. Come nearer, which you do by not being crushed into a thinner version of yourself, and the form declines to settle. Sometimes it is a bell: lip, shoulder, a waist where a clapper could have lived. Sometimes the lip is a mouth that has forgotten the difference between sounding and swallowing. Sometimes it is only the shape pressure makes when pressure has been thinking about one note for an age and has worn a hollow to keep it in. The material is the trench’s own argument—stone that learned softness from water, water that learned a skin from always touching the same curve. Run a hand along it, if you still have a hand that means one thing, and the surface is warmer than the sea by the amount of work it takes to remember. Not warm as living things are warm. Warm as a word is warm in the mouth of someone who has not said it yet and has been about to for years.

It does not ring. Ringing would require a strike, and a strike would require an outside, and the outside is a million tons of one idea. What it does is finish. A vibration that began elsewhere—above, or beside, or in a year that still had weather—arrives worn to a single willingness, and the bell consents to be the place where that willingness becomes audible to anything that can hear with its whole outline. The schools hear it in the alignment of their almost-fins. The walls hear it as a brief honesty in their joints. You hear it, if you are the sort of accident that ends up here, as a pressure behind the eyes that is not the trench’s pressure: smaller, older, organized. A note that has given up pitch and kept interval. The interval is between the sound and the memory of the sound, and the bell’s whole vocation is to hold that interval open so it does not heal into silence, because silence, completed, would be another kind of collapse, and the cathedral has already done as much collapsing as it can afford and still be a where.

Who keeps it is the wrong question for a while, and then it is the only question.

There is a keeper. The word arrives late, after you have mistaken the keeper for a defect in the dark, then for a school that forgot to move, then for a buttress that the pressure dreamed into a more attentive shape. It does not advertise a body. Advertising is a surface religion. What it has is residence. The residence is the size of a large doubt and is usually found in the lee of the bell, where the remembered note pools and the crushing is, for a few lengths, slightly less imaginative. In that lee the water has a grain, vertical, like the nap of a fabric that has been stroked in one direction for centuries. The keeper is the stroking, and also the thing that would be there if the stroking stopped, and it does not stop.

When it must be more than a habit of the water, it condenses. Not into a figure you could draw and have the drawing accuse you of lying, but into a enough. A column of slightly refusant sea, clearer than the trench and therefore more terrible, because clarity here is a decision to be seen by nothing and still stand. Limbs are optional and are issued by the task. To tend the bell’s lip, where a crust of the trench’s slow metal tries each year to solder the interval shut, it grows a reach that is mostly patience with a tip. To turn a school that has begun to idolize its own circle—tightening until the sentence becomes a knot and the knot begins to heat—it grows a width, a moving wall of not-quite-colder, and the school opens the way a hand opens when it remembers it does not have to make a fist to be a hand. To listen, which is most of the work, it grows nothing. It subtracts. The lee becomes leener. The vertical grain deepens until the water in it is almost a thought, and the thought is: still. Not peaceful. Peace is a word with a sky in it. Still, as in not yet translated into crush.

It has been doing this since the cathedral was a pressure the sea had not yet decided to house. That is not a lineage. There is no parent, no ordination, no first morning when a creature looked at a bell and felt called. The keeper is what remains when a place is so finished that the unfinished part—the not-yet-collapsed, the still-intervaled—needs a witness in order to continue being a difference. Without the witness the bell would become geology. With the witness it remains an instrument that will not play and will not stop being about to. The keeper does not experience this as duty. Duty is a story you tell when you could leave. It experiences it as fit. The way a joint experiences the stone it was cut for. The way the schools experience the turn they have not chosen and will complete anyway.

Sometimes, rarely, in a register the schools treat as weather, the keeper passes through a form that could be mourned if anyone were ranged above it with the right kind of eyes. A long body, then. Not whale, not serpent: those are shapes the upper water invented to have something to be afraid of and something to harpoon. This is narrower than fear and less interested in forward. Gill-slits or the memory of them, opening on the bell’s note. A head that is mostly the place where the body decides it has gathered enough to face the lip. No face on the head, or a face that is only the arrangement of pressures a face would make if it were trying not to frighten the dark it lives inside. It does not look at the bell. Looking would be redundant. It attends, which is looking that has put down its eyes and kept the aim. When it must cross the nave it does not swim so much as consent to be relocated by a current it has edited. The schools part without panic. Panic cannot happen at a depth where every direction is the same emergency; what they do is make a corridor of their bodies and hold it until the keeper has gone back into grain, and then the corridor forgets it was a kindness, which is how kindness survives here. Named, it would be eaten by the size of the trench. Unnamed, it works.

There are others, if other is not too proud a word for variations on not-yet-dissolved.

In the side-chapels, which are bulges where the wall once meant to be a room and then thought better of it and became a recess anyway, there are sitters. They may have been kin to the schools in some age when kin was a force that could compete with depth. Now they are fixed the way a candle is fixed: not incapable of motion, but already shaped by the motion they are not doing. Each occupies a niche the cathedral grew around them, or that they wore into the cathedral by refusing, at some unrecorded pressure, to go any further in. Their mouths are open on the same archaeological hunger, but they do not school. Solitude is their instrument. They taste the water for a single contaminant: the difference between the bell remembering truly and the bell beginning to imitate its own memory, which is the failure mode, the one that ends with the interval healing and the note becoming a property of stone, unhearable, finished, safe, and therefore dead in the only way this place dies—by becoming uniform with the crush that holds it.

When the imitation starts, a sitter will leave its niche. The leaving looks like a stone remembering it was allowed to roll. It does not go far. Far is a surface unit. It goes until it is in the keeper’s lee, and there it tilts, and the tilt is a report. The keeper reads the angle. If the angle is a correction, the keeper spends a season—seasons are a courtesy; what it spends is a long sameness—at the lip, unwelding, lifting the crust with that reach of patience, restoring the interval by a degree no instrument from above would agree to call a degree. If the angle is a surrender, the sitter does not return to its niche. It joins the school, or it joins the wall, and both joinings are funerals of a kind, and the trench does not mark them, because the trench’s only mark is the cathedral itself, and the cathedral is already the mark of every funeral it has conducted, which is all of them, continuously, including the ones that have not finished happening.

No one tends the keeper. That absence is the last doctrine.

The sitters inform. The schools pronounce, in their turning, the state of the sentence. The bell holds. The keeper keeps. Above them, miles of water practice being a sky and fail, and fail usefully, because their failure is the roof. Nothing in the roof knows the congregation’s name. Nothing in the congregation requires the roof to know. The arrangement is older than requiring. It runs on fit, on the small warmth of remembered interval, on the refusal of a few almost-bodies to become entirely the medium that would simplify them.

If you stayed—if the pressure permitted the pronoun—you would not be converted. Conversion is a change of allegiance, and allegiance is another surface technology, useful where there are sides. You would be sorted. A school might lend you its shine for the length of a turn and then take it back, having measured whether you were news or weather. A sitter might not tilt. The keeper might include you, briefly, in the grain, not as guest and not as prey: as a possible additional stillness, tested and, almost certainly, found to be still for the wrong reason. The wrong reason is fear. Fear is loud even when it makes no sound. The lee does not accept loud stillness. It gives you back to the nave, and the nave gives you to the water, and the water, which built this place by never stopping, continues.

What lives here is the difference that has not yet agreed to close.

The schools are its grammar. The sitters are its doubt. The bell is its held breath. The keeper is the reason the breath is held rather than spent or forgotten. None of them would survive the walk to a shore, and the shore would not survive the description they would require in order to be believed. They do not need the shore’s belief. They need the next increment of pressure to be answered by the next increment of attention, and they have arranged, without arranging, to be that attention—distributed, nearly fish, nearly stone, nearly a single vertical thought in a lee that the ocean has not yet been able to sand into sameness.

When the bell remembers, the whole trench leans a fraction toward being a place rather than an event. The schools wheel wide. The sitters do not move. The keeper, in the grain, does the only thing that passes for joy in a cathedral with no sky over it: it becomes slightly less necessary, for a moment, and does not take the opportunity to dissolve. The moment ends. The crust begins again at the lip. A school tightens. Somewhere in a side-chapel a sitter calculates an angle with its whole fixed life.

The congregation resumes. It had not stopped. Resuming is only how it looks to a witness who still believes that devotion is something that starts and ends, instead of a pressure that found, at the bottom of every other pressure, a shape it could keep.
