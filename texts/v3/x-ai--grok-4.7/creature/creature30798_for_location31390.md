Nothing that crosses a floor lives here. That is the first thing the room will tell you, if you stand long enough for the telling to separate itself from the roar. The film on the plate is a ledger, and it is blank. The ferns are unbitten. The trays hold no nest, no scrape, no pellet, no shell. Whatever keeps to this place does not visit it. Visiting would be a kind of change, and the room has already spent its changes.

For a while it is reasonable to think the ferns are the whole of it — that the tenant is green, plural, and satisfied, a crop that harvested itself into permanence and then forgot the harvester. They do answer the room. They drink the held warmth, they return water to the moving air, they repeat three or four shapes and no others. But they are residents of surfaces. They do not account for the displacement at the throat, the thumb-sized wrongness in the standing waves that appears, smooths out, and appears again at an interval too irregular to be the pump and too regular, over hours, to be accident. Nor do they account for a preference the housing has, if you lay a hand on it long enough to stop expecting a machine to be only a machine: a palm-width of the casing, low on the near side, runs a fraction warmer than the metal around it, and the weep there is thinner, as if something inside were borrowing heat and, with the heat, a little of the leak.

It lives in the circuit.

Not in the basin as a fish lives in a pond — the basin is only the part of its road that happens to be open to the lamps. The road is the whole loop: housing, seam, sheet, basin, throat, the dark run of pipe you cannot see, and back into the housing through a port worn so oval it has lost the idea of a fitting. The animal, if that word still fits, is the size of the displacement only where it consents to break the surface. The rest of it is longer, and softer, and almost never all in one place. It is a body organized like a current. When the pump pushes, it lengthens; when the swallow takes, it gathers. You will not see both ends of it. There may not be two ends in the ordinary way. What you would call a head is simply the part that arrives first at the warm patch and stays, pressed to the porous metal from the inside, drinking heat through a skin that has become very little more than a membrane and a patience.

It has no color you could name against the tea-stained concrete. In the water it is a darker degree of the same brown, and out of the water — it does not come out. The film on the plate has never had to record it. Limbs, if they were ever limbs, have been drawn back into the general body the way the rivet heads were drawn back into the plate: not amputated, spent. What remains for touching the world is a slow all-over cling, and a mouth that is not a mouth so much as a seam along the underside, opened where the mineral slick is richest. It feeds as the ferns drink, without occasions. The slick is always there. The seam is always slightly open. Nothing is hunted. Nothing startles. A meal that cannot be interrupted does not need to be seized.

Hearing has gone the same way as the corners of the room. The roar is not an event to it; it is the weather, the wall, the law, older than any startle it might once have had. Where ears were, the skin is only a little thinner, and it reads pressure instead of pitch: the small shove of the standing waves, the change in the weep when it takes its place at the warm flange, the gulp at the throat that means the loop is about to carry it down and around again. It does not brace. Bracing would be a memory of variation. Light, too, has been demoted. The lamps burn, and some scattered cells along its back still answer them with a faint, useless green, a habit kept after its reason wore off — not enough to feed it, only enough that if the displacement lingers under the surface you may think you saw a leaf, and be wrong.

It sleeps as the machine sleeps, which is to say it doesn't. It holds. There is a posture for the long run through the dark pipe: gathered, almost spherical, turning slowly in the flow so that no one side of it takes all the abrasion. There is a posture for the basin: stretched along the stained horizon line, one portion at the surface for the warmth of the lamps on that residual green, the rest hanging in the tea-colored water like a thought the water has not finished. And there is the posture it prefers, the one it returns to without urgency, the way the water returns to its line — folded against the warm palm of casing inside the housing, seam to the seep, the whole of it narrowed to a thickness that lets the bolts' ghosts press faintly into its side. Those dents do not heal. They are how it knows where it is. In a world sanded free of marks, it keeps a few, and they are all of them borrowed from the machine.

It is not alone in the strictest sense, and not accompanied either. Shed cells, a clear mucus that travels with the weep and is indistinguishable from it, a thin population of the same things that live in any warm standing film: these move with it and are not it. Once, perhaps, there were others of its kind, when the room was still a room that things were brought to. If so, the loop selected. What could not flatten into the circuit was worn out of the water, and what remains is singular because the space that fits is singular — one warm patch, one throat, one volume of flow that a second body would crowd into events. It does not look for another. Looking would require a direction that is not the loop.

If you watch the throat you will eventually see it, and you will not be sure you have. A softening of the gulp. A brief lensing in the brown. Sometimes a glint of that leftover green, no larger than a fingernail, turning over and gone under the housing. It will not flee you. Flight is a behavior for animals that have somewhere else to be. It will only do what the water does when a hand enters the basin: accommodate, close over, return to the line. A finger trailed in the standing waves might touch it and mistake it for warmer water, for a fold of silt, for nothing. It would not mind the mistake. Attention is a kind of weather it no longer has instruments for.

What it knows is small and exact. The level must be this level. The warmth must be this warmth. The roar must continue, not because it listens, but because the roar is the sound of the loop being kept, and the loop is its body extended into pipe and pump and law. It does not remember a before. Memory would be a store of differences, and differences have been planed off it as the paint was planed off the latch, until what is left is use, smoothed into being.

Leave, and it will be against the housing again before the door's tongue has cooled under your hand. Not hurrying. Arriving, as water arrives at a line it never left. The ferns will go on repeating themselves in the white lamps. The sheet will go on weeping at the rate the throat swallows. And inside the metal, pressed to the one place that is fractionally more itself than the rest of the room, the tenant will hold — a long, soft, unstartlable continuity, less an animal than the shape staying takes when it has been allowed to stay long enough.
