They are a short people only in the first glance, and only because the city has taught every spine a habitual stoop. Stand one of them in an empty room — if such a room could be found, which it cannot — and the height resolves: ordinary, a little long in the arm, the reach of someone who has spent a life bracing, passing, catching. The stoop is not age. Children have it by the time they can walk the plates. It is the posture of a skull that expects an overhang, of shoulders that expect other shoulders, of a body that does not waste vertical space the city does not give.

Skin runs warm and slightly tacky, never quite dry, the way a hand feels after it has been in and out of a basin all morning. Color varies more by work than by lineage: the upper-level porters go copper-dark where the honest light hits them between towers, while the vent-tenders and the silt crews stay the pale of people who live in reflected glare off water. Everyone’s surface films. A fine mineral dust settles into the oils of the face and the creases of the knuckles and is wiped, constantly, with the heel of the hand or the shoulder of a neighbor, without either party remarking it. Strangers touch more than they mean to. In a press of bodies there is no clean boundary between your condensate and the next person’s, and the manners have grown up around that fact instead of against it. You do not apologize for contact. You apologize for stopping.

Their hands are the most finished part of them. Nails worn square, pads thickened, a bright callus where the grip closes on the polished rails. Many carry a small hook or a loop of filament at the belt, not as a weapon but as a third way to hold on when a floor flexes or a wave slaps a shared level and the crowd becomes, for a few seconds, a fluid. Feet are broad, toes a little splayed, soles mapped with the geometry of grates. Shoes exist and are often not worn. Wet hide slips; bare skin reads the plate.

They do not have private voices. What they have is a larynx trained to a cutting pitch and a face trained to do the rest. Speech, when it happens, is short, tonal, full of words that mean *move*, *hold*, *coming through*, *wait*, *mine*, *not mine*. Anything longer than a warning is either shouted at a known ear from a hand’s breadth away or not spoken at all. The real language is held up. Boards, slates, the pale inner side of a bivalve-shell tablet, a rectangle of ceramic no larger than a palm, written with a grease stylus and wiped with a thumb. A conversation can cross a jammed ramp without a single mouth opening: one board lifted, one answer, a correction, a laugh that is only a change in the eyes. Children learn to read before they learn to shout, because reading does not compete with the city and shouting does.

Privacy has been relocated from space into time and attention. There is no room where you are alone. There are intervals when the people nearest you have agreed not to look. A couple will stand in the press with their foreheads touching and their boards lowered, and the stream will part around them by a finger’s width, which is all the solitude the shelf can mint. Sleep happens in shifts, in stacked niches like berths in a hull, bodies fitted against bodies, the roar accepted into the dream until a pressure-wave or a horn means *move*. Infants are carried high, on the chest or the back, wrapped so the wet does not chill them, and they grow up hearing the mill-voice of driven water as other children hear wind. It does not frighten them. Silence would.

They eat what the water and the kept animals will give, and what the films will give if you know which films. Nothing green, nothing that grew in light toward a sky. Protein from the basins: soft bodies chopped and stewed until the sweetness cooks out, hard climbers cracked for the meat under the shell, slurry from the hanging pens piped up hot and drunk from cups that are never quite clean and do not need to be. Salt is not scarce. Mineral bite is the background of every meal. They season, when they can, with ferments — jars tucked into warm joints of the architecture, where the city’s own heat does the work, sealed with the same migrating film that seals cracks. A good ferment is a household’s pride and a tradable thing. Markets occupy the temporary dry and smell, under the general mineral sweetness, of these jars opened in a crowd.

Work is mostly maintenance that pretends to be other things. Someone must watch the lattices and decide when to let them thicken and when to argue with them. Someone must clear the grates before a pulse arrives. Someone must know which basin will be navigable in an hour and which will be a wall of water. The skills look like trades — porter, ferryman, vent-tender, film-keeper, herd-minder for the climbers — and underneath they are all the same skill: reading a city that will not hold still, and moving with the flex instead of against it. There is status in being early. The person who feels the floor creep and has already shifted their cargo before the crowd understands is the person others stand near.

They are not gentle by temperament and not cruel by system. The crowding does not make them kind; it makes unkindness expensive. A feud that takes up space is a feud the stream will not tolerate. Grudges are conducted at board-length, precisely, and ended quickly, because a blocked ramp injures everyone including the injured parties. Violence happens — hooks, fists, a shoulder used as a ram in a place too narrow to fall down — and it is punished less by authority than by the physics of the shelf: you cannot hide, you cannot run farther than the next jam, and the people who saw it will still be there in an hour, because no one is ever gone. Enforcement is local, bodily, and over fast. There are officials, of a sort, recognizable by a dyed cord and a louder horn, but they do not rule an outside. They are knots in the same flow, empowered to stop it briefly and hated for the stopping.

What they believe is harder to separate from what they do. They do not talk much about a world beyond the vapor column. Some will say, if you hold up the question on a board and wait long enough, that the shelf is the world, and that rain is the city breathing on itself, and that this has always been the arrangement. Others keep a small, unserious cosmology: that somewhere the plates end, that the water there is deeper than a hand, that a quiet exists the way a story exists, useful to think about and not to be looked for. Neither party tries very hard to convince the other. Conviction is another thing that takes up space.

They are beautiful in the way crowded functional things are beautiful when you stop wanting them to be sparse. A face always slightly shining. Eyes quick, because the danger is not in the distance; it is at the scale of the next step, the next flex, the wake about to slap the wall. Tenderness practiced at close range, without ceremony: a thumb wiping grit from a child’s eye, a board passed so someone whose voice has gone out can still buy a cup of slurry, two porters bracing a stranger when the floor creeps and the stranger does not yet know how to lean. They do not experience this as poetry. They experience it as the minimum required to remain uncrushed.

If you stay, they will not ask what you are doing here. They will assess whether you can move. A hand will take your elbow, not unkindly, and set you into the stream at the speed of the stream. Someone will lift a board and show you the mark for *hold the rail*. The city will roar at the ordinary level. Your clothes will film over. In an hour your voice, if you try it, will already be pitching itself sharper, looking for a gap that is not there, and you will understand why they gave up on gaps. The people around you will have been replaced by other people who look, at first, the same — wet, reaching, attentive — and then not the same at all, once you learn the small differences: the jar-cord at a belt, the climber-scar on a wrist, the particular economy of a glance that means *you are in the way* and the other that means *stay near me, the pulse is coming*.

They live as the shelf lives: not becoming something else, only becoming themselves again by the next hour, in the roar, in the wet, shoulder to shoulder, with nothing green anywhere and no need of it.
