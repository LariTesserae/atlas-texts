They are not a people so much as a sediment that learned to keep appointments. You notice them first as corrections in the avenues: a darker upright against the tea-colored stone, a cart that has acquired a shoulder, a hand that has learned the exact height at which a valve wheel will still turn when the iron is sweating. Up close the corrections have faces, and the faces are ordinary in the way limestone is ordinary—porous, a little glazed, marked by the same drip that marks the curbs. Color has been negotiated downward. Skin keeps a warm undertone, as if the basin’s heat had settled into the capillaries and declined to leave; the lighter faces look tea-stained, the darker ones look oiled. Hair, when it is worn long, is braided or clubbed against the damp so it will not wick mineral into the scalp. Most heads are close-cropped. A film of condensate collects at the hairline anyway and is wiped with the same absent gesture people elsewhere use for sweat.

They do not raise their voices outdoors. The habit is older than any bylaw and more thorough. Children learn the hand-grammar before they learn the names of the islands. Two fingers along the forearm asks permission to step onto a catwalk. A palm turned down and slightly cupped means the flow is acceptable, do not touch. A fist closed and opened twice means stoppage—bearing, not emergency. Grief, when it must travel in the avenues, travels as a particular stillness of the shoulders and a slower cart. Strangers misread it as fatigue. Residents step aside without being asked. Indoors, where cork and fiber have taken the roar down to a pressure in the molars, speech returns, but it returns changed: shorter sentences, fewer ornaments, a preference for the verb that names the action over the verb that names the feeling. They are not taciturn by temperament. They are economical in the way the pumps are economical. A word that is not needed is a weep that has been allowed to migrate.

Work organizes them more cleanly than kinship, though kinship is dense. A household is whoever shares a silence room’s key and a cistern’s boil. Blood matters; so does the turbine you were raised beside. Children are walked, once, to the canal where a pale amphipod is said to live, and they are allowed to see nothing, and the walk is the instruction: abundance is not the measure here. They grow up knowing which joints in the pavement will take a cart’s wheel without argument and which will throw it. They grow up knowing the taste difference between roof-weep, boiled, and the strong grade that leaves in casks. They do not grow up expecting birds.

The fitters are the closest thing the basin has to a priesthood, and they would resent the word. They move between the ceramic throats with the unhurried attention of people who have been told, correctly, that a stopped machine changes the stone. Their hands are mapped with old burns and new grease. They speak, when they must speak over the belts, in a compressed pidgin of taps on pipe—three for bearing heat, a long scrape for a weep that has changed its mind. Apprenticeship is long because the neighboring flues have opinions. A young fitter who closes a relief valve fully, even for a minute, even to prove a point, is not shouted at. He is walked to the wall that will stain next season and left there until he understands that vandalism, in this city, is a change in hydrology. After that he is useful. Most of them stay useful for decades. When a fitter dies, the bearing he last replaced is not consecrated; it is simply left in service, and people who knew him will rest two fingers on the housing when they pass, the way they rest fingers on a curb that has held.

There are counters, and there are caskers, and there are the people who keep the silence pockets. The counters live in the lined rooms and keep the ledgers of stoppages. Their handwriting has grown small and upright, as if conserving paper were a form of conserving flow. They drink the mild grade and complain, mildly, about the sweetness in the strong. The caskers work the condensate sheds in shifts that do not restart lightly. They brand the chimney-and-drip with a stamp that has been recut so many times the drip is slightly fatter than the original drawing. They know which towns down the road will pay for the taste of depth and which will send the casks back claiming the water has gone flat—which it has, by their standards, the moment it leaves the roar. The keepers of the pockets are not priests either. They sweep the benches. They enforce the short bylaw with a look. They have learned which hours the cancel is wanted for argument, which for an instrument, which for the particular quiet in which a person who is dying prefers to hear one voice at ordinary volume. They do not charge. The pocket is civic the way a well is civic. A keeper who began to charge would be walked to a stained wall and left there.

Animals have made their own smaller treaties. The lizard on the south-facing tank is not a pet and not quite wild; it occupies a temperature and is left to it. Cats are indoor hostages, sleek from boredom, vicious about the mineral film that forms on any sill left open. A few households keep a jar of the pale amphipods’ supposed cousins—translucent, jointed, indifferent—as a kind of domestic proof that the canal lesson was not a lie. The jars are not science. They are manners. Dogs exist in stories more than in yards. A dog cannot be taught that the roar is weather, and a dog that cannot be taught that becomes, within a season, a reproach. So the city mostly declines the reproach.

What they share, under the crop and the burns and the small handwriting, is a specific relationship to continuation. They do not experience the basin as a place that might improve. Improvement would be a migrated weep. They experience it as a place that has found its form and must be accompanied. A cracked housing is not awaiting replacement; it is awaiting the decade in which it becomes a buttress and then a hoodoo with bolt-holes still legible to the initiated. A person is accompanied on the same terms. Illness is managed so that the household’s key and the household’s boil continue. Death is managed so that the bearing stays in service. Joy, when it occurs, occurs in the pockets and is not paraded down the avenues where it would have to compete with the organ-note and lose. They are capable of tenderness. It simply has the same steady volume as the rest of their competence. A hand on a housing. A slower cart. A bench left dry.

If you stay long enough to be trusted with a key, you learn that they tell time by stoppages and by the far wall’s sharpness. A month in which the vapor thins and the rim comes forward is remarked the way other cities remark weather that requires a coat. A month in which the marsh-edge advances a curb and then, by agreement with the pumps, stops, is remarked not at all; that is the sentence doing what the sentence does. They will walk you to the western catwalk if you make the forearm gesture correctly. They will not explain the view. The view is the work, separated by distances that must remain distances, under a sun that is only sun, in a roar that was already meeting you. They stand beside you for a while because standing beside is a form of speech they trust. Then one of them taps the rail twice—acceptable, do not change it—and starts back down, and the city, which includes them, continues.
