What lives here does not announce itself by crossing a gap. The gaps are already full of the wrong kind of quiet, and the roar would eat a footfall even if the mat did not take it first. You know it is present the way you know a pump has changed its load: a note that was one thickness becomes two, or a vibration that had been seated in the bench lifts out of the stone and starts to walk.

It keeps to the living floor. The tracks it leaves are not paths so much as delays in the moss—places where the mat was pressed flat and has not yet sprung back, a darker sheen of water squeezed sideways, gone by the time you bend to look. Where the press is deepest the peat holds a print for an hour: broad, not hoof and not boot, four weight-points under a low body, the outer two set wide so the cold sheet does not tear. It has learned the bearing of this ground the way the trunks have. Step narrow and you punch through to the raw cut. Step wide and the hollow carries you.

At a distance it reads as one more vertical that is not quite a trunk. Closer, the vertical breaks. It is low through the middle and high at the shoulders, a body that grew up under a rim and never needed to stand into weather. The hide is the color of wet bark and old galvanized, not fur so much as a close nap that holds damp and sheds frost in a pale rime along the spine when it has been still. The cold lives in it the way it lives in the metal. It does not shiver. Shivering would be an event, and the hollow does not reward events.

The head is narrow and set forward, almost no external ear left to catch a sound that cannot be caught. What it listens with is the feet, the long bones of the forelimbs, a keel of cartilage under the jaw that it lowers until it touches the mat or a pipe or the lip of a sump. The roar arrives there already sorted. It can tell a healthy lift from a lift that is chewing air. It can tell a seep that is about to bead from a joint that has gone dry. Eyes are small, set for the gray that comes down off the rim, useless past the next trunk and not much needed. It navigates by the spacing—the regular emptiness between columns—and by the cable trays, which tick, and by the temperature of water, which is the only map that updates.

It eats what the hollow reliably makes. The film in the black pools carries a thin crop: larvae, a skin of algal cold-matter, the drowned wings of the moths that settle on flanges and never leave. It takes these without drama, mouth at the surface, not disturbing more than a thumb’s width. From the mat it pulls the pale rhizomes that run under the sedge, sweet with stored seep, and from the rusted conveyor it scrapes a lichen that only grows where iron meets perpetual damp. None of this is abundance. It is enough, and it is local, so the body has stayed local. There is no herd because there is no reason to be more than one. A second body would double the press on the mat and halve the film in the pools. The hollow has room in the horizontal and almost none in the living.

What it does that is not feeding is the thing that makes the place continue. At the intakes the mat is always closing. A season of neglect and a sump becomes a black pool with no pipe, and the floor above it softens, and a column that trusted the cut leans by a degree you would not see until the roots tear. It clears these the way it feeds, because the two acts have become the same metabolism. The hands—if they are hands—are spatulate, edged with a hard pale callus that is half nail and half mineral stain. They lift the mat in a sheet, set it aside, free the grate of peat and root-hair, and lay the sheet back with the living side up. The pump’s note drops from the panicked thinness of air back into the thick work of water. It stays with a palm on the housing until the note is the one it came for. Then it moves on. No one taught it the sequence. The sequence is older than its memory and younger than the excavation: whatever kept the first pumps from seizing taught the next body, or the same body, by the simple fact that the floor remained walkable.

It has no den you would call a room. It sleeps where a bench holds a little of the works’ wet warmth, or in the lee of a trunk-plinth where the mat is deepest and the roar arrives as pressure instead of edge. Sleep is a lowering. The jaw-keel settles. The wide feet stay planted, still reading, so that a change in load can stand it up without the delay of waking all the way. It dreams, if the small tremors mean dreams, in pump-intervals. A long run of the same note and the limbs loosen. A flutter in a duct and one shoulder lifts, tests the air, sets down again.

When the rare maintenance comes down the ramp, it knows before the boots reach the floor. The ramp has a different ring—angular edges, no give—and a body that does not know the mat walks wrong on it, heel-first, loud in the bones. The inhabitant does not flee toward a heading. Flight would read on the sheet as a tear, a dark line the visitor might follow. It goes instead to height that is not height: up a root-weave where the cut is still sharp, out along a cable tray, into the narrow dark between a buckled pump-house wall and the living mat that has climbed it. It makes itself continuous with the partial machines. A caged bulb, if one is switched on, finds corrugated metal, orange rust, hung moss, and a shape that is only wrong if you already believe the hollow is empty. The visitor clears an intake with a bar and a curse that vanishes into the roar, props a duct, leaves. The boot-path begins to narrow before they reach the rim. The inhabitant comes down, checks the work with its jaw against the pipe, corrects what was done too coarsely, and the note settles.

It does not know the country above the rim except as a change in pressure when the wind up there is strong enough to lean down the cut. It has been to the ramp’s foot and turned back. The air there is thinner in the ears it no longer has, and the light is a violence, and the mat ends. Nothing it eats lives in that brightness. Nothing it listens to speaks there. The original ground is a story told by absence: no tick in the trays, no film to read, no reason for the wide step.

Over years the body has taken on the hollow’s unfinishedness. Iron freckles the forearms where it braces on flanges. A scar along one flank matches a conveyor roller it misjudged when the mat was thinner. The nap along the spine grows a little greener in the season when frost does not needle, not plant and not not-plant, a surface the damp colonizes because the body is still enough and cold enough to be another substrate. It does not scrape this off. The green is insulation of a kind, and a camouflage that required no decision.

If you stayed, and stayed quiet in the bones, you might see it at the work that is not quite work: moving from pool to pool along a route that follows the migration of seeps, pausing where a new heading shows raw stone, touching the fresh face with the keel as if measuring how long until the first skin of green. It is not grieving the cut and not defending it. It is the part of the hollow that notices when the machines and the mat fall out of agreement, and that spends its small heat putting them back. In that sense it is not an intruder in the valley that was lowered. It is the lowered valley’s way of lasting—patient, local, almost invisible, deaf to everything but the difference between water being lifted and water beginning, somewhere under the sheet, to be lost.
