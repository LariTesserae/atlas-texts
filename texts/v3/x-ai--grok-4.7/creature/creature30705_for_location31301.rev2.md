The chamber’s emptiness is the first fact, and it holds for a long time. Light arrives whole, water replaces itself, stone keeps every edge the mason left, and nothing crosses the column or ticks in the joints. The three sounds remain three. The leaves tug and spring back. If something lived here it would have to live inside those conditions rather than against them, small enough or matched enough that the turnover does not carry it out and the light does not show it as a separate shape.

What stays is not a body at first. It is a persistence in the way the brightness bends. Over the drain the threads of light kink once, as the description has them kink, pulled by the throat of the channel. A little aside from that kink, nearer the pots, the same threads waver a second time, smaller, and the second waver does not follow the upstream pulse. It lags, then leads, then settles into a short repeating lean, as if a patch of the water had decided to occupy the same hand’s-breadth of volume and was spending a little of the current’s energy to keep the decision. The patch is no cloudier than the rest. It is only busier at its edges, a faint stacking of tiny shears where warmer water from the spout meets the slightly cooler skin that has already crossed the floor.

Left alone, the patch circulates. It does not swim. It rides the slow replacement and corrects, again and again, so that its center remains just upstream of the strongest draw and just downstream of the leaves. When the inflow tears higher for a moment, the patch compresses against the near wall of one pot, using the small shelter of the coil-built curve; when the tear falls back, it unfolds into the lit square the shaft lays on the floor and spreads until its margin almost touches the trembling edge of that square. It has no eyes, yet the square matters to it. The light arrives as a steady extra warmth and as a direction, and the patch keeps a portion of itself inside the warmth the way a sleeper keeps a hand near a hearth without waking.

It came with the seep. Farther back, in the dark runnel, similar persistences drift in the mineral warmth, loose and brief, dissolving when the channel widens or when a board is shifted. This one did not dissolve. The chamber’s newness suited it: constant volume, constant exchange, no film yet to clog its margins, and a single vertical shaft that delivers the day’s changes as a clean signal. It has been here since the water first stood to the soffit. The few green threads in the high joint and the narrow leaves in the pots are younger than it is. It does not feed on them. Their motion is only another small weather it reads. When a leaf springs back, the patch’s edge flinches a fraction inward, then resumes its circuit, as if the leaf were a draft under a door.

Over longer watching the patch acquires preferences that are not mere physics. It avoids the exact mouth of the drain even when the pull would be an efficient way to shed heat; something in its coherence fails if it is stretched too thin across that lip, and it has learned the distance at which the throat-sound begins to tug harder than it can answer. It favors the hour when the shaft’s light reaches the floor in a sharp square rather than the diffuse gray of cloud; on those brighter intervals it slows its circulation and holds more of itself inside the square, and the second waver in the light becomes almost regular, a soft pulse slower than the spill’s clatter. It does not mind the hand, when a hand is lowered through the shaft, except as a sudden cold and a sudden shape. The patch withdraws at once along the floor, flattening into the angle where wall meets slab, and for a short while the stone there feels, under the fingers, not gritty but briefly less resistant, as though a warmer film had moved aside and the true dressing of the block returned. The film leaves no residue. By the time the hand is withdrawn the patch has already begun to re-expand from the corner, following the returning warmth of the spout.

It has no separate appetite and no separate waste. Minerals and dissolved gases arrive, are held in the pattern of its shears for a time, and leave again by the drain or the shaft; the holding itself is what continues. The three sounds—the bright shatter at the lip, the round note from the channel, the thin tear at the trough—do not merely surround it. Their chord falls inside the same range as the small oscillations that keep its margins from diffusing, and when the upstream pulse is steady the patch’s internal motion locks loosely to that chord and spends less of itself on correction. A missed beat, a board shifted too far, forces it to spend more; it shrinks, rides closer to the pot, and waits until the chord restabilizes. In that waiting it is most visible, if visibility is the word: a slight darkening no thicker than a breath on glass, gone as soon as the sounds agree again.

Nothing else has joined it. No second patch has drifted in from the seep and kept its shape; the chamber’s particular light and particular chord seem, so far, to suit only this one. The green threads lengthen by a finger’s width and still do not reach it. The leaves continue their small obedient bend. The stone remains sharp. The water continues to leave by the low mouth and the high, wasting a little warmth on the paving so the volume below can stay exactly full. Inside that exactness the patch turns, corrects, and turns again, occupying its hand’s-breadth without ever becoming a thing the light can name, and in the long hours when no hand disturbs the shaft it keeps to the square of brightness as if the day itself were the shelter it came here, out of the dark runnel, to find.
