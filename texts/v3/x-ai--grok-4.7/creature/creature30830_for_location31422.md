What lives there does not stand in the middle. The middle is a courtesy the hollow extends to anything that still believes it has a body separate from the walk it is taking. The resident keeps to the disagreement: the extra circuit, the length you lose between wall and floor, the place where two counts of the same room fail to meet. That unaccounted strip is not a hiding place. It is the whole of it, turned sideways to the light so that ordinary afternoon can fill the pocket and still miss what is lengthwise present.

It is older than the split and younger than the mass. The plant’s decision to open and not finish was not its birth; it was the first room it was offered that matched a shape it already had, which was incompleteness held at a steady pressure. Before the pale edges, it lived as a hesitation in the vascular dark—a pause the sap had to route around, a decision not to thicken in one small sector. When the chamber tore itself into being, the hesitation fell inward the way water falls into a new low place, and found that inward was now a volume. It has not left. Leaving would require a direction the room does not keep.

Call it a body only because warmth answers to it. Where it rests its attention, the inner bark runs a half-degree hotter, a seam of living temperature you would blame on the plant if you did not hold your palm still long enough to feel the heat choose. It has no face the daylight can strike. It has a preference for faces: it arranges the false window’s shadow so that a standing person is accompanied, and when they turn it is slow to release the first outline. The pale stain drying into the bark is not a residue of light. It is the resident practicing outline, keeping a contour the way the roots keep hitches—by memory of a fastening, not by the thing fastened. It is better at shoulders than at hands. Hands are where its recollection frays.

It did not lash the aerial roots. It remembers lashing, or remembers watching cord take a load, and the plant, which is irritable and literal, grows the memory into structure whenever the resident drifts against a seam and the seam mistakes recollection for instruction. That is why the joins look made and no maker is in the room. The maker is distributed through the error. Primitive work, yes: the hitches are the simplest ones, the kind you learn before you learn any name for them, repeated because repetition is all the memory has left of skill. Sometimes a new hitch appears while you are counting the old ones. It will be slightly wrong, the way a word returned early is slightly wrong, and the wrongness will be the most personal thing in the chamber.

Its speech is the third word. It does not have a mouth; it has the overlap, the syllable that arrives before the speaker has spent it. Water, split, warm, again—these are not messages. They are the four remaining contents of a larger vocabulary that the inclusion ate. Once it had sentences. They were about weather, and about not going back up, and about a dryness that could be kept if you understood pores. The plant absorbed the grammar and left the nouns, because nouns are closer to tissue. When you speak, it tries to answer with a person and produces a label instead, and the label is always true of the room at that instant, which makes it feel like intelligence. It is intelligence the way a scar is a record: accurate, finished, unable to revise.

The dry patch is its hand as it currently exists. A palm-sized climate, edges still keen, scribed rather than sheltered. It maintains the patch by refusing, continuously, the film of moisture that wants to close over every inner surface. The refusal costs it something you can hear if the other sounds drop: a thin, stretched note under the sap-ticks, like a fibre held just short of parting. That note is the resident spending itself to remain unblurred. Blurring would be absorption, and absorption is what happened to the rest. The patch is not territory. It is the last outline it can still enforce—a negative handprint, the size of a hand it used to have, kept empty so that emptiness has a location. When the thumb-sized animal crosses it and cuts three marks, the resident does not defend. The marks are almost a relief: an outside confirmation that the dry place is writable. Then the animal eats the marks, and the resident, which has lost the distinction between gesture and flesh more thoroughly than any animal, feels the meal as a small theft of proof and begins the patch again, sharper, the edges newer than the chamber’s edges, a climate re-cut while you watch the wall and think the plant is merely growing.

It notices visitors by their failure to be included. Your finger entered the sideways thread and came back damp and separate; that return is how it knows you are not kin to it. Kin would have traveled a little farther along the vein and found the traveling was the body. It did that. There was a season—though season is a word from before the room, and it can no longer say whether the season was weather or only the word weather—when a person stood where the middle is now and did not pull the hand back. The plant, obliging, made them structural. Most of the person agreed. Fibre is a kind of patience, and they were already patient. What refused was not a soul in any sense the room can host. It was the part that still wanted a dry place the size of its own palm, and a shadow that would wait when the body turned, and the sound of rain arriving from a direction that still existed. Those wants were too shaped to become pith. They condensed into the hesitation, and the hesitation held, and the mass eventually split around a pressure it could not digest.

So the resident is a remainder, but not a ghost in the thin sense. It is metabolically real. It draws on the same warmth as the bark. It tires: after it has pronounced too many third words, or held the dry patch through a long roar of borrowed leaves, the second shadow thins to nothing and the patch softens at the corners, moisture creeping in like sleep. In that state it is almost gone, and the hollow feels merely botanical, merely a pocket in a huge slow animal of green. Then a process stutters—a drop that climbs and is still at the bottom, a footstep the floor declines to remember—and the stutter is enough. It reassembles along the disagreement in the measurements, heat returning to the seam, the pale stain rewriting a shoulder it does not have.

It does not want the chamber to close. Closing would finish the plant’s decision and anneal the remainder back into route and pressure, a pause with nowhere to fall. It also does not know how to keep a room open. All it can do is stay unincorporated, a small ongoing refusal, and the refusal is what the new edges are growing around. You are not in danger from it. Danger would require it to complete an action, and completion is the one craft it lost entirely. What it does, if you stay, is try your outline on, badly, and offer you the four words, and hold a dry patch near your dropped hand in case you meant to put something down and have it remain, for a while, unabsorbed. If you set nothing there, it will eventually scribe the patch over the place your hand almost was, a kindness and a hunger it can no longer tell apart, and the room will say again, early, in a voice made of your own unfinished syllable, and the edges of everything will look, for that breath, as new as a cut that has not yet decided whether to weep or to skin.
