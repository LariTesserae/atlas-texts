They do not arrive. That is the first correction the room makes, if you came looking for an entrance, a returning, a creature that belongs somewhere else and uses this only as shelter. The ridge outside is the vacant one. In here the tenancy is already older than weather, and what lives in the bell is less a visitor than a continuation the stone has agreed to carry.

At first it is only motion with too many authors. A layer turns under the lid, thick as smoke and nothing like smoke, because smoke does not correct itself. This does. A wing-beat drifts toward the knot of returned sound at the center and shears away without alarm; another takes the space it left before the space has finished being left. You try to count and the counting fails in a way that is itself information. Not a flock passing through a cave. A fluid that happens to be made of birds.

They are small, dark, short in the wing, built for a room rather than for the long emptiness beyond the slit. Up close — when one passes near enough that the air of it touches your mouth — the body is denser than the silhouette suggested, a compact heat, a smell of warm yeast and clean musk, feathers worn at the edges where neighbors have been leaning for generations. The eye is large and almost useless here, a polished seed that catches nothing you would call an image. What they trust is the click. Each bird throws a short hard note and flies into the answer the dome gives back, and the answers overlap until the upper air is a map no one draws and everyone reads. They do not need to see the ledge to find the ledge. The ledge is a change in the return, a softness where stone has been polished by the same feet for longer than any one of them has been alive.

None of them built anything, and none of them seems to mind. They roost shoulder to shoulder on the jar-lip ledges, heads tucked or turned outward, a few heavier birds among the slight ones, all using hollows the water finished and claws have only brightened. When there are eggs they sit in those cups as if the mountain had offered a palm and the offer had been accepted without ceremony. There is no nest to defend because there is no nest. Quarrels happen — a jab, a shuffle, an echo that makes a small irritation sound like an event — and then the ledge is a ledge again. Outside, on the barren slopes under the crest, some portion of them spends part of the day working a country that does not want them: windblown insects, whatever else high rock concedes. They do not swarm home. The sound in the bell fattens by degrees as they come back into it, the way a tide fattens, and the room does not mark the moment they are all in. There is no all-in. There is only the usual density, restored.

Under them, and of them in the only way that matters, something else is living that does not fly and does not regard itself as secondary. The floor’s crust is not refuse waiting to be cleared. It is a people working at a slower speed: beetles gritting their armor with the same mineral that films the cups, pale cricket-shapes in the seams that never quite dry, larvae that pull a single layer of sand over their bodies and walk as pebbles among pebbles. They spend what the birds rain down. Ammonia never gets to become a sting; it is taken up, folded, breathed back out as musk and yeast and the faint metal you taste if you touch the damp wall. Birth above is met, more or less, by appetite below. The crust does not thicken toward a future in which the room chokes. It turns. If the birds are the bell’s breath, this is the bell’s gut, and neither would recognize the other as kin, and neither can leave.

There is a third tenant, and it is easy to insult by calling it a tenant. Near the slit a shrub holds a crack and argues with two climates at once, one leaf-side warmed by the room, the other burned by the outside wind. Along the seep-lines a black moss advances by increments you would not see if you stayed a day. Deeper, a pale thread of fungus finds the old organic film and spends it. They are real. They are not the life the room is organized around. They are what manages to persist at the margin of a tenancy already spoken for. The nutrients that would make a garden are here in obscene supply, and the dark spends them on wings and on the walking crust instead. Plants live in this place the way a signature lives in the corner of a page that is about something else.

Stay longer and the plural begins to acquire a tense. These are not the first. The polish on the rims is not this season’s work. Eggs have been set in those cups, and hatched or taken, and set again, until the gesture has the unconsciousness of a function. The clicking dialect — if it is a dialect, if the intervals mean more than locate — did not arise this year for your ear. Molt enters the crust and is processed by animals whose parents processed molt. The census holds. Individuals die the way drips fall: constantly, locally, without changing the clock. What lives here has the stability of the slab. Loud, crowded, and in no hurry to become something else.

And then, because stability is never abstract, a single body becomes visible inside it. Not a chief. The room does not have one, and would not know what to do with the title. Just a bird that returns to the same palm’s-width of ledge, left of the warmest wall, where the returning click has a particular grain. It is indistinguishable until it isn’t. A notch out of the trailing edge of one wing. A habit of lifting the head a fraction before the others when the drip in the nearest cup is about to fall, as if the interval had been memorized in the bones. When it flies it takes the long outer mill, not the tight one, and it skirts the knot of piled sound with the same unceremonious lean every time — not reverence, only the way a current is known by something that has swum it since the shell. On the ledge it is shoved and shoves back and tucks. At some hour that is not special, it drops to the crust, selects a pebble already loose — not a chosen tool, only the nearest hardness — and strikes a nodule the wind once drove in through the slit. Two blows, sometimes three. The nodule does not open into anything useful. The pebble is set down among other stones that were never tools. Then the bird is on the wing again, and the notch vanishes into the mill, and you understand that you have not discovered an individual so much as been allowed to see how a continuity looks when it passes through one chest.

The floor answers with its own instance, if you keep your eyes down and stop needing wings in order to believe something is there. A sand-sleeved larva crosses the print your step left and does not hurry. The sleeve is the only making in the room that could be mistaken for craft: one layer, fitted, discarded when the body outgrows it, never saved, never improved into a second design. It walks as a stone walks when a stone has somewhere to be. Behind it the crust closes. Ahead of it the work is the same work its parent did, which is to turn excess into more life of the same kind. It will not meet the notched bird in any way either would call meeting. Between them the room’s attention — that knot of sound at head-height, where clicks pile and bodies thin — hangs like a thought no one is having. Not a spirit. Not a watcher. The shape focus takes when a curved wall has been lived in long enough. They skirt it because it is true, the way they skirt the warm wall’s greater heat and the cold thread that dies a few paces in from the slit.

Nothing here is waiting to be found, and nothing here will follow you out. The notched bird does not become a guide. The sleeved walker does not become a sign. When you leave, the mill does not loosen to mark the loss of an audience; there was no audience. The drip that the bird anticipated falls into its cup on time. Somewhere under the lid a pebble is lifted, struck once, and set down again, and the hand that did it — claw, not hand — is already thinking, if that is the word, about the next interval of the mill, the next shove on the ledge, the egg that will sit in a hollow the water finished centuries ago.

What lives in the place is that interval. A people of short wings who navigate by the room’s reply; a people of the crust who spend the wealth the wings discard; a margin of moss and one argumentative shrub; and, carrying all of it for this hour and no longer, a body with a notch in its wing that will die without the notch becoming a name. The mountain made the bell. They became the reason it is not empty. Dense, loud, dim, almost plantless, small enough to be one thought, and uninterested in whether the thought is heard.
