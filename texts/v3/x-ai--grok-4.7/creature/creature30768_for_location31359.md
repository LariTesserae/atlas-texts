They are not numerous enough to be a people in the way the shelves are a country, and they are not scarce enough to be a secret. You notice them the way you notice the glass spine: by a difference that is almost a courtesy. The herds are moving as herds move here, line and pause and line, and one shape in the line is not cropping. It stands with the same weight, the same refusal to startle, and it is watching the cloud-sea the way the others watch grass. When the line moves on, it does not. The gap closes without comment. That is the first time you see one, and you will not be sure, afterward, that you did.

Closer, the body declares itself slowly, and not all at once as a category. Upright, but not tall—about the height of the pale posts in the ring-basin, and built on a similar patience. The skin takes light the way the weeping walls do: a film, not a color, so that in sun they read as wet clay and in the pepper-leaf as a darker green than they are. Heat does not sit on them. It seems to pass through and continue into the ground, as if the arrangement the place made with warmth had been extended, locally, to flesh. They do not sweat. When the brief warm rain comes they do not hunch. Water beads on the shoulders and is allowed to finish its errand.

The face is a face, and then it is more specific than that. Eyes with the same double lid as the finger-long waders, which you only understand when one of them blinks against a thread of condensate and the second lid follows a fraction later, unhurried, not quite in time with the first. No dramatic difference in the mouth. What is strange is the attention. They look at a thing until the thing has finished being looked at. A platter-leaf tipping. A vein of the pale crust carrying water a little way uphill. You. The look does not sharpen into appraisal and it does not soften into welcome. It completes, and then it is done, and they are already in the next small labor, which was waiting without impatience.

What they do all day is not farming, though the basins would forgive it, and not hunting, though the herds would not run. They walk the agreed threads between wet places, and at certain points they stop and put a hand to the film on the rock, or to the dark glass where it sweats harder than stone, or to the inside of a post in the ring. The hand stays. Something in the thick air changes by a degree you feel on the inside of your arm and cannot name—less sweet, or more, a weight shifting its seat in the lungs and settling again. Then they take the hand away and go on. If this is maintenance, it is the maintenance of a song by people who have forgotten the words and kept the pitch. They do not discuss it. When two of them meet on a shoulder they stand near enough to share the same tick of falling water, and some exchange occurs that does not require mouths: a long shared look at the wrong-shadowed tower, a small adjustment of stance, and then each takes the path the other has just left, as if the route were a single loop and they were only its moving parts.

They eat what the shelves offer without ceremony and without waste that lasts. A palm of the pepper-leaf, crushed, spat, the pulp pressed back into the film so the place can have it again. Eggs of the sheet-things, taken from the edge of a drift and not from the heart of it. Now and then a grazer that has lain down and not risen—the herds permit this; nothing here hurries, including dying—and the body is opened along the grain of the muscle and portioned, and what is not eaten is walked to a weeping wall and pressed into the moss until the wall accepts it. By morning the moss has made its decision. They do not cook. The heat is already in everything, and fire would be an argument the country has declined to have.

They sleep in the open, or in the lee of the works, never in anything you would call a house. Night thickens the condensate until their skin holds a bright scatter of lenses, and they lie where the ground is warmest, which is often beside the glass, often inside the ring where the marsh is a hand deeper. They sleep as the calls sleep: thinned, not stopped. One of them is always upright. Not guarding. Attending. You can sit near the upright one for an hour and be included in the attendance without being addressed. If you speak, they turn the double-lidded look on you and wait, the way they wait on a leaf, to see if the sound will complete itself. Most sounds, here, do. Yours may not. They are not unkind about the failure. Kindness is a hurried virtue. They have a slower one, which is room.

Over days the room resolves into a life with edges. There are young, though you will miscount them, because the young are trusted to the sedge and the sideways forest and come back glazed with the same film as the adults, indistinguishable until one of them has not yet learned the second blink and does both lids together, a small incompetence the others do not correct. Learning here is erosion: it happens, and it has a floor, and past the floor it does not accumulate into a new shape. An elder is visible mainly by how little they interrupt the path. One, on the far shoulder near the tower, no longer walks the whole loop. Others divert by a few spans so the loop still closes through the place where the elder sits, hand on stone, doing the pitch-keeping with less travel. When that one is gone, the stone will keep a warmth that is not quite the ground's, for a while, and the birds in the upper courses will not mind, and the path will forget by the method the cliffs forget—grains taken back into the film, not piled into a marker.

They have no name they offer you. Among themselves they use a sound like the soft clap of a platter-leaf tipping its drink, easy to miss inside the real sound, and they use it rarely, only when the loop requires a body to be somewhere the body is not. Most coordination is prior to that. The place has already agreed on so much—where water may climb, where breath stays easy, where erosion stops—that a people living inside the agreement do not need a large vocabulary of exception. What they have instead is memory of a different order: not stories, or not stories you can be told, but a fidelity to the remnants. The tower's delayed shadow is not a wonder to them. It is a colleague whose timing they have matched their own blink to. Stand with them at the hour the shadow arrives late and lies a little aside from the sun, and you will see every upright head turn that same fraction late, a courtesy returned so old it has become anatomy.

If they were the ones who raised the works, they do not remember it as making. Making would be a haste. They remember, if that is the word, as the fine rings remember: by having not been asked to start over. The instruction too old to be read as one is legible to their hands, not to their speech. A newcomer who tried to learn it as a technique would be given the room, and the rain, and the herds' indifference, and would not be given the method. There may not be a method left. There may be only the continuance.

You can leave. The rim is where their attention ends, as cleanly as the weight in the lungs ends when you step down into the white. They will watch you go with the completed look, and one of them may walk with you as far as the last shelf, not to keep you and not to see you off, but because the loop had a reason to include that thread today. At the edge, where the air is about to remember it is high, they stop. The cloud-sea does not interest them the way it interests you. It is the outside of the agreement. They put a palm to the last wet stone, hold it until some small sweetness in the breath shifts and settles, and turn back into the heat, which was never weather, and into the ticking, which was never an event, and into a life that has already arrived at itself and is only, carefully, still being kept.
