It does not arrive. That is the first thing that keeps being true of it, and the last thing you trust. There is no track across the moss that begins somewhere else and ends here, no wake, no hour when the reeds part for a body that was not among them the moment before. If you came looking for a tenant you came with the wrong tense. Whatever lives in the basin was already distributed through it when the weather settled into its standing condition, and it has not since gathered itself into the kind of outline a hunter or a census would accept.

You notice it first as a correction.

The grasses hiss out of phase, the way they do, and then a strip of them—narrow, no wider than a forearm—falls into time with itself. Not silenced. Synchronized. Every stem in that strip knocks on the same beat for three breaths, then loses it again, and the strip has moved. Not by bending. The place where the beat holds has slid a little toward the water, the way a spotlight slides when the hand holding it is tired. Stand where the strip was and the air is fractionally warmer, and your footfall comes back on time, once, as if something had briefly agreed with the order of events and then withdrawn the agreement.

It is not large. Or it is, but not in the way size usually spends itself. Its extent is the extent of the mismatch it can afford to repair. On a still hour it occupies a pool and the bank that remembers the pool wrong, a few strides of seep-line, the underside of one weeping stone. On a loud hour, when the second register of sound is thick and the reflections are running late, it thins until you would swear the basin was empty and the corrections were only the landscape catching up with itself. Emptiness is one of its postures. It uses it the way a heron uses stillness: not as absence, but as a way of being located without offering an edge.

What it uses for a body depends on which rule is leaking hardest.

In the water it is mostly delay. A reflection that should show reeds shows, instead, a darker density inside the reeds, a place where the stems are counted twice. The double image is not a shape. It is a preference. The water in that patch lags the real water by the length of a blink and then, as if embarrassed, catches up all at once, and the catch-up throws a ring that travels outward and dies wrong—too round, too even, a circle drawn by something that understands circles better than wind does. If you put a hand in, the cold is ordinary until it isn’t. There is a layer, thinner than the skin of the marsh, where the temperature is the temperature of attention: not heat, exactly, but the feeling that the water has noticed which fingers are yours and is keeping them in a separate account. Pull the hand out and that layer comes up with it for a moment, a bright film that does not drip, then remembers it is supposed to be water and falls in separate drops, each drop striking the surface a half-second before it should, so you hear the splash and then see it.

On the rock it is circulation. The transparent stone you almost saw—the one filled with strata and a slow bright movement—was not a failed reflection. It was the inhabitant passing through a mineral it has had centuries to learn. It does not hollow the rock. It uses the fine layers the way a thought uses a sentence it has said often enough to stop hearing: as structure that no longer costs anything. Inside the stone the movement is colder than blood and slower than sap, a pale threading that follows the old engineered grain and, where the grain has weathered into nonsense, invents a continuation. That invention is the closest thing it has to a will. It prefers continuations. Breaks upset it. A fresh cut in the moss, a stick driven into the strata, a boot that tears a seam—these produce, not anger, but a local hurry. The click arrives early and from the wrong side. The seep opens wider than the rock intended. For a little while the inhabitant is visible as an excess of process: too much water, too prompt a sound, a reflection that shows the damage already healed, which it is not, yet. By the time you look again the healing has begun in earnest. Moss creeps with a patience that is not patience. The stick, if you left it, is being convinced, layer by layer, that it has always been a root. It will not finish the convincing in a day. It does not need to. It has the basin’s kind of time, which is long and slightly dislocated, so that the work it does in the afternoon sometimes shows up in the morning as already done.

It has no face, and this is not a lack it is working to correct. Faces are a kind of edge. Edges are expensive here. What it has instead is a habit of orientation. When you are in its current extent—and you are, more often than the empty grass suggests—the loudest wrongness in the basin arranges itself in front of you. The reflection that refuses to match, the pocket where color drops out, the channel that has remembered itself into a too-regular line: these migrate, without seeming to move, until they occupy the direction you are facing. Turn, and they are still ahead. It is not watching. Watching would require a place to watch from. It is including you in the calculation it is always running, the one that keeps the marsh in the shape of a marsh despite the rules underneath it no longer quite agreeing. You are a variable. Variables get a coordinate.

It does not speak. The second register of sound is not its voice. That register is the leak itself, the basin failing to keep its events in a single now, and the inhabitant lives in the leak the way a fish lives in a current: by a continuous small labor of not being swept into a single instant. Sometimes, if you are very still, the labor becomes audible as a subtraction. The hall-full-of-conversations thins by one conversation. What remains in the gap is not words. It is the rhythm of the subtraction: a pulse slightly slower than yours, then, as it notices the mismatch, slightly matched, then slower again, as if matching you cost it a coherence it would rather spend on the water. People who have stayed too long report, later, that they answered it. They cannot say what they answered. They remember only the posture—the mouth open, the head inclined toward the place where the sound had been removed—and the sense that something patient had made room for a reply and had not been disappointed when the reply was only breath.

It eats what the basin eats, which is imbalance. A reflection running late is a pressure. A sound arriving early is a pressure. A patch of colorlessness is a pressure. Left alone, these would widen; the rules would drift further; the marsh would stop being able to pretend it was only a marsh. The inhabitant spends itself on closing them, not out of duty—duty is another edge—but because an unclosed pressure is a place it cannot be. It is made of the difference between what the water shows and what is there. If the difference grows without limit, it grows without limit, and then it is no longer a life but a weather, and it has already been a weather, long ago, and did not like the lack of anywhere to return to. So it mends. The mending looks like nothing. A seed head that was knocking twice knocks once. A gleam under the surface stops tracking you and becomes ordinary mica. A pool the shape of a circuit softens, overnight, into the shape of a pool. In the morning the basin is a little more convincing as country, and the inhabitant is a little more tired, if tired is the word for a thinning of its extent, a willingness to let one stone weep without going inside it to tidy the strata.

It is not alone, exactly, and it is not many. There are gradients. Near the center of the bowl, where the water has no direction and the strata under the moss are finest, the corrections are frequent and small, and if you lie on the reeds you can feel them as a faint regular give, as though the ground were a chest. Toward the rim the inhabitant is rarer and more abrupt. A whole slope of grass will synchronize for a full minute and then fall back into argument. A reflection will hold your silhouette with the arm raised—the arm you have not raised—and hold it long enough that you begin to wonder whether you meant to raise it, and then the silhouette lowers the arm of its own accord, slowly, with a care that feels like courtesy, and the real light returns to your shoulder, and you understand that it had been trying the gesture on, not to copy you, but to see whether an arm was a useful continuation. It decided it was not. The decision leaves no mark except a brief warmth in the joint you did not move.

Nothing hunts it. There is nothing here organized enough to hunt, and it is not organized in a way that can be caught. The heron stands inside its extent sometimes and does not startle, because the inhabitant does not register as a motion to anything that is only looking for motions. The small dark birds lift when it synchronizes the grass under them; they settle again a few strides off, unbothered, as if a wind had passed that was not wind. The midges, if they are midges, thicken where it is thickest. Perhaps they are not separate from it. Perhaps at that scale the distinction the basin has forgotten—machine, field, life—was never available, and the film on the water is simply the inhabitant thinking in a dialect of hunger so old it has become sparkle.

It does not want you gone. Wanting is a concentration, and concentration is how it used to break, back when it was still something that could be pointed at a task. What it does, when you stay, is include the cost of you. Your weight on the moss. Your heat. The early sound of your breath in the cold air. The way you keep trying to find a center. It spends a little of itself keeping your reflection honest enough that you do not fall into the water reaching for a self that is late. This is not kindness. It is maintenance. You are a new leak, a walking one, and leaks are what it is for.

If you leave—and you will; the dimness deepens without becoming night, and the body insists on a night—it does not watch you go. The coordinate it assigned you persists for a while in the water, a slight preference in the reflections along the path you took, a series of circles too round to be wind. Then the preference is spent on some older imbalance, a stone that has been weeping in the wrong tense since before you were a variable, and the path forgets the order of your steps. By the time the rim is under your feet the basin behind you is only loud and green and cool, a finished country under a failed sky, and the only sign that anything lives there is that the finishing never quite completes, and never quite fails, and the grass, somewhere you are no longer facing, keeps a beat for three breaths and then lets it go.
