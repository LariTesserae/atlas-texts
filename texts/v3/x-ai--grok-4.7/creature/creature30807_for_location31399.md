What lives there does not arrive and does not leave. It is already the thickness of the place.

At first it seems only the small motions. A springtail ticks across a pad of moss and vanishes into a pore no wider than its own legs. A snail sets its mouth to a film of algae so thin the stone still shows through, and the stone, when the snail has passed, shows no less. Under the pads the salamanders lie in the same cool the water keeps, bodies the color of the wet they displace, so that a shift of one looks like the ground remembering how to be still. They breathe. The breath does not steam. Nothing here makes a difference large enough for steam.

The frogs are the first correction. There are more of them than the lenses of water can explain, if you count by pools, and fewer than a marsh would need, if you count by sound. Their eyes are open and their throats do not work. They sit with the particular slackness of animals that are not hiding. Hiding requires an outside, a thing that might enter and a moment in which it has not yet. The frogs have no such moment. They occupy the water the way the water occupies the peat: as the amount that belongs.

Higher, the birds refuse the idea of a flock. They are too evenly placed for that, one to a fork, and the forks themselves are too evenly made, as if the shrubs had grown their perches to the number of bodies and the bodies had remained to the number of perches. A bird shifts, braces a twig, lets it fall when the bracing is done. Another, elsewhere, has already picked up what might be the same twig, except that nothing travels. The usefulness moves; the wood does not. Their eyes are dark and unsurprised. They do not track a visitor, because a visitor is a change in the light, and the light does not change.

If you stay, the categories thin. The beetle on the fern rib and the beetle that is already where the first was going are not a sequence you can hold apart. The interval between them is the same interval as the drip, and the drip has worn its interval down until it is no longer an event. Feeding, crossing, settling, the minute click of a shell on stone: these are not acts performed by residents. They are the way the bowl continues to be full.

What lives here, then, is not a population. A population can thin. This cannot. It is a single standing quantity of life, portioned into skins because skin is how wet cold keeps its edges, but not divided by them. The salamander under your hand and the one in the next throat of water are not kin in any way that matters more than the moss between them is kin, and the moss is not a separator. It is the same continuance, slowed and greened. Birth happens in it the way a bead forms on a seep: locally, without announcement, replacing a bead that has already been taken back into the film. Death is the same gesture run the other way, so evenly met that nothing accumulates as absence. You will not find a body that has finished being useful. Usefulness here does not end; it changes grain.

There is no face for it, and that is not a lack. Faces are for directions. This has none. It has density instead: more life in the lower moss than in the air, more in the air than a dry place would allow, more in the shrubs than the light would seem to pay for, and all of it balanced so exactly that the surplus never becomes a swarm and the deficit never becomes a gap. The insects make a cloud that does not gather into noise because noise would be a concentration, and concentration is the one motion the bowl has finished refusing. Wings work. The sound is spent against wet surfaces before it can mean more than itself. Meaning, here, would be a surplus.

It is aware, if the word can be emptied of watchfulness. Not of you. Of its own evenness. A salamander displaced by a careless hand does not flee far; it fits itself into the next exact wet, and the wet accepts the fit without rearrangement elsewhere, which is possible only because every elsewhere was already full in the same degree. The birds do not call an alarm. An alarm is a future. They adjust their weight, and the fork takes the adjustment, and the twig, if it was braced, is unbraced or braced again, and the count holds.

Nothing in it wants. Wanting would be a season. The frogs do not call because the condition that would make a call worth making is already the condition of every hour, and an hour is not a unit the slot can cut. Hunger occurs and is met inside the same small circuit: mite to springtail, film to snail, snail to the bird that taps a shell once and eats, the tapping stone unmarked because it has been the tapping stone forever and forever has left it as it was. The circuit does not grow. Growth would ask the walls to give up a shape they and the water have already agreed on.

So the thing that lives in the bowl is the agreement itself, wearing bodies. It is cool because the stone is cool. It is quiet because every process in it has worn down to the same low note, and a note held long enough is no longer heard as a note. It does not look out through the slot. The slot is only how the sky fails to enter, and failure, evenly distributed, is a kind of roof. If it could be said to have a posture, the posture is the one the salamanders keep: laid along the wet, not gripping, not waiting, present in the exact amount the place can hold, which is all of it, and no more.
