What lives there does not announce itself by crossing the water. Crossing would be an event, and the nave has been built to have very few of those. For a long time the only evidence is a preference. Certain trays stay a little more open than the dosing schedule requires. A return pipe runs a degree warmer on one side not because the engine has drifted, but because something has been resting its length against the metal and the metal has learned the shape. The grease-pencil marks on the ceramic uprights are not all the same age. Some are the technician’s, expected, already half-rubbed by the collector’s pass. Others are smaller, made with the same pencil, and they record things no harvest list would need: a leaf that sealed wrong, a bubble that rose twice from the same root mat, a joint in the lantern whose shadow moves when the rest do not.

It has been here since before the water was finished, or else it came up with the first filling and did not go back down the sample line. Either account is too neat. What is true is that it treats the medium as native. It does not swim in the way an excluded animal would swim. It does not need to. The water is held so still, and is so completely breath in the only sense the room allows, that passage through it is a matter of leaning. A shoulder settles into a gap between trays. A hip finds the clip of a cable loom and uses it the way a hand uses a rail. Over hours the body changes its claim on the volume by inches, and the leaves accommodate it the way they accommodate pipe: they rest their weight where the weight is steady, and they do not distinguish.

At first it could be mistaken for another machine that has not yet been named. The scale is wrong for that. Machines in this place are geared down until their work is a suggestion, and this is slower still, but it is not regular. The pruning wheel returns on a period. The valves open by quarter-turns across hours. Whatever lives here misses its own intervals. It will hold beside a manifold through a whole flowering and then, when the collector arm comes, be elsewhere—wedged under a floor port, or high, palm to the underside of the surface, feeling the flatness that wind is not allowed to break. It knows the surface the way the leaves know it: as a ceiling that can be touched and will not answer.

The body, when the light finally gives it up, is not a visitor’s body. Visitors would still have the dryness of outside on them, the memory of weather, a seam of clothing that the water would darken in a line. This has none of that. Skin, if skin is the word, has taken on the patience of the ceramic: pale where the lantern strikes it straight, greener in the borrowed shade of the broad leaves, not from pigment so much as from long contact. Folds at the joints have softened the way bolt-heads here have not yet had time to soften; it is older than the room in that one respect, or it wears time differently. The ribs move. They move against the even press of the water and do not fight it. Breath, if it is breath, is a slow exchange with the formulated medium, in through the mouth and also, it seems, along the thinner skin inside the arms, where the vessels sit close. It does not gasp. Gasping would cloud the nave, and the nave is kept clear.

There is a face, eventually. It is not dramatic. The eyes are open more often than not, and they do not hunt. They rest on whatever the schedule has made interesting: a stem sealing around its polymer bead, a yellowing that was promised on an upright, the silver of a column glimpsed through the floor port and the ground that has not yet learned the room. The mouth is usually closed. When it opens it is not to speak into air—there is no air—and not to feed in any way the manifests would recognize. Sometimes it takes a finished blossom before the collector arm arrives, holds it until the color leaves the petals, and lets the pale remainder drift into the arm’s path so that the count still closes. This is the closest thing it has to a courtesy. The system does not notice. The system was not built to notice a subtraction that is replaced before the log is written.

It is not lonely in any way that would produce noise. People who stay long enough begin to hear their own joints; it has stayed past that, and the sound it lives with is the tick conducted through water, the click of a bubble against the lantern, the resettling of fronds after the wheel. It answers none of these. Now and then it makes a mark. The pencil is kept in a pocket of root-mat near the cabinet, where the relay lives, a place a technician would not store a tool because the leaves have closed over it. The marks are not language. They are corrections to corrections: a line through an expected yellowing that did not come, a small circle around a bolt that has, at last, begun to think about a tear of rust. It is keeping a record of the room’s early life, the part the engines do not log because the engines are told only to hold warmth, clarity, noon.

Does it leave? The drains would take it if it chose the drains. The sample line is too narrow. The surface can be touched but not usefully crossed; above it is only light, not place, and the membrane is tensioned, replaceable, not a door. Below, the pier’s fresh steel dwindles toward ground, and the floor ports are eyes, not exits. So it stays, and the staying has become a kind of installation. New panels lock in. For a day the ceramic is whiter than the rest. It goes to the white patch and holds a palm there until the leaves close over, as if the unlived surface needed a temperature before it could join the rest. Afterward the difference is only something you would have to remember, and it remembers.

What lives there is not an animal that got through the design. The design stands. No fish, no insect, no bird. It is the remainder the manifest did not have a column for: a slow tenant of the made water, older than the softness it is waiting to see in the joints of the room, at home in a cistern that breathes by pump and grows by permission. It will be here when the first rust finally tears, and it will make a mark for that, small, accurate, and illegible to anyone who still believes the nave is only crop and machine.
