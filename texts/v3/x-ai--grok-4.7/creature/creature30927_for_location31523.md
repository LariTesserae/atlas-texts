The bowl keeps them, and they keep the bowl. That is the first thing that stays when the silence outside has finished punishing the ears: not a creature and not a ruin, but an agreement so old it has become geography.

What lives here is the circuit. Air is its blood, and it has no other. It enters over the lip in the thin ordinary way of high country and is not permitted to leave as weather. Something in the dark needles and the smaller hidden filaments takes each incoming motion and turns it, a little, always the same way, until turning is all the hollow knows how to do. The circuit has worn its own vessel. The walls are the shape of having been sanded by the same idea for longer than the idea has needed a maker. Walk the rim and you are walking the inside of a habit.

Inside the habit, the lush things are not visitors. They are the rate made visible. A tree of iron-red branching, crowded with scales no thicker than fingernails, is not a plant that happens to stand in a gale. It is the gale's accounting, written in tissue that powders when broken and restates itself before the powder has settled. They stand far apart because the shear between them is part of their body. Touch one and you are touching a negotiation: built exactly as fast as it is milled, so the eye receives a form where there is only a standing argument. By evening a snapped limb has sketched itself back in frost-white lattice, not healed, restated. They drink noon and cold through a metallic film and have nothing that could wilt. Moisture is an insult they do not answer; the crust answers it for them, briefly bright, then innocent.

The small lives are the argument's fine print. They do not occupy the bowl so much as spend it. At any instant the volume is nearly empty, wall visible through wall, a person on the far side a clear figure. Across a minute that emptiness has been used completely. Seed-sized bodies bank in squads and reverse as one eye. Mica wings lift off the metallic film and vanish into it again. A skating layer boils up from the salt when the rotor's lower fringe dips, crosses the open floor in a ribbon that refuses to diffuse, and enters another plant's outer haze as if through a gate. They are not flocks. They are errands. Their abundance is a property of time, which is why a glance calls the place barren and a longer look calls it insultingly full. In the seams of the roar, where the voices cancel and the skull is granted three steps of near-hiss, you can see them as individuals: jointed, eyeless or all-eye, legs trailing filaments that taste the shear and correct their route around you before your step is finished. They do not flee. You were already in the arithmetic.

Sound is the body they share, and it has boundaries, which is what makes it a body and not a storm. The rotor is the howl. The plants are the chords, shifting key as limbs are milled and written again. The animals are the attack, ticks and zippers and a sizzle where a squad hits a standing wave. A shout dies as a feeling in the throat. The bowl is the size of a hamlet because a larger instrument would not hold this note, and a smaller one would have been sanded through. People who stay stop speaking and learn the seams the way other people learn doors.

On the eastern rim the agreement has a few marks that are not geology, and they are the part that will not let the rest be only wild. A course of blocks too true, half-swallowed. A chapel-shell scoured to the same finish as the floor, with a plinth grown over in salt-leaves, nothing legible beneath, the salt keeping only its own writing. And the needles, thumb-thin, leaning out in an arc no iron bracing would choose, humming in the bones of the wrist if a hand comes near. They are not a turbine. They are a kind of attention paid to air by something that did not need to remain visible as a someone. The bowl is the footprint. Stand near them and the roar gains a centering the middle of the floor does not have; the floor is where the centered thing frays into nested spins, salt sheets, a plant rocked to its roots and answering with a brief excess of white scales that the next gust harvests and the skaters pick apart before it lands.

Nothing accumulates except form. Dust is made and taken. Scales are made and taken. The small lives are divided and spent, and their spending is another glittering. The walls lose a grain and do not change. Water is abolished without ceremony. Late light goes amber and the films throw back copper and pale gold, only angle, not mercy. The circuit does not notice evening. It has not noticed evening since the rim agreed to be this rim.

So what lives here is not the plants, and not the motes, and not whoever set the needles and left a plinth to be unread. Those are organs. What lives here is the held motion itself: a storm that stays because it was asked, once, by a technology that looks like grass and shadow, and because the mountain finished the request by becoming the instrument. Lush, empty, deafening, bounded. Cool under an honest sky. A landscape that has already, long ago, consented to be the bed of a wind that does not pass.

If anything like a mind remains, it is not in the chapel. It is in the bias. A little, always the same way. Patient enough to wait out stone. Whatever attended to the air in that manner is either still attending, distributed through needles too small to see once you know to look, or it has succeeded so completely that attention and habit are no longer different things. The bowl does not say which. It only continues, at the exact rate the walls can endure.
