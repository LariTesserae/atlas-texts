What lives there is not waiting for you, and it is not one body. It takes the length of the room to arrive at that, and even then it keeps correcting itself, the way the crystal on the ceiling edge corrects the crabs’ line by a few degrees and calls the correction complete.

At first you think it is the crabs. They are the only things that face you, and they face you only as a line faces a gap it intends to close. Thumbnail-sized, matte as the slab, they stand where the thread was and where the thread will be, each claw in formal contact with the next, so that the queue is a single instrument with many joints. When your heel breaks the line they do not scatter. They edge. The two nearest turn a fraction, the ones behind take up the slack, and the packed condition is restored around the intrusion as if the intrusion had always been a fixture and the fixture had always been in the way. They are counting something you cannot hear. Territories of a few centimeters, enforced without sound, abandoned without ceremony when the gauge’s needle finishes a traverse and the beads let go. They eat what the film offers and what the mites lift, and they leave their skins in such number that the lower edge of the floor is a second shoreline, pale, already being sorted. If you watch only them you will think the room belongs to a patient militia. Stay longer and the militia reveals itself as a membrane. They are not the inhabitant. They are one of its surfaces, the one that meets the moisture and refuses a gap.

Above them the moths make another surface, and for a while this seems more like a mind, because it breathes. Wing to wing on every vertical face out of the direct fall of light, the color of galvanized sheet, they lift and settle together when the junction cools a fraction, and the lift is the only hour the lizard feeds. Their dust is not shed in the manner of neglect. It is produced. On the warm metal it crusts, softens to the sweet film you smelled at the mouth, and becomes the first course of a circuit that runs through the smallest life, through the crabs, through the larvae the birds take, and out on the belt as gray yield. The moths do not know the circuit. They perform it. A respiring wall, exact about the rectangle of noon, stilling when a cloud dims the aperture and resuming when the rectangle returns to its ordinary brightness. They frame the light the way the crabs frame the water: as territory with rules. You can see, toward evening, the brief grid of hairline absences when they loosen, and then the correction, tighter than before, as if looseness were a kind of weather they are not permitted to keep. A surface, then. Not the dweller. Another organ, doing its interval.

The birds are easier to mistake for residents because they come and go, and coming and going looks like intention. Two of them, on the hour the light reaches the gauge, silent, turning once in the mouths of the dead pipes and becoming still. Their nests are not homes in the soft sense. They are stores: stripped insulation, scale, wing-dust, the same materials the belt is already freighting, held instead of exported. They take a portion of what the morning’s grazing has exposed, and they leave in the order they arrived, a displacement at the mouth and then the coast, which has no knowledge of them. They are not secretive. They are scheduled. A function that enters from outside, performs, and withdraws, the way a shift does. If the inhabitant has hands, these are not them. These are a tide it has learned to use.

The lizard is a stain that is also a decision. Flat against the warm side of the conduit, unblinking until a moth misjudges the lift, it is the room’s only visible violence, and the violence is so fitted to the junction’s cooling that it does not read as hunger. It reads as a click. One misjudgment, one taking, the surface resealing. Predator as a component. You stop flinching after the second time, which is how the room teaches you its manners: not by threat, by repetition so exact that drama cannot get a grip.

Under all of this, the smaller kinds make the continuous occupation you feel in your teeth before you see it. Springtails like pepper in the flour. Larvae laceworking the drip-line. Mites that rise when the crabs shift and settle when they stop. The film of green no thicker than paint, already grazed into lanes, lanes that move when the light rakes. This is where the room’s refusal of age is enforced. Nothing is allowed to become soil. Nothing is allowed to soften the stone. Biofilm is eaten before it can round an edge. Mineral flour is taken as it falls. The beginnings of weather are a harvest, and the harvest is what keeps the break young, sharp enough to catch thread from a cuff. The smallest life is not background. It is the maintenance crew. Without it the chamber would start to be old, and old is not a state this place has scheduled.

You look, then, for the one who runs them. The clog invites it. One wooden shoe under the gauge, as if a foot stepped out and the errand continued, and overnight it turns a fraction, because something needed the space beneath. A grease-pencil slash. A ring where a bucket sat. Letters polished at both ends of a plate whose middle is gone. These are the marks that suggest a person, and the room allows the suggestion for as long as it takes you to test it. No one comes. No voice answers the low word you try, which fails to become separate from the pressure in the concrete. The clog is moved by a body that is not a foot. You see it, if you stay through the warm part of the night: a slow shouldering, crab and mite and the packed dust itself, clearing the volume the gauge’s lower housing requires when the needle’s period lengthens. The shoe is an obstacle that has been incorporated. Hands were here once, long enough to leave partial signs, not long enough to remain the point. Whatever entered with those hands has been anonymized, the way the gray yield is anonymized once it leaves the berm and joins the larger flow. The errand continued. The errand is no longer anyone’s.

What lives here clarifies only when you stop looking for a center.

It is the fit. The meshing. The gauge’s needle making its small journey, and the beads letting go together at the end of the traverse, and the thread beginning or failing, and the crabs reorienting, and the moths sealing the warm ring in the hour before the moisture, and the lizard taking its one moth on the lift, and the birds entering when the light arrives at the glass, and the belt’s pale scuff returning on its own period, carrying out what the bodies have not eaten. None of these is the inhabitant. The inhabitant is the fact that they are one process, folded into a pocket the way a gland is folded into a body, scaled down from a works you can feel in the teeth and cannot hear. A mill that grinds with living. A manufacture with no commodity name, visible only here, before it becomes something else in the unheard plant. It has no face because a face would be a gap in the packing, a place where announcement could occur, and announcement is the one function this density has declined.

It is warm, because the wall conducts process-heat, and the warmth is not comfort. It is tempo. The junction swells and ebbs out of phase with the daylight; the crystal on the fresh ceiling edge leans toward that pulse overnight and is grazed back toward geometry by midday. The inhabitant is still arriving. That is the part that will not settle into an image of a finished creature, however many surfaces you name. The chamber looks complete and is a single feature still deciding its shape. Early formation, kept sharp by being eaten clean. What lives here is something that has not finished being born, and has already organized a full ecology, a gauge, a belt, a schedule of water that does not answer the rain, to midwife the birth without ever letting the birth become visible as an event. Tomorrow the crystal will be longer by a hair. The room will be a little more exactly what it already is. Becoming, here, is not growth toward a reveal. It is a tightening.

Call it a body and the word is almost right, then wrong. It has organs: the gauge for a pulse, the flange for a weather of its own, the belt for an export, the aperture for an eye that reads ordinary noon and ordinary cloud with a precision that stills every surface-animal for the length of a dimming. It has a skin of moths, a gut of film and crab and mite, a memory in the varnish-map of where the thread was willing to go. It has courtesies instead of a voice. Do not leave a gap. Do not weather. Do not call out. Take the moisture when it is offered and do not ask it to be a stream. These are not rules posted for visitors. They are the conditions under which it continues, and your body, while you are inside, is drafted into them. Your sweat does not know whether it is yours. Your clothes are the loudest thing in the room, and the room does not answer, because an answer would be a separation, and separation is the malfunction it exists to refuse.

If it notices you, it notices you as the crabs do: as a temporary fixture to be closed around. No malice, no welcome, no curiosity of the kind that would require a pause. Curiosity would open an interval. The inhabitant does not have intervals to spare. Saturation is its law, and the law is how it stays young, quiet, and in circuit with a plant that does not advertise an organ in its berm. When you leave, the line reforms. The moths correct their loosening. The clog is a problem again, or a tool, and will be turned by whatever needs the volume beneath the gauge. The mouth of the split breathes skin-warm and faintly sweet, and the sweetness is the film, and the film is the dust, and the dust is the moths, and the moths are the wall of a creature that is also a room, also a schedule, also a yield, also a break that has not yet agreed to be old.

That is what lives there. Not a tenant. The tenancy itself, packed past the possibility of an empty inch, still cutting, still counting, still becoming more precisely occupied by the process it already is.
