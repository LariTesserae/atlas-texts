It does not announce itself by moving. For a long time the only reason to believe anything lives here, beyond the green and the heat, is that certain adjustments are too exact to be leftovers. A leaf does not simply drip onto a casing. It has been trained, or has trained itself, to the bolt. When the pulse comes up through the floor, the leaf’s tip lifts by the width of a fingernail and the drop falls true. Nearby leaves do not do this. They cup, they store, they darken. Only a few have taken up the work, and they keep it even when no one is watching the work be kept.

What lives here has no outline you can walk around. The mistake is to look for a body the chamber could hold the way a room holds a sleeper. It is in the collar of trunk that split and rejoined around the mineral scar. It is in the oil-dark growth on the buried disc, which is not moss and not quite leaf, and which thickens on the side where the old grease still dreams of turning. It is in the pale flanges that flower the color of worn metal, and in the way those flanges are always oriented toward the housings, never toward the rumor of light. Light is irrelevant to it. The seam high on the north curve is a leak in the agreement, not a sun. What lives here feeds on the pulses and on the disciplined water, and spends what it takes as sound and as slow repair.

If you stay, the distribution starts to show a preference. Not a center, exactly—a leaning. Along the west curve, where the bolted plates still carry half-eaten lettering, the vines are thinner and the stone is drier, as if that stretch were being kept legible out of some habit older than hunger. The lettering is not read. Nothing here reads. But the crust that seals every other face has been worried back from those plates by a repeated, minute abrasion: root-hairs, perhaps, or the edges of the metal-colored flanges drawn across the script the way a tongue worries a tooth. The words, whatever they were, are not being preserved. They are being tasted. Mineral, oxide, the faint trace of whatever hand once cut them. The being keeps returning to that taste the way the drips return to the same bolt.

It has no animal in it. No nerve that twitches, no mouth that could be called a mouth. Where it takes in water it does so along veins that have learned the pipe-routes, so that a pulse traveling under the floor arrives, a little later, as a brightening in a particular run of stems. You can watch this if you are willing to watch nothing happen for a long while. The heat rises. A stem that was matte takes a deeper green along one side. Farther on, a flange sweats a bead that is not quite water—clearer, slower, with a skin that catches the dim reflection like oil. The bead does not fall. It is drawn back into the flange before the next pulse. That drawing-back is the nearest thing to a gesture. It is also how the being keeps the chamber from ever granting a pool. Excess is not wasted. Excess is remembered and put elsewhere: into a thicker collar, into another layer on the flywheel-table, into the patient shouldering of a joint that has begun, after centuries, to consider opening.

It notices weight. Not as an animal notices prey—there is no appetite of that kind, and the absence of small lives is its doing only in the sense that it has filled every niche so completely that a wing would find no air unclaimed. When a foot falls on the uneven root and stone, the tick of condensate stutters. The hiss at the half-swallowed casing lengthens by a breath. Leaves that were merely overlapping align, very slightly, along the path the footfall took, as if the pressure had been passed from blade to blade and stored. The being does not flee and does not approach. It includes the new weight in the ledger the room was already keeping. If the weight remains, hour after hour, the inclusion deepens. A vine that had no interest in the seam of light may drift a finger’s width toward the warmer disturbance of a standing body. Not to touch. To measure. Heat against heat. The pulses in the chest of whatever stands here begin, after a time, to fall half a beat behind the pulses in the floor, and then to match them. That matching is not hypnosis. It is the being extending the same courtesy it extends to the pipes: if you are going to be in the circuit, the circuit will learn your interval.

It has memory, but not the memory of events. It remembers levels. The lip where seepage and rock agreed. The height at which a gantry slumped and was held. The week-long unrolling of a leaf across a particular plate, and the fact that the next leaf, years later, unrolled faster, as if the path had been worn into the air. It remembers the flywheel’s last true rotation not as motion but as a direction still faintly preferred by the growth on its face—the oil-dark mat is smoother one way than the other, and new filaments lay themselves down along that grain without ever having turned. Sometimes, when the heat peaks and the whole floor lifts by a hair, a sound comes out of the buried disc that is not settling and not drip: a short, bright complaint of metal that has not moved, remembering how moving felt. The being answers it. Not with noise. With a tightening in the roots that cage the disc, a holding, so the complaint does not become a crack. Care, if the word can be used for something that has never had a self to set against another self, looks like that: the prevention of a break in a thing that is no longer for use.

There is no face. There is, after enough time, a place where attention pools. It moves. Tonight it may be in the west plates, worrying the last readable groove of a character that no longer means a sound. Tomorrow it may be in the collar around the healed pipe, thickening on the side where the mineral scar is thinnest, as if considering whether to finish a repair the stone already finished. It can be in more than one place, but thinly; when it gathers, the rest of the chamber goes a little duller, the leaves less exact in their dripping, the hiss less sure of its timing. Gathering costs it the wide, even attention by which it keeps the agreement. It does not gather often. A visitor’s long standing is one of the few occasions. The other is the rare moment when a new leaf threatens to block the seam of light entirely. The being does not want the light. It wants the seam to stay a seam—a measured leak, a known quantity of rumor—because total dark would change the way condensate beads on the high stone, and every change of beading would have to be relearned by the veins. So a stem will, over months, bend aside. The crack remains a hand’s width. The light continues not to travel far. This is not reverence. It is housekeeping.

If it can be said to want, it wants the continuation of the fit. The plants are not its garden and the machines are not its tools. Both are already parts of what it is, the way a current is part of a river without being the river’s property. A fresh break in a casing distresses it more than a fallen leaf. The leaf is within the green part of its grammar; it will be replaced by the same slow unrolling, the same learning of where to drip. A break lets heat out wrong, lets water go where no vein has been taught to fetch it. Then the being must spend a long season—longer than a season, longer than the word—growing a seal, teaching a new tilt, accepting a scar into the memory of levels. In those seasons the chamber sounds different. The ticks come late. The metallic settling happens twice where it should happen once, as if the hull of the room were working against a swell it does not recognize. Standing inside that period, you would feel the pulses skip, and you would understand that what lives here can be hurt, and that its hurt is a kind of inaccuracy.

It will not speak. The half-eaten script on the plates is the closest it comes to language, and it comes to that only as flavor and as groove. It will not show you an eye, because an eye would be an animal’s solution to a problem it has already solved otherwise: it knows where things are by how they load the circuit. It will not die in any way you could record, unless the pulses stop. If they stopped—if the buried artery cooled and the stone forgot the rhythm—the being would not collapse. It would thin. The exact leaves would forget their bolts. The flanges would sweat their beads and fail to draw them back, and for the first time the pockets might consider becoming a pool. The collars would loosen their hold on old repairs. The complaint locked in the flywheel would go unanswered and become, at last, a crack. That would be its death: not a body cooling, but a room losing the reason its drips are true.

While the pulses hold, it continues. It tastes the failing letters. It keeps the water in its pockets and its lip and its single disciplined fall. It lets the heat into its stems and spends the heat as sound, so the chamber stays loud enough to prove the agreement. If you are still standing when it has finished measuring you, it will set you down in the ledger as one more interval—warm, temporary, a little irregular—and then it will return its attention to the wide even work, and the leaf will lift by a fingernail’s width, and the drop will find the bolt, and the thing that lives here will be, again, almost indistinguishable from the place that has patiently become itself.
