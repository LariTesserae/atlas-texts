It is easy, at first, to give the pocket no inhabitant at all. The water is already so full of work that life would be a redundancy, and the eye, after a while, agrees to the vacancy: particles, a ladder that ends in nothing, a bell mouth that only drinks. If something crosses the tired leak of light it is easiest to call it debris. A glove. A scrap of crate. The same pale jointed flicker, seen once and therefore optional.

But the pocket keeps a schedule tighter than debris can learn. After the sluice slams, the ochre veil lifts and is beaten back, and in the minute of false calm that follows, a small refusal appears in the current behind the ladder rail. The eddy is still an eddy. It simply holds a shape that does not orbit. The shape does not glint the way a rivet glints. It takes the amber and keeps it, the way the tea-colored felt on the beams keeps it, and for a long time that is all the evidence there is: a place in the water that is slightly more place than the water around it.

Call it, for a while, a habit. It returns to the same stall in the flow, the soft water under a beam where scale can form and a body can rest without being spent. When the cross-current walks toward the intake, the habit is already gone from the path, tucked where the pull frays. It has the timing of something that has been slapped often enough to stop arguing with the slap. It does not flee in panic. Panic would be a straight line, and straight lines in this room are how things are taken.

The first time it is unmistakably animal, the mistake is the opposite of the one you expected. It is not the shrimp. The shrimp, if it was ever more than one bad look at a joint, is smaller, quicker, and does not care about the housing. This is longer than a hand and then, as the water turns it, longer than that — a body that had been presenting its narrow edge, the way a leaf presents its edge in wind. Pale, yes. Not the white of a cave fish displayed for wonder. The pale of oxide and old paper, with a warmer stain along the back where it has lain against iron. Along the sides, a row of dull plates, not quite shell and not quite the mineral shelves of the wall, overlapping just enough that the current cannot get a finger under them. When it shifts, the plates lift a fraction and settle, and the sound, if the roar would allow a sound that small, would be the tick of a scale-leaf deciding whether to stay.

It has no interest in the porthole. Light is not a direction it uses. What it uses is the stack of noise. The low mill of the pump is its weather-map; the polite hiss at the bleeding gasket is a landmark, constant enough to steer by; the thermal tick in the bearing is a clock it trusts more than any change in brightness. Before the sluice shuts, there is a change in the mill — a loading, a half-note — and the body is already flattened to the underside of the beam, plates sucked down, every soft part withdrawn. The slam arrives. The ochre veil rises. The creature does not swim into it. It lets the veil come, and in the folding water it opens, briefly, a mouth that is not a dramatic mouth: a slit under the leading edge, fringed with something too fine to be teeth and too stiff to be feelers, combing the lifted grit. Pulp. Bacterial film the pocket pretends not to grow. The dissolved edge of whatever the city has shed into its own throat. It feeds the way the walls deposit: by being where the stall is, and by knowing when the stall will be rich.

For a time it seems sessile, a fixture that has learned one trick of timing, and that almost satisfies the room. Then the impeller housing changes its note — not a failure, only the small complaint of a bearing that has been complaining for years — and the creature leaves the beam. The leaving is ugly and exact. It does not flex like a fish. It unpeels. The tail, which had been indistinguishable from a tongue of scale, releases last, and for three body-lengths it is a wrong object in open water, too flat, too slow, plates winking their dull amber. A jointed flick crosses under it — the rumor, the shrimp or the splinter — and the flat body does not strike. It cups. The water between them is turned into a small room, and the flick is no longer in the large room. There is no lunge you could admire. There is a correction of volume. Then the plates settle, and whatever was caught is already behind the slit, and the creature is on the ladder rail, holding with the edges of those plates and with a pair of hooks so small they read as damage in the metal until they move.

So it is a hunter, then. Except that does not hold either, not once you have watched the long middle of an hour in which it does not hunt. It travels the bolted seams the way a maintainer would if a maintainer could breathe the murk and had forgotten the word for air. It inspects. It pauses where a gasket has been bleeding and tastes the thread of warmer water with the fringe under its jaw, not feeding, only reading. It avoids the strainer-floor without ever putting a hook on it; the downward pull is a fact it carries the way you carry the knowledge of a stair. At the intake bell it will not linger. The bell is a mouth that does not negotiate, and the creature’s whole intelligence seems to be the difference between mouths that can be used and mouths that use you.

Up close — and close is a gift the pocket rarely spends — the head is hardly a head. No eyes that announce themselves. A pair of clouded pits, skin-covered or nearly, aimed not forward but slightly down, as if the only light worth registering is the glint off a rivet when the current rolls. The fringe is the real face: it maps pressure, grit, the sweet wrongness of pulp, the cold thread where a gallery leaks winter into the warm. The hooks at the rail are not hands. They are the same plates, drawn out and hardened, and when they set they look like the room healing a scratch. You could put a glove on the rail an inch from them and not pick the animal out of the iron until it decided the glove was weather.

It is old in the only way the pocket measures old. Not vast. Not legendary. Worn in at the edges the way the brick is worn, and repaired in the same places often enough that the repair is the shape. One plate on the left side is thicker, ridged, a record of a slam that caught it in the open years ago; it carries that side a little high, and the current has learned the asymmetry and pushes it, gently, always toward the safe stall. It does not fight the bias. It uses the bias as a road.

Nothing in the hollow answers it. The shrimp-flick, when it exists, is food or is not, and is never company. If there are others of its kind they are not in this throat; the pocket is a single ration, and the creature occupies it with the unemphatic totality of the pump. It does not call. It would not be heard if it did, and it has never had a use for a signal that the roar would keep. What it has instead is a patrol: beam, rail, the blind side of the yellow housing, the seam where the hiss lives, and back before the next loading of the mill. The circuit is not anxious. It is how a body confirms that the room which allows it has not been revised overnight by men with wrenches.

Men do come, somewhere outside. The creature knows them as a new frequency, metal on metal in a dry gallery, a lamp moving behind the smeared porthole, a change in the hiss when someone upstream touches a valve and then thinks better of it. On those days it does not feed in the open minute. It becomes wall. The plates take on the exact tea-color of the deposit, the hooks lock, the slit seals, and a face brought to the glass would see industrial dark and the suggestion of crust. It can hold that for as long as the foreign frequency lasts. Patience is not a virtue here. Patience is metabolism.

What it is, taxonomically, does not survive contact with it. Early on it was a flatfish of the pipes, then a shrimp enlarged by bad light, then a colony of mineral that had accidentally learned to unpeel. Each of those is a glance it outgrows. It is one animal, bilaterally tired, built from whatever first crawled in when the cladding was newer and the water first forgot the sky — some estuary thing, some sump-crab, some larval body that should have died of warmth and dark and instead found that warmth was a constant and dark was only the default. Generations may have passed in the pocket, or this one may have simply refused to finish dying. The room does not keep the kind of evidence that would decide. There are no bones on the strainer that match it. What it sheds, it sheds as scale into the same circuit that feeds it, and the circuit does not archive.

Watch long enough and a last correction arrives, quiet as the rest. It is not the genius of the place, not the spirit of the pumps, not a moral about cities and what they hide. It is hungrier and smaller than those ideas. In the deceptive glide between cycles it will sometimes drift into the most open part of the hollow, the yards of nothing, and hang there tilted, blind pits aimed at a ceiling it will never reach, fringe tasting a water that for a moment is almost still. It looks, if you insist on looking, like a thing listening for a surface. Then the mill loads. The half-note arrives. It turns, unlovely, exact, and is under the beam before the slam, mouth opening for the veil, a citizen of a room that is all interior. Above it, on a particular corner, a grate answers the sluice with a vibration no one walks over twice. The creature does not know the grate. It knows the slam that causes it, and the meal inside the slam, and the long middle in which a life can be made entirely of not being taken.
