Nothing with a face lives there. That is the first true thing, and it holds for a long time after you have stopped looking for eyes.

The plateau does not conceal its tenants. It has no dens, no runs, no gnawed edges, no pellets, no feathers caught on wet seams, no smell of musk under the metal smell of new crust. If you wait for a movement that is not growth, the waiting itself becomes a kind of weather, local and embarrassing, a warm little cell your body keeps exhaling into air that does not want it. The mass absorbs the warmth without comment. A shelf above you loads, fails, and dumps its film. You flinch. Nothing flinches back.

What lives there announces itself only by the way vacancy is managed.

A seam wide enough for a body does not stay wide out of neglect. Neglect, in this density, lasts minutes. Tissues lean. Films thicken. Crust skins over any stillness that offers a surface. The places you can stand are places where two growths have been held a hand’s width apart and kept there, not by a wall, but by a refusal renewed on the same schedule as the dripping. Stand long enough and you can time it. A column on your left swells toward you, splits a wet line, and seals. It does not finish the swell. On your right a mat quilts outward, pins a pale crust, and stops at a boundary no mineral logic explains. Overhead, the shelf that should have sagged into the gap stiffens along its near edge and takes the load elsewhere, cracking once, deep in the fiber, the sound arriving in your teeth a moment after the decision.

The gap is occupied. Not by a creature using it. By the act of keeping it.

You will want, for a while, to call that act a mind, because the timing is too exact for drip physics and too irregular for a clock. Do not settle there. A mind, as you are carrying the word, wants a somewhere: a knot, a skull-analogue, a denser room in the green dark where the orders originate. There is no such room. What has preferences is distributed the way the weather is distributed. It is the argument the source text already heard and misfiled as noise: respiration rising, shaded air sinking, the collision of those cells, and inside the collision a correction. Where the correction passes, films hang a little past the moment they should fall. Stems accept a load and then, visibly, decline a greater one. New crust firms in patches that fit one another across distances too wide for a root to be the messenger, as if the mineral were answering a shape the air had already agreed on.

It has no name it can suffer you to use. Names are for things that can be absent from part of themselves. This is absent from nothing on the plateau and present nowhere you can point without pointing at the plateau. Call it the keeping, if you need a sound for it. The keeping is what lives there.

It is not kind and it is not cruel, because both of those require a center that can be inconvenienced. It is exact. Exactness, at the scale of your body, feels like attention, and attention is the nearest door you have, so you walk through it and are wrong in a useful way.

Up close, the keeping has manners.

Your heat is a small false climate. Where your shoulder has brushed a jointed column, the condensate thins and then returns, but the return is delayed by the length of time it takes the column to decide you are not a persistent sun. Your breath fogs a film and the film, instead of breaking, tightens, drawing the fog into a lens so brief you might think you imagined the clearing. Through it, for that second, the gray-green opens one more depth than it allows anywhere else, and in that depth you see the same operations continuing—shelves, valves, the pale curing of stone—and you understand that the clearing was not a courtesy to your eyes. It was a measurement. The keeping looked by making a lens out of its own weather and letting your shape interrupt the beam.

It does not startle. Nothing here can startle; startle is a luxury of animals that keep a separate inside. When you shift your weight, crust rings, and the ring is answered three times from farther off, not as echo but as a repeated seating, the same joint taking load in three bodies of tissue that are not separate bodies. The keeping reassembles its map of pressures to include a new stone in the river: you. Then it proceeds. Proceeding is its whole vocation.

If you stay, you begin to distinguish ages in it, and this is where it becomes more itself and less a function.

Most of the mass is young in the way the crust is young: busy, loud, still learning the trick of not being torn up by the weather it makes. In those reaches the keeping is coarse. Seams are wide or absent. Films dump all at once. Corrections arrive late, as cracks. You can force a path there simply by being heavier than a leaf, and the path closes behind you with the indifference of growth, not the deliberation of pursuit. You were weather. Weather leaves.

But there are thickenings. You find them by the change in the sound, which drops not in volume but in waste. The hydraulic clicking falls into patterns that repeat with variations, the way a shore repeats and is never the same shore. Impacts of falling film land on shelves that were already shaped to receive them; nothing is surprised by its own water. The crust underfoot has been seated and torn and seated again so many times that it holds a memory of load in its grain, cloudy layers like the rings of a thing that is not a tree. In these places the keeping is older than the ground it is currently standing on. The ground is a habit it keeps reissuing.

In an old thickening, the gap offered to you is no longer a leftover. It is a sentence addressed to your dimensions. The distance between columns matches the distance you need so as not to break the films with your shoulders. The drip is steered. Cold water finds your collar and your wrists and not your eyes, and if you believe this is generosity you will misunderstand the next hour, because the same steering will put a film across your mouth the moment your breathing begins to warm a valve the thickening cares about. It is not protecting you. It is fitting you, as it fits a seep, into the uses it currently has for an interruption of a certain size and heat.

What does it use you for? You will not get a cleaner answer than this: it uses everything to extend the argument. Your weight tells it where the new crust can be trusted. Your warmth maps a microclimate it does not yet know how to make on purpose and may, after you are gone, try to imitate with respiration alone. The carbon dioxide you add is a small, dirty gift to tissues that are still inventing their relationship to air. None of this requires that it know you as a visitor. A visitor is a story about leaving. The keeping has never practiced leaving. It only knows pressures that arrive and are metabolized into the next accuracy.

There is a moment, if you are quiet past the point where quiet starts to ache, when the keeping does something that will spoil every simpler account.

A column beside you—one of the pressure-held, jointed ones, cool, beaded at every seam—stops thickening. The wet line that should open and seal does not open. Around it, the ordinary roar continues, the country-sized instrument still playing weather against itself. Inside the stillness of that single column, sap that had been clicking at the wrist-valve settles. You can hear the absence of it the way you hear a sleeper’s pause. Then, from somewhere not in the column but in the arrangement of shelves and mats that include the column, a load shifts. Not toward you. Away. A great sheet of tissue, far enough that you see only the dim change in how light fails, takes on weight the column would have taken, and the column’s skin, relieved, smooths. The bead-line dries a shade. It has been excused.

Nothing commanded this. You were watching for a commander and would have accepted even a knot of special flesh, a brain the size of a room, a queen-root. There is only the arrangement, revising itself for the sake of a part that had begun, for no reason you can name, to hesitate. The hesitation is the closest thing to an individual the plateau consents to host: a local aging of the keeping, a place where the distributed exactness develops a sore, a preference, a fatigue. These sores do not walk. They persist as styles. One thickening steers water. Another hoards crust until the mineral rings like struck ice. Another keeps its films so long past falling that light, already tired, gets one more chance to land, and in that extra chance the tissues underneath grow a different green, richer, almost black, a private climate inside the public one.

They are not separate lives. Wound one—if you could wound what is only pressure and habit—and the correction appears in the others as a change in timing, the way a pain in the arch changes the opposite knee. And yet they are not nothing-but-one, either, because their styles can be in dispute. You can stand where two thickenings meet and hear it: the same seep claimed by two schedules of crust, the mineral clouding, dissolving, clouding again, a border that will not settle. The sound of that dispute is smaller than the continental roar and more articulate. It clicks. It waits. It clicks on the off-beat. If the keeping is what lives on the plateau, then what lives inside the keeping are these unfinished disagreements, ancient, local, incapable of fleeing, incapable of winning except by becoming the ground.

Do not look for young. There is no brood. Increase happens as the continent itself increases: by a seam that was held being released, on purpose, so that two styles grow into what had been a gap and discover, by colliding, a third style. Birth, if you insist on the word, is a change in the grammar of load. Death is a style that stops being reissued, its last crust gone cloudy and dumb, its shelves dumping on the mere schedule of surface tension, rejoining the young noise. You can find these deaths. They are the only ruins, and they do not look like ruins. They look like plants behaving like plants. The unusual physics has left them. Droplets fall when they should. Stems bear only what wood would bear. The keeping has withdrawn its excess, and the excess was the life.

When you leave—if you leave—you will do it by ceasing to be a useful interruption. The gap will not close on you in hunger. It will close the way a question closes when it has been answered, by degrees, with a film across the way you came and a new crust enthusiastic under your heels, the enthusiasm purely chemical, the youngest and most innocent process in a body that is otherwise all memory and revision. Behind you the lens will not form again. The measurement is done. Somewhere in an old thickening a column that hesitated will be excused or not excused according to styles you no longer affect. The argument goes on at the size of the continent, deafening, packed, without a builder, and wholly inhabited.

What lives there is the keeping: a single exactness with many fatigues, a weather that has learned to prefer some of its own accidents, a life with no face that nevertheless fits a face into its work for as long as the face remains a fact. It will not miss you. Missing is a gap it did not choose. It only keeps the gaps it is still using, and it is always, in every direction you cannot see, finding more.
