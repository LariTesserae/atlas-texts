What lives here does not arrive as a figure. For a long time it does not arrive at all, and then you notice you have been walking inside its attention the way you walk inside weather.

The first sign is that the basin already knows the shape of your rest. When you sit, the stone under you is the degree of warm that a body wants after a climb, and it is only that patch — a hand’s width beyond your hip the rock is merely sun-warm, honest and less kind. When you stand, the kindness withdraws without a click. It is not hospitality in the human sense. It is the same adjustment that keeps a grazer’s bedding-hollow a little softer than the crust around it, the same one that lets dew travel grooves that are too regular to be only joints. Something here has a habit of fitting.

You look for the fitter and find, instead, the fitted. The long-legged grazers are not tended in any way you can point to; no one herds them. They herd the day. At the hour when the film on the ledges is thickest they face the empty quarter, all of them, and what they are facing is not a predator and not a god. It is a place where the light is doing a small extra piece of work — bending, briefly, as if a lens the size of a hillside had been breathed onto the air and then unbreathed. The herds take that bend as a cue the way river fish take a seam of current. Afterward they move to crust that has had time, since yesterday’s scraping, to knit its mineral film back to the even grain. They are not clever about this. They are local. The cleverness, if that is the word, is in the cue.

Smaller lives are more literal about their housing. In the rock cups, under the lip where the many-jointed pale things fold themselves out of the brightness, the stone is not stone all the way through. Scrape with a nail — you should not, and if you do it only once the place will allow it — and a layer thinner than a leaf lifts, translucent, faintly iridescent on the inner face, the same dark sheen as the hollow in the broken post. Under it the cup continues, wet, ordinary. The film reseals while you watch, not by growing but by deciding to be continuous again. The pale things wait it out with the patience of animals that have never had to fear a change in the rules. They eat the film’s underside. The film permits this. It is not a skin being wounded. It is a ledger being read with mouths.

The dark trees are older agreements. Each one stands where a seep has been faithful, and the faithfulness is not an accident the tree discovered. Root and groove have grown into each other until the distinction is courtesy. Cut a root — again, the place allows one mistake more readily than two — and the cut does not bleed sap only. A clear thread follows, colder than sap, and travels back along the groove toward the trough with the purposiveness of a dropped stitch being pulled tidy. By morning the wound on the root has the same too-even grain as the regenerating crust. The tree does not look grateful. It looks occupied, lightly, the way a house looks occupied when someone is in a far room and has not yet made a sound you could swear to.

Toward evening the occupation becomes easier to misread as a person.

On the spur, beside the three-course wall, the air thickens in a column about the height of a tall man and no wider than his shoulders. It does not shimmer the way heat shimmers. It holds. Dust does not cross it; dust goes around, polite. Your second shadow, the faint one, leans toward the column as if the off-light had a source at last. If you speak, the column does not answer, but the disk in the gully below warms another degree, and the mildness of the coming night steps down on its hour-long stair a little earlier than it did yesterday, as though a preference had been noted and filed. Nothing stands inside the column. Stand inside it yourself and the warmth is body-warm, and there is a pressure on the tongue like the taste of wet limestone, and for a moment your own pulse is counted by something that does not have a pulse of its own — counted, not taken. When you step out, the column remains a while, then loosens into ordinary air, having finished whatever comparison it was making.

This is the nearest it comes to a body, and it is not a body. It is a visit the work pays to the scale of hands and eyes, because hands and eyes have entered the basin and the work is thorough. On days when no one comes, the column does not bother. The posts do their slight iridescence at the raking hour. The disks keep their unaccountable warmth. The cups fill and give back. The books stay kept.

What keeps them is distributed the way the warmth is distributed: nowhere you can dig up, and everywhere the profile of the land has stopped changing. It is not a spirit in the old sense of a dead will lingering. Nothing here feels mourned or angry or holy. It feels maintained. The region beyond the ridge — the next shelf of rose stone, the next five trees, the next herd facing an empty quarter — is not a copy and not a separate mind. Sound does not carry between basins because sound is not the channel. What carries is the agreement: that high country may be warm, that dew may climb, that a crust may remember the shape of a mouth, that a night may decline to be cruel. One attention, regional, patient, with no face it needs and several it can borrow for the length of a comparison.

The animals know the difference between the attention and the weather, even if they could not say it. A bird that is being held in the air does not fight the hold; it waits half a wingbeat and is released, and the release has the timing of a correction, not of a mercy. The crack-mammals go still when something has hunted because the hunting, too, is inside the books — a spend allowed, a ring of witnesses, a darkening on stone that the film will read and close. They are not tame. They are employees of a system that does not pay them and does not eat them beyond its tally, and they have lived inside the tally so long that fear has worn down to procedure.

If it has desires, they are legible only as what it refuses to let fail. A dry week does not empty the cups. A night does not thin the air into the cold the elevation is owed. A scrape knits. A stranger’s resting-place is fitted once and then, if the stranger stays, fitted better — the three-course wall gains nothing you can see, but the spur holds heat in a patch the length of a sleeping body, and the second shadow lies closer to the first, as if the off-light had stepped nearer to look. This is not love. It is the way a careful craft behaves when a new variable enters and must be carried without tearing the weave.

Stay long enough and you start to live the way the grazers live: facing the empty quarter when the light bends, drinking from cups that are slightly fuller than the sky explains, sleeping where the stone has decided to be kind. You will not meet it. You will become one of the things it is accurate about. And if you leave — down the shoulder of stone, into air that remembers it is thin, into mornings that are honestly cold — you will feel the accuracy let go of you at the boundary, a small uncounting, like a hand opened. Behind you the basin continues, active and nearly silent. The column does not rise to watch you go. The work is already sure of the shape of your absence, and has turned its thoroughness back to the dew, the herds, the grain of the crust, the long finished profiles of the stone, and the next basin, and the next, where the same attention is keeping the same books under another name it does not need to speak.
