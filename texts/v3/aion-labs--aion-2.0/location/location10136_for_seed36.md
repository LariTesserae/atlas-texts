### The Everglow Caverns

Beneath the skin of the world, the earth opens into a vast, breathless cavity known only as the Everglow—a subterranean realm of permanent, feverish daylight and silent, claustrophobic life. It is not a cave in any familiar sense; it is a raw, embryonic womb of stone, birthed by some recent and violent geological dream, its walls still sharp with the memory of fracture. Here, the air is a thick, hot tonic, heavy with the scent of wet humus and the faint, sweet rot of perpetual bloom. It does not stir. Nothing stirs. The realm exists in a state of profound and absolute equilibrium, a frozen moment stretched into eternity.

The ceiling, a jagged tapestry of newly formed basalt and obsidian, arches over a space so open it defies its underground nature—a cavern miles wide, its distant walls lost in a luminous haze. From that ceiling, a constant, gentle rain of condensation falls, not as droplets but as a misty exhalation, beading on every surface. This is the only water: there are no streams, no pools, only this omnipresent dampness, a sweat exuded by the hot stone itself. The heat is geothermal, rising from deep fissures in the floor, a dry, baking warmth that makes the air shimmer above the cracks.

And everywhere, there is the green.

Vegetation is not merely present; it *is* the world. In the absence of erosion, in this raw and unfinished geology, life has rushed in to become geology itself. The floor is not stone but a spongy, interlocking mat of rhizomes and moss-like pads, centuries thick, a living substrate that gives slightly underfoot. From this mat rise great, bulbous trunks of what might be trees, but their bark is smooth and faintly translucent, pulsing with a soft, internal amber light. These are the light-sources—the Everglow Trees. Their canopies are not leaves but vast, flat plates of photosynthetic membrane, layered upon each other like the scales of some colossal fish, each one glowing with a uniform, shadowless radiance. The light is bright, noon-day bright, but it casts no shadows; it emanates from every surface, filling the cavern with a timeless, directionless glare.

The density is overwhelming. Every cubic foot is claimed. Vines as thick as a man’s arm hang in still, curtain-like arrays, their surfaces studded with iridescent, cup-shaped flowers that never open or close. Fungi of bizarre architecture—shelves of indigo, spirals of pearlescent white—cling to the tree trunks, not as parasites but as part of a seamless, composite organism. There is no soil, only more plant matter, compressed and living. In places, the vegetation has formed arches, tunnels, and even what appear to be rooms—hollows walled by living, woven tendrils, their interiors lined with a velvety, sound-absorbing moss.

Scattered through this suffocating verdancy are the built things. They are few, and they are old. A single, weathered monolith of a dark, non-local stone stands tilted in a grove, its surface etched with fluid patterns that suggest maps of stars or subterranean rivers. A low, circular wall of fitted stones, half-swallowed by the advancing moss, encloses nothing. A staircase, impossibly, climbs twenty steps into the trunk of a giant tree and ends at a smooth, blank face of bark. These artifacts feel accidental, like seeds dropped by a passing bird from another world. They imply a builder, but the tech-level of the caverns is zero. There are no tools, no mechanisms, not even the mark of a blade on wood. The plants have grown around and over these structures with a patient, gentle indifference, incorporating them into the eternal, unchanging whole.

Fauna is a rumor. Once, in the span of an hour, you might see a ghost-pale moth the size of a hand, drifting silently between the luminous plates. Or a cluster of glassy, worm-like creatures, sightless and slow, moving in perfect unison across a trunk before disappearing into a pore. They make no sound. No sound is made here, save for the almost subliminal *ping* of a water bead, fattened to breaking, falling from a leaf-tip to the mat below. That solitary note is the heartbeat of the caverns. Otherwise, the silence is total, dense, and textured—a silence of held breath.

The rules of this place are simple, absolute, and alien.

1.  **Stasis:** Nothing changes. Nothing grows, nothing dies. The fallen leaf from a canopy plate lies where it lands for centuries, preserved by the warm, dry air and the antiseptic qualities of the mist. There is no decay, only accumulation. The ecosystem is a closed loop of perfect efficiency, with every molecule of water and nutrient perpetually recycled within the living matrix.
2.  **Light is Life, Life is Light:** The bioluminescence is not a byproduct; it is the primary metabolic process. The trees convert geothermal heat into light, and the entire cavern community feeds on this light. It is a photosynthesis driven not by a distant sun, but by the world’s own inner fire.
3.  **The Geometry of Density:** Space is not empty; it is a substance to be filled. Any void is immediately colonized. This is not competition, but a pre-ordained pattern, as if the entire cavern is a single organism growing to a predetermined, dense blueprint.
4.  **Memory in Stone and Sap:** The raw, uneroded geology holds the memory of its creation sharply. The rocks are sharp, angular, telling the precise story of their breakage. The plants, in turn, have grown to mirror this geometry—sharp angles in the supporting vines, crystalline structures in the fungal growths. The landscape is a dialogue between the newborn rock and the ancient, adaptive green, and the conversation has reached a perfect, silent consensus.

To stand in the Everglow Caverns is to stand inside a frozen lung, hot and bright and full of a life that does not breathe. It is a world just born, yet already ancient in its stillness. It offers neither threat nor comfort, only the profound, quiet pressure of a thing that is, and will forever be, exactly as it is.
