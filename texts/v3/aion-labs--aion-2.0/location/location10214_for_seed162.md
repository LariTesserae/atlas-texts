You stand on a shelf of fractured basalt, glassy and sharp-edged, at the rim of a continent that should not exist. The air is warm, thick with the smell of ozone and loam, and carries a constant, damp chill that settles on the skin like a film of condensation. This is a high place – so high the sky has deepened to a bruised purple, starless and sunless, lit only by a diffuse, sourceless glow that casts no shadows. The light is dim, perpetual twilight, but it reveals everything in stark, flat detail.

The terrain is a brutalist architecture of geology: young mountains claw upward, their slopes not rounded by time but sheared off in great, angular planes, as if cut by a god-sized blade. Between them, valleys plunge into mist so dense it looks solid. Water here is a ghost – it does not flow in rivers but weeps from rock faces in slow, steady beads, collects in shallow depressions as black mirrors, and sometimes falls as a fine, silent rain that evaporates before it hits the ground. The damp is everywhere, a condition of the air itself, feeding the life that cloaks every surface.

And life is lush, absurdly so for such a starved hydrology. It is a dense, layered madness of vegetation. Mosses and liverworts carpet the rock in deep, velveteen greens and umbers. From them sprout fungi of impossible size – shelf-like growths that step up cliff faces in terraces, their undersides glowing with a faint bioluminescence. Trees, if they can be called that, are more like crystalline formations of resin and hardened sap, their branches interlocking in a canopy so thick it forms a second, uneven ground above the first. Their leaves are not leaves but filaments, dangling and stirring in the still air, drinking moisture directly from the fog.

The silence is profound, but it is a silence of vast, absorbed sound. The occasional click, like a stone striking stone, echoes across leagues and fades. Then, a low hum, felt in the teeth more than heard. This is where the industrial parameter manifests. There are no buildings, no smokestacks, no roads. Instead, the world itself is a machine. Great stone plates, miles across, grind against one another with a slow, tectonic patience, releasing puffs of vapor from their seams. In the valleys, pulsating veins of a metallic lichen throb with a rhythmic, hydraulic light, pumping fluids you cannot name from deep subterranean reservoirs up into the fungal towers. The ecology is the technology. The fauna are part of the apparatus.

And the fauna are abundant. They move through the dense strata like thoughts through a mind. Many-legged, chitinous things the color of tarnished brass clamber over the resin-trees, their joints producing soft, precise ticks. In the air, motes of light drift – not insects, but something more like animate sparks, swimming through the thick atmosphere. Larger shapes move in the mist below, their outlines blurred and shifting. They are not quite animals; they have the purposeful, interconnected movements of components in a watch. A flock of blade-winged creatures passes overhead in perfect, silent formation, then dissolves into a shower of light before reforming farther on. Reality is not stable here. It is a suggestion.

This is the breakdown. You watch a cliff face in the distance shimmer like a heat mirage. For a moment, it is not rock but a waterfall of molten glass. Then it is a sheer wall of buzzing, coordinated insects. Then it is rock again, but now with a new, fresh fracture that wasn't there a second before. Causality is turbulent. Effects precede causes. A fruit falls from a filament-tree, and a moment later, the branch sways as if to release it. The dynamic of the place is one of ceaseless, chaotic transformation, but it happens within that enveloping, dense quiet. The landscape is eating and remaking itself, an immense, living factory where the product is its own existence, fresh and sharp and new. It feels awake. It feels like you are standing inside the dreaming skull of a planet that is learning, violently, how to be.
