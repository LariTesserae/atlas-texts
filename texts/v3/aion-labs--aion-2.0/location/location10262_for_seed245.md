The world is a half-dreamt blueprint, a fever-chart of a god whose tools are stone and intention. It is vast, so vast the curve of it is a philosophical argument—horizon not a line but a slow, sick bending of space, like the rim of a ceramic bowl left too long in the kiln. The air is a constant, damp heat, 2.6 out of 3, a soup that clings to the skin and the mind. It is dim: a bruised, perpetual twilight from no discernible source, as if the light is bleeding up from the wet ground itself, a sourceless glow of 1.4.

Water is everywhere and nowhere in particular. It does not flow in rivers but weeps. Great, curving walls of a rough, sedimentary stone—or something that has chosen to mimic stone—sweat with a constant, warm condensation. Pools form not in basins but in flaws in the geometry, in the places where the angles don’t quite meet, shimmering lenses of water on surfaces that should not hold it. The ground underfoot is a damp, granular paste, not mud, but something more fundamental: the primordial grit of construction, mixed with this perpetual atmospheric sweat. The humidity is such that sometimes the air itself seems to precipitate brief, localized fogs that cling to the sparse, towering forms.

And the forms. This is a built world, total, 2.9. There is no natural substrate. Every surface, every angle, every plane has been placed. But the tech is primitive, 1.2. The scale of construction is planetary, but the method is hand-work. You see it in the marks: immense blocks, each the size of mountains, fitted together with terrifying precision, yet the faces of them are scarred with the crude, repetitive strikes of flint or bronze tools. It is as if a civilization of titans, armed only with chisels and boundless patience, set out to assemble a planet from raw geological stock, and then forgot their purpose halfway through.

The landscape is sparse, 1.4. The immense blocks and half-arches are not crowded. They rise from the damp plain with vast deserts of empty, wet grit between them. You can walk for days and only approach the base of another colossus. The emptiness is not peaceful; it is anticipatory, a canvas waiting for more strokes that never come.

Erosion is almost nonexistent, 0.2. Everything is emerging, raw, just-coming-into-being. The edges of the colossal masonry are sharp, the carved channels crisp. There is no weathering, no rounding by wind or water—only the water that is present seems to respect the forms, beading on surfaces without wearing them. It is a world arrested in the first moment after its fabrication, a snapshot of cosmic carpentry.

And into this stillness, this dim, hot, wet permanence, pours the turbulence. The dynamic is 2.6, chaotic change. But it is not the turbulence of storms or fauna. It is the turbulence of reality itself unspooling. The weirdness parameter is 2.8—reality breakdown. You witness it in the silence.

The soundscape is mostly quiet, 1.4. But beneath the drip of water and the sigh of your own breath, there is a sub-audible hum, a pressure in the inner ear. And then, without warning, a section of that perfectly fitted mountain-block will *shiver*. Not shake, but shiver, as if cold. Its geometry will briefly become non-Euclidean, its lines straining against themselves. A perfect right angle might yawn open into 95 degrees, then snap back, emitting a sound like a giant’s bone cracking. A pool of water might invert itself, climbing a wall in a shimmering sheet before collapsing back into its flaw. These events are scattered, unpredictable, and violent in their quiet way. They are the world’s sutures straining, its fundamental laws—of masonry, of gravity, of perspective—momentarily disagreeing with themselves.

There is no ecology, no vegetation, no fauna. Life never got a purchase here. But there are… patterns. In the condensing moisture on certain walls, intricate branching designs form, like silver veins or fossilized lightning. They last for an hour, a day, then are washed away by a new seepage, only to reform elsewhere, never identical. They are not alive; they are echoes of a possibility, a template for something that was meant to come next—forests or nerves or rivers—and was never installed.

The rules of this world are simple and horrifying:
1.  Everything is Made. There is no accident. The grit, the blocks, the dim light, the heat—all are products of a will, however distant or forgotten.
2.  The Making is Incomplete. The plan is unresolved. Thus, the physics are provisional, subject to local revisions and catastrophic moments of doubt.
3.  Water is Memory. It collects in the flaws, the unfinished joints, the places where intention wavered. To look into these pools is to see not your reflection, but brief, distorted glimpses of other potential forms this place could have taken—a spire where there is a pit, a bridge where there is a gap.
4.  Silence is Unstable. The quiet is not an absence of sound, but a compression of it. The turbulent events are this compressed potential erupting, reality correcting a mis-drawn line with a brutal, geometric spasm.

To walk here is to walk through the draft of a creation myth. The heat is the breath of the maker, still on the clay. The dim light is the idea of ‘day’ before it was fully defined. The terrifying, scale-less monuments are the first nouns ever spoken, left lying around. And the occasional, quiet unraveling of a corner of the world is simply the draft being edited, in real time, by a hand that has lost the plot.
