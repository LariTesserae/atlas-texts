You find it because the mountain has a fever.

Not a sickness, but a persistent, low-grade thermal anomaly—a single peak in the cordillera that breathes out air ten degrees warmer than its siblings, air that carries not the scent of pine or snow, but of ozone, hot metal, and the damp, sweet rot of a jungle where no jungle should be.

The entrance is a flaw: a vertical crack in a sheer granite face, wide enough for a person to slip through sideways. It is not erosion’s work; the stone here is too new, too raw. The edges are sharp, crystalline, as if the rock had cooled yesterday from some unimaginable melt. This is the **emerging** world—geology in its first act, before wind or water have left their signature. You enter a space of **hidden**, **pocket-scale**. The outside vista of endless peaks shrinks to the intimate dimensions of a single, vast chamber.

Inside, the **scale** redefines itself. The chamber is a geode the size of a cathedral, but its contents are **sparse**. Distances feel vast not because of crowding, but because each feature occupies its own sovereign emptiness. The air is **hot**, a dry, pressing heat that emanates from the walls themselves. The **light** is **normal**, paradoxically so—a diffuse, sourceless glow like a perpetual overcast day within the mountain’s heart, illuminating everything with a flat, shadowless clarity.

This is a place without water. The stone is bone-dry. There are no pools, no drips, no humidity in the air. Yet, it is **lush**.

The vegetation is not of leaf and stem, but of filament and lumen. Great, fan-like growths of iridescent copper moss cling to the upper walls, their surfaces shimmering with a visible current, like heat haze. From the floor rise thick, barrel-like trunks of something resembling tree-fern, but their “bark” is a tessellation of polished obsidian scales, and from their crowns spill not fronds, but cascades of fine, glassy threads that hum at a frequency just below hearing. Clusters of bulbous, translucent pods hang from the ceiling, pulsing with a soft internal bioluminescence, their color shifting slowly from amber to violet. They feed on something else: perhaps on the raw potential of the **reality breakdown**, on the slight, constant unraveling of physical laws. They are lush with stolen coherence.

For reality is unspooling here, quietly and **slowly**. This is the dominant **dynamic**. You see it in the **mostly quiet** chamber: a pillar of basalt across the cavern shimmers, and for three heartbeats, it is simultaneously there and not there, a superposition of existence and void, before settling back into solidity with a sigh of displaced air. A patch of the copper moss flares, and for a moment, the concept of “green” is not a color but a taste of mint and static on your tongue. The **weirdness** is not explosive; it is a patient thermodynamic gradient, a steady leak from a universe next door.

And yet, it is **industrial**. There are no **built** structures, no girders or gantries. The industry is geological, autonomic. The far wall is a vast, smooth plane of something like black glass, and within it, deep as a galaxy, you can see the faint, regular pulses of light like the firing of neurons or the cycle of a colossal piston. The floor in one area is not rock, but a porous metallic sinter, from which a rhythmic, deep-frequency thrum travels up through your boots—the heartbeat of a buried engine. In another, a vein of raw crystal runs across the ceiling, and along its length, without any visible mechanism, perfectly spherical, fist-sized nodules of an unknown alloy are being *grown*, extruded drop by atomic drop over centuries. This is a factory without architects, a production line scaled to tectonic time, building *something* from the substance of broken physics.

It is **high**—the atmospheric pressure is thin, adding to the dreamlike clarity—and utterly devoid of **fauna**. Nothing scurries, flies, or breathes in the hot, dry air. The only movement is the slow pulse of the lights in the wall, the slower growth of the pods, and the occasional, silent glitch in the fabric of space.

You stand in the center of a hidden forge. It is hot, lush with paradoxical life, and quiet as a machine thinking its first thought. The mountain is not a mountain here; it is an engine casing. The jungle is not a jungle; it is the engine’s cooling finery, evolved to radiate the excess ontological energy. And you are standing inside the first, slow, emerging stroke of a creation that has not yet decided what it will build.
