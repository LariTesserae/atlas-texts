## The Plateau of the Drowned Forest

It is a world not yet worn into memory. The air—what little of it there is—hangs thick and warm, a broth of mist and suspended water that beads on every surface. This is not rain, nor is it ocean in the conventional sense. It is a permanent submersion, a high, silent sea that clings to the shoulders of mountains yet unborn. The landscape exists in a state of aqueous suspension, a raw and embryonic realm caught between creation and dissolution.

**Terrain & Materials:** The ground, when it can be felt, is sharp. New rock, black and glassy, thrusts up from the shallows in jagged teeth and fractured plates, their edges still keen, unsoftened by time. Erosion is a word not yet known here; the stones are fresh from the earth's forge, steaming slightly where they breach the warm water. This is a high plateau, a tableland lifted towards a sun it never quite sees, drowning in its own birth-waters. The scale is open—vistas of gray water stretch to haze-obscured ridges, but the immediate world is dense, crowded with the vertical: rock spires, colossal fungal trunks, and the sinewy pillars of ancient roots.

**Water & Atmosphere:** The water is everywhere. It is not deep, perhaps never more than chest-high, but it is omnipresent, a tepid, mineral-rich blanket. It fills the air as a clinging fog, condenses on skin and stone, drips from the canopy in a ceaseless, silent patter that adds to the shallow standing sea. Light is dim, filtered through this perpetual aqueous veil into a perpetual, greenish twilight. The warmth is constant, humid, and biological—a warmth of decomposition and rapid, silent growth.

**Vegetation & Ecology:** Lush does not begin to cover it. In the absence of fierce erosion and with abundant warm water, life has seized the raw stone with violent fecundity. This is not a forest of leaves, but of membranes, fronds, and bloated trunks. Giant liverworts, thick as pillows, coat the rocks in spongy carpets of emerald and rust. Ferns the size of trees unfurl colossal, dripping fronds that curl into the mist. Strange, bulbous fungi phosphoresce with a sickly internal light, their gills weeping a viscous, sweet-scented sap. Vines thicker than a human torso twist through the lattice of stone and wood, not climbing so much as strangling and merging. It is a dense, layered, suffocating green, a biology that feels both ancient and entirely new.

**The Built:** And within this, the city. If it can be called that. It is a primitive urbanism, a settlement not imposed upon the landscape but extracted from it. Structures are woven from the living vines, lashed together with fibrous cords, anchored to the great fungal monoliths. Platforms of lashed-together logs, waterlogged and mossy, form precarious walkways over the shallows. Dwellings are mounds of clay daubed over frameworks of bend-wood, their shapes organic, bulging, like enormous wasp nests or termite mounds. There is density here—the structures crowd together, connected by rope bridges and submerged stepping-stones, layered vertically on the rock spires. The tech is primal: stone adzes, bone needles, fire-hardened stakes. There is no metal, only stone, wood, fiber, and clay. The city seems less built than grown, a symbiotic outgrowth of the drowning forest.

**Sounds & Dynamics:** Sound is absorbed by the water and the soft, wet biomass. The world is mostly quiet, but not silent. There is the constant, nearly sub-aural drip and seep of water. The deep, occasional groan of a great fungal column settling. The muffled knock of a stone tool on wood from within the mist. Fauna is rare—perhaps a few types of blind, pale crustaceans scuttling in the pools, or the distant, echoing call of some large, unseen amphibian from the deeper channels. The pace of everything is slow. Stone does not crumble. The forest grows, but imperceptibly, adding membrane upon membrane. The city changes over generations, not seasons. Time is measured in the gradual accumulation of silt on the platforms, in the slow upward climb of the vine-bridges.

**The Strange:** The weirdness is in the proportions, in the sense of inverted world. This is a high place that feels like a trench. A drowned forest that is not decaying but explosively alive. A city with no streets, only waterways and aerial paths. The light has no source; it is simply a condition, a green gloom. The water is both the sky and the floor. In the deepest pools, where the rock falls away, the vegetation emits a soft, collective pulse of light, a slow breath of bioluminescence that suggests a single, vast organism sleeping beneath the plateau. It is a world just coming into being, raw and strange and patient, dreaming its first, wet dreams beneath a sun it has forgotten.
