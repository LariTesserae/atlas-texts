The world is a throat. It is a warm, wet, breathing darkness carved not by millennia of slow drip but by something more urgent, more febrile. You stand not in a cave but in a chamber of a planetary lung, a vast bronchial expanse still glistening with the raw sheen of its own formation. The air is thick, carrying the damp exhalation of stone—a mineral tang mixed with the ripe, fungal scent of life without sun.

Above, the ceiling is a chaos of nascent stalactites, like the teeth of a young giant still pushing through the gumline of the rock. They are short, blunt, and wet, gleaming in the soft, pervasive light that seems to emanate from the very moisture in the air. The walls weep a constant, slow condensate, tracing phosphorescent veins of pale blue and green across the dark stone—not plant life, but a slick, symbiotic biofilm of chemosynthetic bacteria and complex fungi that pulse gently with captured geothermal energy. This is the only vegetation: a living, glowing skin that paints the cavern in a twilight of its own making.

The floor is a dense, tangled ecosystem in motion. It is *dense* with presences. Swarms of pale, sightless arthropods click and skitter over every surface, their carapaces the color of cave pearl and bone. They move in shimmering rivers, flowing around your ankles, their collective noise a constant, rustling static that underpins all other sound. Larger shapes, soft-bodied and slug-like, glide through gelatinous fungal mats, leaving iridescent trails. The fauna is *overwhelming*; life here exists in crushing, teeming biomass, a frantic competition in the warm, energy-rich dark. The air itself vibrates with the drone of winged things navigating by echolocation, their calls a sharp, stitching rhythm through the louder backdrop of skittering and dripping.

Water is present not as rivers or lakes, but as a condition. It beads on every surface, gathers in shallow, warm pools that steam slightly, and occasionally falls in fat, sporadic drops from the ceiling with a resonant *plink* that echoes. In places, it has carved shallow, meandering channels in the soft, new rock, but this is a landscape *emerging*, not eroding. The stone itself seems to be accreting, growing. In patches, delicate, crystalline lattices of aragonite sprout from the walls like fast-growing moss, crunching underfoot if you stray from the more worn paths.

And there are paths. This is an *open* space, a network of connected caverns stretching beyond sight, but the immediate chamber is vast, its far walls lost in a haze of luminous mist and flying creatures. Scattered throughout are signs of *primitive* building. Not architecture, but intervention: stacks of smooth stones forming low walls, rough lean-tos propped against boulders using lashed-together fungal stalks, shallow pits lined with heated stones. There are simple artifacts—a knapped flint blade left on a ledge, a crude clay bowl hardened by geothermal heat. The builders are here, too: small, nimble humanoids with large, dark eyes adapted to the low light. They move with frantic, *turbulent* energy, chattering in sharp, rapid clicks, constantly repairing their simple shelters, gathering swarms of insects into woven baskets, and fleeing the frequent, chaotic changes in their environment.

For this world is *turbulent*. The cave is alive with instability. Without warning, a section of the biofilm on a distant wall will dim, and the stone beneath will crack with a sound like splitting ice, exhaling a gust of hotter, sulphurous air. A pillar of flowstone might suddenly sag and collapse under its own wet weight, sending a cascade of rubble and startled fauna into a pool. The ground sometimes shudders, not with violence, but with a peristaltic ripple, as if the entire cavern system is digesting some vast, geological meal. The humanoids scatter and regroup, their patterns never settling, their existence a dance of constant adaptation.

The sound is *loud*: the skittering, the dripping, the drone, the crack of rock, the chattering calls of the humanoids, the squelch of soft things moving through wet fungal mats. It is a symphony of urgent, warm, wet existence. The light, while *normal* in quality, comes from everywhere and nowhere—the gentle bioluminescence of the walls and ceiling provides a uniform, shadowless glow, like an overcast sky trapped underground.

It is a world in its raw, feverish infancy, a subsurface womb where geology and biology are locked in a frantic, noisy embrace, building and tearing down and building again, all under the warm, damp, unblinking gaze of its own glowing skin.
