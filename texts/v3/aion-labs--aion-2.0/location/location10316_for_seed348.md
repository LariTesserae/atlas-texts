The air carries a permanent chill, a damp that settles into the bones rather than the skin. It is the chill of deep cellars, of rusted metal in perpetual shadow. The sky is a flat, featureless grey, not with clouds but with a ceiling of condensation that never quite forms into rain, only beading on surfaces and dripping in a slow, arrhythmic patter. This is a low place, a bowl sunk between worn-down ridges of dark, slick stone. Erosion has done its work here over epochs; the sharp edges of the world have been softened into curves, the steep slopes reduced to gentle, slumped inclines. Everything feels settled, final, as if the land exhaled long ago and has been holding its breath ever since.

In the center of this bowl lies the town. It is small, perhaps a dozen structures in total, but they are urban in their intent: multi-storied, built of soot-stained brick and corrugated iron, with the ghost of a grid street pattern still visible beneath a layer of gritty, damp silt. The buildings are not crumbling; they are weathered into a state of perfect, eerie integrity. Their windows are dark, some broken, but the fractures in the glass are smooth, as if sanded down by centuries of airborne grit. Nothing sags. Nothing leans. It is a snapshot of decay arrested midway.

This is an industrial place. From the largest building—a squat factory with a truncated chimney—comes the sound. It is a loud, dense, and complex thunder: the grinding of massive gears, the hiss of steam, the relentless *thump-thump-thump* of a piston driving something unseen. The sound never varies in pitch or volume. It has been doing this for longer than memory, and it will do so forever. No smoke rises from the chimney; only a faint, shimmering heat-haze, visible as a distortion in the cool air.

There are no plants. No moss, no weeds cracking the pavement, no hint of green. The ground between structures is a compacted slurry of ash, clay, and broken stone. In the perpetual damp, other things have taken root. Fungal shelves, thick as dinner plates and the color of bruised liver, grow in overlapping layers on north-facing walls. Delicate, phosphorescent lattices of mycelium vein the mud, pulsing with a faint, blue-grey light in sympathy with the factory's thrumming. This is a mineral and fungal world.

And there is the fauna. It is overwhelming.

They are everywhere, and they are still. They cover the rooftops, line the eaves, cluster in the open doorways like thick, living drapery. They are not insects, nor rats, nor birds. They are leathery, segmented things the size of house cats, with too many jointed legs and sensory pits where eyes might be. Their carapaces are the color of tarnished boilerplate and wet slate. They do not move to hunt or fight or mate. They are perched, clamped, anchored. They are listening. Every one of them is oriented toward the source of the industrial sound, their bodies vibrating minutely with the vibrations in the air and stone.

This is the stasis. The never-changing. The loud, mechanical pulse of the factory is not just sound; it is a tuning fork struck against the spine of the world, and everything here is locked into its frequency. The creatures do not age. They do not reproduce. They do not even feed, though their mandibles occasionally drip a clear, viscous fluid that dissolves the fungal growths, which regrow at the same rate. The damp does not rust the metal beyond a specific, stable patina. The erosion halted when the balance was achieved.

The strangeness is in the details, in the silent rules that everyone and everything obeys. Walk the streets (though you are the only thing that walks) and you will see that the drips from a specific rusted pipe always fall in the same sequence onto the same spot on a creature's back, which has been polished to a mirror shine by the endless, gentle impact. The mycelial nets light up in precise waves that travel from the factory outward, a visual echo of the sound. The air smells of ozone, wet stone, and the sweet, rotten scent of the static fungi.

Time is not passing here. It is pooled. The loud sound is the heartbeat of a place that is, for all its industrial grit and teeming, alien life, profoundly asleep. And it dreams the same dream, over and over: the thump of the piston, the gleam on the carapace, the slow drip, the soft glow. Forever.
