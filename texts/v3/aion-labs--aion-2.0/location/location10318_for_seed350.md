The world is a fever-dream of industry drowned in a warm, amniotic sea.

You stand—or rather, float, chest-deep in water that is not quite water. It has the viscosity of broth, the temperature of blood fresh from the vein, and it pulses with a slow, sub-audible rhythm that you feel in your molars. This is the primary condition: submerged. The horizon is a liquid line, broken only by the jagged silhouettes of half-sunken constructs and the chaotic verticals of a forest that should not exist here. The sky is a lid of bruised plum, leaking a dim, sourceless twilight that struggles to penetrate the steaming haze. It is never day, never night; only this perpetual, gloomy dusk.

The terrain is a paradox of youth and inundation. This is a young land, freshly vomited from the crust or dreamed into being by a fractured logic. There is no smoothness of ancient erosion. The few emergent rocks are sharp-angled, crystalline, as if broken yesterday. The seabed under your feet is a chaotic topography of sudden shelves, sheer drop-offs, and needle-like spires of black basalt, all slick with bioluminescent slime. The water has not yet had time to wear them down. It is a landscape in its first violent act of being.

And it is lush to the point of aggression. Vegetation riots in the warm, nutrient-thick soup. It is not the green of earth, but a phosphorescent palette of violets, deep indigos, and sickly, pulsating yellows. Giant pneumatophores, like the breathing roots of mangroves but swollen to the size of tree trunks, rise from the depths in dense thickets, their surfaces studded with iridescent fungi that weep a viscous sap. Canopies of broad, rubbery leaves the size of rowboats float on the surface, layered so densely they form a second, shifting floor. Beneath them, filaments of algae drift like submerged nets, glowing gently, connecting everything in a web of cold light. The density is smothering; every cubic foot of water holds a tangle of life, a sensation of near-touching, of being gently, constantly caressed by growing things.

There is no animal life. No fish dart through the roots. No insects hum in the heavy air. The silence of the fauna is a profound vacuum, making the other sounds all the more monstrous. The ecology is purely botanical, but it is not passive. The plants seem to listen. They twitch toward sound, their leaves furling and unfurling with a sound like wet leather. They are the only witnesses.

The scattered built structures are the ghosts of industry, swallowed but not digested. A rusted gantry crane leans drunkenly, its upper third breaching the water like the spine of a dead leviathan. The control cabin is a nest of cable-vines and phosphorescent moss. Farther out, the skeletal remains of a pump station stand on stilts, its turbines stilled, its windows blown out. On a slightly higher rocky shelf, the corrugated iron shell of a single barracks building sits, doorless, its interior a dark cave sprouting a garden of luminescent mushrooms from its floorboards. These are not ruins of decay, but of abrupt abandonment, as if the workers simply stepped into the water and dissolved.

Yet, the tech is not dead. Industrial might thrums through the soup. This is the deafening sound: not the crash of waves, but the deep, grinding groan of immense engines running without purpose or maintenance. It comes from everywhere and nowhere. From deep below, a sub-aquatic pump the size of a city block beats a arrhythmic *thump-thump-WHIRR* that vibrates the water into a standing tremor. From the haze ahead, the silhouette of a factory—a vast, blocky shape half-submerged—emits the shriek of metal on metal, the hiss of ruptured steam lines billowing plumes that are half vapor, half solidified light. The sound is a physical pressure, a wall of noise that fills the skull and leaves no room for thought. It is never constant; it stutters, escalates into screaming peaks, falls into ominous rumbles, a cacophony conducted by chaos.

And chaos is the rule. The dynamic here is turbulent, a storm-state of matter and possibility. The water does not simply flow; it eddies in impossible geometries, forming temporary whirlpools that spin clockwise and counter-clockwise simultaneously before vanishing. Patches of the sea’s surface boil without heat, erupting in geysers of iridescent foam. The floating leaf-mats tear themselves apart and reassemble into new, temporary islands. This is the reality breakdown: the young world is not yet settled into its own laws. Perspective bends; a structure a hundred yards away seems close enough to touch, then suddenly recedes into a vast distance. Time stutters—you might see the same steam burst erupt, freeze in mid-air, and repeat itself three times in a row. Solid objects flicker at the edge of vision, there and not there. The very light sometimes falls in sheets, sometimes in droplets, sometimes in slow, gelatinous strands.

The scale feels open, yet trapped. You can see for miles across the water, to where other industrial hulks and bizarre, sharp-peaked islands break the line, but the density of the atmosphere—steam, particulate matter, a kind of visual static—makes it all feel like a painting smudged before it dried. It is a region, a drowned valley of industry and aberrant life, caught in a single, enduring moment of catastrophic birth.

The warmth of the water is cloying, metabolic. The dim light hides as much as it reveals, turning movement into shadow-play. The deafening industrial dirge provides the soundtrack to a world where reality itself is young, raw, and screaming as it tries and fails to cohere.
