The city is not dead. It is breathing.

You first notice the sound—a deafening, layered roar that is not mechanical, but alive. It pours from every crack, every overgrown archway, every moss-slick stair: the shrieks of unseen birds, the chittering of uncountable insects, the low, resonant hoots and howls of larger things moving in the perpetual twilight. The sound has weight; it presses against your skin, fills your mouth with the taste of damp earth and blooming rot. It is the sound of life in overwhelming, frantic excess.

You stand on a street—or what was a street—now a tunnel of green. The buildings, each one a unique sculpture of fitted stone and massive, dark timber, rise on both sides, their facades swallowed by a second skin of vegetation. Thick ropes of liana hang like rigging from balconies that have sagged and fused with the trees growing through them. Velvet moss carpets every horizontal surface, glowing with a faint, bioluminescent dampness in the deep gloom. The air is warm and thick, carrying the fertile scent of decay and blossom. It is the warmth of a body, not of a sun—the sun is a forgotten memory here, its light strangled by the canopy hundreds of feet above.

This is a place of total making. There is no bedrock, no natural cliff face, no unshaped earth. Every stone was placed, every beam hewn and set, every canal carved. But the tools are gone, the hands that wielded them dust. The technology was only muscle and patience. Now, the architecture has been handed over to slower, more patient forces. Erosion has done its work and settled into a truce. The stone is smoothed, corners rounded, but the structures stand in a state of graceful, stubborn equilibrium. Water seeps constantly—not in rivers, but in a slow, diffuse weeping. It beads on leaves, trickles down mossy grooves, pools in the hollows of carved gargoyles, then overflows in sudden, quiet drips that punctuate the animal din. The moisture is intermittent only in its form: sometimes mist, sometimes drip, sometimes a brief, warm rain that filters down through the layers of life.

The scale is open, yet enclosed. You can sense the city stretching away, a vast, terraced complex built into the steep sides of a high plateau. Through occasional breaks in the green walls, you glimpse other towers, other green-choked courtyards, receding into the misty distance. But the sense of space is vertical as much as horizontal. The city is a dense, multi-layered hive. Vines bridge alleys; flowering shrubs burst from rooftops; the upper stories are woven into the lower branches of colossal canopy trees whose trunks are wider than the buildings they embrace.

And everywhere, the fauna. They are the city’s new citizens. Flocks of iridescent, shrieking birds with too many eyes swirl in the faint air currents. Armored insects the size of your hand click and scuttle over the stones. Something with too many legs moves just out of sight in a shadowed portico. The movement is constant, a rustling, fluttering, skittering tapestry of life. There is no fear of you—you are just another shape in the gloom. They crawl over your boots, brush past your shoulders in flight. The overwhelming presence is not hostile, but indifferent, a churning biomass that has found in this constructed world an infinite array of niches.

Time here is slow. The great changes—the fall of a tower, the gradual subsidence of a plaza into a swampy sinkhole, the slow march of a root through a wall—happen over centuries. The daily frenzy of the fauna is just a shimmer on the surface of this deep, geological patience. The city is being unmade, but so gradually it feels like a form of stasis. It is not decaying; it is transfiguring, becoming something else, a collaboration between the intention of its builders and the relentless, creative hunger of life.

You walk forward, your footsteps silent in the soft detritus. A warm drip lands on your neck. From a dark window above, two large, phosphorescent eyes blink slowly, then vanish. The deafening chorus never pauses. This is the world now: a made place, unmade and remade, a roaring, breathing cathedral of stolen stone and triumphant green.
