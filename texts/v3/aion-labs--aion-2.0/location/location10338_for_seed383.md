The city does not remember its builders. It only holds their hollowed bones, worn smooth by the patience of water and the breath of deep time.

You descend into it not through a tunnel, but through a collapse—a great shaft opened by some ancient subsidence, where the ceiling of the world gave way. The air that rises is cool, carrying a mineral dampness that beads instantly on your skin and gear. It smells of wet stone and something else, a faint, ozone-like sharpness that has no business this far from the sky.

Light is the first shock. It is not the flicker of torchlight or the sickly green of fungus, but a steady, pervasive glow, bright as an overcast day. It emanates from the very fabric of the cavern’s ceiling, hundreds of feet above. Crystalline formations, massive as inverted forests, hang there. They are not mere clusters; they are fused into a continuous, fractured pane, a second sky of solidified light. This "sun-ceiling" pulses faintly with a slow, internal rhythm, casting stark, shadowless illumination over the expanse below.

And it is an expanse. The cavern is not a tube or a chamber, but a swallowed landscape. It stretches for miles, a vast, open underworld. The floor is a plain of dark, polished stone, swept clean of debris by ages of gentle, dripping erosion. Rising from this plain are the structures. This is the urban parameter made manifest, but not as you know it. There are no straight lines, no sharp corners. Every building, every ramp, every tiered plaza has been sanded by eternity. What were once squared towers are now rounded hummocks. Staircases are smooth, flowing descents like frozen streams of rock. Archways have softened into mere suggestions, the stone flowing into itself. The city is sparse; vast tracts of empty, gleaming floor lie between these architectural ghosts, giving the place a lonely, monumental dignity.

There is no vegetation. No moss, no lichen, no root. The stone surfaces are bare, but not barren. They are sheened in a delicate, iridescent film, a mineral condensation that shifts from mother-of-pearl to a deep cobalt blue depending on the angle of view. It is slick underfoot, contributing to the pervasive dampness (0.8). Water manifests not as rivers, but as a patient presence. It beads on the sun-crystals high above, gathers, and falls in sporadic, single drops that ring through the silence with a clear, pure *plink*. Elsewhere, it seeps in slow tears down walls, collecting in carved basins and gutters that have themselves been eroded into gentle bowls. The air hums with humidity, cool (1.3) and thick.

The silence (0.2) is profound. It is not an absence, but a substance. It swallows the sound of your breath, the scuff of your boot, the distant *plink* of water. It is a silence of immense age and absorption, where sound seems to bleed into the stone the moment it is born.

Yet, the place is turbulent (2.8).

Look up.

The air between the sun-ceiling and the city floor is alive with motion. Fauna (1.5). They are not birds, nor bats. They are like shards of darkened mica, each the size of a hand, moving with a silent, frantic intelligence. They swarm in vast, shifting clouds—a thousand individuals moving as one fluid, chaotic entity. There is no pattern, only a constant, boiling reconfiguration. They flow around the rounded towers, spill over terraces, and suddenly contract into a vibrating knot before exploding outward again. They cast no shadow in the pervasive glow, only a fleeting dimness as they pass. And they are utterly silent. Not a wingbeat, not a chirp. Their turbulence is a visual scream in the auditory void.

On the ground, other forms of life manifest. Many-legged, carapaced things the color of the iridescent film scuttle with startling speed across the open stone. They move in sudden, jagged bursts, pausing, then darting elsewhere. They seem to be harvesting the film itself, or perhaps the moisture within it. They, too, are soundless.

This is the dynamic: a place of profound geological stillness and ancient settlement, overlaid with a frantic, silent biological frenzy. The primitive tech (0.7) of the builders is still evident, but only as morphology. A stone ramp that once served carts now serves only the scuttling things. A series of carved troughs, a primitive aqueduct, now channels only the occasional drip into silent, full pools. Tools, if they ever existed, have long dissolved into the general smoothness.

The weirdness (1.1) lies in the juxtaposition: the bright, silent, ancient tomb of intelligence, now host to a teeming, mindless, and voiceless ecology. The rules of this world are written in erosion and ephemeral motion. Time here is measured in the rounding of a corner, not in the turning of a day. Life here has divorced itself from sound, communicating and existing through pure, chaotic visual spectacle. You are an anomaly here—a thing that breathes audibly, that moves with clumsy, echoing steps. You feel the eyes of ten thousand silent, shifting creatures upon you, not with hostility, but with the indifferent attention one might pay to a suddenly stationary stone in a rushing, silent river.
