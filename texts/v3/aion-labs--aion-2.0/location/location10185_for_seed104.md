You ascend beyond the cloud-veils, through layers of thinning air that still hold warmth like a remembered breath—and there it hangs, a wrongness in the sky. A sea in the air. Not a cloud, not a lake, but a continental shelf of ocean suspended miles above the world, a lens of liquid so vast its own weather systems brew sluggishly within it. This is the Sky-Sargasso, the Aqueous Plenum. The ancient, the final.

The water is a warm, heavy broth, perpetually at blood-heat. It does not fall because here, erosion has won utterly: the very concept of ‘down’ has been worn smooth, made ambiguous. Gravity is a local whim. Currents move in lazy, spiraling mesas, and the water itself is so dense with dissolved silicates and long-chain organics that it feels like swimming through languid oil. Light filters from a sun forever muted by the depth and altitude, a dim, pervasive twilight the colour of tarnished pearl. The silence is profound—not an absence of sound, but a swallowing. Water absorbs vibration here, muffles it into something sub-audible, a pressure in the bones.

The terrain is one of sublime, ancient flatness. This is a sea settled into its final form. The bottom—if that word holds—is a plane of polished stone, smoothed over eons by currents that no longer remember haste. It is not featureless, but its features are vast, continental in subtlety: gentle rises that take days to traverse, shallow basins that could cradle mountains. Every edge is rounded, every surface buffeted into a soft, dreamlike geometry. In places, the stone is veined with luminous cracks, a cool, blue-white radiance seeping up—the glow of something older than light, perhaps the leaking memory of a prior cosmos.

And yet, it is lush. Life has crept into this impossible suspension. Forests of cable-kelp rise from the stone, not upwards, but in great, curving arcs that follow the local flow of buoyancy. Their fronds are blackish-purple, dense as velvet, and they move with the slow choreography of deep meditation. Between them hang gardens of siphonophore-like vines, colonies of translucent polyps filtering the rich soup. Bioluminescence is common—pulses of green and gold that travel through the vegetation like neural firings across a vast, sleeping brain. This is a lushness of saturation, not of frenzy; every cubic inch is occupied by some slow, breathing thing.

Signs of building are scarce, and they are ruins of a peculiar kind. A few spiraling towers of fused coral and glass rise from the plains, their tops crumbling into the haze. They feel less like constructions than like outgrowths, as if the landscape attempted to remember architecture and produced these half-formed dreams. No tools remain; no machines. If intelligence was here, it has been dissolved back into the biological murmur.

Fauna is rare, and where it exists, it is barely distinguishable from the landscape. Great, flat rays the size of islands drift through the middle depths, their skins crusted with symbiotic algae and slow-flowing lichen. They move once in a generation, their passage stirring up centuries of sediment. Occasionally, a school of crystal-fish will flicker through a kelp forest, their bodies all prism and refraction, leaving after-images that linger in the water like chromatic ghosts. They are less animals than temporary eddies in the medium of existence.

The active processes here are slow, vast, and deeply weird. This is a realm of reality breakdown. The water sometimes forgets to be wet; you can pass a hand through it and feel only a cool idea of moisture. Currents occasionally flow in impossible directions—inward, toward a central point that does not exist, or in perfect, geometric spirals that trace celestial equations forgotten by time. The light from the luminous cracks does not illuminate; it *informs*, casting not shadows but faint echoes of potential shapes. In the deep quiet, you might hear—or rather, know—the low hum of spacetime settling, a bass note of cosmic fatigue.

Storms here are not of wind and rain, but of concept. A ‘front’ might move through, a zone where causality grows thin. In its wake, effects briefly precede causes: a kelp frond withers before the nutrient current that would starve it arrives; a ripple forms before the stone falls that would cause it. Then the logic of the world re-knits itself, leaving only a lingering sense of déjà vu in the water.

This is the Aqueous Plenum: a warm, dense, silent, and impossibly old sea in the high air. It is not a place for conquest, or even for understanding. It is a place to witness the world dreaming, in its final, fluid thought before the long dissolve. Here, everything is submerged, everything is settled, and reality itself is quietly, luxuriantly, coming undone.
