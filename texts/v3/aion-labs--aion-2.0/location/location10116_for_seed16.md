Deep in the heart of a dead, airless world lies a secret kept by stone: the Cavern of Echoing Crystals. It is a single, self-contained pocket, a bubble of impossible ecology sealed within a shell of impermeable black basalt. Its scale is hidden from the greater void; you could walk the barren surface of the planet a thousand times and never guess at the cathedral of heat and life throbbing below. There is no water here. Not a drop. Yet the cavern is lush.

The lushness is one of mineral and slow light. Forests of crystalline growths rise from the cavern floor and hang from the ceiling, each one a faceted, branching structure of citrine, amethyst, and milky quartz. They are not plants, but they behave as such: they grow, they seek the faint, pervasive geothermal glow that suffuses the air, and they emit a soft, pulsed bioluminescence in hues of amber and violet. This is the source of the dim light—a twilight woven from a billion tiny crystal-fires, casting long, jagged shadows that dance with the cavern's slow breath. The air is hot, a constant 2.8 on some forgotten scale, thick and still, carrying the dry, ozone-tinged scent of energized silica.

The terrain is low, the cavern floor a gently undulating plain of smooth, worn rock, sculpted not by water but by millennia of gentle, abrasive dust carried on thermal currents. Erosion here is established, settled; every surface—the curves of the crystal-forests, the arches of the cavern, the built structures—is softened, polished to a soapy smoothness, as if the very air has been patiently sanding the world for eons.

And there are structures. A city. Primitive, urban in its concentration of intent, but alien in its form. It is built from the same substance as the forest: dwellings and towers are not constructed, but *grown*, guided by some lost, simple craft. They are hollowed-out mega-crystals, their interiors carved with rudimentary hand-tools into rooms and passages, their walls etched with geometric patterns that might be art, language, or mere vibration guides. The tech is primitive, pre-metal; tools are of hardened crystal and volcanic glass. The city climbs the cavern walls in terraces and bridges, a delicate latticework of inhabited light. Yet, for all its organization, the density is profoundly empty. Vast, silent volumes exist between each tower, each dwelling. A single bridge might span a gulf of darkness a hundred meters wide, connecting two points of light with nothing but hot, empty air below. It is a metropolis of isolated lanterns, not a continuous sprawl.

There is no fauna. No scuttling, no breathing, no eyes watching from the shadows. The only movement is the almost imperceptible growth of the crystals—a low, grinding creak if you press your ear to one—and the drift of sparkling mineral motes in the thermal updrafts. Yet the sound is **loud**. It is a constant, resonant hum, a chord struck deep in the planet's gut. It comes from the crystals themselves, vibrating sympathetically with the distant rumble of geothermal forces. It is punctuated by sharp, echoing *cracks* as a crystal branch, stressed to its limit, shears off and falls in a shimmering cascade into the depths. Wind, channeled through unseen upper vents, moans through the canyons between structures, turning the entire cavern into a colossal wind instrument. The dynamic is ceaselessly active: the slow, tectonic growth, the fall of crystal, the rise and fall of heat waves, the drifting dust, the perpetual, deafening song of the stone.

The weirdness is in the equilibrium. A lush, vibrant, active ecosystem built on silence, stone, and heat. A city without builders, grown rather than made. A forest that sings a loud, dim-lit hymn in a world without water, without animals, a hidden pocket of fierce, mineral life clinging to its own strange rules in the deep, hot dark.
