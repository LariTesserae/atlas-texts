The world is a screaming womb.

You stand on a fist of black basalt, thrust raw and teeth-sharp from the vertical sea. There is no horizon, only a curving wall of water that swallows every direction—up, down, sideways—in a roaring, heaving pane of liquid. This is not an ocean you sail upon; it is an ocean you are *embedded in*, a solid sphere of water the size of a cathedral, and your plateau is a mere fleck at its turbulent heart.

The water is warm, thick as blood, and it moves with a sickening, intelligent chaos. It does not simply flow; it *articulates*. Columns of it solidify momentarily into dripping arches before collapsing back into spray. Globes of it, perfectly contained, drift like jellyfish, showing scenes within—a flickering forest, a city spire, a starfield—before popping into mist. This is the **reality breakdown**: the sea is not just H₂O, but the raw stuff of possibility, unformed and overstimulated, trying on shapes and discarding them with deafening indifference.

The sound is a physical weight. It is the roar of a cyclone, the grind of continental plates, the shriek of tearing fabric—if fabric were spacetime itself. It fluctuates, sometimes coalescing into something almost like language: a booming, syllabic nonsense that vibrates in your marrow. To speak here is to have your words shredded before they leave your lips.

The plateau itself is perhaps two hundred paces across, its edges fresh and glittering with obsidian fractures. Erosion has barely begun; every cliff is a razor, every stone is a shard. The rock is warm to the touch, heated from within by a faint, pulsing amber light that seeps from hairline cracks in the ground—the only source of illumination in this dim realm. The light breathes, slow and syncopated against the auditory frenzy.

Vegetation is a memory, a rumor. In the most sheltered crevices, where a film of mineral-laden moisture beads, phosphorescent lichen clings in isolated smudges of electric blue and sulfur yellow. They emit a low hum that is lost in the greater din. A few sinewy, grey tendrils—perhaps roots, perhaps something else—wind from cracks, feeling the air like blind worms before retreating.

There are structures. Or rather, there are *interventions*. A low ring of stacked, sharp stones, too small for a person, sits near the center. Its purpose is unfathomable—an altar, a nest, a drain? Farther off, a single leaning pillar of roughly fitted basalt blocks stands, carved with spirals that seem to shift when not observed directly. They are not ruins; they feel simultaneously ancient and just-made, as if placed moments before your arrival by a mason who then stepped into the wall of water and dissolved.

Fauna does not exist in any enduring form. Things *flicker* into being: a flock of silver, hook-beaked birds made entirely of falling water, swirling once around the pillar before melting back into the general spray. A many-legged shadow, smooth and silent, scuttles across the ceiling of water overhead, inverted, and is gone. They are less creatures than they are transient ideas the chaotic medium briefly entertains.

The turbulence is ceaseless. The very scale and geometry of the place waver. The wall of water sometimes bulges inward, threatening to swallow a portion of the rock, only to recede and leave a new, dripping stalactite of salt and crystal. Gravity is a suggestion. You feel lighter near the edges, and once, a shower of warm droplets begins to fall *upward* from the plateau, beading on the underside of a water-arch before rejoining the maelstrom.

Time is granular, stuttering. You watch a rivulet carve a channel in the soft rock, and see the channel heal itself ten heartbeats later. The deafening symphony of creation and unmaking plays without a conductor, without a score. This is a place that has not decided what it is—a fragment of a world that died during its own birth, or one that is being perpetually born from its own dissolution. It is young, loud, saturated with potential, and utterly, violently indifferent to the solidity of things. To be here is to stand inside the thunder of a thought that will never be fully thought.
