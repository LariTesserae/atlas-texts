Deep beneath the skin of the world, in a pocket of stone that has forgotten the sun, the Weeping City hangs suspended in its own perpetual sigh.

It is not large—you could cross its entirety in less than an hour, a single, vaulted cavern no more than a few hundred paces across at its widest. But within that hidden volume, existence is crammed, layered, dense. Every surface is spoken for, every shadow occupied. The city is carved not from the living rock, but into a strange, soft stone that seems halfway between limestone and hardened ash. Over centuries, millennia perhaps, the walls have been hollowed into a labyrinth of domiciles, workshops, granaries, and nameless alcoves, all stacked and interlocking like a honeycomb dreamed by a somber god. Stairways, more like shallow scoops in the rock, spiral up the walls to higher niches. Bridges of lashed-together bone and dark, untempered metal span the narrower chasms. The technology is that of the hand, the simple blade, the drill worked by muscle and patience. There are no engines, no wheels except for small, crude potter’s wheels. Metal is present—dull gleams of bronze and iron in tools and fittings—but it is precious, recycled, never wasted.

And everything is wet.

Water is the city’s breath and its tears. It seeps from countless hairline fractures in the ceiling high above, beading and swelling until fat drops fall with a persistent, echoing *plink*. It runs in tiny, glittering tracks down the sculpted walls, collecting in shallow channels carved into the floors. These channels form a silent, shimmering web that feeds into a central cistern—a black, still pool at the city’s lowest point. The air is cool and heavy with moisture, a constant, clammy chill that settles into bones and cloth alike. Condensation paints every surface with a slippery film. In the dim light, the weeping stone looks like it is forever perspiring.

The light comes from two sources. The first is a faint, sourceless glow that emanates from the stone itself—a mineral phosphorescence, a pale grey-blue luminescence that is just enough to cast long, blurred shadows. It is supplemented by the second source: thousands of small, shallow clay bowls filled with a thick, odorless oil, each sporting a single woven reed wick. Their flames are tiny, unwavering orange eyes that dot the cavern, but they do little to fight the pervasive dimness. They only deepen the pools of darkness between structures.

There is no plant life. No moss, no fern, no root. What grows here is not green, but crystalline and microbial. In the wettest corners, where water runs ceaselessly, slick rainbow films bloom on the stone—iridescent sheets of bacteria that feed on minerals and the city’s waste. In drier niches, delicate white efflorescences of salt and gypsum sprout like miniature, barren forests. The only other "vegetation" might be the strange, rubbery black nodules that cluster around the oil lamps, feeding on their faint heat and smoke—a chemosynthetic symbiote, perhaps, but it is not alive in any way a surface-dweller would recognize.

Animal life is scarce and furtive. Pale, sightless insects like living slivers of mica skitter over wet rocks. Occasionally, a sinuous, eel-like shape, translucent and veinous, will slip through the black waters of the cistern, but it is rarely seen. The dominant fauna, if they can be called that, are the inhabitants themselves. They are humanoid, but tall and thin, with skin the color of the damp stone and large, black, liquid eyes adapted to the gloom. They are few, perhaps only a few hundred, moving through their dense warren with a slow, deliberate economy. They are the source of the sound.

For the city is **loud**.

The sound is not machinery, nor is it voices raised in cacophony. It is a deep, resonant, and constant **hum**. It emanates from the inhabitants, a tonal vibration produced in their chests and throats, a communal drone that is the auditory bedrock of the city. It is layered with the sharper, rhythmic *tink-tink-tink* of stone chisels, the splash of water into pools, the scrape of bone tools, and the ever-present, metallic ringing of water drops falling from great heights into bronze collection basins. This sonic tapestry is unchanging, day and night (though there are neither here). It has been the same for generations. The hum binds the community, a vibration felt in the stone underfoot as much as heard. It is the sound of equilibrium.

And nothing here changes. The water drips at the same rate. The oil lamps burn without consuming their fuel—the black nodules see to that. The inhabitants are born, live, and die, but their numbers remain constant, their routines etched as deeply as the channels in the floor. The carvings on the walls, depicting strange, geometric histories, are smoothed by moisture but never erode away entirely. It is a place in perfect, damp, dim, dense, and deafening stasis. A finished world. A pocket of eternity where every possible action has already been taken, every innovation made long ago, and all that remains is the maintenance of a perfect, unchanging balance. The air never warms nor cools further. The water level never rises nor falls. The hum never varies in pitch or volume.

To enter the Weeping City is to step into a moment that has been stretched to cover centuries. It is ancient, established, and utterly at peace with its own immutable rules. It weeps, it hums, and it endures, hidden away from the sun, a fossilized heartbeat in the dark.
