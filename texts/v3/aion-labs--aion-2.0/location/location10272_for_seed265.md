The air is a warm, wet blanket that never lifts. It holds the heat close to the ground, a persistent feverish warmth that comes up from below and condenses on every surface. The ground underfoot is not quite solid — it yields like damp peat, but with a sharp, crystalline crunch when you press down. The landscape is a vast, low basin, a saucer of darkness stretching to a horizon you feel more than see. There is no sun, no moon, no stars — only a profound, velvety blackness that absorbs light and thought. Yet, it is not uniform. The darkness has texture, depth, and a faint, sourceless luminescence that clings to the edges of things, a memory of light.

Water defines this place without ever being seen as a clear body. It seeps, it beads, it trickles. It hangs in the air as a permanent, tepid mist that coats your skin and the shells of the silent creatures that move through it. The ground is a network of shallow, warm rivulets cutting through a substrate of black clay and shattered, glass-like rock. The rock is new — its fractures are sharp, its angles violent and clean, as if the basin cracked open yesterday. This is a young land, born of a sudden, reality-shearing event. The contours are raw, unsoftened by time or weather. There are no rolling hills, only sudden shelves, jagged outcrops, and fissures that exhale warm, mineral-scented vapor.

Vegetation is scarce, but not absent. What grows here does not need light. Pale, fleshy nodules push up from the wet clay, like blind mushrooms the size of fists. They pulse faintly with a slow, internal rhythm, a bioluminescent throb in shades of sickly green and bruised purple. Occasionally, tall, slender stalks — bone-white and brittle — rise in clusters, their surfaces etched with intricate, spiraling patterns that seem to shift when you don't look directly. They are not plants as known elsewhere; they are more like geological secretions, or the skeletal remains of a thought.

The density of the place is not in things, but in presences. The warm, wet air is thick with life — but it is a silent, crowding life. Abundant fauna moves through the darkness, felt more than seen. Soft, many-legged things scuttle over the sharp rocks, their bodies leaving faint, phosphorescent trails that linger for hours before fading. Larger shapes, slow and ponderous, drift through the deeper mists, their outlines blurring and reconfiguring in impossible ways — a limb that is there, then isn't, a body that seems to occupy two spaces at once. This is the reality breakdown: the laws of locality and form are weak here. Creatures phase in and out of solidity; a swarm of needle-fine insects might suddenly coalesce into a single, hovering orb of chitin before dissolving into a cloud again. The air itself sometimes *folds*, creating brief, shimmering fractures through which you might glimpse a distorted version of the same landscape, or something else entirely.

The built environment is scattered, half-swallowed by the young land. Rusted iron skeletons of small pump houses or relay stations emerge from the clay, their surfaces weeping rust that stains the ground orange. Broken conduits, like fossilized intestines, spill from the earth. Here and there, a solitary, windowless structure of reinforced concrete stands tilted, its interior dark and echoing with the drip of water. These are the artifacts of industry — a failed attempt to harness or perhaps contain whatever happened here. Some still have power: a single, buzzing arc-light atop a pylon casts a stark white circle on the ground, its light somehow not dispersing the darkness but carving a hard-edged pocket within it. The hum of a buried generator is a rare, industrial heartbeat in the quiet. The technology is present, but it is a ghost, its purpose forgotten, its mechanisms now interacting in strange ways with the unstable physics of the place.

Sound is a rarity, which makes the occasional noise profound. The constant, soft *drip… drip…* of condensation from a metal ledge into a pool. The sudden, crisp *snap* of one of the brittle white stalks breaking in the still air. A low, subsonic vibration that comes up through the ground, felt in the teeth, from one of the great moving shapes in the mist. Sometimes, from a distance, a metallic groan as a ruined structure settles, or a faint, rhythmic *chug* from a piece of machinery that has no right to still be functioning.

Time here is slow, viscous. Change is measured in the gradual spreading of a phosphorescent stain across a rock face, or the incremental subsidence of a concrete slab into the hungry clay. The rivulets change course over seasons, not days. The creatures live and die on cycles that feel geological. Even the reality fractures seem to pulse with a lethargic rhythm, opening and closing over spans that could be hours or years.

The rules of this world are fluid. Gravity is mostly reliable, but sometimes, in pockets, it lessens or twists. Light does not travel in straight lines for long; it bends, pools, or is absorbed by the darkness. Matter is not always constant; the sharp rocks can sometimes be passed through if approached at the right angle, or in the right state of mind. It is said that if you stand still long enough in the silent dark, the landscape begins to dream itself around you, and you might become part of its slow, warm, wet dream.
