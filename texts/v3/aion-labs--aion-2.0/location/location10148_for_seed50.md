It is called the Throat of the Sleeping Mountain, and it is not a place you find by looking. It is a pocket within a pocket, a hidden resonance chamber inside a submerged peak that rises three leagues from the abyssal plain. The mountain is drowned, its summit a thousand fathoms beneath the waves, but here, in its heart, there is a hollow the size of a cathedral, and it is full of a storm that does not fall.

The rules of this place are written in fluid dynamics and resonant frequency. The water is not mindless; it is a medium of memory and impression. Sound shapes it. The sustained drone of the chamber causes certain harmonic basins to form—calm, lens-shaped pools of relative stillness that float unsupported in the mid-air of the chamber. The inhabitants, if they can be called that, use simple horn-shaped tools to pitch their voices into the din, trying to nudge these calm basins toward their ledges, to collect drinking water (water within water, a finer, quieter distillate) or to briefly shield their homes. It is a technology of suggestion, not control.

Time is different. The turbulent dynamic means there is no weathering, only a constant, violent present of formation. A cliff face is not worn old; it is born, reveals its crystalline veins, and is unmade, all in a continuous, roaring now. It is a world in its embryonic phase, a raw, loud, glorious, and terrible prototype of a landscape. To be here is to stand inside the moment of creation, where every form is a sentence being spoken, and the speaking is a deafening roar that never pauses for breath.
