You are buried alive in lush life.

The cavern is a single chamber, no larger than a grand cathedral, carved not by water but by a slow, forgotten chemical exhale. The walls are sharp, crystalline, fresh—edges still keen, facets catching what little light there is and splintering it into faint, sticky glows. They are dry as bone. No trickle, no pool, no dampness. The air tastes of minerals and a sweet, cloying perfume.

And everywhere, life—riotous, improbable, loud. It coats every surface: luminous moss in velvet folds, cascading fungal veils like frozen breath, bulbous growths that pulse with a slow, internal radiance. Vines—or things like vines—hang in thick ropes, studded with flowers that are not flowers: geometric clusters of iridescent scales that click and shiver. The warmth is deep, biological, a constant exhalation from the living mass. It is a sealed system, a pocket of furious metabolism cut off from any aquifer, any rain. The things here drink light and air and something else—perhaps the slow decay of the bedrock itself.

The creatures are many, but they are sparse in their movements. A shimmering, six-legged lizard-thing, carapace like oil on a puddle, picks its way deliberately along a vine. Moths the size of your hand, wings bearing eye-spots that are actual eyes, drift between the fungal towers. They make no sound, but the chamber is far from silent. The sound is a constant, layered thrum—a deep, resonant hum from the largest growths, overlaid with clicks, chitters, and the dry rustle of countless moving parts. It is a loud, biological static, the noise of the system working, never varying in pitch or intensity. It has been this way for centuries. It will be this way for centuries more.

In the sparse open spaces, where the luminous carpet thins, you see the built things. Not structures, but artifacts. A low, curved wall of fitted stones, impossibly smooth, enclosing nothing. A single pillar, carved with spirals that seem to swim in the corner of your vision, standing alone. Tools, if they are tools, lie scattered: a blade of black glass tied to a haft of petrified wood; a bowl-shaped stone holding a pile of desiccated, unidentifiable husks. They speak of hands, of intention, but their purpose is opaque, their makers long gone or transformed.

The rules here are strange, self-contained. Time is not a river but a still pond. Nothing decays; it is consumed, repurposed with perfect efficiency. Nothing grows old; it reaches a state and holds. The ecosystem is in perfect, deafening equilibrium. The warmth is constant, the light is a dim, everlasting twilight, the thunderous hum never falters. It is a museum of life, a curated frenzy locked in a stone vault. A lush, deafening, eternal now.
