**A Traveler’s Preparation for the City of Brackwater**

So you’ve set your coordinates for Brackwater. You’ve read the abstract—forgotten delta, industrial necropolis, perpetual decay. Let me be clear: the abstract does not prepare you. It is a place that works on you, physically and psychologically, with the patience of stone dissolving in acid. Preparing to visit isn’t about packing for a hike; it’s about preparing for a slow, wet siege against an environment that is, in every meaningful sense, alive and hungry. You are entering a digestive system. The city is the meal, and you are a potential nutrient. Here is how you might avoid being metabolized.

### **I. The Mind First: Psychological Armor**
Before you pack a single item, prepare your head. Brackwater’s greatest weapon is despair, dressed in the guise of timeless indifference.

*   **Abandon Hope for Dry Socks:** Accept, completely, that you will be wet. Not rain-wet, but soaked-through-to-the-skin, constantly-damp wet. Your skin will prune and stay pruned. The concept of ‘dry’ will become a mythical memory. Fighting this is futile and mentally exhausting. Embrace the soak.
*   **Embrace the Quiet Panic:** The silence is not peaceful. It’s a blanket over a million small, wet deaths. Your brain, seeking stimulus, will amplify every drip, every scuttle, turning them into threats. You must learn to parse the soundscape. The irregular *plink* is just water. The sudden, wet *slap* followed by silence might be something else. Hyper-vigilance will burn you out. Calm, focused awareness is your tool.
*   **Confront the Scale of Absence:** You will feel profoundly, crushingly alone. The architecture is built for thousands, but you will see no one. This emptiness has weight. It whispers that all endeavor ends in rust and silt. Have a purpose for being there—salvage of specific pre-collapse tech, a geological survey, a grim poetic pilgrimage—and cling to it. Without purpose, the nihilism of the place will seep into your bones faster than the damp.
*   **Time is Different:** There is no sun, no moon, no true day or night. Just the same bruised twilight, forever. Your circadian rhythm will shatter within cycles. Bring a reliable, waterproof chronometer and stick to an artificial schedule: 8 cycles of activity, 8 of rest, regardless of the unchanging light. Sleep with a light-blocking mask.

### **II. The Second Skin: Clothing and Exoskeleton**
Your clothing is not for modesty or comfort; it is a mobile environmental seal. Think of it as light armor.

*   **The Base Layer:** Synthetic, moisture-wicking fabric is a joke here. It will only move the damp around. Go for a tightly woven, silicone-impregnated silk or a modern composite that actively repels water on a molecular level. It must cover every inch of your torso, arms, and legs. No cotton. Cotton kills by staying wet and sucking heat.
*   **The Outer Shell:** A full-body suit is non-negotiable. It must be:
    *   **Chemically Resistant:** The water and mud are likely caustic, carrying a century of industrial toxins. A suit rated for chemical and biological hazards is best.
    *   **Puncture Resistant:** Those “rope-like leeches” aren’t passive. Beetles have mandibles. Things in the shadows have claws. Use a material with a flexible, woven durasteel or graphene-fiber weave.
    *   **Completely Sealed:** Gasketed wrists, ankles, and neck. A integrated, hood with a clear, anti-fog visor. The zippers must be waterproof and covered by storm flaps.
    *   **Climate Controlled:** This is critical. The external heat is a fever; inside the suit, with your exertion, it will be an oven. You need an active cooling system. A micro-compressor cooling unit, powered by high-density batteries, circulating chilled liquid through tubes in a vest. Without it, you risk heatstroke within an hour of hard travel.
*   **Footwear:** Boots are your foundation. They must be:
    *   **Tall:** Knee-high, at least, to wade through shallow muck.
    *   **Rigid-Soled:** The ground is uneven, littered with sharp, rusted metal and unstable. A rigid plate protects against punctures.
    *   **Drainable:** Despite the seal, water will get in. Self-bailing ports are useful.
    *   **Spiked/Cleated:** For traction on wet, sloping stone and slick metal.
*   **Gloves:** Full-fingered, armored, and grippy when wet. You will be touching slime-covered rails and corroded metal constantly.

### **III. The Kit: Tools for Survival in a Digestive Tract**
Pack light for mobility, but every item must be essential and over-engineered.

*   **Navigation:**
    *   **Inertial/Gravimetric Compass:** Traditional magnetics will be useless in a mess of rusted iron. You need a compass that reads local gravity or tracks its own movement.
    *   **Lidar Mapper:** A handheld unit to ping your surroundings. It will map the solid ground beneath water, identify cavities (sinkholes), and create a 3D map of interiors. The diffuse light makes visual reckoning nearly impossible.
    *   **Dyes & Buoys:** Biodegradable, high-visibility dye packets to mark trails in water. Small, lightweight buoys to anchor to stable points and string a guide line through flooded areas.
*   **Shelter:**
    *   Do not trust the buildings. They are in a state of “grim equilibrium,” meaning they could hold for a century or collapse tonight. If you must use one, choose small, ground-floor rooms with multiple exits. Avoid anything with large, unsupported spans (factory floors).
    *   Bring a **suspended habitat**: a hammock made of the same puncture-resistant material as your suit, with a sealed, mosquito-net-like canopy. Suspend it between sturdy, load-bearing pipes or stone outcrops. Get off the ground. *Always off the ground.*
*   **Sustenance:**
    *   **Water:** Assume all local water is a toxic, biological soup. Bring all you need. A high-efficiency still *could* boil and condense atmospheric humidity, but the condensate may carry acidic volatiles. Use a sophisticated chemical/UV filtration system as a last resort only.
    *   **Food:** Concentrated, nutrient-dense bars and gels. Nothing that requires rehydration with local water. Do **not** hunt the local fauna. You are not at the top of this food web. The beetles might be edible, but what parasites or bioaccumulated toxins do they carry? The price of a protein snack could be a worm that bores into your brainstem.
*   **Health & Safety:**
    *   **Medical Kit:** Heavy on broad-spectrum antibiotics, antifungals, and antivirals. Every scratch will be inoculated with brackish filth. Include wound sealants that work on wet skin, potent anti-itch and anti-inflammatory creams for the inevitable bites that get through, and rehydration salts.
    *   **Decontamination:** A small, pressurized sprayer filled with a powerful disinfectant/biofilm breaker. Use it to clean your suit, gear, and any wounds *before* opening your sealed environment.
    *   **Stimulants/Sedatives:** Mild stimulants to combat the soul-crushing lethargy of the environment. Careful, controlled sedatives to enforce sleep despite the alien soundscape.
*   **Tools:**
    *   **A Powerful Light:** The twilight is a liar. It hides depth and movement. You need a broad-beam lantern for area illumination and a focused, intense spotlight to pierce shadowy corners and water columns. Red-light filters to preserve night vision and possibly avoid attracting certain swarms.
    *   **A Cutting Tool:** A machete or short sword, corrosion-resistant. For clearing pulpy, rotten obstacles, not for combat. A compact plasma torch or thermic lance for cutting sealed metal doors or rust-welded hatches.
    *   **A Means of Persuasion:** This is a world of predation. Bring a weapon. Not for grand battles, but for a swift, definitive end to an encounter. A high-caliber, sealed pistol with solid-slug ammunition (energy weapons may malfunction in the humidity). A compact, powerful sonic emitter or UV flasher as a non-lethal deterrent for swarming insects or curious predators.
    *   **Probes & Pitons:** A collapsible pole to test ground depth and stability. Climbing pitons and a rope for ascending stone faces or securing your shelter.

### **IV. The Approach: Conduct in the City**
*   **Movement:** Move slowly, deliberately. Test every step. What looks like solid ground may be a crust over silty ooze. Use your probe constantly. Travel along the high lines of ruins—wall tops, raised pipelines—where possible. Avoid the open, flooded avenues; they are feeding grounds.
*   **The Water:** Treat every canal, pool, and puddle as a hazard. Do not wade deeper than your boots allow unless absolutely necessary. If you must swim, you are in extreme danger. The black water is zero-visibility, leech-infested, and hides both debris and predators.
*   **Interaction:** Do not touch anything with bare skin. Do not sit on stone. Do not lean on ironwork. If you must handle artifacts, use tools. The rust is sharp, the stone is abrasive, and everything is coated in a fine, likely toxic, biofilm.
*   **Rest Cycles:** Secure your suspended habitat in the most defensible, stable location you can find. Seal yourself inside. Run a low-power ultrasonic perimeter alarm. Eat, drink, tend to your body, and *try* to sleep. The city’s sounds will be loudest in your mind when you are still.

### **V. The Unpreparable**
Some things you cannot pack for.
*   **The Smell:** It will be a thick, complex odor of wet rust, rotting metal, stagnant water, and a sweet-sour undertone of biological decay. It will permeate everything, even through your filters, in your memory.
*   **The Taste of the Air:** Even with a filtered respirator, you’ll taste the humidity, a metallic tang on the back of your tongue.
*   **The Erosion of Self:** The constant, slow degradation of everything around you is a mirror. You will feel yourself eroding, your resolve softening, your memories blurring at the edges like the dissolved stone. This is the true test. You must remember who you were before the delta took you. You must hold onto the dry, sunlit memory of your home universe like a talisman.

Brackwater does not care if you live or die. It is not malevolent; it is indifferent. Your preparation is an act of defiance against that indifference. It is a statement that you, a temporary, soft-bodied thing, intend to walk through the belly of the world and walk back out again. Be thorough. Be paranoid. And may your seals hold.
