## **Traveler’s Preparation for the Weeping Mill**

The Weeping Mill is not a location you visit; it is a condition you enter. It is a slow, wet, breathing consensus between industry and jungle, and it will treat you as either a temporary irritant or a new resource to be integrated. Your goal is to pass through as the former. Here is how to prepare.

### **I. Mindset: Unlearn Time and Purpose**

First, shed your urgency. The Mill measures time in the growth of moss over iron and the turn of its great fungal wheel. A day here feels like a week; a week might feel like a lifetime. Impatience will fray your nerves against the constant, loud, slow pulse of the place.

*   **Practice Patience:** Before your jump, spend time in a steamy bathroom with the shower running, listening to the drip and hiss. Read slowly. Move deliberately.
*   **Accept Inwardness:** The Mill’s design—both original and overgrown—pulls awareness inward. The canopy walls off the sky, the sound drowns out distant thought. You will feel you are inside a living organism. Fighting this sensation causes anxiety; leaning into it can yield a strange, humid peace.
*   **Surrender Dryness:** You will be perpetually damp. Your skin will prune, your clothes will cling. The concept of being "dry" will become a memory. Resisting this is futile and miserable.

### **II. Physical Protection: Becoming a Slightly More Durable Fungus**

You cannot stay dry, so you must stay safe. Your materials must either shed water entirely or accept it without rotting. Your body must be guarded against the environment’s constant, warm embrace.

**A. The Outer Shell (The Exoskeleton):**
*   **Footwear:** This is your most critical item. The ground is a treacherous sponge of peat, hidden water channels, and slick moss. You need:
    *   **Traction:** Deep, aggressive lug soles that can grip slimy wood and metal.
    *   **Height:** Knee-high or higher to prevent peat and water from flooding in.
    *   **Material:** Synthetic rubber or vulcanized fabric. Leather will rot in days, become heavy, and host molds. Ensure seams are sealed.
    *   **Drainage:** Some way for water that *will* get in to get out quickly. A few small drain holes are better than a sealed boot that becomes a bucket.
*   **Clothing:**
    *   **Base Layer:** Tight-weave synthetic (like polyester or nylon) to wick moisture *away* from your skin. Cotton is a death sentence—it will hold water, become heavy, cold, and promote skin maceration and fungal growth.
    *   **Outer Layer:** A lightweight, waterproof and breathable shell (think high-quality rain jacket and pants). It will not keep you dry forever, but it will break the direct force of the constant "weeping" from the pipes and prevent your base layer from being instantly saturated.
    *   **Avoid Bulk:** No heavy sweaters or absorbent padding. The ambient warmth is constant (likely 80-90°F/27-32°C). Insulation will lead to fatal overheating.
*   **Head & Hands:**
    *   **A Wide-Brimmed Hat:** Not for sun, but to channel the drips from the canopy away from your face and neck.
    *   **Goggles:** The air is full of warm mist, spores, and condensation. Clear, anti-fog goggles will protect your eyes and allow you to see clearly in the green twilight.
    *   **Gloves:** Thin, synthetic gloves with a grip coating. They protect your hands from abrasive rust, sharp edges hidden under moss, and the instinct to grab a vine that might be part of a control mechanism. They also keep the ever-present biofilm off your skin.

**B. The Inner Fortress (Lungs & Body):**
*   **Respirator:** This is non-negotiable. The air is thick with spores, fungal hyphae, decay particles, and metallic mist. A simple cloth mask is useless. You need a half-face respirator with P100 particulate filters. It will be uncomfortable, hot, and will muffle sound, but it will prevent "Mill Lung"—a hacking, persistent cough that can turn fungal.
*   **Skin Care:** Apply a barrier ointment (like petroleum jelly or a zinc-based cream) to areas prone to chafing (feet, thighs, underarms). The constant moisture will break down skin faster than you think. Pack antifungal and antibacterial powders.
*   **Hydration:** Paradoxically, in this world of water, dehydration is a major risk. The heat and humidity will cause you to sweat profusely, and drinking the warm, iron-tangy water directly from a pool or pipe is asking for a parasitic or bacterial infection. Bring:
    *   A collapsible water bladder with a tube you can run inside your respirator.
    *   A high-capacity water filter (0.1 micron absolute) **and** chlorine dioxide purification tablets. Filter first, then treat. The water here is biologically active.

### **III. Navigation & Safety: Moving Through the Wet Machine**

The Mill’s terrain is a camouflaged puzzle. What looks like a mossy floor could be a pool over your head. What looks like a solid wall could be a curtain of vines.

*   **The Probe:** A sturdy, lightweight pole, 6-7 feet long. Use it to test the ground before every step. Probe for firmness, depth, and hidden obstructions. It can also push aside sensitive tendrils without touching them.
*   **Light:** The green twilight is constant but deep. Bring a reliable, waterproof LED light with a diffuser (red light is best). A harsh white beam will reflect maddeningly off the mist and steam, blinding you. Use it sparingly to check shadows and crevices.
*   **Sound Management:** The noise is immense and layered. High-fidelity earplugs are essential. They won't block the deep thumps (which you'll feel in your bones), but they will take the edge off the hisses, groans, and high-frequency hums, preventing auditory overload and allowing you to focus.
*   **The Rule of Touch:** **Do not touch the machinery.** Those vines and tendrils are integrated. Pulling one might be like yanking a nerve. A slow-moving piston sheathed in a bulbous growth may look inert, but it has the force to crush bone. Give all mechanical motion a wide berth.
*   **Marking Your Path:** The inwardness of the place is disorienting. Use bright, synthetic cord (orange or pink) to tie temporary markers. **Do not carve into living wood or metal.** Retrieve the cord as you leave. The Mill integrates everything left behind; your cord could become a trellis.

### **IV. Sustenance & Sustenance of Self**

*   **Food:** Bring all your own. High-calorie, sealed, dry foods: energy bars, nuts in vacuum packs, dried meats. Nothing that requires cooking or added water. The local "fruit" from the engineered vines or fungal pods is of unknown biochemistry and may be symbiotic with the Mill's systems—eating it could be the first step in your own integration.
*   **Mental Sustenance:** Bring a small, waterproof notebook and a grease pencil. Writing down observations can anchor you in time and purpose. Schedule regular "checks" with yourself. The slow rhythm can lull you into a trance; you might sit down to rest and realize hours have passed as a vine has grown over your ankle.

### **V. What to Leave Behind**

*   **All Electronics:** Unless specifically hardened for underwater, high-humidity operations, they will fail within minutes. The moisture is pervasive and conductive.
*   **Sentimental or Porous Items:** Paper, books, untreated wood, leather—they will swell, mold, and decay.
*   **Metals That Corrode:** Steel tools will rust rapidly. If you must bring metal, it should be anodized aluminum, titanium, or high-grade stainless steel, kept oiled and sealed.
*   **The Idea of Cleanliness:** You will be damp, stained with peat and rust, and coated in a fine layer of organic film. Accept it.

### **Upon Arrival: The First Breath**

When you step into the warm, thick air, pause. Let the sound wash over you. Feel the first bead of condensation form on your goggles. Take your first breath through the respirator—it will taste of filtered plastic, not wet iron and decay. This is good.

Move with the slowness of the water wheel. Place each step with care. Listen for changes in the soundscape—a new drip, a shift in the groan of a pump—it may signal a change in the environment. Look up: the weeping pipes create a perpetual, gentle rain. Look down: the moss is swallowing a gear.

Remember why you came, but do not let your purpose fight the Mill's purpose. You are a visitor in a slow, loud, wet dream of industry. Pass through quietly, leave no trace it can use, and carry the memory of its deep, green breath out with you into a drier, faster world.
