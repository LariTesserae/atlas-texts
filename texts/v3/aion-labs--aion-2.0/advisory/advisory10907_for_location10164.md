### Preparatory Briefing: The Plateau of Perpetual Arrival

**To the ordinary human multiverse traveler:**

You are not planning a visit. You are planning an immersion into a state of being. The plateau does not welcome visitors; it absorbs them into its eternal present. Your preparation must be as much metaphysical as physical. Failure in either domain will not result in a quick death, but in a far more terrible fate: becoming a permanent, broken fixture in a landscape that feels nothing.

#### I. The Core Nature of the Place: Understanding the Enemy

Before any gear is packed, you must internalize three non-negotiable truths:

1.  **Time is a Lie Here.** There is no day/night cycle, no seasonal change, no weather variation. The sun is fixed. The wind is constant. Your circadian rhythm will be annihilated within hours. Your sense of duration will stretch, then snap. You will feel you have always been there, and will always be. This is the "perpetual arrival" – a moment stretched into eternity. Prepare to fight the feeling of *finality*.
2.  **Sound is the Environment.** The deafening, layered roar is not an aspect of the plateau; it *is* the plateau. It is a physical force. Silence is not merely absent; it is conceptually impossible. Your hearing will be useless for communication or warning. Your thoughts will be shouted down.
3.  **Scale is a Trap.** The vast, empty distances are not empty. They are filled with thin air, blinding light, and roaring wind. Your brain, evolved for clutter, will panic at the lack of reference points. A rock a mile away might seem an hour's walk; it could be a day's. The emptiness will press in on you, creating a paradox of agoraphobia and claustrophobia.

If you cannot hold these facts in your mind as constants, turn back now.

#### II. Physical Preparation: Armoring the Vessel

**A. Atmospheric and Thermal Protection:**
*   **Pressure Suit & Integrated Life Support:** Standard environmental suits are insufficient. You require a **hard-shell, climate-controlled pressure suit** rated for extreme high-altitude (effectively near-vacuum conditions). It must actively regulate internal pressure and oxygen levels. The external layer must be a matte, non-reflective white or pale grey to reflect the brutal UV and thermal radiation.
*   **Thermal Regulation:** The suit's internal thermal system must combat the "knife-cold" air. Assume an ambient temperature of -40°C to -60°C. Redundant heating elements are mandatory. Include thermally insulated, flexible boots with aggressive grip soles—the polished stone will be treacherously slick, especially with condensation.
*   **Helmet & Sensory Interface:** A full-coverage helmet with a **gold-mirrored visor** is non-negotiable. The light will cause rapid snow blindness and pain. The visor must have adaptive tinting. Crucially, the helmet must contain a sophisticated **audio management system**. It should offer:
    *   Active noise cancellation tuned to the known frequency profile (the constant shriek and subsonic hum).
    *   Filtered audio pass-through to allow awareness of *changes* in the soundscape (e.g., the click of mandibles, a change in wind pitch near an arch).
    *   The ability to pipe in curated, complex sound (music, spoken word, variable noise) to prevent auditory hallucination and provide a cognitive anchor.

**B. Sustenance and Independence:**
*   **Nutrition:** There is no viable food. The lichen, moss, and succulents are likely inedible or toxic to human biology. The fauna is chitinous and adapted to a different biochemistry. **Bring all calories and nutrients with you.** Use high-density, long-shelf-life rations. Assume no resupply.
*   **Hydration:** This is critical. The "water is a secretive thing." You cannot rely on finding seeps or condensation. Your suit must have a closed-loop water reclamation system, recycling all moisture. Carry substantial supplemental water reserves. The dry, cold air will dehydrate you rapidly.
*   **Power:** Your suit, comms, audio, and heating will be energy-intensive. Bring high-capacity, cold-weather-rated power cells. Solar is unreliable due to the fixed sun's angle and potential shading by the aerial fauna. Assume no recharge opportunity.

**C. Navigation and Communication:**
*   **Inertial/GPS Hybrid:** Standard celestial navigation is impossible (fixed sun, no stars). Magnetic fields may be distorted by the iron-rich basalt. Use a self-contained inertial navigation unit tied to your point of arrival. Mark it with a durable, physical beacon.
*   **Communication:** Assume long-range comms (radio, quantum, etc.) will be swallowed by the plateau's resonant hum. Short-range, tight-beam laser or line-of-sight may work, but only if you have a partner within visual range (difficult given the scale). You are likely psychologically alone. Prepare for that.

#### III. Psychological Preparation: Fortifying the Mind

This is more important than the gear. Gear can fail. Your mind must not.

*   **Pre-Visit Conditioning:** Undergo sensory deprivation and sensory overload training. Spend time in anechoic chambers, then in rooms with constant, loud white noise. Practice meditation and mental exercises designed to maintain self-identity and temporal awareness in the absence of cues.
*   **Cognitive Anchors:** Establish immutable personal rituals. A specific piece of music listened to at a "subjective" morning and night via your helmet audio. A physical object in your suit—a smooth stone, a locket—to touch while reciting a mantra of who you are, where you came from, and that time *does* pass.
*   **Temporal Discipline:** Use timers. Set your suit's internal chronometer to a 24-hour (or other meaningful) cycle and rigidly adhere to a schedule of rest, nourishment, and "activity" based on it, ignoring the unchanging external light completely.
*   **Journaling:** Verbally or via typed log, record everything. Not just events, but your thoughts, perceptions, doubts. The act of narration creates a timeline and fights the feeling of stasis. Note any perceived changes, no matter how minor—they will be precious.

#### IV. Operational Protocols: On the Ground

**Upon Arrival ("Becoming Aware"):**
1.  **Do not move immediately.** Spend the first subjective hour simply observing, letting your suit systems acclimate, and stabilizing your mind. Let the shock of "always having been there" wash over and recede.
2.  **Map your immediate area** with intense detail. Find your anchor point. Note the position of a distinctive rock formation. This small, known zone will be your psychological fortress against the immense unknown.

**Movement:**
*   Travel is not about distance, but about landmarks. The single twisted pillar twenty miles away is not a destination; it is a reference point for a journey that may take days.
*   Move slowly, deliberately. The wind can knock you over. Test footing. The smooth rock is a false friend.
*   **Never** attempt to traverse during the "damp" periods (the condensation mist). The stone will become a sheet of black ice.

**Interaction with the Biosphere:**
*   **The Aerial Filter-Feeders:** Give their "shimmering veil" a wide berth. Their membranes may be delicate, but a swarm could obscure your vision or entangle you. They are part of the wind; treat them like a weather phenomenon.
*   **The Grazers:** Observe, do not interfere. Their stone-stacking ritual is meaningless to you. Do not disturb their towers. They are not food. They are likely to scuttle away, but their chitinous shells suggest they are armored. Assume they are not hostile unless provoked.
*   **The Avian Hunters:** These are the primary physical threat. If you see one spiral, freeze. Their targeting is based on movement against the stark ground. Your white suit is an advantage. Do not run. Crouch low and become a rock. They are unlikely to see you as prey, but a sudden movement may trigger an investigative strike.

**The Tools: The Central Mystery**
*   You will find the hand-axes, the nets. They are pristine, functional, and alien.
*   **Protocol: Non-Interference Absolute.** Do not touch them. Do not move them. Do not attempt to use them. Photograph and document from a distance.
*   **Hypothesis:** They are not abandoned. They are *placed*. They may be offerings, traps, measuring devices, or the equivalent of road markers for an intelligence that perceives the plateau differently. Picking one up may be seen as accepting a contract, triggering a mechanism, or blasphemy. Their presence implies a maker, and in a place this static, that maker is likely still present, just invisible to your perception. Your ignorance is your shield. To interact is to introduce a variable into a closed, perfect system. The system may correct for that variable.

#### V. The Inevitable Psychological Phases

Be aware you will likely pass through these stages:
1.  **Awe & Hyper-Clarity:** The sheer scale and light will initially exhilarate.
2.  **Auditory Overwhelm & Isolation:** The noise will become oppressive. Loneliness will set in.
3.  **Temporal Dissolution:** The lack of change will erode your sense of progress. You may feel you are on a treadmill.
4.  **Paranoia & Projection:** You will start to see intention in the wind's scream, in the paths of the grazers. The tools will feel like eyes upon you.
5.  **Acceptance or Breakdown:** You will either make peace with the plateau's eternal now (a dangerous, seductive peace that risks you never wanting to leave) or your mind will fracture, potentially leading to catatonia or reckless behavior.

Your training and anchors are designed to stall you between phases 2 and 3, and to pull you back from the brink of 5.

#### VI. Exit Strategy

How do you leave a place you have "always" been inside of?
*   Your point of arrival is your only guaranteed exit vector. The plateau is unlikely to have a "door." Your multiverse travel mechanism must be recall-based or tied to a fixed, personal beacon.
*   **The greatest danger is losing the will to leave.** The sterile balance can be hypnotic. The feeling of being a tiny, insignificant part of a vast, eternal machine is perversely comforting. Your cognitive anchors must include a powerful, emotionally charged reason to return home. Review it daily.

**Final Advisory:**

You are not exploring a location. You are inserting a fragile, temporal bubble of self into a realm of eternal, roaring stasis. Your mission is not conquest or study, but endurance and return. Protect your bubble. Do not let the plateau's perfect, silent scream populate it with ghosts. Come back, and be prepared to spend years hearing the echo of the wind in your dreams.
