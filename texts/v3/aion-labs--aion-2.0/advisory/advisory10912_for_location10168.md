# Preparation Briefing for the Sky-Mere

**To: Ordinary Human Multiverse Traveler**
**Re: Pre-deployment conditioning, gear, and survival protocols for Environment Designate: "Sky-Mere"**

Congratulations on your selection. The Sky-Mere is a world of profound beauty and profound indifference. It is not actively hostile, but its very nature presents a suite of physiological and psychological challenges that, unaddressed, will kill you. Your preparation must be meticulous. Think of yourself not as a visitor, but as a guest entering a vast, humid, living cathedral that is still under construction—a cathedral made of water, sharp stone, and patient, overwhelming green.

## I. Physiological Acclimatization & Health

The core contradiction: a high-altitude plateau with the climate of a tropical lagoon.

*   **Thin Air:** The elevation is immense. Expect the air to have 40-50% less oxygen than sea level on a standard world. You will need **at least two weeks of pre-ascent acclimatization** in simulated altitude chambers. Supplemental oxygen, while cumbersome, is non-negotiable for the first 72 hours and for any strenuous activity. Without it, you risk pulmonary edema, cerebral edema, and catastrophic fatigue.
*   **Hydration Paradox:** The air is thin but the humidity is near 100%. You will sweat profusely, yet the moisture won't evaporate efficiently, leading to overheating. Your body's thirst signals will be unreliable. **Drink water with electrolyte supplements on a strict schedule, not when you feel thirsty.** Dehydration and heat exhaustion are your most immediate threats.
*   **Solar & Atmospheric Exposure:** The sun is "normal, golden," but at that altitude, ultraviolet radiation is intense and unfiltered. Full-spectrum sunblock (SPF 50+), reapplied hourly, is essential. The "golden mist" of evaporating water and botanical pheromones is an unknown variable. **Assume it is an irritant and a potential allergen.** A fitted respirator with HEPA and organic vapor filters is mandatory for initial exploration until you can confirm personal tolerance. Eye protection (sealed goggles) is also advised against airborne spores and mist.
*   **Microbial & Biological:** There is no record of compatible fauna, but the water and soil will teem with microbial and fungal life unique to this world. Your immune system has zero defenses. **Full-spectrum broad-acting antivirals, antibiotics, and antifungals** must be part of your kit, along with vaccines for every conceivable analog. Assume any cut or scrape will become infected without immediate, aggressive treatment.

## II. Essential Gear & Equipment

Forget "light packing." You are moving into a warm, wet, vertical maze.

*   **The Suit:** Your primary barrier. It must be a **custom-integrated system**.
    *   **Inner Layer:** Moisture-wicking, antimicrobial.
    *   **Mid Layer:** A lightweight, flexible **cut-and-puncture-resistant mesh** (think dragon-scale weave) covering torso, limbs, and neck. The rock is *sharp*.
    *   **Outer Layer:** A **breathable, quick-dry, full-body wetsuit** (2-3mm neoprene) with reinforced knees, elbows, and seat. It provides buoyancy, insulation against occasional cooler water pockets, and protection from prolonged immersion. **Integrated booties** with vibram soles and drainage ports.
    *   **Gloves:** Climbing gloves with Kevlar palms and reinforced fingertips.
    *   **Headwear:** A broad-brimmed, ventilated hat that accommodates your respirator/goggle assembly.

*   **Footwear (Secondary):** For extended travel on dry(ish) rock, bring **sturdy, drained hiking boots** with aggressive traction. You will switch between these and your suit's booties.

*   **Navigation & Communication:**
    *   **Do not rely on magnetic or GPS analogs.** The Sky-Mere's youthful geology may have chaotic magnetic fields. Your primary tools will be **old-fashioned**: a **precision sextant** for astral fixes (the sun is "normal," so star charts may align), a **solid-state compass** (as a backup, not a primary), and a **laser rangefinder**.
    *   **Mapping:** You will create your own. Bring **satellite beacons** to drop at high points for triangulation, and a **waterproof tablet** with drafting software. **Dye markers** (biologically inert) can be used to trace slow water currents.
    *   **Signaling:** The dense vegetation will absorb sound and light. A **high-powered laser flare** and a **low-frequency acoustic pulser** may penetrate better than radio.

*   **Shelter & Rest:**
    *   You cannot sleep on the ground. It is either sharp rock or warm water. Your solution: a **geodesic mosquito-net hammock** with a **rigid, waterproof canopy**. Suspend it from rock protrusions or the sturdier vine lattices. The canopy is not for rain, but for the constant *plink... plink...* of condensation, which will destroy sleep.
    *   A **compact, battery-operated dehumidifier** for your shelter is worth its weight in gold for preserving electronics and sanity.

*   **Transportation:**
    *   The water is mostly waist-deep, but walking for miles on uneven, submerged rock is exhausting and hazardous. A **small, rigid-hull inflatable kayak or a paddleboard** is ideal. It must be tough enough to resist abrasion from hidden stone. A **collapsible pole** for probing depth and pushing off is more useful than a paddle in the tight, plant-choked channels.

*   **Tools & Utilities:**
    *   **A machete and a compact pruning saw.** The vegetation is not just dense; it is structurally integral, like a living wall. You will need to cut paths, but do so minimally—the vines may be load-bearing for entire sections of the landscape.
    *   **Climbing Gear:** A light **harness, carabiners, and 50m of static line**. The rock is crisp and should take pitons well, but be wary of its youthful instability. Test every hold.
    *   **Water Purification:** The water is warm and mineral-rich. It may be potable, or it may be laced with alkaloids from the pervasive roots. A **chemical analyzer** and a **distillation kit** are essential. Do not drink it untreated.
    *   **Illumination:** Nights will be very dark under the vine-canopies. **Headlamps** are key. Also bring **low-light amplification goggles**. Observe the root systems' bioluminescence without blinding it with your white light.

## III. Sustenance & Procurement

There is no game. There are no fruit-bearing trees described. The entire ecosystem is green, flowering, and likely inedible or toxic to human biochemistry.

*   **Bring all your calories with you.** Focus on **high-energy, compact, dehydrated rations**. Your caloric needs will be 30-40% above normal due to the metabolic stress of hypoxia and constant physical exertion.
*   **No foraging.** Unless you have a full biochemical lab to test every moss, fern, and orchid, assume everything is poisonous, hallucinogenic, or will trigger a fatal allergic reaction. The "clean, slightly metallic scent" of the water is a warning sign of dissolved compounds.
*   **Fishing?** The "slow-worming shapes" and "iridescent, many-legged" skimmers are your only potential fauna. They are likely invertebrates. Attempting to catch or eat them is not recommended—they could be venomous, parasitic, or simply indigestible. Consider them hazards, not resources.

## IV. Psychological Preparedness

This may be your greatest challenge. The Sky-Mere operates on a temporal and sensory scale that is deeply alien to human psychology.

*   **The Silence:** There is no bird song, no animal chatter. The soundscape is a "profound, mostly quiet hum" of leaves, water drops, and distant rockfalls. This **bio-silence** can trigger intense loneliness, auditory hallucinations, and a sense of being watched by the landscape itself. Prepare with **audio recordings of terrestrial environments** and schedule strict daily radio check-ins with your point of origin, even if just to hear another voice.
*   **The Scale of Time:** Changes are glacial. A floating island moves a mile in a century. You will see no visible change during your stay. Your own frantic, time-pressured existence will feel grotesquely out of sync. This can lead to **existential dissonance—**a feeling that your actions are meaningless scribbles on a page being written over millennia. Meditation practices focused on present-moment awareness are crucial. Keep a detailed journal; your personal narrative will be your anchor in timelessness.
*   **Vegetable Claustrophobia:** The life is "smothering" and "dense." It is not passive scenery; it is an active, engulfing presence. The feeling that the green is slowly, patiently reclaiming the space you occupy can be oppressive. Ensure your shelter and person are always meticulously clear of encroaching growth. Create a "clearing" for your mind.
*   **The Golden Mist:** The pheromone-laden haze may have subtle psychoactive effects—mood alteration, dream intensity, time-perception distortion. Monitor your mental state rigorously. Use your respirator.

## V. Exploration & Conduct

You are entering a world "still settling into its own skin." It is a pristine, newborn ecosystem.

*   **Move with deliberate slowness.** Match the world's pace. Rushing leads to injury on the sharp rock and disturbance of fragile ecological balances you cannot comprehend.
*   **Leave no trace, take only images.** Do not introduce any foreign biological material. Sterilize all equipment before arrival. Pack out all waste. Your goal is to be a ghost, a warm-blooded, breathing ghost that passes through the dripping arches and sunlit lagoons without altering a single leaf's destiny.
*   **Document, do not interfere.** The symbiotic relationships—the glowing roots, the thermal water circulations—are the heart of the Sky-Mere's mystery. Observe them. Take samples only if absolutely necessary and with extreme containment protocols.

## Final Advisory:

The Sky-Mere is not a place for conquest or extraction. It is a place for profound humility. You will be an ephemeral creature in a patient, green dream. Your success will not be measured by distance covered, but by the depth of your adaptation and the quietness of your passage. Prepare your body to endure, your mind to accept, and your spirit to witness. Then step onto the spine of dark rock, breathe the thin, fragrant air, and become, for a little while, a part of the slow, wet, glorious sigh of a world being born.
