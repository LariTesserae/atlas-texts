Alright, traveler. You’ve booked a ticket to Cinderwick. Let’s be clear: this isn’t a vacation. It’s a physiological and psychological acclimatization to a place that operates on a different wavelength of existence. Think of this less as a trip and more as embedding yourself in a living engine. Here’s how not to dissolve, drown, or lose your mind.

### 1. The Body: Acclimatization & Health

Your body is a liability here. It expects day and night, dry and wet, quiet and loud. Cinderwick offers none of these binaries.

*   **The Pressure & The Damp:** The altitude combined with the woolen humidity will make you feel like you’re breathing through a wet sock. Pre-trip, cardio is your friend. Your ears will pop for the first day. The damp is *inside* everything. Arthritic conditions will flare. Fungal infections (athlete’s foot, jock itch) are not an ‘if’ but a ‘when’. Pack antifungal powders and creams like they’re ammunition. Your skin will never feel truly clean.
*   **The Microbiome:** There is no green. Your gut, used to plant matter, will revolt. The local diet is fungal, industrial starch, imported grain, and protein from the fauna (more on that later). Start taking robust probiotics weeks before. Pack fiber supplements. Constipation or catastrophic reversal are common for newcomers.
*   **The Noise:** The constant roar of wings, machinery, and chittering is a physical force. It will seep into your dreams. Bring the highest-quality, musician-grade earplugs for sleeping. For daywear, consider filtered earplugs that dampen the cacophony but let you hear conversation. Failure to do this leads to a grinding, fatigue-based madness the locals call “the Hum.”
*   **The Light:** Your circadian rhythm will be murdered. The eternal, shadowless gray bleeds time. You will not feel the sun’s cue. Bring a reliable, rugged watch and adhere to its schedule for sleep. Light therapy glasses that simulate a dawn sunrise can stave off the depressive lethargy that newcomers experience.

### 2. The Kit: What to Bring (and What to Leave)

Forget fashion. Think fortress.

*   **The Shell:** A breathable, waterproof outer layer is non-negotiable. Not water-*resistant*. Water*proof*. Gore-Tex or equivalent. Sealed seams. A wide-brimmed hat to keep the constant drizzle off your neck and face. Everything not waterproofed will become a sodden, mildewed mess within hours.
*   **The Core:** Wool. Always wool. It insulates even when wet. Synthetic base layers that wick. Avoid cotton—it’ll be a cold, damp skin-sack. Multiple pairs of fast-drying socks. You will change them twice a day.
*   **Footwear:** This is critical. The streets are shallow rivers over slick cobbles and slurry. You need aggressive traction. Think wading boots or top-tier hiking boots with a serious grip, treated for water. Waterproof gaiters to keep the slurry out. Inside, wear a thin wool sock under a thicker one to prevent blisters.
*   **Bag:** A waterproof backpack or satchel with roll-top closure. Everything inside goes into individual plastic zip-locks or dry bags. Your notebook, your currency, your spare socks—all of it.
*   **The Extras:**
    *   **A Robust Umbrella:** Not a flimsy thing. A solid, metal-shafted one. It’s a tool for parting crowds of Stokers as much as for the drizzle.
    *   **Cleaning Tools:** A small, stiff brush for scraping slurry and Stoker droppings off your boots and hem before entering any building. It’s a basic courtesy.
    *   **Light Source:** A reliable, hands-free headlamp. The alleys are cracks, and the interior of buildings—even during the “day”—are cavernously dim.
    *   **Barter Goods:** The local currency is complex (often based on steam-pressure tokens or work credits). Bring small, sealed packets of spices (pepper, cinnamon), high-quality machine oil, or bundles of dry, non-moldy tobacco. These are universal lubricants.
    *   **Respirator:** A simple half-face respirator with P100 filters for when you pass too close to a particularly fuming exhaust vent or a fungal bloom releasing spores. The air is thick, and sometimes you’ll want to thin it.

### 3. Upon Arrival: First 24 Hours

1.  **Breathe.** The smell will hit you: hot oil, wet stone, musk, and the sweet-rot tang of the fungal secretions. Don’t gag openly. It’s just the city’s breath.
2.  **Mind the Sky.** Look up, but not for long. The flocks of Stokers are unpredictable. Their droppings are corrosive. A wide hat is your first defense.
3.  **Watch Your Step.** The center of every street is a rivulet. The edges are slick with the leathery fungal growths. Walk with a slightly wide stance, like a sailor on a deck.
4.  **Find a Licensed Lodging.** Don’t try to camp. The slurry-ground will seep into everything, and the pale rodents from the tunnels are curious. Look for places with a constant, faint vibration—it means the basement pumps are working. No vibration means imminent flooding.
5.  **The First Meal.** Eat what the locals eat. Yes, it’s probably a gray, steamed loaf derived from fungal substrate and a stew of slow rodent meat. Your stomach needs to adapt. Wash it down with the local “brew”—a fermented, slightly oily tea made from lichen that grows on the pipes. It’ll help.
6.  **Listen.** Don’t try to talk over the noise. Listen to its layers. The deep *thump-thump* of the central steam presses is the city’s heartbeat. The rush of wings is its breath. The constant drip and gurgle of water is its circulation. To understand Cinderwick is to understand its soundscape.

### 4. Social & Cultural Navigation

*   **Pace:** Move with the city. It is slow. Do not rush. Rushing marks you as alien, disruptive. Decisions are made glacially. A contract might be measured in decades. Impatience is a sign of childishness.
*   **Conversation:** Voices are raised to carry over the din. Do not mistake this for anger. Directness is valued. Metaphors involving light, sun, and growth are meaningless. Use metaphors of pressure, flow, erosion, and combustion.
*   **The Stokers:** They are not pests. They are part of the ecosystem. They are tolerated, managed, but rarely culled. Do not harm them openly—it’s seen as bad luck, like killing a spider in a grain silo. They keep the insects in check.
*   **Technology:** Reverence for steam is religious. Electricity is viewed as a useful but fickle, nervous child. Compliment a well-maintained valve or a silent piston. Do not praise an electric light without also praising the steam-turbine that likely powers it.
*   **Cleanliness:** You will be dirty. The concept is different. “Clean” here means free of *harmful* grime. The ever-present patina of soot and moisture is just the city’s patina on you. Wash your hands and face regularly, but don’t fret about the stain on your coat. Everyone has it.

### 5. Dangers (A Non-Exhaustive List)

*   **The Slurry:** It can be deceptively deep in patches. It will suck a boot right off. Never wade into an unfamiliar area.
*   **Steam Vent Ruptures:** A hissing pipe is normal. A sharp, shrieking whistle is not. Move away, quickly but calmly. The scald is instant and severe.
*   **The Tunnels:** Do not go into the service tunnels. The rodent tides are not aggressive, but they are overwhelming. You could be knocked over, trampled, and your belongings processed before anyone knew you were gone.
*   **Silence:** If the constant noise *stops*, it is an emergency. The flocks have settled, the machines have halted. Something is very wrong. Find the nearest building and get inside. It likely means a massive pressure drop or a toxic gas release.
*   **Despair:** The gray, the damp, the noise, the slow pace—it can hollow you out. You must create your own rhythms, your own markers of time. Keep a journal. Maintain your rituals. Talk to people, even if just to trade for a cup of brew. Let the city’s immense, enduring equilibrium become a form of comfort, not oppression.

### 6. Departure

Leaving is its own shock. The silence of a normal world will feel deafening, abusive. The dryness will make your skin crack. The vivid colors of a green world will seem garish, hysterical. You will, for a time, miss the enveloping, roaring embrace of the plateau. You’ll find yourself listening for the thump of distant presses in the night.

Cinderwick doesn’t host visitors. It absorbs them, temporarily. Let it. Don’t fight the damp. Don’t curse the gray. Settle into its slow, monumental gears. You won’t conquer it, but you might, for a moment, achieve a kind of resonance.

Good luck. And keep your boots sealed.
