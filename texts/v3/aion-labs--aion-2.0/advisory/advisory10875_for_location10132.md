### Traveler’s Advisory: The Plateau of Weeping Stone
**A Guide for the Mundane Visitor**

Congratulations. You have coordinates for one of the multiverse’s most profound, silent wonders. The Plateau is not a destination for the faint of heart, the thrill-seeker, or the souvenir hunter. It is a pilgrimage for those who wish to witness time made manifest. To visit is to be a guest in a living, breathing cathedral of stone and water that operates on a timescale that humbles human life. Preparation is not merely about comfort—it is about survival, sanity, and respect.

#### I. The Mind Before the Body
This is the most critical preparation. The Plateau will work on your psyche from the moment you arrive.

*   **Embrace the Pace:** Leave your sense of hurry at the door. The Plateau’s clock ticks in millennia. A day here is a blink. Frustration at the “slowness” of nothing happening will curdle into anxiety. Practice stillness before you go. Meditate. Sit in a quiet, damp place for hours. Your reward is not action, but perception.
*   **Accept the Scale:** The city is continental. You will not see it all. You are an ant in a termite mound carved from a mountain. Feel awe, not insignificance. The scale is part of the medicine—it puts your own world and worries into a vast, quiet perspective.
*   **Prepare for Sensory Deprivation (and Enhancement):** The soundscape is dominated by water droplets and your own breath. The color palette is silver, grey, pearl, black, and the wet green of the moss. There are no loud noises, no bright colors. This can lead to a form of sensory starvation that makes the mind turn inward. It can be peaceful or terrifying. Be ready to listen to yourself. Conversely, your hearing will become preternaturally sharp. The *plink* of a drop will orient you like a bell. The sigh of the mist will be a conversation.

#### II. Physical Kit: Your Second Skin
Forget fashion. Think function, silence, and waterproofing. Everything you bring will be perpetually damp.

*   **The Shell:**
    *   **Outer Layer:** A **full-body slicker or cloak** of a non-rustle material (waxed cotton or advanced synthetics). It must have a deep hood. Seams must be sealed. The mist is acidic enough to accelerate erosion over eons; for your visit, it will simply soak and chill you to the bone if unprotected.
    *   **Insulation:** Beneath the shell, wear **multiple layers of wool or advanced hydrophobic fleece**. The chill is marrow-deep and constant. No cotton—it retains moisture and becomes a death blanket.
    *   **Footwear:** This is paramount. The moss-bed is spongy, wet, and hides irregularities. **Knee-high, waterproof boots with superb grip** are essential. Felt or rubber soles that can handle slick, algae-tinged stone. Gaiters to prevent the silver moss from creeping in.
*   **Sustenance & Tools:**
    *   **Water:** Ironically, you must bring your own. The pools, while clear, are mineral-saturated and may host microscopic lifeforms your gut biome cannot handle. A closed-system water filter and insulated flask are required.
    *   **Food:** High-calorie, non-perishable, and quiet to eat. Nuts, hard cheeses, dense grains. Nothing with crinkly packaging. The sound will feel like a violation.
    *   **Light:** The cloud-ceiling casts a uniform, shadowless pearlescent grey light. It is perpetual twilight. For exploring the interior chambers of the towers, you need a **soft, diffused light source**. A headlamp with a red or very low white setting. A strong, focused beam will feel brutish and scare the few delicate inhabitants. Bring phosphorescent markers to note your path; the interiors are labyrinths.
    *   **Navigation:** Digital compasses may behave oddly due to mineral deposits. A **traditional analog compass and detailed, waterproof notepad** for sketching your route are wise. Remember, landmarks change over centuries, not days, so your map will be valid for your lifetime.
    *   **Shelter:** If you plan to stay more than a day-cycle, you need a **hammock or a raised bed platform**. The ground is perpetually wet and alive with moss and lichen. Pitching a tent on it is disrespectful and will leave a scar. Suspend your shelter between sturdy, low stone features, using wide, non-abrasive straps to avoid damaging the fluted surfaces.
*   **The Bag:** A waterproof backpack with a soft, non-scuffing exterior. Everything must be secured to prevent clatter.

#### III. Conduct in the Mineral Metropolis
You are a visitor in a city that is not yours. Act with the reverence of a guest in a sacred space.

*   **The Principle of Minimal Trace:** Leave no mark. No carvings. No litter. Pack out every scrap. Walk gently; the moss springs back slowly. Do not chip at the stone “for a sample.” To do so is vandalism on a geological scale.
*   **Movement:** Move slowly, deliberately, and quietly. Sudden movements are jarring in the stillness. Your footsteps will be muffled by the moss. Let them be.
*   **Interaction with the Architecture:**
    *   **Entering Towers:** You may enter the eroded windows and doorways. Do so with care. The stone, though vast, can be fragile where erosion has undercut it. Test your weight. The interiors are networks of chambers, often with multiple levels connected by narrow, water-carved chimneys. Do not climb these without a secure rope; a fall here means silence and no hope of rescue.
    *   **Touch:** You may touch the walls. They will be cool and damp. You will feel the intricate fluting, the smoothness of centuries of water-flow. It is permissible. It is a connection.
    *   **The Water:** Do not disturb the pools. They are lenses to the sky and worlds for the thread-worms and algae. Do not wash in them. Do not drink from them.
*   **Encountering Life:** The moth-creatures, the gliders, the thread-worms—they are fragile and highly specialized. Observe from a distance. Do not attempt to catch or feed them. Your biology is alien to them; your presence is disturbance enough.
*   **Sound:** Speak rarely, and then only in whispers or hushed tones. Shouting feels like a physical blow in the dense, quiet air. Consider communicating with your companions via hand signals. The most profound communication here is often non-verbal.

#### IV. Hazards & Health
*   **Hypothermia:** The insidious, constant danger. The damp chill will seep in the moment you stop generating heat. Change into dry base layers the instant you feel a deep chill setting in. Keep moving at a gentle pace.
*   **Disorientation:** The uniform light, the repetitive stone forms, and the muffling mist can cause profound disorientation. Mark your path religiously. The feeling of being lost in an endless, silent city can trigger panic.
*   **Psychological Strain:** The “Great Quiet” can press on some minds. The scale and age can induce feelings of existential dread or profound loneliness. Have a mental anchor—a mantra, a memory, a purpose for your visit. If you travel with others, maintain gentle, periodic contact. For solo travelers, this is the greatest test.
*   **Unseen Depths:** The plateau is riddled with fissures, sinkholes, and deep shafts worn by water. The moss blankets everything. **Probe ahead with a walking stick** before putting your full weight down, especially near visible water channels.

#### V. What to Expect: A Typical Visit
You will arrive, likely at the mossy edge. The air will hit you—cool, thick, tasting of wet stone and ancient air. The silence will feel like a presence. The mist will bead on your hood and cloak within minutes.

You will walk. For hours. The city will unfold around you, a new vista at every turn—a towering spire veiled in a passing fog-ghost, a perfect arch framing a pool like a mirror, a cavernous opening leading into shadow.

You will find a place to sit, perhaps on a low, lichen-crusted plinth. You will listen. The *plink… plink…* will mark time. You will watch a droplet form, swell, and fall from a stone icicle overhead. You may see the lead-winged moths flutter. You will feel the slow, patient exhalation of the land.

You will feel very small. And then, if you let it, you will feel a part of something immense and beautifully balanced. You are a brief, warm pulse in a cold, slow world. Your visit is but one droplet in the endless weeping of the stone.

Leave as you came: quietly, carefully, carrying all you brought, plus the memory of a place where time is not a river, but a deep, still lake.
