Alright, traveler. You’ve pulled the coordinates for a place called, by the few who map such things, **The Sump**, or sometimes **The Glotzen Metropolis**. Forget every city you’ve ever known. This isn’t New York with rain or Venice with canals. This is a lung that breathes water vapor, a machine that sweats, an ecosystem where the line between built and born has been erased by millennia of damp. You’re not walking into a city; you’re walking into a superorganism’s warm, wet gut. If you’re ordinary—flesh, blood, a mind used to solid ground and quiet nights—you will break here unless you prepare down to your soul. Here’s how not to die, or go insane, in the first hour.

### **1. Mindset: Surrender the Illusion of Control**
Your first and most critical preparation is psychological. This city operates on a logic of fluid dynamics, collective flow, and real-time reconfiguration. Streets change. Walls move. Water levels shift. If you cling to a map, a plan, or a scheduled appointment, you will have a panic attack. The dominant sensation is **pervasive agency**—the city itself feels alive and *indifferent* to you.
*   **Embrace Flux:** Your mantra is "I am a drop in the stream." You will be diverted, merged, and stalled by the city’s traffic algorithms. A doorway that was there yesterday might be a solid wall today because the building needed the volume elsewhere. This isn’t malfunction; it’s homeostasis.
*   **Accept Sensory Overload:** The sound is not background noise; it’s a physical pressure. The thrum from below, the hiss-roar of water, the shriek of mag-lev, the collective hum of billions of lives and machines—it will press on your eardrums and your sanity. You cannot fight it. You must let it become your new silence.
*   **Let Go of "Natural":** There is no green, no soil, no un-augmented life. The "foliage" is tech. The "animals" are part of the maintenance crew. The air smells of ozone and wet smart-polymer. Your brain will scream that this is wrong. Tell it to shut up. This is *their* natural.

### **2. The Body: Fortify Your Vessel**
The environment is actively hostile to baseline human biology. Think of it as preparing for a dive in a toxic, warm sea.
*   **Lungs & Skin:** The air is 95% relative humidity at a constant 32°C (90°F). It’s a lukewarm soup. You will sweat, but it won’t evaporate. You’ll feel perpetually clammy. **Hyperhydration is a must.** You’ll lose water through your skin and lungs constantly. Electrolyte tabs are worth their weight in gold. For skin, you need a **non-occlusive antifungal and antibacterial barrier cream**. Everywhere. Athlete’s foot will be the least of your worries; rampant yeast infections and bacterial rashes in every fold are a given.
*   **Ears:** The noise isn't just loud; it contains sub-auditory frequencies that can cause nausea, disorientation, and anxiety over time. **Custom-molded dampener plugs** are the bare minimum. The rich option is sub-dural implants that filter out the harmful low-end thrum while allowing conversational frequencies through. Without protection, chronic migraines are guaranteed.
*   **Eyes:** The light is diffused, glary, and reflected from a million wet surfaces. There are no true shadows. This causes intense eye strain and depth-perception issues. **Polarized, amber-tinted lenses** will help cut the glare and enhance contrast. Night-vision is useless; there is no real darkness, only varying degrees of hazy luminescence.
*   **Feet:** The "ground" is slick ceramic, submerged tiles, or gridded platforms over black water. It’s like walking on a wet bar of soap. **Magnetic-grip soles** are non-negotiable. The city’s substructure has enough ferrous alloy to make them work. Without them, a broken tailbone or a plunge into a canal is a matter of when, not if.

### **3. Gear: What to Pack (and What to Leave)**
Forget fashion. Think survival in a warm, loud, shifting swamp of tech.
*   **The Suit:** A single-piece, breathable yet water-shedding suit. Think advanced fishing waders meets techwear. It must be made of a non-absorbent, quick-drying synthetic with sealed seams. **No cotton. No denim.** They will stay wet, become heavy, and rot on your body. Integrated climate control is a fantasy—the ambient heat makes active cooling systems work too hard. Passive moisture-wicking is your friend.
*   **The Bag:** Waterproof, obviously. But more importantly, it should be a **hard-shell clam design** with positive pressure seals. The humidity will seep into any zipper or flap and ruin everything—electronics, dry clothes, food. Silica gel packs are a joke here. Use vacuum-sealed dry-sacks for anything critical.
*   **Electronics:** Assume they will get wet. **Full immersion potting** for any device you care about. Wireless charging is ubiquitous, but the protocols are layered and encrypted. Bring a universal inductive puck, but be prepared to barter for local access keys. Old-fashioned, hardened data slates with physical ports can be more reliable.
*   **Currency & Trade:** Hard currency is meaningless. The economy runs on **data**, **energy credits**, and **favors**. Come with something to trade: pristine information from other realities, rare molecular templates, or unique cognitive algorithms. Stored power (in clean, dense cells) is always welcome. Your personality and stories might buy you a meal or a guide, but don’t count on it.
*   **Self-Defense:** Weapons are tricky. The fauna is mostly indifferent, but the 'scrubbers' can be territorial, and the data-jellies in the canals can deliver a nasty neuro-static shock if provoked. More dangerous are other travelers or desperate locals. A high-lumen strobe (to disorient in the hazy light) and a compact, high-voltage taser (effective against both biological and lightly augmented systems) are prudent. A blade will rust into uselessness in a day.

### **4. On Arrival: Navigation & Etiquette**
You’ll likely materialize on a designated transit platform, slick with condensation and shuddering from nearby mag-lev passage.
*   **Don’t Stand Still:** You are an obstruction in a flow. Move with the prevailing current of traffic until you can get your bearings. Stopping to gawk is how you get knocked into the water.
*   **Watch the Liquids:** Not all water is just water. Some pools are coolant runoff, some are data-transmission medium, some are acidic byproducts of the city’s metabolism. The black, mirror-still pools are generally safe to look at, but not to touch. Flowing water in carved channels is usually part of the circulatory system and is safer. Assume any colored or iridescent sheen means **do not touch**.
*   **The Fauna Are Not Pets:** Do not touch the glint-fish swarms. They are filtering the air and might see your hand as a contaminant, giving you a thousand tiny, abrasive cuts. Do not interfere with the scrubbers. They are fixing things. If you obstruct one, a dozen more might come to "resolve" the obstruction—you. The data-jellies are benign unless you try to ingest or hack them.
*   **Asking for Directions:** Be direct. Time is fluid here, but attention is scarce. Offer a micro-credit of data or power for a consultation. Do not ask for street names or addresses. Use landmarks: "the fusion arch that pulses blue," "the geyser that erupts on the half-hour," "the gargoyle that weeps black fluid."
*   **Shelter:** There are no traditional hotels. You can rent volumetric space. A wall will extrude a pod for you, a damp, closet-sized cavity with a gel-mat for sleeping. It might be re-absorbed if demand shifts. Never leave your belongings unattended; the walls themselves might digest them.

### **5. The Real Danger: Assimilation**
The greatest threat isn’t drowning or getting lost. It’s the city’s **vibrancy**. Its relentless, pulsing, wet *aliveness* can be seductive. You’ll start to see the logic in the flow. The hum becomes a lullaby. The constant reconfiguration feels like growth. Many travelers come for a week and become permanent residents, adding their own hum to the chorus, gradually allowing the city’s smart-matter to integrate with their bodies—a grafted gill here, a subcutaneous data-port there. They cease to be visitors and become another organ in the body of the Glotzen Metropolis. If you wish to leave, you must guard your desire for stillness, for dryness, for quiet. Remember the feel of real sun, of dirt, of a horizon that isn’t mist. Write it down. Cling to it. Or you may just decide to stay, and let the slow, patient water claim you, too.

Good luck. You’re going to need it. And pack extra socks. You’ll mourn the concept of dry feet.
