Forget everything you know about preparing for a dive. This is not the sea. You are entering a cathedral of liquid industry, a biome of deliberate, silent growth. The human body and mind are not made for this place. To visit and not become a pollutant, a disruption, or a casualty, you must re-engineer your very approach to existence. Here is how you prepare.

### Phase 1: The Body – Becoming a Ghost in the Machine

**Respiration:** You will breathe water. Not metaphorically. The chamber’s life support will interface directly with your bloodstream via gill-filaments. You cannot rely on lungs here.

*   **Pre-adaptation:** At least 72 hours before transit, you must undergo a full pulmonary flush and implant the standard amphibious synaptic shunts at the carotid and femoral arteries. This isn’t cosmetic; it tricks your brainstem into accepting liquid oxygenation without triggering the drowning panic reflex. The procedure feels like being gently suffocated by light. You will cough up pink-tinged fluid for a day. This is normal.
*   **The Gills:** You will not sprout them. They are part of the chamber. Upon entry, you must locate the induction panels—usually at the chamber’s “nodal points” where the crystalline facets converge. Press your shunted arteries against the warm metal. You’ll feel a filamentous *tingle* as the microscopic tendrils connect. The first breath is the hardest: a cold, clear fullness flooding your chest. You must consciously relax. Fight it, and you’ll black out. Surrender, and it becomes like air—warmer, thicker, but breathable.

**Pressure & Buoyancy:** The chamber is pressurized to several atmospheres, but it is a *constant* pressure. No decompression sickness on entry or exit, thanks to the transit field’s stabilization. The real challenge is navigation.

*   **Orientation:** There is no “up.” Your inner ear will scream for a horizon. You must de-train your vestibular reliance. Spend your final pre-jump hours in a neutral buoyancy tank with randomized light fields. Learn to orient by the *geometry*: the knife-sharp edges of the panels, the direction of the faintest current (which always flows from filtration vents to intake grilles, hidden behind the foliage).
*   **Movement:** You will not swim. You will *drift* and *guide*. Sudden, thrashing motions will shred the glass-spine ferns and alert the patient machinery to an anomaly. Practice in viscous gel. A gentle push-off from a surface, a slow tuck, a minimal adjustment. Think of yourself as a mote of dust in a sunbeam. Your propulsion is the chamber’s own gentle, thermal-current circulation.

**The Skin:** The water is skin-temperature, but it is a *medium*, not a blanket. You will feel every microscopic gradient.
*   **Exposure Suit:** A full-body sheath of neutrally buoyant, non-abrasive polymer. Must be utterly non-porous. Your sweat, your skin oils, a single stray hair—these are biological contaminants this closed system has never known. The suit must be surgically sealed at the wrists, neck, and ankles after you don it. It will feel like a second skin, but remember: it is your primary containment vessel.

### Phase 2: The Mind – Synchronizing to Geological Time

This environment operates on a timescale that will make your human metabolism feel like a frenzied, screaming alarm.

*   **Sensory Deprivation & Overload:** The silence is not empty; it is *thick*. The shadowless light erases depth. The only sounds are the occasional, massive systems event: a valve cycling (*shuuush-click*), a mineral deposit forming (*tink*). You must re-calibrate your attention. Listen for the sub-audible: the hum of the pumps is the place’s heartbeat. Watch for movement measured in inches per hour. The pruning arm’s arc is a meditation.
*   **Patience Drills:** In preparation, lock yourself in a silent, white room with a single, slow-moving pendulum. Your task is to observe a complete cycle without forming a single impatient thought. When you can watch a plant grow for six hours and feel neither boredom nor urgency, you are ready.
*   **The Loneliness:** There are no animals. No consciousness but yours and the vast, silent, algorithmic mind of the garden-factory. This solitude has a weight. Prepare by reviewing logs of deep-space or under-ice isolation. Understand that the longing for a face, a gesture, a quickening, will surface. You must make peace with the fronds, the pulsing vines, the machines as your only companions. They are not passive; they are *working*. Your presence is an audit.

### Phase 3: Equipment – Tools of a Non-Interventionist

You bring almost nothing. What you do bring must be sterile, non-magnetic, and neutral.

*   **Recording:** A light-capture bead, worn on the forehead. It records not just visual data, but thermal gradients and subtle spectral shifts in the fluorescent moss. No active scans—they might disrupt growth cycles.
*   **Sampling:** If you must take a sample (and you should only do so if your mission explicitly requires it), you will use a tool provided by the chamber itself. Observe the robotic tendrils. They use crystalline scalpels that form from mineral deposits in the water. You may be able to request one by gently manipulating a “budding” node—a procedure that requires days of observation to learn. No external knives.
*   **Navigation:** A simple inertial tracker is useless. You will map by *topology*. Memorize the facet patterns. The cable-vines that pulse with light are the system’s data conduits; following their convergence will lead you to central control nodes (if such things exist). The glass-spine forests mark nutrient-dense zones. The metallic moss grows thickest near oxygenation vents.
*   **Sustenance:** You will not eat or drink inside. Your suit’s IV will provide a caloric gel directly into your bloodstream. Any consumption of the local biome—those swollen nutrient pods—would be a catastrophic theft from a meticulously balanced economy.

### Phase 4: Protocols – The Etiquette of a Visitor

You are walking into a living factory. Your protocol is that of a monk entering a sanctum.

1.  **The Entry:** Transit will deposit you in a designated airlock sphere, a bubble of gas that will quickly fill with the chamber’s water. Perform your gill-connection here. Do not proceed until your breathing is calm and automatic.
2.  **The First Hour:** Do not move. Drift. Let the currents position you. Observe. Identify the rhythm of the nearest machine cycle. Note the patterns in the flora’s luminescence. Your initial data should be purely passive.
3.  **Movement:** Always move *with* the prevailing current, never across it. To change direction, use the structural lattice, but touch only the bare, brushed alloy at rivet points. Avoid touching any vegetation directly. The glass spines can cut your suit; the metallic moss can harbor sharp edges.
4.  **Interaction:** Do not interrupt the machines. If a harvest arm is moving through a volume, you wait. Its path is pre-ordained, its timing precise. To block it is to cause a system error that may take weeks to rectify.
5.  **Communication:** There is no one to talk to. Your suit’s comms are for recording log entries only. Speak in whispers, if you must speak. Sound travels far and clearly in this liquid.

### Phase 5: The Exit – Decompression of the Soul

Leaving is often harder than entering.

*   **Disconnection:** Reversing the gill-link is a visceral shock. You will feel a profound emptiness in your chest as your body relearns the poverty of gaseous air. You will cough, violently, for several minutes.
*   **Debriefing:** You must undergo a full biological quarantine. Every molecule on your suit’s surface will be scanned and catalogued. Any trace of the chamber’s unique mineral or biological signature must be contained.
*   **Psychological Re-integration:** The world will seem deafeningly loud, garishly shadowed, and recklessly fast. You will find yourself standing still, waiting for a traffic light to change, with the patience of a stone. This will pass, but a stillness will remain in you. A memory of warm, silent water, and a garden that built itself, one impossibly slow, perfect gesture at a time.

To visit this place is not an adventure. It is a pilgrimage to a temple where the gods are geometry and growth, and the only prayer is perfect, patient observation. Prepare to be humbled. Prepare to be silent. Prepare to become, for a little while, a part of the machine’s breath.
