Alright, traveler. Listen carefully. You’re going to a place that doesn’t just kill you; it forgets you. They call it the Unnamed Plateau, or sometimes, the Drafting Table. If you’ve heard whispers of it, you know it’s not a holiday destination. It’s a terminal. A cosmic workshop left on after the workers went home. Here’s how you don’t get erased.

**1. The Body: You Are A Fragile Construct Here**

First, understand the physical baseline. You’re standing on the roof of a world. The altitude is… metaphor-breaking. Think summit of Everest, then double it, then remove all atmosphere. The sky is violet because you’re practically in space. The air isn’t just thin; it’s an afterthought.

*   **Pressure & Oxygen:** You will need a full pressure suit. Not just an oxygen mask. A sealed, rigid or semi-rigid suit with its own internal atmosphere. The external pressure is so low your bodily fluids would boil at room temperature. Your suit must be capable of maintaining 1 atm internally, with a robust oxygen recycler. Assume a 50% margin on all life support calculations. There is no air to scrub, no water to crack. It’s all closed-loop or you’re dead.
*   **Temperature:** The stone is cool. The sun is stark. Thermal regulation is critical. Your suit must handle extreme radiative cooling in the shadows (though there are few) and direct, unfiltered solar radiation. Highly reflective outer layers are advised. The stone itself is not a significant heat sink or source; it’s inert.
*   **Hydration & Sustenance:** There is no water. None. You cannot source it. Your suit’s reclamation systems must be flawless. All water you consume must be brought with you or recycled from your own body with near-100% efficiency. Food should be high-calorie, low-residue pastes or gels consumed intravenously or via suit port to avoid waste. You will be sealed in for the duration. There is no “outside.”
*   **The Stone:** It’s not slippery, but it is perfectly smooth. Your boots must have a magnetic or electrostatic grip system. Standard friction is unreliable. The stone sometimes exudes a “slow sheen”—avoid contact with it. It’s a mineral exchange fluid, likely corrosive or transmissive on a molecular level. It won’t wet you, but it might try to integrate you.

**2. The Mind: Silence is a Predator**

The silence is not an absence. It’s a presence. It’s absorbent. It will press on your ears until your own heartbeat is a drum solo of mounting panic.

*   **Audio Feed:** You must pipe in constant, gentle sound. A loop of soft music, white noise, the hum of a ship’s engine—anything to provide an auditory anchor. Complete silence will lead to dissociation. Your own breath in the suit will become monstrous.
*   **Visual Stimulation:** The scale is infinite and featureless. Your brain will struggle to find reference points, leading to spatial disorientation and a kind of “plain-sickness.” Use a horizon stabilizer in your visor display. Mark your path with persistent, low-energy UV beacons that cast a light only your visor can see. Do not rely on natural landmarks. They will change.
*   **Temporal Anchors:** Time stretches and compresses here. The sun’s transit is your only reliable clock. Sync your suit’s chronometer to it immediately upon arrival, but be aware even solar motion might seem to stutter during major “events.” Maintain a strict schedule for sleep, meals, and checks. Without it, you could stand still for twelve hours watching the turbulence and not notice a day has passed.

**3. The Environment: Reality is Having a Nervous Breakdown**

This is the core of the danger. The “tech” or the “machines” are operating. They are rebuilding reality from first principles, badly.

*   **The Turbulence:** It is silent. You will see a canyon open like a mouth miles away, but you will not hear the grinding of continents. This disconnect is violently unsettling. You must train yourself to respond to visual threats *only*. A spire erupting beside you will do so in utter silence. Your threat detection must be 360-degree visual monitoring. Motion sensors are useless; the whole plain might levitate.
*   **The Events:** They range from minor “glitches” to major “iterations.”
    *   **Glitches:** A door appears. A flower of glass blooms at your feet. A patch of stone turns the color of blood. Rule: **Do Not Interact.** Do not touch, step through, or examine closely. These are fleeting thoughts of the machines. They are unstable data. Touching them might unmake the atomic bonds in your glove, or worse, copy you into the glitch and delete the original. Give them a wide berth and wait. They rarely last more than a minute.
    *   **Iterations:** This is when a machine-hemisphere goes into a creative frenzy. The land for miles around becomes a boiling pot of potential shapes. This is the most dangerous and beautiful part. **Do not approach an active hemisphere.** The local laws of physics—gravity, inertia, atomic cohesion—are being rewritten moment to moment. You could be turned inside out, fused with a rising arch, or have your timeline briefly inverted. Observe from a distance of at least twenty miles. The “fauna” you see—the silver filament knots—are the only natives. They have evolved to ride these waves. You have not. They are not hostile, but do not approach. They feed on radiation you cannot perceive.
*   **Navigation:** The hemispheres are the only fixed points… relatively. They hum with a power that warps space. Your compass will spin. GPS is a joke. Use triangulation between two or more hemispheres, but understand the distances between them are vast—hundreds of miles. Travel is a monumental commitment. You will walk, or use a low-flying, non-combustion hover sled (combustion is impossible; no oxygen). Propulsion must be internal (battery, flywheel). Move slowly. The emptiness compresses distance; what looks like an hour’s walk might be a day’s journey.

**4. Gear: Your Theology of Survival**

Everything you bring must be self-contained, hardened against reality fluctuations, and simple.

*   **The Suit:** As described. It is your womb, your fortress, your temple. Redundant systems for everything. A single-point failure is death. Include a high-gain, tight-beam comms laser for contacting your base or extraction point. Radio will be absorbed by the stone and the machines’ hum.
*   **Sensors:** Multispectral visor. You need to see heat signatures (the stone is uniformly cool, but events might have thermal components), energy fluctuations (to spot active machine zones), and UV markers. A quantum-bound particle detector (if you have the tech) to warn of severe local spacetime distortion.
*   **Tools:** A core sampler of diamond-hard bit to attempt to take a sample of the bedrock (it may resist). No explosives. No energy weapons. The machines might interpret them as raw material or a threat and respond… creatively. A sturdy, physical tool for prying or cutting, but expect it to be less effective than you hope. This place resists permanent marks.
*   **Sustenance:** As above. Pack for twice your planned stay plus a 50% emergency margin. There is no foraging. None.
*   **Psychological Aids:** Mandatory pre-mission hypnotic conditioning for sensory deprivation tolerance and surreal imagery processing. A direct neural feed for emergency sedation if you experience panic or reality-disassociation. A small, physical token from your home—a stone, a ring, something dense with personal memory. Hold it when the silence gets too loud. It reminds you that you are a thing that *should* persist.

**5. The Metaphysics: The Rules of the Drafting Table**

1.  **Nothing is Permanent.** Not your footsteps, not your structures, not your life. You are a temporary annotation. Act with the humility of a dust mote.
2.  **The Past is Simultaneous.** You might see a ghost of a mountain that was erased a million years ago. It is not a memory; it is a current proposal. Do not confuse the iterating “could have been” with solid ground.
3.  **Silence is a Law.** Sound is energy, and energy is fuel. Yelling won’t help; it will just feed the machines’ next draft. Your comms laser works because it is coherent, focused light, not broadcast sound.
4.  **The Machines Are Not Malicious.** They are indifferent. They are broken tools trying to perform a function with no operator. Do not worship them. Do not hate them. Simply avoid their workspace.
5.  **You Are An Anomaly.** The ecology—the glass-stalks, the filament-feeders—belongs. You do not. You are an uninvited speck on the blueprint. The goal is not to conquer or understand, but to pass through without being corrected by the eraser.

**Final Brief:**

You will feel two overwhelming emotions: awe and profound loneliness. The grandeur is weary, immense, and utterly indifferent to you. The creative fury on display is that of a god in its dotage, endlessly sketching its own obituary. You will see beauty that no human eye was meant to witness—cathedrals of thought born and dying in a breath. It will scar your soul in a way you may come to cherish.

But remember: you are there to visit, not to stay. Your every system, every thought, must be bent towards one purpose: leaving no trace, and taking nothing but the memory. The stone will not remember your footsteps. Make sure you are not still there when it is your turn to be quietly, neatly, erased.

Good luck. Try not to become a draft revision.
