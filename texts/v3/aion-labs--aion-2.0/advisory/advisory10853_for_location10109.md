**Field Preparation Briefing: The Weeping Engine Plateau (Designation: GEHENNA-STASIS)**

**1. PREAMBLE: THE NATURE OF THE PLACE**
You are not preparing for an expedition. You are preparing for an infiltration into a pathology. The plateau is not a location; it is a condition. It is a chronic, screaming imbalance wrapped in geological finality. Your survival will not depend on strength or cleverness alone, but on your ability to embrace contradiction and endure profound wrongness. You will be cold, you will be wet, you will be deafened, and you will constantly question your senses. The landscape is actively hostile to human biology and psychology. This briefing assumes you have a reason to go. If you do not, destroy this document and find a sane corner of the multiverse.

**2. MENTAL & PHYSICAL CONDITIONING**
*Mental:*
*   **Acceptance Drills:** Spend hours listening to recordings of industrial noise (steam vents, piston rhythms, grinding metal) mixed with organic sounds (squelches, wet tears, chitters). Do this in a cold, damp environment. Your goal is not to enjoy it, but to reach a state of detached observation where the sound becomes a background constant, not a source of panic.
*   **Reality Anchoring:** Develop a personal mantra or a repetitive mental task (prime number sequence, reciting a poem) to deploy the moment your perception wavers. You must have a cognitive "hook" to grab when the world starts to bleed.
*   **Solitude Training:** You will feel profoundly alone. Practice extended periods of isolation with minimal stimulation. The plateau's "company" is worse than solitude.
*Physical:*
*   **Hypoxic & Cold Acclimatization:** If possible, train at high altitudes. The air is thin and bitter. Your body must be efficient with oxygen.
*   **Load-Bearing in Mud:** Train walking with full gear on unstable, yielding surfaces. A treadmill in a mud pit is ideal. The sponge-ground will sap your energy with every step.
*   **Stress-Inoculation:** Expose yourself to sudden, jarring sensory changes (lights, sounds) during routine tasks to mimic the reality fluctuations.

**3. CORE GEAR PHILOSOPHY: REDUNDANCY, SIMPLICITY, ANCHORING**
Assume any complex system will fail. Electronics will glitch, succumb to damp, or be scrambled by reality fluctuations. Favor mechanical, analog, and passive systems. Every critical item must have a backup, and that backup must have a primitive fallback. Your gear is not just for function; it is a talisman against the unraveling of everything.

**4. THE KIT: AN ITEMIZED NECESSITY**
***Clothing & Shelter (Your Mobile Reality Bubble):***
*   **Vapor-Proof Immersion Suit:** A full-body suit with a robust outer layer (think armored diving suit material) and an integrated, powered heating system. It must be completely sealed. The internal climate is your only piece of "home." The exterior should be a non-reflective, dark matte color—anything glossy might attract attention.
*   **Powered Respirator Helmet:** Provides warmed, oxygen-enriched air. Contains a broad-spectrum olfactory filter to block the decay/iron/metallic smells. Integrated wide-angle goggles with multiple vision modes (standard, low-light, thermal). Thermal will be confused by the ambient biological heat, but it can spot "cold" anomalies like the gelatinous mist falls. Audio system with adjustable dampening—you must be able to mute the industrial roar to a bearable hum but not deafen yourself to nearby threats.
*   **Boot-Gloves Integration:** Boots must have a wide, spiked sole for traction in the sponge, and seal perfectly to the suit. Gloves must offer dexterity with a reinforced exterior.
*   **Shelter:** Do not plan to sleep on the ground. The substrate may liquefy or absorb you. Bring a lightweight, rigid platform that can be anchored *above* the sponge-mass, perhaps on the lower, solid-looking parts of a chimney-plant (away from the vent). A hammock-style cocoon that hangs from this platform is ideal. It must be insulated and sealed.

***Navigation & Orientation (Fighting the Lie of Distance):***
*   **Inertial Nav Unit (INU):** A mechanical, spring-wound device that tracks your steps and direction. It will drift, but it's immune to electronic failure. Check it obsessively.
*   **The Spool:** Your primary tool. A kilometers-long length of high-tensile, bio-inert line on a reel. You will anchor it at your insertion point (see Protocol) and pay it out as you move. It is your literal lifeline back to a known point. The line should be marked at regular intervals.
*   **Chemical Marker Flares:** Not for light, but for marking. They emit a long-lasting, unique chemical scent and a faint, non-biological phosphorescence. Drive them into the sponge at decision points. The local biology may ignore or absorb them, but they last longer than cairns.
*   **Passive Sonar Pinger:** A device that emits a regular, low-frequency "click" that echoes off the chimney-plants and dense mist. By listening to the echo pattern change, a trained ear can detect large voids or shifts in terrain.

***Sustenance:***
*   **Nutrient Paste & Heated Hydration System:** All food and water must be self-contained. The ambient water is a toxic, organic slurry. You will carry all hydration in insulated bladders with internal heating elements to prevent freezing. Food is high-calorie paste consumed through a suit port. No unpacking.
*   **Stimulants/Sedatives:** Controlled doses of wakefulness agents for critical stretches, and heavy sedatives for enforced sleep. Your body will not want to sleep in that noise. You must make it.

***Tools & Defense:***
*   **Probe-Pole:** A 3-meter collapsible pole of non-organic material (ceramic-coated metal). Used to test ground stability before every step. If it sinks without resistance, the ground is unsafe.
*   **Harness & Tether:** A climbing harness worn at all times, connected to your spool line. If the ground liquefies, you may be able to pull yourself out along the line.
*   **Arc-Cutter:** A short-range, intense thermal cutter. Not for combat, but for emergency egress if the vegetation closes around you. Using it may agitate the entire local substrate. A weapon of absolute last resort.
*   **No Weapons.** You cannot fight this place. Aggression will be met with overwhelming, incomprehensible response. Your defense is avoidance, stillness, and retreat.

***Sanity & Medical:***
*   **Anchor Object:** A small, physically simple object from your home reality with deep personal meaning. Hold it, look at it when the unreal bleeds through. It is a benchmark for "real."
*   **Logbook & Grease Pencil:** Document everything. The act of writing forces linear thought. The log is your testament if you are lost.
*   **Broad-Spectrum Prophylactics:** Anti-fungal, anti-parasitic, anti-everything inoculations and oral courses. Assume the air and mist are laden with spores that want to make you part of the local ecology.
*   **Trauma Kit:** For lacerations, fractures, and hypothermia. Include bio-sealant foam that works in wet conditions.
*   **"Soma" Drip:** An IV system containing a carefully balanced cocktail of neuro-stabilizers, anti-psychotics, and reality-affirming compounds. It is a continuous, low-dose intervention against the breakdown's cognitive effects. It will make you feel dull and distant. This is preferable to raving madness.

**5. INSERTION & INITIAL PROTOCOL**
1.  **Arrival:** The moment you translate in, do not move. Let your suit stabilize. Your audio dampeners should engage at 70%.
2.  **Sensory Scan:** Visually scan 360 degrees. Note the rhythm of the nearest steam vents. Note the density of mist and any large, moving bioluminescent forms. Listen *through* the noise for closer, irregular sounds.
3.  **Ground Anchor:** Deploy your primary anchor—a heavy, corkscrew-like piton—into the sponge-ground. Attach your main spool reel. This is **Point Alpha.** You will return here to extract.
4.  **First Movement:** Using your probe-pole, test the ground in a 5-meter radius around Point Alpha. Find the most stable, least "active" ground (minimal weeping, no visible pulsation). Move there to set up a preliminary observation post for 6-12 hours. Watch. Learn the local patterns of fluctuation.

**6. SURVIVAL STRATEGIES IN-REGION**
*   **Movement:** Slow is smooth, smooth is fast. Test every step. Follow the line of your spool. Never run. Running vibrations may trigger substrate reactions.
*   **Camping:** Your platform must be anchored to something immobile—the base of a large, silent chimney-plant (cold, no steam) is best. Never camp under an active vent or in a depression where mist or liquid could pool.
*   **Fauna Interaction:** You are a ghost. Observe, do not obstruct. If a creature approaches, freeze. Most are parts of the engine and will ignore you if you are inert. Do not touch the glowing ones.
*   **Reality Flaw Response (The Blink Protocol):** If the world stutters, doubles, or tears, immediately: a) Secure your tether to your anchor line. b) Close your eyes. c) Engage your auditory dampeners to maximum. d) Recite your mantra or count backwards from 100. e) Do not open your eyes until you have reached 0. The flaw may pass. If it does not, and the wrongness persists, retreat along your line to the last known stable point.
*   **The Liquefaction Zone:** If the ground begins to churn and soften around you, do not struggle. Signal your harness winch (attached to your main spool) to retract you swiftly along your established line, like a fish being reeled in. If you are untethered, you are already lost.

**7. ABSOLUTE PROHIBITIONS (THE "NEVERS")**
*   Never approach within 200 meters of an active steam-vent chimney. The exhaust is superheated and may contain corrosive or hallucinogenic compounds.
*   Never enter an area where the mist has a cohesive, jelly-like appearance.
*   Never consume, touch, or inhale any local substance without full suit integrity.
*   Never follow a light or a sound that seems "inviting" or forms a recognizable pattern. It is a lure.
*   Never stay in one place for more than 36 hours without moving. The substrate may begin to grow over you.
*   Never, under any circumstances, attempt to "communicate" with any part of the landscape. It is not sentient. It is process. You are a temporary anomaly.

**8. EXTRACTION & CONTINGENCIES**
*   **Planned Extraction:** Return to Point Alpha at the predetermined time. Your extraction mechanism (beacon, portal device) must be mechanical and triggered manually.
*   **Lost Line/Disoriented:** If you lose your spool line and the INU is useless, find the nearest high point (a stable chimney-plant). Climb carefully. Look for the rhythmic pulsing of steam vents—they are the "heartbeats" of the plateau. Your goal is to navigate to a region of lower sonic activity. The edge of the plateau *may* be quieter. This is a Hail Mary.
*   **Suit Breach:** Immediate sealant application from the trauma kit, followed by immediate, direct return to Point Alpha. Every second exposed increases your integration risk.
*   **Cognitive Degradation (The Fade):** If your companion (or you, via log review) notices time loss, memory gaps, or a desire to "join the rhythm," it is terminal for the mission. The Soma drip dosage must be increased, and extraction must be initiated immediately, even if objectives are not met. To continue is to become part of the scenery—a briefly glowing, clicking thing that stumbles into a vent and is gone.

**9. FINAL ADVISORY**
The Weeping Engine Plateau is a wound in causality dressed as a place. You will not conquer it. You will not understand it. With extreme preparation, ruthless discipline, and monumental luck, you might briefly visit it and return. The thing you bring back will be you, but changed, carrying the cold damp and the echo of the engine in your bones. Make sure what you seek there is worth that price.

**End Briefing.**
