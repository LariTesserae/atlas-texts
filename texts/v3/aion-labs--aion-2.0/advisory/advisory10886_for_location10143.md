Preparation begins with understanding: you are not visiting a place, but an event. The mountain’s fever is a symptom of a universe in its natal phase, a pocket of reality where the rules are still settling. Your safety depends on respecting that process. You are an intrusion, a speck of ordered biology entering a forge where causality itself is being hammered out. Act accordingly.

**I. Mental & Perceptual Fortification**

Reality here is a slow leak. Your mind is your first and most vulnerable frontier.

1.  **Cognitive Anchors:** Bring physical, simple objects that embody unwavering constants from your home reality. A polished stone, a cold-forged iron ring, a laminated photograph of a fixed landscape. Hold them when you feel the "taste of mint and static" that signifies a conceptual glitch. They are not magical talismans, but focal points for your pattern-recognition brain to latch onto, reminding you that "green" is a color, "stone" is solid, and "you" are a continuous entity.
2.  **Sensory Dampeners:** The light is flat and shadowless, which can cause profound disorientation and depth-perception failure. Bring goggles with subtle, polarized tints—amber to counteract the flatness, and a set of deep blue for observing the bioluminescent pods without overwhelming your eyes. The constant, sub-audible hum from the glass-thread "fronds" can induce trance, nausea, or unconsciousness. Wear high-fidelity earplugs that dampen low-frequency resonance while allowing you to hear crucial sounds, like the sigh of a phasing pillar.
3.  **Temporal Grounding:** The scale of time is deceptive. Processes that look static are achingly slow; glitches are fleeting. Wear two timepieces: a robust digital chronometer to log your visit in objective minutes, and a simple hourglass (3-minute cycle) to give you a visceral, non-electronic sense of time's passage. The silent, patient pace of the place can stretch seconds into subjective hours.
4.  **Reality Drift Primer:** Before entering, spend an hour in absolute sensory deprivation. Then, slowly reintroduce stimuli—a single scent, a texture, a sound. This "resets" your baseline and makes you more sensitive to the initial, subtle signs of perceptual unraveling. You must notice the glitches before they affect you.

**II. Physical Protection & Sustenance**

The environment is hostile in a quiet, draining way.

1.  **Thermal Regulation:** The heat is dry and emanates from the walls. Standard insulated gear will cook you. You need a passively reflective suit—silvery, multi-layered, designed to radiate your own body heat back inward while reflecting external thermal energy. It must be entirely sealed. No skin exposure. Cooling is achieved via a phase-change garment underneath, using non-water-based coolant packs (water is too precious).
2.  **Atmosphere & Hydration:** The air is hot, thin, and devoid of moisture. You will lose water at an alarming rate through respiration and perspiration. A closed-circuit re-breather is mandatory. It must scrub carbon dioxide and recycle your breath's moisture, supplemented by an internal water reservoir. Plan to consume at least 500ml of water per hour, via a drinking tube inside your mask. The suit's exterior must be absolutely airtight—not against vacuum, but against desiccation.
3.  **The "No-Water" Rule:** Under no circumstances bring liquid water into the chamber in an exposed state. The biota—the copper moss, the glass threads—may detect it as a profound anomaly, a shock of high-coherence chemistry in a realm feeding on breakdown. It could trigger an unpredictable cascade. All internal hydration systems must be self-contained. Your own biological water is shielded by layers of skin and suit; do not test that barrier.
4.  **Footing & Vibration:** The floor is varied—smooth stone, porous sinter, patches of crystalline growth. Wear boots with composite soles, non-metallic, to avoid conducting the deep thrum from the sinter. The tread must be designed for both grip and minimal vibration transmission. The rhythmic thrum can, over time, liquefy your bones if coupled directly.

**III. Tools & Instrumentation**

What you bring is as much about learning as surviving. Do not bring anything that seeks to impose order.

1.  **Passive Sensors Only:** No active lidar, sonar, or radar. The industrial processes here may interpret active probing as a rival pattern, an attempt to overwrite its code. Bring:
    *   A high-sensitivity hygrometer (to confirm the shocking dryness).
    *   A broad-spectrum passive radiation detector (to measure the "excess ontological energy").
    *   A magnetometer (the metallic sinter and copper moss suggest complex, living magnetic fields).
    *   All should record to solid-state, shielded storage. Assume wireless transmission is impossible and potentially dangerous.
2.  **Sampling with Extreme Prejudice:** Do not sample the lush flora. The copper moss is visibly electrified. The glass threads hum—they could be superconducting or vibrational weapons. The pods' bioluminescence is tied to reality decay. Touching them may not burn you; it might unbind the molecular forces holding you together. If you must collect a sample, use a tool of inert, non-conductive ceramic to scrape a minuscule amount of the metallic sinter from an area that seems dormant. Place it in a vacuum-sealed, lead-glass vial. Treat it as both radioactive and cognitively hazardous.
3.  **Navigation & Orientation:** The shadowless light and sparse, repeating features create a potent maze. Drop no breadcrumbs. Instead, use a spool of ultra-fine, carbon-nanofiber thread, anchoring it at the entrance. It is nearly invisible and should be non-reactive. Your reflective suit will help—use a handheld laser pointer (low-power) to create an artificial shadow on a feature to judge distance and texture.
4.  **The "Knife":** Bring one simple, multipurpose tool: a ceramic-bladed knife. It is for cutting your guide-thread, for prying, and as a last-resort anchor. Do not use it on any living or seemingly living structure.

**IV. Conduct Within The Forge**

Your behavior is the final layer of preparation.

1.  **Move Like a Ghost:** Walk slowly, placing your feet with deliberate care. Sudden movements or vibrations are a foreign punctuation in this slow sentence. Pause often. Listen to the silence until you can hear the hum, the thrum, the occasional air-sigh of a glitch.
2.  **The Prime Directive of Observation:** Do not interfere. Do not attempt to feed the pods or divert the growth of the alloy nodules. Do not stand in a location where a pillar is phasing. You are not a gardener or an engineer here; you are a microscope slide placed momentarily on a cosmic workbench. The factory is building something over millennia. Your lifetime is a single tick of its clock. Your job is to witness one tick.
3.  **If a Glitch Affects You:** If you taste a color or see a sound, stop immediately. Close your eyes. Grasp your cognitive anchor. Breathe slowly through your re-breather. Recite a simple, factual mantra about yourself: your name, your birthplace, the chemical formula of water. Do not move until the sensation passes and your chronometer confirms that only a few seconds have elapsed. If it persists, retreat immediately along your thread.
4.  **Duration:** Do not stay longer than two hours by your chronometer. The cumulative effect of the reality gradient is insidious. Fatigue, even with life support, will set in quickly. This is not a place for campfires and contemplation. It is a place for a swift, reverent audit.
5.  **Departure:** Withdraw as you entered, sideways through the crack. Before you leave, pause at the threshold. Look back. The memory will be more vivid than any recording. As you exit, you may feel a profound chill—not from the mountain air outside, but from the re-establishment of normal, stable, boring causality around you. It will feel thin and fragile.

**V. Upon Return**

You have brought nothing out but data and memory. Yet:

1.  **Decontamination:** Isolate all equipment, including yourself, in a sterile, non-conductive environment for 48 hours. Monitor for residual energy signatures, unusual decay in materials, or persistent synesthetic sensations.
2.  **Data Review:** Review sensor logs with extreme caution. Look for patterns in the glitches, correlations between the wall-neuron pulses and the pod color shifts. The data may not make logical sense; it may be aesthetic or poetic. A sequence of pulses might map to a prime number sequence, or to the Fibonacci pattern in a sunflower from your home world. That is the translation—the forging of a bridge between universes.
3.  **Self-Care:** You have stood where physics is soft. You may experience dreams where objects have too many dimensions, or where silence has a color. This is normal. It will fade as your mind re-knits itself to your native reality. If it does not fade after a week, seek consultation not from a physician, but from a philosopher, a poet, or a quantum physicist.

You are prepared not because you have armored yourself against all danger, but because you have acknowledged that some aspects of this place are beyond any armor. You go in humility, with your senses sharpened and your will to interfere surgically removed. You go to witness the fever dream of a mountain that is not a mountain, in the hope that when you leave, you will understand a little less—and in that understanding, perceive a little more.
