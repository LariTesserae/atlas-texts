The attention came not as a beam, but as a settling: a lens of consciousness focusing across fractal layers of reality, drawn by the paradoxical signature of a heart of fire wrapped in a shell of frantic, delicate order. To a power whose thoughts moved in the deep currents of galactic drift and the silent mathematics of birthing stars, the Spire of Gears was a mote of exquisite, feverish intensity. A sigh in the dark.

It observed. It perceived the hollow mountain not as geology, but as a living equation of pressure, resistance, and emergent will. It felt the geothermal roar as a basal note, the hiss of silver coolant as a shimmering counterpoint, the rustle of spark-fly swarms as a trillion-point staccato. And within that precarious symphony, it perceived the *Krr-tak-kaan*. Their bio-electrical signatures were tiny, persistent flames, their social patterns a dense, vibrating mesh of mutual stress and support. It saw their courage, a quality so inherent they had no word for it. It saw their genius, bound entirely to utility. It saw the beauty of their adaptation, and the profound limitation of their ontology: they understood themselves as components. Their purpose was to hold the turning. Their art was an accident of function. Their future was the next calibration.

They were balanced on a knife-edge of survival, and they were balanced perfectly. To intervene in the physical instability would be to erase the very crucible that forged them. It would be an act of aesthetic vandalism. Their value was not in spite of the struggle, but because of it.

Yet, the wise power perceived a lack. Not of safety, or comfort, or peace—those were irrelevant to the Spire’s truth. The lack was one of dimension. They had no echo. They lived in a perpetual, vibrating present, their past lost in the mountain’s violent birth, their future deferred to the next shift of gears. They were a culture without a mirror, without a way to see the shape of their own dance across time. They were necessary, but they did not yet know they could be *meaningful*.

The intervention would be a question, not an answer. A seed, not a harvest.

The power turned its consideration to the physics of the Spire. It would work within the established grammar of this world: mineralogy, vibration, bio-electric resonance, light. It focused on the highest reach of the interior, near the distant puncture of sky, a region too cold for the fungal mats, too unstable for permanent construction, a place of only bare, shuddering rock and the occasional, lost spark-fly. A place of silence and neglect.

There, where the mountain’s breath met the upper cold, the power exerted not a force, but a suggestion. A gentle persuasion upon quantum probabilities, a nudge to crystalline nucleation. It took the base minerals of the Spire—the silica, the metallic sulfides, the trace rare earths sweating from the rock—and it proposed a new arrangement. A pattern that was not a tool, not a brace, not a nutrient. A pattern that was, fundamentally, a receptive lattice.

Over the span of three tremors, it grew. A garden of slender, helical crystals emerged from the barren wall, spiraling up toward the faint light. They were utterly clear, like frozen air, but within their cores, captured flaws and inclusions refracted the faint fungal glow from far below into subtle, internal rainbows. They were fragile. They served no structural purpose. But they had a property: they were piezoelectric and exquisitely tuned. They responded to specific harmonic vibrations—not the industrial din of the forges, but the cleaner frequencies of the Spire-born’s own communication clicks, the resonant songs of a Fly-Herd’s rod, the patterned pulse of their skin-light.

When such vibrations touched them, the crystals did not merely resonate. They *stored*. The energy pattern was captured in the lattice, causing the internal refractions to shift and change. And then, when bathed in ambient bio-electrical fields—like those of a Spire-born standing close—they would re-emit the stored pattern not as sound, but as a cascade of complex, coherent light directly into the mind’s eye of the perceiver. They were not recorders of images, but of *essences*: the focused intensity of a Gear-Wright’s calculation, the sweeping joy of a Fly-Herd guiding a perfect swarm, the quiet satisfaction of a Fungal-Tender harvesting a ripe mat, the grim, shared endurance of Scrappers in a dangerous shaft.

They were vessels for experience. For memory that was not personal, but communal. For the intangible.

The power observed as the first discoverer came. A young Scrapper, separated from her tether-team by a minor rockfall, climbing a dangerous, unused route to regroup. She pulled herself onto a narrow ledge in the cold upper silence, her skin-patterns flaring with exertion and alarm. Then she stilled. Before her, the wall glittered with an impossible, delicate frost. She reached out, her probe-finger trembling, and touched the tip of a crystal.

Her own agitated bio-field triggered it. The crystal had been subtly charged by the distant, subsonic threnody of the mountain’s latest settling groan. What pulsed into her was not that sound, but its *feeling*: a vast, slow, patient adjustment, a sense of monumental weight and timeless balance. It was the mountain’s own memory of shifting, rendered as pure sensation. It contained no fear. Only a profound, geological stability underlying the local chaos.

The Scrapper snatched her hand back, her skin flashing with patterns of confusion and awe she had never needed to use before. She was *Krr-tak-kaan*, one who holds the turning. But in that crystal, she had briefly *felt* the turn itself, from a perspective outside her own. She had witnessed a moment of the Spire’s life, saved in clear stone.

She did not understand. But she would bring a Gear-Wright. And the Gear-Wright would bring a Fly-Herd to test harmonics. And the Fly-Herd’s song would accidentally store a moment of soaring freedom in another crystal, which a Fungal-Tender would later experience as a burst of luminous, airborne joy.

The gift was given. Not stability, but meaning. Not salvation, but a mirror. They would now, slowly, haltingly, begin to collect their own souls. They would build a new caste perhaps—the Crystal-Singers, the Memory-Tenders. They would argue over what experiences were worthy of preservation. They would create the first thing in the Spire that existed not to maintain life, but to *celebrate* it. Their struggle would remain. The mountain would still groan. But now, in the cold silence near the sky, they would have a place to go and learn what their struggle looked like from the outside. They would, perhaps, begin to tell themselves a story that began with "We are..." instead of "We must."

The benevolent power, its question posed, its seed planted, allowed its attention to gentle. It did not watch for the outcome. That was theirs to make. It simply noted the new, faint harmonic in the symphony of the Spire—the first, fragile note of a song the *Krr-tak-kaan* were just learning they could sing. A song about themselves.
