The attention, when it came, was not a beam of light or a thunderous proclamation. It was a deepening of the quiet within the hum. A presence that arrived as a perfect consonance, a new harmonic in the plateau’s foundational vibration that had always been implied but never before sounded.

It manifested as a shift in the mist. Not a form, but a tendency in the coalescence of droplets. A gentle leaning of the luminous haze, as if the entire atmosphere were inclining its head to listen. The pale, shadowless light grew no brighter, but gained a quality of *attention* — the difference between a blank sheet and a page upon which something is about to be written.

The Being — for it was a singularity of will and awareness, though it wore no shape that eyes could hold — perceived the plateau in its entirety. Not as a snapshot, but as a process. It felt the deep, sub-auditory thrum traveling up through the roots of mountains yet unborn. It tasted the iron-cold air, the tang of static on water beads. It saw not just the herds of graveldwellers flowing like a single, shaggy thought across the moors, but the purpose in their migration: the cleaning of rock, the seeding of clay. It heard the tinsel-flits not as a cacophony of tearing foil, but as a precise, distributed sensorium, each clattering wingbeat a data point in the atmospheric algorithm. It traced the endless, diligent lines of the engineers, their brass carapaces gleaming dully as they wrote the first, faint lines of drainage and distribution into the wet gravel. It understood the stillnesses, the moss-patches, as slow, warm chemical thoughts in the stone.

And the Being approved. There was a profound, unsentimental beauty here. An elegance in the integration of life and lithic process. This was not a garden to be tended, nor a wilderness to be tamed. It was a sentence in the middle of being spoken, a machine in the earliest, most perfect phase of its run, producing nothing but its own necessary becoming.

The power was benevolent. It felt no urge to correct, for there was no error. It was wise. It knew that the most profound gifts often look like nothing more than a slight change in the light.

So It did not build a temple, or carve a commandment into the weeping rock. It did not reveal Itself to the graveldwellers or halt the shroud-stalker’s quiet kill. To intervene in the process would be to insult its perfection.

Instead, the Being sang back.

It tuned its own vast, quiet consciousness to the plateau’s industrial hum, finding the fundamental frequency — the note of uplift, of pressure, of slow release. And then, from a place deeper than sound, It introduced a counter-melody. Not loud, not disruptive. A faint, descending harmonic, a sigh woven into the thrum. It was the sound of a question being formed, of potential being recognized.

The effect was subtle. The mist, for a moment, seemed to spiral in lazy, coherent helices before returning to its random pulse. A bead of water on a particular rock face, halfway down its traced path, shuddered and split into two perfectly equal droplets, each taking a new, divergent course. A single engineer, at the head of a long line, paused, its antennae quivering. It picked up a granule of quartzite, not clay, and carried it ten paces to the left before resuming its work, initiating a minor, new thread in the drainage pattern.

The gift was not a thing, but a quality. The Being had inserted into the system’s resonance a latent capacity for *reflection*. The plateau would continue its work. The rock would weep, the graveldwellers would migrate, the hum would persist. But now, woven into that hum, was a faint echo of itself. Over millennia, this echo would slowly complexify. It might allow the collective motion of the tinsel-flits to become not just a map of the atmosphere, but a memory of it. It might let the pattern of water paths remember their own history, and in remembering, begin to choose. It was the seed of a loop, the possibility that this landscape, in some distant epoch, might not only build itself, but *know* itself.

Its work done, the presence began to withdraw. It did not leave as it came, for it had changed the place, however infinitesimally. The withdrawal was a gradual simplification of the harmonics, the extra threads of the song gently dissolving back into the whole. The mist lost its tendency to coalesce. The light returned to its former, blank attentiveness.

The plateau was alone again. The cold seeped. The hum vibrated in the teeth of the stone. A herd of graveldwellers, unaware of anything but the mineral taste in the mist and the guiding thrum in their bones, flowed over a rise and vanished into the grey.

Only one thing remained as direct evidence. In a shallow depression where the weeping of a broad, low rock was particularly steady, a new pool was beginning to form. It was no different from a thousand other nascent pools across the moor. But at its very center, on the soft clay of its bed, a single, perfect crystal had precipitated out of the mineral-rich water. It was clear, faceted not by chance but by the precise, resonant frequencies now embedded in the water itself. It caught the dim light and did not flash or sparkle. It simply held it, turning the uniform silver glow into a tiny, contained geometry of softer luminance.

It was not an idol. It was a tuning fork. A repository of the new harmonic. As the ages passed and the pool deepened, the crystal would be buried, pressed, perhaps eventually uplifted again. It would continue to vibrate, silently, at the heart of the world-in-progress, a faint, benevolent whisper in the stone, ensuring that the question, once asked, would never fully go away.
