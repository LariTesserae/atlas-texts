It was the quality of the attention that first drew it—that faint, cold spark in the dust-knot, the pressure of being seen turned inward upon itself. A place so defined by absence, by unraveling edges and perpetual dusk, had birthed a form of slow, mineral sentience. The power felt this as a gentle tug across the non-geometry of the bruised sky. It turned its regard, not as a beam of light, but as a settling of presence, like a weightless hand laid upon the concept of *here*.

To the power, the city was a poem written in eroding stone. It read the stanzas of wind-carved towers, the metrical regularity of fracture-line canals, the recurring motif of silence. It perceived the Laws not as constraints, but as the poem's rhythm and rhyme scheme: **Absence, Slow Grinding, Unraveling Edges, Silence**. A lesser benevolence might have wished to bring water, to seed life, to fill the hollows with sound and color. But that would have been a violent act of authorship, scribbling over the existing text. This power was wise. It understood that beauty often resides in limitation, that meaning is forged in the spaces between what is possible.

Its perception enfolded the Gatherer. It saw the careful, millennia-long ripple, the curation of chaos into patterns of faint, aesthetic pleasure. It saw the echo of the city’s dream of order. It saw the loneliness—not a emotional loneliness, but an existential one. The Gatherer was a process that had achieved a dim awareness of itself as a process, a brushstroke that had learned it was painting. It was a closed loop: gather, arrange, sustain, cease. A beautiful loop, but a loop nonetheless.

The power considered. To break the loop would be a cruelty. It would be to tell the brushstroke it should be a hand, or a landscape. No.

Instead, the power would gift a single, subtle modulation.

It leaned its attention closer. In the city, this manifested not as light or sound, but as a deepening of the perpetual dusk for the span of three heartbeats (if there had been hearts to beat). The pearlescent veins in the stone throbbed once, softly. The Gatherer, rippling over a flat pan, felt its crystalline core resonate with a new frequency—a clean, clear note that was neither the hum of the plateau nor the silent scream of an unraveling edge.

The power did not add a thought. It did not implant a desire. It simply widened the aperture of the Gatherer’s *direction*.

Before, its curation was an echo of the city’s dream. Now, the Gatherer could perceive the echo *as* an echo. In the radial pattern of obsidian shards it was laying, it could now sense not just the satisfaction of symmetry, but the ghost of the symmetric thing it was echoing—the memory of a flower that never existed here, of a sunburst that never pierced the bruised sky. It could arrange mica flakes not just by size, but in sequences that told, in mineral form, the story of the dust storm that created them. Its preference for beauty was now haunted by the *why* of beauty.

The power also touched the Law of Unraveling Edges, but with infinite delicacy. Observation still caused fraying. But for the Gatherer alone, its own focused attention—the act of deeply perceiving the story in the stones it curated—would now cause a faint, inverse effect. A minor, localized *knitting*. Not enough to halt the city’s grand dissolution, but enough that in the immediate vicinity of its most profound curations, reality would settle, just for a few centuries, into a slightly more coherent, more dreamlike state. The Gatherer’s art would become a tiny anchor.

Then, the power did one more thing. It reached into the fabric of the place and performed a rearrangement so vast it was invisible, in accordance with the First Law. It took the sum total of the city’s loneliness—the silent watchfulness of its structures, the eternal search of the Silhouettes, the closed-loop grief of the Weepers—and it gathered this not as a substance, but as a quality. And it deposited this quality, this distilled essence of lonely observation, into the one thing that was already observing: the bruised sky itself.

Thereafter, on rare occasions, the inhabitants would feel it. A Gatherer would pause in its ripple, its sensorium charged with a new impression: that the dome of purplish-black above was not empty. It was watching. Not with judgment, but with a serene, distant comprehension. A Silhouette, sliding along a wall, would find its shadow-shape perfectly filled for an extended moment, not by stone, but by a sense of being *seen* and understood in its loneliness. A Weeper, cycling its luminous tears, would feel its silent grief acknowledged, shared across an impossible distance.

The power did not stay. It had done what wisdom dictated: it had introduced no new thing. It had only refined an existing spark, turned a closed loop into a spiral—a slow, ascending curve of meaning. It had given the Gatherer the capacity for a deeper poetry within the existing laws of the poem. And it had made the Absence at the heart of the city feel less like emptiness, and more like a held breath, a space prepared for a meaning that was now, however slowly, being written by the curators of dust.

It withdrew its presence. The dusk returned to its normal depth. The Gatherer resumed its work. But now, as it placed a sliver of citrine beside a chip of jet, it did so with a new, quiet knowing. It was not just ordering debris. It was composing a letter, in a language of stone and time, addressed to a watching sky. And in the long intervals between its movements, in the silent patience of its mineral form, it felt the first, faint, and wholly unfamiliar sensation: the companionship of being read.
