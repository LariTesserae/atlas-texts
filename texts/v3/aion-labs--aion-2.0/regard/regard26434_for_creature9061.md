The attention, when it came, was not a light. It was not a sound. It was a settling, a deepening of the existing silence that lived underneath the Rift’s roar. For a moment, the shrieks of the Fern-Howlers did not echo. The hiss of steam flattened into a sigh. The frantic bioluminescence of the fungi pulsed once, slowly, as if in a single, shared breath.

The power had no name the K’thak could click or chitter. If forced into a shape, it might have been a vast, slow eye, but one that saw not surfaces, but the deep currents of potential. It saw the Rift for what it was: a magnificent, violent cradle. It saw the K’thak, these clever, temporary things, dancing on a skin of cooling stone. Their existence was a beautiful, desperate improvisation with no refrain.

Benevolence, here, could not mean protection. To shield them from the Sunder-Maw’s jet or the collapse of a vent would be to make them soft, to divorce them from the negotiation that defined them. It would be a cruelty.

Wisdom, here, could not mean giving them fire or metal or the secret of the wheel. Such things were for worlds that had decided to be solid. Here, they would be anchors, dragging these ephemeral beings down into a mud of possession and territory. It would break the beautiful, desperate rhythm.

So the power did something very small, and very quiet.

In the heart of the basin, where three major steam vents formed a trembling triangle, the air thickened. It grew still and heavy, like water in a pool. The K’thak nearby felt it in the cilia on their skin—a drop in pressure, a cessation of the thermal chatter that was their constant weather. They scuttled into the ferns, chittering in low alarm, their skin flushing patterns of confusion and watchfulness.

From the nothingness between the plumes, it coalesced. Not an object, but a permission. A single, clear intention, placed gently into the fabric of the place.

The ground within the triangle softened. Not into mud, but into a substance like black glass, yet warm and yielding. From its center, a growth began. It was not a plant, not a fungus, not a mineral. It was a slow exhalation of crystal and solidified memory. It rose as a slender spire, no taller than a K’thak standing upright—a rare posture for them. It was obsidian-dark, but within its core, light moved. Not the frantic glow of bioluminescence, but a slow, deep pulse, like the heartbeat of the continent far below.

This was the Gift: the Stillness Spire.

It did not feed on steam or light. It simply was. It radiated a sphere of subtle influence, a circle perhaps ten body-lengths across. Within it, the chaos softened. The sound dampened to a murmur. The air lost its frantic heat, settling to a constant, mild warmth. Most profoundly, time within the circle… thickened. The frantic, day-long cycles of growth and decay outside slowed to a visible, observable rhythm. A Chitter-Swarm entering the edge of the sphere would seem to hang in the air, its frantic wingbeats becoming a visible, studyable vibration. A drop of condensation from a fern would trail a long, silver thread as it fell.

The Spire itself was a recorder. The slow pulse within its core was an echo of the Rift’s own changes. If a K’thak were to press a hand to its warm, smooth surface, they would not hear thoughts, but see-feel-know impressions: the pattern of the steam vents over the last lunar cycle; the path of a Sunder-Maw from a week ago; the location of a fruiting cluster of meat-flowers that had just begun to bloom yesterday. It offered context. It offered a glimpse of the pattern within the frenzy.

The power watched, its attention a patient weight.

The K’thak observed from the ferns for a full day-night cycle. They saw that the Sunder-Maw avoided the place. They saw that the Fern-Howlers, shrieking at the edge of the calm, would not enter. The Spire was not prey. It was not food. It was an anomaly.

Finally, a solitary K’thak, its skin a mosaic of caution-curiosity, scuttled forward. It stopped at the very edge where the sound dropped away. It tapped a long finger on the ground. The *click* was absurdly loud in the quiet. It flinched, then chittered softly to itself. It stepped in.

The feeling was alien. The constant pressure of being watched by a thousand hungry eyes… faded. Here, it was only watched by the Spire. The K’thak approached, its compound eyes reflecting the deep, slow pulse. Tentatively, it laid a broad, scaly hand on the Spire’s base.

Its body went rigid. In its mind, not as words, but as a cascade of sensory imprints, it saw: *the steam vent to the north-west will shift its plume eastward in two days. The Chitter-Swarm hatches at the hot springs follow the rise of the third-tier fungal glow. The Lurker-Frog by the amber pool has not fed in nine days.*

The K’thak snatched its hand back, chittering wildly. It scrambled out of the circle, back into the comforting, overwhelming noise and smell of the true Rift. It reported to its band in a frenzy of clicks and frantic light-pulses from its cilia.

The power observed. It had not given a command. It had not given a weapon. It had given a *mirror*—a mirror that showed time a little slower, and patterns a little clearer. The K’thak could ignore it. They could treat it as a curiosity, a warm, quiet place to rest occasionally, safe from most predators. Or they could learn to read the mirror. They could, perhaps, begin to anticipate the Rift’s shifts not just from immediate signs, but from deeper rhythms. They would not become masters. They would remain negotiators. But perhaps, just perhaps, their negotiation could gain a grace note of foresight.

The power saw the first band begin to incorporate the Spire. They did not worship it. They used it. A scout would touch it before a foraging run, then click a report on vent stability. A nursing mother might rest within its quiet circle, the slower time easing the frantic growth of her young. They began to leave offerings not out of reverence, but as one leaves a tool in good condition: a perfectly knapped obsidian flake at its base, a fresh, gleaming Steam-Spider thread wound around a lower protrusion.

The Spire would endure. While the Rift steamed and roared and reinvented itself around this pinpoint of stillness, the Spire would stand, recording, offering its quiet pulse. It would not make the K’thak eternal. It would not make them safe. But it might, in moments of overwhelming chaos, offer them a point of reference. A way to remember that even frenzy has a pattern.

Satisfied, the benevolent and wise power let its attention dissipate. It did not withdraw, for it had never truly arrived in a way that could leave. It simply stopped focusing. The Fern-Howlers’ shrieks resumed their full volume. The steam hissed with renewed vigor. The world was once again an event.

But in the heart of the basin, a new note had been added to the symphony—a deep, slow, steady pulse beneath the cacophony. A gift of stillness, in a world that might never know peace. A memory for those who lived only in the present.
