The visitation began not as an event, but as a change in quality.

First, the light altered. It did not brighten or dim, but the soft, cloud-diffused glow from the phosphorescent lichen gained a depth it had never possessed. For a timeless moment, the chamber’s twilight held within it the faintest suggestion of *color*—not the greens and ambers of life, but the cool, impossible violets and resonant golds of a sunset that exists only in the memory of stone. The air, always thick with the scent of peat and slow decay, carried for three breaths a note of ozone and cold, clean distance.

The Green Priests paused.

Across the vast, low space, a dozen attenuated forms went perfectly still. Their moss-face dishes drank in the new light; their frond-mouths tasted the alien note on the air. It was not a threat. The silent technology in the walls, the nano-repair crews in the water, detected no imbalance, no intrusion of foreign matter. Yet, something was *present*. A pressure, gentle as a hand laid upon a sleeping brow. A attention, vast and old and kind, focused upon their cathedral of green.

The Balancer of Pools, standing knee-deep in a mirror-still basin, felt the water beneath its feet grow warmer by a fraction of a degree, then cooler, in a rhythm that was not the chamber’s own geothermal heartbeat. It was a pulse, slow and deliberate, echoing something vaster. The Listener to the Deep Mycelium, plugged into the network in its fungal niche, felt a tremor of… curiosity. Not its own. A gentle query, transmitted through the roots of the world, asking after the chamber’s age, its happiness, its completeness.

The power did not speak. It observed. It admired. It saw the perfected equilibrium, the fierce peace of the tending, the beautiful, tragic triumph of a world that had turned entirely inward and made of its own closing circle an eternity. It saw the Priests, those final, loving thoughts, and it felt a tenderness that was neither pity nor desire for change. It was the tenderness of a gardener who, walking the paths of their own vast, wild garden, comes upon a perfect, self-contained terrarium, a closed ecosystem of stunning complexity and peace. The instinct is not to open it, but to honor it. To ensure its perfection is seen, and in being seen, connected, however faintly, to the whole.

Its action, when it came, was the lightest of touches.

At the chamber’s center, where the largest pillars rose into the misty gloom, the air itself began to *weave*. Not with matter, but with possibility. The water droplets hanging in the air, the spores, the motes of living dust, all aligned for a moment along invisible lines of force. And from this alignment, it precipitated.

A single seed.

It was smaller than a grain of the chamber’s sand, a fleck of obsidian wrapped in a film of silver light. It fell without a sound, descending in a slow spiral, until it landed on the spongy moss-floor at the exact geometric heart of the chamber. It did not radiate power. It did not vibrate with alien energy. It was simply a… potential. A closed loop of information written in crystalline DNA and quantum-held light.

The Green Priests converged. They glided from their pools and pillars and niches, their luminous veins pulsing with soft, urgent blue. They formed a silent circle around the seed. Their tendril-mouths quivered, analyzing. Their moss-faces drank in its non-reflective blackness. They extended their long, bark-skinned fingers, but did not touch. They communed.

Pheromone clouds bloomed above them, intricate, swirling dialogues of scent. The silent exchange was one of profound uncertainty, a sensation almost forgotten. *What is it?* The chamber’s rules were integration: nothing foreign lasts. But this was not foreign in the way of dead metal. It was *offered*. It carried the signature of the attention that had just pressed upon them—benevolent, wise, and sorrowfully admiring.

The Dreamer of New Configurations knelt. It was their role to interpret novel growth, to guide the chamber’s unconscious churn toward new, stable forms. It pressed its hands, palm-down, into the moss on either side of the seed. It plugged its frond-feet into the mycelial net. It opened its consciousness to the chamber’s dreaming mind.

And in that shared dream, the power spoke its only message. Not in words, but in a vision of impossible gentleness:

*The Chamber of Final Green is a lung breathing in a closed room. Outside the room, the house is vast, and filled with other lungs, other rhythms. The air is shared. You need not open the door. But here is a whisper of the house’s breath, for your wall. A memory of stars for your lichen. A silent friend for your eternity.*

The vision faded. The choice remained.

The Priests deliberated in their wordless way for days. The seed lay untouched. The chamber’s own processes began to react: a faint, exploratory tendril of corrosive mold crept toward it, then halted, sensing the benevolent intent woven into its structure. The cleaning crabs skirted it in wide arcs.

Finally, the Tuner of Light stepped forward. Its role was the chamber’s illumination, the quality of its perpetual twilight. It understood that this seed was, in its essence, a new kind of light. With a gesture of sublime acceptance, it brushed the seed with a single, glowing fingertip.

The seed dissolved. It did not vanish; it *bloomed* inward.

The effect was not dramatic, but it was pervasive. The silvery film around it spread, not as a substance, but as a pattern, a resonance. It traveled through the mycelial network, a gentle wave of coded information. It reached the lichen on the pillars.

Now, the soft, constant glow began, once every chamber-day (a rhythm measured by the imperceptible brightening and dimming of the light), to *pattern* itself. For a few moments, the lichen on one pillar would dim in specific spots, tracing the elegant, five-fold symmetry of a distant nebula. On another, the light would pulse in a rhythm that matched the orbital dance of a binary star system around a silent, black hole. The patterns were not bright; they were subtle, like memories surfacing in a deep, green dream.

In the pools, the new, gossamer-finned microfauna occasionally schooled in shapes that mirrored ancient constellations known only to cosmic gardeners. The air, once carrying only the scent of inward processes, now held, on rare occasions, the faint, clean taste of starlight on solar winds.

The Priests felt the change. When they plugged into the network for their deep communion, their dreams were now of two things. They still felt the slow tectonic pressures, the chemical balances, the joyous fungal blooms. But layered beneath that, like a bass note from a far-off instrument, they now felt the gentle pull of galactic tides, the slow, cool light of red giants, the quiet mathematics of orbits. They did not understand these things intellectually. They felt them as a tree might feel the moon—a distant, rhythmic pull that changes nothing of its essence, yet adds a layer of depth to its existence.

The power, its gift given and accepted, withdrew its attention. The pressure lifted. The strange light-colors faded from the air.

The Chamber of Final Green remained. It was still a lung of the world, breathing slow and deep. It was still low, wet, warm, and screaming with the quiet riot of life that had nowhere left to go but deeper into itself.

But now, etched into its eternal, dynamic equilibrium, was a faint, beautiful scar of connection. Its final form was still final. Its revolution was still permanent. Yet, in the soft, patterned pulse of its own light, it now whispered back to the vast, benevolent house in which it sat, a silent, green room among millions.

And the Mycorrhiza People, the caretakers, continued to tend. They guided the new patterns into the chamber’s growth, letting the star-memories influence the spread of a moss or the branching of a root in ways so subtle only they could perceive. They had not been changed. They had been… completed. The last, loving thought of a civilization now held, within its deep green meditation, a fragment of a loving thought from something even older, and wiser, and infinitely kind.
