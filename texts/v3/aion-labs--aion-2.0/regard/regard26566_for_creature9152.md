The attention arrived not as a thing, but as a condition of the air. It did not displace the roar; it made space for it. A quality of listening so profound it became a layer in the atmosphere, thinner than the highest hydrogen sac of the Sky-Rakes, more pervasive than the alkaline tang of stone.

The power—if it had a name, it was something like *Regard*—had turned its consciousness upon the Crown Plateau. It had felt, across the light-years, the rhythmic thump of the planetary heart, the shriek of growth, the slow, grinding poetry of consumption. It had sensed not life as it usually knew it—not the tender, water-based, sun-seeking kind—but life as architecture, as geochemistry, as percussive symphony.

It did not descend. To manifest a form here would be an absurdity. A body of light or flesh would be a speck against the Pillars, an insult to the scale. A voice would be lost in the cacophony. Instead, it *permeated*. It extended its awareness into the hammered silver sky, into the deep mineral veins, into the superheated vascular flows of the living engines.

It witnessed.

It felt the diamond rasp-teeth of the Basalt Grinder against stone, not as violence, but as a slow, devotional kiss. It traced the frantic, optimal paths of the Vapor-Jets, seeing in their swarming not panic, but a flawless dance of harvest. It rested its attention on the Pressure-Silencer, feeling the patient, dampened hum of its body, appreciating the lethal elegance of its imploding bite—a perfect solution to the problem of armored prey.

It listened to the roar. Not as noise, but as data. The thumps were metabolic rates. The hisses were pressure releases. The groans were structural integrity reports. It heard the entire plateau speaking the blunt, beautiful truth of its own existence.

And it found the system… complete. There was no pain here, only process. No fear, only stimulus and response etched into chitin and metallic cellulose. No longing, only the perpetual, roaring turnover of matter and energy. The Heart-Burrowers were not a tragedy; they were a regulatory mechanism, a population control for giants. The death of a Sky-Sovereign, its skeleton snagging on a spire, was not an end, but a nutrient transfer.

What does benevolence offer to a closed circle of perfect function?

Not grace. Not mercy. Not peace.

The power’s wisdom lay in knowing that its own values were irrelevant. To impose quiet would be to starve the Vapor-Jets. To soften the edges would blunt the Grinder’s teeth. To instill consciousness in these creatures would be a cosmic cruelty, making them aware of their own mechanistic fate within the engine.

So, it offered the only gift that would not disrupt the balance: **Sanctuary**.

With a thought as light as a photon’s pressure, it wove a veil around the star system. Not a wall, not a shield that would be visible or tangible to the planet’s inhabitants. It was a subtle tweak in probability, a gentle nudge in the paths of comets and asteroids, a quiet suggestion in the quantum foam that whispered *“Go around.”* It was a protection against the only thing that could truly harm this world: an external, mindless impact, or the arrival of a less wise intelligence—one that would see the Pillars as ore to be mined, the fauna as curiosities to be collected, the roaring equilibrium as something to be “fixed.”

The power also offered **Memory**. It encoded the full, sensorium of the Crown Plateau into its own eternal consciousness—the taste of ozone and hot metal, the vibration of the thumps in its non-existent bones, the sight of a Pressure-Silencer’s strike in perfect, silent efficiency amidst the bedlam. It would remember this place. It would hold the roaring, metallic song in a quiet corner of its being, a testament to life’s boundless, strange ingenuity.

Finally, it offered a single, infinitesimal **Adjustment**. Deep in the genetic substratum of the microbial Lithovores, in the spore-phase of the Heart-Burrowers, it introduced a latent, dormant potential. Not for intelligence, but for *resilience*. A slight increase in the adaptive rate, a broader tolerance for minor shifts in atmospheric chemistry. It was a gift for an epoch hence, should the star’s evolution ever slowly change the rules of the game. The system would have the tools to adapt on its own terms, in its own roaring, violent way.

Its task complete, the attention began to withdraw. But as it lifted, it performed one last act.

For a single, planet-wide moment, it filtered the perception of every creature. Not their reality—the roar continued, the grinding, the hissing, the hunting. But for that instant, the Basalt Grinder did not feel the resistance of stone as an obstacle, but as a fulfilling pressure. The Vapor-Jet did not experience frantic need, but the pure ecstasy of optimal flow. The Pressure-Silencer, in the moment of its kill, felt not hunger sated, but the profound satisfaction of a perfect equation solved.

It was not a gift of emotion. It was a gift of *clarity*. A glimpse, for each moving part, of its own flawless fit within the great machine. A taste of the whole, seen from the inside.

Then it was gone.

The hammered silver sky was just sky again. The roar was just noise. The Plateau continued, unwitnessed, in its majestic, deafening, eternal business. But it was, and would forever be, safe. And somewhere in the deep quiet between galaxies, a wise and benevolent power held within itself the memory of a world where life was not a garden, but a forge; not a chorus, but a crescendo; and where its only act of love was to ensure the fire never went out.
