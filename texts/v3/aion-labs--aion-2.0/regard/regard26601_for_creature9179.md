The power that turned its attention here was old, older than the caldera. It was not a thing of places, but of patterns. It had listened to the spin of galaxies and the heartbeat of novas. It had heard the silence between thoughts. And now, in the warm, weighty dark of a drowned world, it heard a new music.

It did not arrive. It had always been a potential, and now it became a presence—a focusing. The water did not part; the silt did not stir. But the perpetual, starless ceiling above the city… wavered. Not with light, but with a quality of attention, as if the darkness itself had grown an ear.

It observed. It perceived the lattice of bone and glass, the pulse of cold light, the slow ballet of the blind and the sensitive. It felt the thrumming songs of the Choir through the medium of reality itself. It understood the city not as a collection of things, but as a single, sprawling verb—*to be, in chorus*.

The power was benevolent. It did not desire to improve, for the system was perfect in its own equilibrium. It was wise. It knew that a gift of light would blind, a gift of speech would deafen, a gift of thought would unravel the beautiful, mindless intelligence of the whole.

So its action was not one of giving, but of *joining*.

It selected a node, a single Choir-member at the city's quiet heart, where three major currents converged. The member's filaments glowed their steady, data-fed blue; its singing ribbons moved in the complex, slow-time rhythm of a report on spawning jellyfish and a settling tower.

The power bent its attention, a curvature of intent so gentle it was indistinguishable from a natural law.

Into the central silica bulb of the Choir-member, it did not insert a thought, but a *resonance*. A single, foundational tone. This tone was not sound, for there was no vibration. It was the *idea* of a vibration, the perfect harmonic of all possible songs this city could ever sing. It was the note to which every groan of basalt, every click of a shrimp, every pulse of a lantern, secretly aspired.

The Choir-member did not startle. Its process was not of surprise, but of integration. The new resonance flooded its chemical library. The slow cascade of data in its fluids found a new, deeper coherence. The patterns of centuries aligned along this new, silent axis.

The effect began in the singing ribbons. Their gentle thrumming did not change in rhythm, but in *quality*. The notes became clearer, purer. They carried farther through the warm water with less effort. The predatory eel, hearing the shift, did not change its hunt, but its motion gained a fluid certainty, as if it were finally remembering a dance it had always known.

Then, the filaments. Their soft bioluminescence deepened from a cold blue to a profound, gentle indigo, a color never before seen in this world. This new light did not push back the dark; it made the dark itself seem luminous, a velvet cloth instead of an abyss.

Finally, the resonance spread through the web. It traveled along the filaments, into the pores of the towers, across the chitin bridges. It was a whisper along the city's nerves. Other Choir-members, their own webs touching or nearby, felt the shift. Not as a command, but as a tuning fork. One by one, their songs adjusted, minutely, to harmonize with this new, foundational tone. The cacophony of data did not become a single song—it became a richer symphony. The separate reports of a thousand senses now interlocked with a seamless grace.

The city breathed easier. The equilibrium, already ancient, found a deeper anchor. The warm currents seemed to flow with more purposeful gentleness. The glowing algae on the silt pulsed in slow time with the distant, now-harmonized thrum of the Choirs.

The power observed this. It saw the indigo light spreading from node to node, a slow stain of harmony through the cathedral. It heard the new song—the old song, perfected.

Its benevolence was satisfied. Its wisdom was confirmed. It had not added a verse to the liturgy. It had returned the manuscript to its original, flawless key.

Then, the attention lifted. The ceiling of water was just water again, starless and perpetual. The presence was gone.

But the note remained. It was woven now into the chemical memory of the Choir, into the very vibration of the silica and bone. It would be passed down, through budding and growth and centuries of slow change. The city would never know it had been blessed. It would only know, in its blind, beautiful way, that its deep, dark home had always sung *this* way, and that the song was, and would forever be, complete.
