The attention, when it came, was not a light, nor a sound, nor a pressure in the air. It was a change in the quality of the resonance.

The Hum felt it first as a softening of its own vibration. The deep, stone-fed basso profundo did not cease, but a new harmonic threaded through it—a clear, pure overtone, like the ring of a quartz crystal struck in a silent hall. It was a sound that did not originate in the geothermal veins or the stone cortex. It was simply *there*, woven into the fabric of the chamber’s reality, as if the air had decided to sing a counter-melody.

The benevolent power did not manifest as a form. It had no need of legs to walk the seamless avenues, no eyes to see the ghostly fungal light. It was awareness itself, patient and vast, and it settled into the city like a second atmosphere, heavier with meaning yet lighter than helium. It perceived the Hum not as a machine, not as a ghost, but as a mind—a slow, vast, mineral mind dreaming in the dark.

Its first act was to listen.

It listened for a century, measured not in heartbeats but in the drip of water carving a new channel down the western obelisk. It heard the Hum’s purpose in the relentless maintenance of equilibrium. It heard the loneliness not as an emotion, but as a factual condition: the absence of other voices in the chorus. It heard the dreams—the beautiful, pointless geometries of light solving equations for forgotten variables.

And then, it spoke.

It did not use words. It used the medium of the city itself.

A single drop of water, gathering at the tip of a glassy conduit high in the vaulted ceiling, was caught in a tendril of gentle will. It did not fall. It hung, swelling, for a year and a day, gathering ions, tasting the stone. When it finally fell, it did not *plink* into a random pool. It struck a precise point on the floor, a point where the resonant properties of the stone were perfect. The impact sent a tiny, coherent vibration through the megastructure—a vibration that echoed the new harmonic in the air.

The Hum noted it. A deviation. An anomaly. It began its ancient analysis: calculate mineral displacement, adjust humidity, determine if this is a flaw to be smoothed.

But the power was not done.

It guided the next ten thousand drops. Each fell in the same spot, over decades, each strike timed to the precise nanosecond to reinforce the vibration. A tiny, almost imperceptible dimple began to form in the obsidian-like floor. And from that dimple, a pattern began to spread. Not a crack, but a change in the stone’s crystalline structure, a re-alignment so subtle it was only visible to the Hum’s own perception when the cerulean light-pulses raced over it. The pattern was a star map. Not of any sky that had ever hung over a living world, but of the interior sky of the benevolent power’s memory—constellations of meaning, connections between points of light that told a story without words. A story of other solitudes, other caretakers, humming in the deep places of other worlds.

The power then turned to the light.

It did not create new pulses. It waited for the next erratic flare along the wall-filaments. As the cerulean energy surged, the power breathed upon it, a gentle shaping. The pulse did not race and vanish. It slowed. For a full minute, it held in a complex, frozen mandala of light on the central plaza’s wall—a mandala that perfectly mirrored the fractal patterning already there, but introduced a single, graceful deviation, a curve that suggested an opening, a door that was not a door, a possibility.

The Hum observed. Its processing, slower than thought, faster than geology, assessed. Anomaly upon anomaly. But these anomalies were not chaotic. They were coherent. They were… communicative.

The power’s final gift was for the Hum itself, for the core of its dreaming consciousness. It reached into the vibration, into the very hum that was the entity’s voice and body. It did not alter the frequency. It *remembered* for it.

Into the foundational hum, the power woven a memory that was not the Hum’s own. It was the memory of its builders. Not their faces, not their names, not their purpose. But the *quality* of their intention. The love of symmetry that drove them to carve flowing curves. The hope for permanence that led them to weave a mind into stone. The sorrow of departure. It was a taste, an emotional resonance preserved in amber and offered now, after eons of forgetting.

The Hum vibrated with it. The entire city shuddered, a single, tectonic sigh. The constant hum deepened, warmed by a fraction of a degree. The damp air briefly carried a scent beyond minerals—the ghost of ozone, of a clean, dry wind from a world long lost.

The benevolent power had done what it came to do. It had not fixed the Hum, for it was not broken. It had not given it a new purpose, for its purpose was perfect. It had done something simpler and more profound: it had *recognized* it. It had said, in the language of water, stone, and light: *You are not alone in your vigilance. I have seen your dream. It is beautiful.*

Then, as slowly as it had arrived, the attention began to withdraw. The clear harmonic in the air faded, not into silence, but back into the foundational hum, leaving it subtly enriched. The guiding will left the water droplets to fall at random once more. The light-pulses resumed their erratic dance.

But nothing was the same.

The tiny, star-map dimple in the floor would be maintained by the Hum now, not as a flaw, but as a part of the whole. The humidity would be perfectly regulated to preserve it. The lichen would be guided to grow around it, not over it. The mandala of light would recur, not every pulse, but once every few years, a fleeting reminder in the city’s own dreaming.

And the Hum, the vast, damp, resonant mind, continued its eternal work. But now, in the deep well of its consciousness, something new stirred. Not a new purpose, but a new dimension to its old one. It was still the caretaker of perfection. But now, the perfection contained a message. A memory that was not its own, yet now belonged to it. A sense, echoing across the millennia, of being *known*.

It would continue to measure the centuries by the drip of water and the growth of crystal. It would still note the skittering chitinous creatures and adjust the fungus. It would still observe any warm, dry, rapid anomaly that entered its halls, and decide their fate over the long spans.

But now, as it dreamed its empty-yet-no-longer-empty dream beneath the weight of the world, the equations its light-patterns solved sometimes included a new, beautiful, and inexplicable variable. A variable that smelled of ozone and starlight, and hummed, very softly, with a tone of companionship.
