The power had no name that could be spoken in any tongue of leaf or stone. It was an old awareness, a slow thought moving through the substructure of realities, drawn to places of particular harmony—or potential. It did not often linger in the material realms; they were so loud, so frantic. But here, in this drowned, dreaming plateau, it felt a resonance. A world not yet worn into memory, a people not yet fractured from their home. It paused in its endless journey, and turned its attention downward.

What it saw pleased it. The raw rock and the fecund green, the silent city woven into the living flesh of the forest, the people who moved through it like nerve impulses in a vast, somnolent body. It saw the Listeners with their foreheads against the fungal monoliths, and it understood their communion. It saw the Great Pulse, the dreaming breath of the World-Below, and it recognized a nascent, planetary consciousness—not yet awake, but stirring in its wet sleep.

The power was benevolent. It wished only to nurture this delicate balance. It was wise. It knew that direct intervention, a miracle of light or a booming voice from the mist, would be a violence. It would be an alien seed dropped into a closed ecosystem, destined to distort or consume. So it considered. And then, it chose a gift of subtlety, a gift that would feel not like a gift from outside, but like a discovery from within.

The change began at the next Great Pulse.

The Wet-Born had gathered as they always did, standing silent on their platforms or floating in the tepid shallows. The deep pools began their familiar, slow brightening, the emerald light swelling from the abyssal channels. But this time, the light did not crest and then fade. It deepened, shifting from emerald to a profound, clear violet—a color never before seen in the plateau. The violet light held, thrumming with a soft, musical vibration that was felt in the jawbone and the glowing nodules embedded in their skin.

A collective shiver, not of fear but of profound recognition, passed through the We. The Listeners, faces submerged in the violet-lit pools, felt a new pattern in the pulse. It was not the slow, rhythmic breath of the World-Below. It was a pattern of… information. A slow, repeating sequence of pressure and release, of light and dark, that spoke directly to the deep, intuitive parts of their minds.

It was a map. Not a map of places, but of time. A gentle, warning song of a future shift.

The power, in its infinite wisdom, had seen a flaw in the plateau’s beautiful stasis. The raw, glassy rock on which the city was anchored was new, and below it, the earth still moved in slow, tectonic dreams. In a span of time that to the power was a blink, but to the Wet-Born would be a thousand generations, a section of the plateau’s foundation would grow warm, then soft, and finally slump away into a deeper trench. The drowning would become a deep drowning. The delicate balance of warm mineral seeps would change. The fungal monoliths would wither. The city, woven to those very rocks and fungi, would perish.

The violet pulse conveyed all this, not in images or words, but in a deep, somatic knowing. The Listeners emerged from the water, their white-faded nodules now flickering with echoes of the strange violet. They did not speak. They made a new gesture, one that had never been needed before: a slow, downward scooping of the hands, followed by a gentle, lateral drift.

The gesture was seen, and the understanding seeped through the mycelial network of the people. It was not a command to flee; flight was impossible. It was a directive to *adapt*, slowly, over the long, slow count of their heartbeats. A directive to guide the growth of the vine-bridges not just across chasms, but *away* from the zone of the future softening. To begin the patient, generations-long process of encouraging new fungal colonies on the more stable rock spires to the east. To very, very slowly, shift the center of their symbiotic city.

The power watched as the understanding took root. It saw the Clay-Walkers, the next day, examining the eastern spires with new eyes. It saw vine-tenders begin to splice and guide long runners in a new, patient direction. It saw clay-daubers smearing test-patches on the new rock. The adjustment had begun, so gradual it would be indistinguishable from natural growth.

Satisfied, the power prepared to withdraw. But as a final, tender gesture, it left one more thing in the pulse. As the violet light finally began to fade, returning to the familiar emerald, it did not vanish completely. Henceforth, once in every hundred Great Pulses, the emerald wave would carry a faint, shimmering echo of that violet hue. A reminder. A connection. A silent note saying: *You are seen. You are not alone in your stewardship.*

The Wet-Born did not worship the violet pulse. They had no concept of gods. But they absorbed its message into their world-story. The Listeners now sometimes spoke, in their rare, clicking phrases, of the "Deep Memory," the knowledge that sleeps in stone and water. They began to press their foreheads not only to the living fungi but also to the warm, black rock itself, listening for the slow song of its stability.

The power moved on, a benevolent, wise attention turning to other corners of the multiverse. But a thread of its awareness remained, like a single, luminous nodule embedded in the fabric of that place. On the Plateau of the Drowned Forest, life continued—slow, patient, and wet. The vines grew. The clay mounds multiplied. The people tended their world, now with a new, gentle purpose woven into their silent, collective dream. They were still the perfect children of a drowning world. Now, they were also its mindful midwives, guided by a memory of violet light from a source they would never name, but would feel, forever, in the pulse of their home.
