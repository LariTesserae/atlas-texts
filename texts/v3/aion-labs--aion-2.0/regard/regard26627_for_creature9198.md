The power that turns its attention here does not have a name that could be spoken by a mouth of flesh and moss. It is older than these young mountains, wiser than the slow, fungal thoughts that pulse through the violet root-networks. Its attention is not a beam of light, but a settling, like a change in atmospheric pressure felt only in the soul of a place.

It arrives not as an apparition, but as a shift in the dance.

First, the light softens. Not by clouds—the sky remains that painful, perfect blue—but as if the air itself thickens just enough to diffuse the sun’s edge. The harsh contrasts melt into gradients. Shadows, for the first time, appear: deep, velvety pools beneath overhangs, subtle shading on the moss-beds. The Still-Dancers pause in their silent work. They do not look up, for they have no concept of ‘up’ as a source of agency. Instead, they feel it through their feet, through the mycelial web. A new rhythm, vast and slow, enters the substrate of the cirque. It is the heartbeat of a continent, a tidal pull of deep time.

The power observes. It sees the delicate, brutal economy of the place: the silent hunt, the guided water, the moss that is both self and home. It sees the ancient soot stains, the relic of a forgotten fear of fire. It sees the potential fragility—a prolonged drought, a rockslide that could sever a major root-node, the slow leaching of a particular mineral from the soil. The system is robust, but closed. It has no recourse but adaptation or extinction.

The wise power does not intervene to prevent these possibilities. That would be to insult the dance, to deny the struggle that gives it meaning. Instead, it offers a tool. Not a tool of metal or fire, but of memory and resonance.

In the center of the largest clearing—a stage of dark, wet stone usually used for mud-tracing—the air begins to *sing*. It is not a sound the ears can hear. It is a vibration that travels through the rock, up through the soles of the Still-Dancers, into the violet moss on their chests. They gather, flowing in from their gardens and hunting runs, until a ring of them stands at the edge of the stone. Their obsidian eyes are fixed on the center.

Where the arrhythmic drips usually fall, a new water feature is born. From the stone itself, a seep-spring wells up, not in a trickle, but in a perfect, domed lens of water. It holds its shape, pulsing gently in time with the new, deep rhythm. Within this lens, images flow. They are not pictures of things, but of *processes*. The dancers see the cirque from a vantage point miles above, watching the mist-rivers flow. They see a map of their own root-network, glowing violet like a nervous system in the rock. They see a possible future: a dry season where the mists fail, and a new, deeper aquifer is revealed by the cracking of a specific basalt layer. They see another: a landslide that buries a moss-bed, and the swift, rerouting growth of the network around it, like light finding a new path through a prism.

The lens shows them not what *will* be, but what *could* be. It is a mirror of potentialities, fed by the wisdom of the power and the inherent knowledge of the cirque itself. It shows them their own history: the first spark of consciousness in the moss, the clumsy, early dancers who feared fire, the long, silent evolution into harmony.

Then, it shows them something else. A slow, soft rain of golden pollen, drifting from a clear sky. It dusts the moss-beds. In the lens, they see this pollen take root, not as a foreign invader, but as a catalyst. Where it falls, the moss deepens its color; the lichens grow more resilient; the furred prey animals develop a richer fat layer. It is a gift of resilience, a slight tilting of the odds in their favor, a gentle enrichment of their closed world.

The power’s work is done. The deep, rhythmic vibration fades. The lens of water loses its clarity, softens, and becomes a simple, beautiful spring, bubbling gently over the stone, its water slightly warmer than the surrounding air, and infused with a faint, sweet mineral scent.

The Still-Dancers do not cheer or prostrate themselves. Their understanding is not of gratitude, but of integration. One, its violet patches pulsing in a complex new pattern, steps forward. It kneels by the new spring, dips a hand in, and brings the water to its seam-like mouth. It drinks. Then, it turns and places its wet hand on the moss-covered shoulder of another. A gesture. A transfer. A sharing of the knowledge, the potential, the gift.

Across the cirque, the birds with four wings wheel in a sudden, synchronized spiral. The diamond-dust ice falls in a brief, glittering shower that melts into the moss, and where it melts, tiny, golden specks are left behind, like promises.

The benevolent power withdraws its focused attention. Its gift is given. Not a solution, but a lens. Not a shield, but a deeper understanding of the weave. The Cirque of Stillness is still itself—silent, sharp, lush, empty, dynamic. But now, in its heart, a new spring flows. And in the fungal mind of the place, a new song, vast and slow and full of possible tomorrows, has been added to the dance.
