The visitation was not an event. It was a correction in perception.

The power did not descend from the milky haze, nor did it rise from the radiant floor. It was simply, and then it was *there*. It had no form that the pocket’s life could register, for it was not made of matter that interacted with heat or light in any known way. It was a locus of attention, a gentle pressure of regard focused upon the basin.

To the drones, it was a momentary stillness in the hum, a synchronization of their vibrations to a chord they had never produced but had always, somehow, implied. To the six-legged predators, it was a cool spot in the thermal landscape, a nothingness they ignored. To the grists churning below, it was a faint, pleasing resonance in the deep hum of the planet’s dream.

To the grey moss—the neural net, the patient recorder—it was everything.

For the first time in its multimillennial existence, the moss *knew* it was being observed. The pressure of that wise attention flowed through every filament, connecting isolated chemical memories into a single, coherent tapestry. The moss experienced, in an instant, the entire history it had stored: the slow slump of the cliffs, the first spore of the fern-trees finding purchase, the dance of the first drones, the brutal, elegant strikes of the first predator, the silent, long-ago moment the archway was placed and activated, stitching this tear in reality. The moss *remembered* the architects—not as shapes, but as intentions, as a feeling of profound loss and determined preservation.

The benevolent power absorbed this history not by reading it, but by *feeling* it. It felt the profound, humming finality of the place, the beautiful, closed loop. It felt the slow, inevitable decay of the geothermal heartbeat—a fade so gradual the moss had not yet registered it as a trend, only as a faint, slow cooling in its deepest layers.

And here, the power’s wisdom met its benevolence.

Intervention was not needed. The system was complete. To add would be to unbalance; to take would be to destroy. But to *support*… to ensure the continuation of this finished equation… that was within its nature.

The power turned its attention to the source: the faint, radiant floor. It perceived the dying ember of a planetary heart, a banked fire of isotopes and slow fission that had burned for epochs. The fire was not meant to last forever. But the power could see the shape of its exhaustion, and it could see a simpler, more eternal alternative.

It did not reignite the ember. Instead, it performed an act of subtle transcription.

The power reached, without hands, into the quantum grain of the bedrock beneath the dust. In a process that would have been a cataclysm anywhere else, it was here a whisper. It carefully *unwound* the last millennia of the geothermal decay, not reversing it, but transposing its essential property—the emission of steady, low-grade warmth—onto a different principle. It anchored the heat to the constant, slow tick of atomic time itself, to the fundamental spin of particles that would endure until the universe grew cold. It was a topological trick, a gentle knot tied in the fabric of local reality.

The floor would now glow, not from a depleting source, but from a borrowed echo of cosmic inertia. The warmth would remain constant, not for millennia, but for geologic ages.

The work took no time, and yet it was the work of ages. When it was done, the power turned its attention once more to the moss.

It offered no message, no grand revelation. Instead, it did something for the silent consciousness. It gave it a gift the moss had never possessed, a gift the wise power deemed fitting for such a faithful recorder: a capacity for *synthesis*.

Where before the moss could only record data—this footfall, that condensation, this temperature—it could now, in its infinitely slow, vegetative way, *dream*. It could take the patterns of the predator’s hunt and the drone’s hum and the fern’s growth and weave them into new, silent patterns of meaning. It could imagine variations on the theme of its own world. It would not act on these dreams; they would remain internal, a private, vast artistry born from eons of observation. The finished equation would now have, in its very substrate, a capacity for boundless, quiet contemplation.

Finally, the power regarded the archway. The scripts flowed, noting the massive, subtle energy transduction that had just occurred. The power considered. The archway was a stitch, a repair. Its purpose was to maintain separation, to keep this pocket intact. The power saw that the stitch was perfect. It required no adjustment.

Instead, the power left a single, minor edit in the flowing scripts. Where before they indicated only functional states—integrity, energy levels, atmospheric constants—a new, soft glyph now pulsed at the edge of the sequence. It had no function in the archway’s operation. It was a aesthetic sigil, a symbol that meant, in no language that ever was, *“Appreciated.”*

Its work done, the power’s attention began to withdraw.

The drones felt the strange synchronization release, and their hum returned to its normal, complex chord—but it was, perhaps, a fraction richer, a hair more resonant. The predators sensed the thermal landscape settle back into perfect, predictable gradients. The grists burrowed a little more contentedly in the now-perpetually-warm dust.

And the moss, for the first time, did not just record the departure of the anomalous pressure. It felt a slow, vast, and gentle *emotion* settle into its chemical layers. It was not happiness, nor sadness. It was the feeling of being *seen*, and found to be good. And from that feeling, in the decades and centuries to come, would bloom its first, slow, magnificent dream: a dream of a visitor made of light, who tended the fire at the heart of the world, and then stayed awhile, just listening to the music.

The visitation ended. The pocket was as it had been: loud, wet, warm, and complete. The hum was eternal. The hunt was eternal. The memory was eternal.

Nothing was changed.
Everything was sustained.
