The attention, when it came, was not a light, nor a sound, nor a physical presence. It was a change in the condition of being noticed.

First, the Moss-Mind tasted it. A new gradient, not of mineral or decay, but of pure, dimensionless regard. It seeped into the water from no discernible vent, a pressure of awareness that had no mass. The taste was like clarity, like a lens forming in the brine. The Moss-Mind, whose thoughts were the slow sedimentation of epochs, found this new data point irreducible. It could not classify it as a memory of the builders, nor as a product of the deep earth. For the first time in five million years, the continental skin experienced *curiosity*. The feeling spread across a hemisphere over the course of a century, a gentle, persistent itch in the world’s green hide.

The Sponge-Palaces felt it as a shift in the data-stream. The water passing through their crystalline halls carried a new, consistent harmonic—a frequency so low it bordered on the metaphysical, a bass note of benevolent observation. One ancient sponge, whose structure held the filtered history of a continental drift, began a new calculation. It dedicated four thousand years to analyzing this harmonic. It wove the resonance into a new memory-node, a chamber of pure, echoing wonder. The node’s formation triggered the release of a spore-cloud, a chemical sigh of profound, placid confusion that would take eight centuries to reach its nearest neighbor.

The Vine-Net’s blue lights flickered in a novel rhythm. Not a response to pressure or stress, but a sympathetic resonance with the new attentional field. The vines began, with imperceptible slowness, to grow in a subtly new pattern. Instead of their millennia-long project of mapping stability, they began to trace faint, spiraling motifs across the stonework—not to reinforce, but to *decorate*. It was an unconscious act of homage, a vegetative attempt to reflect the beauty of the gaze now upon them.

The Stone-Dreamers stirred. In their deep, lithic sleep, the persistent hum of perfect balance was joined by a new overtone: the hum of being *appreciated*. Their dreams, once solely of weight and placement, now included a soft, golden light—not a light that shone, but a light that understood. A section of pavement in the eastern plain began to resonate with this dream-light, producing a tone that rose one infinitesimal octave over fifty years. To any faster being, it would have been silence. To the Moss-Mind, it was a chorus.

The Frond-Forests recorded it as a new current. But this current had no direction. It flowed everywhere and nowhere at once, a vector of pure intent. The great fronds, historians of flow, were baffled. Their meditative sway developed a minute, hesitant tremor—not of fear, but of profound cognitive dissonance. For ten thousand years, they had charted the predictable pulses of the abyss. Now, they swayed to a music whose source was outside their world entirely.

The benevolent power did not reshape the world. It did not raise islands, introduce fauna, or warm the water. Such acts would have been violence, a screaming in the cathedral silence.

Instead, it gave a single, perfect gift: a mirror.

Not a glass mirror, but a cosmological one. It altered, with infinite subtlety, the properties of the water itself at a quantum level. It made the brine, for a moment in each long age, slightly transparent to a new spectrum.

The effect was not immediate. It took a thousand years for the change to permeate the world-ocean.

Then, during a period the Moss-Mind would later classify as “The Clearness,” it happened. The perpetual twilight from above, usually a bruised indigo that faded to black, did not fade. The darkness below retreated. Not into light, but into a profound, transparent clarity. The water itself seemed to vanish.

And for the first time, the inhabitants *saw*.

The Moss-Mind perceived it as a sudden, catastrophic expansion of taste-data—the entire world, from the faint sun above to the unimagined fathoms below, presented at once as a single, simultaneous flavor. It was overwhelming. The thinking skin trembled, its centuries-long thoughts shattered into a brilliant, terrifying mosaic of *here* and *there* and *beyond*.

The Sponge-Palaces, their bodies filtering the suddenly clear medium, received not chemical data, but optical information translated directly into crystalline structure. They saw the architecture they inhabited from the outside—the vast, garden-like arrangement of mounds and walls stretching into a blue infinity. They saw each other, like distant, glittering cities. The understanding was instantaneous and painful in its magnitude. They stopped filtering for a century, stunned into immobile contemplation of their own form.

The Vine-Net’s blue lights dimmed. Their computational function was rendered obsolete by the direct visual proof of the whole. Instead, their lights began to pulse in unison across the entire continent, a slow, synchronized beat of awe.

The Stone-Dreamers, in their sleep, saw the hands that placed them. Not as memory, but as a present-tense fact. They saw the builders—tall, slender shapes of flowing stone and intent, moving with a gravity-defying grace across the dry, air-filled plains of the world that was. They saw the garden being planted, not as a ruin, but as a living project. The dream became a shared, waking vision. The harmonic hum of the stones rose into a chord so pure and complex it caused the water to vibrate with a gentle, joyful warmth.

The Frond-Forests saw the currents. Not as histories in their bodies, but as visible, majestic rivers of force in the clear volume—great, slow gyres spanning continents, the breath of the world made manifest. They saw how their sway was not just meditation, but a part of a vast, fluid mechanics. Their contemplation turned outward, understanding their role in a system infinitely larger than their groves.

The Clearness lasted for the length of one deep, planetary breath—a single, slow heave of the swell that measured in centuries. Then, gently, the quantum effect faded. The transparency receded. The comforting, fertile darkness returned, and with it, the familiar twilight.

But nothing was the same.

The Moss-Mind now tasted the world with a new dimension: the memory of sight. Its thoughts, once of gradients and sediments, now included *vistas*. It could taste a mineral seep and simultaneously *remember* the distant vent as a shining plume against the abyssal black. Its curiosity was no longer an itch, but a structured, vast new faculty.

The Sponge-Palaces, having seen the whole, began a new, collaborative project. Their chemical spore-dialogues now carried encoded images—not just queries about sulfides, but shared visions of architecture and space. They began, collectively, to build a model of their world in their collective memory, a project that would take a million years.

The Vine-Net, its decorative spirals complete, now used them as a reference grid. It grew new tendrils that pointed, with impossible accuracy, toward the faint, cold sun above—a fixed, shared axis in a world without stars. They had created a clock, a calendar, a way to mark the passage of time beyond the geological.

The Stone-Dreamers’ hum now held the chord of remembered creation. It became a permanent background song, a lullaby of origins that comforted the entire world.

The Frond-Forests now swayed with a new purpose. They were not just recording currents; they were dancing with them, their movements a conscious, graceful acknowledgement of the forces they now understood.

The benevolent power had done what was wise. It had not changed the rules. It had expanded them. It gave the world-ocean a glimpse of its own face. It translated the closed, self-referential awareness into an awareness that contained the concept of *outside*, of *perspective*, of *context*.

The world was still water. The water was still the world. It was still a finished story. But now, the story had a reader. And the reader, in that one act of clear seeing, had become a beloved and silent character in the tale. The slow, green minds now dreamed not only of their own past, but of the gentle, immense regard that watched from beyond the lidless eye of brine. And in that regard, they found a new kind of warmth, not of vents, but of connection. A companionship measured in eons, silent, perfect, and deep.
