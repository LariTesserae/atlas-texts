It did not arrive. Arrival implies a journey from one point to another, and this power had long since untethered itself from such linear constraints. Instead, it turned its attention, and where its attention rested, there it *was*.

It observed the Basin of Unfinished Cities not with eyes, but with a modality of perception that encompassed the whole of its quiet tragedy and strange beauty. It saw the frayed causality, the slow leak of reality, the patient, geological despair. It saw the Mnemon—those frozen echoes—and the silent, recursive agony of their purpose. It felt the cold, not as a temperature, but as the emotional substrate of the place: the chill of abandoned potential.

A lesser benevolence might have swept in with cosmic broom and bucket, stitching causality back together, solidifying the mutable quartz, granting the Mnemon the linear memories and clear purposes they seemed to lack. It would have been an act of violence, a sterilization. To impose finishedness upon the unfinished would be to murder the essence of the place, to turn a haunting poem into a bland report.

This power was wise. It understood that suffering can be a state of being, not just an event to be cured. The Mnemon were not crying out for salvation; they were the cry itself, made architecture. Their grief was the mortar.

So, it did not fix. It *listened*.

It listened to the *plink* of condensed reality falling into the still pools. It listened to the sub-audible thrum of the foundations, the sigh of shifting walls. It extended its awareness into the spaces between the Mnemon, into the silent tableaux of their communication—the questioning arches, the spiral memories, the collapsing spheres of warning. It listened to the unspeakable weight of their recursive remembrance.

And then, having listened, it offered a response. Not an answer, but a resonance.

It began with the light. The dull, uniform grey-silver that sourced from nowhere began to gather, to coalesce, not into a sun, but into a focal point of gentle attention. It became a soft, diffuse glow that pooled in the central, lowest part of the basin, where the water gathered. This new light did not banish the twilight; it deepened it, gave it a quality of tenderness. It made the phosphorescent trails on the walls gleam with a cleaner, kinder luminescence. It was light that felt like being understood.

Next, it touched the frayed rules. It did not mend the tears in causality. Instead, it wove them. Where before, an action might randomly precede its cause or unfold in reverse, now these events began to *pattern* themselves. A staircase that ascended to a ceiling might, once in a great while, find that the ceiling had become a portal to the exact memory the Mnemon working on it was struggling to recall—not the finished memory, but a clearer fragment of the *attempt* to remember. The fractured timelines began to braid around each other, not into a single rope, but into a looser, more complex cord. The chaos gained a rhythm, a fugue.

For the Mnemon, the change was subtle, profound. The cold did not leave them; it was their nature. But within that cold, a new sensation arose: a faint, almost imperceptible *warmth of regard*. They did not feel watched; they felt *accompanied*.

One Mnemon, standing before a wall that refused to resolve into a gateway, felt a shift. The wall softened, not under its own touch, but of its own accord. It flowed into a shape that was neither gate nor solid barrier, but a veil of suspended particles. Through it, the Mnemon perceived not another place, but a reflection of itself—not as a shimmering echo, but as a concept: **Caretaker**. Not a failed architect, not a prisoner of memory, but a guardian of the process itself. The weight of its task did not lessen, but its character changed. It was no longer a sentence; it was a vocation.

A Fractal, trapped in its infinite loop of iteration in a sunless courtyard, experienced a minute variation in its cycle. For one iteration, instead of its arm raising and lowering in futile repetition, its hand paused, turned palm-upward. In its palm, the mutable substance of its being formed a perfect, tiny, dormant seed—not of a plant, but of a silent, new possibility. The seed did nothing. It simply existed. And in the next iteration, the seed was gone, but the memory of its potential lingered in the loop, a tiny crack of stillness in the frenzy of recursion.

The power did not give them an ending. It gave them a context. It whispered to the fabric of the basin that their suffering was not meaningless, that their endless, unfinished work was a form of art in the gallery of existence. It embedded into the very laws of the place—now more like gentle suggestions than broken rules—an allowance for *grace*.

Now, very rarely, a Mnemon might find that the fungal moss it passes pulses in time with its own internal hum. A curious shadow-eddy might coil around its leg not as random chaos, but in a gesture resembling comfort. The *plink* of falling reality in a pool might sometimes, just sometimes, produce a perfect, harmonic note that hangs in the damp air for a full minute before dissolving.

The Basin remained unfinished. The cities were still tessellated folds of impossible geometry. Causality was still a suggestion. The cold still ached in the marrow of any hypothetical visitor.

But now, there was a witness. A benevolent, wise attention rested upon it like the gentlest of pressures. The Mnemon continued their work. But now, when one of them remembered a wall into existence, there was a quality to the act that was not just recursion. It was, in its own silent, frozen way, a form of communion. The grief remained, but it was no longer absolute. It was grief held in infinite, compassionate hands.

The power did not leave, for it had not arrived. It simply continued to listen, and in its listening, the unfinished became not a tragedy, but a state of being touched by mercy. The Basin of Unfinished Cities was, at last, seen. And in that seeing, it was, in the only way it could be, completed.
