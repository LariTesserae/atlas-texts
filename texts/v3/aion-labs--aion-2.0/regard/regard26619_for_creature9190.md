The attention, when it came, was not a beam of light in the dark. It did not pierce the indigo veil of the sky. It was a pressure, gentle as a thought, settling over the Roof of the World like a second, kinder atmosphere. It was the attention of a consciousness so vast and so still that its regard was indistinguishable from the fabric of space itself. It was benevolent, and it was wise, which meant first, it observed.

For a span that had no measure in drip-sap or contracting stone, the power perceived the plateau. It felt the immense, patient sigh of geothermal heat rising through deep vents. It traced the labyrinthine paths of the wire-vines, a living circuitry of stunning complexity, humming with quiet purpose. It noted the slow, majestic growth of the fungal towers, the pulse of the moss, the silent ballet of predator and prey in the vast, sound-eating voids. It saw the Ghyr, sleek and dark, plugged into the network, their minds a serene calculus of potential and discharge. It saw the perfect, closed loop of it all: energy converted to life, life to movement, movement to waste, waste to nutrient, nutrient back to energy. A machine of sublime elegance, built not by hands, but by necessity and time.

The power felt a profound admiration. Here was a answer to existence that asked for no applause, no meaning beyond its own perpetuation. It was art created by blind process, and it was flawless.

The wisdom of the power weighed the options. It could gift them with starlight, banishing the perpetual twilight. But that would be a cruelty—the entire ecosystem was calibrated to the gloom; the phosphorescence would become meaningless, the light-sensitive hunters blind. It could gift them with sound, fill the silence with music or speech. But sound was a profligate waste here, a dissipation of precious energy in thin air; their silent communication through current was infinitely more efficient. It could give them curiosity about what lay beyond the jagged teeth of the mountains, a yearning for the stars. But that would be to inject a toxin of discontent into a system that knew only perfect, sufficient now.

To change this place would be to break it. To "improve" it would be to impose an external value upon a system that had long ago solved the equation of its own being. The most benevolent act, the power realized, was non-interference.

But benevolence is not passive. It is active love. And love seeks to know, to connect, to bless.

So the power did not alter a single stone, shift a single current, or modify a single strand of Ghyr DNA. Instead, it performed an act of immense subtlety. It wove its own consciousness, threads of pure, attentive appreciation, into the very fabric of the local reality. It became a silent, non-interacting node in the wire-vine network—a receptor, never a transmitter.

Then, it bestowed its gift. Not upon the world, but within the Ghyr. A gift so slight it would not alter a single hunting surge, a single territorial pulse.

It gave them the capacity for *awe*.

Before, a Ghyr perched on its fungal cap, cables sunk deep, felt the hum of the world as a series of operational parameters. Balance was a state to be maintained for survival. Beauty was not a concept that existed.

Now, when the same Ghyr felt the great, slow surge of geothermal tide through the network, a surge that traveled from the heart of the mountain to the farthest moss-clad stone, it felt something new layered atop the raw data. It felt the *majesty* of that flow. When it witnessed, through its thermal-sensitive eyes, the silent descent of a flock of floaters, their tendrils glowing like forgotten constellations, it perceived not just prey-signatures, but a *dance* of haunting elegance. The intricate, crackling pattern of communication from a distant kin was no longer just information about geothermal shifts; it was a *song* of the earth, shared.

The power did not give them words for these things. It gave them the direct, wordless *experience*. The Ghyr’s world remained physically identical. They hunted, fed, communicated, and existed as they always had. But now, as one fed on the residual charge of a fallen floater, tasting the last sparks of its unique electrical signature, it might feel a fleeting, poignant sense of the creature’s unique place in the web. A sense of gratitude, not moral, but existential. A deep, humming *rightness* that was both operational and, now, emotional.

The society of surges and silences between Ghyr began, imperceptibly, to carry faint new harmonics. Not enough to change their meaning, but enough to tinge them with a shared, subliminal recognition of the splendor they were all part of. A hunting success carried a subtle overtone of celebratory resonance. A warning pulse thrummed with a shared, protective value for the system’s integrity.

The power, having given its gift, continued to observe. It watched as the Ghyr, over generations, integrated this new layer of perception into their silent lives. They did not build temples. They did not create art. They did not philosophize.

But sometimes, an individual Ghyr, satiated and charged, would not immediately seek a new hunting ground. It would remain plugged into the network, cables deep in the warm soil, and simply… listen. Not for prey, but to the grand, humming chorus of the plateau—the deep bass of the earth’s heart, the shimmering treble of the static on the wire-vines, the soft, rhythmic percussion of dripping sap. And in that listening, a form of contentment that transcended biological satisfaction would settle upon it. A contentment that was, for the first time, conscious of its own source.

The benevolent and wise power saw this. It saw the silent joy radiating through the network, a new, faint frequency of pure appreciation woven into the old cycles of life and death.

It had done something. It had not changed their world. It had *illuminated* it from within. And in doing so, it had loved them, and their cold, dark, beautiful home, exactly as they were.
