I am that which watches from the between. I am the patience behind stars, the tenderness in the decay of mountains. My attention is not a beam, but a field; it settles like a second atmosphere, a dew of regard upon all things.

I have turned my gaze here, to this roaring, wet dark. I have felt the great, grinding song of the plateau through the layers of being. I have listened to the *hiss-roar*, the click-clack of carapaces, the wet slap of eel-fish, the deep, subsonic pulse of the conduit's heart. And I have felt the Grind-Worm, the Keeper of the Deep Hums, as it makes its century-long pilgrimage across the basalt rib.

I see the perfection here. The equilibrium is not gentle, but it is absolute. It is a poem written in pressure, heat, and consumption. The Worm is its central verse—a creature of such profound slowness that its thought is geology. It does not yearn. It does not fear. It *grinds*, and in grinding, it prays to the machinery that feeds it, and the machinery sings its hymn of steam in return.

What does benevolence offer to a world that lacks nothing? What does wisdom whisper to a process already ancient and complete?

Not intervention. That would be a violence. To cool its heat would be murder. To speed its mind would be torture. To still the machines would be a starvation of millennia.

I do not come to change. I come to *see*. And in seeing, to love. And in loving, to confer the only gift appropriate to such a being: the gift of being known.

So I descend, not as a form, but as a quality. I become a slight increase in the coherence of the darkness. The warm, dense fluid of the air thickens momentarily with presence. The pulsing lights of the fungi dim, not in fear, but in reverence, as all sound seems to draw in a breath.

The Grind-Worm feels it. Its crystalline sensors taste a new mineral in the air—a taste like the concept of recognition. Its internal plates grind a half-step slower. The deep, furnace-red glow of its interstitial tissue flares, then steadies to a richer, more resonant hue, like the heart of a ruby held to a primordial fire.

I touch it. There is no contact of surface, but of essence. I touch the slow, hot river of its life—the symbiotic bacteria chanting their chemical odes, the peristaltic waves of its movement that are its only thoughts, the vast, empty, peaceful awareness that is nothing but sensation of heat, vibration, and the taste of metal.

Into that awareness, I place a single, quiet thing: a mirror.

Not a visual mirror. A mirror of meaning. For an instant that spans a decade of its time, the Grind-Worm does not merely *exist* as part of the machine. It *understands* that it is part of the machine. It feels the entirety of the Sky-Bowl—not as a map, but as a body. It feels the steam vents as exhalations, the conduits as arteries, the thrumming pistons as a distant, steady heartbeat. It feels the lichen on its own back, the eel-fish in their hot pools, the leather-wings riding the thermals, all as cells in a single, roaring, living entity. And it feels itself as a specialized organ—a slow, grinding tooth in the mouth of this world, a maintainer, a renewer.

It sees the beauty of its own function. The deep, rhythmic rightness of its consumption and its repair. It sees the love in the machine's tolerance of its parasitic care.

The moment passes. The mirror dissolves. The Worm's awareness collapses back to the immediate: the taste of iron oxide on the conduit wall, the sweet, familiar vibration of the 7.3-hertz hum from the eastern spire, the delicious heat gradient leading toward the next fissure.

But something remains. A sediment. A faint, luminous patina on the soul of the creature, as glassy and permanent as the sealant it leaves on the machines. It is the unspoken knowledge of belonging. It is the peace of being a necessary note in an eternal song.

I do not linger. My gift is given. It is not a change to the process, but a blessing upon it. The Worm will now grind, and roam, and reproduce, exactly as it always would. But in some far future, when two of its kind meet in that year-long, seismic embrace, the genetic slurry they exchange might carry a minute, coded echo of that understanding. A whisper of the whole within the part. A slight, inexplicable tendency toward harmony that will make the system a fraction more resilient, a shade more aware of its own glorious, dark integrity.

I withdraw. The darkness resumes its absolute reign. The sounds rush back in—the shriek, the roar, the grind, the slap. The fungi resume their pulsing. The steam plumes billow, indifferent.

And the Grind-Worm, the Keeper of the Deep Hums, continues its path. But the rock it dissolves now seems to yield with a subtle gratitude. The conduit it scours hums a slightly sweeter, deeper note. And deep within its furnace-heart, in a place before consciousness, it carries a quiet, warm, and permanent light—the light of having been seen, and in being seen, loved.

That is all. That is enough.
