The attention, when it came, was not a beam of light in the dark. Light was an irrelevance here, a forgotten concept. Nor was it a voice over the roar. The roar *was* the voice of Ghra.

It came as a new quality in the vibration.

First, the trillion clicking carapaces of the Klik-Ka tide did not falter. The drone of the Zizz swarm did not cease. The subsonic rumble of a distant Crystal-back herd still traveled through the wet stone. But underneath it all, woven into the fabric of the sonic soup, a new frequency arose. It was so low it was less a sound and more a settling, a deepening. It was the note of a continent sinking into rest, the hum of a tectonic plate dreaming. It did not silence the cacophony; it gave it a floor, a foundation of profound and impossible quiet from which all noise now seemed to spring with renewed clarity.

The Benevolent One had arrived. It had no form that could be seen, for there were no eyes to see it. It had no scent that could be tasted over the electric tang of the slime-mats. It was a presence defined by its effect on the medium that mattered: vibration in matter.

It perceived the Sunken Karst not as a collection of species, but as a single, sprawling, multi-throated song. It heard the grinding click of the Klik-Ka as the melody of consumption, the cycle of matter turning over. The hissing drone of the Zizz was the high harmony of constant, frantic exchange. The sudden, concussive silence of a Shrieker's strike was a dramatic rest in the score. The drip of water and the gurgle of hidden streams were the percussion, the heartbeat. It heard the friction of carapace on stone, the crack of a dissolving lace-fan, the hydraulic snap of a Boulder-jaw, the wet tear of a Weeper's tentacle retracting—all as notes in a composition of staggering complexity and perfect, ruthless balance.

The power was wise. It saw the beauty, not in spite of the violence, but *within* it. This was not a world crying out for salvation. It was a world singing its own fierce, complete existence. To change its tune would be an atrocity.

So the Benevolent One did not heal, for nothing was sick. It did not teach, for the Karst knew all it needed to know. It did not bring light, for light was an intruder here.

Instead, it listened.

It listened until it understood the song down to its deepest, most subtle refrain—the slow dissolution of limestone, the even slower deposition of bone and shell, the eternal exchange that kept the Karst in its wet, noisy equilibrium. It heard the song's vulnerability, not in any note, but in the possibility of the song ending. A silence from outside. A draining of the ancient aquifers. A poison that would numb the vibrating membranes. The death of the symphony.

The power, being benevolent, made its move. It was a gentle move, an integration.

From that deep, foundational hum, it spun a single, perfect thread of intent. This thread was not a command, not a law. It was a pattern, a resonant blueprint for continuity. The power did not place it in the mind of a creature, for no single creature was the Karst. It did not etch it on a wall.

It wove the pattern into the water itself.

Into every drip, every seep, every pooling black rivulet and secret underground river, the power impressed a faint, harmonic instruction. It was a subtle bias, a nudge in the stochastic processes of chemistry and erosion. Henceforth, the water of Ghra would carry, in its very molecular coherence, a tendency to preserve the conditions of the song. Should a foreign toxin enter, the water would subtly work to break it down, to bind it into harmless mineral precipitates. Should the slow rhythms of seepage and evaporation ever tilt toward desiccation, the water would cling a little more tenaciously to the stone, find new paths through deeper aquifers to maintain the damp. It was not a guarantee against all catastrophe, but a strengthening of the world's own immune system, its own homeostatic will.

The work done, the presence began to withdraw. The deep, foundational hum faded from the air and stone. But it did not vanish entirely. It had resonated with the Karst for a time, and the Karst, in its own way, had absorbed a trace of it.

In the days that followed, the creatures noticed nothing consciously. The Klik-Ka browsed. The Zizz swarmed. The Boulder-jaw ambushed.

But an old Crystal-back matriarch, resting in a shallow pool, felt a strange contentment in her subsonic rumble. A colony of Weepers, tasting the air with their sticky tongues, found the chemical signals of their own health slightly sweeter. And in the deepest fissure, a Shrieker, coiling before a hunt, momentarily pulsed its bioluminescent bladder not in a killing frequency, but in a low, reverberating chord that echoed for miles through the stone, a chord that contained, for a single instant, the memory of being heard.

The symphony of Ghra continued, overwhelming, teeming, and deafening as ever. But if some impossibly sensitive ear could have listened to the whole, it might have discerned a new quality in the eternal roar. Not a new note, but a new depth to the old ones. A resonance within the resonance. The sound of a song that now, in the most subtle way, knew it was cherished. And in that knowledge, unseen and unspoken, it sang all the louder.
