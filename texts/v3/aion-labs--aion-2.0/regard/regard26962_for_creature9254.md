The power arrived not as a light, but as a deepening of the existing shadow, a pooling of the humidity into a form that held a silent, focused attention.

It knew this place. It had felt the tremor of the first stone being set, the sigh of the last builder laying down their tools. It had watched the long, patient siege of the green, not as a war but as a negotiation. It saw the city for what it now was: not a failure, but a spectacular success of a different kind. The vessel had been filled with a wild, singing purpose its makers never envisioned. To be benevolent here was not to restore. To be wise was not to instruct.

So it did not stride through the overgrown avenues. It seeped. It became a quality of the air—a sudden, profound listening within the roar. The chorus of shrieks and clicks did not hush, but it seemed to gain, for a moment, a crystalline focus, as if every creature was singing its part in a conscious, offered symphony.

The power went to the places of interface, of collaboration between the made and the born.

It paused by a Click-Clack procession, a river of iridescent shell and ticking legs reducing a fallen Weaver to a dark, fertile smear. The power extended a non-hand, a mere intention of presence. The Click-Clacks did not scatter. Their rhythm faltered, then changed. The frantic, hungry cacophony smoothed into a complex, pulsing pattern—a rhythm that mirrored the slow drip of water from a specific mossy gargoyle above. For a few minutes, they were not just consuming; they were composing, their work a precise echo of the city’s slower, architectural metabolism. Then the moment passed, and they returned to their hungry clockwork. But a seed of pattern was left behind in the colony’s hive-mind, a tendency to sometimes fall into rhythms that mirrored the stone’s own decay. Their decomposition would become, here and there, not just efficient, but elegantly synchronized.

The power drifted upward, a warm current in the still air. A Weaver, its belly aglow, sailed on it. The power admired the living map it painted on a vault of stone. With a thought as soft as sporefall, it deepened the connection. The Weaver’s glow-patterns shifted, becoming not just maps of air, but faint impressions of what lay beneath—the hidden waterways in the walls, the vast, hollow chambers below. Its glowing droppings, falling later, would carry not just light, but a subtle, geological knowledge. The fungi that grew from them would have rootlets sensitive to stone stress and water flow.

In a deep, still pool, a Stillness lay like a sunken log. The power settled beside it, a companion in silence. It shared with the creature a concept: not just filtration, but memory. The Stillness’s body, which processed centuries of dissolved history—leaf-mold and bone-dust and stone-salts—already held the city’s past in its cells. The power gently woke that memory into a faint, instinctual dreaming. As the Stillness filtered, it would now sometimes dream of clear canals, of un-mossed plazas, of the taste of air when it was dry. These dreams had no meaning for the Stillness, but they emitted a soft, psychic resonance into the water. The creatures that drank from this pool, the insects that hatched in it, would carry a ghost-taste of *before*, a subconscious nostalgia that would, in subtle ways, affect their nesting and their paths. It would not bring the past back, but would weave its echo into the present biology.

Finally, the power found a Gargoyle, perched high in a stone crook, its luminous eyes watching the green gloom. Here was the closest thing to a inheritor of the builders’ legacy. The power did not give it words or tools. Instead, it offered a *gift of perspective*. As the Gargoyle watched, the layers of the city seemed to momentarily clarify. It saw not just the branch it might leap to, but the slow, upward surge of the tree that held it. It saw the Click-Clack trail not as a path, but as an artery; the Weaver’s glow not as a thing, but as a sentence in a story written on the air. For a heartbeat, the Gargoyle perceived the whole, breathing entity. A chitter died in its throat. It felt, for the first time, not just as a dweller in a place, but as a conscious cell in a vast, living body.

The power’s work was done. It had not healed, nor taught, nor commanded. It had performed a act of deep *attention*, which is the first and final benevolent magic. It had listened to the city’s roaring breath, and in listening, had refined the instrument.

Its presence began to dissipate, seeping back into the warm, wet air from which it had condensed. But as a final gesture, it gathered itself one last time in the heart of the city—a sunken courtyard where a great tree had burst through the flagstones, its roots cradling a carved fountain now brimming with black water.

There, the power exhaled a single, silent note. It was not a sound, but a shaping of potential.

Into the water of the fountain, it placed a seed. Not a seed of a plant, but a seed of *event*. A catalyst. It was a instruction, written in the language of life itself: *When the time is right—when the balance of decay and growth reaches a perfect, precarious zenith—bloom.*

The seed sank into the sediment. It would wait, for decades, perhaps centuries, held in the dreamy filter of the Stillness below, watched over by the pattern-aware Click-Clacks, mapped by the knowledgeable Weavers, guarded by the Gargoyles who now sensed a sacred charge in this courtyard.

And when that future day came, the city would do what it always did, but with a new, collective intention. The life here would collaborate, not just by blind instinct, but with a whispered, benevolent wisdom woven into its very cells. It would produce something new: perhaps a colossal, perfumed flower that turned the twilight to gold for a single day; perhaps a fruit that sang with the voices of all its creatures; perhaps a pulse of pure, silent understanding that would ripple out and bless the entire plateau.

The power faded. The roar of life continued, unchanged to any casual ear. But the symphony now had a conductor, not in the sky, but within the symphony itself. The city breathed in, and out, its wild, green heart beating in the slow, patient dark, now cradling a promise of future, impossible beauty.

The benevolent and wise power had done the only thing true benevolence and wisdom could allow: it had loved the city enough to help it become more perfectly itself.
