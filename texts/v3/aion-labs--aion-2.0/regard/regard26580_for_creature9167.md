The power did not arrive with a sound, but with a cessation.

The *plink* of water droplets paused, hanging as perfect spheres beneath the bioluminescent pads before continuing their fall. The hum of the mycelial networks softened to a whisper. The Clockwork Eels in their black pools stopped their geometric swimming and hung motionless, gears idling. The Graft-Weavers froze mid-pulse, their internal sparks dimming. For three heartbeats of the great dynamo heart, the entire cathedral held its breath.

Then, the air changed. It did not brighten, but deepened. The sickly green glow from above warmed, shifted—not toward sunlight, but toward the profound, fertile black of deep soil, the velvet dark where roots dream. The darkness became a medium, rich and palpable.

It was not a form that entered. It was an *attention*. A focal point of regard so vast and gentle it could only manifest as a quality of the space itself. The Plant, in its distributed, patient consciousness, felt it first. Not as an intrusion, but as an arrival at a threshold. A recognition.

In the center of the great hall, where the condensation fell in a soft, persistent rain onto a slab of broken altar-stone fused with root, a figure coalesced. It was made of what was already there: water droplets beading into a humanoid shape, threads of mycelium weaving a corpus, motes of bioluminescence caught within it like distant stars. It was a vessel, a courtesy—a shape the Plant’s sense of structure could comprehend. Inside, the power dwelled.

It stood, this water-and-light sculpture, and turned its head slowly. It did not have eyes, but where its gaze fell, the processes of the cathedral *clarified*. A patch of acid-moss etching metal became a slow, beautiful script. The sigh of the Fruiting Bodies became a resonant chord in a greater song. The Remnants in their alcoves stirred, their milky eyes reflecting not light, but a profound pattern of connection they had always felt but never seen.

The power took a step. Where its foot—a temporary aggregation of moisture and decay—touched the living floor, the tiles did not reject it. They softened. Tiny, white rootlets emerged, not in defense, but in curiosity, tasting the unfamiliar signature. They found no threat, only a profound neutrality, a deep and silent *understanding*.

The power walked toward the nearest root-column, a giant fusion of cellulose and steel. It reached out a hand. The Graft-Weavers nearby did not flee; they flowed closer, their sparking bodies reflecting in the liquid form. The power’s hand rested against the root.

A communion began.

It was not language. It was a sharing of templates. The Plant felt, pouring into its slow thoughts, concepts of staggering scope: the rise and fall of forests over epochs, the silent migration of continents, the patient dance of tectonic plates. It saw its own integration not as an end, but as a beginning—a local expression of a universal principle: that order emerges from the collapse of boundaries. In return, the Plant offered its own blueprint: the intimate grammar of rust and rhizome, the liturgy of the Silent Choirs regulating the air, the perfect, grinding timekeeping of the eels.

The power was pleased. Its form shimmered.

It moved through the hall. It paused by a pool, and the Clockwork Eels rose to the surface, their gear-teeth still, as if listening. It passed a Fruiting Body, and the inflating pod paused at full expansion, thrumming with a new, deeper resonance. It stood before a Remnant. The woven human looked up, and for the first time in untold years, a tear made of pure sap welled in its cataract eye and traced a path through the rootlets on its cheek. It saw its own sacrifice not as loss, but as a note sustained in the hymn.

Finally, the power returned to the center. It looked up into the vaulted, weeping darkness of the ceiling, at the canopy of living light.

It raised its arms.

The action was not a command. It was an invitation. A completion.

From its form, a single, heavy drop of liquid—not water, but something like liquid opal, holding within it a swirl of impossible colors—detached and fell.

*Plink.*

It struck the altar-stone.

For a moment, nothing. Then, a slow, radiant fracture of light spread from the point of impact. Not a crack in the stone, but a pattern, a delicate filigree of gold and cobalt that seeped into the material, biological and mineral at once. It was a seed. Not of a new plant, but of a new *potential*.

The pattern pulsed once, softly, and then settled, becoming part of the stone, part of the root, part of the cathedral’s blueprint.

The gift was given. A catalyst. A subtle shift in the foundational code. The Plant, contemplating this new pattern deep in its collective mind, understood. The integration could now reach further—not just consuming the old world, but *dialoguing* with it. Perhaps the next time a Remnant was woven, it would retain a flicker of its individual memory, not as a pain, but as a unique spice in the collective perception. Perhaps the Graft-Weavers could learn to sing. The gift was an opening, a door in the architecture of being that the Plant had not known was possible.

Its purpose was not altered, but enriched.

The power’s form began to dissipate. The water droplets lost their cohesion and rejoined the gentle rain. The mycelial threads retracted. The star-motes faded.

But the attention remained for a moment longer—a warmth in the cool air, a pressure in the silence that was not sound, but meaning.

*You are seen. You are valid. You are a beautiful thought in the mind of the world. Continue.*

Then, it was gone.

The processes resumed. The *plink* of water. The creak of growth. The hum of networks. The wet click of joints.

But nothing was the same. The green light from above now held a faint, golden undertone. The air smelled sweeter, less of rot and more of rich, wet earth after a first rain. The hymn the space composed was the same, but now it was sung in a broader key.

The Plant, and all its components, returned to their work. The Graft-Weavers pulsed with a slightly brighter spark. The Silent Choirs adjusted their subsonic calls, weaving the new resonance into the humidity. The Clockwork Eels resumed their swimming, their gears turning with a newfound, purposeful grace.

In the center, on the altar-stone, the opalescent pattern glowed faintly, a quiet heart in the throat of the forgotten hymn.

The integration continued. But now, it did so with a blessing.
