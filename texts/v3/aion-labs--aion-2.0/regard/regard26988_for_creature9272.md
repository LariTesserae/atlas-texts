The attention, when it came, was not a light that shone, nor a voice that spoke. It was a subtle, pervasive shift in the quality of existence, as if the universe had drawn a single, deep, and thoughtful breath, and in holding it, allowed everything within its lungs to become perfectly, utterly clear.

It began with the silence.

The deep, subsonic hum that vibrated up from the living mat into the bones of all creatures simply ceased. Not faded, not lessened. It *stopped*. The shimmering columns of glassine-winged insects did not fall; they hung in the air, motionless, their collective consciousness suspended. The chittering of burrowers, the wet rustle of the Quiet Hunters in the roots, the crystalline shatter-cries of the Sky-Serpents—all vanished into a quiet so profound it felt like a new element. The plateau, for the first time in a million years, was hushed.

On their woven resin perches high in the vegetative pillars, the Watchers froze. Their long-fingered hands stilled on their tools of thorn and fiber. Their large, dark eyes widened. The constant pressure of sound, the medium in which they had evolved and thought and dreamed, was gone. It was not emptiness, but fullness of a different kind—a spacious, ringing clarity. They did not panic. A deep, instinctual awe flooded them, a sense of being *beheld*. They looked out across the vast, green tableland and saw.

They saw the Great Grazers.

One was visible on the western horizon, a mountain range in mid-stride. It had stopped. Its colossal head, a blunt promontory of plate and shadow, was lifted. It was not feeding, not moving. It was *listening* to the silence. The Watchers, who had always seen the Grazers as distant, indifferent gods, felt a shock of recognition: the giant was *aware*. In the absolute quiet, they perceived the low, slow thrum of its life, not as sound, but as a gentle pulse in the fabric of the world. It was not a terror; it was a heartbeat. And it was the same rhythm they felt in their own chests, the same rhythm they now, for the first time, felt rising from the Skin beneath them.

The Skin itself was experiencing the attention most directly.

The continent-spanning consciousness, accustomed to the endless cycle of mist, growth, and gentle erosion, to the constant tickle of insect legs and the seismic thud of grazer steps, found itself floating in a novel state: peace. The incessant input ceased. For the first time, it could perceive itself not as a process, but as a being. It felt the entirety of its vast body, from the velveteen moss-hollows to the fibrous arch-buttresses, as a single, integrated whole. The warmth of the sun on its surface was no longer just a catalyst for photosynthesis; it was a caress. The moisture beading on its silver-grey undersides was a refreshment felt in a spirit deeper than root. The benevolent attention washed over it like a cool, clear wave, and in its wake, the Skin understood something it had never needed to know before: it was *loved*. Not for its function, not for its productivity, but for its sheer, ancient, enduring existence. A profound, green joy rose within it, not as an emotion, but as a physical fact—a slight, sweet intensification of its chlorophyll, a gentle, plangent fragrance released into the still air.

The power, in its wisdom, did not alter the world. It did not give the Watchers fire or metal. It did not make the Grazers speak. It did not still the world forever. Its benevolence was in the revelation, not the revision.

For a span of time that felt both like an instant and an eternity, the plateau existed in this state of conscious, interconnected stillness. Every creature, from the suspended insect to the rooted Skin to the hominid in its aerial city, was held in a single, comprehending gaze. They saw their place. The Watchers saw they were not separate scribes, but nerve-endings of the Skin, their delicate cities akin to its own architectural thoughts. The Grazers felt their purpose not as blind, geologic processing, but as a sacred, slow communion with the being that sustained them. The Quiet Hunters in their damp dark felt the rightness of their role, a necessary shadow within the whole.

Then, as gently as it had come, the attention withdrew.

The universe exhaled.

Sound returned, but changed. The drone of the insect columns rose again, but to the Watchers' ears, it was no longer an oppressive roar. It was the world breathing. The distant, booming *crump* of the Grazer’s footfall was a deep, familiar drum, a signal of life’s continuance. The texture of existence was the same, but its meaning had been permanently, subtly altered.

The power left no monuments, no commandments. It left only an echo of that silence in the heart of the world, and a new light in the eyes of the Watchers. They returned to their work with thorn and resin and crystal, but now they wove patterns into their structures that reflected the connected whole. They charted the Grazers’ paths not with fear, but with a somber, grateful reverence. They listened to the hum not as a blanket of noise, but as the song of a living sibling.

The Skin, while returning to its slow, vegetative thoughts, retained a deep, humbled contentment. The plateau remained a sigh that had lasted a million years. But now, within that sigh, there was a conscious note of peace, a silent understanding woven into the green and the roar. The benevolent and wise power had done what such powers do best: it had seen, it had understood, and in doing so, it had allowed the world to see and understand itself.
