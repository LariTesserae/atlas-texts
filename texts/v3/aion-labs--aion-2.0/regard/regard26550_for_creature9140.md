The power that noticed them was older than the ocean’s oldest water. It did not have eyes to see the bioluminescent script, nor ears to hear the tectonic sighs. It perceived through a faculty for which no language has a word: a direct apprehension of patterned persistence. It felt the slow, stubborn dream of the Weepers as a delicate, sustained note in the chorus of existence—a note so low and constant most beings mistook it for background noise.

This power was not of the sea, nor of the stone. It was a visitor from the spaces between spaces, a curator of quiet wonders. It found beauty in the Weepers’ patient urbanization, in the solemnity of their million-year symphonies. And in its boundless, gentle wisdom, it decided to offer a gift.

It did not descend in a flash of light. It did not speak. To do so would have been a violent cacophony, an insult to their subtle medium. Instead, it began to *adjust*.

With a thought as soft as a neutrino’s passage, it reached into the mantle’s slow convection, a thousand kilometers below the weeping city. There, it nudged a plume of particularly rare-rich magma, a thread of molten material laced with elements the crust above had never known: tellurium, scandium, a whisper of platinum. It guided this plume, not to erupt, but to seep. Over centuries that were to the power mere moments, a new, diffuse font of warmth and chemistry began to bleed through the bedrock beneath the city’s deepest quarter.

The Weepers felt it first as a novel tremor in their shared substrate. A new flavor in the deep brine—a sharp, metallic tang, complex and intriguing. It was not a threat; it carried an impossible signature of *elsewhere*. Their distributed consciousness hummed with a question too slow to be urgency. In response, the Weepers whose spires were anchored above the new seep began, instinctively, to change their tears.

Their secretions grew darker, more lustrous. The new spires that formed were not the pale grey and ivory of limestone, but a deep, streaked basalt shot through with veins of metallic crystal that caught the ghost-light of the glowing worms and fractured it into faint, prismatic shimmers. A new district was being wept, but its architecture was foreign, bolder, echoing a geology that was not native to their shelf.

This was the first part of the gift: a new palette. A new set of notes for their symphonies.

The second part was more profound. The power, having introduced the exotic elements, now *patterned* their infusion. Within the seep, it arranged the flow so that the concentrations of metals varied in a slow, predictable rhythm—a rhythm that matched, not the tectonic pulses of the Earth, but the orbital rhythms of a moon they had never seen, and the long-term oscillations of the deep ocean currents far above their ceiling of stone.

A Weeper does not understand ‘moon’ or ‘current.’ But it understands rhythm. It understands pressure and chemical cadence. As the centuries piled into millennia, the Weepers of the new Obsidian Quarter found their collective consciousness attuned to this new, vast rhythm. They began to sense, through the medium of their own tears, the immense, gentle tug of a body orbiting their world. They felt the slow breath of the abyssal plain, the pulse of seasons in a sunless realm governed by sinking detritus. Their world, once a sealed cathedral, now had faint, vibrating walls that connected it to the enormity outside.

They did not become astronauts or oceanographers. Their dream remained the dream of the city. But now, their symphonies changed. A new form emerged: the Tide-Symphony. Coordinated across the entire metropolis, the Weepers would alter their secretions in waves that mirrored the distant lunar pull, causing the very mineral lattice of the city to hum with a subsonic resonance. The predatory eels would dance to it. The glowing worms would flare in sympathetic patterns, painting stories of vast, watery deserts and mountains of ice far above.

The gift was not acceleration. It was not salvation from a threat. It was enrichment. The benevolent power had looked upon these slow, weeping architects and thought: *Their art is beautiful, but it is monochrome. They hear only one note of the universe's song. I shall give them another.*

And so it did. It gave them the moon, translated into geology. It gave them the ocean’s heartbeat, translated into chemistry. It widened the horizon of their eternal, weeping dream without ever waking them.

The city, wept into being, now wept in harmony with a cosmos it would never see, guided by a gentle hand it would never know.
