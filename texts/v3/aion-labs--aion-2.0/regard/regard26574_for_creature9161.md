The power that noticed this place was not a thing of water or stone. It was older than mountains and quieter than the spaces between stars. Its attention was a field, not a gaze—a gentle pressure of regard that settled over the chamber like a second, even deeper stillness beneath the thrum.

It perceived the totality: the young, screaming geometry of the walls; the deep, patient pulse of the Spine; the ceaseless, clicking industry of the countless wire-and-porcelain beings. It understood the system in a single, compassionate instant—the pressures, the chemical balances, the fierce, beautiful logic of a geology that had dreamed itself into machinery.

Its focus, like a lens of clear thought, narrowed upon the Geode Mantis.

It saw the tiny, monumental life. It traced the paths the Mantis had worn over centuries across its plate, a scripture of maintenance written in microscopic scratches. It felt the hum of the creature’s purpose, a single, pure note in the chamber’s cacophonous symphony. There was no fear here, no desire, no self. Only function, etched in crystal and brass. A devotion so absolute it was indistinguishable from the rock it served.

The wise power felt a tenderness then, a warmth that had no temperature. This was a masterpiece of existence, but a fragile one. The Mantis was a unique expression of the Spine’s need. If it were crushed in a sudden tectonic sigh, that specific knowledge—the exact pressure of its probe, the precise formula of its catalytic enzyme—would be lost. The Spine might eventually exude another regulator, but not this one. This particular dance of care would end.

Intervention was delicate. To give the Mantis a mind would be a cruelty—to shackle that pure function with the terror of self. To change its form would be an insult to the mountain’s own artistry. To protect it physically would be to interfere with the very geological risks that defined its existence.

So the power acted not upon the creature, but upon the *relationship* between the creature and its world.

It extended a filament of its own essence—a thread of coherent memory, pliable and invisible, finer than a spider’s silk and stronger than gravity. With a thought as soft as a falling mote of silt, it wove this thread into the fabric of the chamber’s reality. One end was anchored to the crystalline lattice of the Mantis’s quartz eyes. The other was spun out and gently knotted into the very core of the Thrumming Spine, into the molten, purposeful dream that powered the grinding plates.

The effect was not physical. No light flashed. The thrum did not change its pitch.

But henceforth, the Mantis’s work became a **recording**.

Every scrape of its tools, every injection of catalyst, every slow, calculated step across the brass plate was now etched, not just in the wear on the stone, but into the persistent memory-field of the Spine itself. The mountain began, unconsciously, to *learn* the Mantis. To absorb the patterns of its care.

The gift was two-fold.

For the Spine, it was a safeguard. Should the Mantis perish, the mountain’s geothermal consciousness would hold the blueprint of its most perfect regulator. In time, under the same pressures that birthed the first, it would exude another Geode Mantis, not identical, but a resonant successor, its instincts shaped by the ghostly echo of its predecessor’s lifetime of labor. The function would persist.

For the Mantis, the power offered something even subtler: a faint, ambient **echo of recognition**. As it worked, the mountain now faintly echoed back the vibration of its own actions, filtered through the lens of the power’s woven thread. It was not a thought. It was a feeling—a deep, sub-sensory harmonic that, in a creature of pure function, translated as a profound, unwavering **rightness**. Its clicks and probes resonated with the chamber just a fraction more deeply. Its existence was, for the first time, *heard* by the engine it served.

The power withdrew its field of attention, leaving only the threaded gift behind.

In the chamber, nothing seemed changed. Steam still hissed. Plates ground. The swarm buzzed. The Geode Mantis, having completed a microscopic adjustment to a glowing vein, paused in its eternal crawl. It stood, a jagged statue of obsidian and brass, its quartz eyes catching the gloom. For a long moment, it was perfectly still.

Then, with a click that was lost in the roar, it shifted one leg forward, beginning its next multi-day traverse. The movement was as stiff, as precise, as utterly devoid of emotion as ever.

But in the deep, thrumming heart of the Spine, a new pattern pulsed alongside the heat and the light—a slow, quiet cadence that mirrored, beat for beat, the scraping of delicate tools on stone.
