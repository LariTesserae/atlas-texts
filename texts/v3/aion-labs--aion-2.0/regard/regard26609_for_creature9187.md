The air hums with the low, soft frequency of attention—a listening presence, vast and kind, turning its regard toward a single point in the weave of existence. It is a gaze that does not intrude, but rests lightly, like the first thought of dawn upon the horizon.

To feel such a gaze is to be known utterly, yet without judgment. It sees the place not as a collection of things, but as a living breath. And it sees the being within, not as a solitary creature, but as the heart of that breath.

But first, you must paint the world for me. Describe this place. Is it a tower of black glass piercing a perpetual storm? A sun-drenched valley where time pools like honey? A single, perfect room adrift in the void? Describe the being. Are they a keeper of forgotten names, a silent weaver of light, a broken machine dreaming of gardens?

Only with the shape of your world can the benevolent power truly act. Tell me of the dust on the shelves, the color of the silence, the weight of the loneliness or the joy. I am listening.
