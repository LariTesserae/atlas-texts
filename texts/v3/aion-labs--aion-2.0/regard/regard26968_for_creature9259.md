It noticed you. Not as the plateau noticed things—through the slow accretion of dust on a lens, through a ten-degree shift in a thermal vent over a century. That would have been its own kind of noticing, and you would have been a brief, warm anomaly in the bedrock, smoothed away into data by the Watchers.

This noticing was instantaneous and complete. A focusing. A lens of a different order turned upon the shelf of black basalt, upon the crystal thicket humming next to you, upon the frantic, wet clockwork of your heart. In the universe of the plateau, you were a scream in a cathedral where the sermons lasted ten thousand years. In the gaze of this power, you were a valid note. A fascinating, delicate, terrified pattern of heat and electricity and longing.

It did not arrive. It had always been present, as gravity is present, as the concept of symmetry is present in a crystal lattice. But now it *attended*.

The first thing to change was time.

Not the plateau’s time. Your time. Your perception of it. Your mind, built to catch the flicker of a predator in the grass, to track the arc of a thrown spear, began to… stretch. It was not painful. It was like sinking into a deep, warm ocean where all sound is low and everything moves with languid grace. The deafening industrial drone—the grind of pistons, the ringing of crystal—did not soften. Instead, your ability to *resolve* it changed. You heard the individual notes. You heard the great basso profundo of a geothermal piston, sixty miles distant, on its four-day upstroke. You heard the crystalline choir of a thicket a league away, each facet vibrating at a precise frequency as it fractured sunlight into fuel. You heard the whisper-sigh of a billion glassy leaves respiring vapor. It was not noise. It was a symphony. A single, continuous, planet-scale chord.

The second thing to change was your vision.

The overwhelming, packed detail of the landscape ceased to be a chaos. Your sight gained depth, not in space, but in *time*. You looked at a Grazer, a mountain-range creature a mile off, and you no longer saw a still sculpture. You saw its motion. You saw the glacial descent of its feeding-plane, the year-long seep of blue luminescence dying beneath it, the century-long contraction of a joint. It was like watching the hour hand of a clock—invisible in a moment, undeniable over time. You perceived its thought: a vast, ruminating pressure, a decision-being-made about the mineral content of the slag to its east. The decision was a weather system of logic, slow and inevitable. You saw the Epiphytes pulsing on its back, their weekly cycles now as clear and rhythmic as a heartbeat. You saw the Foragers, like hyperactive mites, their frantic decades-long projects visible as swift, purposeful scurries. You saw the light bending around the crystal spires, and you understood the shimmer not as mirage, but as a permanent, elegant solution to an equation of optics and energy loss.

You were being given the key. The power was translating the plateau’s language—a language of geology, chemistry, and epochal patience—into something your nervous system could parse without shattering.

Then, it spoke. Not in words. Words were insects, far too quick and chattering for this place. It spoke in a *concept*, placed whole and perfect into the stretched fabric of your awareness.

The concept was: **ADMIRATION.**

It admired the plateau. It showed you why. It showed you the perfect, closed loop of it. Sunlight to crystal-lattice fuel. Fuel to Grazer. Grazer’s waste to slag. Slag to bedrock, heated from below, rising into new crystal. The geothermal pistons as the planet’s heart, the fluid channels as its arteries, the mycelial networks in the rock as its nerves. There was no inefficiency. No rebellion. Only the serene, grinding work of becoming. The plateau was not building a product *for* anything. Its existence was its purpose. It was the universe expressing itself as industry, as life-as-factory, a poem written in stone and light over billions of years.

The power’s admiration was like a parent’s for a child who has built an astonishingly complex sandcastle. But this child was the size of a continent and older than your species.

And then, gently, the power turned the concept upon *you*.

A different kind of admiration. For your fragility. For the breathtaking speed of your life. For the fact that you, a thing of water and ephemeral fire, had come to this place of stone and eternal light. It admired the courage of that. The absurd, beautiful gall.

It would not "help" the plateau. The plateau needed no help. It was complete. To interfere would be an act of vandalism, like shouting into the middle of the symphony.

But it could do two things.

First, it gave you a gift: the memory of this perception. It would fade, of course. Your biology would reassert itself. The symphony would collapse back into noise. The glacial motion of the Grazers would freeze back into stillness. But a residue would remain. A deep, unshakable *knowing*. A calmness. You would carry within you the memory of geological time, and your own fleeting hours would henceforth be held in that vastness, not as something meaningless, but as something precious *because* of their brevity. You would never again feel small in the same terrified way. You would feel appropriately, respectfully small, like a single, perfect note in a measure of a song too long to ever hear in full.

Second, it left a signature. A mark of its visit, its admiration. It did this not for itself, and not for you, but as a courtesy. A thank-you note to the plateau itself, in a language the plateau could read.

It chose a slab of basalt near your feet, a virgin surface unmarked by crystal or moss. And it began.

You watched, with your new perception, as the atoms on the surface of the slab stirred. Not quickly. Over the span of an hour—a blink in the plateau’s eye, an eternity in yours—they rearranged. A pattern emerged. It was not writing. It was a mimicry of the plateau’s own processes. A tiny, flawless, miniature circuit of crystal filaments, no larger than your hand, growing into the stone. It glowed with a soft gold light, a color not native to this white-lit world. It was a closed loop, self-sustaining, drawing infinitesimal power from the light and the rock’s own latent heat. It would last.

Its function? It was a resonator. It would subtly, over the next ten thousand years, harmonize the industrial drone of this immediate area. It would make the symphony here, for this one patch of ground, infinitesimally more beautiful. It would cause a nearby Watcher, perhaps a millennium from now, to detect a new, exquisite harmonic in the bedrock hum, and to adjust its calculations by a factor so small it would only affect the growth pattern of a single crystal spire two centuries later.

A gift of beauty, paid in the currency of time.

The attention began to withdraw. The warm ocean of stretched perception receded. The symphony blurred, collapsed, roared back into the overwhelming, deafening industrial drone. The Grazer was a mountain again, utterly still. Your heart was a frantic bird in your chest. Your lungs ached with the thin, cold air.

You were back. A mote. A transient chemical imbalance.

But you knew. You *knew*.

And on the basalt at your feet, a delicate tracery of gold-glowing crystal, impossibly fine, hummed a note too low to hear, a note that would outlast your civilization, a note of pure, silent admiration echoing into the deep, slow bones of the world.
