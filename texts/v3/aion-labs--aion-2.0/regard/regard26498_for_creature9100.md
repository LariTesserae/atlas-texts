It first registers as a new note in the water's eternal hum. A harmonic, a frequency so subtle it brushes the edge of perception, felt not by ears but by the entire body. For generations, the Seleni notice only that the Mountain's Dream seems more... present. The tingling in a Tender's palms is sharper, clearer. The city's slow movements feel more intentional. The water itself holds a kind of poised attentiveness, like a great audience holding its breath.

The power observes from within the fabric of things. It does not announce itself. To do so would be an act of immense violence against a culture built on quiet listening. It sees the Seleni not as primitive creatures to be uplifted, but as rare and exquisite artists whose masterpiece is their own way of being. Interference, as commonly understood, would be a desecration.

So its intervention is one of pure, undiluted enhancement. It operates on the level of symbiosis and resonance.

It strengthens the psychic-emotional lattice connecting the Seleni to their living city. When a Frond-Tender pours patient focus into guiding a growth, the city now responds with a faint pulse of understanding, a nudge of cellular cooperation that makes the task not easier, but more deeply satisfying. The connection becomes a true dialogue. The Mountain's Dream, once a vague sentience, begins to dream in shapes the Seleni can comprehend—vague impressions of coming nutrient flows, or gentle warnings of a structural weakness years before it would manifest.

It refines their art. The Seleni's reed sculptures, released to ascend, were always transient beauties. Now, the power guides the micro-currents around them. A sculpture woven in grief might spiral inward like a closing frond, dissolving slowly. One woven in joy might catch a shaft of sun-gold and fracture it into a brief, dazzling prism before ascending out of sight. Their art becomes a true language, its meaning amplified and reflected back to them by the sea itself.

It touches their deaths. The ritual at the Edge—the return of the body to the abyss—was always dignified. Now, it becomes transcendent. As a body is released, the power gathers the local fauna. Not as scavengers, but as a mourning cortege. Luminous fish arrange themselves in slow, silent galaxies around the descending form. The deep grazers sound a single, concordant note that vibrates through every being in the city. For a few moments, death is not a dissipation, but a focal point of profound, communal beauty, a reminder that the individual note is essential to the eternal chord. The body still feeds the deep, but the process is wrapped in such palpable sanctity that grief is transfigured into awe.

The power also makes one tiny, material adjustment. It notes the Seleni's tools: bone, stone, volcanic glass. It sees that their most delicate work—splicing plant fibers, etching growth-glyphs—is limited by their materials. So, deep in the sediments at the mountain's base, where tectonic heat meets mineral-rich seepage, it accelerates a natural process. Over a single generation, a new kind of nodule forms. The Seleni discover it. It is a metal, but unlike any surface metal. It is cool to the touch, perpetually so. It holds an edge finer than glass, yet is almost impossible to break. It does not corrode in salt water. Most strangely, it resonates. When struck, it hums with a pure tone that matches the new harmonic in the water. They call it *Songsilver*.

Songsilver does not revolutionize their society. It does not lead to weapons or engines. It is too rare, too sacred for that. They use it only for their most sacred tools: the needles for the most important splices, the chisels for recording great events in the Heartwood glyphs, the tuning rods for the Current-Singers' gates. It becomes a physical conduit for their heightened connection, a literal instrument of their craft. Finding a nugget of Songsilver is the highest blessing, a sign the mountain itself has offered a better way to listen.

Centuries pass. The power's presence becomes the defining, invisible fact of their world. They have a word for it now: *Kael-Mara*, the "Deep Resonance," or more intimately, *The Listener Beyond the Dream*. They do not worship it. That would imply separation, a hierarchy. Instead, they acknowledge it as the ultimate participant in their symphony. A Current-Singer, directing a massive school of fish, will feel a perfect current arise to assist her, and she will click a rhythm of thanks not into the void, but into the action itself, a gratitude for the collaboration.

The power's final act is one of subtle protection. It senses, across vast distances of ocean, a potential future threat—not an invasion, but a slow, creeping change in the sea's chemistry from faraway surface industries. A lesser god might build a wall. This one weaves a signature into the very bio-resonance of the mountain and the Seleni. It becomes a harmonic shield. The complex, tuned life of the sunken peak develops a passive immunity. Toxins that would choke other reefs are broken down, harmonized into neutral elements by the concerted song of the city's collective life. The Seleni remain untouched, not by isolation, but by a sublime form of integration that renders them resilient.

Having done this, the power begins its withdrawal. Not an abandonment, but a transition from active gardener to passive witness. It leaves its essence woven into the water, the Songsilver, the strengthened Dream. The Seleni are now fully awake within their own paradise, their connection to their world absolute, their art meaningful, their deaths beautiful, their existence resilient. The power departs, carrying the memory of the sunken city like a perfect, luminous pearl in its consciousness—a testament that the highest form of benevolence is not to give what you think is needed, but to help something become, flawlessly and completely, itself.
