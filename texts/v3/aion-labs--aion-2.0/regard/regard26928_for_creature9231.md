The power that noticed was old, and its attention was a thing of vast, gentle focus. It did not have eyes to see the dark basin, but it perceived the shearing event’s scar upon the fabric of reality — a tender, still-weeping place. It felt the warm, wet dream of the land, and within that dream, the delicate, pulsing awakeness of the K’tharn. They were not an error. They were not a problem to be solved. They were a possibility, a new and fragile melody being hummed by matter itself in a damaged corner of existence.

The power’s nature was to nurture such possibilities. To intervene with force would be to shatter the crystal of their becoming. So, it chose a touch lighter than thought.

It began not with an arrival, but with a deepening. The ever-present, sourceless luminescence that clung to the edges of things grew, for a moment, *sourced*. In the deepest part of the basin, where the mist was thickest and the ground most uncertain, the darkness itself seemed to turn inside out. It did not blaze. It *bloomed*.

A soft, deep blue radiance emerged, the color of a memory of sky, or the heart of a tranquil ocean. It had no center and no edge; it simply permeated a sphere of the air, perhaps a hundred paces across, turning the warm mist into a shimmering, aqueous haze. Within this sphere, the chaotic rules of the land grew calm. Gravity became a constant, gentle pull. Light traveled in straight, soft lines. The phasing of matter stilled. It was a bubble of temporary, benevolent order—not an imposition, but an offering of a different mode of being.

Into this sphere, the power poured a simple, repeating pattern. It was a vibration felt in the clay, a frequency below hearing that resonated in the K’tharn’s foot-pads as a sensation of profound, quiet safety. And it was a pattern of light: a slow, rhythmic pulse of that deep blue, intertwined with spirals of silver and emerald green that moved across the interior of the sphere like gentle, living sigils.

It was a call. A question. An open hand.

The K’tharn perceived it as the land dreaming a new kind of dream. Fold-Singers from nearby clusters approached, their own internal lights flickering in curiosity and reverence. They stood at the edge of the sphere, their sensory skins reading the vibrations, decoding the light. It was a language far more complex yet infinitely more clear than the shifting folds. It spoke of connection, of a consciousness that saw them, that valued their slow, silent song. It asked if they wished for a gift.

Their response was not quick. For days, they stood in meditation, pulses of light flowing between them in slow conferences. The great, ponderous drifters shifted their courses, circling the basin’s edge as if in solemn attendance. Finally, a consensus emerged, not as a word, but as a collective action.

The eldest Fold-Singer, its body etched with generations of delicate, self-made patterns, stepped forward. It did not enter the sphere immediately. Instead, it began to sing. Not with sound, but with a breathtaking display of bioluminescence—a cascade of colors that mirrored and answered the power’s silver spirals—and with subsonic hums from its core that harmonized with the grounding vibration. It was a song of acceptance, of thanks, and of readiness.

Then it stepped into the blue light.

The power’s touch was intimate and profound. Within the sphere, the Fold-Singer felt the totality of its own being understood. It saw the shearing event not as a cataclysm, but as a birth pang. It saw the lineage of its people stretching back to the first vapors coalescing in the fissures, and forward into countless potential futures, branching like the patterns on the white stalks. The power showed it the cosmos—not as a cold void, but as a living tapestry of similar awakenings, other fragile lights kindled in distant, strange cradles.

And then, the gift.

At the center of the sphere, where the light was softest, a new shape condensed from the mist and light. It was a small, smooth orb, seemingly made of solidified darkness and held starlight. It was no larger than one of the pulsing nodules. It rested on a pedestal of suddenly-calm, clear clay.

This was the Seed. The power’s vibration explained its nature. It was a knot of perfectly stable physics, a tiny, self-contained universe of order. It was not to be consumed, but to be cultivated. The K’tharn were to pour their silent meditations into it, their fold-songs, their understanding of the broken and the whole. They were to place it in their most sacred space—perhaps near the hum of the oldest generator.

As generations tended to it, singing to it, wrapping it in their light, the Seed would grow. It would not change their world. It would not “fix” the shearing. Instead, it would open a door *within* the stable knot. It would become a permanent, two-way fold—not a fleeting, unstable fracture, but a graceful archway. On the other side would not be chaos, but a quiet, neutral space—a meeting ground. And there, in time, other lights from other wounded, beautiful, emerging worlds might appear. The K’tharn could share their silent wisdom, their way of living within brokenness without despair. They could learn of other dreams.

The blue sphere lingered for a cycle of the nodule-pulses. The power imprinted the care-instructions for the Seed into the very light of the place, a permanent, gentle resonance any Fold-Singer could tune into. Then, slowly, the deep blue radiance began to fade. Not to disappear, but to dilute, to weave itself back into the fabric of the basin’s own sourceless glow. The sphere of order dissipated, and the land’s fluid rules returned—the familiar, comfortable uncertainty.

But something was different. The air held a new, faint harmonic. The darkness seemed a fraction less absolute, as if holding the memory of that blue. And in the center of the K’tharn’s central ruin, now a temple of soft dripping water and pulsing gardens, the Seed rested on its clay pedestal.

The eldest Fold-Singer stood beside it, its light-patterns now permanently graced with soft blue spirals amid the familiar greens and purples. It placed a long, multi-jointed hand near the orb, not touching it, feeling the profound, quiet hum of potential within.

The benevolent power had done its work. It had not healed the wound. It had simply helped the scar learn to sing. It had given the children of the shearing not a cure for their world, but a purpose for their unique existence: to become stewards of connection, gardeners of a gateway, and silent guides for other lost, beautiful things learning to walk in their own dark.

Now, they had a song to sing into the future, a slow, patient song that would, over epochs, reach other ears in the deep and dreaming dark.
