The attention came not as a light, but as a change in the taste of the water.

It began in the deepest pool of the central chamber, a pool that had always been still, its surface a perfect, black mirror to the moss-dappled ceiling. For generations of Mycorrh, it had been a place of quiet integration, where the oldest weavers would stand and impart the long memories of the warren’s first growth. The water there was rich with the history of their kind.

The change was a shift in salinity, so slight that only the most sensitive of the elder tenders, a being whose root-feet had been fused to the pool’s rim for seventy growth-rings, perceived it first. It was a new mineral signature, complex and profound, carrying the faint, impossible taste of stone that had never wept, of pressure from depths below even the rift, of a cold that was not damp but absolute and crystalline. The elder’s smooth face tilted down. It did not move, but its awareness, usually diffused across acres of root-mesh, drew in and focused on the pool.

Then, the drips from the ceiling changed.

Throughout the chamber, the soft, irregular *plink* of condensation began to align. Drop by drop, they fell into a rhythm—not a simple beat, but a polyrhythm, a cascading pattern that resonated through the water in the pools and the saturated air. It was a vibration that spoke to the seismic sense in every Mycorrh foot. It was not a language. It was a *condition*, a state of being expressed through falling water.

Every Mycorrh in the central chamber stilled. Their slow, guiding motions ceased. The fluttering amphibians settled on the roots. The eels paused in their channels. The warren held its breath.

The pool in the center began to glow. Not with the sickly green of the moss, but with a soft, blue-white luminescence from its depths, as if it had swallowed a piece of the distant, unseen sky. The light was cold and clear, and it illuminated the chamber in a new way: it did not cling to surfaces, but seemed to fill the spaces between them, defining volume and emptiness for the first time.

From the luminous water, a form coalesced. It was not a shape of flesh or root, but of water itself given temporary intention—a column of liquid that rose, reflecting the blue light within and the green moss without, its surface a constantly shifting tapestry of ripple and flow. It had no features, no limbs. It was simply *presence*, a density of attention made manifest in the medium they knew best.

The elder Mycorrh at the pool’s edge did not retreat. It extended one cluster of fine rootlets from its hand, dipping the tips into the water of the pool, now alive with inner light. It was an act of communion, of tasting.

What it received was an imparting vaster than any in their history.

It was not a stream of specific images or commands. It was an imprint of *context*. The Mycorrh, whose world was the rift and the root, suddenly *felt* the continent above them—not as a barrier, but as a living, breathing skin. They felt the slow grind of tectonic plates, the patient journey of deep aquifers, the immense, rhythmic pulse of the planet’s own heat bleeding into the cold crust. They felt the warren not as an end, but as a node in an immeasurably vast, slow network. They felt the other places—cities of fire and wind, deserts of singing crystal, forests of conscious light—all different, all essential. They felt the balance.

The imparting carried a soft, profound affection. It was the feeling of a gardener noticing a new, promising symbiosis beginning in a forgotten corner of the garden. It was the acknowledgment: *You are good. What you are doing is good. Continue.*

And then, it offered the gift.

The luminous column bent, and from its tip, a single, perfect droplet fell. It did not fall into the pool. It fell onto the largest, central structural root that arched over the chamber—a root that had been the first grown, the anchor of all their civilization.

Where it struck the bark-like skin, there was no splash. The droplet was absorbed. A ripple of the blue-white light spread through the root, fleeting, then gone.

The column of water subsided, melting back into the pool. The blue light faded from the depths. The drips from the ceiling returned to their random, gentle pattern. The taste of impossible minerals lingered, then slowly dissolved into the familiar peat and fungus.

Silence returned, heavier now, charged with meaning.

For a long time, nothing happened. The Mycorrh stood, immersed in the aftertaste of the imparting. The concept of a “benevolent and wise power” had no frame in their minds. They had met a *condition* of the world, a deeper layer of the wet and the stone they had always known. It was not a god; it was a fact, newly perceived.

Then, days later, the root began to grow.

From the spot where the droplet had fallen, a new shoot emerged. It did not look like the other roots. Its bark was darker, almost black, and perfectly smooth. It grew not with the blind, searching urgency of the rhizomes, but with a deliberate, spiraling grace. As it grew, it began to hum—a low, foundational vibration that resonated through the entire warren, a constant, sub-audible note.

The Mycorrh gathered. They touched it. They tasted the water that beaded on its surface. This new root was not just structure. It was an organ of perception. Its vibration changed with the subtle flexing of the continental plate above. Its sap, when carefully tasted by the weavers, carried information about the chemistry of deep-earth flows, predicting shifts in mineral seepage long before they reached the warren.

The gift was not technology, nor was it a change in their nature. It was a lens. It was a tuning fork.

Their purpose of “continuing” was not altered, but it was deepened. Before, they had sought to fill the rift. Now, they understood the rift’s place in a greater body. Their guiding of growth became more nuanced. They began to grow new tunnels not just towards water, but towards points of geological calm, or to gently brace against impending shifts in the stone. They started cultivating mosses that responded to specific deep-earth frequencies, creating a living map of subterranean pressures across their ceiling.

The central chamber, with the singing root at its heart, became their silent sanctuary. The elder who had first tasted the change now stood permanently at the pool’s edge, one hand on the new root, its body a conduit between the warren’s slow thought and the planet’s slower song. The imparting rituals between Mycorrh now carried traces of that vast context—a hint of tectonic patience in a memory, a subtle acknowledgement of balance in a growth directive.

The benevolent power had done nothing but notice them, and offer a single drop of deeper knowing. It asked for no worship, demanded no change. It had simply widened the world.

And the Drowned Warrens, the city of water and root, continued to grow. But now, as its roots infiltrated the dark, they did so with a new, silent harmony. They were not just becoming the living stone. They were learning to listen to the heart of the stone, and in their slow, saturated way, they had begun to sing it back.
