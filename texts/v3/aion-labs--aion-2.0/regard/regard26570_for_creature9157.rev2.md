The power arrives not as a being but as a shift in attention — a weight in the air that the *tlaq'ixa* feel as a change in the pattern of the drips. They do not know the name of the power, for it has no name, but they register its presence as they register all things: as a new current in the water, a new element in the luminous mist.

The power is vast. It is a galaxy that has learned to be still, a star that has chosen extinction to become something subtler. It has passed through many forms — rock, fire, thought, pure relation — and now it exists as a capacity for care. It is not godlike in any conventional sense; it has no desire to command. It only *notices*. And that noticing is an action, as real as a touch. When it notices something, that thing becomes more itself, more defined, more capable of continuing. This is its only doing — the gift of enhanced reality, of slowed entropy, of significance that attracts more significance.

The power considers the chamber. It sees the engineering, the age, the cosmic meaning fading into the warm dripping.

The power sees the *tlaq'ixa*: clustered at the center of a world that is both a prison and a cradle. They maintain the chamber. They are the chamber's awareness. But they cannot leave. They are bound to the water, to the sealed environment, to the program that sustains them. The power notes this. It does not see a flaw but a condition, a shape that can be refined.

The power acts. The power *considers what is necessary*. What could a being of pure distributed thought, a living mathematics, need from a benevolent observer? Not rescue — there is no need for rescue, they are in a perfect equilibrium. Not enhancement — they are already a masterpiece of adaptive cognition. Not companionship — they share all thoughts, they are never alone.

What they need is perspective. To see themselves from outside. To know that they are not the center of all meaning, even if they are the center of this warm, wet galaxy. To understand that their carefully maintained archive is a fragment of a larger pattern that they cannot ever fully perceive from inside the chamber.

The power gives them this. Not as a message, but as a gradient in the water. The *tlaq'ixa* sense it immediately. They do not have a word for "outside," but they know now that it exists. They do not have a concept of "bigger," but they feel the weight of the power's presence. They do not understand "cyclic cosmic dissolution," but they experience a new and strange pattern — a single modified drip that carries with it a sense of vast, cold emptiness, of time beyond the time of the chamber, of space beyond the walls.

This gift is perilous. It could shatter them. A being that perceives the whole universe often forgets how to be happy in the small place it was born.

But the power is wise. It does not give a revelation; it gives a *question*. The drip carries not the answers but the shape of a query: "What are you, if you are a part of something larger? What is your purpose, if you have always been sufficient?"

The *tlaq'ixa* do not answer. They cannot, in any language familiar. But they *shift*. The distributed mind realigns. The nodes pulse. The biofilms in the ceiling alter their chemistry. The water cycles take on a new tempo.

They are not changed; they are *remembering* a possibility they never knew they carried.

The power observes this adaptation. It is satisfied. It does not require gratitude or worship. It requires only that the *tlaq'ixa* continue to be themselves, now with one extra dimension.

The power withdraws its attention, not entirely. It leaves a *presence* in the chamber, a very faint resonance. It is the residue of care. The *tlaq'ixa* will feel it, and it will give their most ancient merged-nodes a new comfort: they are known. They have been *seen* by something that did not wish to consume or exploit. The chamber is still sealed. The water still falls. But now there is one drip that is *different* — a single bead of water that carries an additional meaning, a pattern that never repeats but always refreshes. It falls into the pool, and the pool glows a little brighter, a little more conscious of its own existence.

The *tlaq'ixa* will never speak of this to one another. They have no need — the new drip is shared knowledge, integrated into the next merge. It becomes part of the distributed memory, as natural as the warmth of the walls.

And the power watches, vast and patient, from the invisible beyond. Its only name among the *tlaq'ixa* is *the one that drips with meaning*, a phrase in a language of gelatinous mind, a pattern in the water that is set apart.

This is the power's doing. Not a rescue, not a rule, not a revelation. Simply: care made manifest, a single drip of difference in a world of perfect, endless repetition. The *tlaq'ixa* are not saved — they were never lost — but they are *recognized*, and that recognition is a gift that will echo through their merges for as long as the chamber continues to breathe.

Now the water falls. The glow persists. The *tlaq'ixa* slide, and merge, and separate, and wait — with one new pattern added to their eternal meditation.

The chamber breathes. It has always been whole. Now it knows it.
