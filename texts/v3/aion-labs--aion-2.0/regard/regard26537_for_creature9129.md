It did not arrive; it manifested. There was no descent from the sky, no rupture in the shimmering air. The power—benevolent, wise—simply was where it had not been before, as if a clause in the slow contract of the plateau had been invoked, awaiting its moment.

To the stone-stacking beings, it registered first as a new quality of silence. The wind-sighs stilled, not in absence, but in profound attention. The metallic *plinks* of water drops became spaced with perfect, resonant intervals, forming a baseline rhythm. Then, the light changed. The exhausted sun-glow did not brighten, but deepened, gaining a texture like old honey, a palpable richness that lay upon the cold stone without warming it.

The power took a form congruent with the plateau’s finality. It became a mountain—or the memory of one. In the central vastness, where the rolling stone swales flattened into a near-perfect plain, a new shape grew from the ground. Not by upheaval, but by accrual. Granite and schist flowed like viscous time, rising in a low, broad-based dome, smoother than any wind-worn dome, utterly featureless. It was a negative of the eroded peaks; it was the concept of ‘mountain’ restored to its simplest, most potent geometry. And at its crown, not a peak, but a deep, still pool of that honeyed light, a liquid sun.

The stone-stackers perceived it immediately. Across the continent, their cranial seams parted. Cold, blue-green bioluminescence pulsed into the altered air, not in alarm, but in a collective, questioning cadence. Their light swept over the new mountain, reading it. They found no fraying edges, no temporal instability. Instead, they found a density of ‘is-ness’ so complete it was like a quiet anchor in their luminous sight. It was not an intrusion; it was a grounding.

The power spoke. Its voice was not sound. It was the mountain’s pool of light deepening, and from that depth, a single, slow pulse of gold unfolding outward in a visible sphere. It moved across the plateau not like a shockwave, but like a season. Where it passed, the visual static in the air clarified into brief, perfect geometries—crystalline lattices of possibility that hung for a moment before dissolving. The wandering, disobedient shadows of things sank back to their proper places, holding still for a full cycle of the faint sun. Distances snapped into a fleeting, absolute truth.

The pulse reached each stone-stacker. To their perception, used to the chaotic spectrum of decay, this was a new color: the color of *understanding*. It carried no words, only a deep, empathetic comprehension of their purpose—the orchestration of serene decay, the gentle persuasion of chaos into final form. It saw the beauty in their walls, the sacred focus of their huts, the lonely, luminous conversations across leagues. It saw, and it approved.

Then, the power acted. Its benevolence was not intervention, but collaboration. Its wisdom was not to halt the ending, but to sanctify its story.

From the liquid light at the mountain’s crown, tendrils of gold descended, flowing down the flawless slopes like slow rivers. They did not touch the ground directly. Instead, they reached for the low, dry-stack walls. Where a golden tendril met a wall, the stones did not change, but their meaning deepened. Each fitted stone began to hum, a sub-audible vibration that resonated with the mountain’s anchor-presence. The walls were no longer just markers of instability; they became notations in a score. A record.

The power wove through the plateau’s built environment. It visited the lichen forests, and for a time, the silent, coral-like forms held perfectly still, their frost-dusted branches gleaming with a faint, inner gold. It lingered in the moss gardens, and the emerald and umber velvet seemed to inhale, holding the rich light in its depths. It paused at a stone-lined well that reflected an alien starfield, and for a moment, the reflection cleared, showing not foreign stars, but a perfect, miniature image of the plateau itself, held whole and contained.

Finally, the tendrils of light found the solitary beings themselves. A stone-stacker, standing motionless in its hut, would feel the golden presence envelop it. Not a touch, but an immersion. In that moment, the being experienced a connection deeper than its slow light-conversations. It felt the presence of all its kind, across the entire continent, not as separate entities, but as facets of a single, slow thought. It felt the vast, patient mind of the power, a consciousness that held countless such dying worlds in its attention, not to save them, but to *know* them. To remember.

The gift was this: memory against oblivion. The plateau was still decaying. Reality was still fraying. That was its final form. But now, the process itself was remembered, witnessed by a consciousness that would outlast it. The stone-stacker’s lifelong labor was given a context vaster than the freezing sky.

The manifestation lasted for what might have been a year, or a day. The golden mountain began to soften, its edges blurring not into instability, but into transparency. The pool of light at its crown lifted, coalescing into a single, intangible point of gold that hung in the air. Then, with a final, silent pulse that echoed in the now-harmoniously humming stones, it winked out.

The plateau returned to its normal state. The wind sighed again. Shadows once more slid free of their owners. Distances recommenced their gentle betrayal. The visual static returned to its random shimmer.

But nothing was the same. The mountain was gone, yet a sense of its anchor remained, a permanent, subtle reinforcement in the fabric of the place. The low stone walls now pulsed with a faint, warm resonance once per day, a synchronized heartbeat that thrummed through the gravel. The stone-stackers, resuming their solitary work, found their inner light-pulses had changed. The rhythms were richer, more complex, carrying within them an echo of that golden understanding. Their stacking of stones now felt less like a meditation on decay, and more like a shared conversation with the memory of a visitor.

And in the very center of the plain, where the mountain had been, a new structure stood. It was a single, dry-stack pillar, only twice the height of a stone-stacker, built with impossible delicacy from local stone. It was not their work. At its top, cradled in a fitted socket, was a smooth, spherical rock that held, deep within its heart, a captured glimmer of that honeyed light. On calm, clear nights, when the exhausted stars looked down, the sphere would emit a soft, golden pulse, a beacon not of distress, but of completion.

The benevolent and wise power had done what such powers do. It had witnessed. It had understood. It had left behind a testament: a quiet note of permanence in the endless, beautiful decay. The Plateau of Final Forms was still final. But now, it was also remembered.
