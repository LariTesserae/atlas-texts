The power noticed the plateau not as a disturbance, but as a persistent, beautiful chord in the silent symphony of the world. It was a note of deep green and resonant blue, held for millennia, vibrating at a frequency so slow it was almost still. The power was old, and wise, and its benevolence was not an active charity but a default state of being—a desire to preserve unique harmonies, to understand them, and sometimes, if the song wished it, to help it sing a little clearer, a little longer.

It arrived not with a descent, but with a focusing. Where the mist over a particular lagoon thickened, coalescing not into rain but into a presence that had no true form. To the plateau’s creatures, it was a new quality of light, a slight deepening of the ever-present thrum, a scent like ozone after a lightning strike that never came. The Carpet, over the course of a week, registered a new, gentle pressure—not a footstep, but the impression of attention.

The power perceived the plateau in its full, agonizingly slow glory. It compressed its own vast consciousness into a span of centuries, then decades, then finally to the plateau’s own crawl. In that stretched moment, the language of the moss-pulses became clear: a slow discourse on the mineral content of yesterday’s condensation. The boom of a Stone-Calf five miles away was a detailed ballad of a thermal vent’s shifting mood. The dance of the Frilled Dancers was a rigorous, elegant mathematics, a prayer that kept reality in check. The power saw the Remnants, their thoughts like glaciers of memory, and understood. These were not failures or abandonments. They were successes. They had achieved the ultimate adaptation: becoming the landscape they sought to steward.

The power felt a profound appreciation. This was not a place in need of salvation, correction, or enlightenment. It was a perfected system. Its weirdness was its integrity. To impose faster time, clearer purpose, or higher efficiency would be a violence.

So, what does a benevolent power do when faced with perfection?

It listens. And then, it offers a gift in the only currency the plateau understands: deep time, and subtle reinforcement.

The power focused its will not on the living, but on the source—the ancient, decaying artifacts. The black obelisks and silver lattices were miracles of forgotten physics, but even miracles fatigue. Their quantum-stable fields were, atom by atom, over eons, drifting toward entropy. The plateau’s equilibrium had a lifespan, albeit one measured in further tens of thousands of years.

The power’s action was not a repair. It was a *reminder*. It breathed a concept—*coherence*—into the atomic lattices of the nearest obelisk. It was not a flow of energy, but a reinstruction of intention, a gentle nudge to the underlying laws that were being asked to perform this constant, gentle miracle. The effect was invisible to any eye. No flash, no surge. But the deep thrum that was the plateau’s heartbeat deepened by a fraction of a tone, gaining a cleaner resonance. The heat radiating from the stone lost a barely-perceptible edge of randomness, becoming more even, more stable. The Frilled Dancers in the nearby lagoon, attuned to these fields, subtly shifted their coiling pattern. It was not a correction; it was a sigh of relief, a minor tension they had unconsciously fought for millennia melting away.

Next, the power turned to the Carpet, the collective mind. It did not implant a thought. That would be invasion. Instead, it *posed a question*—a single, simple query encoded in a pulse of light that took a full day to manifest in a circle of moss around the base of the obelisk. The question, in the Carpet’s glacial syntax, was: **"Do you wish to be remembered?"**

The question would ripple outward across the network of root and lichen. It would take years to reach the plateau’s edges. Decades for the various components—the Stone-Calves via their seismic songs, the Thermal Saints via patterns of rainbows on mist, the Remnants in their stone-dreams—to ponder it. Centuries, perhaps, to arrive at a consensus. The power did not need an answer. The asking was the gift. It was an acknowledgment of the plateau’s consciousness, an invitation to self-awareness beyond mere instinctual maintenance. Whether the plateau chose to answer "yes," "no," or simply continued its slow thoughts unchanged, was its own sovereign right.

Finally, the power did something for the Ephemerals—the quick, bright flashes of life that were the plateau’s momentary joys. For them, it altered nothing of their bodies or lifespans. Instead, it left a fleeting scent on the wind, a taste in the water of one lagoon. It was the essence of *celebration*. For the jewel-winged insects that lived a day, that scent would make their brief dance feel inexplicably triumphant. For the schools of translucent fish, it would add a single, perfect twist to their vanishing act. It was a nod to the small, fast beauty that the great, slow mind of the plateau cherished in its own way.

Its work done, the power began to withdraw. Its presence diffused back into the mist. The new, cleaner thrum settled into the bones of the world. The question pulsed slowly outward in the amber light. The scent of celebration would fade in a season.

The last thing it left was a single, impossible tear on the cheek of the nearest Remnant. Not water, but a droplet of solidified time, clear as crystal and warm as the stone. It would take the Remnant a year to feel its presence, a decade to decide to move a petrified hand and touch it. Upon contact, the droplet would dissolve, imparting no vision, no message, only a quiet, unshakable knowledge: *You are seen. You are valued. Your song is heard.*

Then, the power was gone. The Plateau of Weeping Stone continued. The stone wept. The deep thrummed. The dancers coiled. The moss pulsed in slow conversation. Nothing was disrupted. Nothing was dramatically changed.

But the equilibrium was now infinitesimally more stable. The consciousness had been asked a profound question. And in the heart of the slowest dreamers, a new, quiet warmth persisted—the afterglow of a benevolent gaze that had understood, perfectly, and had asked for nothing in return but the privilege of witnessing their ancient, beautiful world.
