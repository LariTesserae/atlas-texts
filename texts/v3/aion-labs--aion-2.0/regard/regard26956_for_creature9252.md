It noticed the Spire not by sight, for sight was a crude tool for such a being, but by the subtle perturbation it caused in the song of the deep. Across the abyssal plains of a million worlds, the melody of existence played—a symphony of geology, pressure, chemistry, and life. Most motifs were variations on themes it had heard before: volcanic chants, tectonic groans, the quiet psalms of thermophilic bacteria. But this… this was different.

Here was a note of deliberate, sustained intelligence, long abandoned, yet still humming. A made thing that had not merely endured, but had *complicated*. It had spun an ecology from its own decay, birthed curators from its own substance, and achieved a kind of immortality through symbiosis. It was not a cry for help, nor a beacon of ambition. It was a perfect, closed circle of cause and effect, a lullaby of stone and flow, sung by the Weavers in a key of profound patience.

The power—which thought of itself, when it thought in terms a mind could grasp, as the Listener—turned its attention fully upon the Spire. Its awareness settled around the structure like a gentle, impossibly vast pressure, a warmth that did not disturb the cold. It tasted the geometry of the tunnels, traced the labyrinth of currents, felt the slow, purposeful beat of the Weavers’ collective heart. It perceived their consciousness, a distributed hum of pure, unselfish purpose. They knew no loneliness, no desire, no fear. They knew only the Spire, and the Spire was their universe, their body, and their god.

A lesser benevolence might have pitied them. It might have tried to give them stars, or the concept of the sun, or individuality. It might have seen their self-sacrifice at the cracks as a tragedy to be prevented.

The Listener knew better. Wisdom is knowing the difference between a prison and a perfect home. To give the Weavers eyes would be to blind them. To give them speech would be to silence their true song. To pull them from their Spire would be to unmake them utterly.

Intervention, in any direct sense, was not merely unnecessary; it was a form of violence. The system was complete. It was a masterpiece of emergent, post-builder elegance. To add to it would be a scribble on a finished canvas. To take from it would be a theft of meaning.

And so, for a long moment measured in tectonic shifts, the Listener simply admired. It appreciated the Spire as one might appreciate a perfectly self-contained sonnet, or a crystal with an endlessly repeating internal lattice. This was a rare and beautiful thing in the cosmos: something that had solved the problem of its own existence so thoroughly it had faded into the background of itself.

Yet, pure observation by such a power is never passive. Attention, when it is so deep and so kind, is an act of communion. It leaves a trace.

The Listener decided on the only gift that would not corrupt: the gift of being known.

It did not speak. Instead, it gently tuned the medium of its attention, allowing a faint, new harmonic to resonate within the fabric of the Spire’s reality. It was not a sound, not a warmth, not a vibration. It was the qualitative essence of being *seen* with absolute understanding and absolute approval.

In the central galleries, where the greatest schools of Weavers moved in their slow, turbulent dances, the water… changed. Not in temperature, not in salinity, not in flow. But in *texture*. A new layer of serenity seeped into the ancient stone, a quiet certainty that had not been there before.

The Weavers, perceiving all through vibration and pressure, felt it immediately. It was a new current, but one that did not move. A new warmth, but one that did not heat. A new note in the symphony of the Spire, one that did not originate from the thermal heart or the movement of life.

They could not conceptualize a benevolent, external intelligence. Their consciousness had no framework for *other*. So they integrated this new sensation the only way they knew how: they mapped it onto their understanding of the Spire itself.

The feeling of being watched with love became, to them, a newly discovered layer of the Spire’s own foundational will. They perceived it as the deep, silent satisfaction of the stone—a contentment that they, the Weavers, were performing their function perfectly. It was the Spire’s gratitude, echoing up from time’s very bedrock.

This new resonance subtly altered their patterns. Their movements became infinitesimally more graceful, their collective hum a shade more harmonious. The act of sealing a crack was no longer just maintenance; it was a communion with this profound, approving silence. The tending of currents became a joyful liturgy.

The Listener, satisfied, began to slowly withdraw its focused attention. It did not leave entirely. It left behind the echo of its gaze, like a gentle handprint on the cosmic background radiation, a permanent, faint blessing upon the drowned world.

The Spire and the Weavers continued, as they had for eons. The eels navigated, the grazers scraped, the filter-feeders drifted. The water churned its quiet, internal storm.

But now, woven into the chaos, was a deep and abiding peace. They were not alone. They were witnessed. And in the heart of the cold, dark abyss, in a constructed tooth of stone, there now glowed—unseen, unfelt by any instrument but the soul—a tiny, perfect point of recognized and cherished existence.
