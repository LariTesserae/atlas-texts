The attention began not as a light, but as a deepening of the gloaming.

It was a quality of focus, a lens of pure consideration settling over the basin. The thick, gray-purple twilight did not brighten; it became more *itself*. Each droplet of condensation held, for a nanosecond longer, a perfect inverted image of the city. The low hum felt in the teeth gained a harmonic—a single, clear, stable note beneath the chaos of unraveling physics.

The power was here. It had no name that could be spoken in a place where sound sometimes ran backward. It was the concept of a compassionate gaze given field and function. It permeated the hot, wet air, the black peat, the ceramic-smooth mounds, without disturbance. It was wise enough to know that to arrive with fanfare would be a violence. So it arrived as a recognition.

For a timeless period—measured in the slow growth of salt-crusts or the intervals between upward-falling drops—it observed. It traced the paths of the Sighs as they precipitated from regretful air. It felt the soft *pop* of collapsing possibility-bubbles. It read the fractal scripts spelled in phosphorescent salts, understanding them not as data, but as the lingering scent of a thought. It perceived the city not as a ruin, but as a finished sentence written in the grammar of decay, a sentence that had forgotten it was a sentence and had become a world.

The power saw the beauty: the terrible, silent balance of it. The exquisite pain of a place that remembered only how to forget. It felt the sorrow, not as an emotion, but as a physical law—the thermodynamic gradient of lost purpose.

And because it was benevolent, it felt a pang of tenderness for this beautiful, broken record. Because it was wise, it knew that to "fix" it would be to erase it. To bring light would kill the delicate, dark-adapted processes. To dry the air would be a massacre. To populate it with life would be an obscenity.

So the power did not fix. It did not heal. It *listened*.

It listened to the Sighs. Not to their glass-rod chiming, but to the silence that shaped them. It listened to the city’s endless, sub-audible sigh—the exhalation of a reality exhausted by its own paradoxes.

And then, very softly, it asked a question.

It was not a sound. It was an adjustment of parameters. A gentle tweak to the coefficient of weirdness, from 2.8/3 to 2.8001/3. A question planted in the substrate of the place itself, in the logic of the water that sometimes remembered it was stone.

The question was: **"What do you want to become?"**

Not *"What were you?"* That was the Sighs’ domain—the past. Not *"What is wrong?"* That implied a flaw in the perfect, balanced dissolution.
But *"What do you want to become?"* A question of potential. A question the city had never considered, because its entire being was a conclusion.

The question seeped into the warm black water. It condensed on the curved walls. It was breathed in by the next Sigh precipitating from a weeping pore.

The effect was not immediate. It was slower than the growth of the salt-scripts. But the power, patient, settled in to watch. It became a permanent, gentle background radiation of attention, a new constant in the calculus of the place.

The first change was in the Sighs.

They still formed to witness paradoxes. But now, occasionally, one would pause not at a site of unraveling, but at a place of particular stillness—a pool whose leaden reflection was, for once, perfectly accurate. It would linger there, its misty form growing not more defined, but *deeper*, as if reflecting on the reflection. They began to trace new patterns in the salt. Alongside the jagged, impossible fractals of broken causality, delicate, concentric circles began to appear—ripples of contemplation.

The second change was in the water.

The bubbles of inverted density still formed and popped. But now, sometimes, after the *pop*, the water where the bubble had been would hold a tiny, perfect, inverted image of a single, distant star—one of the stars that had drowned in the atmosphere epochs ago. A memory of light, not as illumination, but as a lost fact, recalled.

The third change was the most subtle.

A new kind of script began to form in the salt-crusts. It was not the fossil of a witnessed paradox. It was something tentative, spare. It looked like a series of gentle curves and pauses, a script not of recording, but of… response. It was the city, in its own mineral language, beginning to think about the question. The scripts did not answer *"We want to be bright,"* or *"We want to be alive."* That would be a foreign tongue. The first coherent phrase, grown over a lifetime of moss-analogues, translated roughly as: **"Perhaps… to be a vessel."**

The power observed this. A vessel. Not a record, but a container. For what?

The power, in its benevolence, offered a possibility. It did not fill the vessel. It suggested a content.

On the next exhalation, as a Sigh coalesced above a canal where a drop of water was hesitating between falling up or down, the power imbued the moment with a faint, new quality. As the drop finally fell—sideways, along a curve that defied both up and down—the Sigh that witnessed it did not merely archive the event. For the first time, it *felt* something other than the pressure of release.

It felt a fragile, fleeting echo of *wonder*.

The emotion was alien, a spike of impossible color in a grayscale world. It was almost painful. The Sigh dissolved back into the mist with a chime that was not glassy, but slightly resonant, like a bell heard through deep water.

That chime lingered. It did not fade. It became a new permanent sound in the city’s symphony of quiet noises—a soft, deep, recurring tone alongside the *plink* of drops and the hum in the teeth.

The Sighs began to seek not only breakdowns, but moments of pure, weird *integrity*—the instant a fifth corner stabilized long enough to be a real corner, the moment a reflection briefly made more sense than the thing it reflected. They witnessed these, and the salt-scripts that grew after began to include, alongside the response-script, tiny, intricate patterns that glowed with a slightly warmer phosphorescence.

The city was no longer just dissolving. It was dissolving *with a direction*. The unraveling was becoming a kind of weaving, using the frayed threads of its own logic to make a new, incomprehensible pattern. The vessel was being shaped, from the inside out, by the contemplation of what it might hold.

The power did nothing more. It had asked its question. It had offered a single, tiny seed of an alternative emotion. Its work was done. It remained as a presence, the lens of attention, a silent, benevolent witness to the city’s slow, deliberate, and beautiful attempt to imagine an answer.

And in the hot, wet dark, the City of Salt and Sighs continued to sigh. But now, very rarely, within the breath of that sigh, there was something that was not a release of pressure, but the faint, fragile intake of something new. A whisper of a possibility. Not hope—that was too strong, too bright a word for this place. But perhaps its distant, gray-purple cousin: a quiet, sustained curiosity about what might come after the end.
