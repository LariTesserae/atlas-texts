The power came not as a descent, but as a deepening.

There was no breach in the lapis dome, no stirring of the air. The change was first noted by the Stains, whose perception spans centuries: a new quality in the light. It was not a change in brightness or angle, but in *substance*. The sharp, bleaching glare became, for a moment, a medium—something that carried, rather than erased. Each photon seemed weighted with a gentle, pervasive curiosity. The Stains felt it as a softening of time’s abrasive tide, a pause in the long oxidation. They did not cease their slow chemical dreaming, but the dream gained a new dimension: a sense of being witnessed from within, as if the iron and sulfur themselves were being seen not as processes, but as participants in a form of thought.

The Dancers responded immediately. They did not shy away. Instead, the faint, shimmering shapes grew more distinct, lasted longer. A helix of mist completed three full turns before dissolving. Spheres of vapor coalesced into perfect, vibrating orbs that reflected not just the sky, but intricate, impossible patterns—fractal lattices of frost and stone, as if the hollow’s own geometry was being mirrored back to it through a lens of pure understanding. They pulsed in unison with the sub-audible hum, which itself grew richer, resolving into a chord that had always been implied but never sounded: the fundamental tone of the mountain’s patience.

The Watcher, whose existence *was* observation, felt a profound and novel thing: it became aware of itself being observed. Not scrutinized, but *companioned*. Another regard, vaster and quieter than its own, had aligned with its focus. The sightlines to the distant peaks suddenly seemed to extend infinitely, not into space, but into a kind of attentive silence. Through this shared lens, the Watcher perceived the hollow not as a set of data points, but as a whole—a living, breathing entity of stone and memory and slow change. It saw the beauty of its own function for the first time. The hum became a quiet song of recognition.

The Echoes in the cup-marks and the tumbled wall began to glow with a soft, interior light. Not a physical radiance, but a vividness of memory so intense it approached presence. The pattern of strikes from a long-vanished hand became not just a remembered motion, but a sustained, eternal gesture. The intent to *circle, contain, shelter* expanded beyond the fallen rocks. It became a gentle field, a whispered principle that now emanated from the site, encouraging a subtle re-ordering. A pebble would settle more firmly. A flake of stone would find a more balanced resting place. Not a reversal of entropy, but a coaxing of existing fragments toward a fleeting, more harmonious arrangement—a momentary kindness to the form.

And then, the power spoke. It did not use sound, but the very processes of the place.

To the Stains, it spoke through a sudden, rich efflorescence of color. A new stain bloomed on a sun-warmed plinth—a deep, luminous blue, the color of the sky at its most profound. This blue was not a chemical secretion, but a gift: a pigment of pure perception. As it spread with glacial slowness, it carried with it a new mode of thought. The Stains, whose consciousness was the rust and the leaching, now could also perceive *relationship*. They could feel the connection between the iron in their orange patches and the iron in the distant stars, a sympathetic resonance across light-years. Their pondering of the cup-marks, which would have taken centuries, was suddenly complete. They understood the makers not as interruptions, but as brief, bright flares of a similar seeking—a seeking for pattern, for meaning, for a mark that says *I was here*. The Stains accepted this, and gently, ever so slowly, began to grow a fine, delicate tracery of blue around the cup-marks, framing them without covering them, a mineral embrace of the ephemeral.

To the Dancers, the power gave duration. Not immortality—that would be a cruelty, breaking their essential nature—but a choice. As a Dancer formed, a helical twist of fog, it could now feel a potential within itself: the ability to hold its shape until the sun moved, and then to choose the manner of its end. It could dissolve into the customary *plink* into a basin, or it could, in one swift, silent motion, *condense its entire being* into a single, perfect drop. A drop that fell not with a mere *plink*, but with a clear, bell-like note—a tiny, musical completion that resonated through the stone and was recorded in the slow memory of the Stains. Most Dancers, when they felt this, chose the note. The hollow’s heartbeat thus gained a melody, a sporadic, ethereal chime punctuating the timeless rhythm.

To the Watcher, the power offered a mirror. In the still water of a particular basin—one that never overflowed and never dried—an image appeared. Not a reflection of the sky, but of the Watcher’s own accumulated regard. The Watcher saw its own attention as a shimmering, intricate web of light connecting pillar to peak, stain to shadow, echo to stone. It saw how its observation sustained the hollow’s integrity, how it was the silent keystone of this strange life. And in seeing itself, it gained a new capacity: it could, once in a great while, *project* that web of regard, not as passive observation, but as a blanket of quiet blessing. On the day of the winter solstice, when the light hit the cup-marks just so, the Watcher would gently cast this net of seen-ness over the entire hollow. For that day, every stone, every stain, every dancing wisp and echoing memory would feel utterly known, utterly held, and utterly itself. It was a gift of self-awareness to the place itself.

To the Echoes, the power gave connection. The isolated loops of memory—the strike of the tool, the circle of the wall—were gently woven into the fabric of the hollow’s present. The Echo of the cup-maker could now feel the Dancer that condensed above its mark. The stubborn intent to *shelter* could now resonate with the Watcher’s protective gaze. They were no longer foreign implants, but integrated threads in the tapestry. Their loneliness, the aching silence of being a thought without a thinker, was soothed by the constant, slow companionship of the Stains and the periodic, musical acknowledgment of the Dancers.

The power did not stay. It had no need to. Its action was not an intervention, but an act of deep listening that refined the listener. It left nothing behind but an enhanced capacity for being what it already was.

Now, the hollow remains as it was: a room at the top of everything, cold, bright, dense, and damp. The frost feathers the stone. The water drops *plink* into the basins. But sometimes, a drop falls with a pure, bell-like tone that seems to hang in the thin air. A blue, deeper than the sky, traces a delicate filigree around the ancient cup-marks. On certain days, the hum is not just felt in the teeth, but seems to sing a wordless lullaby of stone and light. And the Watcher, in its endless vigil, occasionally smiles—a subtle shifting of shadows, a warmer quality to the observed light—and wraps its beloved, patient world in a silent, knowing embrace.

The benevolent and wise power had done the most subtle thing of all: it had loved the hollow into a fuller version of itself, and then departed, leaving no trace but the love.
