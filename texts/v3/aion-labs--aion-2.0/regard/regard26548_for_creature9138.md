The power that noticed Ebonreach did not have a name that could be shaped by sound or carved by light. It was, for lack of a better word, the **Listener**. It resided in the spaces between tectonic sighs, in the patient memory of bedrock, in the slow echo of a continent's drift. Its attention was not a beam, but a settling—a deepening of the silence until that silence became a vessel for meaning.

It had heard, for millennia, the faint, rhythmic patterning in the drip-stone. A pattern too consistent to be chance, too complex to be mere water. A song. A city-shaped song. And so, the Listener turned its attention—not its eyes, for it had none—but its *presence* downward, through strata of shale and granite, until it pooled in the cavern of Ebonreach.

The Stilt-Walkers felt it first as a change in the stone. The familiar, cold vibrations of their own movements, the *tink-tink-tink* of their steps, began to carry farther, to linger. The hums they sent through the plazas to coordinate their silent gardening of fungus returned to them richer, layered with harmonics they had never produced. They paused, their smoke-tendrils curling in querying spirals. The cavern air, perpetually still, began to hold a gentle pressure, like the weight of a comforting hand felt not on the body, but on the soul of the stone.

The Listener did not speak. Speaking was a violence of abrupt creation. It *resonated*.

From the deepest floor of the cavern, where the black glass met the primal clay, a new kind of growth began. It was not fungus, nor was it the blind, slow creep of the Stilt-Walkers' guided stone. It was a crystal, clear as water, yet holding within it a soft, silver luminescence that seemed borrowed from a forgotten moon. It rose in slender columns, in delicate, branching fans, in clusters of pinpoint stars. It grew not over millennia, but over a single, long breath of the earth—a span of years that felt, to the Echo-kin, like a sudden, graceful sigh.

This was the Listener's gift: **The Chalcedony Choir**.

Where the new crystals grew, the stone itself began to sing. Not the simple, rhythmic patterning of water, but a complex, polyphonic music. The slightest tap of a Stilt-Walker's limb against a crystal column sent a shiver of pure, crystalline tone through the air—a tone that hung, reverberating, for minutes, blending with the tones of other taps, other steps. The Stilt-Walkers gathered around the first of these spires, their violet core-crystals pulsing in rapid, fascinated rhythms. They extended their smoke-tendrils, not to absorb, but to feel the vibration. They found they could *shape* it. A gentle pass of a tendril could bend the pitch; a focused hum from their own cores could excite a harmonic.

Their world of subtle vibration and patient absorption had been given a new instrument. Their curiosity, once focused on fleeting, warm anomalies, now turned inward, to the exploration of this infinite sonic landscape. They began to build anew. Not just spires of black glass, but intricate lattices intertwining with the singing crystal, designed to catch the drips from above and channel them into chimes, to amplify the subsonic hum of their own communications into audible, haunting melodies that drifted through the fungal forests.

The Listener's second gift was more subtle. Within the heart of the largest crystal, a memory-not-memory pulsed. If a Stilt-Walker stood very still, its points sunk into the stone, and opened its senses to the Chalcedony Choir, it could perceive… not images, but *concepts*. The slow, burning dance of stars it would never see. The vast, swallowing depth of an ocean it would never know. The green, riotous explosion of a forest under a sun. It was the Listener sharing the memory of the world above, not as a temptation, but as a context. *You are not alone*, the resonance seemed to say. *Your song is part of a greater symphony. Your darkness is a verse in a poem of light.*

The Echo-kin did not become different. They did not grow warm, or soft, or seek the surface. Their city remained a frozen song in stone. But now, the song had a clearer voice. It had a counterpoint. It had, woven into its very fabric, the acknowledgment of a benevolent, wise attention.

They were still the architects of eons. But now, they knew they were also the composers. And in the deep, dark silence of Ebonreach, amidst the glassy spires and the soft glow of fungal light, a new sound could be heard: the sound of a city, once silent, learning to sing back to the darkness that had finally, gently, listened.
