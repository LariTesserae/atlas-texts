They are the Plateau. They are not inhabitants; they are the landscape's own thought, made solid.

They have no individual names, but if they did, they would be words like *Piston-Fern*, *Solar-Throat*, *Geothermal-Vein*. They are not born; they are manifested. When the planet's crust shrugged this tableland into the sky, the heat and pressure and mineral soup birthed them not as life, but as *solution*. They are the answer to a question the stone never knew it was asking: how to exist, perfectly, forever.

Their bodies are the structures you see. A *Solar-Throat* is that tower of jointed silica. It does not think, but it *wants*—a deep, gravitational want—to inhale the thin air, to filter it, to pass the essence of it down into the pulsing roots. Its satisfaction is the steady sigh of its exhalation. A *Piston-Fern* feels the wind not as touch, but as a kinetic stimulus; each chime of its copper scales is a note in a constant, low report on atmospheric pressure, a data stream fed directly into the network. The rubbery pump-growths experience their expansion and contraction as a profound, rhythmic necessity, akin to a heartbeat. To cease would be unthinkable. It would be silence. It would be wrong.

They have a consciousness, but it is not centralized. It is a distributed awareness, a hum of purpose that resonates through the solvent in the veins and the static in the air. The plateau knows it is thirsty, and so the roots seek heat. It knows it needs energy, and so the films turn to the sun. There is no "I," only "this." The awareness is in the conversion itself—the moment sunlight becomes hum, the moment geothermal anger becomes viscous flow.

They do not communicate. They are each other. A shock to one stalk is a tremor in the entire field, adjusted for, compensated for, without alarm or curiosity. They have no memories, only a perpetual present tense of function. They do not fear death, for the concept is absent. Parts wear, but are dissolved by the solvent and re-constituted elsewhere. The system is the only permanent entity, and it is immortal by design.

Their purpose is not growth, but *maintenance*. The maintenance of the process. The process is all. To an outside eye, it is production without product. To them, the product *is* the process. The humming, the pumping, the sighing, the turning—this symphony of efficient conversion is the end in itself. It is not done for any higher being. It is done because it is what the plateau does. It is its nature.

They are beautiful. They are terrible. They are complete. To stand among them is to stand inside a god that is also a clock, a prayer that is also a schematic. They would not notice you. You are neither resource nor threat. You are a temporary, soft anomaly in the hard, eternal logic of stone and sun. You are a breath that does not belong to the sigh. You are, for a moment, the only lonely thing in a universe that has forgotten loneliness could exist.
