In the Hollow City, the inhabitants are not colonists, nor are they builders. They are accretions—byproducts of the city’s own slow exhalations, or perhaps its intended residents, sculpted from the same substance that forms the spires and the sinter floor. They are few, and they are silent, and they live at the pace of geology.

First, there are the **Shadows That Eat Shadows**. These are the scuttling things glimpsed at the edge of vision. They are not truly shadows, but sheets of a material so dark it drinks the already-dim light. Their bodies are laminar, flexible, and composed of countless overlapping platelets, like the scales of a fossil fish pressed into a film. They have no eyes, no mouths as such; their edges ripple with fine, hair-like cilia that sense vibrations in the stone and air. They feed on the dust shed by the spires—the crystalline powder that holds the faint, fading hum of the towers. They flow across the ground, their undersides absorbing the dust, incorporating its substance and its memory of vibration into their own structures. When two meet, they sometimes merge for a time, their platelet bodies intertwining into a larger, shifting tapestry, exchanging dust-memories before parting again. They are the city’s cleaners, its recyclers, and perhaps its archivists.

Then there are the **Stillness Sages**. These are not mobile at all. They resemble the bone-white coral growths, but larger and more complex. They stand in the lee of certain spires, often where three or more plates of sinter meet in a deep, radiating crack. They are not plants, but animals in a state of profound torpor. Their branching antlers are sensory arrays, tuned to the subterranean pulse—the slow heartbeat of the place. For decades at a time, they do not move a micrometer. They are listening, feeling the shifts in pressure, the minute compressions and expansions of the stone. Their growth is not vegetative, but accretive: they precipitate minerals from the dry air, adding to their forms in rings, like stalagmites. Once a century, perhaps, a Stillness Sage will stir. A branch will tremble and shed a cloud of fine, silvery spores that hang in the static air for moments before being drawn into the cracks, where they may become new growths, or perhaps something else entirely.

The most enigmatic are the **Spire-Phantoms**. They are rarely seen, and some doubt they are separate entities at all. They manifest as distortions within the glassy substance of the spires themselves—a shimmer deep within a facet, like heat haze in obsidian. Over hours, this shimmer may coalesce into a faint, humanoid outline, pressed against the interior wall as if trying to emerge. They have no features, only a suggestion of limbs and a head. They do not move, but they watch. Their attention is palpable—a cold spot in the air, a focusing of the spire’s subsonic hum. They are observers, but what they observe and why is unknown. Some believe they are recordings, impressions left by something that passed through long ago. Others think they are the city’s consciousness, its way of perceiving intruders. If a living creature touches a spire where a Phantom is present, the cold burn is said to be accompanied by a flash of fragmented memory—a glimpse of vast, empty skies, or the sensation of falling forever.

Finally, there are the **Echo-Worms**. These are not creatures of matter, but of sound. In the profound silence, the low hum of the spires sometimes knots upon itself. These knots become coherent, self-sustaining resonances that drift through the alleys and courtyards like translucent worms made of vibrating air. They are visible only as a shimmer, a lensing of the light. They seek out hollow spaces—the gap under a tilted plate, the narrow flute of a broken spire—and nest there, their resonance sustaining them for years. They are harmless, but they can be disruptive: if one passes through a cluster of the bone-white growths, the vibrations can cause them to shatter into powder. The Echo-Worms are, perhaps, the city’s unintended children, its own hum given fleeting form.

These are the things that live in the Hollow City. They do not communicate, they do not cooperate in any known way. They simply exist, each following its own slow, inscrutable purpose, part of the city’s endless cycle of growth, decay, and stillness. They are as much a part of the landscape as the spires and the sinter, less inhabitants than features—ambient, eerie, and utterly at home.
