They are called the Mycorrh, though they have no word for themselves that translates as a name. They are the weavers, the tenders, the slow thought in the root. To see one is to mistake it for the architecture. An adult Mycorrh is a bipedal form, but only vaguely, a upright gathering of thick, root-like tendrils fused at a central core. Their skin is the same fibrous, bark-like texture as the structural pillars, mottled in greys and blacks, often studded with the same phosphorescent moss that grows on the walls—a deliberate cultivation. They are blind. Their faces are smooth, featureless planes save for a vertical slit of a mouth, lipless and dry. They “see” through a combination of seismic sensitivity in their broad, root-padded feet and a refined sense of pressure-change in the saturated air. They taste the world through the humidity, reading the chemical signatures of growth, decay, and other Mycorrh in every droplet.

They do not build; they guide. Their entire civilization is an act of patient horticulture. The giant rhizomes that form the Warrens are not native to this rift; they are a cultivated species, a semi-sentient plant whose growth patterns can be subtly directed. The Mycorrh spend their long, slow lives in a state of near-stillness, pressed against a root-wall or standing knee-deep in a pool. They exude complex pheromones and enzymes from their fingertips—which are not fingers, but clusters of fine, mobile rootlets—into the living structure. This steers the growth: here, a chamber must widen; there, a new passage must fork to access a fresh seep of mineral-rich water. Their architecture is grown, not made. The crisp, unfurled edges of new galleries bear the unique chemical signature of the Mycorrh who guided them, a living signature of authorship.

Their society is a network, not a hierarchy. They communicate through root-vibration (a slow, deep thrumming that travels for miles through the living mesh) and through the exchange of nutrient-rich fluids, dripped from their mouths into the mouths of others in a silent, solemn ritual. Concepts are not spoken but *imparted*—a complex cocktail of hormones and neural peptides that conveys not just information, but emotional and experiential states. A memory of a distant water-taste, a warning of a predator’s location, a directive for growth—all are shared this way. Their collective mind is diffuse, a slow consensus that emerges like capillary action.

Time for them is measured in drips and growth-rings. A “day” is the cycle of the ceiling mosses brightening and dimming with a rhythm independent of any sun. A “year” is the time it takes for a new tier of the warren to be fully woven and inhabited. They are young as a civilization, and their growth is aggressive, filling the rift with a desperate, silent fecundity.

Other creatures are part of their extended body. The blind shrimp in the pools are cultivated, harvested by careful hand-nets woven from fungal fibers. Their iridescent shells are ground into a paste that seals new growth-nodes. The moth-winged amphibians are tolerated, even encouraged; they pollinate certain luminous fungi the Mycorrh cultivate for light. The predators—the eels and moss-clump ambushers—are the necessary counter-pressure. The Mycorrh do not fight them. They simply grow their personal chambers in patterns the eels find difficult to navigate, and they learn the subtle textural difference between true moss and the hunter’s skin. Death here is not a tragedy; it is a recycling. A fallen Mycorrh is laid into a designated pool, where its body is rapidly broken down by specialized algae, its nutrients feeding the next generation of roots.

To walk among them is to be ignored, unless you block a growth-path or disturb a pool. Then, the nearest will turn its smooth face toward you, not with eyes, but with the full weight of its atmospheric reading. You will feel assessed, not as an individual, but as a variable in the humidity, a potential source of beneficial salts or disruptive turbulence. They are the city, and the city is them. Their purpose is not to conquer, explore, or understand. It is to *continue*. To infiltrate the dark, to fill the cold, to become, slowly and surely, the living, breathing stone.
