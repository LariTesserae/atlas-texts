The inhabitants of Crysthane are not a people in any terrestrial sense. They are processes given flesh, manifestations of the city's deep, metabolic pulse. They do not dwell within the city; they are its cellular activity, its immune response, its reproductive system.

First, there are the **Condensers**. Vast, sessile organisms fused to the geothermal vents in the city's underbelly. They resemble colossal, inverted jellyfish, their bell-shaped bodies anchored to the hot rock, trailing forests of translucent, capillary tendrils through the substrate's upper layers. They breathe the planet's inner heat and exude the moisture that gives the Weeping City its name—a steady, warm exhalation that rises through the labyrinth, meeting the killing cold and becoming the perpetual, glowing fog. They are the city's lungs and circulatory system, and their slow, peristaltic rhythms set the base frequency for the sonic tapestry.

Then, the **Weavers**. If the city is grown, the Weavers are the gardeners. Armored, multi-limbed, and roughly the size of a terrestrial bear, they move with a deliberate, clockwork grace across the architectural thicket. Their six limbs end not in claws, but in complex, tool-like assemblages of chitin and crystal: drills, spinners, soldering filaments that emit a cold, blue chemical light. They are constantly at work. You see them on a new, growing spine, applying precise secretions that catalyze the deposition of the diamondoid lattice. You see them carefully pruning aberrant growths from a fungal mat, or gently herding clots of bioluminescent spores into optimal airflow patterns. They are silent, communicating through subtle pheromone trails laid down on the wet plates. They are not builders by design, but by symbiosis; their digestive systems process raw mineral slurry from the depths, and their excretions are the perfect binder and catalyst for the city's growth. They eat the city's waste, and their waste becomes the city's bones.

The **Chittering Clouds** are the most numerous fauna: trillions of insectoid creatures the size of a thumbnail, moving in cohesive, intelligent swarms that look like rivers of black oil flowing across surfaces. Individually, they are simple—a carapace, mandibles, basic photoreceptors. Collectively, they form a distributed cleaning and repair system. They scour the fungal mats, consuming decaying matter and microscopic pests. They will, in minutes, strip the flesh from a fallen larger creature, leaving a perfectly clean skeleton that the Weavers might later incorporate into a lattice. They also fill gaps in the structure, their own bodies interlocking to form temporary bridges, plugs, or patches until permanent growth can catch up. Their sound is the dominant "chittering" layer of the city's noise, a constant, reassuring hum of industry.

There are predators, of course. The **Gloomgulpers** are the silent, bat-like fliers. They have no bioluminescence of their own; they are pits of absolute black, their fur absorbing every stray photon. They navigate by reading the disturbances their wingbeats create in the fog's luminescence, seeing in negative. They prey on the Chittering Clouds and on the slower, nutrient-rich **Rime Slugs**—the huge, phosphorescent gastropods that graze the fungal mats. A Gloomgulper's attack is a sudden, soundless vortex of darkness plucking light from the air.

But the most unsettling inhabitants are the **Echo-Moths**. They are drawn to sources of concentrated energy, like the crackling nodes of pale violet power. They have delicate, crystalline wings that resonate with specific frequencies. When they gather at a node—a pulsing knot in the city's neural-analog web—they do not feed on the energy. They absorb the data. Their wings vibrate, capturing the information pulses that regulate growth, ventilation, condensation flow. Then they disperse, carrying these resonant patterns across the city, alighting on distant structural veins. There, the vibration of their wings stimulates or inhibits growth, subtly tuning the city's development. They are the broadcast system, the pollinators of information. A swarm of Echo-Moths settling on a new growth is the city dreaming its next shape into being.

Finally, there is the question of mind. Is there a governing intelligence? Perhaps it is emergent, a gestalt born from the Condenser's rhythms, the Weaver's programmed diligence, the Chittering Cloud's hive logic, and the Echo-Moth's data-dance. Or perhaps it is something older, sleeping in the planet's hot core, dreaming this cold, wet, luminous bruise of a city into existence on the surface. The inhabitants do not question it. They are its thoughts, moving slowly through the frozen, weeping dark.
