What lives on the Sighing Plateau are not discrete beings so much as specialized organs of the whole. They are expressions of its wet, slow metabolism, their forms and functions so interwoven with the Mycelial Conduits that to separate "animal" from "machine" or "plant" is to misunderstand the fundamental truth of the place: everything is a component of the terraforming engine. The fauna is quiet, not from timidity, but because noise is an inefficiency in a system that communicates through capillary action, chemical gradients, and resonant vibration.

The largest mobile creatures are the **Mossbacks**. Imagine a bulky, low-slung body, six legs splayed wide to distribute weight over the sighing peat. Their "hide" is not skin but a living, integrated carpet of mosses, liverworts, and tiny, moisture-trapping lichens, cultivated from spores ingested at birth and maintained by symbiotic mites that dwell in the dense underfur beneath. Their heads are broad and wedge-shaped, used for gently pushing aside spongy undergrowth to reach the tender fungal rhizomes and water-weeds they consume. Their most striking feature is their "processing hump"—a massive, bony dome on their shoulders, hollow and lined with a specialized tissue that is part lung, part fermentation chamber. Here, they digest their food with the aid of geothermal heat siphoned from the Mycelial Conduits via crystalline nodules embedded in their spine. The process generates a steady, warm exhalation that vents from nostrils on top of the hump, contributing to the plateau's mist. Their waste is a rich, pre-composted paste excreted directly onto Conduit rootlets, which absorb it within hours. They move in silent family groups, their passage marked only by the deeper, squelching sighs of the ground and the soft, rhythmic *puff… puff…* of their venting humps.

The grey, downy birds are **Veil-Tappers**. They are less birds than flying lichens with a centralized nervous system. Their feathers are filamentous fungal strands that absorb ambient moisture, making them perpetually damp and heavy. They cannot sing; their vocal cords have atrophied. Instead, they communicate by tapping their beaks—which are hardened, conductive chitin—against the crystalline outcroppings of the Conduits. This produces precise vibrational frequencies that travel for miles through the mineral network, conveying information about water salinity, predator locations, or the ripeness of certain fungus-fruits. They feed on the iridescent insects and the sugary excretions from the steam vent chitin, but their primary ecological role is as mobile sensors for the system. A Veil-Tapper’s tap is a data point. A flock’s dispersed tapping is a real-time topographic survey.

The amphibians in the peat waters are **Silt-Thinkers**. Blind, with translucent skin showing the slow pulse of internal organs, they are long, serpentine, and possess a shocking multiplicity of tiny, fin-like legs. They are not hunters in the conventional sense. They spend their lives burrowing through the cold, anoxic silt at the bottom of channels, their digestive tracts hosting colonies of anaerobic bacteria that break down the most recalcitrant organic compounds. Their "thinking" is a chemical computation. As they digest, they release specific signature peptides into the water. These chemicals are detected by sensitive cilia on nearby Conduits, which adjust nutrient flows accordingly. A Silt-Thinker is a liver lobule for the plateau, filtering toxins and managing biochemical balance. Occasionally, one will emerge, glistening and pale, to writhe upon a particular mossy bank, its body undergoing a rapid, programmed dissolution—returning a concentrated burst of processed minerals directly to the soil in a spot the system has deemed deficient.

The insects are where the biomechanical integration becomes most overt. **Geared Beetles** trundle along Conduits, their back not a shell but an exposed, intricate assembly of interlocking, chitinous gears and sprockets. These are not for locomotion; they are clockwork dampeners. The beetles climb to points where the Conduit’s internal pulsations are too strong, latch on, and their gears—turned by the creature’s own metabolic energy—mesh with vibrations, converting the excess rhythmic energy into a gentle, harmless heat that radiates from their bodies. This heat encourages the growth of specific mosses on the Conduit's surface, providing insulation. **Piston-Flies** are thumb-sized, with bodies like polished brass. Their wings beat in exact, piston-like strokes, and they are drawn to the steam vents. They fly into the controlled jets at the precise moment of exhaust, their bodies using the sudden, humid pressure to trigger an internal reaction that fertilizes their eggs. The eggs are then laid in the damp, reactive fungus that drives the vent's "pistons," completing a life cycle that also services the machinery.

Then there are the **Weavers**. These are the closest thing to a visible, active caretaker caste. Spider-like, but the size of a large dog, they have eight long, multi-jointed limbs that end not in points but in complex, tool-like manipulators—blunt scoops, fine pincers, capillary injectors. Their "abdomens" are not silk-producing organs but bioreactors, glowing with a soft, fungal bioluminescence. They are mobile maintenance units for the Conduits. They meticulously clean the vent mouths of debris, using their injector limbs to administer symbiotic bacteria to cracks in the crystalline structures, which then secrete a healing mineral paste. They "weave" in the sense that they repair the living fabric of the system. They are intelligent, but it is a distributed, task-focused intelligence, seemingly directed by the electromagnetic fields generated by the Conduits themselves. They show no curiosity about things outside their function. An intruder who does not damage the network is ignored. One who scrapes a Conduit with a tool will be surrounded within minutes by a silent, clicking circle of Weavers, their bioreactor abdomens pulsing with a warning amber light, their manipulators raised not in threat, but in readiness to repair the damage—even if that means dissolving the offending tool, and its wielder, with digestive enzymes and returning their components to the peat.

Finally, there are the **Echoes**. These are not true fauna at all, but a phenomenon. In places where the mist is thickest and the Conduit vibrations intersect in perfect resonance, the moisture and light can coalesce into temporary, semi-corporeal forms. They might look like a fragment of a Mossback from yesterday, or a stranger, bipedal shape that walked here centuries ago. They are acoustic and visual memories, etched into the vibrating stone and released under the right conditions. They are harmless, silent, and fade within moments, but they reinforce the sense that the Plateau is a recorder, a living museum of every footfall, every sigh, every pulse of its endless, self-sustaining industry.

To live here is to be a cog, a valve, a sensor, a filter. There is no predation for sport, no reproduction for mere genetic propagation. Every act of consumption is an act of recycling. Every death is a direct transfer of biomass into the Mycelial network. The quiet is the sound of perfect efficiency. The sigh is the sound of the engine breathing. And the inhabitants are that breath given form, moving silently through the mist, forever maintaining the only thing that matters: the Plateau itself, complete, and eternal.
