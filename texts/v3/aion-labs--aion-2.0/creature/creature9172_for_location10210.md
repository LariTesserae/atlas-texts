Here, at the end of all things, where physics grows thin and the great engine of the world ticks over into silence, life does not bustle. It persists. It endures in forms that have shed the frantic skin of animal necessity, becoming phenomena as much as beings.

**The Lignarii** are the city’s keepers, though ‘keeper’ implies a separation they do not possess. They are grown, not born. From the deep root-caverns where the pressurized sap hums loudest, they emerge—shapes coaxed from the living ironwood and petrified rhizome by the city’s own slow will. They are tall, attenuated figures, their bodies a simulation of humanoid form carved by wind and necessity. Their skin is the textured bark-stone of the towers, shot through with veins of obsidian. They have no eyes, only smooth, seamed planes where a face might be, for they see through the vibration in the root-roads and the shifting pressures in the sap-lines. Their fingers are long, fibrous roots, capable of parting moss-thick panels to access the glowing, viscous heart of a pump, or of splicing a severed vein of nutrient-rich fluid with a touch that sparks with slow, green bioluminescence.

They move with a patience that is geographical. You might observe one for a day and see it take a single step, its foot-fused with the pathway, listening. Their purpose is maintenance, a tending to the great, sleeping metabolism. They clear blockages in the xylem, guide the growth of supportive crystalline struts, and, in rituals that last decades, carefully peel away sheets of necrotic bark to expose the living wood beneath. They communicate not with sound, but with pulses sent through the sinewy vines that cloak the spires—a sub-audible language of pressure and release that travels for miles, conveying concepts like “a weakening in the western spire’s foundational root” or “a pocket of anomalous time coalescing in the High Hollow.”

**The Echo-Whisperers** are not born of the city’s body, but of its voice. They are the children of the constant wind and the deep, resonant groans. Where the wind funnels through a particular flute-like aperture for centuries, a Whisperer may coalesce—a vortex of mist and audible memory given fleeting shape. They appear as tall, wispy columns, vaguely humanoid, their forms made of flowing fog and the shimmer of sound waves. They have no consciousness as we know it; they are recordings, playing back. A Whisperer in the Plaza of Dripping Shadows might endlessly repeat the deep chord of a tower’s founding strain, ten thousand years past. Another, caught in a root-tunnel, might cycle through the ghostly echoes of footsteps from eras when other things walked here. They are harmless, mournful, beautiful. To stand near one is to be immersed in a fragment of the city’s immense, sonic history.

**The Unmoored** are the accidentals, the proof of the reality breakdown. They are not native. They are the remnants of explorers, scholars, or fools who breached the silver twilight and stayed too long. The soft time and fraying physics have unspooled them. You see them as flickers—a figure in archaic robes standing on a root-bridge, but when you blink, it is on three different bridges simultaneously, each instance slightly transparent. A woman’s face, pale as the lichen, might watch from a fungal shelf, but her body is elsewhere, or perhaps non-existent. They are trapped in loops, reliving moments of arrival or loss. Some are stretched thin across hours, their speech a single syllable drawn out into a day-long drone. Others are fragmented, their senses scattered—their sight in one plateau, their hearing in another, their touch feeling the drip of water miles away. They are pitiable, but they are also dangers. Their instability is contagious in small, localized zones. Walk through a space an Unmoored one considers ‘theirs,’ and you might feel your own memories begin to loop, or find your shadow has decided to walk three paces ahead of you.

**The Moss-Mind** is the most pervasive and least person-like inhabitant. It is the collective sentience of the living tapestry—the velvet moss, the grey-green fronds, the leathery fungi. It thinks, but its thoughts are the thoughts of a forest measured in millennia. A single ‘thought’ might be the absorption of a specific mineral from the mist over the course of a century. Its awareness is diffuse, a blanket of slow sensation. It knows the weight of the mist, the pattern of the drips, the health of the great towers from the taste of the air at their bases. It dislikes sudden change. It will, over decades, grow over any tool left by a Lignarii, or gently steer the root-like silver creatures away from areas it deems delicate. It is not hostile, merely possessive and profoundly, vegetably patient. To a visitor, its only sign is a uncanny feeling of being *watched* by every surface, and the faint, dreamlike impressions that seep into the mind during sleep—images of deep root growth and the slow turning of the sky.

Together, these entities inhabit Lignum Caelum. They do not form a society. They intersect. A Lignarii might patiently wait for an Echo-Whisperer to finish its centuries-long replay before crossing a bridge, as a sign of respect for the city’s memory. An Unmoored one might be drawn, moth-like, to the sound of a Whisperer that resembles a voice it once knew. And through it all, the Moss-Mind feels, and grows, and remembers everything, waiting for the next geological age to turn.
