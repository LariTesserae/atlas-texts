They are not a colony, nor a hive. Those terms imply purpose, hierarchy, a collective striving. Here, there is only the perfect alignment of individuals to a single, immutable fact: the Sound.

They cling to every vertical surface, a second skin of tarnished metal and damp leather over the brick and iron. Up close, the texture of a carapace is not smooth. It is a landscape of minute pitting and whorls, like a river stone subjected to a billion uniform droplets. Their legs—six, eight, ten, the number varies but is always even—end in hooked claws that have, over timeless eons, worn precise sockets into the stonework. They are not gripping; they are socketed. A part of the architecture.

Where a head should be, there is only a smooth, forward-facing plate, perforated by a dozen or more sensory pits. These are not eyes. They do not see light. They read vibration, pressure differentials in the dense, damp air, and the steady, overwhelming pulse of the factory’s heart. Their world is a map of resonance. The thick *thump* from the center is the north star, the prime meridian, the absolute center. Every other tremor—the drip from the pipe, the almost-silent creep of the mycelial strands through the mud, the infinitesimal shift of a brick settling a micron further into the silt—is plotted in relation to that central datum.

They do not sleep, for they are already in the only state of awareness they possess: a perfect, consuming listenership. They do not eat, but a metabolic process occurs. A clear, oily secretion wells from the seams of their mandibles—which are not for tearing, but for delicate, precise scraping. The fluid falls, drop by scheduled drop, onto the fungal shelves below. It does not kill the fungus. It dissolves a precise, paper-thin layer, which the fungus immediately regenerates. This is not predation. It is pruning. A maintenance ritual as mechanical as the factory’s pistons. The fluid, now enriched with complex sugars from the dissolved matter, is reabsorbed through pores in their underbellies. The exchange is silent, odorless, and leaves no waste.

Their stillness is not inertia. It is a profound and active absorption. Their entire bodies are tuning forks, struck once at the beginning of time and never allowed to dampen. The Sound holds them in a permanent state of sympathetic vibration. This vibration is their circulation, their neural activity, their everything. To be plucked from their perch would be to sever them from their own heartbeat. They would not struggle; they would simply cease, as a note ceases when a string is cut.

They have no names, but if they did, they would all be the same name: Listener.

And they are not alone in their listening. The fungal lattices in the mud pulse with the same rhythm. The condensation on the corrugated iron trembles in perfect time before falling. The very grit in the air seems to oscillate. The entire bowl of the town is a resonator, a bell jar placed over a single, endlessly ringing tone. The creatures are merely the most articulated expression of this resonance, the nodes where the sound condenses into a form that can cling to walls and drip oily fluid.

There is no past for them, no memory of a before. There is no future, no anticipation of change. There is only the everlasting, enveloping Now of the Sound. They do not dream, for they are the dream—the persistent, industrial dream of the factory itself. A dream of order so absolute it has sculpted life into a still, silent, and eternal audience to its own monotonous creation.
