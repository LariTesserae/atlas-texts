Nothing “lives” here in the way you understand life. There are no inhabitants, only processes wearing temporary shapes. The Plateaus are a single, continent-spanning organism, and what moves upon it are its tools, its senses, its maintenance crews—ephemeral manifestations of its will, woven from the same sighing matter.

They are the Silent Staff.

**The Seismographs** are the first you might notice, if you stand still long enough. They emerge from the felt-like surface nearby: a cluster of slender, jointless stalks, pale as bone, each tipped with a quivering, bladder-like sac. They do not face you. They orient themselves to the subtlest tremors in the living substrate, mapping the heave of a new hummock or the collapse of a fluid chamber deep below. Their sacs inflate and deflate in rhythmic patterns, a silent language reporting on the stability of the factory floor. They are living gauges, and when their reading is complete, they slowly melt back into the mat, leaving no trace.

**The Glyph-Mites** are more visible in their work. You’ll see a patch of fibrous ground suddenly darken, as if stained by ink. Then it parts, and a flowing carpet of them pours forth. Each is no larger than your thumbnail, a hexagonal plate of waxy, dark green matter, edges serrated. They move as one, a sheet of living sandpaper. They descend into a gully or ascend a curved buttress, and where they pass, the surface is subtly altered—smoothed, engraved with faint, geometric channels, or reinforced with a new layer of glistening membrane. They are the painters, the engravers, the repair crew for the Plateau’s endless self-modification. Their work done, the sheet flows to the edge of their assigned zone, and each mite simply… unstitches itself from its neighbors, its body going limp and porous, merging back into the substrate it just finished editing.

**The Vasculators** are the internal couriers. You see them as sudden, swift bulges moving beneath the skin of the world, like a mouse under a rug, but faster and purposefully. They are pressurized packets of fluid or gas, encapsulated in a tough, synaptic membrane, directed along pre-defined capillary highways. Sometimes one will breach the surface at a junction node: a momentary blister that pulses, connects with a waiting vein, injects its cargo, and then deflates into a wrinkled scar that is absorbed within minutes. They are the silent trains in the subway of the flesh, carrying chemical instructions, nutrient loads, or the refined products of the distant distillation columns.

**The Chimney-Swallows** attend to the atmosphere. Where a warm vent opens, issuing its plume of solvent-scented vapor, several of these will coalesce. They look like nothing so much as tattered, black lace kites, formed of dry, papery membranes stretched over a framework of brittle spines. They flutter on the thermal itself, silent as ash, their intricate surfaces chemically stripping the plume of particulates or catalyzing final reactions. After the vent seals, they lose their lift. They drift down, crumpling upon contact with the ground, their structure dissolving into a powdery residue that is immediately drunk up by the hungry fibers.

**And then there are the Custodians**—the closest thing to the “scavenging pods” you glimpsed. These are for breakdown and recycling. When a whole section of frenetic tissue exhausts itself, dying in a sudden, simultaneous wilt, the Custodians emerge. They are oblong, low-slung, the color of old bruises. Their movement is the only thing that approximates sound: a faint, wet *prehensile rustle* from their hundred tiny cilia-feet. They swarm the dead zone, not eating, but dismantling. Acidic secretions melt connections; delicate, hook-tipped tendrils tease apart complex structures. They reduce vibrant, engineered tissue into a slurry of base components, which they then absorb through their undersides. Once the area is a blank, damp patch, they don’t leave. They settle, their own bodies flattening, spreading, and integrating, becoming the fresh foundational layer from which new, frantic growth will erupt.

There is no predation. No cooperation beyond the systemic. No sleep, no play, no society. There is only function within the great, silent, industrial metabolism. The inhabitants are not born; they *precipitate* from the saturated matter, summoned by need. They do not die; they are *decommissioned*, their substance returned to the whole.

To witness them is to witness a body thinking. A lung does not “live” apart from the body; it functions. So do these. They are the Plateau’s reflexes, its hands, its eyes. And if you, a static, warm, noisy bundle of separate organs, remain too long in one place, you will notice a new kind of tool begin to form in the vegetation around you. Slender, sensing stalks tipped not with bladders, but with concave, light-sensitive disks—**Oracles**—will rise. They will orient, not toward the tremors of the earth, but toward you. They will track your slow, inefficient movements, your futile exhalations of clouded air. They will report your presence as an anomaly, a thermodynamic hiccup, a curious stasis in the churning flow.

And the Plateau, in its silent, patient, industrial way, will begin to devise a process for you.
