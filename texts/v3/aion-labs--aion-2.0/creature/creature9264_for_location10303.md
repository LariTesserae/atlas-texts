They call themselves the **Stone-Breath**, or in their own silent tongue, a series of clicks and exhales that translates to *Those Whose Lungs Forged the Sky*. They are the inhabitants of the City of Stone and Breath, and they are as much a part of its ecology as the lichen and the gliding raptors.

**Physiology:** Generations in the thin, cold air have sculpted them. They are short of stature, with barrel chests housing lungs of remarkable efficiency. Their blood is rich with hemoglobin, giving their skin a faint, bluish tint beneath the weathering of wind and cold—a hue most pronounced in the high-altitude elites who rarely descend. Their hands are broad, fingers strong and blunt-tipped, perfect for gripping rock and tool. Their eyes are adapted to the crystalline light: dark, capable of narrowing to slits against the glare, and possessing a depth of field that allows them to judge dizzying vertical distances with uncanny accuracy. They move with a deliberate, economical grace; wasted motion is wasted heat. Their voices, when used, are soft and low, carrying further in the thin air than a shout would in the denser atmospheres below.

**Social Stratification: The Altitude Castes.** The vertical axis is the absolute determinant of status. Society is divided into three broad strata, though within them exist a hundred subtle gradations.

*   **The Cloud-Sculptors (High):** They inhabit the highest spires, where the air is thinnest and the view encompasses the entire world. Their role is governance, astronomy, and the sacred management of the Water-Chants—the complex calculations that predict condensation cycles and allocate flows. They are the most physically adapted, often suffering from "thick-air sickness" if they descend too far. Their lives are ones of ritual silence and contemplation. Their homes are austere, carved with perfect, stark geometry.
*   **The Vein-Walkers (Middle):** The artisans, merchants, and bulk of the population. They occupy the tiered plateaus and the main labyrinthine streets. They are the masons, the channel-tenders, the weavers of beast-wool, the coordinators of the silent markets. They are pragmatic, their carvings functional but often adorned with intricate, repeating patterns that map water channels or star paths. They maintain the delicate treaties with the fauna.
*   **The Deep-Dwellers (Low):** They live and work in the sunless canyons, the quarries, the ice-mines, and the vast, echoing underhalls where the city’s waste heat (from thousands of bodies and lamps) collects. They are the excavators, the ice-cutters, the handlers of the massive counterweight lifts. They are stockier, more tolerant of damp and darkness. Their world is one of constant, careful labor, the rhythm of chisel-strokes, and the distant drip of meltwater. They are looked down upon by those above, yet the city would starve and thirst without them.

**Culture & Daily Life:**

*   **Water Rights:** Water is not owned, but usage is allocated by the Cloud-Sculptors based on ancient formulae. Each household has a designated time to fill its cistern from the communal channel. Theft of water is the gravest crime, punishable by exile to the dry, wind-scoured exterior slopes—a death sentence.
*   **The Silent Language:** Speech is for necessity and ceremony. Daily communication is a rich language of gesture, facial expression, and most importantly, **tap-code**. Using fingernails, stones, or tools, they tap on rock surfaces. The density and conductivity of the stone carry these signals over surprising distances—a complex conversation can occur between people on opposite sides of a canyon, unheard by those in between. Different rhythms mean different things: a quick triplet is a greeting; a slow, single tap can be a warning; a complex, rolling sequence might be a market price or a piece of gossip.
*   **Symbiosis with Fauna:** They do not merely tolerate the fauna; they cultivate it. The sure-footed, goat-like **Cliff-Striders** are semi-domesticated. Their wool is spun into dense, warm cloth; their milk is fermented into a sharp cheese; they are led to specific terraces to graze the tough grass, their droppings carefully collected to fertilize the lichen gardens. The great raptors, the **Sky-Keepers**, are sacred. They are believed to carry the breaths of the dead into the high wind. Disturbing a nest is taboo. In return, the Sky-Keepers keep the rodent and insect populations in check. Some Deep-Dwellers are said to keep the pale, blind insects as pets or lights in jars.
*   **Art & History:** Their history is not written; it is excavated. To know a family's lineage, you examine the layers of carving in their dwelling, each generation adding its own mark. Art is functional and geometric: channel maps, star charts, and echo-patterns designed to shape the wind into soft harmonic tones in specific plazas. Their music is the wind through sculpted holes, the drip of water in calibrated basins, and the rare, deep-throated chant sung only during the Solstice Drip, when a specific ice formation melts in the main plaza.
*   **Beliefs:** They worship **Patience** and **The Given Stone**. Their cosmos is a mountain being slowly carved by time and breath. They believe their city is not finished; it is a work in progress that will only be complete when the entire mountain has been shaped into perfect, inhabitable form—a task requiring millions of years. Death is seen as a return to the stone; bodies are interred in sealed niches, where the slow processes of mineralization reclaim them. A person’s true monument is not a tomb, but the space they carved to live in, the channel they helped maintain.

**Conflict & Governance:** Major conflict is rare and slow-burning, like the widening of a crack. It usually concerns water allocation or quarrying rights. Disputes are resolved through stone-trial: opposing parties carve their arguments into a designated wall over a period of months or years, and a council of Cloud-Sculptors and senior Vein-Walkers judges which carving demonstrates greater technical mastery, aesthetic harmony, and adherence to principle. Warfare, as known elsewhere, is impossible here. But a form of silent, architectural warfare exists: sabotaging a rival’s water channel, or subtly altering the carving of a public space to throw insulting shadows upon their doorway.

**The Individual:** A Stone-Breath citizen wakes in a cell-like room carved from granite, the air cold enough to see their breath. They don layers of Strider-wool, check the condensation level on the designated "breath-stone" by their door (a predictor of the day's humidity), and begin their work. For a Cloud-Sculptor, this might mean hours of observing the ice-fields through polished crystal lenses. For a Vein-Walker mason, it is the meticulous shaping of a block, the sound of their hammer a quiet, rhythmic heartbeat in the vast silence. For a Deep-Dweller, it is the perilous, rope-hung work of enlarging a quarry face, their world lit by flickering fat-lamps, the only loud sound the occasional crack and rumble of freed rock. Their pleasure is in the feel of perfect joinery, the sight of clear water flowing in their channel, the shared warmth of a family cistern-room in the evening, communicating the day's events in swift, silent tap-code on the floor.

They are a people of profound stillness and immense inner resolve. They have traded the frenzy of growth for the permanence of stone. Their ambition is not to conquer the horizon, but to perfect the interior. They are the architects of their own world, and in its relentless, silent, cold grandeur, they have found a meaning as hard and enduring as the mountain itself.
