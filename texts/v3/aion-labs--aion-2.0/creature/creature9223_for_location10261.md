The **Mare-Nimbus Range** is not drowned land. It is an architecture born wet, a spine of continental rock that never knew air. It exists in the permanent midnight of the abyssal plain, yet it is not a low place. Its summits are high — relative to the ocean’s crushing floor — forming a drowned skyline of peaks and plateaus suspended in a cold, heavy firmament. The pressure here is a cathedral, a silent, omnipotent presence. The temperature hovers just above the freezing point of saltwater, a chill that seeps into rock and bone alike.

The geography is raw, as if the planet’s crust is still dreaming itself into being. Erosion is a forgotten concept. Cliffs shear away in knife-edged facets, not rounded by time but fractured in clean, violent planes. Avalanches of stone slide in slow motion through the viscous medium, their impacts muffled to deep, tectonic groans. The scale defies human measure. A single basin between two peaks could swallow a mountain range from the sunlit world. The density is so low, the emptiness so profound, that traversing it feels less like travel and more like a failure of space to contain anything at all.

There is no fauna. No flicker of scale, no pulse of jellyfish, no scurrying in the silt. The only movement is geological and the relentless push of currents. Vegetation is sparse, a blasphemy against the mineral dominion. In sheltered lees, where some faint, chemosynthetic energy trickles from vents, grow forests of **glass-whip**. They are not plants, not truly — more like solidified currents, filaments of silicate that capture passing minerals to elongate their cold, transparent forms. They do not sway; they resonate, humming at frequencies just below hearing as the deep currents play across them.

The light is normal, which here is a profound strangeness. No sun pierces this depth. The illumination comes from two sources. First, a pervasive, sourceless cobalt radiance, as if the water itself remembers a sky it has never seen. Second, and more dominant, is the light of the **Tech-that-Isn’t**. This is the parameter that defies categorization: a technology beyond modern comprehension, woven into the fabric of the place, with no builder in sight.

It manifests in the **Choral Crystals**. They stud the cliffs and plains — geodes the size of cities, their interiors a labyrinth of prismatic lattices. They are not inert. They process. The tremendous pressures and subtle thermal gradients of the range drive fluid through their capillaries, and within that flow, information is sorted, patterns are reinforced, echoes of quantum states are amplified. They are geological computers, running programs written by thermodynamics and gravity. Their light is a byproduct: a shifting, algorithmically complex aurora that spills from their fissures in pulses of sterile silver and quantum-blue. Sometimes, in harmony with a particularly strong seismic tremor, they flare in unison, bathing the entire range in a momentary, shadowless day, revealing the terrifying scope of the emptiness.

The sound is loud, a constant, turbulent symphony. It is the roar of the **Abyssal Conveyor**, a continent-scale current that smashes against the western flank of the range, its fury channeled into canyons that howl like damned choruses. It is the grind of tectonic plates, the sharp *crack* of a mountainside calving. It is the harmonic, crystalline ringing of the glass-whips, and beneath it all, the deep, subsonic hum of the Choral Crystals, a bass note felt in the marrow.

Nothing is built here. No ruins, no cables, no intelligently placed stone. Yet everything feels engineered. The alignment of the peaks seems to funnel energy in specific ways. The distribution of the Crystals follows a logic that is not random, but natural law raised to an arcane power. The turbulence is not chaos; it is a dynamic, roaring equilibrium, a system in a state of perpetual, violent creation. To witness it is to feel the eerie certainty that this is a machine — a vast, wet, mindless machine — performing operations whose purpose is as alien as its form. It is a world that is all engine, with no vehicle in sight, burning geological time as its fuel.
