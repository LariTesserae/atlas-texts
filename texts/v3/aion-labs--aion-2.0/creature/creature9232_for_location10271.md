The inhabitants of the valley are not separate from its processes; they are its processes given slow, stubborn form. They do not live in the valley; they are the valley living.

**The Steam-Tenders.** These are the most visible and audible of the valley's residents, though "visible" is a matter of patience. They are not creatures of flesh, but of sinter and silica, shaped by millennia of directed condensation. Each steam vent of significant size has one. They appear as humanoid stalagmites, roughly two meters tall, built up from the mineral-rich droplets that fall from the vent's exhaust plume. Their "bodies" are layered in bands of white calcite and dark iron oxide, their surfaces smooth and gleaming with perpetual dampness. They have no faces, only a rounded head-like nodule and stumpy approximations of limbs fused to their torsos. They are not statues; they move. Once every twenty-four hours, coinciding with a slight, predictable dip in the subterranean hum, each Steam-Tender performs a maintenance ritual. With a grinding slowness that would be imperceptible to a casual observer, they extend a limb of porous travertine and sweep it across the vent's nozzle, clearing accumulated mineral debris that might, over a century, slightly alter the pitch of the steam's whine. The motion is exact, economical, and silent except for the faintest grit-on-grit whisper. They then return to perfect stillness, absorbing the vent's heat and moisture, their own form slowly accreting further. They have no consciousness, only a geochemical tropism—a programmed response etched into their crystalline structure by the valley's relentless, rhythmic conditions.

**The Pool-Skimming Derms.** In the opalescent pools, the biofilm mats are not the only life. The Derms are their ambassadors, or perhaps their mobile organs. They are sheets of the same leathery, black-and-rust biofilm, but detached and buoyant. Each is roughly circular, a meter across, and impossibly thin. They float on the pool surfaces, drifting in the gentle convection currents. Their purpose is sensory and distributive. They "taste" the chemical gradients of the pool—shifts in pH, temperature, and sulfide concentration—through their entire membrane. When they detect a sub-optimal zone, they release a cloud of adjustor cysts from their underside. These microscopic packets sink and merge with the static mats on the pool floor, altering their metabolic pathways. The Derms also act as heat exchangers, their dark surfaces absorbing the ambient warmth and transferring it to the cooler edges of the pool. They are silent, and their movement is so slow and fluid it seems like a trick of the steaming water. If touched, they would feel like warm, wet oilskin, and they would simply flow around the obstruction, reforming seamlessly on the other side.

**The Hum-Resonant Spines.** In the areas of deepest subsonic vibration, the very rock bristles. The Spines are crystalline growths, sharp and needle-like, projecting at uniform angles from the glassy bedrock. They are made of a piezoelectric quartz variant, and they are not passive. They vibrate in sympathy with the valley's foundational hum, converting the acoustic energy into a faint, continuous electrical charge. This charge does nothing obvious; it crackles invisibly in the humid air between spines, a static potential with no circuit to close. The prevailing theory (if there were theorists here) is that the charge influences the deposition of minerals from the air, guiding the formation of the terraces into their mathematically perfect bands. The Spines are also, perhaps, the valley's nervous system. When a steam blast is out of rhythm—an event that happens perhaps once a decade—the disturbance in the acoustic field causes the Spines in that sector to shiver, producing a higher-pitched harmonic that echoes through the bedrock. Within hours, the Steam-Tenders in adjacent sectors will adjust their cleaning rhythms by a millisecond, a cascade of correction that restores the precise interval. The Spines feel the valley's pulse, and in their own mineral way, they remember its proper rhythm.

**The Dust-Seed Carriers.** These are the only entities that approach what might be called fauna. They are the pale, steel-shelled arthropods mentioned in passing, but they are not mere scavengers. They are farmers of a sort. Each is a flattened, six-legged creature the size of a human hand, blind and deaf, its carapace a mirrored surface that reflects the hazy glow. They are not born; they precipitate. In certain cool, shadowed crevices where silica-rich condensate drips with glacial slowness, complex self-assembling crystals occasionally form around a template of organic molecules left by decaying biofilm. Over a year, a Carrier accretes into existence, its limbs clicking into place as the final droplets solidify. Its life is a single, purposeful journey. It emerges and moves with a startling, skittering quickness across the hot stones, following gradients of humidity and geomagnetic subtlety too faint for any instrument to detect. Its goal is a specific, mineral-poor patch of cracked mud. There, it stops, and its carapace unfurls like the petals of a metal flower. Inside is not guts, but a dense packet of dust—spores of the black biofilm, along with tailored mineral nanocrystals. It expels this payload into the crack, and then, its purpose fulfilled, it simply stops. Its body becomes a sheltering overhang for the new colony. Within weeks, it is cemented in place by fresh sinter deposits, becoming just another part of the terrace, its form slowly smoothed over by the eternal, gentle rain of condensation.

These are the things that live there. They do not compete. They do not reproduce in a biological sense. They iterate. They are components in a vast, self-regulating system. They exist to maintain the equilibrium, to fine-tune the hum, to clear the vents, to spread the mats, to ensure the valley breathes in and out on its eternal, metronomic schedule. To witness them is to witness a geology that has become ritual, a physics that has become habit. They are, all of them, employees of a factory whose only product is its own perpetual, unchanging now.
