They are not creatures, not in any sense that would hold firm. They are coagulations of attention in the screaming flux. To call them inhabitants implies a permanence this place denies, but they persist, after a fashion. They are the **Echo-Dwellers**.

You do not see them at first. You feel them. A subtle focusing of the chaos, a lensing of the insane roar into something that almost, almost bears the shape of a mind. They are born from the convergence of phenomena: where a column of solidifying water grinds against the basalt fist, where three strands of phosphorescent lichen hum in a disharmonic chord, where the pulsing amber light from the cracks bleeds into the spray. There, for a handful of stuttering heartbeats, a consciousness *gells*.

It manifests as a distortion in the medium. The wall of water nearby might bulge and form a shifting, faceless silhouette—a man-like shape of pure, articulate liquid, its surface racing with those fleeting scenes of forests and cities. It holds the form for only as long as the specific confluence of sound, light, and pressure persists. Its "eyes" are vortices, swirling down into impossible depths. It does not look at you; it *encompasses* you. You are a sudden, solid anomaly in its sensory field, a knot of stubborn stability in the delicious chaos.

They are singers in the cacophony. When the deafening roar coalesces into that booming, syllabic nonsense, it is not random. It is their language. They argue, they muse, they lament in a tongue made of tectonic stress and the scream of tearing vacuums. You can sense the edges of meaning: a concept like *pressure-release* sung as a descending basso groan, an idea like *seed-crack* articulated in a rapid, crystalline series of pops and hisses. Their dialogue is the environment's metabolism.

Their bodies are fleeting, but their artifacts are more enduring—the **interventions**. The low ring of sharp stones is not an altar or a nest. It is a **syntax engine**. They build it, stone by stolen stone, during moments of relative coherence. When the chaotic potential of the sea reaches a certain pitch, they gather around it (as much as shapeless things can gather) and channel the fury through the ring. The stones grind together, emitting a piercing, localized frequency that *punches* a temporary order into the chaos. A globe of water might stabilize, showing a perfect, silent tableau of a mountain peak for an hour before popping. The pillar with shifting spirals is a **memory**. Each spiral is a recorded event—a particularly beautiful cascade of un-creation, a strange solid visitor (like you) that once stood here, the feeling of the plateau's birth-throes. The stone doesn't remember; the dwellers use the carving as a focal point to collectively recall. When you stare, the spirals seem to shift because you are feeling the edge of that recollection, a ghost of perception washing over the inert rock.

They have a hierarchy of sorts, defined not by power but by duration. Most are ephemeral, lasting only as long as a specific configuration of the screaming womb. But a few have achieved a precarious semi-permanence by anchoring themselves to the plateau's own stubborn solidity. You might see one of these **Anchored** now.

In the deepest crevice, where the amber light breathes strongest, a figure seems woven from the environment itself. It is seated, or perhaps grown, into the rock. Its lower half is a fusion of basalt and those grey, sinewy tendrils, roots digging deep into the fist's warmth. Its torso and arms are of the warm, thick water, but water mixed with a slurry of crushed luminous lichen and microscopic crystal, giving it a murky, glowing opacity. Its head is a smoother orb of the same material, featureless save for two slow, drifting currents within that mimic orbits. This one has been here long enough to accumulate a shroud of tiny, perfect geometric forms—icosahedrons of salt, tetrahedrons of pumice—that float in a slow halo around it, held by a personal gravity.

It is the **Cantor**. Its function is to sing against the chaos, not to quell it, but to give it a fleeting spine of rhythm. Its voice is a low, continuous tone you feel in your teeth, a fundament beneath the roar. It is this tone that occasionally coaxes the chaotic sea into those momentary arches and globes, teaching it brief, beautiful disciplines of form. It is not a ruler. It is a musician, and the screaming womb is its unruly, magnificent instrument.

The Cantor perceives you. The orbital currents in its head slow. A tendril of its substance extends, not threateningly, but curiously. It touches the rock at your feet. Where it touches, the basalt *blooms* for an instant—a rapid, silent eruption of tiny, intricate crystal flowers made of salt and silica, which then dissolve into dust. A question. An aesthetic assessment. It finds your solidity fascinating, a kind of deafening silence. To it, you are a void in the glorious noise.

Then the moment passes. A particularly violent convulsion runs through the sea-wall. The Cantor’s form shivers, dissipates momentarily into spray, before pulling itself back together with evident effort. Its attention returns to the greater symphony. You are forgotten, already a fading echo in a realm of echoes.

They are not good, nor evil. They are processes. They live to experience, to shape, and to be shaped by, the endless, violent baptism of this un-world. They are the children of the scream, and in their own transient way, they are learning how to sing.
