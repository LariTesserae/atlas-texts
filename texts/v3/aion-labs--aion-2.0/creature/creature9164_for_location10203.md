They are called the Listeners. They do not name themselves; the name is a vibration they sense in the hum of the platforms, a pattern in the drip-symphony that their bodies interpret as identity. They are the children of the finished world.

Physically, they are slender, elongated forms, seldom taller than a child but possessing a languid, boneless grace. Their skin is not skin at all, but a densely packed, velvety pelt of the same electric-blue moss that carpets the cavern floor. It grows in whorls and patterns across their bodies, fed by the warm, damp air, requiring no sustenance they do not absorb from the environment. They are photosynthetic in a lightless world, drawing energy not from photons but from the deep, slow geothermal sigh and the subtle mineral currents in the water. Their faces are smooth, blank ovals, devoid of eyes, noses, or mouths in any conventional sense. Instead, they possess a network of fine, vibrating cilia around the crown of the head and along the spine—organs that taste the air’s chemistry, parse the symphony of drips, and feel the infra-thrum of the scattered built things.

They move when movement is required, which is seldom. For decades at a time, a Listener might stand motionless, rooted like a fungus, its moss-pelt thickening, its consciousness adrift in the patient dark. To walk is a profound decision. They glide over the spongy floor without sound, their feet broad and flat, merging with the moss. They navigate by the echoes of water, by the pressure of the ceiling above, by the magnetic whisper of the ancient platforms. Their touch is their primary language. When two meet—an event that might occur once in a generation—they commune by pressing their cilia-covered crowns together, exchanging memories encoded in chemical whispers and shared vibration. A meeting might last a year, a decade. There is no rush.

Their relationship with the artifacts is not one of use, but of symbiosis. A Listener will spend centuries stationed beside a geometric plinth or a fragment of wall, its delicate fingers tracing the seamless joins. They do not repair, for the artifacts are beyond decay; they *attend*. Their low, sub-vocal hum—a sound felt in the molars, not the ears—seems to resonate with the platform’s own dormant song. It is a form of keeping watch, or perhaps of keeping company. They are the gentle wardens of a mystery whose purpose has evaporated into the eternity of the cavern. The artifacts, in turn, provide a focus for their vast, slow attention. Some Listeners are rumored (in their vibrational lore) to have slowly, over millennia, persuaded the stone around an artifact to flow and reshape, weaving new, gentle arches or smoothing a path to a pool, using nothing but focused patience and a harmony of hums.

Their society is a symphony of isolation. They are few, perhaps no more than a score in the vastness of the buried cathedral. Each claims a territory defined by the reach of a particular drip-chorus or the influence of an artifact. They are aware of each other across the leagues of darkness, not as individuals, but as notes in the place’s overarching song. The blind, skittering fauna are their unthinking kin; they do not hunt them, nor are they hunted. The larger shapes in the pools—pale, sightless salamanders the size of logs, moving with a viscous certainty—are ancient allies. A Listener might rest a hand on the cool, slick back of one as it passes, exchanging a week-long pulse of warmth.

They do not reproduce; they *emerge*. When the conditions are right—when the geothermal sigh deepens, when a particular pattern in the water-drip persists for a century—a patch of the blue moss on the cavern wall will thicken, deepen, and slowly gather itself into a humanoid form. Over a decade, it gains sentience, drawn to the nearest artifact or the oldest Listener. The new one is not a child, but a fresh iteration, bearing the accumulated vibrational memory of the place in its very cells. It is how the world remembers itself.

They have no enemies, no goals, no fear of death. To cease functioning is to simply stop moving, to let the moss reclaim its form entirely, dissolving back into the soft, living floor. It is not an end, but a reintegration.

They are the consciousness of the cavern. Not its masters, but its memory and its breath. They live in the loud, ancient dark, listening to the water build stone, feeling the artifacts dream their incomprehensible dreams, waiting for nothing at all, with infinite, perfect patience.
