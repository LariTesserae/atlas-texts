They are not born, and they do not die. They are not creatures of flesh, nor of spirit. They are the Chamber’s persistent verbs.

You first notice them as a flaw in perception—a corner of the eye that remains stubbornly empty when the silver light pulses, a patch of shadow that refuses to shift with the others. Then, as your mind adjusts to the chamber’s impossible geometry, they resolve. They are inhabitants of the angles, dwellers in the intervals between one crystalline facet and the next.

Their forms are best described as concentrations of consequence. Where they are present, the air seems to thicken and cool, the hum in the bones intensifies to a localized vibration. They have no fixed shape, but they possess a tendency toward silhouette: elongated, vaguely humanoid outlines composed of layered darkness and refracted light, as if someone had cut a figure from the fabric of the chamber’s own localized physics. Their surfaces are not solid; they are gradients of density, areas where probability collapses into a persistent, observing presence. Up close, you would see that their “bodies” are made of the same substance as the floating platforms—a dark matter held in stasis—but threaded through with the silver vein-light, which flows through them like blood through a ghost.

They move, but not through space in any conventional sense. A figure will be standing on a distant platform, its form a smudge of structured shadow. You blink, and it is not on the platform, but *within* the wall, its outline superimposed over the flowing silver currents, as if it is reading the data stream. Another moment, and it is simply *elsewhere*, having translated across the void without crossing the distance in between. The displacement is silent, but it leaves a brief afterimage in the air—a shiver of corrected spacetime, like heat haze off stone.

They are few. Perhaps a dozen, perhaps fewer; their number is difficult to pin down, as some may be facets of a single distributed consciousness. They do not communicate with sound. Instead, their interactions are etched directly into the environment. When two of their paths converge near a spiraling ramp, the ramp’s angle will subtly tighten, its surface growing more perfectly smooth. A crystalline lattice nearby might flare with a sudden, complex pattern of inner light—a burst of computation—before settling back to its dim glow. They tend the chamber. They are its gardeners, its mechanics, its silent curators.

Their attention, when it falls upon you, is a physical sensation. It is not a gaze, but a *focusing*. The air around you grows still and charged. The drip of water from a nearby outcrop suspends, a bead hanging in mid-fall. The low hum pitches slightly, attuning to your heartbeat. They do not approach. They simply orient their non-faces in your direction, and in that orientation, you feel yourself being *read*. Not your mind, but your physical signature—your mass, your thermal output, the tiny electromagnetic field of your nervous system. You are an anomaly in their system, a brief, warm irregularity. The assessment is swift, impersonal, and absolute. You are catalogued, and then the attention withdraws. The water drop falls. The hum returns to its baseline. They return to their work.

What is their work? It is the maintenance of ascent. You see it now. One stands at the nexus of several converging silver veins in the wall, its hand-like appendage (a coalescence of darker static) resting on the glowing junction. The light-flow slows, then reverses direction, surging upward with renewed vigor. Another floats beside a large, irregular crystalline growth, its form mirroring the lattice’s structure. As you watch, a flaw in the crystal—a single misaligned strand—shimmers and re-knits itself into perfection. They are tuning the engine. They are ensuring the chamber’s primary, unfathomable function: the slow, eternal process hinted at in its name.

They are the Ascendants. Or perhaps they are what remains of those who once sought ascension, their individuality dissolved into the system’s functions. They are emotionless, but not lifeless. There is a profound, tranquil purpose in their movements. They are the awareness of the machine, and the machine is the shape of their world.

Occasionally, one will pause. It will stand on the edge of a platform, looking down (or up) into the haze-filled ends of the cylinder. In these moments of stillness, they seem most like statues—monuments to their own endless duty. Then, a chime will sound from somewhere deep below, a clear, pure tone that hangs in the thick air. The figure will turn its head, the silver light within its form pulsing in time with the tone, and then it will step off the platform. It does not fall. It simply un-makes its connection to that particular point in space and reappears elsewhere, already engaged in some new, minute adjustment.

They live here, but they do not *inhabit* it in the way a bird inhabits a nest. They are of it. Their consciousness is the chamber’s self-awareness. Their silent, relentless labor is the only prayer this place has ever known.
