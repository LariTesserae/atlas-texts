The place: a sunken city known to the few who have glimpsed it as **Ebonreach**. It lies not in water, but in a vast, lightless air pocket beneath the crust, a cavern so immense its ceiling is lost to a perpetual, starless night. The city itself is carved from a single, glassy black stone that seems to drink the faint luminescence of the fungal forests that cling to its spires. The air is still, cold, and carries the scent of ozone and deep, damp earth. There is no wind, only the occasional drip of mineral-heavy water from stalactites far above, each drop echoing in the profound silence.

What lives in Ebonreach are not creatures of flesh and blood as we know them. They are the **Stilt-Walkers**, the **Echo-kin**.

They are tall, impossibly slender, their bodies resembling bundles of polished, jointed obsidian rods held in a loose, tense configuration. They have no heads; instead, a cluster of faintly pulsing violet crystals rests where a neck might be, and from this cluster sprout several long, whip-like tendrils of a substance akin to frozen smoke. These tendrils are their senses, their manipulators, their voices. They move on limbs that end in sharp, precise points, tapping and clicking against the stone with a sound like distant chimes. Their "walk" is a stuttering, stilted gait, each movement sudden and precise, as if they are navigating an invisible grid.

They do not eat, but they absorb. They gather in the plazas where the fungal forests are thickest, extending their smoke-tendrils into the bioluminescent caps. The light dims, the fungi wither, and the Stilt-Walkers' core-crystals pulse brighter. They communicate through modulated hums transmitted through the stone itself, a language of vibration that shapes the slow drip of the cavern into faint, rhythmic patterns.

They are not hostile, not in any animal sense. They are curious. To them, a warm-blooded visitor is a riot of chaotic heat, sound, and scent—a fascinating anomaly. They will surround such an intruder, not to attack, but to study. Their tendrils will pass through the air inches from the visitor's skin, sampling the heat, the exhaled breath, the minute electrical field of a living body. They might, in their curiosity, gently probe with a pointed limb, leaving a cold, numb scratch. Their interest is absolute, and terribly patient. They have all the time in the world.

They are the architects of Ebonreach, though their construction is never seen. It is a slow, geological process. They channel the mineral-rich drips, guide the growth of the stone itself over millennia, coaxing new spires and arches from the living rock. They are tending to a city that is both their home and their monument, a frozen song in stone, played out across eons.

To see them is to see the dark made animate, a ecology of silence and subtle light, where life has dispensed with softness and embraced the enduring, crystalline chill of the deep earth.
