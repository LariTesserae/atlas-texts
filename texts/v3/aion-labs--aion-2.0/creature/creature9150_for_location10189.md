They do not have names, for names require a voice to speak them and a mind to conceive of separateness. Here, separateness is an illusion. The inhabitants of the throat are a single, breathing membrane of life, a collective skin stretched over the stone. They are less individuals than they are organs in a vast, slow-pulsing body.

The first layer is the Chorus-Crust: the uncountable billions of six-legged *tarnished ones*. Each is a carapace of dull brass, cold to the touch, with a dozen sensory hairs along its back that read the vibrations of the rock. They do not eat in any conventional sense. They are lithophages, their mouthparts delicate drills that tap into the quartz veins, sipping the mineral-laden moisture that beads there. Their movement is not random; it is a tide. The wave that sweeps across the wall is a feeding cycle, a collective shift from depleted veins to fresh ones, dictated by some chemical signal passed through the contact of legs. Their bioluminescence is not their own; they cultivate colonies of phosphorescent bacteria in microscopic pores on their backs, trading minerals for light. They are the bedrock of the ecosystem, the primary converters of stone into slow, cold life.

Above them drift the *Hummers*, the moth-like creatures. Their bodies are plump, furred with dense filaments that trap the constant mist. Their wings are not for graceful flight but for precise hovering; they are aerial harvesters. They drink from the weeping walls with proboscises like hypodermic needles, extracting the same mineral soup the *tarnished ones* drill for. Their hum is the sound of their wings beating in perfect, resonant synchrony—a sound that calms the predatory instincts of the larger hunters. They are floating larders, passive and stupid, their only defense their sheer numbers and the anesthetic quality of their fur, which carries a paralytic dust.

The hunters are many, and they are patient. The *Shriek-Spiders* are not true spiders; they are more like crablike ambushers with eight multi-jointed legs ending in hooked claws. They nest in the deeper fissures, their bodies the color of dried blood. They hunt the *Hummers*, launching themselves on strands of elastic, adhesive silk that they secrete from abdominal spigots. Their call is the shriek-click—a rapid vibration of a chitinous plate against the rock, used to coordinate pack takedowns of larger prey. They are intelligent in the way of wolves: tactical, communicative, but bound to the immediate.

Then there are the *Acid-Worms*. They are blind, pallid, segmented things as thick as a man's arm, moving in gelatinous concert. They are the chamber's sculptors. They exude a viscous fluid that smells of ozone and rot, which dissolves the obsidian into a slurry they then absorb, extracting rare metals and silicates. Their grinding drone is the sound of their internal gizzards, stones kept within their bodies to pulverize what the acid softens. They are slow, methodical, and their paths—smooth, glassy grooves in the rock—are the closest thing to roads this place has.

From the abyss below comes the whistle. That is the *Long-Throat*. It is a creature of the deep chimney, a serpentine predator thirty feet in length but no wider than a barrel. It is limbless, its body coated in a mucus that gleams with stolen bioluminescence. It has no eyes; its entire head is a parabolic dish of cartilage and skin, designed to focus sound. It "sees" through echolocation clicks of terrifying precision. It feeds on the *Acid-Worms* and the occasional careless *Shriek-Spider* colony, rising with terrifying silence, its jaw unhinging to reveal a ring of inward-curving teeth. It is the apex, a force of geological patience. It may sleep for months between feedings, metabolizing so slowly it seems dead.

But the intelligence… the nascent curiosity… that belongs to the *Glimmer-Fingers*. They are small, perhaps the size of a squirrel, and utterly unique in form. They have a central, flexible torso from which sprout six long, multi-jointed appendages. Each appendage ends not in a claw, but in a cluster of fine, prehensile tendrils that can manipulate with astonishing dexterity. Their skin is a deep, matte black, absorbing all light, making them ghosts against the obsidian. But their eyes—two large, forward-facing orbs—gleam with a captured, intelligent light. They are omnivores, scavengers, and observers. They are the ones who wear the sitting-depressions into the ledge. They watch the *Long-Throat's* migrations, the *Acid-Worms'* progress, the patterns of the *tarnished ones*. They are the puzzlers. They stack pebbles not as markers, but as experiments in balance and form. They collect the shed carapaces of the *tarnished ones* and arrange them in spirals on flat stones. They probe the blue pitcher fungi with sticks to see the larval things writhe. They have not yet conceived of a spear, or a wall, or a god. But they have conceived of *pattern*, and of *cause*, and of a self that is separate from the stone and the tide of life. They live in small, silent family groups in high, narrow caves, communicating through subtle shifts in the bioluminescent bacteria they cultivate on their own chests—a silent, flashing language of simple concepts: *danger*, *food*, *curiosity*, *sleep*.

They are the soul of the chamber, the first flicker of a mind in the dark. They breathe the metallic air, feel the slow tectonic dreams of the mountain, and in their silent, glowing conversations, they begin to wonder.
