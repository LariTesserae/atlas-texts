They call themselves the Spire-born, though in their own vibrating, clicking tongue the name is closer to *Krr-tak-kaan*, “the ones who hold the turning.” They are not human, though they share a bipedal form, a necessity for navigating the narrow, vertical world. Their physiology is a testament to deep, rapid adaptation to a realm of stone, metal, and resonant energy.

Their skin is the color and texture of the pale gray fungal mats—tough, rubbery, and slightly luminous. It is not flesh as much as a dense, photosynthetic hide, capable of absorbing faint nutrients and trace metals from the air and from direct contact with the mineral-rich walls. This integument is crisscrossed with fine, dark lines like circuit diagrams, patterns of conductive tissue that channel the static charges that constantly fizz in the dry air. A Spire-born caught in a swarm of spark-flies will often stand still, arms slightly spread, as the tiny creatures alight on these dark lines, their discharges feeding a mild, pleasant sensation and topping up the individual’s own bio-electrical reserve.

Their frames are slender but incredibly dense, bones laced with metallic sulfides, making them heavy for their size and resistant to the sudden, violent tremors that shake the Spire. Their joints are complex, multi-hinged affairs, allowing for articulation in every direction—crucial for bracing against curved walls or climbing across webs of piping. Their hands are long-fingered, with opposable thumbs and an extra, vestigial digit that acts as a delicate probe for testing circuit integrity or the flow of the silvery coolant. Their feet are broad and prehensile, capable of gripping narrow rails.

Their faces are dominated by large, multi-faceted eyes that gather every photon of the fungal glow. They have no external ears, but a line of sensitive pores along the jawbone detects vibrations in the air and rock—the hum of the generators, the groan of settling stone, the communicative clicks and subsonic pulses of their kind. They speak by modulating these vibrations, created by rubbing together plates of chitin in their throats, and by the deliberate flexing of the luminous patterns on their skin, a silent language of light.

Their society is a rigid, interdependent caste system, a necessary order imposed by perpetual crisis.

**The Gear-Wrights** are the highest caste, the engineers and architects. Their skin patterns are the most complex, often accentuated with inlaid flakes of obsidian or copper. They spend their lives mapping the shifting stresses in the mountain’s shell, designing the colossal braces and dynamic foundations that keep entire districts from shearing away into the abyss. They commune directly with the geothermal vents, interpreting their roars and pressures to regulate the Spire’s power. Their word is law, for a miscalculation means annihilation.

**The Fly-Herds** are the most nomadic. They are taller, with longer limbs, and their conductive lines glow a persistent, warm amber. They use specially-tuned harmonic rods to direct the great swarms of spark-flies, guiding them to where electrical stabilization is needed most, or harvesting them (gently, reverently) for their glass-like filaments and charged carapaces, essential components in the Spire’s analog technology. A Fly-Herd’s success is measured by the steadiness of the lights in their sector.

**The Fungal-Tenders (or Matriarchs/Patriarchs)** are the nourishers. They are often broader, with particularly thick, luminous hide. They cultivate the fungal gardens, scraping patches bare with crystal-tipped tools for the crawlers to then re-seed, encouraging specific growth patterns and light rhythms. They oversee the harvest, processing the rubbery mats into foodstuffs, insulation, and the base compounds for their few medicines. Their role is sacred; a failing garden means a starving district.

**The Scrappers and Salvagers** are the lowest, yet most numerous, caste. Their skin is often scarred and stained with coolant and soot. They are the miners of the interior, descending into the newest, most unstable fractures to retrieve raw ore, salvage usable metal from collapsed sectors, or brave the scorching vents to extract rare minerals. They are tethered to cables, their bodies often crudely augmented with reinforced limbs or extra bracing to survive falls or rockfalls. They live short, brutal lives, but the entire Spire depends on their daring.

**The Crawler-Wardens** are a specialized sub-caste, often children or the elderly. They maintain the symbiotic relationship with the armored crawlers, guiding them with chemical cues to keep the fungal mats in check and prevent overgrowth on crucial machinery. They are the quiet gardeners of the gears.

Life is communal and claustrophobic. Families don’t own space; they are allocated a cubicle, a platform, a section of wall. Privacy is an alien concept. The constant background noise—the hiss of coolant, the rustle of spark-flies, the deep thrum of the earth—is their silence. Their art is one of functional beauty: the precise patterning of a brace, the harmonic song of a well-tuned fly-herd’s rod, the deliberate, rhythmic pulse of light from a tended fungal wall.

They are a people without a past beyond the Spire, and with a future measured in the stability of the next gear-tooth, the next tectonic sigh. They are not hopeful, nor are they despairing. They are *necessary*. They are the calculus that balances the chaos of a newborn mountain. To live in the Spire of Gears is to be a temporary, thinking part of a vast, unstable machine, your every breath synchronized with the hiss of silver fluid and the distant, fiery breath of the world below.
