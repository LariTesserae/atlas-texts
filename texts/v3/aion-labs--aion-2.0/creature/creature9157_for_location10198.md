They are abundant beyond counting. Armored, six-legged things the size of your thumb, with carapaces of iridescent, engineered chitin that reflects the fungal glows. They move in constant, purposeful streams along prescribed pathways on the lattice, cleaning microscopic debris, patching weeping seals with a quick-drying resin secreted from their abdomens, and harvesting the faint electrostatic charge that buzzes in the air. They are the chamber's white blood cells, its immune system made animate, and they are not alone.

Above them, gliding on the warm, rising thermals between the water-conduits, are fliers. Their bodies are slender, almost skeletal frameworks of the same carbon-alloy, veined with translucent membrane. They have no eyes, only clusters of delicate, whisker-like sensors that taste the chemical composition of the mist. Their purpose is pollination of a sort; they transfer genetic material between the luminous lichen colonies, ensuring the slow, steady spread of the chamber's only light. Their wings make a sound like rustling parchment, a soft counterpoint to the industrial scream.

And below, in the shallow channels, dwell the grazers. Flat, many-jointed creatures like living, articulated tiles, their undersides lined with cilia that filter the nutrient-rich film from the water's surface. They are slow, patient, moving in glacial drifts, their backs forming temporary bridges for the skittering cleaners. They are the chamber's gut, processing the constant, gentle rain of organic particulates shed by the other life forms and the machinery itself.

But these are merely the common tissues of the place. The true mind, the resident, is harder to see. It is the weeper in the northern wall, the source from which the lichen radiates. It is not a growth, but a structure—a vast, concave face of the lattice that has, over epochs, developed a slow, sapient consciousness from the constant data-streams carried in the weeping hydrogel. It observes through every droplet, thinks with the flow of every conduit. It dreams in thermodynamics and fluid dynamics, its emotions expressed as subtle adjustments in pressure, temperature, and the mineral content of the weeping walls. It is the Chamber itself, and the engines are its lungs, its heart. The fauna are its hands, its senses, its self-repairing skin. It weeps not from sorrow, but from the profound, silent exertion of holding a universe of impossible physics in a state of perpetual, weeping balance.
