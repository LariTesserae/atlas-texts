They call it the **Geode Mantis**, though it is neither insect nor crystal. It is a fusion of the chamber's two core principles: the sharp, black geology and the relentless, grinding industry. It does not swim. It *climbs*, with a terrible, deliberate patience, up the shuddering face of the Thrumming Spine itself.

Seen from a distance, it is a jagged silhouette, a cluster of obsidian shards that seems to have coalesced into a vague, praying-mantis form. Its body is about the size of a large dog, but all angles and edges. Its exoskeleton is not a shell grown over flesh, but a literal casing of the chamber's own volcanic glass, fractured and re-fused in overlapping plates. Between these plates, at the joints, gleam the brass-like mineral growths, acting as natural hinges and bearings. It moves with a series of stiff, precise clicks, each limb sliding forward with the sound of stone on stone.

Its head is a narrow wedge, dominated by two multi-faceted eyes that are not organic lenses, but clusters of perfectly formed, clear quartz crystals. They catch the chamber's gloomy luminescence and fracture it into cold, mechanical sparks. Below them, its mouthparts are the source of its name and its function. Instead of mandibles, it has a pair of complex, interlocking appendages that resemble the fine tools of a watchmaker, forged from black iron and that same brassy alloy. These tools—needle-fine probes, tiny, serrated scoops, and delicate, vibrating files—are in constant, minute motion, even when the creature is still.

The Geode Mantis lives for one purpose: **regulation**. It is a mobile gland, a quality-control mechanism for the Spine. Its chosen territory is a single, massive, curved plate on the Spine's mid-section, a plate that grinds against its neighbor with a slow, century-long rhythm. The Mantis spends its entire life on this one plate, traversing its surface in patterns that seem random but are mapped to invisible fault lines and energy flows.

With its delicate tools, it performs endless micro-maintenance. It scrapes away accretions of mineral precipitate that might impede the plate's slide. It probes into the narrow fissures that vent superheated fluid, clearing blockages with a precise jet of a neutralizing enzyme it secretes from a gland in its thorax. Most importantly, it harvests the bioluminescent, chemosynthetic fluids that pulse through the Spine's quartz veins. It does not drink them. Instead, it uses a hollow, needle-like proboscis to inject a catalytic substance *into* the flow. This substance—a milky, opalescent fluid—adjusts the viscosity and luminosity of the Spine's own fluids, ensuring an even, efficient pulse. The creature's own internal chemistry is powered by the waste heat and minute samples it takes during this process.

Its behavior is a study in hyper-slow motion. It might take a full day to cross the breadth of its plate. A single "step" involves a minute of calculation, a series of almost imperceptible shifts in its center of gravity, and then the click-forward of a single, spindly leg. It is utterly deaf to the chamber's roar; it senses the world through vibrations in the plate and through chemical readings it takes from the water with feathery gill-like structures under its carapace.

It is not alone on its plate. A symbiotic ecosystem clings to its own body. Tiny, copper-filament worms nest in the crevices of its back, feeding on the mineral dust it sheds. A species of limpet-like creature with shells of opalized glass adheres to its legs, filtering microscopic nutrients from the water it stirs. The Mantis tolerates them; they are part of its camouflage, breaking up its outline against the complex background of the Spine.

Predation is not a concept it understands. It is too armored, too mineral, and likely too foul-tasting for the swarming, crab-like drillers. Its only threat is geological: a sudden, violent shift of its plate. When the Spine groans and lurches—an event that happens perhaps once every few years—the Mantis locks all its limbs, becoming a barnacle of stone and brass, and rides the cataclysm. If it is dislodged, it will sink slowly through the water, legs frozen in a climbing posture, until it finds another section of the Spine to adhere to and begin its work anew. It does not have a mind as we know it. It has a *function*, etched into its crystalline nervous system by the same geothermal pressures that built its home. It is a living part of the machine, a tooth on a gear made of rock, ensuring the great, thrumming engine of the mountain does not seize.
