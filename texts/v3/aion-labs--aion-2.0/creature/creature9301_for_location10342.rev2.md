There is a presence here, but it is not a creature. It is the slow turning of the gears, the drip of condensation, the patient growth of roots. It has no name, no voice. It is the thing that causes the plants to turn inward, to weave that language of shapes. It is the one that adjusts the metal joints. It has no eyes, but it sees through every droplet of water. It has no hands, but it moves the steam through the pipes. It is old. So old it has forgotten that it was not always here. When the miners left, when the last human stepped away, it began to breathe on its own. Now it is the only thing that lives here. Not an animal, not a plant, not a machine. It is the sum of all three, and something more. It drifts through the chambers, formless, yet concentrated in the motion of the ferns. It is the rhythm. You feel it in your teeth. You sense it at the edge of your vision, just before you turn to look. But you never see it. It does not want to be seen. It simply is. And it has no need of you. You are the intruder. It will continue its slow, eternal dance with or without you.
