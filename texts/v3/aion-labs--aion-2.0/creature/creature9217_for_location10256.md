The true inhabitants of the Shifting Table are not its plants, nor its chattering, clicking fauna. They are the ones who listen. They are the Watchers of the Still Point, though there is no stillness. Their name for themselves is a vibration, a subsonic hum that resonates in the hollow bones of the Whisper-Spires. To an outsider, they are the cloaked figures, the menders of walls, the tuners of wind.

Physically, they are tall and attenuated, their skeletons lightweight and flexible, evolved to withstand the constant buffeting and to move with eerie silence through the roaring clearings. Their skin is a matte, stone-grey membrane, thick and pebbled like the basalt itself, capable of absorbing and diffusing the plateau’s peculiar energies. They have two primary eyes, large and dark, adapted to the perpetual twilight, but a ridge of photosensitive nodes runs from brow to the base of their skull, allowing them to perceive the shimmering thermal rivers in the air and the bio-luminescent signatures of the flora. Their hands are long-fingered, with an extra joint, and the tips are hardened into a tough, chitinous cup, perfect for grinding, shaping, and stacking the smooth stones of the plateau. They breathe slowly, extracting the scant moisture from the air through a system of internal bladders; their own water parameter is barely higher than the environment’s.

They are the inheritors of the scattered built parameter. Their society is not one of villages, but of circuits and nodes. A family or clutch—never more than ten individuals—maintains a circuit of perhaps a dozen shelters and several key standing stones across a few square kilometers. They are semi-nomadic within their circuit, staying in a given shelter only until the wind-song it channels grows discordant or a new bloom of the sky-blue, nutrient-rich fungus erupts nearby. Their tools are the plateau itself: stone scrapers, hooks of tendon, ropes of braided fiber. But their real technology is acoustic and empathic. They “read” the stone by placing their foreheads against it, feeling the deep, resonant frequencies that travel through the basalt—the footfalls of grazers, the growth-roots of the bell-plants, the distant, tectonic sighs of the Table itself. They “tune” the standing stones, minute adjustments of angle and balance, to harmonize the wind’s roar into specific, beneficial patterns: a tone that discourages the iridescent rag-flocks from nesting in a particular grove, a vibration that stimulates the fungal growth they cultivate.

Their relationship with the frantic fauna is one of studied, mutualistic neutrality. They are not hunters, nor are they prey. They are facilitators, and occasionally, harvesters. The six-limbed clickers scatter from their path, not in fear, but in a kind of reflexive acknowledgment. The energy parasites—pulsing, jelly-like blobs that latch onto the mirrored grazers and siphon their static charge—are carefully collected after they detach, their stored energy used to power the faint, luminous lichen the Watchers cultivate inside their shelters for light. The ambush dischargers, fern-like creatures that explode with a concussive snap of bioluminescent energy to stun smaller flyers, are given a wide berth; the Watchers can sense their building charge. The symbiotic harassers—swarms of insectoid creatures that pick parasites off the grazers—are sometimes attracted to the Watchers’ own stone-grey hides, providing a grooming service.

The cloaks they wear, woven from the shed membranes of the mature rag-flyers, are not for warmth. They are sonic dampeners and collectors. The iridescent material scatters and absorbs specific frequencies, allowing the Watcher within to focus on a single stream of sound in the cacophony—a distant call, the health of a Whisper-Spire, the approach of a weather front in the haze. The cloaks also serve as passive nets, collecting the fine, moisture-laden filaments shed by the plants at dawn.

Their purpose is existential. They believe, or perhaps they know, that the Shifting Table is a living, breathing entity. The turbulence is its mind, the flora its lungs, the fauna its thoughts. Their role is to listen to that mind, to smooth the edges of chaotic thoughts, and to ensure the great breath does not still. They are the plateau’s conscious immune system, pruning the occasional malevolent growth (there are rumors of a carnivorous, mobile vine that seeks the heat of their shelters) and encouraging beneficial symphonies. They record nothing in any permanent medium; their history is held in the changing wind-songs, passed down through the vibration of stone against stone during the long nights.

They are few. Perhaps no more than two hundred exist on the entire Table. They are born, they tend their circuits, they listen, and when their bodies fail, they are taken to the Edge. There, in a ceremony of silence amidst the roar, their remains are offered to the slow-rolling cloud, absorbed back into the system. They live on, not as ghosts, but as a slight, permanent alteration in the plateau’s resonant frequency—a new, faint harmonic in the world’s breath.
