The inhabitants of the Everglow Caverns are not separate from the green; they are its slow, sentient rhythm. They are called the Stillborn by the few surface-dwellers who have glimpsed them, but they have no name for themselves. They are the children of the equilibrium, and they are as unchanging as the stone.

They are humanoid in only the broadest sense—tall, slender forms that seem woven from the cavern’s own substances. Their skin is a smooth, bark-like integument, translucent in patches, revealing the soft amber glow of their internal fluids—the same light that pulses in the Everglow Trees. This light is not constant; it dims and brightens in a cycle that may last a decade, a slow tide of luminescence that mirrors no sun or season, only some deep, metabolic clock. Their hair, if it can be called that, is a cascading growth of fine, velvety moss, ranging from deepest emerald to pale, ghostly silver. It never sheds.

Their faces are smooth planes, devoid of nostrils or external ears. They have mouths—thin, lipless seams that remain sealed—and eyes. The eyes are the most striking feature: large, lidless orbs of solid, crystalline clarity, like lenses of polished quartz. They do not blink. They reflect the cavern’s unwavering light, but give back no spark of independent thought—only a profound, imperturbable observation. They see everything, and they see nothing. They are sensors, not windows to a soul.

They do not walk so much as translocate. Movement is a rare event, measured in geological time. For centuries, a Stillborn might stand, rooted not literally but in intent, in a hollow woven by the vines, one hand resting on the trunk of a tree, a living part of the circuit between geothermal heat and light. When they do move, it is with a silent, gliding slowness that disturbs not a single mote of mist. Their feet, broad and pad-like, merge with the spongy mat of the floor, leaving no imprint. They might cross the cavern’s vast breadth once in a millennium, their purpose inscrutable—perhaps to touch a dying leaf (though nothing dies) and return it to the cycle, perhaps to simply be in a different place in the eternal pattern.

They are few. No more than a dozen exist in the entire expanse, though counting them is impossible, for they are often indistinguishable from the trees when at rest. They wear no clothing, carry no tools. Their only artifacts are those few, enigmatic structures—the monolith, the wall, the staircase—and these they ignore. If they built them, the memory of that building has been absorbed by the stasis, rendered irrelevant. Now, they only tend, in a way that is indistinguishable from non-action.

Their tending is a form of resonance. They exist in a state of perpetual, silent communion with the cavern. The gentle rain of condensation beads on their moss-hair and runs down their limbs in rivulets that are immediately reabsorbed by the substrate. The glassy, worm-like creatures sometimes coil around their ankles or wrists, a temporary symbiosis where the worms feed on minuscule secretions from their skin. The great, pale moths occasionally alight on their shoulders, fanning wings that cast no shadow, before drifting away.

They do not eat. They do not sleep. They do not reproduce. They simply *are*. They are the guardians of the rules, though the rules need no guarding. They enforce the Geometry of Density by their very presence—where they stand, the vegetation grows in perfect, ordered arrays. They embody the Memory in Stone and Sap; in their crystalline eyes, the sharp angles of the cavern’s birth are reflected and preserved. They are the living expression of the Light is Life cycle, conduits for the energy that sustains all.

To encounter one is to feel the full, silent weight of the Everglow. It will turn its quartz eyes upon you, and in that gaze, you will not find hostility or curiosity. You will find only a perfect, unbearable mirror of the cavern itself: hot, bright, dense, and still. It will not speak. It will not move toward you or away. It will simply observe until you, a thing of change and breath, become an irrelevance in the eternal equation, and leave. And when you are gone, the Stillborn will return to its vigil, a statue of flesh and light, waiting for nothing, in a world where nothing ever happens.
