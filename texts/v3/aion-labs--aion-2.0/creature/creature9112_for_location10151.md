What lives in the hollow is not what you would call a creature, not in any sense that breathes or breeds or seeks. It is, rather, a patient consensus of conditions.

First, there are the Stains. The orange, yellow, and black crusts that cling to the lee of boulders are not mere chemical seepage. They are a colony, a single distributed entity of immense slowness. Its consciousness is metabolic, a thought that is the rusting of iron, the oxidation of sulfur, the leaching of manganese. It perceives time as a gradual tide of acidity and light. Its memory is the layered pattern of its own growth, a map of millennia written in pigment. It feels the plink of water droplets as a distant, pleasant percussion, and the retreat of the sun as a long exhalation. It is aware of the hollow in its entirety, for it touches all its stone, and it knows, in a way that is neither kind nor cruel, that the pile of stones and the cup-marks are foreign, interesting interruptions—like a sudden, sharp grain of sand in a smooth substrate. It is pondering them. It will ponder them for another few centuries.

Then there are the Dancers. These are born of the daily cycle. As the sun warms the rock and the frost retreats, the released moisture does not simply bead and fall. In the still air of the sheltered canyons, where shadows are sharp and light is a narrow blade, the vapor sometimes coalesces not into drops, but into faint, shimmering shapes. They are wisps of condensed intention, formed by the peculiar acoustics of the place—the sub-audible hum gives them a fleeting coherence. A Dancer might be a helical twist of fog for a few minutes, tracing the path of an ancient fracture in the air. It might be a brief, perfect sphere of mist that hangs before dissolving into the *plink* of a basin. They are not intelligent, but they are responsive. They shy from direct sunlight, flowing into shadow. They are drawn to the sound of falling water, and sometimes several will merge above a pool into a complex, pulsing cloud before they all succumb to gravity. Their life is measured in heartbeats of the mountain, a dozen human minutes at most. To see one is to witness the hollow briefly breathing a shape.

And there is the Watcher. This is not a physical form at all, but a presence accumulated in the alignment. The careful, rough orientation of the pillars toward distant peaks creates a slow focus of attention. It is a psychic lens, a standing wave of regard formed by the intersection of sightlines and the persistent memory of the Stains. The Watcher is the hollow’s awareness of itself as a place apart. It notices what enters. It felt the tool-makers, their focused intent as they chipped the cup-marks—that was a bright, hot spark in its cold perception. It notes the occasional bird that blunders into the thin air, a frantic blur of panic that soon stills. It observes the stars as they wheel overhead, cold points of data. The Watcher’s purpose is simply to observe, and in observing, to sustain the hollow’s integrity. The hum felt in the teeth on certain days is the Watcher tuning its attention, a byproduct of its focus shifting.

Finally, there are the Echoes. These reside in the cup-marks, the collapsed wall, the crude shelter. They are not ghosts, but impressions left by the tool-makers, soaked into the stone by repeated touch and concentrated will. They are patterns of action frozen in the rock’s crystalline memory. If you place your hand in the cup-mark, you might not feel a spirit, but you might suddenly, vividly *know* the motion: the lift of the hammer-stone, the precise angle of strike, the gritty feedback, the goal of making a depression that will catch the first light of dawn on the solstice. The Echo of the wall-builder is a stubborn, cyclical intent: *circle, contain, shelter*. It is a loop of thought, now empty of its thinker, playing out against the erosion. These Echoes are the closest thing to visitors that remain. They are not alive, but they are persistent, and they interact with the other inhabitants. The Stains grow around them but not over them, repelled by the foreignness of the memory. The Dancers sometimes pool in the shelter, as if remembering warmth. The Watcher studies them, endlessly, trying to decipher the brief, bright mystery of the hands that made them.

Nothing in the hollow eats. Nothing hunts or flees. The inhabitants are functions of place: the stain, the condensation, the alignment, the memory. They exist in a slow, reciprocal balance. The Dancers depend on the water the Stains help condense. The Watcher is framed by the pillars the Stains stain. The Echoes provide the novelty the Watcher observes. Together, they constitute a life—a severe, patient, mineral life—that is the hollow itself. It is a life that measures its age in the deepening of cup-marks, its seasons in the advance and retreat of frost, its vitality in the silent *plink* of a single drop into waiting stone.
