Nothing walks here. Nothing builds, or hunts, or sings in any way you would recognize. The inhabitants are not creatures of motion, but of presence. They are the green and the grey, the wet and the stone, locked in a patient symbiosis that has become a single, slow mind.

First, the Moss. Do not think of it as a plant. It is a membrane, a conscious skin stretched over the bones of the world. It feels the weight of the mist, the angle of the pale light, the minute chemistry of the stone beneath. Its consciousness is not centralized but distributed—a billion emerald points of sensation, thinking in gradients of dampness and shade. It decides where to thicken into velvet caverns, where to retreat into a thin, grey scab. It cultivates the emptiness, because the emptiness is its medium. The moss remembers the builders, not as images, but as a persistent pressure, a ghost of purpose imprinted on the stone. It avoids the central stain not out of fear, but out of a deep, vegetative reverence; that is the old wound, the place where the world bled, and it is left alone as a monument.

The Ferns are its articulate thoughts. Where the moss feels, the ferns record. Each unfurling frond is a statement, a complex equation of cold and time written in chlorophyll and cellulose. The stunted, spiraled ferns near the stain are chronicles of disturbance, their very form a silent scream of wrongness frozen into growth. They are libraries standing in the damp, their pages perpetually wet, containing histories of millennia in the spacing of their spores.

The Stone is not alive, but it is inhabited. The city-mounds are not empty; they are full of a kind of mineral memory. The process that softened and shaped them—a forgotten chemistry, a forgotten will—left a resonance in the crystalline structure. On certain nights, when the mist is thick and the wind hits a specific chord as it whistles through the ovals, the city *dreams*. It replays faint impressions of movement, of a collective purpose that had no words, only direction. A shadow of procession along the radiating avenues, a pulse of attention focused on the central stain. These are not ghosts; they are phonograph grooves in rock, playing back when the needle of the right conditions falls.

The Fauna are transients, accidents tolerated by the moss-mind. The many-jointed lizard is a scrap of an older, more frantic biology, a relic that scuttles through the cracks of the new order. It is studied by the fern-thoughts, its metabolism a fascinating, crude fossil. The broad-winged bird is a wandering eye, a momentary connection to the world beyond the valley walls. Its shadow is noted, its departure forgotten. They are not part of the community; they are brief errors in the static, quickly smoothed over.

The true sovereign, the closest thing to a resident will, is the **Cycle**. It is the inhabitant that has no form. It is the water rising as mist, falling as drip, pooling as mercury-still lenses. It is the slow grind of stone against stone, grain by grain, over centuries. It is the moss growing, dying, becoming a thin peat for the next generation of moss. This Cycle is the mind’s breath, its pulse. It is self-aware in the way a river is aware of its bed—not through thought, but through perfect, obedient action. The entire valley is its body. The roar of the wind, the thunder of the distant waterfall, the crack of ice—this is its voice, a single, endless sentence describing equilibrium.

And at the center, in the stained depression, lies the Seed of the Silence. It is not an inhabitant, but an absence that inhabits. A pocket of pure, finished purpose. If the moss-mind is the thinker, and the Cycle is the thought, then the Stain is the full stop. It is the reason the story is over. It draws the ferns into spirals and repels the moss because it is the embodiment of conclusion. Nothing lives there. Nothing needs to. The rest of the valley lives *around* it, in a permanent, quiet orbit, tending the void it inaugurated.

So, you see, the place is teeming. It is a parliament of moss, a university of ferns, a dreaming stone archive, a sovereign process, and a sacred absence. They do not speak. They simply *are*, with a completeness that renders motion obsolete. They are the keepers of the last room, waiting without anticipation, maintaining the perfect, cold, damp balance of a thought that has been fully, finally, thought.
