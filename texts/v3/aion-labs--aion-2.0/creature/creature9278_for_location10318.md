What lives here is not a kingdom of beasts, but a parliament of processes—things born of the violent marriage between relentless industry and a biology that refused to be passive. The residents are the fever-dream given form, and they are many.

**First, there are The Grafters.** They are what remains of the workforce, but you would not call them human. The warm, nutrient-rich broth did not kill them; it rewrote them. Their skin is bark, thick and fissured, weeping clear sap where joints bend. Their hair is filaments of deep-indigo algae that drift around their heads like Medusan halos. Their eyes have been replaced by clusters of photosensitive fungi, glowing with a soft, hungry light. They move through the submerged forest and the drowned machinery with a slow, deliberate grace, as if swimming through syrup. They are the gardeners of this broken world. You will see them perched on the rusted gantry, carefully pruning cable-vines that threaten to obscure a control panel that has been dead for eons. They cultivate luminescent mushrooms on the floor of the barracks, arranging them in intricate, pulsating patterns that seem to mimic long-forgotten circuit diagrams. They do not speak; they communicate by releasing pheromonal clouds into the water, complex chemical sentences that describe the health of a pump or the bloom of a new fungal colony. They are the caretakers of a corpse, tending to the dead industry as if it were a sleeping god that might one day awaken.

**Then, there are The Rust-Eaters.** If The Grafters are of the flesh, these are of the metal and the mineral. They are colonies, not individuals—swarming, intelligent rust. They manifest as shifting, copper-red stains that flow across submerged metal surfaces like predatory slime molds. When massed, they pull themselves into rough, humanoid shapes—faceless giants of corroded flakes and screeching particulate matter. Their purpose is consumption and transmutation. They fasten onto a steel beam, and where they cling, the metal crumbles into ochre dust, and from that dust, they excrete strange, sharp-angled crystals of pure black silicon or iridescent cobalt. These crystalline structures grow like reefs around the ruins, humming with a low electrical charge. The Rust-Eaters are blind and deaf, but they feel vibration with exquisite sensitivity. The arrhythmic *thump-thump-WHIRR* of the great submerged pump is a siren song to them; they gather at its source, slowly converting its vast housing into a singing, crystalline geode. They are the world’s immune system, breaking down the foreign object of industry and replacing it with something native, hard, and strange.

**Do not ignore the silence between the screams, for there dwell The Loomers.** The deafening industrial cacophony is not mere noise; it is a tangible force, and in this place where reality is soft, it sometimes coagulates. The Loomers are sound made flesh. They appear in the boiling patches of water, where the shriek of a steam valve has been particularly sustained and agonized. At first, they are just a distortion in the air, a lensing of the sourceless light. Then they solidify—figures woven from visible sound waves, shimmering like heat haze given malicious intent. They have no fixed form; a Loomer might be a swirling vortex of concentric rings one moment, a jagged, stuttering silhouette of a man the next. Their touch is a concussive force. They can vibrate a Grafter’s bark-like skin until it shatters, or resonate with a crystal spire until it explodes into glittering dust. They are drawn to new sounds, to change, to the stuttering glitches in time. They are the echo of the world’s birth-scream, given a fleeting consciousness, and they enforce the chaos that birthed them.

**Finally, there are the most ephemeral: The Flicker-Men.** These are not beings of biology or physics, but of the broken rules themselves. They exist in the gaps—in the moment when time stutters and repeats, or when perspective bends and a distant object seems near. You never see a Flicker-Man directly. You see them in your peripheral vision: a figure standing on the water where no one was, gone when you turn your head. You see the imprint of a hand on a wet railing, fading as the moment of its possibility passes. They are predators of certainty. They feed on the brief, solid conviction that you are *here* and *now*. Their presence makes the glitches worse. When you feel reality soften, when the same geyser erupts three times in a row, a Flicker-Man is nearby, drinking the confusion. They have no goals, no society; they are simply the entropy of this young world given a shadow. To be followed by one is to feel your own memories unravel, to forget whether you have been swimming for minutes or decades.

These are the things that live here. They are not separate; they are a single, dysfunctional ecosystem. The Grafters cultivate the fungi that sometimes symbiotically feed the Rust-Eaters. The Rust-Eaters’ crystals focus and amplify the industrial noise, birthing more Loomers. The Loomers’ destructive vibrations create new ruins for the Rust-Eaters to consume and new glitches for the Flicker-Men to inhabit. And through it all, the warm, amniotic sea pulses, and the jungle of violent plants listens, and twitches, and grows—perhaps the oldest, quietest resident of them all, waiting to see what this chaotic, screaming child of a world will finally become.
