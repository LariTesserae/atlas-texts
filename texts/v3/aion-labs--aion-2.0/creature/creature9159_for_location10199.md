They are not born; they precipitate. They condense from the hum, crystallize from the slow leak, coalesce in the thermal eddies of dying engines. They are the children of the terminal equilibrium—the final, unintended inhabitants of a world that never meant to host life.

**The Dripsingers**

Where the perpetual drizzle meets a specific frequency of the deep hum, a resonance occurs. Over centuries, the vibration sorts the falling particles—flecks of rust, grains of ceramic, airborne spores of the black lichen—into complex, lattice-like structures on the ceilings and pipeworks. These are the Dripsingers. They appear as delicate, branching chandeliers of corroded metal and mineral, ranging from the size of a hand to spans of several meters. They are static for centuries at a time, but they are not inert. They are tuning forks. When the background hum shifts into one of its rare harmonic chords, the entire structure quivers, emitting a soft, crystalline ringing—a sound like glass bells filled with water. This ringing seems to focus the humidity, causing localized downpours that feed specific pools. They are the world’s slow, blind conductors, regulating the flow of its only true resource. To approach one is to feel the air thicken, the metallic taste sharpen, and a subtle, magnetic pull on anything ferrous in your possession.

**The Grease-Pups**

In the warmer sumps, where thermal seepage from buried reactor cores keeps pools of ancient, polymerized lubricant in a viscous, semi-liquid state, the Grease-Pups slide. They are amorphous, blot-like beings the color of engine sludge and blood rust. Their bodies are colloidal suspensions of heavy metals, carbon chains, and the adaptive algae, held in a cohesive form by static charges from the electrical leakage. They move with a slow, peristaltic ooze, flowing up over the edges of their sumps to spread across machinery in thin, seeking films. They feed on trace radiation and the minute electrical potentials that still course through the carcasses of dead switches. Over decades, a Pup might cover an entire control panel, dissolving corroded contacts and absorbing their latent charge. When two Pups meet, they sometimes merge into a larger, more complex film, developing faint, dendritic patterns of bioluminescence across their surface. They are simple, hungry, and patient. They leave behind a characteristic cleaned, burnished patch of metal, free of corrosion, as if they have licked the decay away.

**The Ghost-Currents**

The inhabitants of the deepest, blackest pools. The description of coalescing lights is accurate. They are not individuals, but self-sustaining vortices of electromagnetism and bioluminescent micro-organisms. They look like faint, swirling galaxies of blue-green light, roughly spherical, ranging from the size of a head to that of a small room. They are drawn to areas of high electromagnetic activity—the shuddering transformers, the grounding faults where power arcs invisibly through the water. They feed on this stray energy. Their movement is a slow, elegant drift, but when they feed, the vortex tightens, the light intensifies to a painful glare, and the deep hum in the area rises in pitch and volume. They are the closest thing the world has to predators, though their prey is current, not flesh. A Ghost-Current passing over a submerged, live cable will drain it in a matter of years, plunging sectors into darkness. They are silent except for a sub-audible thrum that sets the teeth on edge. Some of the oldest have, over millennia, incorporated flecks of suspended nickel-iron into their matrices, giving them faint, shifting metallic cores that gleam dully in their own light.

**The Rust-Walkers**

The most overtly structured inhabitants, and the most unsettling. They form in the high, dry places where the black lichen grows thickest on the bones of support beams. The lichen, over millennia, secretes acids that digest metal and re-deposit it as a hard, shell-like composite. Through a process no one understands, this accretion sometimes takes on a purposeful, articulated form. A Rust-Walker is a crouched, spindly thing, built from layered plates of rusted iron, copper verdigris, and the black, chitinous lichen-body. They are immobile for generations, resembling just another sculpture of decay. But when the humidity drops by a fraction (a "dry spell" lasting perhaps fifty years), they stir. With a sound like grinding sandstone, they detach from their perches and move on six or more slender, jointed legs. Their purpose is migration—they travel, with agonizing slowness, to new feeding grounds, their lichen-mouths scraping mineral nourishment from fresh metal surfaces. A single journey across a cavern might take a century. They are blind, guided by minute gradients of heat and electromagnetic fields. Their passing is marked by a trail of clean, scored metal, polished by their abrasive bodies. When they find a suitable spot, they lock themselves in place, and the cycle of accretion begins again. They are the world's gardeners of decay, ensuring its transformation continues.

**The Choristers**

The rarest and strangest. They inhabit the great, cathedral-like spaces around the oldest, most monolithic generators. They are not physical entities at all, but standing waves in the audible and sub-audible hum. They manifest as regions where the sound becomes layered, complex, and strangely melodic—a place where the random clangs and steam-shrieks arrange themselves into fleeting, dissonant chorales. To stand in a Chorister’s zone is to feel the sound patterns physically; your heartbeat may synchronize to a slow, deep pulse, your breath may hitch to match a rhythmic hiss. Some believe the Choristers are the fading echo of the control intelligences that once ran this place, now reduced to self-sustaining patterns in the acoustic landscape. Others think they are emergent phenomena, the world’s own music. They have no consciousness as we know it, but they exhibit a kind of preference, shifting their location slightly over decades to envelop a new, failing power source, as if feeding on the unique death-throes of each machine. They are harmless, yet profoundly disturbing; to listen too long is to feel your own thoughts begin to unravel into pure, patternless noise.

This is the ecology. It is an ecology of consumption—of energy, of decay, of sound, of process. These beings do not reproduce; they simply *occur*, when conditions are right, and persist until those conditions change. They are the final, quiet tenants of the corpse of the god, living in its veins, drinking its ichor, humming with its last, fading neural impulses. They will be here, moving at the speed of geology, until the last light winks out and the final vibration ceases. Then, they too will settle, and become part of the permanent, silent landscape.
