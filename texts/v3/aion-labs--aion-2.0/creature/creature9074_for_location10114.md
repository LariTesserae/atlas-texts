They are not a people of the light, nor of the swift. They are the **Silt-Readers**, the **Current’s Children**, and they have turned the austerity of the abyss into a kind of civilization.

Do not imagine cities. Their dwellings are **temporary**. They follow the great, slow drifts of nutrient-rich upwellings, the sporadic “blooms” of bacterial mats that are their croplands. A settlement might be a cluster of **burrows** excavated into the lee-side of a silt-hill, where the current’s force is broken. The entrances are marked by those cairns of smooth, dark stone—not as territory markers, but as current-breaks and navigation aids, their shapes telling of the flow direction and the safety of the hollow within. The burrows themselves are soon reclaimed; the silt, in its infinite patience, fills them within a generation.

### Physiology

They are elongated, sinuous beings, their bodies a map of pressure adaptation. Their bones are not brittle calcium, but a flexible, cartilage-like matrix reinforced with silicate deposits, gleaned from the glass sponges. They appear **ghost-pale**, their skin translucent over a web of dark, slow-circulating blood. They have no eyes. In the absolute dark, sight is a forgotten extravagance. Instead, their world is perceived through a hypersensitive **lateral line system** that reads the minutest perturbations in the water—the approach of prey, the shift of a current, the collapse of a distant silt slope. Their faces are dominated by a wide, lipless mouth lined with fine, comb-like teeth for filter-feeding on suspended particulates, and a secondary set of stronger jaws for crunching through crustacean carapaces.

Their “hands” are their most distinctive feature. Four pairs of long, jointed appendages, each ending in a fan of delicate, tactile filaments. These filaments can detect the texture, density, and temperature of the silt with a sensitivity that borders on clairvoyance. A Silt-Reader can press a filament-hand into the seabed and “read” the history of the past week’s currents, the passage of creatures, even the faint chemical signature of a bacterial patch meters away. Their movement is a slow, graceful undulation, a constant negotiation with the water’s weight. They rarely swim against the flow; they travel in its margins, using eddies as shortcuts.

### Society and Intelligence

Their intelligence is **ecological**, not technological. It is focused on memory, pattern recognition, and symbiosis. Their language is a complex series of pressure waves, clicks, and low-frequency hums, conveying not just immediate information but layered data about currents, resource locations, and historical events. A single greeting might encode the speaker’s origin current, the bounty of their last foraging, and a warning about a predatory anglerfish two hills over.

Their “tech level 0.8” manifests in tools of profound simplicity and perfect adaptation:
*   **The Current-Hook:** A shard of rock or bone, lashed with tough sponge-fiber to a heavier stone. Not a weapon, but an anchor. They place them in strategic points to tether themselves during violent upwellings, or to hold position while “reading” the silt.
*   **The Sift-Basket:** A woven mesh of fibrous bacterial threads and their own shed filaments, used to gently sift the silt for specific micronutrients or tiny, fragile organisms.
*   **The Lure-Light:** A carefully curated colony of bioluminescent bacteria, housed in a hollowed-out nodule of rock. Used not for vision, but for communication over longer distances—a specific pulse pattern can summon a hunting party or signal danger. The light is used sparingly; energy is sacred, and light attracts more than it assists.

They have no concept of individual ownership, only of **usefulness**. A tool is used until it is lost to the silt or worn away, then a new one is made. Their stories are not of heroes, but of **currents** and **silt-falls**. Their epic poems describe the thousand-year journey of a particular nutrient plume, or the slow, glorious collapse of a particularly ancient silt-hill.

### Interaction with the Ecology

They are the ultimate opportunists in a landscape of scarcity. They tend the groves of glass sponges, carefully cleaning them of choking sediment to maintain the waterflow the sponges—and the tiny ecosystems they support—require. They follow the ghost-pale eels, not to hunt them, but to scavenge the debris they stir up. They understand the tripod fish are not competitors, but sentinels; where a tripod fish stands patiently, a Silt-Reader knows the current will eventually deliver something of worth.

Predation is rare and terrifying. When a giant, fang-toothed horror erupts from the murk, the Silt-Readers do not fight or flee in panic. They **still**. They become one with the silt, retracting their filaments, slowing their heartbeat to a near-stop, allowing the turbulent cloud of the attack to hide them. They understand that the predator is also just an expression of the deep’s harsh laws, a fleeting explosion of energy in the long quiet.

### The End of a Life

When a Silt-Reader dies, the community does not bury them. That would be an affront to the silt, which takes in its own time. Instead, they perform the **Current-Release**. The body is carefully cleaned of all tools and useful materials. Then, in a silent ceremony felt through pressure and touch, they carry it to a known, strong confluence of currents. They release it into the flow. The body becomes part of the nutrient cycle, a final gift to the system that sustained it. Within hours, it is gone, dissolved into the eternal, packed, particulate gloom.

They are the quiet custodians of the final plain. They live so completely within the rules—the sacred energy, the patient structure, the all-encompassing silt, the absolute law of the current—that they have become, themselves, a kind of rule. A rule of persistent, adaptive quiet in the oldest, coldest, most silent riot of water the planet has ever known.
