They are called the Saturated. To them, the city is not a habitat; it is an extension of their biology, a calcified third lung, an external cortex. They are humanoid in only the loosest sense—a bipedal template warped by millennia of symbiotic existence. Their skin is the first thing you notice: a dermal layer of translucent, grayish film, perpetually moist, shot through with faint capillary networks that glow a soft ceramic blue. This is not skin that sweats; it breathes water directly, absorbing ambient humidity through millions of micro-vesicles, expelling excess salts and metabolic heat in a constant, barely perceptible vapor. They are walking pieces of the city’s climate.

Their faces are elongated, noses reduced to slight ridges with slit nostrils that can seal shut against mist or immersion. Eyes are large, dark, and lidless, protected by a permanent nictitating membrane that gives their gaze a strange, liquid shimmer. They see not just in the visual spectrum, but in layers of data—the thermal plumes of machinery, the flow-rates in hidden pipes, the density of the airborne glint-fish swarms. This sight is not a gift but a necessity; to navigate the city is to read a living, chaotic schematic.

Their hands are long-fingered, with slightly webbed interstitials. The fingertips are hard, black, and conductive, capable of interfacing directly with the smart-matter walls, querying a street’s intended route, or calming a malfunctioning geyser valve with a touch. Their relationship with the city’s material is intimate, tactile, a constant low-level conversation. They do not open doors; they persuade thresholds to dilate.

But the Saturated are not the only intelligence here. The city thrums with a hierarchy of crafted life. Above them, in social station and literal altitude, are the **Cantors**. They are taller, thinner, their bodies seemingly carved from the same luminous crystal that grows on the spires. They are the curators of the city’s memory and the conductors of its energy flows. They do not walk the flooded causeways; they move along prescribed beams of solid light, their voices a harmonic part of the deep geothermal thrum, issuing directives that reshape districts. They are aloof, ancient, and considered by the Saturated to be as much a part of the infrastructure as the geothermal taps—vital, but impersonal.

Below, both in the waterways and in status, are the **Gill-Grins**. They are the truly amphibious ones, their bodies sleek and otter-like, but with hard, manipulative fins that can handle tools. They live in the submerged arcades, the overflow channels, the deep canals where the data-jellies pulse. They are the city’s plumbers, its repair-fauna, its scavengers. Their faces seem fixed in a wide, toothy rictus—not a smile, but the permanent set of jaws adapted to prying open clogged gratings and wrestling with malfunctioning scrubber-drones. They trade in salvage, secrets, and the rare, potent data-nutrients harvested from the deepest jellies.

And then there are the Unspoken—the **Fused**. These are beings, Saturated or otherwise, who have, through accident, obsession, or punishment, become irrevocably integrated with the city’s smart matter. A man who leaned too long into a healing wall, now half-absorbed, his torso emerging from a pillar, his mind diffused into the local network, endlessly calculating pipe diameters. A Gill-Grin who got trapped in a filtration heart, now a sentient, complaining part of the water purification cycle. They are seen as holy fools, warnings, and utilities all at once. You might ask one for the day’s pressure differentials, or leave an offering of charged crystals at its base.

Life for the Saturated is a calculus of flow. Their society is organized around hydrological and data-logical principles. Districts are named for their function: The Sump, The Condensation Array, The Diffraction Quarter. Time is measured in tidal cycles of the city’s own making—the **High Saturation** and the **Clear Breath**. During High Saturation, the water rises, the mist thickens, and the city’s metabolic processes slow into a dreamlike, communal lull. During the Clear Breath, levels drop, pathways solidify, and the frantic commerce and construction erupts.

They eat synthesized proteins cultured in vats fed by mineral runoff, and they drink by simply opening their mouths in the mist. Their art is one of controlled erosion and light refraction. Their conflicts are fought not with blades, but with redirected flows—flooding a rival’s chamber, hacking the environmental controls to create a localized, desiccating drought, or unleashing a swarm of aggressive, data-corrupting glitch-jellies.

To live here is to be in a state of perpetual, negotiated symbiosis. You are never dry, never alone, never silent. You are part of the drip, part of the thrum, part of the endless, seeping life of the city. You are, forever, Saturated.
