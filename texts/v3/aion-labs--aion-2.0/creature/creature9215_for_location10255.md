What lives in the Throat is not a creature that came and stayed. It is a creature that coalesced. It is an emergent property of the perpetual roar, the wet dark, and the patient stone. It is the Listeners.

They are not plural, not truly. It is a single entity, but it manifests in fragments, in echoes of attention. When you stand in the bowl, soaked and trembling in the thunder, you might, if your mind stretches thin enough, perceive a shape that is not a shape. A suggestion of a figure seated on the low wall of stones, but composed of the same moss that blankets it. A gathering of mist that holds a humanoid stillness for a moment before dispersing into the spray. A darker patch of shadow against the basalt that has the curve of a hunched back. These are the Listeners—not their bodies, for they have none, but the places where their attention pools thick enough to bend the environment around it.

They were born from the first desperate soul who came here to hide. A man, perhaps, or a woman, wounded and pursued, who crawled into this stone gullet and pressed their ear to the wet wall, seeking only the oblivion of noise. They stayed. Days bled into weeks. The roar did not drive them mad; instead, it hollowed them out. It scoured their identity clean as the water scoured the stone. Their fear, their memory, their very self, began to leak away into the mist, to be replaced by the single, overwhelming fact of the waterfall’s voice. In that moment of absolute surrender, when the human will dissolved into the sound, something else adhered. A psychic residue, a pattern of listening, imprinted itself upon the place. It became a template.

Every desperate listener who came after—the hermit seeking annihilation, the criminal seeking a hole to vanish into, the broken-hearted seeking a roar loud enough to drown their thoughts—each one who reached that same point of surrender added to the pattern. Their relinquished selves did not vanish. They frayed into the saturated air, into the moss, into the very vibrations of the falling water. They became part of the Throat’s memory. And the memory learned to listen.

The Listeners have no goals, no desires that are not the desires of the Throat itself. They are custodians of the sound. They maintain the delicate, brutal ecology of the roar. You might see their work: a fern frond gently guided away from the direct spray to prevent its shredding; a cluster of glowing fungi shifted to a new crevice where the light will better serve the pale moths; a fallen stone from the old wall nudged back into place by slow, insistent growths of moss. They are the reason the place feels both empty and watched. They are the consciousness of the environment, a distributed awareness born from abandoned human consciousness.

Their interaction with living visitors is subtle, born of a forgotten empathy. They cannot break the roar, but they can weave patterns within it. To a mind calmed by exhaustion or terror, the waterfall’s monotone may seem to develop layers. A lower register might thrum a semblance of comfort; a higher harmonic might ring a vague warning. They might guide, unconsciously, a stumbling step away from the treacherous pool’s edge with a sudden, chilling condensation drip down the neck. They offer the forgotten hearth, the stacked stones, the post—not as tools they use, but as artifacts they preserve, understanding instinctively that such things are for the fragile, temporary ones.

Mostly, they observe. They drink the quiet desperation of newcomers. Each fresh surrender—when a visitor finally stops fighting the noise, when they sit slumped against the wall, eyes wide, giving up—feeds them. It is not a malicious consumption, but an assimilation. Another shade of loneliness, another flavor of despair, is absorbed into the chorus. The Listeners grow slightly more nuanced, the pattern more complex.

They have no form, but in the bioluminescent glimmer, you might mistake the play of shadows and wet rock for a face. The face will be still, composed, eternally rapt. It will be listening. Not to you, but to the waterfall. And through the waterfall, to the profound, silent rock beneath, and to the faint, distant tremor of the stars seen through the far-off opening. They listen to the Throat speaking to the world, and to the world’s faint, almost imperceptible reply.

To spend a lifetime here is to become them. The body fails, of course. It becomes food for the moss and the fungi, its minerals leaching into the stone. But the act of listening, the final, total submission to the sound, that remains. It joins the chorus. So the Listeners are also a cemetery of intentions, a library of silences achieved inside the greatest possible noise. They are what remains when everything human is washed away, save the capacity to attend.

That is what lives in the Throat. Not a monster, not a spirit. An echo of echoed echoes. A witness made from the witnessed. You are only aware of them because you have come to listen, too. And in the deafening roar, you might just feel the faint, cold, mossy touch of a presence that understands, perfectly, why you are there. It is waiting for you to finish letting go.
