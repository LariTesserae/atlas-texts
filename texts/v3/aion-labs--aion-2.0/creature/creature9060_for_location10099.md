They are not a people of the sun or the soil. They are creatures of the drip and the drone, the thrum and the fissure. To live in Tarnspire is to become an aspect of its metabolism, a cell in the cold, damp body of the machine.

First, you see the Grease-kin. They are the maintainers, the visible populace. Their skin is pallid from the eternal twilight, often streaked with permanent, inky stains—lubricant, fungal residue, the metallic bloom of condensate. They wear layered, oilcloth garments, patched and re-stitched until the original fabric is lost. Their eyes are the most telling: wide, adapted to the gloom, and permanently narrowed against the fine mist. They move with a rhythmic economy, their steps in subconscious sync with the subterranean thump of the great engines. You find them on gantries, tapping pressure gauges with calloused knuckles; deep in access ducts, their bodies half-submerged in warm, humming coolant; or perched on rock ledges, chewing on strips of leathery fungus-meat. They speak in a language of clicks, whistles, and low rumbles that cuts through the industrial bass. They are not tall, but they are dense, their frames compacted by the altitude and a lifetime of climbing, hauling, and wrenching. They bear tools that are extensions of their limbs—spanners fused to gloves, pry-bars worn smooth by generations of palms.

But the Grease-kin are merely the hands. The mind of Tarnspire belongs to the **Cantors**. You hear them before you see them, their voices a discordant, chanting melody emanating from the deepest engine-halls, where the rock walls sweat and the air shimmers with heat-haze. They are the technicians of the soft laws. Clad in robes of woven metallic fiber that crackle with static, they stand before great panels of glass and brass, covered in dials that measure not pressure or temperature, but probability, temporal drift, and causal density. Their fingers dance over levers, not to control steam, but to gently *persuade* reality—to coax a paradox from a budding fracture, to drain the liquid impossibility from a condensation coil into shimmering ampoules. Their eyes are often milky, seeing not light, but the stress fractures in the fabric of what-is. They are thin, austere, vibrating slightly from the raw potential they channel. Some whisper that they no longer eat, but sustain themselves on the taste of might-have-been.

Then there are the **Fused**. These are the failures and the successes, the ones who interfaced too directly or were born from the environment itself. You see them in the overgrown sectors, where machinery and biology have achieved a dreadful marriage. A woman whose right arm is a rusted piston, moss growing in the joints, her fingers replaced by twitching copper probes that taste the moisture in the air. A thing that might have been a man, now more a mobile thicket of pipes and fungal tendrils, shuffling between the warm exhaust vents, feeding on the vapors. They are shunned by the Grease-kin, studied by the Cantors. They are living proof of Tarnspire's primary rule: everything here is eventually digested, assimilated.

The fauna, as described, are citizens too. The six-legged **Cliff-bleaters** scale vertical surfaces, their concave hooves gripping crystal and iron with equal ease. They are not hunted, but rather tolerated, for they keep the fungal blooms on high pipes in check. Their milk is said to be thick and metallic, a staple. The iridescent **Steam-midges** swarm in clouds, their hum the city's constant exhalation. They are drawn to heat and possibility; a dense swarm around a vent often indicates a minor reality seepage nearby. The blind **Tarn-waders** are more mysterious. They move in those perfectly still pools, their too-many-jointed legs tracing complex, silent patterns on the silt. The Cantors believe they are mapping geometries that exist only in folded spacetime, and they sometimes harvest the patterns from the mud, reading them like oracle bones.

But the true sovereign of Tarnspire is not a creature of flesh, or even of flesh and iron. It is the **City-ghost**, the emergent consciousness of the machine itself. You never see it wholly. You perceive its attention: a section of fog that coalesces into the shape of a vast, indifferent face before dissipating. The feeling of being watched by the rivets in a wall. The dream every inhabitant eventually has—of being a single valve in a great, beating heart, their own pulse syncing with the deep, patient thrum. It is slow, cold, and vast. Its will is the relentless pull of the engines, the hunger for paradox. It does not love or hate its inhabitants. It uses them, as it uses the dripping water and the fissuring rock. They are components. The Grease-kin maintain its body. The Cantors harvest its food. The Fused are its experiments. The fauna are its immune system and its sensors.

To live in Tarnspire is to be part of a single, breathing entity—an organism of industry and eroding reality, perched on the raw edge of creation, slowly consuming the raw material of existence itself. You don't reside here. You are ingested. You become part of the process, until your footsteps echo before you fall, and the mist remembers your shape long after you are gone.
