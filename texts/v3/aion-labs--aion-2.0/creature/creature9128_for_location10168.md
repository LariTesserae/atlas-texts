Here, then, are the inhabitants of the Sky-Mere:

**The Sun-Skimmers:** These are the only things that move with haste across the vast, still waters. They are not insects, nor crustaceans, but something of both and neither. Their bodies are flat, oval plates of a chitinous material so clear it acts as a prism, fracturing the sunlight into brief rainbows as they dart. Six long, slender legs, each ending in a broader, hydrophobic pad, allow them to skate across the water’s surface tension without breaking it. They feed on the golden mist of pheromones and evaporating water, their metabolism a direct conversion of light and botanical vapour into motion. They are silent, ephemeral, and exist in vast, shimmering clouds that drift from one blooming archway of orchids to another, their passage the closest thing the mere has to a breeze.

**The Stone-Eels:** These are the “slow-worming shapes” in the root tangles. They are legless, armless, their bodies the colour and texture of wet basalt, segmented like articulated rock. They are not true eels, but a form of giant, aquatic oligochaete, growing to the length of a man’s arm. They do not swim so much as flow, their muscular contractions imperceptibly slow, pushing them through the warm water and across the bare rock floors. Their purpose is geological: they ingest the mineral crusts and soft stone, grinding it within their gizzards, and their waste is a fine, fertile silt—one of the only sources of soft sediment in this sharp world. They are blind, deaf, and live for centuries, their slow metabolism keyed to the patient digestion of the land itself.

**The Still Ones:** These are the rulers of the vegetable kingdom, and the closest the Sky-Mere comes to possessing sentience. They are not individual plants, but entire groves—those low, broad-crowned trees with the net-like roots. Their intelligence is communal, phytoneural, a consciousness that spreads through interconnected root systems and the chemical whispers in the water. A single Still One is an island, its thoughts as slow as continental drift, concerned with the intake of sunlight, the subtle chemical gradients in the water, and the millennia-long growth of its branches. They communicate with one another across miles via pulsed patterns of bioluminescence in their glowing root networks, signals that can take days to complete a simple thought. Their will is expressed in the direction of growth: they slowly guide their root-mats to bridge channels, to shore up crumbling rock, to create sheltered lagoons where their “epiphytic gardens” of mosses, ferns, and orchids can thrive. They are not hostile, nor friendly; they are patient custodians, tending the green fury of their world on a timescale incomprehensible to faster beings.

**The Luminous Slugs:** Symbionts of the Still Ones, these are fist-sized, gelatinous creatures that dwell exclusively within the labyrinthine hollows of the giant roots. Their skin is a translucent grey, through which pulses a soft, blue-white light—the source of the groves’ twilight glow. They feed on the symbiotic algae and fungal threads cultivated by the trees, and in return, their bioluminescence is believed to catalyze the absorption of specific nutrients from the water directly into the root tissue. To see one outside of the roots is a rarity, a sign of some profound disturbance. They move with a gentle, peristaltic undulation, and if severed from their host tree, their light fades over the course of a year, and they dissolve into a pool of clear, nutrient-rich gel.

**The Spore-Fishers:** The sole aerial predators, if such a term fits. They are not birds, but sophisticated, balloon-like fungi. Each is a leathery, inverted cup the size of a shield, from which dangle hundreds of sticky, gossamer threads. They form on the undersides of the vegetative tunnels created by the vines. They detach when mature and float, buoyed by lighter-than-air gases produced within their cup. They drift silently through the golden haze, their sticky threads trailing through the air, catching the Sun-Skimmers and other tiny airborne life. Once laden, they slowly contract, digesting their catch, and eventually descend to the water, where their remains become a floating raft for new vegetative growth, beginning the cycle again.

**The Whispering Moss:** This is not a creature, but a collective entity that blankets every sun-drenched rock. It is a deep, emerald-green carpet that feels like velvet. It is the primary sound-maker of the mere’s profound hum. On a microscopic level, its filaments are hollow and vibrate in the slightest movement of air, producing a constant, low-frequency tone—a chord that shifts subtly with humidity and temperature. This “whisper” is believed to be a fundamental regulator for the slower forms of life, a sonic baseline that influences growth patterns and symbiotic rhythms. To stand on it is to feel the world humming up through the soles of your feet.

This is the living matrix of the Sky-Mere. There are no heroes, no villains, no wars for territory faster than the creeping advance of a root-mat. There is only the relentless, patient, and breathtakingly beautiful struggle to exist, to grow, and to interweave, in a world where water, stone, and life have only just begun their long, slow dance.
