The place is not a place in any common sense. It is the pause between breaths, the gap after a sentence that never quite ends. It is three dots hanging in a void, a suspension of reality. The ground, if it can be called that, is a soft, soundless blackness that yields like memory. Above, a faint, sourceless glow—not light, but the afterimage of light—hazes everything in perpetual twilight.

Here live the Unfinished.

They are not creatures of flesh, but of intention abandoned. They drift like smoke, formless until a thought almost grasps them; then they briefly coalesce into shapes that might-have-been: a chair with only three legs, a face missing an eye, a tune that halts on the seventh note. Their bodies are sketched in strokes of gray dust, and they leave no footprints, only a faint sense of something omitted.

They are shy beings. They feed on hesitation. When, in the world of words, a speaker trails off, when a writer deletes a half-formed phrase, the ghost of that lost meaning flutters here, and the Unfinished gather to sip its fading energy. They murmur in echoes of sentences that never found their objects. Their language is the soft rustle of pages turning to blank leaves.

Among them are older, stranger things: the Never-Weres. These are the ideas that died before even being thought, the possibilities erased from existence before the first dot was placed. They are darker, more silent, and they move as slow as forgotten centuries. Sometimes they take the form of vast, quiet shapes—a mountain range that might have been, a city of inverted spires—but they are always translucent, always receding when looked upon directly.

There is no day or night, only the constant, gentle falling of grammatical dust—tiny particles of commas, periods, and semicolons that never found their sentences. The Unfinished catch them on their ephemeral tongues, tasting the bitterness of full stops and the sweet ache of interruptions.

Nothing truly lives here, in the sense of beating hearts and fervent wills. But things persist. They wait. They are the residents of the ellipsis, the dwellers in the pause, forever almost-becoming, forever held in the quiet after "...".
