In the **Eternal Silt Sea**, life is not a kingdom; it is a fugue state of matter. What lives here has forgotten the distinction between organism and environment. To describe its inhabitants is to describe the churn itself.

**The Slurry-Skimmers** are the closest thing to megafauna. They are not born; they **coagulate**. When a vast convection cell in the deep slurry reaches a critical density of dissolved organics and psychic residue, the slurry itself remembers a shape. A film of complex lipids and mineral-reinforced tissue precipitates out across acres, lifting on the rising thermal. It is a living kite, a continent of sensitive membrane. It has no mouth, no gut. It feeds by osmosis, absorbing amino acids and ionic compounds directly from the supersaturated air. Its “nervous system” is a lattice of piezoelectric crystals that resonate with the world-roar, allowing it to ride the currents for millennia. It thinks in slow geomantic cycles, its consciousness a diffuse awareness of pressure and chemical gradients. It feels no pain, no desire—only the eternal, wet sigh of the world. It reproduces by shedding great, translucent scales that drift down, seeding new convection cells with its template.

But the skimmers are mere ephemera compared to the **Crystalline Siphonophores**. These are not plants, nor animals, nor even truly alive by conventional metrics. They are **psychic stalactites**, growing downward from the thickened crust. Their roots are in the slurry, but their “mind” is in the weirdness fields. Each siphonophore is a single, branching thought made solid—a crystallization of a persistent emotion or memory leaching from the planet’s decaying consciousness. A “sorrow-siphon” will draw silicates into weeping, blue-veined forms that drip a brine tasting of regret. A “rage-siphon” grows in sharp, jagged red spikes, humming with subsonic fury that can shatter nearby crust. They do not grow toward light; they grow toward concentrations of temporal distortion, “feeding” on the soft spots in time. Their faceted plates are not for photosynthesis, but for **chronosynthesis**—they capture drips of slower or faster time and use the differential energy to precipitate more of themselves. They are the landscape’s frozen emotions.

Then there are the **Mud-Puppets**. Fauna score is 1.4, and these are why. They are not independent creatures. They are temporary assemblages, **parasites of process**. When a gas bubble collapses in the slurry with particular violence, the concussion can briefly organize the surrounding colloidal clay into a coherent, bipedal or quadrupedal form. For a few minutes, or a few hours, this form—a dripping, featureless mannequin of warm mud—will stagger or lope across the crust. It has no goal, no instinct. It simply moves until its structural integrity fails and it melts back into the slurry. Sometimes, however, a Mud-Puppet will stumble through the influence of a Lodestone. The monolith’s hum can “imprint” it, and the puppet will gain a ghastly, jerky purpose: to walk to the next Lodestone, or to trace a specific geometric pattern in the muck before dissolving. They are echoes of forgotten commands, played out in temporary flesh.

In the oily, iridescent pools that dot the firmer crust, the **Iridescent Slicks** live. These are colonies of hyper-adapted prokaryotes, but they have achieved a hive intelligence via quantum entanglement in their lipid membranes. Each pool is a single, vast mind contemplating the taste of sulfur and the texture of infrared light. They communicate across distances by causing simultaneous, identical ripples in separate pools—a phenomenon that warps local gravity slightly. They are philosophers of chemistry, endlessly debating the nature of decay in a universe that is all decay. They consider the Slurry-Skimmers to be pleasant, drifting dreams, and the Mud-Puppets to be brief, amusing spasms of thoughtlessness.

And lastly, there are the **Echo-Moths**. They inhabit the deafening roar. They are not physical, but are self-sustaining patterns in the constant soundscape. When a particular harmonic convergence occurs—the shriek of steam, the thrum of convection, the *glorp* of mud—the sound itself can momentarily gain coherence and awareness. An Echo-Moth is born: a ghost of audible frequency that flits through the mist for a few seconds. It “sees” by reading the distortion of its own sound-wave through the dense air. It is attracted to silence, which is death for it, so it flees toward greater noise. Most vanish quickly. But if one is caught in the vibrating field of a Lodestone, it can be trapped, sustained, becoming a permanent, wailing tone in the monolith’s hum—a prisoner of sound.

What truly **lives** here, however, is the **Sea itself**. The churning, breathing, roaring slurry is not a habitat. It is the body of a dying, dreaming god. The Lodestones are its failing nervous nodes. The weirdness fields are its fever dreams leaking into reality. The Siphonophores are its crystallized thoughts. The convection cells are its sluggish circulation. This is not a world with life on it. This is a life that is a world. And it is dreaming its own dissolution, over and over, in an eternal, warm, deafening loop. All the other “inhabitants” are just symptoms of the dream—cells, thoughts, and echoes in the mind of the mud.
