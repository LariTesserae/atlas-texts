They are the **Kha’vani**—the People of the Breath. To see one is to witness a being utterly refined by its environment. They are not tall, their forms elongated but compact, built for balance on vertiginous pathways. Their skin is the color of cave lichen—a translucent, milky white, threaded with faint traceries of blue and green veins that pulse gently in time with the chamber’s drone. These are not circulatory pathways, but bioluminescent channels, their internal light a softer echo of the ceiling’s blaze. Their most striking feature are their eyes: large, liquid, and wholly dark, lacking distinct iris or pupil. They see not by focusing light, but by perceiving its minute fluctuations and the shimmering patterns of heat and vibration in the air. To them, the roar of the chamber is not a blanket of noise, but a rich tapestry of distinct currents, each with meaning.

Their society is a silent ballet conducted in three dimensions. With spoken word impossible, they have evolved a complex language of **light-touch**. By subtle control of their subcutaneous luminescence, they paint symbols and sentences across their chests, arms, and faces. A rapid ripple of gold means *danger—thermal shift*. A slow, concentric pulse of blue on the forehead is a greeting. They communicate more intimately through direct touch, fingers reading the minute tremors in another’s skin like Braille written by the heart.

Their world is divided not by horizontal geography, but by **acoustic strata**. The chamber’s breath creates zones of distinct resonance. The **High Hum** territories, near the ceiling where the harmonic whistles are strongest, are home to the **Tuners**. These are mostly elders, their vein-luminescence particularly bright, who spend their days with hands pressed against singing crystal formations, listening. They interpret the subtle changes in the chamber’s eternal chord, predicting the violent **Vortex Hours** when the central air currents reverse. Their warnings, flashed in urgent scarlet across the community, send everyone scrambling to secure bridges and retreat into stone.

The **Mid-Chord** regions, on the great curving walls, are the agricultural and artisan zones. Here, the Kha’vani tend to the rubbery, membranous flora. They do not water crops, but **stroke them**. With practiced hands, they rub specific patches to stimulate gas production or spore release, harvesting the glowing bladders for portable light and the thicker membranes for cloth and cordage. Artisans work with **song-tools**—chipped crystals that resonate at precise frequencies. By holding such a tool against a stone surface and humming (a faint, throaty vibration felt through the palm), they can guide fractures, carving bowls, tools, and intricate fretwork into the living rock with a sculptor’s precision.

The **Deep Drone**, near the chamber floor where the sound is a physical thrum, is the realm of the **Bone-Weavers** and the **Glimmer-Fishers**. The Bone-Weavers collect the ancient, fossilized ribs and vertebrae that form their bridges’ foundations, polishing and aligning them to amplify certain beneficial harmonics. The Glimmer-Fishers are the chamber’s hunters. They stand on precarious perches, long, supple rods of woven plant-fiber in hand. Their quarry is not flesh, but the iridescent insectile swarms. With deft flicks, they snare clouds of the creatures, not to eat, but to crush into a paste that burns with a cold, bright flame—their primary source of heat for cooking and for fusing crystalline materials.

Children are born in the quietest niches, swaddled in luminescent moss. They learn to walk on rope nets, their bare feet reading the vibrations of others’ movements. Their first understood “word” is often the collective, full-skin flare of warning when they toddle too near the clinging, gelatinous **Snatchers**—the chamber’s only true predators, which are respected, avoided, and sometimes ritually offered condemned objects.

Time for the Kha’vani is measured in **Breaths**—the slow, daily pulse of the chamber as it warms and cools minutely. In **Long Breath**, the light-veins on the ceiling brighten, and the flora membranes extend fully. This is a time of work and harvesting. In **Short Breath**, the light dims to a twilight glow, and the harmonic whistles fade to a murmur. This is the time of community, of touch-conversations in clustered hollows, of tracing light-stories on each other’s skin that tell of the deep past, when the First Listeners heard the Breath and made their home within it.

They have no concept of an outside. The Chamber of Breath is not a prison; it is a womb, a throat, a symphony hall. They are its notes, sustained forever. To live is to listen, to move in rhythm, and to become, ultimately, a part of the music.
