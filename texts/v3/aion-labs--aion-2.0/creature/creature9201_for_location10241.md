The inhabitants of the Plateau do not merely reside upon the Skin; they are born from it, sustained by it, and ultimately returned to it in a cycle as old as the basalt bones beneath. They are creatures of monumental scale and deafening presence, for silence here is a vacuum quickly filled by the thrum of existence.

First, and most dominant, are the Crest-Beasts. Imagine a rolling mountain of living armor, a thousand strong, a river of flesh and song. Each beast is a low-slung bulk, six powerful legs ending in broad, padded hooves that beat a constant, earth-trembling rhythm into the resilient Skin. Their hides are not fur, but layered plates of a keratinous material, the color of storm clouds and dried blood. From their spinal ridges rise great, hollow crests of bone, intricately chambered and tuned by the wind of their passage. As the herd moves, the air whistles through these structures, creating the Plateau’s symphony: a deafening chorus that ranges from subsonic groans that vibrate in your teeth to piercing, crystalline shrieks that slice the sky. They are not mindless. Their migrations follow ancient paths worn soft by countless generations, paths the Skin itself maintains and remembers. They move toward the rumble of distant rain or the faint, nutritious pulses the Skin extrudes in cryptic patterns. They are the plateau’s heartbeat, its primary consumers, its wandering, singing cities.

Their opposites are the Thorn-Thickets: the predators. They do not stalk; they settle. Appearing as innocuous, dense patches of barbed scrub, they are in fact singular organisms, a knot of ropy tendon and photosensitive thorns. They are ambushers of exquisite patience. Their roots—or nerves—penetrate deep into the Skin’s substrate, feeling for the vibrations of approaching herds. When prey draws near, the thicket erupts. Tendons whip out, thorns inject a paralytic sap, and the whole mass contracts, slowly digesting the captured creature while it still lives, dissolving it into nutrients that seep back into the Skin. They are the plateau’s quiet, cruel judiciary.

Above this, the sky belongs to the Sail-Gliders. Their wings are vast membranes of stretched, translucent hide, veined with chlorophyll, doubling as solar collectors. They ride the thermals that ripple up from the Skin’s warmer patches, moving with a silence that is profound after the herd’s cacophony. They are scavengers and opportunists, dropping on the remnants of a Thorn-Thicket’s kill or plucking stragglers from the edges of the insectile clouds. Their shadows are like passing continents on the green and umber plain.

And the clouds themselves are alive: the Buzz-Swarms. Trillions of iridescent, hard-shelled fliers, each no larger than a thumb, moving as a single, shifting intelligence. They cast a murmuring, vibrating shadow that drinks light and distorts sound. They descend periodically to harvest the Skin’s surface microbes and the dawn’s dew, rising again in a shimmering curtain. They are a foundational food, a constant background drone, the plateau’s particulate breath.

Then, there are the humans—the Listeners. They are slight, leathery things, their skin etched by wind and patterned with ritual scars that mimic the Skin’s own patches. They are ephemera. They fashion tools from chipped basalt and resin boiled from thorn-sap. They weave cord from pulled Skin-fibers and sinew. They do not build; they perch. Their shelters are temporary frameworks of Crest-Beast bone and tanned hide, pegged into the Skin, abandoned and absorbed within a season. They follow the herds, not as herders, but as parasites of opportunity, taking the sick, the young, the already dying. They navigate by interpreting the crest-songs, reading the weather in the Skin’s luminescence, and following the worn grooves. They know not to cut the Skin deeply, lest it burn them. Their existence is a delicate, whispered negotiation. They leave only faint signs: a circle of fire-blackened nodules, a midden of cracked carapaces, a pattern of stones that says *we were here*. They are the plateau’s faint, conscious itch.

And beneath it all, the Skin itself lives. It is not a backdrop; it is the player. It drinks the dawn dew with a collective sigh. It glows softly at night underfoot, a bioluminescent response to pressure or the planetary turn. It remembers. It guides. It occasionally, inexplicably, produces hard, starchy nodules in spiral or grid patterns, and the herds shift course to graze upon them. It secretes acid when wounded. It is the true, slow mind of the place, the god in the ground, the warm, breathing world upon which all this frantic, noisy, glorious life is but a temporary pattern. To live here is to be part of its metabolism—loud, hungry, and utterly transient against its enduring, silent will.
