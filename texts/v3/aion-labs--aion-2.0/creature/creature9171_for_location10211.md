They are the Drip-Watchers, the Treaders-on-Softness. They do not *live* here, not in the way you mean—no hearth-smoke curls in this saturated air, no child’s cry splits the metronomic drip. But they are of this place as much as the blind millipedes and the glowing slugs. They are its human echo, its conscious shadow.

They come singly, or in pairs at most. To come in a group would be an imposition, a crowd. They are seekers of a particular quality of silence, scavengers of the rare *Luminous Cap* fungi that grow only where the cliff’s weep-water falls in a perfect, unvarying rhythm. Their needs are few, their presence a carefully calculated subtraction.

**Appearance:** They are wraiths in the moss-light. Their clothes are of a felted, moss-colored wool, spun from the hair of mountain goats that graze the sunlit slopes far below. Over this, they wear cloaks of treated, translucent slug-skin, which sheds the constant moisture and glimmers with a faint, borrowed bioluminescence. Their feet are bare, or bound in soft, sock-like bags of the same felt, allowing them to feel the subtlest sigh of the moss-bed, to avoid the deeper, water-filled sinkholes. Their tools—a curved harvesting knife of sharpened mountain-sheep bone, a digging stick of heartwood from a petrified pine, a collecting pouch of tightly-woven fern fiber—are lashed to their belts with sinew. Their faces are often pale from a life spent in gloom, their eyes adapted to the blue-green dark. They move with a slowness that matches the millipedes’, a pace that leaves no lasting mark.

**Practices & Belief:** They operate under a single, uncompromising law: **The Equation Must Hold.** The Hush-Moss is a perfect, closed system of life, water, and time. To take is to create a deficit; to leave is to create a surplus. Both are violence. So their harvesting is a precise ritual. They count the drips from a specific overhang to find the fungi at the peak of their luminous spore-release. They cut the stem with the bone knife at a precise angle, ensuring the mycelium beneath is undisturbed. They take no more than one in ten. They eat nothing here; the food of this place is for its own children. They drink only by catching the bead of water that forms on a moss-tip directly into their mouth, violating nothing.

They believe the rhythmic drip is the mountain’s heartbeat, and the soft creak of the moss is its breathing. To listen—truly listen—until your own breath syncs with that creak and your own pulse with that drip, is to step outside of linear time. It is a form of time-travel into a perpetual, peaceful present. Some come for this experience alone, sitting for days in a single spot, becoming just another soft, breathing shape in the dark. They are not monks of a god, but physicists of a sacred stasis.

**Knowledge & Role:** They are the memory of this place for the world outside. They know the patterns of the glowing trails, which indicate the health of the slug colonies. They can read the density of the drifting spore-motes like a weather forecast. The rare, sightless eels in the pools are never taken, but their shedding, translucent skins are sometimes collected—prized as a material of incredible strength and flexibility in the world below, used for lenses and filters. The Listeners trade these sparingly, for the wool and bone they need, speaking of the Moss in hushed, reverent terms that only confuse the market-folk.

**A Portrait of One:** Her name is Elara, though she rarely thinks it here. She is kneeling in a hollow, the moss giving way to her knees like a mother’s lap. Before her, a *Luminous Cap* fungus, no wider than her thumb, pulses softly. Her breath is still. In one hand, the bone knife. In the other, a small, flat stone onto which she will lay the harvest. She has counted two hundred and forty drips from the cliff-face above. The next drip will coincide with the fungus’s faintest dimming—the perfect moment. She is not thinking of the medicine it will make in her village below, for a child’s fever. She is thinking only of the space between the drips, the hanging tension of the water bead, the way the glow seems to breathe. She is a variable willingly reducing herself to zero. When she cuts, it will be with such focused gentleness that the surrounding moss will not tremble. When she leaves, the only trace will be the memory of her attention, already being absorbed into the timeless, wet dark.
