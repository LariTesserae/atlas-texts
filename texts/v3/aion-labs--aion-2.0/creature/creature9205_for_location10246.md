**The Fen-Folk**

They are called the **Listeners**. To call them inhabitants implies a permanence they reject. They are more like currents within the medium, patterns that persist.

**Physiology:** They are tall, attenuated, their frames an adaptation to wading through deep silt and distributing weight across unstable ground. A full-grown adult stands eight to nine feet, but their shoulders are narrow, their chests shallow. Their limbs are long and double-jointed, ending in broad, four-fingered hands and feet that are more like paddles, webbed with tough, flexible hide. Their skin is the color of wet silt, mottled with patterns that mimic the iridescent Glisten—these are not pigmentation, but colonies of symbiotic bacteria that aid in nutrient absorption and camouflage. They have no hair. Their heads are elongated, with large, dark eyes set deep under a pronounced brow-ridge. Their nostrils are narrow slits they can seal shut against the grit-laden wind. Their ears are not external flaps, but complex spirals of cartilage almost flush with the skull, capable of fine-tuned directional hearing—a necessity in the deafening din.

**Metabolism:** Their biology is a masterpiece of slow efficiency. They are cold-blooded, their core temperature matching the cool damp (**temperature: 0.6/3**). This renders them torpid in the deepest chill, but they require little food. They are opportunistic omnivores with a strong scavenger bent. Their primary diet is the **Glisten** itself, which they scrape from rocks with horny, ridged tongues and digest via specialized gut flora. They supplement this with the grubs and worm-like creatures that burrow in the silt beneath the crust, the occasional fallen Skythread, and most importantly, the nutrient-rich afterbirth and weathered flesh of the **Rumblebacks**. They follow the herds not to hunt the living giants (an impossible task with primitive tech), but to harvest the dead and the shed.

**Society & Technology:** They are not a people of cities, but of **routes** and **rituals**. Their social unit is the **Drift**, a loose kinship group of 20-50 individuals. Drifts have traditional migratory circuits that take years to complete, timed to the slow bloom cycles of different Glisten strains and the predictable paths of the Rumbleback herds. Their technology (**tech: 1.3/3**) is an art of subtle manipulation. They don't build to conquer the Fen; they build to **tune** it.
*   **The Weirs:** These are not for irrigation, but for **composition**. By slowing a stream, they cause specific minerals to precipitate out, cultivating a desired, nutrient-dense Glisten strain. A weir is a library and a larder.
*   **Wind-breaks & Sound-Snares:** Using hides, woven sinew, and strategically placed Spire fronds, they construct temporary baffles that don't stop the wind, but shape its song. Certain harmonics are believed to encourage spawning in silt-grubs or calm skittish grazers. A well-placed camp is a place of (relatively) modulated sound.
*   **Tools:** Obsidian knives for butchering. Bone hooks and sinew lines for fishing the blind, pale fish of the deep pools. The most important tool is the **Net**, woven from the resilient gut-fiber of Skythreads. Nets are used for catching, for carrying, for constructing shelters, and as climbing aids on the smooth Spires. A Listener's net is their most personal possession, often inherited, constantly repaired, its pattern a map of their life's travels.
*   **Fire:** They use it sparingly. Their fires, fueled by dried Spire-core and rendered Rumbleback fat, burn with a hot, blue, nearly smokeless flame. It is used for cooking (mostly to render toxins from certain foods), for firing obsidian, and for the **Making of Eyes**—the process of heating and quenching certain stones to create "star-metal," a glassy slag used for the sharpest blades and, in polished chunks, as trade items.

**Culture & Belief:** Their worldview is acoustic. They perceive the Fen not as a landscape, but as a **symphony of pressures**. The subsonic Rumbleback groans are the bedrock, the foundation of the world. The wind's drone through the Spires is the voice of the ancient basin itself. The Skythreads' whistles are the fleeting spirits of the air. Silence is not peace; it is emptiness, a void to be feared. Their language is a combination of soft, breathy consonants (produced with minimal lip movement, to avoid grit) and a complex system of **hand-taps** and **body-resonances**. They can communicate over distance by tapping their knife-hilts on resonant stones, sending patterns through the ground.

They worship **The Sigh**—the great, geothermal exhalation that drives the wind. It is not a god, but a process: the world breathing. Life is the inhalation (the condensation, the growth of Glisten), and death is the exhalation (the decay, the return to silt and mineral). They see themselves as part of this breath. To die is to be released into the wind-song. They practice excarnation: placing the dead on high, windy stone spires for the Skythreads and the elements to scatter. A person's memory is kept not in a grave, but in a specific harmonic added to the wind's song at their favorite resting place, a tune their Drift will recognize and hum when they pass.

**Interaction:** A Listener encountering an outsider (a truly rare event) would be initially still, assessing not with sight, but with sound and vibration. They would listen to the stranger's footfall in the silt, the rhythm of their breathing, the timbre of their voice. Their curiosity would be profound but cautious. They might offer a piece of high-quality Glisten crust or a sip of water from a seep they know to be sweet. Their concept of trade is abstract: they might exchange a beautifully knotted net for a story told in a strange voice, or a chunk of star-metal for a demonstration of a technology that produces a novel sound. They have no concept of permanent ownership of land, only of **right passage**—the temporal permission to travel a route without disrupting its cycles.

To live in the Sighing Fen is to be a musician in an orchestra you did not assemble, playing an instrument made from your own body and the things you find, trying to harmonize with a song that is older than life, and louder than death.
