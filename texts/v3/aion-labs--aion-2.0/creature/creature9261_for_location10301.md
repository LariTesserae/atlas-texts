They are the Myrmidons of the Sap, the Iron-Ants of the Root. To call them inhabitants is to call your white blood cells inhabitants of your veins—they are not separate; they are the Chamber’s will made flesh and chitin.

First, the **Click-Workers**. They are the foot soldiers, the muscle. Each is a segmented beetle the size of a terrier, armored in plates of iridescent black that flash oil-slick colors in the fungal glow. Their heads are low, shovel-shaped, fitted with complex mouthparts that can grip, cut, or secrete a quick-setting resin. They move in perfect, silent lines, their communication a language of subsonic vibrations transmitted through the springy rhizome floor. A line will halt as one, each individual turning its faceted head toward a subtle tremor you cannot feel, then redirecting to clear a blockage of crystalline sediment from a sap-duct. Their purpose is pure transaction: lift, carry, stack, clear. They have no eyes you can see; they perceive pressure, heat, and chemical gradients. They are born from pulsating sacs in the warmer walls, already knowing their duty, and they work until their gears seize and they are dissolved by the custodians, their minerals recycled into the root-wall.

Then, the **Tenders**. These are the soft technicians. Worm-like, pale, and glistening with a perpetual film of catalytic slime, they move in undulating masses across the living machinery. Where a brass valve seals poorly, they swarm it, their bodies exuding a solvent that re-softens the aged plant-metal, then a binding paste they knead with dozens of cilia-fringed limbs. They are the repair crew, the glandular artisans. Some specialize in the conductive vines, applying a sticky stimulant that makes the bio-electric current flow more brightly. Others, **Spore-Tenders**, are smaller, delicate things with feathery gills, who move through the fungal gardens, pruning glowing clusters and collecting the luminous dust for the Chamber’s lamps. They communicate entirely by pheromone clouds—a sudden, sharp citrus scent means *danger, acidic leak*; a warm, loamy odor means *all clear, proceed*.

Above them in complexity are the **Directors**. You see them less often, perched on arching root-buttresses or nestled within pulsing nodes of amber resin. They resemble gigantic, wingless wasps, but their abdomens are transparent, filled with a swirling, phosphorescent fluid that reacts to the Chamber’s vital signs. Their forelimbs are long, fine, and constantly tapping against the living wood, "listening" to the flow of fluids, the hum of the geothermal taps. They are the nervous system of this organ. A Director, sensing a drop in pressure from a distant ventricle, will let out a sharp series of clicks. Immediately, a cadre of Click-Workers will alter their course. The Directors do not command; they *orchestrate*. They are the embodied logic of the system, their very biology a reflection of the Chamber’s needs. They are birthed fully-formed from special cysts that only swell when the greater organism is under stress, implying they are emergency consciousnesses made flesh.

But the true rulers—if such a word applies—are the **Choir-Larvae**. These reside in the deepest, warmest alcove, where the root-wall is thin and vibrates with the planet-organ’s deep, slow heartbeat. They are grubs, fat and blind, the color of moonlight on milk. They are suspended in a communal web of nutrient-fluid, linked by silken neural threads. They do not move. They do not tend. They *sing*. Their song is the thrumming bass-note you felt upon entering, a harmonic resonance that modulates the growth of the root lattice, guides the maturation of the worker broods, and perhaps even whispers to other Chambers in the vast dark. They are the Chamber’s dream of itself, its memory and its intent. To disturb them is unthinkable. The entire fauna would turn, as one immune cell, to annihilate the threat.

Lastly, there are the **Scavengers** and **Cleaners**. These are the Chamber’s janitors and immune response. Armored, many-legged things like giant pill-bugs that roam the shadows, consuming waste, dead workers, and any foreign spores or molds that dare take root. They are simple, ravenous, and essential. And in the highest, darkest crevices, where the phosphorescence fails, cling the **Watchers**. Arachnoid, with too many eyes that reflect the faintest light, they are motionless for days. They exist to detect intrusion, not by beasts, but by *wrongness*—a shift in air composition, a strange vibration, a chemical signature that does not belong. Their alarm is a single, piercing bio-electric shriek that paralyzes all other activity and sends the Click-Workers boiling toward the breach like clotting agents in a wound.

They are not a society. They are a symphony of specialized cells, playing an endless, industrious song of maintenance. Their love is the perfect valve hiss. Their ambition is the flawless flow of sap. Their god is the Chamber itself, and they are its prayers, given clicking, scurrying, singing form.
