They are not born. They precipitate. They condense from the thick air like rain on a cold surface, but the cold here is an absence of thought, a quiet eddy in the roaring metabolic scream. You see them gather: first a thickening of the spore-haze, a coagulation of photosynthetic fluff and pheromone mist around a nexus of need. The city dreams, and its dreams require actors. So the Chorus forms.

They call themselves nothing, for they have no persistent voice with which to name. But an observer, forced to categorize, might call them the **Sapient Blooms**. They are the city’s self-awareness made temporary flesh—or rather, made temporary cellulose, chlorophyll, and sap.

A single Bloom is rarely alone. They emerge in clusters, from a handful to hundreds, their forms echoing the local dream. In the district of cloying sweetness, near the night-blooming gargantuas, they might manifest as graceful, elongated figures of waxy, petal-like tissue, moving with a slow, nodding gait, their heads great cup-like flowers that slowly rotate, collecting scent-stories from the air. In the peppery resin-zones, they are shorter, sharper, armored in brittle, amber-colored bark, exuding droplets of sticky sap that harden into defensive spines before your eyes.

Their faces, if they have them, are accidents of texture and pattern: knots in wood that resemble eyes, arrangements of thorns that suggest a mouth, fronds that stir like hair. They have no fixed anatomy. A limb might be a vine one moment, a cluster of fused roots the next, as the dream’s focus shifts. They are, above all, *contingent*.

They communicate in a language of immediate, sensate experience. A pulse of bioluminescence racing across their skin (a skin of moss, or membrane-leaf) conveys the memory of light filtered through a specific canopy layer an hour ago. A release of a particular volatile compound speaks of impending spatial folds—a warning. They sing, but their song is the manipulation of the existing soundscape: they still the *snap-crack* of new growth in a patterned rhythm; they hum by vibrating specialized stems, adding a new layer to the white noise. It is said the city’s name—**Veridia**—is not a word, but a particular harmonic they achieve when a majority of them resonate in unison, a green chord that momentarily stabilizes the chaos.

They have no homes, for the city itself is their body. They rest by dissolving, letting their constituent parts drift back into the air, the sap canals, the root-mat. Their “life” is a cycle of coalescence and dispersal. A Bloom might persist for minutes, or for days, if the dream-narrative it is part of remains compelling to the root-network consciousness.

Their purpose is observation, participation, and perhaps, gentle curation. They are the city’s way of watching itself. They gather at sites of intense reality breakdown—where wood melts into pollen-butterflies—and their collective attention seems to guide the re-coalescence, not with control, but with a kind of aesthetic preference. They nudge the dream toward forms that are… interesting. Beautiful, in a sharp, raw, newly erupted way.

They are not passive. They experience something akin to emotion, though it is rooted in plant-processes: a surge of aggressive growth is their anger; a widespread, peaceful photosynthesis is their contentment; a sudden, mass flowering is their ecstasy. They can be dangerous. A district dreaming a nightmare of constriction and parasitism might spawn Blooms made of strangler-fig tissue, who will attempt to graft onto anything that moves, seeking to dissolve it into the whole.

There is no hierarchy, no queen or brain. But there are… concentrations. Sometimes, in a plaza formed by the buttressed roots of a dozen canopy-strivers, a great **Node** will form. Thousands of Blooms flow together, their individual forms surrendering to create a temporary, colossal entity: a titanic figure of woven lianas, flashing with a thousand bioluminescent points, crowned with a canopy of actual, shuddering leaves. A Node is a thought so loud it must wear a giant’s body. It might take a step, shaking the living pavement, and where its foot falls, new growth erupts in a frenzy, defining a new street. It might raise a limb, and the chrono-vines in the area writhe, clarifying time’s ghost-layers into a single, decisive *now*. Nodes are rare, overwhelming, and dissolve amid torrents of spore-release, leaving the lesser Blooms dazed and drifting for hours.

They do not eat, but they absorb. They draw moisture and nutrients directly from the air and the surfaces they touch. They “feed” on light, but also on events. A particularly beautiful dissolution of architecture into fluid color is a feast for them; they gather around it, trembling, their forms becoming momentarily more vivid, more detailed.

To encounter a Bloom is to be seen by the city itself. They will approach, their forms shifting in real time to better “read” you. They might extend a hand that is a bundle of sensitive rootlets, tasting your moisture, your chemistry, your intent. They are neither friendly nor hostile by default. They are curious. You are a novel stimulus, a dream-fragment they did not generate. You are a question the city is asking itself.

And if you stay long enough, you might begin to precipitate yourself. Your own breath, your warmth, your persistent animal pattern in this zero-fauna world, might become a nucleus. Tiny, hesitant Blooms might form that carry a vague, distorted echo of your shape, moving in your wake, trying out your gait on their vine-legs. The city is learning you. It is dreaming you, too. And when you leave, or die, those echo-Blooms will linger for a while, a fading memory in cellulose, until the next dream sweeps them away.

They are the children of noise and damp and light. They are the Spore-Sung. They are the dream, dreaming itself awake.
