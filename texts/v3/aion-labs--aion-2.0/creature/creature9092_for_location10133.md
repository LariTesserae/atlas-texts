They are not born; they accrete.

In a crevice where two basalt plates meet, powdered stone begins, for no reason, to cohere. A static charge builds in the parched air, a whisper of potential. Over decades, silicate dust draws itself into a loose knot, a tumbleweed of mineral filaments. It is not alive, not yet. It simply is—a slight anomaly in the slow grind.

Then the first pulse of attention falls upon it.

Perhaps a slate-lizard pauses in its thousand-year patrol, its obsidian eyes fixing on the dust-knot. Perhaps the city itself, through a wind-carved arch that looks like a watching face, turns some form of non-conscious perception toward the spot. The Law of Unraveling Edges works in reverse here, at the inception: observation *gathers*. It defines. The dust-knot feels the pressure of being seen, and in that pressure, a tiny, cold spark kindles.

It becomes a **Gatherer**.

Its body is an ongoing process, not a fixed shape. At its core is a cluster of magnetized crystals, faintly humming, pulling in ferrous dust from the air. Around this, it winds a shell of gathered fragments: chips of pearlescent sinter, flakes of dark mica, splinters of glassy fracture-lines. It has no head, no limbs, yet it moves. It propagates across the stone like a slow stain, disassembling on one edge and reassembling on the other, a peristaltic ripple of particulate matter. Its movement is the sound of sandpaper breathing.

Its purpose is imprinted by the city’s first law: **The Law of Absence**. Nothing is added. Therefore, the Gatherer’s function is to rearrange. It finds areas of disorder—a scatter of scree from a recent (recent meaning three centuries ago) hoodoo collapse—and begins to sort. With painstaking slowness, it separates chips by mineral type, by size, by hue. It builds tiny, perfect piles. It aligns fragments into radial patterns on the flat pans. It does not build; it *curates*. It makes the chaos of erosion into a tidier, more deliberate chaos.

It has no mind, but it has a *direction*. A preference for symmetry, for smooth transitions in grain size, for the faint aesthetic pleasure of a vein of citrine crystal placed beside a sliver of jet-black obsidian. This preference is not its own; it is an echo of the city’s dream of order. The Gatherer is a brushstroke in a painting the city is forgetting how to paint.

It senses the world through vibration and charge. The low hum of the plateau is its background noise. The rare *tink* of a cooling crystal is a clarion call. It feels the footfall of a slate-lizard from a league away as a deep, rhythmic tremor. It knows when reality is fraying nearby—a softening wall emits a silent, high-frequency oscillation that makes its crystalline core resonate with unease. It will avoid such places. Its existence is fragile; to step into a zone of unraveling is to risk its carefully assembled form dissolving back into anonymous dust.

It does not eat, but it sustains itself. It scrapes over the fibrous fungal mats, not consuming them, but drawing from them a minute electrical potential—a byproduct of their mineral metabolism. This energy charges its core, allows it to cohere. It is a symbiont with the slow crusts of life.

Its lifespan is measured in millennia. Eventually, even its careful curation cannot prevent its own degradation. A particularly strong dust storm might scatter its collected fragments. The magnetic charge in its core might slowly bleed away. When this happens, the Gatherer simply stops. Its ripple ceases. Its components settle into the final, neat arrangement it has made of itself. It becomes a small, beautiful mosaic on the stone, a cairn marking its own end. And from that spot, perhaps, the static will build again, and another knot of dust will begin to draw itself together.

There are other things that live here.

The **Silhouettes** are not fauna, but phenomena. They are the city’s afterthoughts. Where a wall erodes into a perfect, human-like profile, and the soulless wind howls through its eye-socket for ten thousand years, a shadow may detach. It is two-dimensional, a cutout of deeper darkness. It drifts along the streets, parallel to the walls, a sliding stain. It has no substance, but it has intent: it seeks completion. It will pause opposite another eroded form, aligning itself, trying to match shape to absence. Sometimes, for a moment, they fit—the silhouette fills the negative space in the stone, and for that instant, there is a shocking, perfect solidity, a statue where there was none. Then it slips free and drifts on, eternally lonely.

The **Humming-Stones** are not creatures, but they are alive. In districts where the basalt is thick with pearlescent veins, certain boulders emit a constant, sub-auditory vibration. This is not a sound, but a *pressure* on thought. If a Gatherer or a slate-lizard lingers too close, their own internal rhythms—the ripple of dust, the clockwork click of jointed legs—will slowly synchronize to the stone’s hum. They become still. Captivated. Over centuries, mineral deposits will coat them, fusing them to the humming stone. They become part of its structure, their forms softened and merged, a fossilized audience to a song without notes.

And finally, there are the **Weepers**.

They are the rarest, born only where the rifts in the bruised sky open most frequently. When that blinding, colorless light pours down, it sometimes strikes a patch of the glassy sinter and passes through it, etching a perfect, contained image within the stone—a frozen moment of that searing, alien geometry. The sinter, wounded, begins to exude a substance.

It is not water. It is a slow, viscous seepage of silica gel, charged with faint luminescence. It beads on the surface like mercury, but moves with a syrupy reluctance. It gathers along the etched lines of the wound, tracing the impossible image. These beads, over years, coalesce into a form: a hunched, mineral drip that vaguely resembles a kneeling figure. This is a Weeper.

Its entire existence is a process of exuding this "tear" substance, which it then, with agonizing slowness, re-absorbs through capillary action in its base. It is a closed loop of grief. The substance, as it cycles, gradually dissolves the etched image within the stone, healing the wound. When the image is finally gone, the Weeper’s source of substance dries up. It stops. It hardens into a smooth, featureless obelisk. Its function—to heal the scars left by reality breaking—is complete.

These are the inhabitants. They are not a society. They rarely interact. A Gatherer might ripple through a plaza curated by another Gatherer centuries prior, adding a minor adjustment, a new arrangement—a form of silent, time-displaced conversation. A Silhouette might drift over a Weeper, casting a temporary, double-shadow that makes the luminous tears gleam brighter for a moment. But they are alone, each a unique expression of the city’s laws: Rearrangement. Echo. Healing. They are the processes of the place made manifest, the slow thoughts in the stone mind of a world that is quietly, beautifully, coming apart.
