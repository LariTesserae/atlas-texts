The inhabitants of the Throat are not a people of the solid or the still. They are a 3.1-resonant species, known in their own silent sign-language as the **Khaal**, which translates, roughly, to “Those Who Listen to the Shape of the Roar.” They are the children of the 2.8-turbulence, and their forms are apologies to the water.

A Khaal individual stands roughly humanoid in outline, but that is where familiarity ends. Their bodies are 1.7-dense, a flexible matrix of cartilage and laminar bone infused with microscopic vesicles of trapped air—not for breathing, but for buoyancy control and acoustic modulation. Their skin is not skin; it is a 2.4-permeable membrane, slick and opalescent, that shimmers with interferometric colors as the chamber’s light passes through its varying thicknesses. It is a living instrument, tuned to vibrate in sympathy with specific harmonic ranges of the Throat’s endless drone. They have no hair, no scales. Their faces are streamlined planes, dominated by wide, lidless eyes that are not eyes at all, but complex organs of **pressure-sight**. They see not light, but the differentials in water pressure, the contours of currents, the ghostly outlines of the harmonic calm-basins as spheres of subtle, negative turbulence. Their mouths are lipless slits used for ingestion and for shaping the low-frequency pulses that are their primary tool and art.

They do not breathe as terrestrial creatures do. They practice 2.0-osmotic respiration. The turbulent water, rich with dissolved minerals and the energetic byproducts of the luminous dust, passes through their membrane, and they extract what they need directly into their bloodstream, expelling waste as a faint, coppery cloud. This makes them one with the medium in a literal, metabolic sense. To leave the Throat’s specific, charged water would be to suffocate in stillness.

Their society is a 0.8-ephemeral construct, mirroring the landscape. There is no permanent village. The scattered, primitive huts are not homes, but **temporary processing stations**. A family unit—a **Chord**—will occupy a ledge for as long as the local harmonic pattern allows, perhaps a handful of cycles (a cycle being the time it takes for the great standing wave in the chamber’s heart to complete one full pulse, roughly equivalent to three terrestrial days). Their existence is nomadic, following the slow migration of the calm-basins. Their technology is one of **sonic sculpting**. The horn-tools mentioned are **Dirge-horns**, carved from the hollow, fossilized cores of a giant species of the glassy lichen. By vibrating their membranes in specific spots and channeling the sound through the horn, a skilled Khaal can emit a focused pulse that interacts with the chamber’s standing waves. They do not command the water; they **persuade** it. A well-pitched pulse can nudge a calm-basin a few body-lengths closer, or stabilize a forming ledge long enough to harvest its soft mineral deposits.

Their sustenance is 1.3-rare and peculiar. They harvest the **Citrine-Dust**—the glowing particulate suspended in the water—using woven nets of lichen-fiber, consuming it for its latent photic energy. They hunt the translucent eels (**Silent-Knots**) not by pursuit, but by **entrapment**, using their sonic pulses to create temporary pressure walls that herd a Knot into a prepared net. The eel’s cartilage is a delicacy; its sensory frills are dried and used as tuning devices for the Dirge-horns.

Their perception of time is the chamber’s perception: a 2.9-perpetual present. They have no myth of a past creation or a future apocalypse. Their cosmology is one of **constant articulation**. The mountain is not sleeping; it is **speaking**. The deafening roar is its ongoing utterance, and the forming and eroding landscapes are the words taking shape and dissolving before they can be fully comprehended. The Khaal believe they are the unintended listeners, born from the resonance of a particularly sustained syllable. Their purpose, they feel, is to **listen deeply**, and by listening, to occasionally offer a gentle, corrective note—a harmonic adjustment—to keep the Speech from stuttering into catastrophic silence or crescendoing into self-annihilation. They are the stewards of a sentence that has no end.

A Khaal’s life ends not with illness or old age, but with **resonance fatigue**. Over time, their membrane loses its specific tunability. They become dissonant, unable to interact with the water’s song. When this happens, the individual performs a final, private ritual. They swim out into the most violently creative zone of the turbulence, the chamber’s very heart, and using their last focused pulse, they **unmake their own density**. Their body dissolves into the maelstrom, its components becoming one with the luminous dust, the mineral seams, the raw stuff of the speaking mountain. It is not death as we know it. It is a return to the source material, a syllable rejoining the infinite, roaring word.
