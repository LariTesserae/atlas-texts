They are the Keepers of the Thrum, the children of the Vat. They are perhaps two hundred souls, though none count them. Their ancestors—engineers, botanists, system-architects—sealed the great doors generations ago and adapted or died. The ones who remain are not the builders; they are the cultivated crop, a human strain pruned and shaped by the roaring dark.

They are, to an outside eye, a silent people. The ceaseless thunder of the pumps and fans has, over centuries, rendered them functionally deaf. Not from damage, but from a strange, symbiotic adaptation: their auditory cortex has repurposed itself. They do not hear the roar; they feel it as a constant, grounding vibration, a sixth sense that orients them in the iron lattice. Their world is one of pressure changes in the inner ear, of seismic tremors in the grates, of textures transmitted through their bare soles. Their primary language is a tactile sign speech, fingers moving against another's palm or forearm, nuanced and rapid. In the middle distance, they communicate with the intricate, rhythmic flickering of the sodium and argon lamps that line the catwalks—a grammar of colored light.

Physically, they are lean and strong, with a density to their bones from a lifetime spent fighting the subtle, constant pull of the high-altitude pressure differential. Their skin tends toward a pallor, never touched by true sun, but is often marked with the same mineral stains that streak the walls: handprints of rust, smudges of copper-green. Their eyes are large, dark, and liquid, optimized for the diffuse, green-tinted gloom. They blink in slow synchrony with the misting cycles.

They have no names as such. Identities are fluid, tied to function and moment. One might be *Pump-Minder* until the watch changes, then become *Vine-Tender*, then *Glass-Watcher*. Their society is a perfect loop of maintenance, a human feedback system integrated into the machine. Their entire culture is built around the Preservation of the Hum—the steady-state drone that means all is well. A change in pitch, a stutter in the rhythmic *ka-chunk*, is a communal crisis, a silent panic that ripples through the lamp-codes.

They live in the interstitial spaces. Their dwellings are not rooms, but sheltered niches: the warm cubbies around steam vents, the hammock-nets slung between giant pipe runs, the platforms built onto the service gantries overlooking the Heart. They sleep to the lullaby of metal expansion, eat a diet of protein-rich fungal mats cultivated on composted plant matter, and drink the pure, distilled water that collects in designated runoff channels.

Their relationship with the created biosphere is one of reverent husbandry. They do not see the vines and epiphynes as decoration, but as vital colleagues—the lungs and filters of their world. The six-limbed chitinous fauna are both pest and partner; some species are cultivated to clean algae from crucial gauge-glasses, others are cautiously harvested for their iridescent shells, which are ground into pigment for the lamp-filters.

Their cosmology is simple: the Cloud-Vat is the Universe. The curved steel walls are the sky. The geodesic glass roof is the firmament, beyond which lies only the lethal, purple Void. The great amber Heart in the central hollow is God—not a benevolent god, but a sleeping, hungry one that must be fed with compacted peat-bricks and soothed with rituals of lubrication and coolant. The misting rain is its breath, the thrum of the pumps its pulse. To maintain it is to pray. A Keeper's highest honor is to be chosen for the close-inspection of the Heart, to feel its radiant heat on their face and check the sacred pressure seals.

They are born, live, and die within the two-hundred-pace diameter and the two-thousand-pace height. Their dead are committed to the deepest mulch-beds, their bodies becoming sustenance for the vines, completing the cycle. They know no other life. The concept of "outside" is a myth, a ghost story told in finger-taps to explain the occasional shudder that runs through the structure—the wind of the Void, they say, beating against the walls of reality.

They are happy, in their way. Theirs is a world of total, deafening purpose. Every action has meaning, every gesture sustains the whole. In the roaring, warm, wet, green engine of their sky, they are the tiny, careful greasers of the gears, the silent singers of the endless song of equilibrium. They are the life the Vat was built to contain, and they have become, perfectly, its keepers.
