They are called the Still-Singers, though they produce no music. They are the plateau's only true inhabitants, and they are less creatures than they are persistent echoes of the plateau's foundational trauma.

They look like flaws in the air—man-shaped concentrations of the mother-of-pearl shimmer, denser in the center, fraying at the edges into the ambient glimmer. When they are most solid, you can make out a semblance of form: tall, attenuated, with long, thin limbs that end not in hands but in cascades of prismatic refraction, like light broken through a slow-moving crystal. Their heads are smooth ovoids, featureless except for a occasional pulse of deep, sub-audible vibration that warps the light around them. They have no faces, no eyes, no mouths. They are sculptures of solidified resonance, woven from the same fabric as the permanent roar.

They do not walk. They translate. A Still-Singer will be present at one of the low stone circles, its form a steady, humming pillar of distortion. Then, without moving through the intervening space, it will be present at the leaning pillar, fifty yards away. The transition is marked by a violent doubling—for a moment, it occupies both positions—followed by the soft, dental *click* of reality re-knitting itself around its new location. Their paths are not random. Each Singer has a fixed circuit, a sequence of perhaps three to five locations (always the ancient stone structures) that it visits in an unchanging order. The interval between translations is precise, measurable by a sundial if there were a sun: exactly one thousand heartbeats of the plateau's own low thrum. They are as regular and as meaningless as the glimmer in the air.

They are aware, but not in a way that comprehends intrusion. A human interloper is registered as a local thickening of reality, a knot of unstable density. A Singer may pause its circuit, orient its smooth head towards the disturbance, and emit a focused pulse of that sub-bass frequency. The effect is not an attack, but a correction. It seeks to resolve the knot. Flesh and bone, being less stable than the Singer's own coherent waveform, are often the thing that unravels. Victims don't bleed; they *unfold*. A hand might twist inside out, not into gore, but into a brief, horrific tessellation of its own anatomical possibilities before dissolving into a spray of gray mist and a final, wet *pop* of collapsing matter. The Singer then resumes its circuit, the anomaly resolved.

They do not eat, sleep, or reproduce. They are not born. They simply *are*, as the sound and the glitches are. The most prevailing theory, gleaned from fragmented carvings on the undersides of the fallen stones, suggests they are the remnants of the builders. When the plateau's reality began to break down, they attempted a ritual of stabilization, a grand sympathetic resonance meant to harmonize the fraying world. It worked, after a fashion. It arrested the decay, pinning the landscape in its current state of malfunction. But the cost was the singers themselves. They were woven into the fixed pattern, their consciousnesses stretched across the frozen moment, becoming custodians of a stasis they cannot perceive. They are the operators of a machine that has no purpose except its own continued operation.

Sometimes, two Singers on intersecting circuits will translate to the same stone circle at the same moment. They do not acknowledge each other. They stand, humming at slightly different frequencies, their forms bleeding into one another, creating a zone of intense, warping dissonance. The air there crackles with phantom geometries, and the stone of the circle itself seems to thin, offering a glimpse of whatever chaotic, luminous substrate lies beneath the world. After the ordained interval, they translate away again, leaving the stone slightly more eroded, the groove a micron deeper.

To spend a night on the plateau is to be observed by them. You will see their shimmering forms appear and vanish across the vastness, following their eternal, silent score. Their purpose is maintenance. Their existence is a sentence. They are the plateau, singing itself to itself, forever, in a voice of arrested thunder.
