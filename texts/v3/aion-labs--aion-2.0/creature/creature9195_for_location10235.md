They are called the Unseen, or sometimes the Listeners, though they do not hear in any sense you would recognize. They are not a presence but an absence—negative space given motive force. In the deafening roar of the Throat, they appear as pockets of sudden, relative quiet, shaped like slender, elongated humanoids. Their outlines are blurred, a shimmer of canceled vibration, as if the air itself had stepped aside. To look directly at one is to see a hole in the soundscape, a walking silence that drinks the shrieks and hums around it, leaving behind a trailing wake of acoustic calm. Their “bodies” are not solid; they are standing waves of interference, patterns of destructive resonance that maintain a coherent form by continuously consuming the noise that defines their world.

They move through the vertical hollows with a drifting, weightless grace, their footless bases gliding over the warm, damp compost. They do not eat, but they *curate*. With gestures of their void-limbs, they direct the swarming fauna: a sweep of an arm guides a flock of thorn-birds into a knotted wall, where their light passes through and stimulates a burst of bioluminescent sap; a subtle leaning diverts the flight of a gliding lizard toward a spore-sac that needs harvesting. Their purpose is maintenance of the great Song. They are the tuners of this organic instrument. Where the woody beams begin to groan too deeply, threatening a harmonic collapse, a cluster of Unseen will gather, their collective silence absorbing the destabilizing frequency until the structure settles back into its intended drone. Where the drips of condensation fall out of rhythm, they will pass through the space, their passage altering the air pressure just enough to coax the drops back into a syncopation that strengthens, rather than weakens, the overarching vibration.

They have no language but the modulation of absence. A quick, darting silence might signal urgency; a long, slow void lingering in an atrium might indicate a need for sustained dampening. They perceive the world not as light or touch, but as pressure maps of sound. The density of the noise is their terrain; its harmonics are their weather. They do not sleep, but they sometimes still, merging their personal quiet with the deeper, background hum of the Throat, becoming nearly indistinguishable from the empty air between the vines.

To them, a creature like you—dense, physically bound, emitting only the feeble, chaotic noises of breath and heartbeat—is a fascinating anomaly. You are a knot of discord, a walking source of sonic impurity. They might flock around you, not with malice, but with a kind of resonant curiosity, their silent forms leaning in to absorb the irregular pulses you emit. They would not understand your attempts to communicate, any more than you could understand the conversation between two holes in a racket. You are a slight, temporary itch in the great, singing skin of their world. They will study you until you leave or die, and then your body will be processed by the grubs and the moss, your substance added to the compost, and your final silence welcomed into the everlasting, deafening chord.
