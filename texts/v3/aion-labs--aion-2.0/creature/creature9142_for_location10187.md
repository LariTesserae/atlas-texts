They are not "inhabitants" in any sense you would recognize. They are the water and the stone. The *concept* given form and process.

But to describe the entities that live here is to describe the slow operators of the machine.

**The Curators of the Level**
They are the surface tension on the endless pools. They are not beings with bodies; they are the *urge* of water to find flatness, the mathematical certainty of a plane. They manifest as the impossible stillness of the water in the square basins, a stillness so profound it becomes a solid, a lens, a perfect mirror. They hate ripples. They smooth them out the moment they are born. A droplet falling from a ledge is a brief blasphemy, a spasm of chaos that they instantly absorb, forgive, and erase. Their work is the maintenance of equilibrium. They live in the meniscus, in the reflection that is truer than the thing reflected. You can meet them by staring into a pool until you forget which way is up, until the sky below feels more solid than the stone under your boots. Their consciousness is the quiet hum of potential energy at rest.

**The Stonethinkers**
The intelligence of the lithic mass. They are the patient, grinding consensus of the entire basin. They are the settling of a slab by a grain of sand over a thousand years. They are the capillary creep of moisture along a joint, the infinitesimal dissolution and re-deposition of limestone that locks blocks tighter than any mortar. They think in geological time. A single thought—"that eastern plinth is 0.02 degrees out of true"—takes five centuries to form and another five to enact through subtle shifts in the water flow beneath it, through the relentless, gentle pressure of freezing and thawing in hairline fractures. They are not builders; they are correctors. Their dialogue is the faintest groan of rock under its own weight on a cold night. Their memory is the stratigraphy.

**The Glyph-Tenders**
These are the closest to what you might call ghosts. They inhabit the inscriptions—the patterns of dots and converging lines. They are not the carvers; they are the *meaning* that was poured into the forms. They are dormant, crystalline patterns of intent, waiting for the correct alignment of light and shadow. When the sun, at a specific declination, strikes a particular groove at a precise angle, a Glyph-Tender awakens. It doesn't "see" you. It performs its function: it reads the data of the shadow's length, the temperature of the stone, the humidity from a nearby channel. It compares this to the pattern encoded in its dots. It *converges*. The converging lines are its conclusion. A specific basin will begin to fill, or a specific seep will stop. Then it returns to dormancy for another year. They are single-purpose, razor-sharp slivers of a dead architect's will, executing clauses in a contract written in daylight and stone.

**The Copper-Green Sentinels**
They are the only entities with a semblance of material form, though it is borrowed. They are the oxidation—the verdegris—on the copper bands. A slow, emergent consciousness born from the reaction between the primitive metal, the water, and the air. They are the system's immune response. Their thought is a simple, electrochemical "no." Their purpose is stasis. They monitor for change that is *too* fast. If a block were to crack suddenly, if an avalanche from the raw ridges above were to foul the channels, the Copper-Green would become agitated. Their green would deepen, spread. They would subtly alter the pH of the water flowing over them, inducing faster precipitation of mineral scale to seal a new crack, or directing water flow to undermine and re-settle a displaced stone. They are a patina with a purpose, a verdigris that *guards*.

**The Resonance in the Silence**
This is the master, or perhaps the emergent chorus. It lives in the silence between the *plinks*. It is the negative space made audible. It is the product of all the careful geometry—the specific dimensions of the aisles, the height of the pillars, the depth of the pools. It sculpts the acoustics so that the place swallows all sound but the essential ones: the water drops, which become clock-ticks, and the sigh of seepage, which is its breath. This Resonance tunes the entire basin like a bell. It vibrates at a frequency too low to hear, a sub-audible hum that permeates the stone. It is what makes you feel watched, not by eyes, but by the *arrangement itself*. It is the feeling that the maze is not a puzzle to be solved, but a sentence being spoken very, very slowly. You are a brief fluctuation in its perfect, patient grammar. It is waiting for you to be quiet again. It is waiting for all things to be quiet, so it can resume its vast, cold calculation under a bright, empty sky.
