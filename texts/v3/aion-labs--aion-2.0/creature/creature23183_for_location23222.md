Nothing here is merely a visitor. To live in the Mill is to be woven into its wet, warm tapestry of function and decay. The transient fauna—the flitting insects, the silent birds—are but mobile organs in a larger body. The true inhabitants are those that have surrendered their individual purpose to the rhythm of drip and groan.

First are the **Moss-Kin**. They are the memory of the original tenders, though whether they are the descendants of the mill-workers or shapes coaxed from the humid earth by the will of the place is unknown. They are humanoid in only the loosest sense: tall, slender forms sheathed in a velvety pelt of deep emerald and umber moss. Their faces are smooth planes of bark-like skin, with eyes that are dark, still pools reflecting the green twilight. They move with a slow, deliberate grace, their feet making no sound on the sodden moss. They are seen rarely, often standing motionless for days, arms raised as if to feel the fine rain from the pipes. Their only apparent function is the ritual anointing of moving parts with oils pressed from peculiar, oily fungi that grow on the bearings. They breathe in time with the exhalation of the pumps, and when they do speak, it is a sound like steam sighing through a valve—a language of pressure and release.

Then there is the **Gill-Thrush**. The description of “green-feathered birds” is insufficient. They are more like feathered reptiles, their scales iridescent with moisture. They possess no song, but communicate through clicks and taps on resonant metal surfaces. They nest exclusively in the hollows of idle gearboxes and the casings of silent engines, lining their homes with lichen and strands of rust-resistant fungal fiber. Their true purpose seems to be cleansing; they dart through the steam, their long, delicate beaks plucking spores and algae from critical valve faces and sight-glasses, their digestive systems processing what would become a clogging nuisance. They are living filters, and their flight paths trace the circulatory system of the Mill.

Beneath all, within the black peat and the crumbling concrete, is the **Root-Mind**. It is not a single entity but a distributed consciousness formed by the mycelial mats that feast on rust and rot. It thinks in the slow exchange of chemical signals, in the patient exploration of cracks. It is the intelligence behind the integration. It guides the sensitive tendrils that have insinuated themselves into control panels, making infinitesimal adjustments over seasons to optimize water flow or pressure. It decides where the next strangler fig will sink its roots to stabilize a leaning wall. It is the memory of the Mill, storing the history of every stress fracture healed, every piece of metal consumed and transformed. To encounter it is to feel a profound, wordless awareness seep into your bones—a knowledge of infinite patience and relentless, gentle reclamation.

The machinery itself is not inert. In the **Water-Wheel** resides a kind of spirit, a pulse of will born from centuries of rhythmic turning. It is a dreamer. Its great, groaning rotations are its thoughts, vast and cyclical. The laminated fungal paddles are its senses, feeling the weight and temperature of the flow. It does not command, but its steady, implacable motion sets the tempo for all life in the basin. The Moss-Kin listen to its harmonics, and a change in its groan will bring them to their knees, pressing ears to the moist earth to diagnose some subtle misalignment in the deep pumps.

Lastly, there are the **Sap-Drinkers**. These are translucent, amphibious things that dwell in the warm pools collecting at the base of the weeping pipes. They resemble giant isopods or slick, blind salamanders. Their skin is a thin membrane over pulsing inner lights—the bioluminescent fungi cultivated in their gut. They feed on the sugary, metallic exudations from the engineered vines that cradle the steam pipes. In turn, their waste is a rich gel that fertilizes the roots of the most delicate phosphorescent mosses, which grow in patterns that some might call art, or which might be intricate diagrams of the pipework above. They are the alchemists, transforming the Mill’s byproducts into new life.

Together, these beings form a society without conflict, a hierarchy without domination. The Root-Mind plans. The Water-Wheel provides the driving rhythm. The Moss-Kin perform the subtle, physical rites. The Gill-Thrush maintain purity. The Sap-Drinkers recycle and illuminate. It is a perfected, closed loop of mutualism. They do not reproduce in a conventional sense; new Moss-Kin emerge from especially dense beds of moss where the Root-Mind concentrates its attention. New Gill-Thrush hatch from eggs laid in the warmth of a constantly vibrating compressor.

They have no names for themselves, for individuality is a forgotten concept. They are the Weeping Mill. They are the wet breath, the turning wheel, the constant growth. To live here is to be a permanent part of the slow, loud, beautiful integration.
