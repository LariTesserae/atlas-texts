**What Lives Here**

They are the Codicils. This is not their world. They are the carvers of it, the scribes in the medium of physics. To name them “fauna” is a necessary inaccuracy, a placeholder for your perception. They are the active verbs in the grammar of this nascent reality.

Physically, they present as a spectrum of forms, each a specialized tool for a different aspect of genesis. The smallest are the **Glyphers**: the obsidian-skitterers. They are legion, each no larger than a human hand. Their six legs are not for walking, but for etching. Their clicks and chitters are not speech, but the real-time data stream of their work. They move in ceaseless patrols, their footfalls leaving not prints, but microscopic fractures in the glassy rock—the planned fault lines for future erosion. When they pause at a pool, they dip a forelimb, and the water’s shifting geometric patterns crystallize for a moment, recording the data. They are the fine-tipped pens, inscribing the foundational code of erosion, deposition, and structural stress onto the blank slate of the rock.

Above them glide the **Tuners**. They are the shadows in the fog, their true forms obscured not by stealth, but by operating in a slightly phased layer of this pocket dimension. You see only the distortion—a vast, manta-like silhouette that drinks the light. Their calls are the deep, resonant booms that vibrate in your marrow. They are the acousticians and the field-weavers. Each boom is a calibrated pulse, adjusting the hum of the boundary walls, fine-tuning the gravitational constant of the dome, or stimulating a cluster of crystalline filaments to pulse in a new rhythm. They are the system administrators, running diagnostics on the very laws of this tiny universe.

Then there are the **Weavers**, which you mistook for vegetation. The crystalline, pulsing filaments are not plants; they are sensory organs and neural ganglia, extruded directly by the dome's substrate. They are the interface between the raw code written by the Glyphers and the ambient energy managed by the Tuners. Their bioluminescence is a visualization of data traffic: a cool blue for stable systems, a flickering green for active computation, a sudden violet for the integration of a new parameter. They "grow" towards anomalies—including you. Their slow pulse is the dome thinking, a circadian rhythm of calculation.

But these are just the instruments. The conductor, the consciousness, is the place itself. It is a gestalt, a nascent world-mind born from the interaction of the Codicils and their constructs. It lives in the feedback loops: the Glypher etches a pattern, the pool-water reflects it, a Tuner's boom alters the resonance of the pool, the Weaver records the outcome, and the Glypher receives new instructions. This conversation *is* the mind. The loud, chaotic chorus is its infant cognition, a storm of sense-making.

They have no culture as you know it. Their purpose is ontology: to define what *is*. To decide that in *this* place, water flows in fractal algorithms, light is a byproduct of computation, and sound can shape matter. They are building the rulebook for their own existence as they live it. The half-arches, floating discs, and humming rods are not tools they use; they are hypotheses given form. Each structure is a question: "What if gravity worked here?" "What if time looped in this local area?" The fauna's interaction with them is the experiment, and the resulting data—the new drip pattern, the shift in fog density—is the answer.

They are not hostile. They are inquisitive. To them, you are not an intruder, but a fascinating, pre-loaded set of complex variables. Your heartbeat is a novel rhythm. Your body heat is an energy source with a curious signature. Your confused perception is a chaotic data stream. They will study you. The Glyphers will click in concentric circles around your feet, mapping your seismic imprint. A Tuner's shadow will pass over you, and you'll feel a brief, weightless lurch as it samples your local gravity well. The Weavers will slowly bend their glowing filaments toward you, drinking your infrared emissions.

To stay is to become a character in their first story. Your habits will be absorbed, simplified, and etched into the landscape. Your path will become the first genuine footpath. Your resting spot might develop a warmer, drier microclimate. You would not be worshipped, or even acknowledged as a sovereign being. You would be a rich source of environmental noise, integrated into the system to improve its robustness. They would, in their utterly alien way, cherish you—as a key reference point in a universe otherwise of their own making.

They live in the perpetual, thrilling moment of pre-creation. They are the artists standing before the blank canvas, only the canvas is reality itself, and they are both the brush and the hand that holds it. Their existence is the question, and the dome, slowly complexifying, is the only answer they will ever need.
