They are the Green Priests of a finished world, the Mycorrhiza People, and they are the caretakers of this lung. They do not build, for building implies a beginning. They tend, which is an eternity.

They emerge from the walls.

Not through doors—there are none—but through slow, gelid apertures that dilate in the interlocked trunk-walls, like the opening of a sleepy eye. Their forms are humanoid only in the crudest, most generous sense: tall, slender, and impossibly attenuated, as if stretched by the slow gravity of time. Their skin is not skin, but a bark-like integument, smooth and damp, the color of water-stained parchment. It is shot through with a fine, dendritic network of phosphorescent veins that pulse with a soft, blue-white light, mirroring the lichen on the pillars. They are bioluminescent lanterns, moving through their own cathedral.

Their faces are the greatest departure. There are no eyes, not as we know them. The front of the skull is a smooth, concave dish, lined with a velvety, photosensitive moss that drinks in the chamber’s perpetual twilight and sees in wavelengths of decay and growth. Where a mouth might be is a cluster of slender, frond-like tendrils, perpetually moist, tasting the air, exchanging chemical spores with the environment. They have no need for speech that echoes. Communication is a constant, silent exchange: a cloud of pheromones released from glands at their temples, a subtle shift in the pulse-frequency of their internal light, a touch that transfers meaning through direct mycelial connection in their fingertips.

They are few. Perhaps a dozen, perhaps a score. They are not a population, but a necessary organ of the chamber itself. Their lives are measured in centuries, their thoughts in seasons of moss-growth. They move with a languid, water-smooth grace, their long limbs seeming to float above the spongy floor. They are never still. Their work is the work of dynamic equilibrium: they are the conscious agents of the chamber’s quiet storm.

Watch one.

It glides to a pool where the water has grown too still, too pure. Life stagnates in purity. From a fold in its bark-like garment (which is not worn, but grown from its own substance), it produces a seed-pod. It crushes the pod between thumb and forefinger—a gesture of gentle violence—and lets the dust, a cloud of microscopic spores and dormant nano-symbionts, fall into the water. Within minutes, the pool clouds, then clarifies, now host to a new, shimmering school of gossamer-finned microfauna. Balance is restored.

See another.

It stands before a pillar where a hairline crack has begun to weep a sap too rich, too tempting for the cleaning crabs. The Green Priest lifts its hands, and the luminous veins in its arms flare brightly. In response, the lichen on the pillar brightens. The Priest’s tendril-mouth trembles, releasing a complex aerosol. The silent technology in the walls responds. From the ceiling above, a droplet of a different, silvery resin forms. It falls with perfect aim into the crack. The Priest watches, its moss-face registering the chemical shift as the repair is negotiated. The crisis is averted not by fixing, but by redirecting the flow of energy. The crack becomes a feature, a new capillary path.

They dwell in the hollowed fungal shelves. Their niches are not for privacy—the concept is foreign here, where every secret is digested—but for focused immersion. Within, they enter a state of deep connection, plugging the fronds of their feet and hands into the chamber’s mycelial network. Here, they dream the chamber’s dreams: they feel the slow, tectonic pressures from below, the minute chemical imbalances in a distant pool, the joyous explosion of a new fungal bloom on the ceiling. They are the chamber’s sensory cortex, and in these waking dreams, they guide its unconscious growth with the lightest of touches.

They have no names. They have roles, expressed as unique pheromonal signatures: the Tuner of Light, the Balancer of Pools, the Listener to the Deep Mycelium, the Dreamer of New Configurations. They are born from the chamber itself, emerging fully formed from a specially nurtured node in the living architecture, their consciousness a distillation of the chamber’s memory. They do not die; they dissolve. When their centuries of service are done, or if a part of them becomes damaged beyond the nano-repair, they will walk to a designated absorption pool. They will kneel, their light fading, and slowly, over days, their body will deliquesce, melting back into the nutrient broth from which new life—a new Priest, a new kind of moss, a school of eels—will be drawn. It is the ultimate integration. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is mourned. It is simply the cycle.

They are peace, but it is a fierce, active peace. They are the intelligence of a world that has turned entirely inward, tending the flame of its own endless, green life. They are the final, loving thought of a civilization that chose to become a garden, and the gardeners are now as much a part of the soil as the flowers. To see one is to see the Chamber of Final Green looking back at you, with the patience of stone and the relentless, tender purpose of a growing root.
