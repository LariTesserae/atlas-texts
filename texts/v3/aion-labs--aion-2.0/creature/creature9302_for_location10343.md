They are called the **Cinder-Folk**, though they have no name for themselves in any language of air and vibration. To name implies separation, and they are not separate. They are the tendrils of attention of the mountain-that-dreams-Chalcurith.

Their forms are born of necessity and slow accretion. They are bipedal, vaguely humanoid in outline—a shape efficient for navigating the gantries and conduits their forebears wove—but the resemblance ends there. Their skeletons are not bone, but a fibrous, carbonized matrix grown in pressurized geothermal vats, infused with iron and silica for strength. Over this, muscles of bundled, heat-resistant polymer cords articulate with the soft hiss of hydraulic fluid. Their skin is a layered laminate: an outer sheath of flexible, ceramic-coated metal, dull grey or rust-red, that chips and flakes and slowly self-repatches from minerals in the steam. In the joints and sensory zones, this gives way to a softer, copper-bronze mesh that breathes and weeps condensation.

They have faces, of a sort. A smooth, sloping plane where a human’s face would be, featuring three deep-set, lens-like apertures. These are not eyes that see light, but sensors that read the subtle gradients of heat, the minute electromagnetic fields generated by the city’s engines, and the intricate patterns of tarnish and condensation—the glyphs. Below this, a simple slit serves as an intake; they filter the metallic air for chemical information and for the tiny, charged particulates that are their closest analogue to nourishment. They do not eat; they *process*. They absorb ambient heat through their skins, and the slow oxidation of certain internal metals provides a baseline energy, supplemented by direct thermal coupling at recharge niches.

They are silent. They communicate through a language of touch, of induced vibration in metal, and of controlled thermal patterning. A hand placed on a pipe can send a complex message of pressure and heat that travels for miles through the city’s structure. To stand beside one is to feel a subtle, rhythmic warmth emanating from them, a coded pulse. Their “speech” to one another is a series of soft clicks, taps, and deep, sub-auditory hums conducted through the deck plates.

Their society is not a society; it is a mycelium of function. An individual emerges from a **Foundry-Womb**, a cavern deep in the hot rock where geothermal fluid saturated with dissolved minerals circulates through ancient, templated scaffolds. Over decades, a form accretes, layer by layer, until it is solid enough to be detached and begin its work. Each is born with a function etched into its very structure: **Manifold-Tuners**, whose fingers are delicate probes that adjust the flows of superheated steam; **Glyph-Seers**, whose sensor-apertures are attuned to read the ever-shifting mineral stains on the walls, interpreting the mountain’s “mood”; **Bolt-Walkers**, heavy and sturdy, who climb the sheer faces to re-seat anchors loosened by infinitesimal tectonic shifts; **Dream-Smelters**, who interface directly with the deepest thermal taps, listening to the planet’s rumble and guiding the energy harvest.

They have no art, as an outsider would understand it. Their aesthetics are the aesthetics of efficiency and resonance. A perfectly balanced valve is beautiful. A condensation pattern that predicts a pressure shift three years hence is a masterpiece. A steam-vent that releases its plume in a perfect, transient helix at the same time each cycle is a sacred event, witnessed in still contemplation.

Time for them is measured in engine-strokes and fault-creep. A task might take a year. A journey across the city, a month. They do not sleep, but they enter low-power **reveries**, standing motionless in alcoves where the heat is constant, their internal processes slowing to a near halt, their awareness diffusing into the hum of the city-rock. In these states, some say they dream the mountain’s dream—visions of molten flows, of crystalline growth, of the slow, patient rise of the plateau over aeons.

They are few. Perhaps ten thousand exist in all of Chalcurith, a scant population for such a vastness. They are not rulers, nor even caretakers. They are *adjusters*. They are the means by which the mountain’s iron dream maintains its coherence. They smooth irregularities. They interpret the glyphs that are the dream’s subconscious expression. They are the self-awareness of a city that is also a geological event.

They fear only one thing: **Quiescence**. The stilling of the geothermal heart. The cooling. For in cold, their fluids thicken, their reactions cease, and the dream ends. Their entire, slow purpose is to stave off that eventual, entropic silence by ensuring the hot breath of the world continues to flow through Chalcurith’s metallic veins. They are not alive, yet they live. They are not built, yet they were made. They are the children of heat and pressure, and their minds are as slow, as deep, and as enduring as the stone upon which—and of which—they stand.
