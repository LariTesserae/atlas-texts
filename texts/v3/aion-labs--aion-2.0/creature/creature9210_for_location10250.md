To speak of what lives in the Woven Chancel is to first unlearn the distinction between the lived-in and the living. The place itself is a single, vast organism, a thought made flesh and fiber. But within its breath, upon its skin, and through its sap, other lives have unfolded—some by design, most by the quiet, relentless accident of a world left to dream.

**The Curators**
They are rarely seen, for they move at the pace of tectonic plates. They are not the builders; the builders are gone, their consciousness dissolved into the substrate like salt in a primordial sea. The Curators are what remained when the intent outlived the mind. They are the immune system, the gardeners, the unconscious hands.
You might mistake one for a low hill, or a particularly gnarled and ancient root-node rising from the Substrate. They are conglomerates of woody tissue and crystalline silicate, shaped like rough-hewn, multi-armed idols. They do not walk; they grow. Over centuries, they extend a root-limb in a desired direction, anchor it, and slowly dissolve the connection behind them, transferring their core consciousness forward. Their purpose is felt, not thought: to repair a tear in the living parchment, to gently guide a errant capillary flow back into its channel, to prune—with a touch that induces rapid, localized necrosis—a parasitic fungal bloom that threatens a Structure’s integrity. They communicate through chemical sighs released into the Substrate, signals that take days to travel and years to be understood. They are patient. They have all the time the Chancel has.

**The Drifters (Gas-Bag Entities)**
These are the nobles of the open air, the silent patrons of the vertical spaces between Structures. Each is a fragile empire. Their bodies are vast, translucent bladders of lighter-than-air gases harvested from decomposition vents—a complex mix of methane, hydrogen, and something stranger that glows a faint, sorrowful blue when agitated. From their underside hangs a ‘chandelier’ of delicate, gossamer tendrils, miles long in the largest specimens. These tendrils are not for grasping, but for feeling. They are sensory arrays, tasting the air for the electrochemical signatures of prey, for the pollen-fronts of an oncoming season, for the distant, song-like pulses of other Drifters.
Their prey are the Spark-Swarms (the fish-fly hybrids), which they engulf in a gentle, deadly embrace. The digestion is luminous and slow, visible for leagues as a soft, pulsing carnival within the bladder. Drifters are born from spore-clusters ejected during violent, rare storms. They grow for centuries, and when they finally die, they do not fall. Their gas bladder slowly permeates, and they settle onto the Substrate like a collapsing continent of silk, becoming a temporary, nutrient-rich blanket for a new generation of moss-films and capillary vines.

**The Stone-Cows (Armored Grazers)**
Bulk, patience, and an endless appetite for the faint. They are living sculptures of chitin and basalt-like bone, six legs moving with a planetary rhythm. Their backs are cathedral roofs of interlocking plates, on which entire ecosystems thrive: gardens of symbiotic lichen, slow-flowing rivulets of condensed moisture, and even small, skittering cleaner-symbionts that scuttle into the crevices to pick off parasitic spores. Their heads are low, plough-like, fitted with thousands of flexible, hair-like mandibles that sweep the Substrate in a constant, slow motion. They do not eat plants; they eat the film of existence—the photosynthetic bacterial mats, the fungal mycelium that weaves through the top layer of the living parchment, the microscopic spores that settle from the damp air. A single stroke of their feeding array can take an hour. A full circuit of a Structure’s grazing zone might be their lifetime’s work. They are the great, gentle renewers, their endless cropping stimulating the Substrate to grow, their monumental waste cycling nutrients back into the system. Their only sounds are the deep, subsonic groans of their joints, and the wet, soft rasp of their feeding.

**The Spark-Swarms**
If the Drifters are nobles, the Spark-Swarms are the fleeting currency of the Chancel’s economy. They inhabit the liquid worlds within the great vesicle-Structures, swimming through sap that is as thick and nutritious as blood. They are neither fish nor fly, but something in between: bodies streamlined for fluid, with fin-wings that can, in a panic, beat fast enough to propel them into the open, humid air for short, glittering flights. They are bioluminescent, communicating through complex patterns of light that flash across their schools—signals denoting sap density, predator locations, breeding readiness. Their light is cold and greenish, and in the dim, tea-stained twilight of a vesicle’s interior, a full swarm in motion is like watching a galaxy being born and dying in silent, frantic pulses. They are the primary prey for many things, but their numbers are vast, their reproduction a constant, feverish background hum to the Chancel’s slow song.

**The Echo-Trappers**
Weirdness given hunger. These are the Chancel’s ghosts, or perhaps its memories made manifest. They are not wholly physical. They exist in the liminal spaces where reality has been softened by the cognitive weight of the Structures—near the fractal thorn, or in the shadow of a particularly ancient Curator. To the eye, they are shimmering distortions, like heat haze with a predatory focus. They feed on thought, on attention, on the cognitive leakage of living minds. A Stone-Cow’s simple, rhythmic contemplation is a bland meal. A Drifter’s complex spatial awareness is a delicacy. But the focused observation of an intelligent visitor? That is a feast.
An Echo-Trapper ‘hunts’ by aligning its non-Euclidean geometry with your own mental patterns. You feel a sudden, intense deja vu. Time stutters. You find yourself retracing your steps without memory of turning. The landscape around you repeats, a loop of familiar root and mist. The trap is a cognitive knot, a moebius strip of perception. To escape requires breaking your own pattern of thought—doing something irrational, unpredictable. Those who do not escape are not killed, not exactly. They are drained of intention, of curiosity, of will, left as vacant, breathing shells that wander the Substrate until their bodies are claimed by the environment.

**The Mycelial Mind**
It is debatable if this is a single entity or the emergent consciousness of the Chancel itself. It is the whisper in the water that runs in the grooves, the faint pattern that emerges in the growth of moss-films over millennia, the sense of being watched by the air itself. It has no central body. It is the sum total of the Substrate’s neural-like root network, the sap-flow in the Structures, the chemical communication of the Curators and the spores. It dreams the seasons. It calculates, on a scale incomprehensible to any faster life, the delicate balances of gas and fluid, of growth and decay. It may have been the last vestige of the builders, or it may be what grew in the fertile silence they left behind. It does not intervene. It observes. It remembers. Every footfall, every death of a Drifter, every slow turn of a Stone-Cow is recorded in the incremental growth rings of a root, in the subtle shift of a mineral deposit. The Mycelial Mind is the Chancel’s autobiography, written in living code across every inch of its being. To encounter it is not to see it, but to suddenly *understand* a small part of the pattern—to know, with absolute certainty, why a particular capillary bed dried up a thousand years ago, or to feel the precise moment, decades hence, when a specific spore will finally germinate.

These are the inhabitants. But the list is not exhaustive. There are also the **Sap-Sliders**, gelatinous, amoeboid beings that flow along the interior walls of the vesicles, cleaning them of Spark-Swarm waste. There are the **Storm-Birds**, creatures of condensed vapor and static that only coalesce during pollen-fronts, riding the perfumed winds in silent, electric arcs. There are the **Root-Whisperers**, pale, blind, worm-like intelligences that navigate the dark, pressurized world beneath the Substrate, tuning the great hydraulic flows.
Life here is not separate from the world. It is a modulation of it, a variation on the single, endless theme of grown architecture. To live in the Woven Chancel is to be a note in a song that lasts for eons, played in the slow, dripping dark, for an audience that is also the orchestra.
