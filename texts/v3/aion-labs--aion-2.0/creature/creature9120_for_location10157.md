Nothing *lives* in the Chamber of Root and Steam in the way you might understand it. Nothing arrives, and nothing leaves. The residents are not visitors; they are the Chamber’s own flesh and breath, its persistent dream. They are the manifestations of a process—the slow, wet marriage of stubborn industry and relentless life. They are the children of that collaboration, born in rust and sap. To list them is to dissect a body and call the organs ‘inhabitants.’

But if you must have a census of this green, iron lung, here it is.

**The Moss-Wall.** It is not a colony, but a single, vast consciousness spread like a felted neural net across every brick and beam. It thinks in gradients of moisture and the faint, decaying currents of electricity that still wander the old wiring. Its thoughts are slow, photosynthetic, concerned with the distribution of humidity and the cultivation of rust, which it consumes as a mineral supplement. It patterns itself, sometimes, in branching, labyrinthine whorls that mimic the circuit diagrams of the world it has digested. It does not sleep. It pulses, softly, in time with the deep hum from the switchboxes, a green and amber meditation.

**The Root-Web.** Where the Moss-Wall is mind, the Root-Web is muscle and vein. A collective of a thousand ancient, thirsty systems—oak, strangler fig, rubber vine—their identities long since blurred into one fibrous, searching entity. It is purposeful, patient, and profoundly physical. Its work is the work of centuries: to gently disassemble concrete, to cradle and penetrate iron, to weave a stable floor from its own interlocking bodies. It feels the great piston’s *thunk* as a familiar, comforting heartbeat. It directs trickles of water, guides new tendrils into cracks warmed by residual heat, and occasionally, with the immense, silent force of growing wood, recalibrates the alignment of a pipe by a fraction of an inch. It is the Chamber’s sculptor and its skeleton.

**The Lichen-Sheath.** The diplomat. The hybrid. A crusty, black-and-orange symbiosis of fungus and algae that has learned to partner with a third kingdom: metal. It clothes the great piston, the valve stems, the exposed gears. Its role is regulation and translation. It insulates the hot iron, preventing scalding of the tender root-mat, and in return, it feeds on the metal’s slow oxidation and the constant moisture. It is a living heat-sink, a breathing corrosion. In places, it has grown into precise, perforated grids that seem to mimic radiator fins, a form both grown and engineered.

**The Conductor-Vines.** Thick, rubbery creepers with a deep, internal rhythm. They have not merely overgrown the electrical cables; they have fused with them. Their sap, rich with electrolytes, mingles with the fading current. They pulse, slowly and rhythmically, a bioluminescent throb visible beneath their thick skin when the amber dial-light hits them just so. They are the Chamber’s nervous system, conducting not pure data, but a low-frequency signal that is part directive, part nutrient flow. A message here is not a binary spike, but a wave of chemical encouragement that might take a day to travel from one end of the hall to the other: *More growth near the west wall. The piston is thirsty. The Moss-Wall reports a new seep.*

**The Glow-Fruits.** The children of the Conductor-Vines. Clusters of translucent, bulb-like pods that hang like forgotten lanterns from the old cables. They are batteries and beacons. They store the soft, bio-electric charge of the place, glowing with a cool, foxfire radiance that provides the only illumination in the deepest root-curtained alcoves. If one is gently plucked (a violent, almost unthinkable act here), it will continue to glow for weeks, its light fading as its stored life depletes. They are also seeds. When one falls, it does not rot; it embeds itself in the sodden mat and extends fine, wire-like filaments into the moisture, seeking a connection to the root-web or a bare, charged metal surface to begin the cycle anew.

**The Pulp-Grubs.** The only legacy of fauna. Blind, pallid, soft-bodied things the length of a finger, dwelling in the perpetually decaying sponge of the floor. They are not insects, nor worms, but something simpler—consumers of final decay. They turn the last of the rotted cellulose and the shed cells of the Moss-Wall into a richer, blacker mud. They have no eyes, only vibration sensors tuned to the steady rhythms of the Chamber, so they know to burrow deeper when the great piston fires. They are the gut flora of this body, essential, unnoticed, and utterly devoid of anything you would call a will.

Together, they are not a community. They are an organism. The Chamber does not *host* life; it *is* life, of a singular, composite kind. The breaths it takes are the sighs of steam from hot pipes meeting cold, wet air. Its thoughts are the patient, photosynthetic calculations of the Moss-Wall. Its movements are the imperceptible, yearly creep of the Root-Web. Its heartbeat is the *thunk-sssssh* of the sheathed piston.

To ask what lives here is to stand in a stomach and ask who lives inside. You are already in its belly, breathing its humid breath, and the soft, sucking sound under your feet is the sound of it, very slowly, digesting you too.
