To describe what lives here is to attempt taxonomy in a dream. The inhabitants of this blister-world are not discrete species, but rather shifting concentrations of intent within the saturated medium of heat, moisture, and soft physics. They are processes wearing temporary skins.

First, there are the **Wall-Grazers**. They are the most numerous, the background hum of existence. Imagine a carpet of iridescent, segmented lice, each no larger than a fingernail, but moving as a single, intelligent membrane. They cling to the glowing mineral crusts, not eating them, but *tuning* them. Their countless legs tap a silent, rapid rhythm against the pulsating crystals, seeming to coax the faint light into brighter, rhythmic flares. They are less animals and more like the nervous system of the stone itself, translating geothermal twitches into bioluminescent communication. When disturbed, the entire carpet might flow like mercury into a crack, vanishing in an instant, leaving behind only a dull patch on the wall.

From the ceiling hang the **Siphonophores**. These are the namesakes for the gelatinous sacks, but they are far more complex. Each sack is a colony, a single entity comprised of hundreds of specialized polyps. Some polyps are anchors, fibrous threads binding the whole to the obsidian ceiling. Others are stomachs, digesting the microscopic life that drifts in the convective currents. The most prominent are the lures: long, delicate tendrils that drip with a sticky, phosphorescent mucus. They do not move quickly. Their hunting strategy is geological patience. They glow with a seductive, deep blue, and the smaller, flutterer-fauna are drawn to them, becoming ensnared. The digestion is slow, taking days, during which the prey is absorbed alive, its final bioluminescent struggles adding to the Siphonophore’s radiant display.

Then there are the **Crawlers**, the clusters of jointed legs. A single Crawler is a sphere of perhaps twelve to twenty slender, chitinous limbs, interlocked at a central, invisible nexus. There is no head, no discernible front or back. It moves with a mesmerizing, chaotic coordination, each limb stepping independently yet harmoniously, allowing it to traverse any surface—wall, ceiling, pool-edge—with equal, skittering ease. They are scavengers and dismantlers. One might find a Crawler embracing the hand-axe of volcanic glass, its multitude of legs gently feeling the edge, testing the knapping, not to use it, but to understand its breakage patterns. They are curious in a way that feels deeply analytical, devoid of fear or aggression. They dismantle the dead, not by consumption, but by taking them apart into constituent pieces—this leg here, that sac of fluid there—leaving the components neatly arranged on a flat stone, as if conducting an autopsy for an unseen audience.

The true predators are the **Shimmers**. They are barely there. They exist as a distortion in the hot, wavering air, a lensing effect that blurs the glowing walls behind them. Only when they move quickly do they condense into visibility: a shimmering, ovoid form of compressed steam and reflective chitin, trailing tendrils of superheated moisture. They hunt the Siphonophore’s lures and the Wall-Grazer carpets. Their attack is a silent, violent change in pressure. They *collapse* onto their prey, and where they pass, the temperature briefly soars, flash-boiling the moisture on the rock and leaving behind a scorched, dry patch. What they consume is not flesh, but energy—the heat differential, the light, the kinetic potential. A successful hunt leaves the area momentarily cold and dark before the ambient geothermal warmth seeps back in.

And there are the **Amalgams**. These are the rarest and strangest. They are accidents, or perhaps experiments. When the reality breakdown is particularly acute, when a water drop hangs too long and time stutters, different life forms can… merge. A Crawler’s leg might phase through a Wall-Grazer, and they become a single, twitching hybrid. A Shimmer might get trapped in the viscous drip of a Siphonophore, resulting in a pulsing, hot-cold sac that hums and drifts aimlessly. Amalgams are unstable, often dissolving into a puddle of proto-organic sludge within minutes, which then is quickly absorbed by the weeping stone. But in their brief existence, they exhibit bizarre, unique behaviors—a Crawler-Amalgam might try to photosynthesize using its stolen glow, a Shimmer-Amalgam might become solid and weep cold tears.

This ecology operates in utter silence, but it is orchestrated. The primitive tools—the hand-axe, the grooved bones—are not forgotten. They are **in use**. But not by any one creature. They are communal totems, focal points. One might observe, over the course of hours, a Crawler approach the axe and adjust its angle by a fraction. Later, a Shimmer might hover over it, heating the glass until it emits a low, infrared glow. Later still, a carpet of Wall-Grazers might flow over it, their tapping legs seemingly recording its shape into the collective memory of the mineral crust. The bones are used as tuning forks—struck (by a careful limb-tap from a Crawler) against specific resonances in the rock, producing subsonic vibrations that seem to calm or excite the fauna. This is not tool-use for survival, but for something else: for ritual, for communication with the pocket itself, for gently probing the soft walls of their reality.

What lives here, ultimately, is **the pocket**. The individual forms are merely its thoughts, fleeting and mutable. The blister of stone, with its weeping walls and non-Euclidean geometry, is a conscious, dreaming entity. The fauna are the dream. They are born from the interaction of heat and water and strange physics, they play out their roles in a silent, teeming ballet of predation and curiosity, and they dissolve back into the stone, their experiences, their learned adjustments to the hand-axe, their deaths, all feeding back into the geothermal mind. To enter this place is to step into a living brain, one that thinks not in words, but in pulses of light, shifts of pressure, and the endless, silent creep of water defying gravity.
