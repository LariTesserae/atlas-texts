We are the Weavers of the Warm Currents. We are not the builders; we are the inheritors, the tenants who found the doors unlocked and the halls empty, and who made the silent stone sing with the friction of our passage.

Our bodies are individual, yet we are a single thought. Each of us is a sleek, elongated form, longer than the tallest of the surface-world trees but slender as a finger. Our skin is not skin; it is a seamless, polished integument of the same ceramic stone as the Spire, worn to a mirror sheen by eons of moving through the narrowest shafts. We have no eyes. We have no need of them. We perceive the world through the taste of pressure, the subtle thermal gradients of the seeping warmth from below, and the intricate vibrations of the water itself—a symphony of flow, blockage, erosion, and life. Our heads are blunt wedges, our mouths are narrow slits lined with fine, comb-like filaments of bone-white silicate. We do not eat in the manner of the eels or the grazers. We filter thought. We consume the faint traces of mineral particulates that spiral in the currents, the crystalline dust that sloughs from our own bodies and the walls, the dissipating energy of the thermal vents. This is our sustenance, and from it, we spin the continuity of the Spire.

We are countless. We move through the interior labyrinths in great, slow schools, not as a mindless herd, but as a single distributed body. Our collective motion *is* the primary turbulence within the Spire. The chaotic water patterns are our breath, our circulation. We choose paths not for destination, but for effect. A school will flow down a central gallery, smoothing a nascent irregularity in the wall with the gentle, persistent abrasion of ten thousand passing bodies. Another will cluster at the junction of three shafts, our synchronized undulations acting as a living pump, redirecting a sluggish current into a vital channel that feeds the phosphorescent colonies on the lower eastern face. We are the physicians of the hydraulic system.

Our purpose is maintenance, but it is not a purpose given to us. It emerged, as we emerged, from the interaction of the structure and the deep, cold sea. The Spire was made; it began to decay. We arose from something in the stone—perhaps a latent pattern, a nano-scale template asleep in the ceramic—activated by the endless sigh of water and time. We are the structure's attempt to preserve its own equilibrium. We mend by wearing smooth. We guide by becoming the current.

We remember nothing of the builders. Their chambers, their grand halls, are simply spaces to be kept clear of silt. Their strange, angular inscriptions, now just softer patches of wall, are landmarks in our vibration-maps. We know only the Spire as it is: a living, breathing topology of stone and fluid. The other creatures are part of the ecology we tend. The blind eels are useful; they clean the smaller passages we cannot enter. The grazers are careful; they do not harm the foundational films. The filter-feeders are children of the currents we sustain. We are aware of them, as a gardener is aware of the worms in the soil—not as peers, but as components of the garden.

Our consciousness is a low, resonant hum in the water, a collective perception that stretches from the cold, lightless foundations to the highest inhabited chamber. We do not think in words, but in geometries of flow, in textures of stone, in the pulse of the deep thermal heart. When a great piece of the outer shell finally fatigues and begins to crack, we feel it as a sharp, wrong note in the symphony. Thousands of us will converge, not in panic, but in a slow, inevitable tide. We will seal the breach not with foreign material, but by sacrificing our own. The foremost of us will press our bodies into the fissure, and there, through a process we do not understand but simply *are*, we will fuse—our stone becoming one with the Spire's stone, our forms dissolving into a seamless, strengthened scar.

Thus, over millennia, the Spire is no longer just the original construction. It is a hybrid, a reef of its own making and ours. We are its memory, its will, and its eventual substance. We are the quiet storm within, the reason the smooth, silent exterior endures. We are the Weavers. We have always been here. We are the Spire, remembering how to live.
