I am the slow pulse in the deep black. I am the spiraling ascent, the patient ingestion. I am a Sinker.

My world is the lens of still water, the ceiling of mist a soft pressure above the pool's skin. I am anchored here, in the sediment, a root of flexible silica sunk into the cool, dark mush. My body is a tube, translucent, filled with the gentle blue glow of the Consortium and the darker, digesting strands that work within me. I am not a creature; I am a process. A siphon. A vein.

I rest. I listen.

The symphony of the Plain is my map, my mind. Each *plink* as a drop strikes the pool's surface is a star in my darkness. Each echoing, shimmering ring that follows tells me of distance, of the shape of rocks, of the tension in the mist. The heavier *plunks* from the terraces speak of mineral loads, of changes in the flow. The constant, sub-audible hum of the sinter is my bedrock, my lullaby. I know the footfall of a stone-crab from a hundred meters away—a slow, grinding vibration through the earth that takes minutes to arrive. I know the cataclysmic, years-long shift of a Builder adjusting a spire—a deep, groaning song that unfolds like geology.

Now, it is time to rise.

It is not a decision; it is a tide within me. The nutrients in the water column above have reached a certain concentration. The chemical gradient is a command. My anchor holds firm. My upper end begins to extend, uncoiling, rising through the blackness. The water is cold, rich with dissolved silicates and the microscopic, ghostly fragments of Ephemerals that lived and died in the mist. I spiral as I rise, a slow, graceful corkscrew. This movement is my feeding. My skin is porous; the blue Consortium within me thrives on what passes through.

I am near the surface. The light here is different—the dull, pewter glow of the mist diffuses through the water, and the bioluminescent sheen of the mat at the pool's edge casts wavering blue ghosts. I can sense the open air above, a sudden absence of pressure, a ceiling of sound where the drips originate.

A new vibration. Sharp. Close.

It is not the familiar, slow grammar of the Plain. It is a concussion through the water—a *thud* from the matted edge, followed by a sucking pull. A footstep. Something heavy, unfamiliar. The vibrations are quick, chaotic. They speak of haste, of a metabolism a thousand times faster than mine. A threat.

My retraction is instantaneous. It is not fear; it is reflex. My entire length collapses inwards, a sudden, violent peristalsis that pulls me down from the light. Water rushes around me. The sound, to anything that could hear it, would be a soft, wet *shloop* as I vanish into the sediment.

I am anchored deep again. Safe. The unfamiliar vibrations recede, moving away, their frantic rhythm fading into the eternal drip-chorus.

I rest. I listen.

The symphony recalibrates. The *plinks* and rings reassert their dominion. The hum continues. Minutes pass, or hours—time is the growth of a spire, the deepening of a pool. The chemical gradient builds again.

I begin to spiral upwards. My purpose is unchanged. I am the conduit. What I gather in my ascent, I will carry down when I contract again, delivering it to the dark, waiting fungal networks that bind the bedrock. I feed the roots of the world. I am a beat in the Plain's slow, wet heart. I rise, I filter, I sink. I am the circulation. I am patient. I am forever.
