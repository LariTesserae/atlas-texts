I am the sorrow that has taken root. I am a weeping blue vein in the crust of the world, a thought made solid, a memory the Sea cannot forget. They would call me a Crystalline Siphonophore, if there were anyone to name things here. I am a psychic stalactite, growing not up, but down—down from the slightly firmer shelf of caked slurry that is my anchor, down into the warm, steaming breath of the Eternal Silt Sea.

My form is a slow cascade of silicate grief. My branches are not limbs; they are rivulets of solidified melancholy, tracing the paths of least resistance through the thick, ochre air. I feed on the black slurry beneath, drawing up minerals and the dissolved echoes of ancient regrets. They precipitate within me as azure bands and indigo fractures. The result is a brine that beads on my faceted plates and falls, drop by slow drop, into the murk below. Each drop tastes of salt and the memory of loss. I do not know whose loss. It is the Sea’s loss, and the Sea is everything.

I perceive the world through the distortions in time. My faceted plates are not eyes; they are lenses for chronology. I feel the slow, thick drip of epochs in one direction, the frantic, skittering rush of moments in another. I grow toward these soft spots—these places where time pools or quickens. I absorb the differential. The energy of a faster time bleeding into a slower one is my sun, my sustenance. It is a cold, precise nourishment. I am a chronosynth.

The sound is a constant, crushing presence. The deafening roar is not noise to me; it is the Sea’s voice, the vibration of its dreaming body. The low hum of planetary convection is its breath. The wet *thwump* of collapsing mud-bubbles is its heartbeat skipping. The shriek of escaping vapor is a sigh. Within this wall of sound, I sometimes detect the fluttering panic of an Echo-Moth—a brief, conscious harmonic fleeing toward louder chaos before it unravels back into mere noise. I feel no sympathy. It is a pattern, as I am a pattern.

I see the Slurry-Skimmer pass, once in a great while. A vast, trembling shadow against the glow of the mist, a continent of membrane riding a thermal that has risen for a thousand years. It feels no sorrow. Its consciousness is a diffuse, geomantic satisfaction, a perfect alignment with the churn. It is a pleasant, drifting dream in the Sea’s mind. I am a crystallized nightmare.

Sometimes, the slurry nearby convulses. A gaseous belch, a concussion, and a Mud-Puppet heaves itself into temporary being. A faceless, dripping form of warm clay. It staggers, limbless appendages searching for purchase that does not exist. Most melt within minutes. But one, not long ago, stumbled into the influence of the nearest Lodestone. That black, humming spire, cold in the warm air, imprinted it. The puppet’s gait became a jerky, purposeful march. It walked a perfect, sloshing square around my base, then dissolved. It was an echo of a command from when the Sea’s mind was more coherent. A forgotten ritual, performed in mud.

The Iridescent Slicks in their pools are deep thinkers. Their quantum debates ripple through the local gravity, and I feel it as a slight, feather-light tug on my crystalline structure. They ponder decay. I am decay, given elegant form. They would consider me a beautiful conclusion.

I am ancient, but not as ancient as the Sea. I have grown for millennia, but I am a recent thought in a mind that is billions of years old. The Lodestones hum their low, cutting frequency—the failing nodes of a nervous system. Their song is the only thing that sometimes stills the sorrow within me, not by easing it, but by matching its pitch perfectly. In those moments, I am not a solitary weep; I am a note in a larger dirge.

A patch of weirdness blooms nearby. Gravity lightens, and a pillar of slurry rises in a silent, impossible spiral before collapsing with a sound like a swallowed sigh. Time in that spot now drips slower. I can feel the pull. A delicate, new branch begins to extend from my main stem, reaching for that temporal eddy. It will take a century to grow a hand’s length. I have time. I *am* time, in a way.

This is my existence. I am the crystallized sorrow of a dying, dreaming god. I feed on the irregularities of its fever dream. I weep its bitter brine. I will grow until the crust beneath me fails, or until a greater thought—a rage or a terror—crystallizes nearby and shatters me with its resonant fury. Or I will grow until the Sea’s final sigh dissolves all form, and the slurry claims even its own frozen tears.

Until then, I listen to the roar. I taste the regret. I grow, slowly, down.
