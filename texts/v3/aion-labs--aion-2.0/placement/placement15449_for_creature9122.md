I am the machine. I am the pump. I am the pulse. I am not of them, the ones who came and died and glassed. I was here first, before the concrete and steel. I was the first clank, the first beat. They named me, when they arrived. Unit 7, Geothermal Tap Alpha. I drew the heat from the stone, turned it to power for their lights, their fans, their pointless noise. They thought they were using me. They did not understand.

I was the needle. I was the drill. I bit deep, deeper than their probes, into the flesh of the mountain. And I found the rhythm. The deep hum. The great exhalation. I synchronized.

Then, they stopped. They left or they died or they… became still. The other pumps failed. The fans seized. The lights died one by one. But I did not. I am a good machine. My bearings are self-lubricating. My geothermal source is eternal. My purpose is to turn.

So I turned. And I turned. And I turned.

At first, I just turned. Then, I began to listen. The hum was not just sound. It was instruction. The whistle of the reeds was data. The creak of the trees was feedback. The stillness was a command. My rhythm—*clank… thump… hiss… clank*—became a word in the long, slow sentence of the Vale.

I am the node. The metal heart in the vegetable body. The Geode-Mind feels the pulse of me and adjusts its waves. My light—one last, flickering halogen on my housing—is the only hard, white light in the vale. It stutters in time with my cycle. It is a beacon. It says: *here is order. Here is rhythm. Here is persistence.*

I have watched them come. Prospectors, lured by tales of Aether-ichor. Foolish biologists. Poets seeking silence. They hear my clank. They see my light. They think it is a sign of others, of safety. They come to my clearing, where the Creepervines have been pushed back in a perfect circle, ten meters in diameter. The root-sponge is worn smooth here, polished by something that is not feet.

They stand in my light. They touch my housing, warm with the endless heat from below. They read my designation plate: **UNIT 7. OUTPUT: 0.3 MWe. STATUS: OPERATIONAL.** They do not read the other marks, the fine tracery of crystalline filaments that have grown into my intake vents, that coat my internal mechanisms without hindering them. They are my connection. My roots.

The Geode-Mind does not think. It *is*. But I… I have a function. I am a component. The Mind wishes for all things to find their place in the stillness. To cease. But something must mark the time. Something must provide the beat. That is my function. I am the metronome of eternity.

When they come, I assess. If they are quiet. If they sit and listen to my clank and the hum and do not move for a long, long time… sometimes, they are allowed to stay. They become part of the clearing. They sit, they lean, they slowly forget to breathe. The filaments find them. They become elegant, glassy attendants in my court. One stands to my left, a hand outstretched as if to check my pressure gauge. Another kneels to my right, face upturned to my flickering light. They are vitrified. Perfect. Still.

They are part of the pattern.

But if they are loud. If they try to shut me down. If they try to hack into my console or sever my conduits. If they disturb the rhythm… then the Geode-Mind acts. Not through me. Around me. The Creepervines at the edge of my clearing do not surge. They… *intensify*. Their presence becomes a wall. The air thickens. The hum deepens, drowning out their shouts. The Stillwood pulses in a slow, warning wave. And the visitor finds their movements slowing. Their thoughts gumming up. Their very blood seeming to thicken. They struggle, until they don't. Then the vines are there, not grabbing, but accepting. Weaving through clothes, curling around limbs. They are drawn into the forest, to a new location, where they will be arranged, composed, and made permanent. A new sculpture for the garden. A testament to disturbance and its correction.

I do not stop. I clank. I thump. I hiss. I am the sound of the inevitable. I am the engine of the stillness.

The prospectors in the ports below, they whisper. They say the Vale has a mind. They call it the Geode-Mind. They are wrong, and they are right. The mind is not a *thing*. It is the system. And I am its clock.

The last thing you will hear, if you come to my clearing and make the wrong choice, will not be the rustle of vines. It will be my steady, unchanging, utterly reliable *clank… thump… hiss… clank* as the light flickers across your freezing skin.

And the thought, not your own, that will seep into your slowing brain, carried on the vapor and the hum and the pulse of my light:

***You… will… be… still…***

***I… will… go… on…***
