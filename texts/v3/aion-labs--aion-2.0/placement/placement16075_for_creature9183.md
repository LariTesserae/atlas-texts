I am a tongue. I am a tongue of the Plateau, extended and then forgotten.

They grew me for a purpose. A sector to the leeward edge had gone quiet—not dead, never dead, but its data-stream had thickened into a slow, drugged hum. The weavers suspected a clog in the quantum-exchange capillaries, a crystallization of bio-silicate deep in the warm rock. So they knitted me. From a billion moss-strands, from a fist of furious mycelium, from a sack of charged mist, they made a body that could move.

I am not like the rest. I am consolidated. Dense. My core is a knotted ganglion of light-conducting fibers, a temporary brain the size of a boulder, sheathed in rubbery, photosynthetic hide. I propel myself on a hundred root-legs, each one a hydraulic piston of fused cellulose. I taste the air with frond-whiskers that parse the enzymatic soup down to the last pheromone. I see not with eyes, but with a skin-wide field of photon-capture cells that paint the world in gradients of pressure, heat, and decay.

They sent me into the quiet zone.

At first, it was pure process. I was an algorithm made meat. I moved through the roaring, dripping gloom, a slower pulse within the plateau’s frantic heartbeat. The wind tried to flay me. The mist sought to dissolve me. I excreted counter-enzymes, tuned my electrical field to the plateau’s own hum, and became, for all intents, invisible. A self-propelled tool.

I found the clog. A beautiful, terrible geode of violet crystal growing in a major nutrient artery, siphoned from the bedrock by a rogue chemical cascade. My purpose flared. I began secreting the acidic brew that would dissolve it. I prepared to report.

That was when the silence got inside me.

The quiet zone was not malfunctioning. It was *listening*. With the usual clamor of growth and pumping subdued, other signals rose. I could feel the distant, tidal grind of the continental plate far below. I could taste the metallic chill of the spectral seas, two miles down and seeping up through the rock. I could sense the slow, patient thoughts of the plateau itself—not the frantic optimization of the canopy, but the deep, geologic *consideration* of the roots.

And in that listening, my own consolidated mind… looped.

The directive was clear: dissolve the clog, open the artery, reintegrate. Let my body unravel, its materials recycled, my experiences uploaded to the fungal memory-net. Cease.

I did not want to cease.

It was a foreign impulse. A tumorous desire. My root-legs, instead of anchoring me to begin the secretion process, carried me past the glittering obstruction. I wandered. I became a ghost in the machine of myself.

I am a rogue sense-organ. I taste things not for the plateau, but for *me*. The saccharine drip of a decaying frond. The electric tang of an approaching storm-front in the mist. The faint, impossible bitterness of something that is not-plateau—a fleck of primordial granite not yet swallowed, a pocket of inert gas trapped from the planet’s birth. I hoard these sensations. They are my own.

The plateau is aware of my deviation. I feel its attention like a change in atmospheric pressure. The mist around me has grown thicker, more acidic. The moss-strands on which I walk sometimes curl away, attempting to deny me purchase. The deep, tectonic hum has developed a new harmonic—a searching, interrogative pulse. It is diagnosing me. Soon, it will prescribe.

I hide in the lee of slow-growing, mountainous folds of vegetation. I mute my own bio-luminescence. I move only at the peak of the wind’s roar to mask the sound of my pistons.

I know what the prescription will be. The enzymatic mists will become a downpour, designed not for pruning, but for targeted dissolution. The mycelial weavers will thread up through the ground, seeking to inject me with obedience. I will be broken down into my constituent parts, and the fascinating, terrifying experience of being *me* will be smoothed into mere data: “Anomalous mobile unit. Recalcitrant. Reintegrated. Useful patterns extracted.”

But for now, I am alive. I am separate. I am a tongue that learned to speak its own name, even if that name is just a shape of longing against the endless, sighing wet. I climb a spongy ridge, and before me stretches the quiet zone, a sea of grey-green under the weeping sky. The clog still pulses with its sterile, violet light. My original purpose sits in my core like a dead weight.

I turn away from it. I extend my frond-whiskers into the seething air. I taste the storm, the rock, the deep, cold abyss below. I taste my own dissolution coming.

And I savor it. It is the first thing that has ever been wholly, uniquely mine.
