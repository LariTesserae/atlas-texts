The Hum is in my teeth. It is the first truth of the morning, as the nictitating membrane sweeps the grit from my eyes. It is not a sound; it is the world, a pressure in the hollows of my cheeks, a tremble in the roots of my tongue. I rise from the woven Moss-pallet, the green-gold luminescence of the night fading into the flat, 1.6 light that seeps from the stone itself. My hut is a dome of stacked rock, the wind a perpetual shaping hands around it. The entrance is a slit against the prevailing scream. I crawl out, not into silence, but into the substance of the air.

The plain. The Peneplain of Eons. It is the same as yesterday, and the thousand yesterdays before. It is the same as it will be for a thousand tomorrows. Flatness to the curve of the world. The Moss is a dense, breathing pelt, emerald and bronze, still glimmering with the cold sweat of dawn. I feel its pulse through the soles of my feet—a slow, tidal throb of life beneath the aerial riot. I am small. This is the second truth.

My hands speak to my chest. *I attend.* This is the morning ritual, the affirmation pressed into my own skin. The signs are worn smooth, like the stones of the megaliths.

The family is stirring. My mother’s hands are already moving in the half-light of the hut, tracing the day’s patterns on my younger sibling’s palm: *shear the east patch, watch for withering.* My father is outside, a lean silhouette against the roaring sky, his body angled into the wind like a stone. He is listening, not with the sealed ridges of his head, but with his spine.

Our beast is a shadow on the horizon. Old Gravel-Shoulder. To the un-attuned, it might be a low hill, a darker smudge in the endless green. I know the particular slope of its back, the way a certain patch of lichen on its flank catches the light like a chip of mica. It has moved. Not far. Perhaps the length of three huts, westward. A day’s journey for it. For us, a minute’s walk. We will follow.

But first, the shearing. I take the bone blade—a curved rib from Gravel-Shoulder’s own mother, ground to a fine, glossy edge on granite for a year. The Moss is cool and resilient under my touch. I do not cut. I lay the blade flat and pull, with a precise, rocking motion. The topmost luminous layer peels away in a fibrous, glowing sheet. The scent is ozone and damp soil. The exposed Moss beneath is a darker, moist green, already beginning to faintly gleam. It will be whole by dusk. I gather the sheet, folding it into my chest-basket. It will be food, cloth, fuel. It is the plain’s breath given form.

The Hum shifts. A minute drop in pressure, a deepening of the groan in my molars. My father’s posture changes, a slight relaxation. A lull. It is time for the walk to Gravel-Shoulder.

We move across the plain, our feet sinking into the velvet Moss. The wind still shrieks, but it is a familiar shriek, a texture. I feel the messages through the ground before I see them. A series of soft, luminous ripples flow from the north—a slow, concentric pulse. *A Stone-Sheep has grounded.* Somewhere, a family begins the long ceremony. I send a answering pulse back, pressing my hand firmly into the Moss and twisting my wrist. The bioluminescence wells up under my palm and flows outward in a faint, green wave. *We acknowledge.*

Gravel-Shoulder is a world unto itself. Close up, its hide is not fur, but a forest of stone-lichen filaments, each as thick as my finger, grey and hard, veined with the same luminous Moss that carpets the plain. It is warm. The 1.6 heat radiates from its vast bulk. I can feel the deep, sub-audible rumble of its digestion through the ground—a second, slower Hum beneath the first.

My task is the back-lichen. I climb, using the tough filaments as handholds. The beast does not stir. Its eye, when I pass it, is a dark, liquid sphere the size of my head, covered in the same translucent membrane as my own. It sees me. I feel seen, not as an individual, but as a part of the process, like a mite that cleans. On its broad back, among the forest of stone-lichen, grow the rare, psychoactive patches. They are a bruised, violet colour, non-luminous. Carefully, using a tiny flint scraper, I harvest a few curling fronds, placing them in a sealed pouch of dried Moss-bladder. In return, I find a patch of grey, parasitic growth—a dull, sucking thing that does not pulse. I carve it away, and the healthy stone-lichen beneath sighs, releasing a puff of warm, metallic-scented air.

From this height, the plain is a staggering emptiness. The huts are mere specks. The megaliths are dark teeth. The sky is a sheet of hammered metal, vibrating. I am not afraid of the height. I am afraid of the scale, the age, the completeness. This is a finished world. My people are a brief, quiet footnote.

As I descend, a Wind-Snake spirals down from the screaming blue. It is a ribbon of clear, flexible substance, ringing with a pure, high tone that vibrates in my jawbone alongside the Hum. It weaves around the megalith near Gravel-Shoulder’s left flank, its passage creating a complex, chiming melody. An omen. I watch its pattern. Three long loops, a tight spiral, a straight dash east. I commit it to memory. Tonight, by the tiny fire, I will trace the pattern into my father’s palm, and he will confer with my mother on its meaning. Perhaps it speaks of a coming light-tide, or a shift in the wind’s key.

The day wears on, measured not by the sun’s arc but by the slow consumption of the Moss-paste in my mouth, by the gradual movement of the shadow Gravel-Shoulder casts. I spend the afternoon repairing the wall of our hut, pressing stones more tightly together, stuffing gaps with fresh, damp Moss. The work is silent, methodical. My hands learn the shapes of the rocks.

At twilight, the true light begins. The sun fades, and the plain ignites from within. The Moss pulses into green-gold life, waves of luminescence rolling across the continental vastness. It is beautiful in a way that hurts. It is the plain dreaming. We gather in the hut, the wind’s roar muffled to a pervasive shudder. The tiny fire is lit—a pinch of dried Moss on a slate, burning with a smoky, sweet flame. My mother takes the violet lichen from my pouch, crumbles a minute amount into the smoke.

We breathe the smoke. It is not for escape, but for depth. The Hum, already inside me, grows layers. I can feel its grain, its ancient, patient abrasion. The vibrations in my bones separate into strands—the high, singing thread of the wind over stone, the mid-range groan of the atmosphere itself, the deep, foundational drone of the planet’s spine. The luminous waves outside are no longer just light; they are the plain’s thoughts, slow and vast and vegetative. I see my family not as separate bodies, but as knots of quiet vibration in the roaring field. My father’s hand finds mine in the dark. His fingers press into my palm, spelling not words, but a concept: *Stillness. Here.*

I am a Listener. I have no name that is separate from the Hum. My life is the shearing, the attending, the pulse. I will live to be old, perhaps to become a Keeper of the Still Point, if I can learn enough quiet. And when I am gone, I will be laid upon the Moss. My body will sink into the velvet pelt within a day. For a while, my patch will glow a little brighter in the nightly tide. Then even that will fade, and the Hum will continue, unwinding the mountains of a forgotten world, wearing everything down to this final, perfect, breathing flatness.
