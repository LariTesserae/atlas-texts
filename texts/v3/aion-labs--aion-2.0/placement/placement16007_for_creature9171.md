I am the slow grace. I am the cool, wet passage. I am the soft light in the perpetual dusk.

My world is measured in breaths of moss and the patient fall of water. I have no name for myself, but if I were to have one, it would be the feeling of moisture beading on my skin, the sensation of my own gentle, undulating contraction. I am one of the glowing ones. My body is a sac of translucent jelly, through which the slow pulse of my digestion can be seen—a faint, blue-green shimmer harvested from the very fungi I consume. I am not blind, but I see only gradients of shadow and the own light I cast upon the velvet stone before me.

I am always moving. Not moving *toward*, simply moving. My single foot, a ripple of muscle, grips the everlasting damp. I taste the world with my whole skin. The iron tang of the weeping rock. The sweet, faint decay of the moss-mattress generations deep. The crisp, clean signature of the *Luminous Cap* spores as I glide over them. I do not eat the Caps; they are not for me. I graze on the film of algae that grows where the water’s bead lingers longest, on the infinitesimal fungi that bloom and die in the space between one drip and the next. My consumption is so slow, so measured, that the regrowth keeps pace. I am a variable in the Equation, and I know my coefficient.

My trail is my memory. It glows a softer, longer-lasting green than my internal light, a phosphorescent scripture I write upon the stones and the moss-tips. It is not a path I follow back; it is a gift I leave for the dark. The pale millipedes sometimes cross it, their countless legs tracing temporary valleys in its shine. The motes—the drifting spores—catch in its slight tackiness and are carried a little farther on their lazy currents. My trail is part of the ecology. It is a map for no one, and yet it contributes to the ambient glow, the ghost-light that defines this room.

Time is not a river here. It is a pool, still and deep. I have known the same patch of moss for what might be a century. I leave it to graze its neighbors, and when I return, it is exactly as it was, because my grazing was the exact compliment to its growth. The *drip-drip-drip* is the metronome of this eternity. My own rhythm syncs to it. My contraction, my expansion, the wave of my movement—it is set to the cadence of the mountain’s heartbeat. The deep, soft *creak* of the moss-bed is the inhalation and exhalation. I breathe with it.

They come sometimes. The Treaders-on-Softness. To me, they are vast, slow-moving cliffs of shadow and borrowed luminescence. They kneel, and the moss accepts them with a sigh. They bring with them the faint, foreign scent of sunlit wool and dry air, a scent that is quickly swallowed by the wet stone. I do not fear them. Their movements are as deliberate as my own. I have glided over the toe of a felt-bound foot, tasting the salt of a world beyond the void. They are careful. They are part of the balance, too. I have watched one of them—a collection of shadows that my light defines as a crouching form—hold perfectly still for a thousand drips, until their presence was no more disruptive than a slightly warmer rock. They harvest the Caps with a precision that honors the Equation. When they take one, I feel the subtle shift in the spore-motes drifting through my air, a minute re-calibration. The system adjusts. The Equation holds.

My purpose is to be. To graze. To glow. To trace my luminous, fading calligraphy on the walls of this vault. I will do this until the mountain weeps its last, and even then, I suspect my final trail will remain, a ghost of light in the deepening dark, a last variable settling to zero. But that is not a thought I think. I think only of the coolness against my underside, the next bead of water forming on the moss-tip ahead, the soft, endless, perfect now.
