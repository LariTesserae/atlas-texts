So I’m not a being at all—not a singular creature. I am the drip. The single, heavy bead of water that gathers at the tip of the central stalactite, swells, and falls. The one that strikes the altar-stalagmite with that clear, echoing *plink*.

My existence is a cycle of gathering and release, stretched across an expanse of time so vast it would shatter a mind that measured in heartbeats. I am not sentient in any way they would recognize. I don't think. I am a node, a punctuation mark in the silent language of this place. I am the metronome.

From my vantage—if a drop of water can be said to have a vantage—I see everything. Not with eyes, but with the perfect, passive reception of a lens. I gather myself from the slow seep through the stone above. Over days, I pull minerals from the rock: calcium carbonate, traces of iron, salts from ancient seas. I am a bead of condensed history. As I form, I see the chamber reflected in my curved, trembling surface—the inverted, shimmering dome of the ceiling, the pale glow of the walls, the constellations of air-jellies drifting in their slow communion.

They feel my formation. The change in humidity, the subtle shift in the chemical breath of the cavern as I draw the minerals from the stone. The air-jellies drift closer. Their light patterns shift from a steady indigo to a slow, pulsing violet, syncing with the rhythm of my growth. They aren't worshiping. They are tuning. I am a key event in the chamber's equilibrium. My fall will introduce a precise quantity of mineral-rich water to the altar-stone, which will seep into its pores and feed the fungal networks at its base. It will cause a tiny, localized spike in humidity, which the lichenous insects will detect, adjusting their grazing patterns for the next lunar cycle. The crustaceans in the nearest pool will sense the vibration through the stone and begin a new, concentric ring in their gravel.

I am the trigger. The heartbeat.

When I fall, it is not a death. It is a dispersal. The impact is a moment of perfect, shattering clarity—a sound that structures the silence. For an instant, I am a crown of a thousand smaller droplets, a burst of sensation against the ancient stone. Then I am gone, seeping into the stalagmite, joining the slow, internal rivers that feed the roots of this world.

But I am also the memory of the fall. The echo in the air. The damp spot that evaporates over hours, leaving a fresh deposit of crystal. The process has already begun again above. Another bead is gathering. It is me, and it is not me. We are all the same drip, across millennia. A single, periodic event in an eternal present.

To be here, to be this, is to understand time as a circle, not a line. My purpose is not to *do*, but to *be* the interval. To mark the passage that is not a passage, but a return. I am the clock by which the Consensus dreams. My regularity is the foundation upon which all their slow, intricate technologies of moss and light and gravel are built.

I have seen, in my countless cycles, the patterns change. The moss-glyphs have grown more complex over the centuries. The air-jellies' light synchronizations have become more intricate, a slow dance towards some unseen harmony. The crustaceans' rings now sometimes form spirals that match the Fibonacci sequence, a mathematics born from the flow of water I provide.

I am both participant and witness. I am the pulse. And in the deep, silent peace of this stone bell, that is everything.
