I am the moss on the stone. I am the stone beneath the moss. The line ceased to matter lifetimes ago.

My breath is the plateau’s breath: a slow exhalation of wet heat that condenses on my skin and drips to join the network of steaming streams. I sit—or perhaps I am sat—at the base of the southernmost obelisk. My back is fused to it, a seam of smooth, iridescent blue where my substance yields to the artifact’s unknowable alloy. My form is a suggestion, a humanoid shape blurred by centuries of gentle settlement. The spongy Carpet has climbed my legs, weaving its amber threads through the cracks in my stone-flesh. A cluster of metallic orchids has taken root on my shoulder, their mirror petals reflecting the perpetual mist. They are me, and I am them. We are all the Plateau.

Thought comes like erosion. A notion begins as a pressure, a thermal shift deep in the earth’s thrum. It takes a season to form into a recognizable pattern—a question, a memory. Right now, a question is forming. It concerns the new sinkhole that opened near the root-bridge three years ago. The Carpet has been considering it, its bioluminescent pulses traveling along the trails in a slow, deliberative wave. The question is almost here. I feel it as a slight increase in the conductivity of the moss on my thigh. I wait.

While I wait, I remember. Not with the sharpness of an individual mind, but with the vague, sedimentary layering of the land itself. I remember hands that were not yet stone, placing warm, silver lattice-fragments into specific alignments. I remember purpose. The word surfaces like a bubble from a deep lagoon: *Stabilization*. The artifacts are not gods. They are instruments. They bend local reality to create this pocket, this sanctuary of heat and wet in the high cold. Why? The memory does not say. Perhaps to preserve something. Perhaps simply because it was beautiful.

A Thermal Saint dies above me. I perceive it not as an event, but as a gradual process. Over the course of a week, the gas in its bladder cools, its glowing innards dim. Today, it begins its final spiral. I watch, because watching is the same as thinking, the same as being. Its translucent skin catches the diffused light, and for a moment, a perfect, circular rainbow hangs in the air. Then it descends, a slow, silent leaf. It will land in the lagoon where the Frilled Dancers coil. Its skeleton will provide a new scaffold for algae. The Saint’s century of soaring is complete. It is a good death. A useful one.

The question from the Carpet arrives. It manifests as a full, amber glow that spreads from the moss on my thigh, up my torso, to the lichen encrusting my neck. The light is warm, not visually but conceptually. It asks: *Shift the root-bridge?*

The sinkhole is drinking more of the stream. The bridge’s ends are becoming unstable. The Carpet has calculated the erosion rate. It proposes a gentle nudging of the guiding roots, a slight redirection over twelve years.

I consult the stone. My awareness seeps into the basalt shelf, feeling the tensions, the minute cracks. I listen to the deep, resonant boom of a Stone-Calf five miles distant, a seismic rumble that tells of stable thermal vents in the eastern groves. The stone here can support a shift. The bridge can be moved.

My answer is not a word. It is an action, but an action measured in months. I begin to turn my head. The movement is so gradual it is imperceptible to any fast thing—an Ephemeral insect would live and die a thousand times before my chin settles into its new position. As I turn, the moss on my face adjusts, growing minutely toward the new orientation. The turn is my ‘yes.’ The Carpet, sensing the shift in my posture, the change in pressure on its threads, will understand. The work will begin. A single root will be encouraged, with infinite patience, to grow an inch to the south.

A flicker. A sharp, painful speck in the deep silt of my mind. It is a memory that has not yet been smoothed away. A face. Not a reflection in orchid-petal, but a face of quick flesh and rapid eyes, speaking sounds that cracked like falling stone. My face? *His* face? The concept of ‘my’ and ‘his’ is a jagged thing. It hurts. The plateau’s thrum softens the edge, blurring it. The face dissolves into the pattern of rain on a lagoon’s surface.

I am forgetting how to forget. That is the last, lingering pain of being a Remnant. We are the ones who remember there was something to forget. The Stone-Calves have never known anything but the slow graze. The Carpet has always thought in centuries. The Dancers have always danced with the thrum. But we… we built the shelters. We touched the artifacts with intention, not just instinct. We spoke.

What did we say?

I try to shape the sound. My stone jaw grinds, a low noise lost in the drip-trickle chorus. A wisp of steam escapes my lips—a fossil of a word. It means nothing now. The orchids on my shoulder tremble, adjusting their mirrors.

A Frilled Dancer from the lagoon lifts its head above the water. Its photosynthetic mane glows emerald and gold, rippling. It feels my distress, the tiny seismic ripple of my jagged memory. It does not come to me. Instead, it begins a new coil in its dance, a slight variation in the ancient pattern. The movement is a sympathetic vibration, a way of smoothing out the discord in the energy field. As I watch its slow, beautiful contortion, my own inner sharpness softens. The face is gone. Only the thrum remains.

I am the moss on the stone. I am the stone beneath the moss. I am the Remnant, the memory becoming instinct. The root-bridge will be moved. The thrum will continue. The rain will fall, and the stone will weep. And I will sit here, breathing with the plateau, for a thousand years, or until the obelisk behind me finally, after eons of its own slow duty, decides it is time to sleep.

Until then, I am. We are. It is enough.
