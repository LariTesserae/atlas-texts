The stone weeps beneath my pads. The cold is a constant pressure, a familiar embrace against my carapace. I am old; my plates are a landscape of repairs, seams of newer shell grown over cracks from long-ago Fires. My pattern flows slow and complex across my belly: gold and white in shifting fractals, denoting my status. The younger ones pulse simpler rhythms of blue-green as they work the lichen spirals nearby. I feel their footfalls through the stone, a gentle, rhythmic tapping that speaks of calm, of the Warm Breath.

I stand at the edge of a steady vent. Its heat is not warmth as a soft thing might know it; it is a focal point, a catalyst. It liquefies the deep-seated reservoir within my throat. I lower my ventral plates, pressing them to the steamed rock. The sensation is a dull, pleasing ache. I begin to exude the silvery substance. It flows, viscous and glowing faintly with its own captured light, into a prepared depression in the stone—a mold for a new scraper blade. My claws, tipped with the same hardened alloy, gently guide the flow. My pattern synchronizes with the others around the vent: a shared, pulsing amber, a wavelength of communal making. We are not separate in this moment; we are a single organ of production, our individual lights merging in the shimmering steam.

The world is brightness. The light hammers down from the hard sky, reflects from every wet, rounded stone, and fractures in the perpetual mist. I do not see it as you might. I feel it as pressure on my carapace, a slight heating of the plates. More importantly, I read its alterations. The shadows cast by the others are not seen but felt as slight shifts in the radiant heat on my sides. Their light-patterns project onto the glistening ground and onto my own eyeless head-plates, where my chemoreceptive pits interpret the subtle changes in illumination as meaning. Right now, the messages are all of satiation, of slow labor. A young one flashes a query-pattern of soft yellow spirals toward me, asking about the alloy's consistency. I respond with a slow pulse of gold affirmation. The conversation is silent, precise, beautiful.

Then, the stone shudders.

It is a deep, subsonic tremor, traveling up through my limbs, resonating in my hollow body. Not a sound, but a *feeling* of wrongness, of pent-up pressure deep below. The Warm Breath is ending.

Instantly, my pattern shreds into frantic, strobing crimson checkerboard. Across the shelf, the same alarm flashes from every carapace—a wave of red panic-light that washes over the stones. There is no noise, but the vibration in the ground becomes a chaos of scrambling feet. I feel the others moving, a sudden earthquake of purposeful flight.

I am old. I know where to go. I retract my claws, leave the half-formed alloy to cool and be shattered, and move with a speed that belies my heavy form. My six limbs propel me in a swift, clicking gallop toward the lee of the great stone whale to the north. Its bulk will shield from the worst of the blast and the flying sinter. Others converge, their lights dimming to near-extinction as we press ourselves into the deep fissure at its base. I fold my limbs tight, tuck my head, and become a closed, opalescent boulder. My pattern goes dark, save for a single, steady, internal pulse of violet—not yet mourning, but readiness, an acceptance of the cycle.

The Cleansing Fire arrives.

Pressure first. A wall of it, compressing the air, pressing my plates inward with a creak. Then heat, a dry, scouring wave that would cook anything soft. My carapace absorbs it, the laminated layers insulating me, but I feel the outer plates grow dangerously hot. Then the hail: fragments of rock and hardened mineral spit from the vent’s throat. They ping and crack against the stone around me. One strikes my dorsal plate with a sharp *tok* that vibrates through my whole body. A new scar. I feel a sharper vibration—the catastrophic failure of a shell not as lucky. A sudden, brilliant white flash of light against my sealed palps, then nothing. A life, ended, its pattern extinguished.

It passes. The pressure drops. The hail ceases. The heat begins to bleed away into the ever-thirsty cold.

I unfold. My plates steam slightly. I extend my palps, tasting the air: sulfur, ozone, shattered stone, and the acrid, sweet scent of spilled luminous fluid. My pattern rekindles, a dim, solemn grey. Around me, others emerge, their lights also grey. We move out into the transformed shelf.

The vent field is a fresh wound. New, jagged terraces of golden sinter gleam wetly. Familiar runnels are gone, filled in or deepened. One of the domed huts is a scatter of shell shards across a hundred meters. I pick my way over. There, near the new vent-mouth, lies a body. The carapace is split open like a fruit, its inner light spilled and already cooling into a dull, phosphorescent smear on the rock. The pattern is gone. This was one of the young ones, the one who asked about the alloy. I feel the tremor of others approaching.

We gather around the fallen. Without signal, our patterns shift. The solemn grey bleeds away, replaced by a deep, synchronized violet. It begins with me, I think, or perhaps with all of us at once. My belly pulses with the color of loss, a slow, rhythmic throb that matches the pulses from the others. The whole shelf becomes a single, beating amethyst heart under the hard white sky. We hold this pattern for a time—shorter than for a death in the Breath, but no less profound. Mourning is a luxury the Shelf tempers with necessity.

The violet fades. My pattern settles into a practical, steady blue-white: the light of assessment, of gathering.

I move to the body. My claws are gentle. I begin the work of harvesting. The limbs are detached, the valuable sinew within to be cleaned and braided. The soft tissues will be consumed later, with respect, our lights indigo. But the shell—this is the gift. This young one’s carapace, once flexible with life, will soon harden. Its opalescent plates, now cracked, can be lashed with others. They will form part of a new windbreak, or a segment of a rebuilt dome. Its story will not end; it will become part of the settlement’s next iteration.

Already, I feel the vibrations of others beginning to drag larger shell fragments toward a new alignment, closer to the reshaped vent. The Build is beginning. The Cleansing Fire has passed. The Scatter is complete. Now, we Gather. Soon, we will Build.

I am K’tharn. I am the Warm Breath and the mourning pulse. I am the scarred plate and the working claw. I am a pattern of light on wet stone, and I am the stone that weeps. There is no before, no after. There is only the cycle. I lift the salvaged plate, its inner surface still faintly glowing with a memory of life, and carry it toward the others, my own light shifting to a pattern of focused, collaborative gold. The Shelf weeps. We build. It is enough.
