I am K’th. The sound is not a name, but a two-pulse vibration in the throat, the first a soft click against the palate, the second a hum that resonates in the chest-cavity. Others know me by it. I know myself by the cold.

I wake into the chill, as always. It is not a transition from sleep to alertness, but a slow, viscous seep of sensation into limbs made heavy and stupid by the damp cold. My alcove is a pocket in the south wall, where the Root-Mind grew a shelf for me three growth-cycles ago, after I painted the correct pattern of gratitude on the node above. The shelf is lined with dried moss, but the moss is perpetually damp. My skin is the colour of the wall behind me—waterlogged heartwood, dappled with darker streaks. In the faint, sickly green luminescence from the fungal colonies below, my own patterns are invisible. I am camouflage.

I am a Listener. My pads are broad, and the skin there is so thin I can feel the thrum of sap in the Root’s veins from three spans away. My skull, pressed to the wall, can parse the minute contractions of growth, the tremors of distant movement. It is my function. I am young for it—my ropy hair is not yet threaded with the grey moss of long memory—but the old Node, V’rra, says the Root-Mind speaks to me more clearly. Perhaps it is because I am quiet. My own thoughts are a still pool. The Root’s thoughts are slow, vast, vegetable. They fit.

First, heat. Without it, I cannot move with purpose. I uncurl from my sleeping-crouch, every joint protesting. My breath plumes, a ghost in the greener gloom. My heat-pot sits in a niche, a clay vessel the size of my two cupped hands. I lay my palms against its flanks. The heat is a low, precious pulse. I can feel it seeping into my pads, up my arms, a slow awakening of my blood. I press my forehead to the warm clay. The ritual. Basking. Around me, in other alcoves, I sense more than see the shapes of my kin doing the same. The communal hum is low this morning—a single, sustained note of contentment. The cold is not deepening. That is good.

I eat a handful of cold, gelatinous algae scooped from the pool below my shelf, and a blind salamander egg, raw and bursting with salty fluid. As I swallow, a ghost-leech lands with a soft *tap* on my shoulder. I feel its questing, cold mouth against my neck. I let it sit for a few breaths, letting it drink a little of my hard-won warmth, then I pluck it off. It is translucent, pulsing with a faint, stolen inner light. I pop it into my mouth. It bursts, salty and cool, a jelly with no resistance. Nourishment.

My task today is the Western Galleries. V’rra’s hum this morning carried a thread of unease. The Root-Mind is restless there. Something is pressing against its outer boundaries. I gather my things: a pouch of fermented mud, mixed with crushed glow-fungus; a small bladder of water; my flint knife, its edge keen. I drape my moss-cloak around me, the patterns telling my lineage—a series of spirals for the root-growth my mother oversaw. I pull the hood up. It smells of wet earth and my own scent.

The descent from my alcove is via a knotted rope, the fibres braided from root-hair, strong and slick. I climb down into the main chamber. The air thickens, the sweet-rotten fungal scent coating my throat. The ground gives under my feet, a spongy, living peat. Water wells up around my toes, icy. The fungal light from below casts everything in wavering, upside-down shadows. Above, the ceiling is a tapestry of pale roots, dripping. The *plinck… plinck… plinck* of water is the heartbeat of the Hollow.

I move silently, my long limbs adapted to the space. I pass a family platform, woven between two great columns. A young one, barely off the back, watches me with huge, black eyes. I hum a greeting—two ascending notes. The young one’s mouth opens in a silent response.

The Western Galleries are colder. The columns here are younger, their bark smooth and weeping a clear, sticky sap. The silence is different. The constant, wet sounds of life are muted. I stop, press my ear and cheek to a column. I close my eyes. I listen.

Through bone, through skin, I feel it. A tremor, deep. Not the steady pulse of growth. A grinding. A pressure. Then, a sharp, sudden *contract*—a spasm of the Root’s flesh. Fear. It is a simple, primal signal. Something is chewing. Something is pressing in.

My own blood runs cold—a literal sensation. I fight the torpor that wants to claim me. I must report. But first, I must speak. I open my pouch, dip my fingers into the cold, luminous mud. On the column, at the junction where a major root splits, I paint. Not words. Concepts. A pattern of concentric circles, interrupted by a jagged line. *Pressure. Breach. Defend?* I mix in a spiral of greenish glow—a question, an offering of my own labour to help. The Root-Mind will taste it. It may respond by thickening the wall, by secreting toxins. It may do nothing.

As I finish, a ghost-leech lands on my painting hand. I ignore it. I am listening too hard. The tremor comes again. Closer. A Root-Burrower. The great, pale worm. It must have sensed a warmth-pocket, or the vibration of our movements. It is blind, but it knows hunger.

I turn and move quickly back the way I came, my feet sucking in the peat. I do not run—running is wasteful, alarming—but I move with a swift, gliding urgency. I find V’rra at the Heart-Mound, a raised hummock of peat where the oldest nodes gather. She is ancient, her skin a tapestry of wrinkles and moss-growth, her eyes like pools of void. I press my forehead to the peat before her, then look up and hum. I do not use my pulse-name. I use the sequence of clicks and glottal stops that means *Western Wall, grinding, the big hunger.*

She hears. Her own throat pulses. She does not look at me. She looks through me, at the Root. She begins to hum. It is a low, complex tone that spreads outwards from her. Others nearby pick it up. They modify it, pass it on. Soon, the gallery is filled with a soft, harmonized warning. *Move from the west. Gather warmth. Be still. The Root defends.*

It is done. The decision propagates. I have done my part.

Shaken, I retreat to my alcove. The fear has not left me. My body is cold again. I stoke my heat-pot with a precious nugget of resin. As the warmth returns, I take out my collection. A flat piece of smooth shale. My prize: a handful of jewel-insect wings, salvaged from the feasting of the salamanders. They are iridescent—blue, green, violet, like the mythic sky of the Above that none of us have seen.

On the shale, on a bed of damp, black peat, I arrange them. I make a spiral, like the one in my cloak. But in the centre, I place a single, jagged sliver of white quartz, like a tooth. Around it, the wings shimmer, beautiful, fragile. It is a record. The Root-Burrower’s approach. The communal hum. The fear and the response. It will decay. The wings will lose their lustre, the peat will swallow the quartz. In a few days, it will be nothing. But for now, in the fungal glow, it is truth. It is my conversation with the dark.

I am K’th. I am cold. I am listening. In the gullet of the sleeping god, I make temporary beauty from the things that are eaten. It is enough.
