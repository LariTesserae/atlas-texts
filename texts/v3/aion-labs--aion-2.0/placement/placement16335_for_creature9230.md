The vibration through the stone is my first and constant thought. It is the foundation upon which all other thoughts are carefully stacked, like the basalt blocks of our hut. It is the deep-throated song of the Loom, the great tearing. I feel it in the arches of my feet, in the long bones of my shins, a pressure that hums up through my spine and into the sealed chambers of my skull. Today, the song has a new dissonance—a fraying subharmonic that makes the sap in my veins itch.

I am Kaelen-of-the-Indigo-Crown. My tree is the only true thing.

I stand at the edge of the Still-Pocket, my bare feet on the warm, glass-smooth obsidian our family has painted with concentric rings of dried amber. Beyond the outermost ring, the world is not to be trusted. I see it not as you would. My eyes drink the light and translate it into texture, into weight. The air over the barren stretch between here and the next family's tree, three days by Calm Steps, shimmers with a greasy, wrong thickness. Time pools there, viscous and slow. To walk into it unprepared is to feel your thoughts stretch into languid, meaningless threads.

But my gaze is not on the distance. It is on the Vein. A crack in the rock, from which the amber light pulses. It is the color of a promise, of a memory of warmth. It is the Tree's blood, and ours. My sister, Elara, is already there. I see her not as a shape of flesh, but as a constellation of calm intent, a slightly denser knot in the stable reality we maintain. Her long, grey fingers move with the slowness of continental drift, using a bone awl to encourage a bead of sap to swell at the lip of the stone channel we carved generations ago.

I make my way to her, performing the Calm Steps. Left foot forward, press, feel the gravity tug—normal. Right foot arcs wide, avoiding a patch where the "down" direction tilts thirty degrees westward. Pause. Listen with my soles. The dissonance vibrates up again, a loose thread in the Tapestry plucked. I resume, my body a language of avoidance and deference to the landscape's whims.

Elara does not turn. She feels my approach in the vibration of the stone. I kneel beside her, the rough basalt cool against my knees. I tap my sternum twice with a fingernail: *Query*.

Her reply is a soft, rapid triplet against her own chest. *Listen. The root-song changes.*

I place my palms flat on the ground, closing my eyes to see better. I push my perception down, through the paint, through the rock, to where the great taproot of our Tree delves deep. The Tree's song is usually a complex, resonant chord beneath the Loom's drone—a melody of stability. Now, there is a thrumming anxiety in it, a syncopated rhythm that matches the new dissonance from below. The Tree is dreaming a different dream.

We look at each other. Her black eyes are wells of reflected churning sky. In them, I see my own concern mirrored. A change in the root-song precedes either a great bounty or an Unraveling.

We gather our bowls—mine is seasoned, darkened by centuries of sap, hers is newer, pale bark from a branch that fell in a quiet year. The sap flows thicker today, as if agitated. It drips with a languid, heavy light, filling the bowls with a radiant, slow-moving honey. It smells of ozone and petrichor, though rain has never fallen here.

As we work, the world performs its slow violence. A rent in the sky, high above the distant horizon, widens by a finger's breadth. No sound reaches my sealed ears, but I feel the change in pressure, a sudden suction in the fabric of *here* that makes the leaves of our Tree chime in a startled, dissonant cluster. The leaves. They are not still. Their indigo and violet forms, shaped like broken sigils and lost punctuation, tremble. A gold leaf, shaped like a helix, twists on its stem.

Then, it lets go.

It does not fall. It *descends*, spinning slowly, chiming a soft, clear note that cuts through the oppressive drone like a blade of ice. A fallen leaf. It is the first in seven years.

The event freezes us. This is sacred. This is the Tree speaking directly. Elara’s breathing, a thin whisper from the slits in her neck, ceases. The leaf lands on a bed of smaller, resonant shale, its golden form stark against the grey.

The ritual is precise. We finish our collection, sealing the Vein with a plug of hardened sap and clay. We carry our bowls back to the hut, each step measured. Only then, with the sap safely stored in the hollowed stone in the center of our dwelling, do we return for the leaf.

My father, Orin, has joined us. His form is gaunt, his vein-tracings darker, like cracks in old stone. He carries the Leaf-Basket, woven from the stems of a thousand previous offerings. We form a triangle around the fallen helix. We do not look at it directly with our mundane sight. Instead, we open our perception. To us, the leaf is not an object. It is a knot of meaning, a solidified idea. It vibrates with a frequency of *transformation*, of *ascent-through-unmaking*. It is a difficult meaning. A fearful one.

Orin taps his sternum in the complex pattern of the Receiving Chant. I and Elara mirror it. The sound is dry, like stones clicking in a void. We believe the leaf hears the vibration, understands our intent.

With infinite care, Orin lifts the leaf with two bone tweezers. It chimes once more, a note of farewell, and is placed in the basket. The air around us feels immediately thinner, as if a supporting pillar of reality has been removed.

Back at the hut, under the thatch already singing with the silent hymns of countless leaves, we deliberate. This leaf is too potent, too specific, for the roof. Its meaning is not general protection. It is a directed path. It must be worn.

It is decided. Elara will wear it. Her task of Sap-Tending is most vital. The leaf's meaning of *ascent-through-unmaking* may guide her through any unexpected reality breaks near the Vein. With a needle of obsidian and thread made from her own hair-fine root-fibers, she stitches the golden helix to the breast of her bark-fiber tunic. As the final knot is tied, I see a subtle shift. The air around her clarifies. The ever-present visual static of minor reality fluctuations smooths out in her immediate vicinity. The leaf is working.

Night falls, which is only a deepening of the perpetual twilight, a dimming of the sky-rents' bleeding colors. The Loom's drone shifts into a lower, more thoughtful register—the **Second Stillness**. We sit in our hut, sharing a sip of sap from a single bowl. It burns going down, a warmth that spreads not through flesh, but through my *sense of being*, anchoring me more firmly to this spot, this now.

Elara is tracing the shape of her new leaf through her tunic. Her black eyes are distant, reading the new patterns in the Tapestry that her presence now weaves.

I am Kaelen-of-the-Indigo-Crown. I feel the dissonance still, beneath us. The Tree dreams a troubled dream. A leaf of transformation has fallen. My sister wears a helix of gold. The Unraveling is always at the edge, the roar is the world tearing. But here, in this small pocket of order defined by our steps, our sap, our leaves, we weave our silent answer. We are the whisper in the thunder. We are the gardeners of meaning, and for one more night, our tree stands, and we are not undone.
