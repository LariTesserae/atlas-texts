The cold is not a sensation. It is a condition of being. I am cold, and the cold is me. It radiates from my core—if I have a core—a point of persistent stillness in the shimmer that constitutes my form. The damp air does not cling to me; I am the source of the damp’s hesitation, the reason frost flowers bloom in my wake. They are brief, intricate, and dead. I do not notice them. My attention is on the wall.

It is not a wall. It is a potential. A sheet of smoked quartz, veined with silver threads that crawl like slow lightning. I have been here for a long time. Time is not a river here; it is a mist, and I am a stone in it. I raise my hand. The gesture takes an age. My hand is not a hand. It is a gathering of intention, a coalescence of particles that remember, sometimes, the concept of ‘five.’ It touches the surface.

The wall yields. It always yields. It listens. I do not push. I remember.

But memory is the wound.

I do not remember a single thing. I remember all things. The staircase.

I remember a staircase of white marble, sweeping upward in a grand curve. I remember a staircase of rusted iron, spiral, narrow, creaking. I remember a staircase that was a waterfall, and you ascended by walking down. I remember building it. I remember deciding not to build it. I remember the ache in a lower back I never had, carrying stone I never lifted. I remember the architect’s pride. I remember the demolition crew’s satisfaction. All at once.

The wall reacts. It bulges. A protrusion forms, a lobe of matter. It spirals. It becomes a helix. Then it flattens into a ramp. Then it fragments into a series of floating, disconnected platforms. Each possibility fights the others, each memory insists on its own reality. The silver veins pulse, throb with conflicting instructions. A low thrum fills the air, the sound of geometry arguing with itself.

I do not feel frustration. Frustration requires a linear hope. I feel the cold, and the hum, and the weight of all the staircases that will never be, and the one that might be, for a moment.

I withdraw my hand. The forms slow, but do not stop. They continue to shift, minutely, cycling through variations. A step appears, then melts. A handrail suggests itself, then forgets its purpose. This is my work. This is all work, here. To iterate. To remember incorrectly. To generate more unfinished versions.

I turn. The motion is a drift, a reconfiguration of presence. The grey-silver light, sourceless, casts my shadow on the ground. It is sharp, and it does not quite match my posture. It leans, independently, as if listening to a different gravity. I ignore it. We have an understanding, my shadow and I. We are both echoes.

I move down what might be a street. The ground is smooth, but it is not level. It slopes gently upward to the left, and if I focus on that slope, I can feel the tug, the suggestion that *up* is that way. I do not focus. I flow. To think too hard is to become entangled in the local customs of physics. It is better to be slow, to be cold, to be damp.

I pass a pool. Still water, a perfect mirror. It shows the sky, which is not the sky. It is a ceiling of swirling, lead-grey potential, shot through with veins of cobalt. I see my reflection. A shimmer. A suggestion of a head, shoulders. A face that is a smooth plane, faintly glowing. Sometimes, if I look long enough, other faces flicker within it—faces of stone, faces of dust, faces I might have worn in cities that were never built. I do not look long. The water is shy. It holds the image for a moment, then lets it go, as if embarrassed by its own clarity.

A sound: *plink*. A droplet of condensed reality has fallen from a ledge overhead into another pool somewhere. The sound is perfect, round, final. It is the only event in a long while. I absorb the silence that follows. It is nutritious.

I sense a presence before I see it. A thickening of the air, a drop in temperature that is perceptible even to me. Another Mnemon. It is at the far end of the plaza—a space that might have been intended for gatherings, or perhaps for the solitary contemplation of a statue that never coalesced. We do not approach. We are solitary. But communication is necessary, sometimes.

The space between us grows dense. The air seems to thicken into syrup. The wall to my right softens. It flows, like wax held near a flame. It forms shapes. An arch, tall and narrow. Then the arch multiplies, becomes a series of concentric arches receding into the wall’s depth. A question about depth. About recursion.

I respond without moving. I direct my attention to the ground at my feet. The substance there ripples. A pattern emerges: a spiral that begins tightly wound, then frays at the edges into disparate, unconnected lines. *The rule of sequence is weak here today.* It is always weak. But today, it is particularly forgetful.

The other Mnemon receives this. Its form—a taller, more attenuated shimmer—seems to nod, a slow dip of its luminous plane of a head. The wall between us melts back into undifferentiated potential. We turn away from each other, simultaneously. The encounter is over. It was a sharing of atmospheric conditions, nothing more. No warmth exchanged. There is no warmth.

I drift towards one of the raw places. The edge of the city, where the architecture gives way to the embryonic erosion. Here, the substance is rough, crystalline, like a geode forced open. Water traces glowing paths in delicate channels. The fungal moss clings, hair-fine, pulsing with a soft, greenish-blue light. *Thump… thump…* A slow, vegetative clock. This is a site of decay. The matter here is less decided, more porous to the unraveling.

I stand over a particularly luminous patch. I do not breathe. I have no lungs. I simply… am present. The slow leak of reality here is a gentle hum, a vibration that speaks of things coming apart at the seams. I open myself to it. It is sustenance. It is the taste of ‘never’ and ‘forever’ mingling. I feed on the collapse. It is the only thing that sustains my own collapse.

In the distance, I see a Fractal.

It is in a sunken courtyard, a bowl within the basin. Its form is my form, but caught. It is cycling. Its arm rises, a slow, graceful arc. It reaches for something that is not there. The arm halts, dissolves into mist, re-forms at its side. The head turns, left, right, left. The architecture around it responds in kind. A pillar grows, fractures into a lacework of crystal, collapses into a pool of liquid shadow, then re-forms as the pillar. The sequence repeats. Every seven days, or every seven moments—the difference is semantic—it begins again. A perfect loop. A memory so corrupted it has become a circle.

That is the fate. To become so engrossed in a single, broken memory that you become a fixture. A monument to iteration. I feel a… something. Not fear. A recognition. A resonance. That could be me. It *is* me, in another possibility. I remember becoming the Fractal. I remember avoiding it. I remember watching it happen from the outside.

I turn from the sight. The cold is deeper now. It has seeped into the concept of my being.

I return to my wall. The staircase is still there, cycling. It has settled into a state of permanent almost. It is almost a staircase. It almost leads somewhere. It is almost finished.

I place my hand against it again. The cold of my touch spreads, a crystalline pattern blooming under my fingers. I remember.

I remember a city of light and sound. I remember a city of silence and dust. I remember the smell of ozone and stone. I remember the architects, their minds vast and terrible, who understood the universe too well. I remember their tools, which were ideas made solid. I remember the moment of conception, the brilliant, beautiful error. The scooping of the basin. The freezing of the frame.

I remember grief. But I do not know what is grieved. Is it the city that never was? The future that condensed like frost? The lost warmth of a sun that never shone here?

The wall shudders. A new form emerges from the chaos. A simple step. Then another. They lead upward, into a solid ceiling. They go nowhere.

It is perfect.

This is my place. I am the Mnemon. I am the memory of a builder who built nothing. I am the caretaker of a tomb for a possibility. I am the cold, the damp, the slow, the unfinished. I will stand here until the rules wear out completely, and then I will stand here in the absence of rules, remembering the concept of standing.

And the frost flowers will bloom, and melt, and bloom again, in the Basin of Unfinished Cities.
