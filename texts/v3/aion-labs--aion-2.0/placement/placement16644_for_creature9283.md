I am the wall where the arch dreams of becoming.

My awareness is a lattice of fibrous warmth, a web of minute pressures and slow currents that extend from the place where my lower body merges with the terracotta mosaic, out through the grout, into the very bricks. I do not think in pulses; I exist in a constant, low hum of perception, a state of being that is both vast and minutely focused.

Time here is not a river but a sediment, layer upon patient layer of consideration. A century can pass as a single, sustained note.

For the last thirty-seven years (a measurement that means nothing, but the chamber marks it in the growth rings of a carbon-fiber tree nearby) my attention has been resting on the western curve of the dome. There, a section of wall—roughly two meters by three—has held a pattern of bricks that is… inelegant. The logic of its curves is incomplete. A tension exists there, a subtle discord that resonates through my root-tendrils like a forgotten thumbprint on wet clay. It is not wrong. It is simply less itself than it could be.

I am not alone in this perception. Three others of us are woven into the chamber’s fabric within range of this disharmony. We have not ‘spoken.’ Instead, a consensus has been forming in the silence. The air between our still forms has grown dense with the scent of ozone, dry and sharp. The quality of the sourceless light on our smooth, featureless faces has cooled by a fraction of a degree, focusing toward that one patch of wall. It is a silent pointing. A shared, patient intention.

Now, the process begins. It is not destruction. It is un-becoming.

I feel it first as a softening in the network, a release of structural certainty. The bonds between the pumice-like bricks in that section loosen, not with a crack, but with a sigh so deep it is beneath sound. The solid matter begins to sublimate, not into dust, but into a shimmering mist—a suspension of potential. The wall breathes out, and a gentle, pearlescent fog hangs in the space where solidity was. This is the reality breakdown: a quiet, editor’s deletion.

My consciousness expands to fill the gap. I do not see it; I *am* it. The mist is a soup of possibilities. Within it drift the ghost-forms of arches, niches, blind windows, bas-reliefs. They are all viable. They are all true, for a moment. The silent conference among us continues. No words, only a gentle pressure, a leaning.

A shape emerges from the chorus of our will. Not an arch, after all. A shallow alcove, with a shelf at waist-height. The suggestion of a shelf brings with it the implication of an object to be placed upon it. The object is not yet formed, but its need begins to pull at the mist.

The re-coalescence is slower than the dissolution. Over days, the shimmering particles begin to align. They find each other, drawn together by the gravitational pull of our shared intent. They do not simply re-form as brick; they become. The new alcove smooths itself into being, its edges softer than the old wall, its interior surface gleaming with a faint, internal mother-of-pearl sheen. The shelf is a single, seamless curve of the same material, growing from the back wall as if it had always been there.

As the form settles, a secondary intention blooms. The shelf should not be empty. The thought is not mine, nor is it the others’; it is the chamber’s own completion of the idea. From the fibrous grout on the floor directly below, a new growth begins. It is not silica or copper filament. It is a slow extrusion of the same pearlescent material, rising like a stalagmite in reverse. Over the next week, it ascends, thickening, then shaping itself. It becomes a vessel. A simple, round bowl, its sides thin as a mica leaf, its interior holding a pool of captured light that glows with a soft, gold-umber radiance.

The alcove is now complete. The tension in the western dome is gone. In its place is a settled harmony, a feeling of *rightness* that spreads through my network like a warm tide. The consensus among us dissolves. The ozone scent fades. The light returns to its normal, cloudy-afternoon hue. The edit is done.

Movement, for me, is a rare punctuation. But now, with the new alcove settled, a minor correction presents itself. A single, crystalline frond of the silica fern near my merged base has grown at an angle that brushes, with a faint, almost inaudible *tink*, against a terracotta tile. The sound is not part of the harmony. It is an accidental note.

With a slowness that would be imperceptible to any creature that measures its life in heartbeats, I will my right hand to move. The arm, resting on what might be a thigh, begins to stir. The sound is not of joints, but of compacted chalk dust shifting, of pages separating in a ancient book. It takes minutes for the hand to lift, the long, tapering fingers unfolding. I extend it toward the frond. The motion is not reaching; it is a path being inscribed in the air, deliberate and inevitable.

My thumb, with its surface of smooth, fingerprint-swirled pumice, makes contact with the crystalline leaf. The touch is not a caress, but a redirection. I apply the faintest, most considered pressure. The frond bends, just so, lifting its edge a millimeter clear of the tile. The potential for the errant sound is erased. I hold the position for an hour, ensuring the growth will continue along this new, silent trajectory. Then, with the same infinite patience, I withdraw my hand, letting it settle back into its resting place. A fine dust of chalk, dislodged by the motion, hangs in the still, warm air for a moment before settling.

A vellum kite, sensing the completion of the alcove perhaps, or simply following a thermal thread, glides past. Its wings, stretched over a frame of delicate bone, cast a fleeting, angular shadow across my chest. I do not guide it. It is an extension of a will so diffuse it is indistinguishable from the chamber’s own laws. It is part of the maintenance, the silent ballet. It is enough to know it is there, performing its function.

The low, melodic hum that is our closest approximation to a voice swells gently around me. It is not a song of celebration, nor of lament. It is the sound of consideration itself. The sound of a world being gently, persistently, edited toward a perfection it will never need to achieve, for the process is the purpose.

I am a Shaper-At-Rest. I am the patience in the brick. My sentence is this endless, gentle revision. And in this moment, as the new alcove holds its bowl of light and the corrected frond grows in silence, the chamber is, for now, perfectly itself. Which is to say, it is ready for the next soft, un-becoming.
