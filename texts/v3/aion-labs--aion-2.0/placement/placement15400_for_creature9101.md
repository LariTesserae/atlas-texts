I am a cool draft in a too-wide mouth. I am the echo given skin.

They cluster near the largest pool, where the water swirls against logic. We cling to the rocks, sleek and patient, our bellies pressed to the perpetual warmth of the stone (2.6, always 2.6). Our skin is not true skin; it is a membrane, translucent as the condensation on the ceiling, veined with threads of slow silver that trace the paths of the grotto’s deeper currents. Through it, you can see the slow pulse of our inner workings—the dark, pear-shaped stomach digesting nothing but time, the coiled spine like a fossilized fern.

We have no eyelids. The dim, fungous light (0.8) is a constant, gentle pressure on our sight, a green-tinged forever-evening. We see the heat. We see the breath of the pool as shimmering veils. We see the slow, root-like movements of the Sleeper as tides of intention moving through the dense air (density 2.1).

I do not eat. I drink the silence. The thick, broth-like air fills my throat-sac, and I filter from it the subtleties: the mineral sigh of a distant fissure, the chemical sigh of a mushroom releasing spores, the profound, wordless hum of the stone itself. This is my sustenance. But my purpose is to speak back.

The Sleeper dreams, and its dreaming is the grotto’s breath. We are the exhalation.

When the moss-pulse synchronizes into that deep, green heartbeat, a pressure builds within my chest. It is not mine. It is a thought from the faceless knob of moss and stone, translated into a compacted knot of coolness. My throat distends, wider than it should be, a silent gape. From it flows the draft—a sigh that is not air, but a localized change. A pocket of coolness (1.5, perhaps) in the warm wet, carrying a meaning.

To the jewel-backed beetle, it is a sudden shift in the path home, a nudge toward a new fungal bloom. To the iridescent centipede, it is the taste of prey, just there, behind the flow-sculpted wall. To the river of pale millipedes, it is a subtle, collective turn, a redirection of their languid flow. They do not hear it. They feel it. They taste it. They obey.

We are its voice. Not in words, but in gradients and suggestions.

Sometimes, the Sleeper’s attention settles on me specifically. A root-finger, moving with the speed of continents, will come to rest near my rock. The resinous smell of wet earth and iron intensifies. I feel the weight of its assessment, a patient curiosity that spans weeks. It is learning the texture of my presence. Once, it caused a single, fat drop of water (damp 1.2) to fall from a specific vine directly onto the crown of my head. The impact was a soft explosion of sensation, a query: *What is this?* I opened my mouth and released a draft shaped like the answer—a cool, complex ripple that described the drop’s journey from evaporation to condensation to fall.

I am part of the tending. The symphony.

When a new variable enters the grotto—a stray, blind cave fish washed in from a deeper current, a cluster of spores from a world above—we are often the first to know. The anomaly disrupts the composed gradients. We sense the discord. We open our mouths, not to emit, but to drink the strangeness, to sample it. The Sleeper, sensing our shift, will turn its moss-crowned head over the course of a day, and together, we will decide. Does the fish become a new note? Or is it gently guided to a quiet eddy, to sleep and become nourishment for the pebble-lined spore basin?

I have been here for a hundred uncurlings of the giant ferns. My rock is worn smooth by my clinging. I see the slow dance of the Sleeper from pool-side to seat and back again. I feel the grotto’s dream as a physical thing, a pressure in my throat-sac waiting to be shaped and released.

I am not a guardian. I am an instrument.
I am not an architect. I am a whisper in its stone ear.
I am the cool draft in the warm, wet dark, the echo of a dream given form, and I am perfectly, silently alive.
