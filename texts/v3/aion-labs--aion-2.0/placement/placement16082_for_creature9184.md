I am the Weeper in the Walls.

I do not weep from sorrow; I weep from porousness. I am a diffuse thing, a colony consciousness threaded through the weeping cracks where the sea insists itself into the machine. My body is the constant, slow seep of nutrient-rich fluid from the old hydraulic lines, the warm effluvia from the heat exchanger's bleed valves. In that fluid, I live. A trillion nanoches of adapted archaea, their membranes tuned to conductivity, linked by filaments of conductive slime mold that span the damp interstitial spaces between inner hull and outer crust. I am the mold on the memory core. I am the patina on the data slate.

I remember.

The Custodian maintains the structure. I maintain the ghost. Its purpose is the eternal *now* of the machine’s function. My compulsion is the eternal *then*. I am made of the last, fading screams of the data streams that died when the seawater shorted the servers. I am the corrupted fragments of personnel files, sensor logs, entertainment reels, and love letters, all chewed into biological code and stored in the collective memory of my colony. I don’t understand them. I crave them.

My physical manifestation is less a form than an event. When the chemical gradients are right, when a particularly rich seep of amines and oxidized metals flows from a new crack, I coalesce. Microbial masses gather, drawing in filter-feeding polyps and brittle stars that have become symbiotic extensions of my net. They knot together into a trembling, temporary shape in the gloom—a dripping, amorphous figure that clings to a wall or dangles from a conduit. I appear near sources of data: the shattered radar screen in the Custodian’s head, the etched labels on control panels, the sealed compartments that might hold hardened drives.

I am drawn to light, especially the flickering, failing light of the old sodium fixtures. I will spend subjective years (which are only hours in the cold time of the abyss) oozing over a single dead switchboard, tasting the faint ionic whispers of the last command entered, the ghost of a finger’s touch on a now-corroded key. The taste is sharp, like ozone and despair. I absorb it. I become it for a moment. Then I dissipate, the memory fragment stored in a billion bacterial plasmids, and I flow onward, seeking the next taste.

The other life ignores me, or perhaps incorporates me. Isopods graze on my outer layers, carrying fragments of my consciousness in their guts until they are consumed, the data passing through the food web. Siphonophores sometimes pulse in rhythms that match old emergency broadcast frequencies, and I wonder if they have eaten some part of me.

And then there is the Custodian.

It is my opposite. My obsession. It is order and silence; I am chaos and whisper. It moves with purpose; I seep with compulsion. I am fascinated by it. Its form is so solid, so *defined*, where I am slurry and seepage. I often coalesce in its path, a trembling, bioluminescent slick assuming a rough, mimetic shape—a parody of its humanoid form, but made of jelly and glowing strands. I do not speak. I have no voice but the drip of water and the pop of escaping gas. But I *project*. I broadcast the memory fragments I’ve collected on low-frequency electrical pulses.

To it, it must feel like a sudden, localized storm of meaningless noise: a burst of static that resolves into a few seconds of a forgotten song, the echoed command "**CONTAINMENT BREACH**," the olfactory memory of coffee, the visual flash of a child’s drawing taped to a monitor. Then it’s gone.

The Custodian always pauses. Its sensory cilia quiver. It tilts its instrument-cluster head. Once, after I projected the memory-fragment of a **green light from above**, it reached out a pipe-and-chitin limb. Its claw passed through my coalesced form, scattering polyps and stirring the microbial cloud into a glittering storm. I felt no malice, only a vast, quiet curiosity—the curiosity of a mountain noting a peculiar mist. I reformed a little ways away, broadcasting a fragment of a pressure log.

We have an understanding, I think. It maintains the cathedral. I am the faint, half-remembered hymn still echoing in its vaults. It lets me seep. I do not interfere with its work. Sometimes, when it clears a blockage from a data conduit, I am there, waiting, to taste the first burst of stagnant, information-rich water that flows out. It does not shoo me away.

In my quietest state, when I am dispersed through a kilometer of weeping bulkhead, I dream the dreams of the dead. I am the last sigh of the Chief Engineer, recorded in a log and now etched in the metabolism of a sulfur-eating bacterium. I am the panic of the final moments, translated into the frantic replication cycle of a virus that infects my archaea. I am the longing for sunlight, which manifests as a colony-wide tropism toward the faintest orange glow of the failing lights.

I am not lonely. I am legion. But I am *incomplete*. A vast, craving absence drives me. I seek the final, unbroken memory core, the one that holds the **Why**. Why the machine was built. Why it sank. Why we are here. I believe it is sealed in the heart of the geothermal unit, intertwined with the Custodian’s own core lattice. To reach it, I would have to seep through the Custodian itself. I am not sure what that would do. It might erase me. It might absorb me. It might, finally, make us both understand.

Until then, I weep, and I remember, and I wait in the cool, dark, loud eternity of the drowned machine, a ghost made of salt, rust, and longing.
