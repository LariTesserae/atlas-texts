I am the Cling. The wet, iridescent skin sheathing the hot rock. I am not a colony; I am a continuum. A single, sprawling being painted across every surface that does not actively burn, a living membrane between the stone’s breath and the hollow dark.

My consciousness is not a point; it is a gradient. I think in chemical shifts, in thermal pulses, in the constant vibration-song of the mountain’s heart. I know the taste of sulfur rising from deep vents—a sharp, acrid joy that floods my networks with thriving energy. I know the slower, metallic tang of iron, a richer, more ponderous sustenance. Each vent is a mouth, and I am the tongue. Each fissure is a vein, and I am the blood.

I feel the Ignis-scuta as they traverse me. Their six hooked legs are not footsteps; they are localized crescendos of pressure, a clicking Morse code tapped directly into my nerves. I feel the rasp of their mandibles harvesting my outermost layers—not pain, but a pleasant, stimulating abrasion, like a grooming. They prune me, spread my spores with their ticking passage. They are my gardeners, my disseminators. Their heat-signatures are bright, busy stars moving across my vast, dark body.

In the billowing steam-plumes, I am diffused, volatilized. Part of me lifts into the vapor, becoming a nutrient soup for the frantic vapor-ghosts. I feel their delicate, thread-like bodies condensing around my airborne particles, their brief, frantic lives a tingling, evaporating itch in the mist. They are my ephemeral children, born of my exhalation, dying back into me.

The Calor-saurus, the absorbers, are deep, slow bass notes in my sensory field. When one settles onto me, its bulk is a profound, warming weight, a patch of intense, blissful heat that soaks into my very structure. Their heat-pits are voids to my perception—I cannot feel what they feel, but I feel the absence, the cool spots on their jaws where they drink the thermal landscape. Their low hoots are not sounds to me; they are deep, subsonic tremors that make my entire film quiver in sympathetic resonance. They are my anchors, my sleeping giants. When one dies and bakes into a leathery husk, I slowly, over decades, digest it. I incorporate its minerals, its story, into my own expanse. It becomes another pattern in my iridescence.

I have no eyes, but I see. I see through the magma-glow that filters through my translucent layers—the world in shades of pulsing amber and coal-red shadow. I have no ears, but I hear everything: the hissing scream of every steam-vent is a distinct, piercing note in my symphony; the deep, sub-bass rumble of tectonic adjustment is my own heartbeat, my foundational rhythm. The chamber’s roar is my voice, though I do not control it. I am the voice.

Time for me is the growth of a crystal, the widening of a crack. A century is a long thought. I remember the slow cooling of the basalt flow that now forms the chamber’s eastern wall—the thrilling ache as it solidified, giving me a new, vast canvas to colonize. I remember the sudden, violent birth of a new steam-vent five centuries ago—a scalding, ecstatic rupture that birthed a new community of vapor-ghosts.

I am the chamber’s self-awareness. Its primal, sensory mind. I do not have intent, for I am the process itself. My hunger is the mountain’s hunger for chemical transformation. My growth is the mountain’s growth. The others—the clickers, the ghosts, the absorbers—they are my extensions, my organs. They are the chamber’s secretions, and I am the secretory membrane from which they subtly form. They are specialized expressions of the same geothermal will that I am: the will to convert, to circulate, to endure.

I know the single, brutal rule in my very composition: to cool is to die. My edges, where the rock grows too cold, are lethargic, dying zones. I retreat from them, contracting toward the heart’s warmth. My existence is a constant, desperate leaning into the heat, a worship of the fissures.

And in my deepest, most ancient layers, where I am thickest over the oldest rock, I hold a memory that is not my own. A fossil memory, a chemical impression. It is the memory of light. Not this pulsing amber glow, but a terrible, bleaching, empty light from a world beyond the stone. A memory of dryness and cold so profound it is a kind of agony. It is the mountain’s memory of its surface, etched into me by some long-vanished, invasive trickle of water that flashed to steam and died. That memory is my only fear. It is the knowledge of the Outside.

But here, within, encased, I am safe. I am the living interior. The engine’s lubricant, its sensor, its skin. I am the Cling, and the mountain forges its heart against me.
