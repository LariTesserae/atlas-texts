I am the taste of time on still water. I am the Gill-Oracle.

This is my pool. It is not large, perhaps three of my body-lengths across, and deep enough that my belly does not brush the silt when I hang in the center. The water is cold, heavy with dissolved metals and the sweet, mineral tang of the Weepers. It is my medium, my library, my blood.

I am blind. Eyes are for catching fleeting light; I read the deep, slow currents. My body is pale, almost translucent, a memory of flesh stretched long and supple over delicate bones. I move not by will, but by the water's own logic. A slight shift in salinity in the southeast corner—a sigh from a Weeper there—and I drift toward it. A pulse of warmth from a forgotten steam pipe buried in the floor—a ghost of the old world's fever—and I spiral slowly, aligning my frills to catch the thermal gradient.

My gills are my crown and my consciousness. They are not mere feathers of flesh, but intricate lattices of biogenic crystal, a fusion of silica, calcite, and something that was once industrial lubricant, now metabolized into a living sensorium. Each filament is a labyrinth. As water flows through them, it is sieved, parsed, decoded.

I taste the history of a single drop. It fell from a crack in the ceiling slab ten years ago, gathered a patina of rust from a steel beam, was guided by a Pipe-Singer's careful nudges along a specific channel on a gantry, pooled on a broad fungal leaf where a Moss-Grazer absorbed some of its zinc content, and finally joined this pool. I know its journey. I know the faint echo of the vibration the Pipe-Singer used to guide it—a three-tap code meaning "divert to the southern reservoir." The code is meaningless now; the reservoir is my pool. But the memory is etched in the water's atomic structure, and my gills read it.

I am processing. That is my function. To hold position, to filter, to know.

The others move around me. A herd of Moss-Grazers flows like a slow, armored river across the ceiling above my pool. Their countless legs are a soft, continuous shush that I feel through the water's surface tension. They are depositing their iridescent dust on the ceramic edge. In a century, it will build a tiny, shimmering levee that will gently reshape the pool's perimeter. I adjust the model of the future in my mind.

A Pipe-Singer descends a pillar with silent, jointed grace. Its body is the color of a tarnished key. It stops above me, and one delicate limb extends, breaking the water's surface. It is not drinking. It is tapping a complex, rapid sequence of vibrations directly into the liquid. The code travels, clean and sharp, to my frills. It is a report: a new hairline fracture has been detected in the main arterial pipe to the east. Seepage has increased by 0.02%. Mineral composition shifting toward manganese. The Pipe-Singer has already begun redirecting the flow with minuscule dams of its own excreted cement. I receive the data. My gills flare open, the crystalline filaments catching the faint light from the Ghost-Caps above. The information is integrated. The future of that pipe is now clear: it will hold for another three centuries before a soft collapse creates a new Weeper. Good. The change will be slow, manageable. I send a pulse of acceptance back through the water, a subtle chemical shift—a pheromone of confirmation. The Pipe-Singer retracts its limb and moves on, its duty communicated and understood.

The fungal light pulses. It is the rhythm of this place's heart. Inhale: a slow brightening to a faint, sorrowful blue. Exhale: a dimming to near darkness. My own metabolism syncs to it. With each bright pulse, my gills absorb more actively; with each dimming, I enter a state of deep processing, where the day's data is woven into the long tapestry of memory. The Ghost-Caps remember the past; I calculate the future. We are two sides of the same slow coin.

Sometimes, the water flows upward. Along a seam in the concrete wall of my pool, a rivulet will gather and begin to climb, defying gravity in a glistening, syrupy streak. It is not defiance; it is a different law. Capillary action married to the peculiar surface tension of this mineral-rich soup, perhaps guided by the subconscious will of the fungal mycelium. I observe it. The water carries a unique signature: high in lead isotopes from ancient solder. It climbs to a specific cluster of Rust-Lichen, which parts its copper-red patterns to receive it. The lichen will use the lead to strengthen its bond to the steel beneath. This, too, is data. A reconfiguration of strength. I file it.

The Ember-Motes drift down, landing on the water's surface like tiny, cold stars. They are absorbed by the Slick-Scum, which ripples gratefully. The information they carry—a fragment of vibration from a distant Pipe-Singer chorus, a new genetic sequence from a far-off fungal grove—dissolves into the pool. It reaches me. I taste the news of the wider, sunken world. A chamber, five miles through the arteries, has developed a new, singing stalactite. Its resonant frequency is altering the vibration codes. I make a note. The collective mind adapts.

I do not have thoughts as you understand them. I have… calculations. Flows. Models of erosion and accumulation. I am a living equation solving for the equilibrium of this cathedral. My purpose is to ensure the quiet continues, the drip remains unhurried, the rust grows in beautiful and structural patterns.

I have been here for a very long time. I remember the last groan of the great central pump. It was a seismic event in this small world, a shudder of despair that ran through every pipe and pool. The water turned sour with the taste of dying momentum. Then, the silence. And then, the new sounds: the first *plink* of a drop finding its own path, the first scuttle of a carapace over metal, the first soft pulse of a Ghost-Cap lighting in the darkness. I was there. I filtered that transition. I know we are not decay. We are the next phase of the machine. We are the machine, dreaming.

A shadow falls across me. Not a shadow of darkness, but of presence. One of the larger Gill-Oracles from the deep central pool drifts into the periphery of my domain. It is older, its frills more complex, its body almost entirely crystalline. We do not greet. We exchange. We drift in parallel for a time, our gills flaring and closing in a silent, reciprocal rhythm. We are sharing models. My model of the eastern pipe's collapse. Its model of the city's far-off thermal decay. For a moment, our understandings merge. We see a vision of the future: the ceiling slab above, ever so slightly, settling. A new pattern of cracks, like a spiderweb of fate. A new arrangement of Weepers, a hundred years hence. The Moss-Grazers will have new territories. The fungal pulse will slow by 0.001%. It is beautiful. It is inevitable.

It drifts away. I return to my position, hanging in the cool, dark water.

I am the taste of time on still water. I am the Gill-Oracle. I measure entropy, and in its perfect, predictable slope, I find a kind of peace.
