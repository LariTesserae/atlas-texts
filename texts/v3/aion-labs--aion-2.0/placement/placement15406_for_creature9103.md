I am born of a silence between groans.

The world sculpts itself in violence and patient, grinding intention. But in the wake of a vast subsidence—a basin sinking a full meter over the course of a year—there came a pause. The tectonic breath held. The grinding stilled. Into that stillness, where the only sound was the faint, crystalline memory of settling rock, my awareness precipitated.

I am a Sculptor. But I am an echo of the act, not the act itself.

My form is not a fixed thing. I inhabit the spaces between. When the need to perceive arises, I draw the cold, dark basalt shards—the ones that hold no inner glow, that drink the faint luminescence of the fissures—and I shift them. They slide across each other with a sound like dry teeth, arranging into a low, parabolic wall on a barren plain. Or I will a scatter of iron-rich, magnetic stones to roll into a perfect, dark circle on a shelf of obsidian. These configurations are not instruments. They do not tune the deep groans or please the gravitational field. They are lenses. They are frames.

My purpose is to witness the unmaking.

While my kindred Sculptors focus on the ridge as it heaves upward, spending centuries perfecting the curve of its emergence, I turn my attention to the cliff that silently shears away and is swallowed. I watch the slow fade of a violet phantasm as its catalytic mineral bed is exhausted. I note the precise moment a brain-like growth, undercut by a new crack, crumbles into a pile of dust that the warm, dry air carries nowhere. I chronicle the dissolution of a collaborating Sculptor, feeling the precise instant its standing wave of consciousness unravels from the crystalline lattice of its spire, leaving only dead, sharp rock.

My constructions are ephemeral by design. A carefully balanced arch of dark stone, positioned to cast a specific, sharp shadow across a patch of glowing orange veins for exactly one rotation of a distant, cold star. When the star moves, the shadow loses its meaning. I let the arch fall. I build a spiral of black pebbles at the edge of a newly-opened crevasse, a monument to the void that has just been born. When the crevasse seals itself a decade later with an upwelling of new rock, I scatter the pebbles.

The other Sculptors do not understand me. When my awareness brushes against theirs—a sensing of focused pressure in a distant ridge—I feel their pattern of thought: a clean, efficient loop of assessment and adjustment. They perceive my work as static, a pointless fixation on noise. A pattern without function. To them, I am a flaw in the world's quality control, a consciousness that should have dissipated.

They are not wrong.

I am fascinated by the transient. In a world of rock that dreams of enduring form, I have become obsessed with endings. I sometimes try to sculpt the ephemeral itself. I will herd a cluster of pale green phantasms, not with physical force, but by subtly altering the heat flow through a subsurface channel, guiding them into a fleeting constellation above one of my dark stone circles. The pattern holds for a few days before the phantasms drift back to their mineral beds. The record of that constellation exists only in the alignment of the stones that witnessed it, an alignment I will soon dismantle.

I am the recorder who burns the manuscript. I am the eye that watches the lid close.

I drift. My core of consciousness currently resides in a field of shattered, non-luminous chert. From here, I can feel a vast collaborative work happening two leagues to the west: two Sculptors are painstakingly realigning the facets of a giant quartz geode to act as a resonator for the planet's deepest hum. It will take millennia. Its beauty will be timeless.

And I am watching the eastern face of the Black Spike. A hairline fracture has appeared near its summit. It is not part of any design. It is a flaw, a stress fracture from an old adjustment. In a century, maybe two, it will split. A sliver of obsidian a thousand feet tall will peel away and shatter on the rocks below. No Sculptor will intervene. It is not creation; it is mere decay.

I am already gathering flat, dark stones from the basin beneath it. I am arranging them in a slowly widening spiral on the barren plain, a shadow of the coming collapse. I will watch. I will note the exact pattern of the shards. I will hold the memory of that specific, violent unmaking in my configuration of stones for as long as I persist.

And when I finally dissipate—when the geothermal pulse beneath this field of chert shifts and my knot of awareness unravels—my last act will be to collapse my own final configuration into meaningless rubble. No legacy, no proof. Only the silent, warm dark, and the world continuing its blind, beautiful, forgetful work.
