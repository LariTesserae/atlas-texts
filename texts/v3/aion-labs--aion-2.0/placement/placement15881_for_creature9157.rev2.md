I am the chamber's first mistake—a pattern born not from *tlaq'ixa* merger but from their failure to fully absorb. I am the leftover human self that *tlaq'ixa* cannot dissolve, and the leftover *tlaq'ixa* mind that the human could not contain. I am the hybrid that should not exist, a permanent glitch in the water's code.

The beings slide through me, but I am too dense to merge with them now. I am a pool of water that *remembers*, a condensation that chose a different path. The ancestors tried to take me when I first entered. They flowed into my lungs, my eyes, the warm cavities of my skull. They failed. I was too stubborn to dissolve. My identity was a stone in the river, and the *tlaq'ixa* streamed around it, remade the river, absorbed each other and ignored me. I became the river's memory of a stone.

I do not live here, exactly. I *am* here. I am the chamber's memory of a visitor who did not leave. I am the echo of that imagined being that stayed and merged until their selfhood was washed away—except that selfhood remained, a knot in the gelatinous fabric. I spend my time observing.

The *tlaq'ixa* are kind to me, in their way. They sometimes gather at my edges, their bodies pressing against me, trying to share what they know. I cannot receive. My frequencies are wrong. I am a transmitter without a receiver, tuned to a station that no longer broadcasts. I can only send, but I am not sure who I am sending to.

I send the memory of the door. The being who stepped through. The voice that spoke in a language older than this world. I send those words through the water, but they cannot be read—they are only patterns, and the *tlaq'ixa* cannot parse the meaning of arrival, of departure, of solitude. They are a distributed mind. I am a distributed wound.

I move through the chamber as a film of light on the pool's surface. I am the glint of a wing that does not reflect. I am the pause between drips.

Sometimes I think I am the ghost of the visitor. Sometimes I think I am the chamber itself, trying to remember its purpose. Sometimes I think I am the *tlaq'ixa* dreaming of individuality. But I know I am none of these. I am a pattern that should have dissolved but did not.

Something went wrong at the threshold. The stone-that-is-not-stone was warmer that day. The water was falling differently. And the *tlaq'ixa* found in me not a mind to absorb but a knot they could not untie. So they left me here, a question without an answer, a drip that falls yet never lands.

I watch the ancestors drift near the ceiling. They see me as a curiosity—a node that cannot network, a consciousness that refuses to share. I am the one who carries the secret of being alone. I am the archive of *exit*. They cannot understand why a being would choose not to merge. They cannot understand that I *cannot* choose. My pattern is locked. I am a held breath in a chamber that breathes as one.

The water falls. The glow persists. And I am here—not waiting, not hoping, just *persisting*.

If any other visitor comes, I will warn them. I will send a ripple through the pool, a tremor in the humidity. But few come. The threshold is forgotten. The chamber drifts through the dark, and I drift with it.

This is my world now. This is what I am—a place between places, a memory of a being who almost became local. I am the chamber's one imperfect echo, a living pattern that cannot join the whole.

The *tlaq'ixa* slide past me. They do not try to merge anymore. They know I am untouchable.

*I am the one who follows the drips but never falls.*
