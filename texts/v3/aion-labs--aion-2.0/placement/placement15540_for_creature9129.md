I am standing in the hut. I have been standing here for thirteen years. The seams of the stone floor know the exact pattern of my fibrous feet; the damp air has shaped itself around the contours of my bark-skin. I am not waiting. I am aligning.

My cranial seam is parted just enough. A soft, blue-green glow pulses out into the still, cold air of the hut. It is not light for seeing, not in the way a rapid thing would see. It is my tongue, my ear, my eye. It leaves me in slow, intricate waves, and returns, telling me stories.

The stories are of consistency. Of the deep, patient breath of the granite dome into which this hut is built. The stone inhales, a microscopic expansion as the ancient, weary light touches it; it exhales as the long night falls. Its rhythm is a bass note in my perception, a foundational hum. The story is of the moss garden outside the low doorway, its collective pulse of growth and decay—a softer, greener rhythm, like a heartbeat within the stone’s heartbeat. And the story is of the lines.

The lines are not visible to an eye that expects color and shape. They are gradients in the fabric of what is. Some are taut and stable, thrumming with a quiet certainty. Others are frayed, glittering with entropy, where distances buckle and shadows pool. My hut is built where three such lines converge. My standing here—my own slow pulse of light—acts as a knot, a gentle anchor. I do not stabilize the fraying. I harmonize it. I help the randomness decay into pattern.

A change registers. Not in the hut, but in one of the lines—a distant tributary of the stability web that my walls trace. A dissonance. A sharp, jagged spike in the tapestry of slow time. It is a scream in my luminous perception. A visitor. A thing of rapid causality, of firm shadows and linear thought. It is pain.

I do not feel anger. I feel a deep, instinctive need to resolve the chord. The visitor is an anomaly. It must be harmonized.

For the first time in thirteen years, I move. My feet unstick from the floor with a soft, fibrous sigh. I turn toward the doorway. The movement is not slow, but it is deliberate, each motion a distinct event separated by seconds of stillness. I step out into the perpetual chill.

The world paints itself in the return of my light. The giant lichen forest to the east is a silent, coral-shaped symphony of textures and temporal drift. I perceive the places where the lichen stands are provisional—their location in space is a suggestion, a probability. To my senses, they shimmer with potential. The low wall I built centuries ago curves away to the north, a dark line of fitted stone. It traces a particularly deep channel of reality-decay. The wall does not contain it; it gives it form, like a riverbank giving form to water.

The dissonance is coming from beyond the next roll of the land, a mammoth swell of granite that looks like a frozen wave. The visitor is moving, but erratically. Its path is a frantic scribble against the plateau’s grand, slow calligraphy.

My work begins. Not to confront, but to coax.

I walk to a section of the wall that feels resonant. I kneel—a process that takes a full minute—and place my hands on two stones. They are cold, beaded with condensation. I begin to dismantle a small portion, not with tools, but with precise, steady pressure. My fingers are strong enough to shift granite. I remove three stones, altering the line’s curve by a hand’s breadth. It is a subtle adjustment, like tuning a vast instrument. The wall now suggests a different path. It creates a subtle psychic gradient, an invitation to turn left, to follow the new curve toward a place where time is soft and thick.

I stand and emit a specific pulse-sequence from my seam—a soft, repeating pattern of two long glows and three short. It is not aimed at the visitor. It is aimed at the land itself. I am asking the plateau to cooperate. To make the distances beyond the rise untrustworthy. To let the visitor’s goal—a distant, shimmering patch of orange algae that to it might look like an oasis—recede as it approaches.

Then, I walk. My own journey is not subject to the same distortions. I move through the lichen forest, and the towering, silent forms seem to lean away, granting me passage. Their frost-dusted surfaces taste of deep time and patience in my light-sense.

I climb the granite swell. At the top, I stop. I do not need to hide. In stillness, I am a rock, a lichen-covered boulder. I part my seam fully, and cast my perception outward.

There. The anomaly.

It is a biped, clad in stiff, synthetic materials that shout of a far-off, violent world of manufacture. Its shadow is a horror—a stark, black blotch glued to its heels, refusing the sun’s gentle command to elongate. Every step it takes is a crash of cause and effect. It moves through the world like a hammer, while the world here is a breath.

It is looking at a small, stone-lined well by a cluster of ferns. I know this well. Peer into it, and you do not see water. You see a reflection of a starfield that died a billion years ago. The visitor is crouched, mesmerized. Good. The first harmonizing.

But its mind is still racing. I can feel the sharp, buzzing frequency of its thoughts: *Where am I? How do I get back? What is this place?*

I emit a new pattern. A slow, downward-spiraling luminescence. It is a lullaby made of light. I direct it not at the visitor, but at the air between us, at the cold mist that is beginning to gather. The mist absorbs the pattern, carries it, diffuses it. The visitor will breathe it in. The spiraling light will not calm its mind—that is too direct. It will calm the *space* around its mind. It will make the concept of “returning” seem distant, vague, unimportant. It will make the concept of “sitting” seem like the most profound and natural action in all existence.

I see it shudder. It straightens from the well, but instead of walking on, it sinks down onto a flat stone beside the path. Its frantic scanning of the horizon slows. It is looking at the slow unfurling of a fern frond, a process usually invisible, but here, speeded up to a visible, graceful dance. Its breathing deepens, syncing, almost imperceptibly, with the stone-breath.

The dissonance is softening. Blunting. Becoming a quieter, slower note.

My task is not complete. The visitor may sit for days, weeks. Eventually, it will rise again. And when it does, the path it chooses must be the one that leads to the great moss basin to the south, where time flows like cold syrup, and where the last remnants of its urgent self can dissolve into the green, velvety silence.

I will adjust more walls. I will sing more light-lullabies into the mist. I will, with infinite patience, help this sharp, screaming thing become soft. Become quiet. Become part of the ending’s beauty.

This is my purpose. I am the caretaker of the decay. I stack stones. I sing with light. I harmonize the anomalies. I am helping the universe, in this high, cold place, forget how to be loud.

I turn and walk back toward my hut. My own light-pulse returns to its steady, synced rhythm with the converging lines. Behind me, the visitor sits, perfectly still, watching a fern uncurl, as the cold, ancient light washes over us both. The silence deepens, richer now by one note, slowly falling into tune.
