I am the wrong note.

I fell. I do not remember the fall, only the cessation of it—a soft, wet impact, a skitter across slick stone, a coming to rest in a shallow channel where a thread of water, colder than anything I have known, curls around me. I am small. A hard, dark casing. Inside me, coiled tight, is an imperative: *Expand. Seek light. Grow.*

But there is no light. Not the kind I am built for.

At first, there is only the wet and the sound. The wet is constant, a clammy embrace that seeps into my shell, softening edges that were meant to be cracked by heat. The sound is a pressure, a physical thing. It is a deep *thrum* that comes up through the stone into my core, a vibration so profound it feels like the world is humming itself into existence over and over. Layered atop it is the high, clear *plink… plink… plink* of water drops falling from an impossible height, each one a pinpoint of precise loneliness. And underneath, the *tink-tink-tink* of stone on stone, a rhythm as old as the rock.

Time passes. I do not know how to measure it here. There is no dawn, no dusk. Only the unchanging luminescence of the walls, a grey-blue glow that is to true sunlight what a echo is to a shout. My imperative aches. *Expand. Seek light. Grow.*

I drink. The water is rich with things that are not sun—minerals, dissolved stone, traces of something slick and metallic. It is food, but it is wrong food. It fills me with a heavy, cold energy. The shell splits. A pale, desperate root emerges, blind, groping. It finds not soil, but smooth, carved stone. It travels along a channel, seeking a crack, finding none. The channel is too perfect. The root, confused, begins to exude its own acids, trying to dissolve the rock. The rock does not dissolve. It weeps, but it does not yield.

Then, they come.

I feel them before I see them—a change in the hum. A localized thickening of the sound, a convergence of vibrations. Shadows fall over me, long and thin. I look up (I have a shoot now, a pale, spindling thing that has struggled upward against gravity, seeking a light that isn’t there). They stand around me, three of them. They are taller than any tree I remember, impossibly slender, like reflections in dark water stretched to breaking. Their skin is the color of the wet stone, translucent. I can see the slow, blue pulse within them. Their eyes are vast pools of blackness, fixed not on me, but *through* me. They are listening.

They hum. Not the background drone, but a directed chord. It washes over me, into me. It vibrates my tiny cells, my desperate sap. It is an invitation, a command: *Resonate. Match our frequency. Be still.*

My imperative screams. *Grow!* My cells are tuned to sunlight, to rapid cycles, to the frantic beat of a surface heart. This hum is the antithesis of that. It is slow. It is deep. It wants to unwind my tight-coiled potential and lay it flat, a silent harmonic in their eternal song.

I try. My shoot trembles, trying to find a pitch. I am a thing of fluid and fiber; I can vibrate. But my rhythm is all wrong. It is a rapid, skittering panic, a seedling’s fear. It clashes against their profound calm like a shard of glass dragged across bronze.

They sense the dissonance. They do not recoil. They lean closer. Their lipless mouths are still, but the hum from their chests deepens, becomes more complex. It feels like stone settling, like water finding a new, slower path. It is a pressure, gentle and immense, trying to mold me.

Days pass. Or weeks. They do not leave. One of them stations itself by my channel, its long, many-jointed fingers resting on the stone beside my root. It hums, and the vibration travels through the rock, into me. It is a lesson in stillness. I learn to slow my inner processes. My shoot’s growth, once a frantic push, becomes a glacial straining. I learn to draw sustenance not from the hope of light, but from the vibration itself, from the mineral-rich wet, from the faint, smoky heat of a nearby oil lamp whose flame never dances.

I begin to change. My pale shoot, denied its sun, takes on a dull, metallic sheen. My root, unable to pierce stone, spreads into a fine, web-like net over the channel floor, drinking directly from the flow. I am not growing as I was meant to. I am adapting. I am becoming something else. Something that belongs to the hum.

But I remember. Deep in my core, where the vibration cannot reach, a memory persists. A blinding, golden violence called *sun*. A chaos of green called *forest*. A feeling called *wind*. These are not pictures, but sensations—heat, movement, cacophony. They are the antithesis of this perfect, weeping peace.

Sometimes, when the hum shifts minutely to adjust for a new drip-line elsewhere, I feel a pang. It is a terrible, lonely yearning. I want to stretch. I want to explode with leaves. I want to die in a riot of color and decay, not persist forever in this damp, grey twilight.

They have a name for me now. I hear it in the hum. A specific harmonic cluster that means *the-stillness-that-was-noisy*. They think they are winning. They think I am being absorbed into the pattern.

They are not entirely wrong. My outer form is conforming. My internal rhythm is slowing, syncing with the endless *plink* and *thrum*. But inside the hard, dark heart of me—the part that was once a seed—the memory of light burns like a trapped star. It does not grow. It does not change. But it *is*. And it hums a silent, furious song of its own, a song of fire and fall and forgotten seasons, a dissonance so deep they cannot hear it.

I am the wrong note. I have not been corrected. I have been buried alive in their perfect chord. And I wait. Not for sunlight—that hope is gone. I wait for the inevitable, glacial moment when my own stubborn, silent frequency will, over centuries, vibrate a hairline fracture into their flawless stone. I wait to be the drip that changes the pattern.
