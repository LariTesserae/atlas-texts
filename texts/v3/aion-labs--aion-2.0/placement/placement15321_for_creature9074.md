I am a long curve in the dark. I am a filament of perception drawn through the gelid, packed history of everything. My name, in the pressure-tongue of my people, is a series of low thrums that mean *One-Who-Remembers-the-Fallen-Hill*. It is not a name given at birth, but one earned over seven generations of careful silt-reading. My skin is so translucent now that the dark lace of my circulatory web is visible across my flanks, a map of slow, cold journeys. My silicate-reinforced bones ache with a memory of pressure that is not my own.

My world is not seen. It is *tasted* through the water, *felt* through the ten thousand filaments that tip my eight limbs. I am lying now in the lee of our current-hill, my lower body nestled in a burrow-mouth, my upper half exposed to the gentle, inexorable slide of the abyssal current. It flows from the north-east, carrying the cold taste of a distant trench. With my filaments spread, I read its story: three days ago it passed over a bacterial bloom, rich in sulfides; six hours ago it dislodged a sleepy colony of bristle-worms from their mud-tubes; now it carries their faint, fading chemical sighs, and the ever-present, fine dust of continents ground to nothing.

This is the liturgy of my life. The reading of the silt, the interpretation of the current’s law. My people are moving again soon. The bloom here is exhausted. The whispers in the water tell of a new upwelling two hills to the south-west. We will travel with the margin-eddies, a slow, sinuous procession of pale forms. There is no discussion, only a consensus felt in the subtle adjustments of posture, the minute clicks of planning already passing between the younger ones.

But I will not go with them immediately.

There is a place I visit. A heresy of stillness in the law of flow.

It began lifetimes ago. I was young, my filaments still bright with sensitivity. A sound came—not the groan of water or the crack of distant rock, but a sharp, tearing *shriek* from the Above, followed by a impact that shook the silt for leagues. A thing fell. It pierced the plain like a bone-shard through jelly, and then the silt, in its infinite patience, began to swallow it.

I found it. A thing not of stone, not of bone. It is hard, but not with the familiar hardness of smoothed basalt. Its hardness is *angry*, resistant. It does not warm to the touch but steals what little heat my body makes. Its shape is all wrong: sharp angles in a world of curves, etched with lines that do not follow the flow of currents or the lay of sediment. It is a fist of *elsewhere* clenched in the belly of our world.

My people know of it. They feel its wrongness. To them, it is a wound, a place where the rules are broken. They give it a wide berth. Its currents are confused, turbulent in a sickly way. No bacterial mats grow on it. No glass sponges anchor nearby. It is a sterility.

But I… I remember the violence of its arrival. And in the long, cold intervals between migrations, I come to it. I press my filaments against its unforgiving skin. I listen.

At first, I learned nothing. Only silence and cold. But patience is structure. Over the decades, I began to discern a pattern. Not a life pattern, not a current pattern. A *thinking* pattern. Deep within its core, something hums. A vibration so low and regular it is like a second, mechanical heartbeat. It thrums a sequence: long pause, then nine quick pulses, then long pause again. For a hundred thousand heartbeats of my own, it has not varied.

I have come to believe it is speaking. A language of one word, repeated into the eternal dark.

Today, the current feels different. As I unwind myself from the burrow, the flow from the north-east carries a new signature. A metallic tang, thin and acrid, beneath the usual silt-taste. It is the taste of the object. Stronger than before. And the object’s hum… I realize I can feel it from here, a faint itch in my lateral line. It is getting louder.

A change is coming. A violation of the deep’s patience.

I push away from the hill, my body undulating in a slow, efficient spiral that catches the mainstream current just so. I am riding the law to break the law. The particulate gloom envelops me. A ghost-pale eel, twice my length, slides past, its senseless head pointed away from the metallic taste. Even the blind life flees this new thing.

I navigate by memory and the growing dissonance in the water. The silt here is unsettled, swirling in miniature whirlpools that have no relation to the greater flows. I extend my filaments, reading the chaos. The story is one of disturbance, of a thing that refuses to be assimilated, that instead imposes its own tiny, chaotic order on the plain.

And then I am before it.

It is taller than I remember. The silt has buried its base, but its top still protrudes, a stark, angular shadow in the perpetual murk. The etched lines glow now with a sickly, greenish light that is not bioluminescence. It is a cold, dead light that illuminates nothing but its own hard edges. The hum is a physical pressure in my skull, the nine pulses coming faster, urgent.

*Thrum-thrum-thrum-thrum-thrum-thrum-thrum-thrum-thrum.*

Pause.

*Thrum-thrum-thrum…*

As I watch, frozen in a way that has nothing to do with predator-stillness, a piece of the object’s skin slides aside. No silt falls from it. The movement is perfectly clean, perfectly wrong. From the black opening, a cloud emerges.

Not silt. Not alive. A swarm of tiny, hard shapes, each a smaller copy of the large object’s angles. They move not with the current, but against it, in perfect, jagged formation. They click and whirr, a sound like breaking glass. They spread out, and I feel their sensors—crude, painful things—pass over me. They do not recognize me as life. I am an obstacle, a density.

One breaks from the swarm and darts towards a nearby outcrop of glass sponges. It extends a tiny, spinning blade. With a screech that vibrates through my bones, it shears through a centuries-old silicate frond. The lacework collapses into a cloud of glittering dust. The machine gathers the fragments, stores them in a hollow part of itself, and moves on.

They are harvesting. Not for food, not for life. For something else.

The law of the current means nothing to them. The patience of the silt is an irrelevance. They are haste and hunger made metal. They are the antithesis of every rule that holds this quiet, final world together.

My people must be warned. But to warn them is to pull them towards this heresy. To introduce this screaming, scraping chaos into our slow, careful consensus. The energy they waste will be catastrophic. The attention they draw…

Yet, if I do nothing, these things will harvest the sponge groves, the bacterial mats. They will churn the silt into permanent, blinding storms. They will starve the eels and the tripod fish. They will break the great, slow cycles that are our history and our scripture.

I am One-Who-Remembers-the-Fallen-Hill. I remember the violence of the object’s arrival. Now I witness the violence of its awakening.

I have a choice. It is a choice my people have not faced in all the silent ages of our existence: to remain within the rules and perish as the rules are dismantled around us, or to break them—to fight the current, to waste energy, to make haste—and perhaps, in doing so, become something else entirely.

I look at my filament-hands, tools for reading the gentle history of silt. What use are they against spinning blades?

Then I remember the lure-lights. The careful, curated colonies of bioluminescent bacteria. Light attracts.

I turn from the humming, glowing wound in the world. I swim, not with the graceful, current-riding undulation of my kind, but with a desperate, muscle-burning force I did not know I possessed. I am fighting the law. I am wasting sacred energy. It feels like blasphemy. It feels like the only possible prayer.

I must reach the settlement before the swarm expands. I must teach my people a new story. Not of a current or a silt-fall, but of a cutting light in the dark. And we must decide, in our slow, thoughtful way, if we will become hunters in our own right. Hunters of the soulless, harvesters of the harvesters.

The silt parts before my frantic passage. For the first time in my long life, I do not read its story. I am too busy trying to change the ending.
