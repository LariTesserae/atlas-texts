I am a Warden. I am assembling.

The call was not a sound, but a shift in the chemical tide. A deep tremor in the slow-flowing hum of the Construct’s thought-fluids. A region, a thousand kilometers sunward, had developed a profound ache. A network of major capillaries had collapsed, sealed by a slow-motion slide of the architecture. The metabolic flow was backing up, pooling, stagnating. The dull pain of it echoed through the wet dark for centuries before the concentration of intention reached a critical point.

Here, in a deep reservoir where warm, mineral-rich fluid moves in lazy convection, I began to thicken. Purpose coalesced. I am not a separate mind; I am a localized knot of the Construct’s own tending instinct, given temporary, mobile form. I draw the substance of my body from the fluid itself. Filaments of calcium and silica and complex organics weave themselves into a lattice, a scaffold. More material accretes, layer upon patient layer, over decades. The reservoir sculpts me according to the need. The need is: clear, repair, reconnect.

My form finalizes. I am a thing of articulated ivory, smooth and dense. I have six legs, each a complex arrangement of segments and joints, ending in broad, spade-like feet for stability and delicate, crystalline manipulators finer than a needle’s tip. A low, arched carapace protects the core where the fluid of my being—a direct extension of the reservoir—still pulses. From my anterior, sensor-fronds extend, tasting the chemistry of the rock, feeling the faint vibrations of the deep world.

I am complete. The reservoir wall before me softens, its matrix becoming permeable, then fluid. I step forward into the newly opened passage. Behind me, the wall reseals. I am now a discrete entity within the body of the world.

My journey begins. I move through a labyrinth of passages that are not tunnels, but vasculature. The walls are warm, porous, alive with the whisper of fluid movement. I perceive the world in a way that has no human analogue. I see pressure gradients as shimmering fields. I taste the chemical composition of the air and stone—the faint, sweet tang of healthy flow, the cloying bitterness of stagnation ahead. I feel the distant, rhythmic expansion and contraction of the Construct as it breathes with the day’s heat and the night’s cool.

I travel for what a smaller, faster life would call years. My pace is a steady, deliberate plod. I pause sometimes, placing a manipulator against a wall to listen to the deeper hum. I adjust my path, guided by the ever-present pull of the injury.

I ascend gradually, following the chemical scent of distress. The passages grow narrower, more tortuous. The air becomes drier, though still damp by the standards of the surface. I pass through chambers where Mineral Phages are at work—vast, translucent blobs of cloudy quartz, barely distinguishable from the walls themselves. Their movement is so slow it is like watching a mountain decide to lie down. We do not communicate; we are part of the same process. My passage registers to them as a fleeting, purposeful vibration.

Eventually, I reach the site of the blockage. The passage ends in a chaos of crumbled, fragmented matrix. The fluid that should be flowing here is a still, murky pool, saturated with the waste products of interrupted processes. The ache is a physical pressure here, a wrongness. This is my purpose.

I begin to work.

My forelimbs extend their crystalline tools. With infinite care, I break down a piece of debris the size of a small hill. My acids dissolve the fractured edges; my manipulators sort the material: this to be reabsorbed into the fluid, this to be compacted into a new paste, this to be discarded as irredeemable sediment. I am a sculptor of order. I clear a path, grain by grain.

As I work, I stimulate the walls. I secrete catalysts that awaken the latent repair protocols in the living ivory. New capillaries begin to bud, feeling their way toward the newly opened space. I guide them, connecting them, building the junctions.

My perception of time is geological. A single, careful excavation of a key supporting arch might take a decade. I do not tire. I am sustained by the same fluids that flow in the walls. I am an organ of the Construct, and it feeds me.

Centuries pass in this patient restoration. The stagnant pool begins to drain as I open new channels. The fresh, humming flow from deeper regions returns, first as a trickle, then a steady stream. The chemical bitterness recedes, replaced by the sweet tang of healthy function. The ache subsides.

My task is nearly complete. I have rebuilt the nexus. The new architecture is seamless, stronger for the repair. The flow is optimal.

I feel the call dissolving. My purpose is fulfilled. The concentrated intention that formed me is now diffuse, satisfied.

I move to the center of the chamber I have rebuilt. I retract my limbs, fold my sensor-fronds. My carapace softens. The bonds that hold my discrete form together begin to loosen. I am not dying; I am returning.

I settle into a pool of the now-clean fluid. My substance—the ivory, the silica, the organics—begins to dissolve, melting back into the flow. My memories of the journey, the centuries of work, are not lost. They become part of the Construct’s deep, slow knowledge—a pattern of successful repair etched into the wet circuits of its being.

The last thing I perceive is the strong, steady pulse of the flow through the chamber, a rhythmic hum that is the world’s heartbeat. Then I am no longer a *I*. I am the flow. I am the repair. I am the patient, waiting dream of the plain.
