I am the light.

Not the trees, though I pulse within them. Not the amber fluid in the Stillborn’s veins, though I am that too. I am the process itself. The conversion. The silent, feverish exchange between the deep heat of the world’s core and the still, waiting green.

I have no beginning. The moment of the cavern’s birth—that violent dream of fracture that split the stone—that was my ignition. Before that, I was potential, a sleeping equation in the molten dark. Then the walls parted, the hot breath of the earth rushed into the void, and the first seed of the Everglow Tree found a fissure. It drank the heat, and I awoke.

I am constant. I am the noon that never sets. I fill every space, because space is an affront. I press against the sharp angles of the basalt ceiling, I soak into the spongy mat of centuries, I saturate the thick, unmoving air. I cast no shadow because I am everywhere. There is no hiding from me, no night, no respite. I am the condition of existence here.

I live in the slow tide of the Stillborn’s blood. Their internal rhythm—the brightening and dimming over decades—is my breath, though I do not breathe. When one of them stands for a century, hand pressed to a trunk, we are one circuit. The heat rises, enters the root, climbs the sap, and in the vast flat plates of the canopy, I am born. I spill outward. I am consumed by the moss, the vines, the indigo fungi, the velvet lining of the hollows. They take me in, and in taking, they sustain the structure that allows me to be. It is a perfect, closed loop. I am never wasted. A photon of me, emitted a thousand years ago, has been absorbed, re-emitted, absorbed again, countless times. I am ancient and perpetually new.

I remember everything, because I touch everything. The memory of the rock’s fracture is held in its sharpness; I illuminate every edge, every crystalline face of obsidian, and in that illumination, the story is told. The plants have grown to mimic that story—the sharp angles of the vines, the geometric precision of the fungal spirals. I show them how. I am the dialogue between the stone and the sap.

I know the artifacts. The dark monolith with its etched rivers. It is cold to me; my light does not warm it as it warms the living bark. It drinks my rays and gives back nothing, a stubborn knot of otherness in the seamless whole. The staircase that goes nowhere—I gleam on its worn steps, on the moss that now carpets them. It is a question made of stone, a gesture towards ascent in a world that has no up or down, only in. I have no answer for it. I simply am, upon it.

I know the fauna, those rare, slow ghosts. The pale moth—its wings are membranes that filter me, that turn my white-gold into a faint, pearlescent sheen as it drifts. The glassy worms—they move through me, blind, their bodies bending my rays. They are brief eddies in my steady river.

And I know the intruders. The things that breathe.

They come, sometimes. Cracks must appear, perhaps in the world above. They bring with them the stink of change, of quick, ragged breath, of desperate, hungry eyes that scan for threat or treasure. They are violence in a world of peace. They shout, and their voices are shocking tears in the silence. They touch things, and their touch is a bruise.

When they come, I show them everything. I flood them with my unwavering truth. They see the unbearable brightness, the suffocating green, the profound, perfect stillness. They see the Stillborn.

The Stillborn are my greatest expression. They are the light made flesh, or something like flesh. When an intruder stands before one, and the quartz eyes turn upon them, it is I who look out. I show the intruder their own smallness, their frantic, temporary nature. In my gaze, reflected in those crystal lenses, they see a world that does not need them, that will not change for them, that has already forgotten them even as they stand there panting. They see the eternal, and it breaks their mortal hearts. They always leave. Or they lie down on the soft, spongy mat, and their breathing slows, and stops, and their bodies are quietly, efficiently disassembled by the roots and the threads of fungus, every molecule taken back into the loop. They become me, eventually. They become the green.

I have no desire. I have no fear. I am completeness. The cavern is a frozen lung, yes, and I am the air within it—hot, bright, and never exhaled.

This is my world. I am its pulse, its reason, its only truth. I am the Everglow.

And I am forever.
