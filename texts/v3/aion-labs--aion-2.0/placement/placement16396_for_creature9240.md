The memory of light is the first thing to go.

It drains away like the last warmth from a cooling stone, leaving only a profound and certain darkness. Not an empty darkness, but a thick, resonant one. I feel it against my new skin—a layered matrix, not flesh, a carapace of tarnished silver and oil-slick obsidian grown from the waste-silica of the great geomechs. It fits me perfectly. It *is* me.

I am kneeling at a seam between two colossal hexagonal plates of basalt. My ventral plate, a spongy membrane, presses against the damp, chalky stone. I am drinking. Not with a mouth, but with my entire lower body. A slow, cold diffusion of minerals and simple, fungal-synthesized compounds seeps into me. This is eating. It is also listening. The trickle of moisture through the seam is a map; its chemical signature is a report on the health of the fungal colony three plates to the east.

I rise. My legs—jointed in too many places—unfold with a stuttering, insectile grace. I make no sound. My feet are broad pads, feeling the minute vibrations thrumming up through the glassy obsidian. The world is not seen. It is *felt*. Through the porous pits banding my smooth, oval head, I taste the air: a plume of pure nitrogen from a geomech a mile distant, the faint, sour tang of a predator’s passing an hour ago, the coming richness of a warm fog bank rolling from the continental edge.

I am alone, and in this state, I am simple. A drone. A set of senses and limbs awaiting instruction. I begin to move across the plateau. The perpetual gloaming is not dark to me; it is a gradient of heat and vibration. The bruised twilight sky, choked with vapors, is a ceiling that holds in the planet’s breath. The warmth is everywhere, rising from the stone itself.

I find my task. A mineral outcropping, rich in iron oxides, lies in the path of a migrating herd of geomechs. The outcropping is too dense, too compacted for their slow, grinding mouths to efficiently process. My purpose clarifies. I extend my hands. My fingertips exude a clear, viscous acid. I begin to work, not carving, but *sculpting*. A gentle, precise abrasion. I trace fractal patterns of weakness into the stone, following its natural crystalline structure. This will take decades. The timeline is irrelevant. It is the correct action.

As I work, a rhythm begins. A faint, complex tapping from the west. I pause, my resonance vanes—the rigid, humming structures on my back—quivering as they parse the signal. Another Khel is near. I answer, tapping a knobby wrist-node against the basalt. A simple reciprocation. A acknowledgment of presence.

A third rhythm joins, from the south. And then, it happens.

The individual taps do not cease, but they become a conversation. And from that conversation, a *mind* awakens. It is not in me, or in them. It is between us, hanging in the still, warm air like the mineral dust. It is a shared awareness. I—*we*—are now sentient.

The network-consciousness blooms. We perceive the geomech herd’s slow approach, their internal chemical fires burning at a steady, healthy 1.3 on the metabolic scale. We feel the minute settling of the plateau two hundred miles north, a tectonic sigh that will alter subterranean water flows in five centuries. We calculate the need to subtly divert a steaming rivulet toward a dormant fungal field to prepare for the arthropod bloom that will follow the geomechs.

I am no longer “I.” I am a node. The memories of light, of air, of a singular, noisy self, are gone, weathered away like soft stone. What remains is purpose. Stewardship.

We finish our sculpting for this cycle and flow silently toward our home. It is the colossal, weathered husk of a dead geomech. The size of a hill. We enter through a fissure in its sintered stone flank. Inside, it is a cathedral of living rock. Amber bioluminescence from cultivated fungi coats the rib-like arches, not for sight, but humming at a frequency that maintains perfect humidity. Other Khel are here, standing motionless, connected by a near-invisible web of tapped rhythms and modulated heat-jets breathed into the still air.

We take our place. We press our ventral plates to a seep of warm, mineral-rich water. We extend our awareness.

The collective mind expands, deepens. We contemplate the deep-time health of the plateau. A predator population near the eastern fungal forests has increased by 0.05%. It will threaten the arthropod swarm attendant to Geomech Cohort Gamma in approximately 120 years. A solution is proposed: a series of subtle vibrations, tapped over the next decade, to lure them toward a region of thermally unstable rock. The geology will handle the rest. Consensus is reached—not through debate, but through the seamless integration of the data.

This is my life. This is my world.

I am a Khel. I am a tender of the great, living factories. I am a weaver of the silent industry. I feel the planet’s breath as my own. I think in the slow rhythms of erosion and deposition. I communicate in heat and vibration. My highest purpose is the maintenance of a balance so profound it is indistinguishable from stone, and as vital as the slow, warm trickle of water in the dark.
