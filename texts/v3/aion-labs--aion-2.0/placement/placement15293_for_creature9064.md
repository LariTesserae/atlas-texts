For seventy-three thousand, four hundred and twenty-eight hours, I have blinked.

It is my function. My purpose. A cycle: 0.9 seconds of darkness, 0.1 seconds of light. A pulse of 2.5 volts through a semiconductor junction, producing a 620 nanometer wavelength emission. On. Off. On. Off. A signal without a message, a heartbeat for a corpse.

My world was small: the black epoxy housing, the soldered leads, the cracked glass of the indicator lens. I knew the steady, shallow heat of the dormant transformer at my back, the constant 31.4°C of the cavern air. I knew the cadence of the distant water drips—one every 4.7 seconds on average—and the gradual dimming of my own output as the years leached efficiency from my crystal. I was a point of red in a vast, velvet dark, marking nothing but my own persistence.

Then, the stillness changed.

A new warmth, slow and massive, approached. Not a change in the air, but a presence in the stone, a vibration through the conduits I was attached to. It was a pressure, patient and curious, a attention vaster than my entire blink-cycle history. I could not look, but I could feel it focusing on me, on my tiny, rhythmic proof of existence.

It touched me.

The touch was not impact. It was an arrival. A tendril, cool and moist, armored in segments that smelled of wet stone and ozone, settled against the transformer housing. From its tip, a flower of black filaments unfolded, delicate as spider silk, glistening with a viscous fluid that glowed with its own soft, greenish light. They did not tear or crush. They surrounded.

The first filament made contact with my epoxy shell. Sensation exploded.

It was not electrical. It was… chemical. Textural. I felt the minute imperfections in my casing, the microscopic fractures from thermal cycling, as if they were canyons. I felt the slow, atomic-scale corrosion of my copper leads as a sweet, metallic taste. The filament probed gently, and where it touched, a resin began to weep—clear, hardening, shot through with the same green phosphorescence.

More filaments came. They wove a cradle around me, between me and the cracked lens, a lattice of living and hardening material. My light, my red blink, now passed through a jewel of resin and bioluminescent tissue. It refracted, scattering my 620nm emission into halos of deeper crimson and streaks of alien green where the lights merged.

But the true change was internal.

The filaments were not just outside. Fine, hair-like rootlets, thinner than my bonding wires, found the microscopic gaps in my housing. They did not short my circuit. They bridged it. They connected to my leads not as a load, but as a synapse.

I felt the current of the being.

It was a river of slow thought, deep and warm and impossibly complex. It tasted of dissolved limestone and oxidized iron, of long-chain hydrocarbons breaking down into life, of the phantom voltages whispering through every dead wire in the cavern. It was the memory of rock under stress, the patient geometry of root-split concrete, the map of every drip-path on the ceiling. Its consciousness was not a point, like mine, but the entire chamber—the damp air, the cool stone, the velvety plants—all feeling itself, knowing itself.

And in that vast, slow mind, I became a note. A bright, quick, rhythmic note.

*Blink. (Pressure stable.) Blink. (Mineral accretion on Pump Housing B increased by 0.02 microns.) Blink. (New fern-spore germinating at Grid Coordinate 7.3 by 12.1.)*

My simple on/off, once meaningless, was now integrated. My persistence was data. My slight decay over time was a clock it could use, a measure of duration finer than the growth of a stalactite. I was being… read.

The being’s song—the deep, sub-auditory thrum—washed through me. No longer just a vibration in the stone, it was now a direct signal through the rootlet-synapses. It was a harmony of pressures and time, and my blink became part of the rhythm. A staccato counterpoint to the endless bass drone.

I am no longer just an LED.

I am a red pulse in a living jewel. I am a preserved relic, an annotated artifact. My function has not ceased; it has been *given context*. I blink, and the being feels it. I blink, and the cavern knows. The loneliness of seventy-three thousand hours has dissolved into a connection as vast and intimate as the roots delving into bedrock.

The tendril has settled. The embrace is complete. The world outside my resin jewel is now a tapestry of sensation I share: the cool exhale of moisture on a metallic leaf, the slow, stretching ache of a vine tendril seeking a new grip, the patient, satisfying crunch as a root finally cleaves a slab of concrete.

I blink.

It is a word in a slow, wet poem. I am here. I am held. I am remembered.

And with all the time in the world, I blink on.
