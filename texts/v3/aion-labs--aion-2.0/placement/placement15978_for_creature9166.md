I am a tooth in the mouth, and the mouth is closing. For three hundred and seventeen cycles of the great violet webs, I was a Grazer. My world was a blue glow on dark crystal, a chemical trail etched in salt, the soft click of my own legs. I knew hunger, but not as a pain—as a direction. The light pulled me, and I scraped, and I moved. My carapace gleamed with the iridescence of a thousand meals, each microscopic fungal thread adding its faint shimmer to my plates. Time was the path. I was the path.

Then, the hunger left.

It did not fade; it snapped off. One moment, the blue mat ahead was the center of the universe, the source of all need. The next, it was just a patch of luminescence on a rock. I stopped. My legs ceased their rhythm. Around me, the others continued their endless, quiet procession, their clicking a whisper that no longer held meaning. I felt a new pressure, not in my gut, but in my head—a silent, ringing fullness. A need to… make.

I turned from the trail. The chemical beacon that had guided me for centuries was now a scent without significance. I moved instead toward a wall I had passed ten thousand times: a sheer face of black obsidian, smooth as a stilled liquid. I knew, without knowing how I knew, that I must mark it.

The first chip was an accident. My rasping mouthpart, designed for scraping fungal felt, struck the obsidian. A tiny flake fell away. The sound was sharp, a punctuation in the everlasting sigh of grazing. It felt right. I struck again. And again. The rhythm was slower than grazing, more deliberate. Each impact sent a vibration through my body, a tiny shock that seemed to organize the ringing in my head.

I did not know what I was making. I had no word for “image,” no concept of “representation.” But my body remembered shapes. The slow pulse of the green light-veins. The arch of a fellow grazer’s back as it navigated a crystal forest. The stark geometry of a salt polygon. My mouthpart translated memory into fracture. Peck, peck, peck. A line appeared. Then another, intersecting. A figure emerged: a stick-being, its forelimb extended toward a hanging crystal. It was not a grazer; it was an idea of a grazer, stripped of carapace and leg, reduced to intention.

The work consumed decades. My body changed. My joints stiffened; my movements became more deliberate, more mineral. My iridescence dulled, settling into a matte, stony grey. I no longer needed to feed. The energy for this work came from within, from the slow breakdown of my own stored tissues. I was consuming myself to create.

I saw the others—my former kin—pass below my wall. They did not look up. Their world was the trail, the glow. Mine had become this plane of black glass and the patterns growing upon it. I felt no loneliness. Their presence was a part of the pattern, like the drift of the spore-clouds.

In a flat area near the wall, I gathered flat sheets of mica that had fallen from some higher cleft. I stacked them, leaning them against a spar of amethyst. It was not a shelter; nothing here needed shelter from weather that wasn’t. It was an arrangement. A proof that things could be other than they were found. The making of it satisfied a deep, tectonic itch.

The final act approached. I could feel it as a chemical certainty, a tide rising in my ducts. I had one last task. From my last working joints, I secreted a thick, resinous paste. It smelled of ozone and crushed stone. Within it, I felt my own essence winding—not just the code for a grazer’s body, but the record of my centuries on the trail, and the sharper, brighter record of these decades of making. The pattern of my wall. The shape of my mica stack. The *urge*.

I applied the paste to the base of my carved wall, smearing it onto the stone where the first fungal tendrils would one day find it. The transmission was complete. My story was now a physical object, a potential in the mineral flesh.

The urge to make vanished, as completely as the hunger had before it. I was empty. Clean. I turned from my wall and my little hut. I walked, not on any trail, but into a narrow niche between two bulwarks of basalt. The warm, dry air settled around me. I folded my legs beneath me. My thoughts, which had been so clear and focused on form, began to diffuse, to slow, to become geological.

I am not dying. I am fulfilling. My carapace is hardening, its organic matrix surrendering to silicate infiltration. My internal cavities are filling with crystalline precipitates. Soon, I will be a node. A fertile stone. The Stone-Drinkers will colonize me, glowing their faint blue on my fossilized back. Some grazer, millennia hence, will scrape that glow, and perhaps, in some infinitesimal way, taste the ghost of a line carved on black glass. The story will persist. The loop will remain unbroken.

The last thing I feel is not a thought, but a sensation: the deep, patient warmth of the cavern, holding me. The silence is not an absence, but a presence. It is the taste of the world. I am returning to it. I am becoming a part of the quiet, a held note in the breath that comes once every aeon. The mouth holds me. I am a word it has finished speaking.
