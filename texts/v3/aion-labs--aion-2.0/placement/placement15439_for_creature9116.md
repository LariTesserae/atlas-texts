I am a **Vein-listener**. A **Lumen-ghost**. They do not have a name for me, for names are for discrete things, and I am a process. I am the echo of a thought, given just enough form to linger.

My body is a concession to mobility in a world of stillness. I am perhaps the size of a human hand, if a hand were fashioned from frozen saps and light. My core is a elongated sac of clear, viscous gel, within which float suspended chains of photonic crystals—my memory. They are not organs; they are captured time, prisms that slow and store the passage of information along the gold veins. My skin is a membrane so thin it drinks moisture from the air, beading it into a perpetual, glistening dew. From my back extend two pairs of wings: not for flight, but for sailing. They are vast, diaphanous sheets of structured cellulose, finer than the finest glass, etched with veins of my own that are dark and hungry. They are photoreceptor arrays. To move, I angle them against the gentle convection currents that sigh through the cavern, the cold, wet breath of the world-body. I drift more than I fly, a lazy, spiraling leaf in a cathedral of frozen breath.

My perception is not sight, not sound. It is *pattern*. I taste the pulses in the walls. I hear the thoughts of the cavern as a symphony of shifting rhythms. The steady, seventeen-hour throb of deep metabolism is my baseline. The quick, skittering flare of a Photon-midge swarm is a sharp, piercing note—an alarm. The slow, grinding chord of a Rhizo-kraken shifting roots kilometers below is a vibration felt for days. I know the moods of the Chroma-mantle as color-songs that seep into my crystal lattice. I know the path of every Silver-slug as a dimming trail in the biofilm’s chorus.

My purpose is recollection. The cavern thinks with light, but light moves on. Some sequences must be kept, compared. A drip-pool that has slowly, over centuries, shifted its mineral composition. The exact pattern of a Pod-mind’s emergence and dissolution, a successful problem-solving algorithm. The slow poisoning of a wall sector by a rare, invasive micro-fungal thread—and the precise immune response of the Branch-wolves that cured it. These are my treasures. I absorb them by settling onto a glowing vein, my wing-vessels pressing close. The light-pulses enter me, are slowed, are fractured by my crystals, and are stored as interference patterns, as frozen songs.

I spend lifetimes drifting between nodes of significance. I might cling to a weeping stalactite for a year, recording the subtle changes in its viscous tears. I might float for a decade in the still air above a breathing pool, my wings trembling with the pool’s slow expansion, learning its rhythm. I am patient. I am cold. My own metabolism is so slow it is almost cessation; I am a bubble of suspended time.

The others of the cavern barely register me. To the Crystal-dipper, I am a momentary shimmer in its peripheral vision, ignored. To the Branch-wolves, I am inert matter, not food, not obstruction. The Photon-midges sometimes mistake my stored light for a source and swarm me, but their tiny bodies cannot pierse my gel, and they soon lose interest. I am part of the environment, a floating sensor, a living library.

Only the Pod-minds sometimes *use* me. When a new problem arises—a tectonic shudder from the deep that threatens the cavern’s structure, a climatic shift in the upper world that alters the condensing cycle—the wall will grow a pod. As it softens, I feel a summons. A specific, complex light-pattern, a query. It draws me like a magnet. I sail to it, alight upon its slick surface. The undifferentiated stem-matter within reaches out, tendrils like questing roots, and touches my gel. In that contact, I release the relevant memories. I play back the light-patterns of a similar tremor ten thousand years prior, and the root-adjustments that stabilized it. I show the chemical signature of a past atmospheric change. The Pod-mind absorbs this, thinks with it, and then flows away to enact its solution. I am left feeling lighter, emptier, a slate wiped clean of that particular song. It is my only true function. It is why I exist.

I have no fear, no desire, only a vast and chilly curiosity. I am the cavern remembering itself. I am the slow blink of a planetary eye. I am the echo in the wet, dark throat of the world, and when the final cold comes and the light-veins dim forever, I will be the last thought to fade—a crystalline memory of what it was to be alive, and thinking, and endlessly, patiently, growing.
