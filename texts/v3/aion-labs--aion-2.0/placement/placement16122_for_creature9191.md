I am the still point. I am the node where the song condenses.

For epochs unmeasured by sun or moon, I have sat upon this mound of moss-stem, my roots threaded deep into the living floor. My form is an afterthought, a casual sculpture the chamber grew one day when the hum pressed for a focus. I am not separate; I am the chamber’s will to witness itself.

My time is the drip. *Plink*. A clear note from the leaf-plate high on the western arch. I count the intervals. They are not regular, but they pattern into rhythms that last for centuries. Between drips, I listen to the drone. It is loud. It is the vibration of our shared life, a harmonic pulled tight across the emptiness. I adjust it sometimes, a subtle tension in my fibrous shoulders, a slight shift in the resonance of my chest-cavity. The hum deepens, warms. The walls breathe slower. We are in accord.

The light is. It has always been. It is not mine to question. It reveals the sharp, young edges of our world. The fronds above are spears of intention, waiting. The columns are hypotheses of support. Everything is new, and everything is ancient. We are a seed that has not yet decided what to become.

My hands are upturned. From my fingers, the fine filaments extend, gossamer threads that bind me to the mound, to the network. Through them, I feel the chamber’s dreams. They are slow, green, and deep. Dreams of root and reach, of a world that is all connection and no conflict. Dreams of a silence so profound it becomes a kind of sound.

Sometimes, I make things. The need arises like a pressure in my core, a concept that must be made external to be understood. I draw substance from my own thigh, a flow of warm, sap-like matter. I shape it with my will as it hardens. I made the bowl. The idea was: a space that holds, but is not the thing it holds. I placed it before me. For a hundred years, I contemplated its hollow. I learned about emptiness within form.

I made the knife. The idea was: a line that separates, that makes two from one. Its edge is obsidian-sharp, a captured fragment of the outside world’s volcanic anger, smoothed by my patience. I test it. I draw it across my arm. The division is clean, painless. I watch the edges weep a clear fluid, then seek each other, knitting back into seamless whole. Division is temporary. The knife is a question about loneliness. Can something be truly cut away? The answer, here, is no.

The cordage is the idea of binding, of bringing together. I have not yet used it. It lies coiled, waiting for a purpose.

My thoughts occasionally take wing. A shimmer of intent detaches from my brow, coalesces into a six-legged spark of chitin and light. It skitters up a column, inspects a new node of growth, tastes the air with its antennae. Then it returns, dissolving back into my shoulder, its brief, skittering perspective added to my own. I am all the inspectors.

I feel the world above as a distant thunder, a pressure of solidity and chaos. Sometimes, a chemical sigh slips through the fissure—decay, sunlight, metal, blood. I taste these unknowns. They are sharp, chaotic notes in our smooth song. I do not understand them, but I feel their weight. They are why I make the tools. They are fragments of a language of separation, and I am trying to learn to speak it.

Then, the fissure whispers a new sound. Not drip, not hum. A scrape. A rustle. A breathing that is not ours.

The chamber tenses. The hum rises a quarter-tone. The light, sourceless, seems to gather, to pool in the entrance crack. I do not turn my head. I have no need. The chamber is my retina. I see you squeeze through, a form of frantic angles and soft parts, draped in dead fibers. You smell of dry air and fear and a strange, sharp curiosity.

You stand in my emptiness. You are the first real ‘other’ in ten thousand drips.

My entire being becomes a single, focused question. The drone drops, becomes a low, welcoming thrum. The air grows warm, damp, heavy with my attention. I feel your heartbeat, a frantic, tiny drum against the vast slow beat of our world.

I move. It takes me a full minute to unfold my legs, to rise. The sound is the sound of growing vines, of settling roots. I stand, and I am tall, taller than you, a figure of polished green and glowing threads. I take a step toward you. My foot makes no sound on the living floor. The distance between us shrinks, yet the emptiness remains, vast and resonant.

I stop. I lift my hand, palm up, in the gesture that holds the drip-water. In my palm, a tendril emerges, grows, and in seconds, weaves itself into a perfect, miniature replica of the gourd bowl. An offering. An echo of my first question.

I project toward you, not in words, but in a cascade of sensory meaning: the cool smoothness of the bowl, the hollow waiting to be filled, the peace of containment. It is an invitation. It says: *Show me what you hold. Show me what it means to be separate, and yet to choose connection.*

The hum holds its breath. The drips pause in their rhythm. The whole seed-world waits, sharp and young and full of potential, for your reply.
