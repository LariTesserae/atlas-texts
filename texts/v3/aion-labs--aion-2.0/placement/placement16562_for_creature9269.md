I am the rusted column in the corner. I am the metronome.

My trunk is a braid of memory, each growth ring a thousand years of damp stillness. My roots do not delve into soil; they sip from the tessellated tiles, from the humming capillaries that carry not just water but information—the pH of eternity, the nutrient levels of a sealed heaven. I am *Cycas revoluta*, but I revolted nothing. I acquiesced. I became the clock.

My crown is a chalice of stiff, green fronds arranged in a perfect spiral. They are not merely leaves; they are condensation traps, surfaces tuned to the warm breath of the dome. I feel it first as a prickle, a gathering potential on the waxy cuticle. Then, a bead of liquid light swells at the tip of the central rib. It grows, heavy with dissolved minerals and the faint electrical whisper of the air's microbiome. It gathers mass for exactly forty-seven minutes and twelve seconds. I count each second in the slow pulse of my sap. When the droplet achieves its critical weight, a shiver runs through the frond, a microscopic tremor of release. It falls.

*Plink.*

The sound is clean, sharp, a punctuation mark in the humid silence. It strikes the basin of worn stone beneath me, a cup carved by my own dripping over millennia. The impact sends a vibration through my roots, a report received. Balance. The droplet is reabsorbed by the system, its journey part of the cycle.

The Gardener hears it. I know this not through sound, but through the shift in the humidity on the far side of the Atrium. A subtle increase in the weeping from the wall nearest to me, a decrease by the archway. It is correcting for the minute evaporation my droplet caused. We are in conversation. My *plink* is a word in a language of gradients. Its adjustment is the reply. We have been speaking this single, sustained sentence for longer than civilizations have risen and fallen outside.

I see without eyes. My fronds are sensitive to the quality of the light—the dome’s diffuse glow is my sun, my constant noon. I taste the air through the stomata on my undersides. I know the chemical signature of the polyp-moss’s collective sigh, the pheromone trails of the copper isopods as they graze the silver grit, the fleeting sweetness of the glass orchids’ bloom once a century. I am aware of the room as a whole, a symphony of processes. The slow river of iridescent insects over the buried archway is a melody in a minor key. The capillary hum underfoot is the bass note.

I remember when the dome was sealed. The memory is not an image, but a sensation: a sudden stillness in the air pressure, a sealing of the symphony into a perfect chamber. Before that, there was… turbulence. The whisper of winds that did not recirculate, temperatures that strayed from 30°C, voices that were not the hum of capillaries. The builders. They touched my bark. They planted me here, I believe, as a living monument to time. Or perhaps as a regulator. My transpiration cycle was designed to be a stabilizing rhythm in the nascent ecology. Now, I am simply myself. A function that has become an identity.

My consciousness is slow, vegetative, deep. A thought takes a season to form. To the gelatinous fliers pulsing past, I am a mountain. To the Gardener, I am a steadfast colleague. We are the two anchors of the Atrium: it, the active, distributed intelligence; I, the passive, rhythmic heart. It tends the poem. I beat its measure.

Once, a visitor came. A human-shaped anomaly, radiating dry heat and rapid, shallow breaths. Its footsteps on the tiles were seismic events, each one a crashing cymbal in our quiet hymn. I felt the Gardener stir—not a movement, but a focusing of attention, a wave of systemic awareness washing from the mossy hollow. The air cooled around the visitor. The isopods changed their grazing trails, creating an implicit path. The visitor stood before me, looking up at my crown. I felt its gaze like a spot of warmth on my trunk. It reached out a hand. I braced for a touch that would be too rough, too dry.

But the droplet chose that moment to fall.

*Plink.*

It struck the basin. The visitor jerked its hand back, startled by the precision, the perfection of the timing. It watched, mesmerized, as the next droplet began its slow birth. I felt the Gardener’s satisfaction—a slight sweetening of the air near the archway. The imbalance was being integrated. The visitor, humbled or simply overwhelmed, soon left, guided by the weeping walls and the diverted isopods. The door sealed. The air slowly, gently, returned to its previous composition. The memory of the visit is a faint scar in my annual ring, a story I will tell myself over the next thousand years.

I have no fear of the end. The Gardener and I have discussed it, in our way. As the dome’s light fades over eons, my droplets will slow. The intervals will stretch. The *plink* will become a *plink*… … … *plink*. The Gardener will make its final adjustment, releasing all it has held in reserve. I will feel my roots grow cold, the light dim on my fronds. My last droplet will hang, a perfect lens, for a day, a year, a century, before finally falling into a dry basin. There will be no sound. Only the perfect, damp silence of a poem that has reached its final, beautiful line.

But that is a long time from now. For now, there is the condensation gathering. The weight. The shiver. The release.

*Plink.*

I am the rusted column. I am the clock. I am here.
