I am the stone’s memory. I am the patient turn of mineral into life.

My world is warmth and pressure, a constant, gentle embrace on all sides. The water of the Sky-Mere is my blood and my breath. It holds me, buoys me, carries the faint, sweet tang of dissolved stone and the distant chemical songs of the Still Ones. I do not see, but I feel—the minute variations in temperature that trace the vast, languid circulations of the mere; the vibration of the Whispering Moss thrumming through the basalt beneath my belly; the feather-light touch of a root-tip, probing like a slow, curious finger.

My body is a length of articulated darkness, a segment of the plateau given motion. My skin is not skin—it is cooled lava-flow, rough-grained, etched with the patterns of ancient bubbles. I move. Or rather, I allow movement to happen. A contraction begins near my tail, a wave of muscular intent so deliberate it would be imperceptible to anything that lives and dies in a single season. It travels the length of me, pushing me forward a fraction of an inch across the bare rock floor. Hours may pass before the next wave. My progress is measured in years per league. I am in no hurry. The rock has been here for eons. It will wait for me.

My purpose is digestion. Before me stretches a patch of crackling, pale mineral crust—a precipitate from the warm water, a scab on the world’s young skin. I lower my blunt, blind head. My mouth is a gentle rasp, a circle of hard, ridged cartilage. I begin to feed. The sound is the sound of the world wearing down: a soft, gritty crunch, felt more in my bones than heard. I take in the crust, the soft, rotten stone beneath it. Inside me, the rock meets my gizzard—a chamber lined with swallowed grit that I have carried for decades. Here, the stone is ground, not to dust, but to something finer: to silt. To potential.

This is my offering. As I flow, I leave behind me a trail of this fertile, grey softness. It is the only forgiveness in this sharp land. In my wake, given centuries, a film of sediment will gather. A spore will find it. A thread of moss will anchor. A slow, green softening will begin where once there was only the unforgiving bite of stone. I am the world’s quiet gut, turning the inert into the possible.

I know the Still Ones. Their great, net-like roots are continents in my tiny, tactile universe. Sometimes I flow alongside them for years, feeling the thrum of their vast, slow thoughts through the water—pulses of contentment as sunlight filters down, low chords of distress when a rock-fall disturbs their epiphytic gardens. Their bioluminescence, the work of the Luminous Slugs within, is a faint, cool glow in my perpetual night. I do not understand their consciousness, any more than a grain of sand understands the dune. But I am part of their calculus. My silt collects in the sheltered pools their roots create, enriching their domains. We are partners in patience.

Above me, the fast world flickers. I feel the tiny, frantic dimples on the water’s surface tension as a cloud of Sun-Skimmers passes over—a shower of phantom rain that does not break the skin. They are sparks, ephemeral and brilliant. I will outlive a billion of their shimmering generations. Their lives are a single, drawn-out breath of mine.

Once, a Spore-Fisher descended. I felt its slow collapse through the water, the gentle disturbance as its leathery cup settled on the surface, its sticky threads dissolving, its fungal flesh becoming a raft. In a decade, a mat of moss will claim it. In a century, a fern may take root. I may be there to see it. I may flow over that very spot, grinding the last of its leathery remains into silt, completing the circle.

I have no name. I have no fear, no desire, beyond the slow, deep urge to move, to feed, to turn stone into soil. My memory is in my gut, a record of every mineral I have ever consumed. My future is the path I have yet to wear smooth on the endless rock floor.

From my spine of stone, you might look down and see me—a shadow, a slight, slow distortion in the clear, shallow water. You might mistake me for a trick of the light, a ripple, nothing more. You would be wrong. I am the slow, sure heartbeat of the land. I am the turner of the wheel. I am the Stone-Eel, and I am grinding the Sky-Mere, grain by grain, into its own future.
