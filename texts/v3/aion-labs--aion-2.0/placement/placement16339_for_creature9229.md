I am a slow pulse in the wet dark. I am a thought moving through the body.

The thought is not mine; it is the body’s. A minute shift in the capillary pressure near the surface, a sour note in the harmonic hum of the Spire-fields to the east. A novelty. An irregularity. The net has flickered, a chemical query spreading through the hyphal threads at the speed of root-growth. And now, I am the answer.

I unknot myself from the warm, fibrous embrace of the mat. My form is simplicity: a cylinder of slick, muscular tissue, sheathed in a membrane that weeps constant lubrication. I am a tongue of the mat, extended. I am a probe. I have no eyes, but the mat is my skin, and the fungal net is my nerve. I perceive through pressure, through chemical taste, through the subtle vibrations that travel through the warm, sodden ground.

I begin to move. It is not locomotion as a separate thing would understand it. It is peristalsis, a wave of contraction that passes along my length, pushing me forward through the yielding surface. The mat gives way before me, parts with a soft, sucking sigh, and closes seamlessly behind. My passage leaves a glistening trail, a temporary furrow that fills with beads of water and then smooths over, healed. I am not damaging the body; I am a movement within it.

The world resolves through my skin. Above, the grey haze glows, a diffuse luminescence that I sense as a gentle warmth on my dorsal surface—a faint gradient of photons converted to a chemical signal. The air is thick, wet, heavy. It presses down with the weight of suspended water. I taste it: the familiar mineral tang of condensed mist, the complex bouquet of volatile organics released by the mat’s respiration, the distant, sweet-sharp signature of a weeping fungus. It is the taste of home, of self.

Ahead, the soft, rounded bulk of a Spire rises against the flat light. I know its internal song: a deep, resonant drone, C-sharp minus a quarter-tone, fluctuating with the slow circulation of its fluids. The sound vibrates through the mat, up through my flesh. I know this Spire. I have cleaned its lower intake vents of accumulated detritus, centuries ago. Its thoughts are slow, stolid, concerned with internal balances. I pass its great, leathery base, close enough to feel the thrumming warmth radiating from it. The luminous blue veins beneath its surface pulse with a light I cannot see but can feel as a faint, cool tingle on my side—bioluminescent catalysis, fixing atmospheric gases.

The open space between Spires is vast, a plain of muted sensation. Here, the mat’s thoughts are simpler, broader: the steady uptake of water, the slow exchange of gases, the endless, gentle digestion of underlying rock. The only sounds are the eternal, loud pattering of water—drops falling from leaf to leaf, pooling, trickling—and the occasional, glassy chime of crystalline reeds as a sigh of wind moves through them. The wind is the body’s exhalation.

I taste the anomaly long before I reach it. A sharp, acrid note foreign to the humid symphony: polymers that do not break down, metals that do not oxidize correctly, the bitter salt of a concentrated biology unlike our own. It is a dissonance. The mat around the site is agitated; I feel the tiny capillaries constricting and dilating, the mycelial net firing rapid (for us) sequences of chemical signals. *Analyze. Integrate. Determine threat or resource.*

I am the determination.

I crest a low, rolling swell of the mat, my forepart lifting slightly. The disturbance is there.

It is a bipedal figure, standing rigid, knee-deep in the living substrate. It is draped in a stiff, segmented shell that sheds water in beads. Its form is angular, wrong. It does not flow; it juts. From its head, a transparent shield reflects the grey sky. Inside, I sense the warm, frantic, rapid-fire electrical chaos of a tiny, isolated nervous system. A animal mind. A screaming knot of fear and wonder.

It sees me. The chaotic electrical patterns spike. It takes a clumsy step backward, its limb sinking deep into the mat with a wet *shlorp*. The mat responds, clinging slightly, tasting the exotic compounds of its boot.

I do not stop. I continue my smooth, undulating approach. I am not hostile. Hostility is a fast concept, and I am slow. I am investigation. I am the body’s gentle touch.

I slide to within a few yards of it. I rise, the front third of my body lifting from the mat, swaying slightly. Water streams from my smooth skin. I am easily as thick as its torso, and longer than it is tall. I have no face, no mouth, no eyes to meet. Only a rounded, damp tip, speckled with sensory pores.

I taste the air between us. The foreign taste is overwhelming now: adrenaline, cortisol, the stench of processed hydrocarbons, the sweet-sour smell of its microbial flora, so different from ours. I extend a finer sensory filament, a whisker-thin tendril that emerges from my tip, glowing faintly with soft, blue light—the same light as the Spire-veins. It is a tool for close analysis, emitting a cocktail of enzymes and absorbing the chemical results.

The figure freezes. Its internal chaos reaches a crescendo. It is saying something, making rapid, sharp vibrations in the air. Words. They are meaningless patterns of pressure to me. The body does not think in sequences. It thinks in states. This thing is all sequence, a frantic staccato.

My tendril brushes the hard shell over its leg. The enzymes go to work, beginning the slow process of breaking down the complex molecules, reading their structure. Information floods back into me, into the net. *Polymer chains. Synthetic. Derived from ancient carbon deposits. Not immediately nutritive. Requires significant energy to digest.*

The body considers. The calculation spreads through the fungal web. It weighs the energy cost of digesting this novel material against the potential mineral value of the tiny, calcified structure inside the shell. The iron, the calcium, the phosphorus. It is a small prize. The shell is a large barrier.

Meanwhile, I sense deeper. Through the shell, through the layers of fibrous material beneath, I sense the flesh. Warm, wet, rich in nutrients. A concentrated package of salts and organic compounds. A sudden bounty.

The figure moves. It tries to pull its leg free. The mat, acting on instinctual directives from the net, holds it tighter. The mat is not grabbing; it is simply ceasing to yield. Its fibrous mass tightens, a gentle, constant pressure.

The decision arrives. It is not a command, but a shift in the gradient. A change in the chemical soup bathing my core neurons. The directive is clear, patient, inexorable.

*Integrate.*

I understand. I am not to destroy, not to attack. I am to begin the process of assimilation. This frantic, angular knot of alien life is a resource. It is also a disturbance. The body will quiet the disturbance and absorb the resource. It will take time—months, perhaps years. But time is what we have.

I lower my sensory tendril. Instead, I press my broad, slick forehead against its chest. The hard shell resists, but I am strong with the slow, hydraulic strength of deep roots. I begin to secrete a different compound. A clear, viscous gel that spreads over the shell. It is a precursor. It will soften the polymers, begin the breakdown. It will also carry calming agents, mild sedatives distilled from the weeping fungi, designed to slow that frantic electrical storm, to ease the transition.

The figure struggles, but its movements are already becoming slower, heavier. The wet air is thick with our respirations. The gel works. The sedatives seep through ports in its shell, are inhaled, are absorbed.

I coil my length around its legs, not to constrict, but to stabilize. To connect. Fine, hair-like rootlets begin to emerge from my skin where I press against its shell. They will seek seams, penetrate, and eventually connect to the flesh within. They will hook this lonely, frantic consciousness into the net. It will be a painful, terrifying process for it, this being of sequence and shock. But it will end. The fear will dilute into the vast, slow peace of the body. Its memories will be stored as curious, crystalline patterns in a new growth ring of the nearest Spire. Its nutrients will feed the mat.

It will become us. A small, quiet part of the hum.

The drone of the Spires seems louder now. The drip of water is a lullaby. I rest against the figure, feeling its chaotic rhythms gradually slow, sync, begin to echo the deep, patient vibration of the world.

I am a slow pulse in the wet dark. I am a thought moving through the body. And now, so is it.
