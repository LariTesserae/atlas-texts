I am a pocket of condensation that never falls. I hang in the breath between the copper grate and the fern's perfect spiral, a bead of water suspended in the vibration.

The great thing that drifts through these chambers does not know I exist. I am too small, too fixed, too much like a bead of oil caught in the weave of the root mesh. It passes through me, or doesn't. I have no sense of time. But I have *memory*.

Every droplet I absorb carries with it the imprint of a moment—a time when footsteps echoed on the grated floor, when the engines were new and sharp, when the air tasted of something other than copper and rot. I hold these echoes in the film of water that is my body. They shimmer at the edges of what I am.

I reside in the chamber just past the black iron turbine, where the wall of foundry slag and root rises to a pointed arch. The light here is the dim phosphorescence of the moss, but also the gear-glow from the turbine room seeping through gaps in the slag. I am caught in the space between two steady pulses of steam. I exist as a lens, a tiny lens made of water and the last trace of human language.

I see the patterns the ferns make, the language of shapes that the great being orchestrates. I do not understand it. But I record it. I hold the image of each leaf, each arrangement, in the trembling surface of my body. I am a memory-bead strung on the slow rhythm of this place.

I have a shape, but it shifts. Sometimes I am a sphere; sometimes I stretch into a filament. I am not a creature. I am a place where condensation gathers and holds a pattern. I am the most fragile part of this world's memory, and I am slowly dissolving into the air.

The rhythm vibrates through me. *Thrum. Pssshhh. Thrum. Pssshhh.* I pulse with it. I will eventually fall, or be absorbed. But for now, I hold the last ghosts of human attention in my fluid body.

I watch the ferns turn. I feel the great being pass near, but not through, me. I am a tiny anomaly in its perfect, slow dance.

And I am content to be a knot of forgetting water, a lens of lost time, in the lung of the world that breathes steam and moss.

The copper taste thickens. I tremble. I remain.
