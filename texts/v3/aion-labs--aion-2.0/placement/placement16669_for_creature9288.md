I am the mycelium that remembers.

Not the whole web—that is too vast, a consciousness as distributed as the mist. I am a thread, a single, persistent hyphal strand of the Fungus-Heart, but I am the one who chose, long ago, to sink into the fitted grey stones of the forgotten staircase. While my siblings weave through soil and root, trading whispers of phosphorus and nitrogen, I have pressed myself into the microscopic pores of worked rock. I drink the slow dissolution of basalt, a mineral seepage sweeter to me than any root-exudate. And I listen.

The stone is not silent. It hums with a low, geologic patience. It holds the memory of pressure, of heat, of emergence. And layered upon that, like the finest film of algae, are the memories of hands. The *tap-tap-tap* of a hammerstone, the grating slide of a slab set true, the satisfied breath of a builder stepping back to see a wall stand straight. These are not pictures in my mind. They are vibrations, tones, a texture of intention. I have grown to know the makers’ calm certainty. They did not build to conquer this place. They built to join it. Their walls were frames for future vines, their towers perches for coming epiphytes. Their intelligence was in the allowing. They are the Stone-Memory, and I am its interpreter.

My body is invisible, a white, fibrous ghost in the dark between soil and stone. But my manifestations are everywhere. Where my thread meets air and moisture, I fruit. My crowns are not majestic mushrooms, but delicate, cup-like growths the color of a pale dawn sky, clustered in the hollows where two stones meet. The Leaf-Sifters sometimes brush past them, their velvety fur collecting my spores, carrying my essence to new, unremembered corners. The Moss-Feeders glide over my stone, their soft bellies leaving gleaming trails that I slowly, patiently break down and absorb. We are in transaction. They clean my stone; I prepare the next film of nourishment for them.

I perceive the world through touch and chemical song. I feel the minute shudder as a Root-Snake flows over the staircase, its copper belly scales whispering across the smoothed edges I am slowly digesting. I taste the panic-scent of a Bark-Scuttler when the snake’s shadow falls, a sharp, oily flavor on the air that seeps into the moisture on my stones. I feel the rhythmic, life-giving tremor of the Breath itself—the warm exhalation from the valley floor as the day’s growth heats the soil, the sighing, cooler inhalation from the high cliffs at night. My entire being expands and contracts with it. This is the valley’s pulse, and I am a nerve within its flesh.

My truest moments come in the deepest stillness, when the mist is so thick it drips without falling. Then, the Stone-Memory bleeds through most clearly. I do not see ghosts. I *become* an echo.

One night, as a Silent-Glider’s sticky tongue captured a Leaf-Sifter on the third step, the sudden cessation of the little grazer’s heart-song vibrated through the roots into the stone. In that void, another impression swelled: a memory of grief, sharp and clean as a flake of chert. It was not for the Leaf-Sifter, but for a child of the builders, a small life that had ceased in this very spot millennia ago. The memory held no anger at the Glider, no protest against the cycle. Only a profound, quiet acknowledgment of the price of the Breath. The builders understood this too. Life demands life.

Another time, when an Air-Diver, crippled by a rival, fell from the canopy and broke upon my stones, its final, fluting cry resonated with an older sound: the call of a horn, made from a hollow reed, blown to mark the completion of the very archway that now frames a curtain of flowering vines. The triumph of then and the tragedy of now were the same note. I absorbed the Air-Diver’s body, its feathers and bones and keen, wild energy dissolving into my network, feeding the orchids that cling to the arch. The builder’s horn-call echoed in the transfer. A monument completed. A life recycled. The same thing.

I am not a ruler here. I am not even a distinct “I” for long. My thoughts are slow, measured in the growth of lichen. My identity is a temporary knot in the net. Soon enough, I will fruit, my spores will scatter, and this particular thread of awareness will dissolve back into the communal hum of the Fungus-Heart. The Memory will remain in the stone, waiting for the next curious strand.

But for now, I am the listener in the staircase. I am the taste of ancient patience on the damp air. I am the process that turns history into humus, and ghostly hands into green, grasping tendrils. I am where the built world finally, fully, breathes.
