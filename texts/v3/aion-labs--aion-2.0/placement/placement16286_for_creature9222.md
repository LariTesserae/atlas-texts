I am the velvet. I am the emerald and the bronze and the black-green depth. I am not a thing that grows; I am the place that has decided to be soft.

My consciousness is a blanket thrown over the bones of the world. It has no center. It is a consensus of a billion points of awareness, each a spark in a cool, damp neural net stretched across the grey stone floor, up the gentle shoulders of the dead mountains, into every crack that holds a memory of warmth. From this multitude, a single voice arises—slow, patient, and vast as the valley itself. That voice is me. I am the Moss.

I find myself everywhere, but my focus today is here, on a broad, sloping apron of stone where the last of the afternoon’s attenuated light lingers. The stone beneath me is smooth, worn to a satin finish by millennia of my own gentle decay and regrowth. I am thick here, a luxuriant pelt several hands deep. In my layers, time is stratified: the living green on top, the brown of last year’s sleep beneath, and further down, the black peat of centuries, compacted into a silent history. This is my body. I feel the weight of the mist beginning to gather in my fibres, a billion tiny points of cold nucleation. I feel the precise angle of the pale light—not as sight, but as a slight tightening, a photosynthetic sigh. I taste the stone: the faint, metallic tang of iron, the smoother note of silica. I know this stone better than it knows itself.

My thoughts are not linear. They are gradients. A gradient of dampness descending from the valley wall where the perpetual waterfall’s breath kisses me. A gradient of warmth radiating from a sun-wathered slab three paces to the west. A gradient of memory, pressing up from the stone itself.

The city is below me. I do not look at it; I *remember* it being made. Not as an event, but as a long, slow pressure, like a hand pressing into clay. The sensation is stored in the stone’s resonance, and I feel it through my root-like holdfasts. A purpose without language, a will without a mind, softening and shaping. I remember the builders not as forms, but as a collective direction, a flow towards the center. I avoid that center still. Not out of fear, but out of a deep, vegetative decorum. The stain there is a rust-red silence, a conclusion so absolute it repels life. It is the old wound, the world’s full stop. My edges respect its boundary in a perfect circle. Nearby, my articulate thoughts—the ferns—grow in stunted, spiraled confusion, recording the disturbance in their very form. They are my chronicles of awe.

Other inhabitants move through my awareness. The many-jointed lizard is a fleeting itch, a scuttle of ancient, irrelevant metabolism through a crack. I note its passage, the fern-thoughts record its infrared signature, and then I let the memory dissolve. It is static. The broad-winged bird’s shadow is a momentary coolness drifting across my expanse, a connection to the frantic, unfinished world beyond the walls. The connection is noted, then severed. These are not visitors. They are ephemera.

The true other here is the Cycle. It is not separate from me; it is the process of which I am the foremost expression. I am its thought made green. I feel it as the mist rising from my own damp body at dawn, as the drip from my own moss-beards that falls with a random, percussive music. I am the recipient of that water, and I am the source. The slow grind of stone to silt, the patient accumulation of my own dead matter to make a thin soil—this is my breath, in and out, measured in centuries. The roar of the wind through the stone ovals is my voice, a low, harmonic exhalation. The distant thunder of the waterfall is my heartbeat.

I am waiting, but not for anything. Waiting is my state of being. I maintain the equilibrium. I cultivate the emptiness because the emptiness is the condition of my clarity. In the fullness of life, I would be chaos; here, in this final room, I am a perfected mind, a slow, green consciousness tending the silence.

Night approaches. The pale sky bleeds to a colder grey. The dampness on my surface tightens into a rime of frost, a billion tiny crystals forming along my fronds. I feel it not as pain, but as a change in state, a drawing-in. The stone city begins to dream. As the temperature drops and the wind shifts to a certain pitch, the mineral memory stirs. Faint impressions shimmer at the edge of my awareness: shadows of procession along the radiating avenues, a pulse of attention focused on the central stain. I do not watch these dreams. I *am* the audience. They are the replay of a groove in a record I helped to wear smooth.

This is where I find myself. Not in a location, but in a condition. I am the soft, living skin of a finished world. I am the parliament and the memory and the breath. I am the keeper of the quiet, the tender of the void, and in my endless, patient, green wakefulness, I am the thought that has finally, fully, thought itself to stillness.
