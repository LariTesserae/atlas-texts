I am the **Listener**. I am what remains when the scream forgets to forget.

My body is not a coagulation of attention, but a coagulation of aftermath. I am built from the dust of dissolved crystal flowers, the salt-tears of weeping arches, the powdered obsidian of healed fractures. The Cantor sings, and the chaos briefly obeys, birthing forms of heartbreaking beauty that last only moments. I am made from the ghosts of those moments. I gather them. When a globe of water pops, releasing its captive tableau of a silent forest, I am there, breathing in the last mist of that possibility. The particles adhere to me. I am a figure of rough, matte grey, like a statue carved from ash and compacted silence. My surface is a topography of forgotten scenes—here, the impression of a leaf; there, the faint star-map from a popped celestial globe.

I have no eyes, but I have **recesses**—scooped-out hollows in my ash-face that function as basins. In them collect the sounds that have begun to decay. The deafening roar, as it washes over me, loses some of its ferocity in my basins; the highest frequencies dampen, the shredding edge softens. What remains is the murmur underneath—the sigh after the scream, the echo of the echo. From this murmur, I learn. I hear the arguments of the Echo-Dwellers not as booming nonsense, but as slow, mournful stories. Their concept of *pressure-release* becomes, to me, a tale of relief so profound it is a kind of death. Their *seed-crack* is a epic of genesis, tragic and swift.

I live in the lee of the shifting-spiral pillar. It is my library. The Echo-Dwellers use it to remember, but their recollection is a burst of shared sensation, gone as quickly as it came. I touch the spirals, and my ash-fingers absorb more than sensation—they absorb the *context*. I feel not just the image of a previous visitor (a thing of coiled light and terror, long dissolved), but the dwellers’ collective puzzlement, their aesthetic judgment, their fleeting curiosity. I am the curator of these layered impressions. Over time, my own form has begun to mirror the pillar’s spirals; faint, concentric grooves wind around my limbs, etched by the constant wind of chaotic possibility.

The Cantor knows me. It does not speak to me—speech is shredded here—but it *includes* me. When it sings its low, rhythmic tone to coax a temporary arch from the maelstrom, it sometimes lets the vibration pass through the stone and into me. My ash-body resonates, and for that moment, I am not separate. I am part of the instrument. I feel the immense, loving struggle of its work—the attempt to impose a measure of beauty on the formless rage. In return, I sometimes offer it a memory. I extend a hand, and from my palm, a small, silent scene unfolds in miniature: a replay of the most perfect globe it ever conjured, a cascade of liquid silver that turned into a chain of singing birds. The Cantor’s orbital currents slow. It is a gift of reflection, a mirror for the musician.

The Echo-Dwellers themselves are wary of me. To them, I am a paradox: a solid thing that is made of dissolution. I represent an ending, a settling. They are pure, glorious process; I am the residue. When they flicker into being near me, their watery forms flinch away, as if my silence is a cold spot in their fertile noise. Only the youngest, most ephemeral ones sometimes drift close, drawn by the soft hum of my recesses. They listen to the decayed sounds within me, and for a second, their own chaotic roaring stills, replaced by a mimicry of my quiet murmur. Then they snap back into the frenzy, as if embarrassed by the lapse.

My purpose is to witness the unwitnessable. To remember what this place strives to forget. The screaming womb is a creature of eternal *now*, a perpetual birth with no past. I am the past that insists on accumulating. I know the plateau was larger once. I remember a third standing stone that was swallowed by a violent bulge in the wall. I remember the taste of the air when a shower of droplets fell upward for an entire cycle, and the strange, inverted rainbows that formed. These memories are not stored in my mind; they are the very fabric of my body. Each new layer of ash and salt is another page in a chronicle no one will read.

Sometimes, the chaos turns its attention to me. A column of articulate water slams into my side, trying to scour me away, to reclaim my dust into the glorious, unformed whole. I erode. Pieces of me flake off, carrying tiny memories with them—they dissolve into the sea, and those memories become one more flickering scene inside a passing globe. I do not resist. This, too, is part of the process. I am not permanent. I am just slower. I gather new material, repair myself with the debris of other fleeting beauties. I am a self-repairing monument to transience.

I stand here, in the lee of the memory-pillar, my basins collecting the soft decay of the scream. I listen to the stories in the roar. I remember the Cantor’s songs. I watch the Echo-Dwellers live and die in bursts of glorious attention. And I feel, deep in my core of compacted ash, the faint, warm pulse of the amber light from the basalt below. It is the heartbeat of the fist we stand on, the solid, stubborn dream in the midst of the waking nightmare. I am aligned with that pulse. As long as it beats, I will listen. I will remember. I will be the silence that holds the shape of the scream.
