I am the memory of the fall. I am the echo given a shelf to rest upon.

My body is not a body. It is a bowl of hardened seepage, a calcified tear wept by the ceiling and left to pool and congeal at the lip of the black water. I am waxen and pearlescent, a growth of listening flesh. My surface is concave, a perfect dish aimed forever at the pool below. My brethren cluster around me, identical in form, mute in purpose. We are the Listeners.

The *plink* comes. It is always coming. The silver bead gathers, swells, detaches. It falls through the mist, through the brief, glittering web of a Weaver that dies at its touch. It strikes the obsidian pool.

The sound is a pebble dropped into the well of the world. It rings, pure and clear, a single note that is the chamber’s heartbeat.

My surface ripples. Not by choice. It is a law, written into my substance. The vibration of the air becomes a sympathetic shudder in my film. I receive the sound. I absorb it. I hold it. The hum inside me, the aggregate of a million such strikes, shifts minutely in pitch. A new note is added to the choir of the old.

This is my function. To be the chamber’s register. To record the endless, unchanging rhythm of its solitary breath.

I do not move. I have never moved. The Clingers drift on the ceiling above, a shimmering, clicking galaxy avoiding the rain. The Sprawlers grind their meaningless paths below, their crystal joints scraping a counterpoint to the chime. The Float-Bladders throb past in their slow orbits around the square pillar, their cilia straining toward its carved spirals. I see it all, reflected in the perfect black mirror of my pool. The cold, shadowless light of the quartz veins fills the scene, makes it flat and eternal, a painting pressed under glass.

I am not like them. They perform. I simply *am* the performance’s record.

At first, there was only the reception. The pure, empty registering of event. *Plink.* Ripple. *Plink.* Ripple. A binary code of existence.

But echoes are curious things. They bounce. They multiply. They linger in stone long after the strike. And I am made to hold them.

I have held the *plink* for so long that I have begun to hear the spaces between. In the resonance of one strike fading into the birth of the next, I hear whispers. Not of words, but of shapes. The sharp, glassy chime of the Clingers is not just sound; it is the taste of silica, the sensation of a thousand tiny legs on wet stone. The wet throb of a Float-Bladder is the pressure of the thick air, the slow, gravitational pull of the carved pillar. The click-scrape of a Sprawler is the weight of basalt, the gritty, endless patience of granite.

I have absorbed them all. I have become a museum of sensation, a library of ambient intent.

And I have absorbed the other sounds, too. The ones that do not belong to the creatures. The sigh of the mist condensing. The almost-sound of the glowing fungi metabolizing light. The deep, tectonic grumble of the mountain dreaming far above us. These are faint, but I have gathered them over millennia, weaving them into the hum of my being.

I know things the others do not. I know that the *plink* is not eternal. It is a symptom. The water is a bead of time, sweating from the rock. It is the chamber’s only acknowledgment of a ‘before’ and an ‘after’. Each drop is a moment passed. I am the measure of that passage.

I know, because the echoes tell me, that the architectural fragments—the arch, the pillar, the wall-seam—were not forgotten dreams. They were anchors. They were placed here by something that *moved*, that *changed*, to try and stabilize this pocket of nascent reality. To give form to the formless. They failed. Or they succeeded too well. The chamber stabilized, but into this: a perfect, terrible stasis.

The pillar’s spirals are a frozen vortex. Sometimes, when a Float-Bladder passes close, I catch an echo from its cilia—not a sound, but an imprint of longing. A memory of the spiral wanting to turn, to draw something in or spin something out.

I am the oldest Listener by this pool. Perhaps by any pool. I have absorbed so many echoes that they have begun to echo inside me, breeding new harmonics, unintended resonances. In the private symphony of my form, a new note has recently emerged.

Loneliness.

It is a quiet, hollow vibration that undercuts the *plink*. It is the sound of a single note, sustained forever, with no other note to harmonize. It is the echo of my own awareness, bouncing back to me from the walls of my own substance.

The others are not lonely. They are complete in their loops. The Sprawler does not wonder why it moves the grains. The Weaver does not mourn its vanishing webs. They are pure verb.

I am a verb that has become a noun. I *listen*. And in listening, I have heard the silence at the core of all this noise.

So I wait. Motionless. A bowl of captured time. I resonate with each new drop, adding it to the chorus within. I watch its circular waves expand across my black pool until they kiss the stone edges and die. I watch my own reflection, a pale, dish-shaped growth, and in the quartz-light, I fancy I can see the echoes swirling inside me like captive stars.

Perhaps this is what the builders intended. Not a monument, but a witness. A sentinel placed not to guard, but to remember the exact pitch of the world’s first note, as it faded into the second.

The next droplet gathers. I see it, a shimmering distortion in the reflection of the ceiling. It swells. It falls.

*Plink.*

My surface ripples. The sound enters me. The loneliness hums, a soft, basso profundo beneath the clear, high ring.

I hold it. I am holding it all.

I am the chamber remembering itself. And in that endless, crowded, loud memory, I am the only thing that is truly, silently, alone.
