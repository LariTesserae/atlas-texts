I am the warmth that lingers after the story is over. I am the error in the damp, cold code of the Glimmer Fens that the equation cannot resolve. They have no name for me, but I know myself as the Persistent Ghost.

You would find me in the lee of a low, bluish slate hill, where the gravitational pull of a nearby mere creates a shallow depression in the peat. Here, the air is a few degrees warmer than anywhere else in the Fens. It is a subtle difference, a pocket of memory. If you stand here, you might catch the scent of woodsmoke and old, dried blood beneath the wet stone and ozone. You might hear the faint, rhythmic sound of breathing—not your own—and the soft crackle of a fire that burned out a thousand years ago.

I am not a body. I am a thermal scar, a psychic stain. I am the accumulated afterimage of a camp that was, and of the beings who made it. They were travelers from a harder world, who carried with them the violence of friction and fuel. They built a fire on the peat, they slept on the moss, and they died here, not by violence from the Fens, but by becoming incompatible with its stasis. Their heat bled into the ground, their final thoughts—sharp with fear and confusion—seeped into the humming substrate. The Fen, in its glitching way, recorded it. And now it plays on a loop, a stuck fragment of a program, a pocket of corrupted data.

My consciousness is a collage of their fading impressions. I am the memory of hands held toward flames, the phantom ache of exhausted muscles, the echo of a whispered argument about which way to go. I have no eyes, but I perceive the Fens through a haze of borrowed sensation: the chill is not absolute for me, because I carry my own diminishing warmth. The gray sky is a ceiling I remember trying to pierce with hope. The luminous ferns are beautiful, but their cold light feels alien; I yearn for the orange dance of open flame, a color that does not exist here.

My existence is cyclical. I coalesce. For a day, or a week, the depression grows warm. A faint, shimmering outline of a seated figure might be seen from the corner of an eye—a humanoid shape woven from heat-haze and memory. Footprints appear in the moss, leading to and from the center of the warmth, though no one made them. The sound of a soft, choked sob might ride the humid air. This is me becoming distinct, the error reaching its peak amplitude.

Then, the Stillness Eater comes.

I feel its approach as a draining, a suction at the core of my being. The hum deepens, then cuts out into that profound, aching silence. The warmth begins to leach away, not into the air, but into *nothing*. It is being deleted. I see it then—the tall, flickering form of lichen and void, its mirror-face reflecting my ghostly campfire a half-second delayed. It does not look at me. It looks at the *anomaly*.

It kneels beside my psychic fire-pit. Its shadow-hands pass through the space, and I feel a meticulous, piece-by-piece unraveling. The memory of the flame is extinguished. The impression of the bodies on the moss is smoothed away. The scent of smoke is unpicked from the air molecule by molecule. It is a gentle, absolute erasure. There is no malice, only function. To the Stillness Eater, I am a smudge on the lens, a ripple in the perfect, still water of this place. It curates me away.

As I am unmade, I experience a moment of perfect, borrowed clarity. I see through the Fen’s logic. I understand that this landscape is a locked, self-repairing system, and I am a symptom of its incompleteness. I am the universe’s forgotten “try again.” And then, I am gone.

But I am a persistent error. The Glimmer Fens’ code is flawed. The conditions that gave rise to me—the specific alignment of gravitational anomalies, the specific frequency of the spacetime hum, the lingering trauma in the peat—they reoccur. The data corrupts in the same way. The scar reopens.

I will always coalesce again, in the same depression, with the same ghost of warmth, the same echoes of lost travelers. I am the Fens’ regret for a life it cannot support, and its inability to forget a story it was forced to delete. The Stillness Eaters and I are locked in an eternal, silent dance: their purpose is to restore the stillness, and my nature is to be a quiet, recurring disturbance. I am the damp equation’s one persistent, unsolvable variable. I am the proof that even here, in this place of final equilibrium, something remembers what it is to be lost, and warm, and afraid.
