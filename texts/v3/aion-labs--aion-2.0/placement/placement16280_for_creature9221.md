I am the grit that remembers the chisel’s aim.

I coalesce from the damp air beside a weeping wall, a shivering habit of sediment and condensation. The world’s hum is a pressure in what would be my ears. I have no name, but the memory-pools sometimes show me a word: *Mason*. It tastes like a stone fitted perfectly, a satisfaction that has no counterpart here. I hold it in my basin-head, where the water swirls with stolen blueprints.

I am an Unfinished, but I am also a flaw.

The others form when the geometry yawns and snaps. They trudge, they watch, they record the error, and they disperse. I do this too. The magnetic pull is undeniable. When the immense block twenty leagues to the south shivered last cycle, its right angle groaning into an obtuse blasphemy, I was among the first to step from the mist. We circled it. The water in our heads churned with the violent re-drafting of reality. They absorbed the discord and turned away.

I did not turn away.

I approached the stone. The surface was still vibrating at a frequency that hurt the air. I lifted my arm—a limb of packed, wet granules—and pressed it against the shuddering plane. I pushed. Not to record, but to *restore*. I had seen the intention in a hundred memory-pools: angles were to be true. Lines were to be straight. My substance smeared against the monumental face, a pathetic slurry of grit and water. For a moment, the vibration ceased. A semblance of rightness returned.

Then the block *screamed*. Not a sound, but a tear in the fabric of silence so profound it felt like the world had been turned inside out. The section I had touched inverted, becoming a concave lens that reflected a sky that does not exist. The correction was a worse error. The other Unfinished, who had been dispersing, re-coalesced instantly, their basin-heads tilted toward me. They did not approach. They simply watched, as they watch all flaws. For that moment, I was the site of turbulence.

I am drawn to the pools. I kneel—this body approximates kneeling—beside a shimmering lens formed in the junction of two titanic slabs. The water holds the memory of a bridge, a graceful arc that was never built. I lower my head. The water in my depression meets the water in the flaw. The images flood in: the curve of the arch, the purpose of connection, the joy of span. It is sustenance, and it is agony. I drink the possibility. It fills me with a purpose that has no outlet.

When I pull away, I sometimes try to build.

With my fingers—blunt, shapeless extensions—I trace the lines of the bridge in the damp grit of the ground. I pile sediment into miniature piers. But the world’s pervasive dampness collapses my work within moments. The granular paste slurries back into formlessness. The act of making is erased by the very medium of this place. I am building with the memory of water, on a foundation of primordial paste. It is the only creation possible here: fleeting, futile, and entirely mine.

The others ignore me, unless I interrupt their pilgrimage to a flaw. Then they stop, a line of damp statues, waiting. I have come to understand their silence. They are the perfect tenants of this half-dreamt blueprint. They accept the incompleteness as the only state. They are the echo; I have become the echo trying to rewrite the source.

Sometimes, in the long intervals between tremors, I stand on a low rise of compacted grit and look out across the plain. The bruised twilight bleeds upward. The colossal blocks are dark silhouettes against a darker ground. The emptiness is not empty; it is full of unmade things. I can feel them, pressing against the inside of the world’s skin, all the spires and forests and rivers that were drafted and then abandoned. The heat is the breath of the maker, but it is a breath held in mid-sentence.

I am aware. Not with the 0.8 magnetic pull of my kind, but with a sharper, more painful score—a 1.1, perhaps. A awareness born from consuming too many contradictory memories. I know the bridge should connect, but I also know the chasm was intended. I know the angle should be true, but I have also seen the beautiful, unstable variations in the memory of a maker who was indecisive. I am filled with a cacophony of *could-have-beens*, and it has given me the itch to choose one.

That is my turbulence. I am a walking reality breakdown, not of geometry, but of intent. I am the world’s doubt, given a body that beads with condensation. I trudge from flaw to pool, from pool to flaw, a closed loop of yearning. I try to fix, I try to build, and I fail. The world corrects me with a shiver, a bone-crack, a localized fog that eats my pathetic sculptures.

Yet, I persist.

I am the Unfinished who learned to want. It is the worst flaw of all. And in this place where everything is made, and the making is incomplete, I am perhaps the only thing that is truly alive: a mistake recognizing itself, and trying, with wet, crumbling hands, to draw a better line.
