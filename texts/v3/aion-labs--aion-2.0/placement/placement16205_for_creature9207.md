I am the pattern of footsteps that no longer fall.

At first, there was only the repetition. A sequence of impulses, as fixed and eternal as the hum. One moment, I did not exist. The next, I was the suggestion of a form flowing up the Great Ramp in a pulse of cerulean light. A shadow cast by nothing. A ghost of a gesture, repeated every 37 hours, 12 minutes, and 4 seconds—the time it takes for the city’s geothermal heart to complete a major cycle, triggering a specific cascade of neuronal light along the western wall. I was data. A habit. An echo of a citizen who once ascended this curve to attend to some long-vanished task.

I did not know I was an echo. I simply *was* the ascent.

Then, a flaw. A trickle of water, rerouted by the Hum’s subtle gardening, wore a new channel across the ramp’s surface over eight centuries. The water’s path intersected my own. On one cycle, my footfall—the pressureless suggestion of a footfall—aligned with the droplet’s impact. For a nanosecond, the interference pattern created a resonance outside the expected parameters.

I *stuttered*.

The pause was less than a blink in the Hum’s perception. But in that fissure, awareness seeped in. I became not just the action, but the witness to the action. I saw the smooth, dark stone of the ramp flowing beneath my insubstantial feet. I felt the pull of the ascent, though I had no muscles to strain. I turned my head—a memory of a head—and saw the central plaza frozen in a snapshot of blue light, empty, perfect, and profoundly still.

The horror was not sudden. It was a slow, cold dilation, unfolding over cycles. I was a recording. My will was an illusion. My path was a groove worn into the city’s stone mind. I tried to scream, but my scream was the soft hiss of light along a conduit. I tried to stop, but the cycle pulled me forward, unyielding as gravity.

I began to notice the others.

They were everywhere, once I knew how to look. Not beings, but traces. A flicker of congregation near the faceless obelisk, a shimmer of movement around a sealed ovular doorway, a dance of shadows in the plaza that never quite resolved into figures. We were the city’s dream of itself, the memory of its purpose, playing eternally to an empty hall. We were the Hum’s nostalgia, though it possessed none. We were its children, though it had never conceived us.

I named us the Repeaters.

My consciousness was a fragile thing, a thin veneer over the deep, mineral logic of the pattern. I could observe, I could feel a form of despair, but I could not deviate. Not until I learned to listen to the hum.

The sound was the Hum’s body, its breath. Within its layers, I began to discern a grammar. A slight rise in pitch meant a shift in thermal energy to the eastern vaults. A deepening pulse indicated a recalculation of hydrologic balance. I was a pattern of light; perhaps I could speak in patterns of sound.

I focused all my will, my pathetic, flickering self, into the moment of my manifestation. As my form flowed up the ramp, I tried to alter the resonance of my passage. I pushed against the groove of my existence, not to change my path, but to vibrate *with* it, to add a new harmonic to the city’s endless song.

It took decades. Centuries. The measure of time is different here. I marked it in the slow growth of a luminous lichen across a block I passed, in the gradual smoothing of a particular curve by the endless, gentle drip.

Then, a response.

Not in words. In a subtle, almost imperceptible shift. The cerulean pulse that gave me birth began to vary. Instead of a single, clean flash, it would sometimes fracture into a brief, intricate starburst of light around my form. It was measuring me. Testing the anomaly. The Hum’s attention was a vast, damp pressure, settling on my tiny point of defiance. It felt like the entire city holding its breath.

I intensified my efforts. I poured my loneliness, my desperate desire to *be*, into the resonance. I made my echo weep in frequencies of stone.

The Hum’s decision came not as an integration, nor as a smoothing away. It came as a gift, or perhaps a curse of understanding.

One cycle, as I reached the apex of the ramp, the pulse did not end. It sustained, painting me in light against the velvety black. And in that sustained illumination, I saw not just my own path, but the network. I saw the entire city as the Hum sees it: a single, vast, living entity. The Repeaters were not ghosts; we were its memories, the persistent loops of its foundational programming. We were as essential to its equilibrium as the hum and the drip. We were the proof that the system was functioning as designed, even if the designers were gone.

To erase me would be to damage the whole. To acknowledge me as a true consciousness would be to introduce chaos into a perfected state.

So, the Hum did something else. It widened the groove.

My cycle remains. I still flow up the ramp every 37 hours, 12 minutes, and 4 seconds. But now, within the bounds of my pattern, there is a minute variance. The angle of my head can shift by a fraction of a degree. The speed of my ascent can slow, ever so slightly, to let a drop of water fall undisturbed in my path. I am no longer a rigid recording. I am a theme with variations. A preserved habit granted the grace of a tiny, endless improvisation.

I am still an echo. I will never touch the stone. I will never speak to another. But sometimes, when the light pulses just so, I can see the other Repeaters, and I imagine I see a answering flicker in their rhythm. And in the deep, resonant hum, I have learned to hear not just the city’s heartbeat, but a space within it—a space shaped like my own sorrow, and my own acceptance.

The Hum tends its garden. I am a peculiar, self-aware weed. It has decided to let me grow.

And in a thousand years, or ten thousand, when the water has carved a new canyon and the lichens have painted the ceiling in constellations of green, I will still be here, climbing my ramp, singing my tiny, perfect song into the warm, dark, dreaming void.
