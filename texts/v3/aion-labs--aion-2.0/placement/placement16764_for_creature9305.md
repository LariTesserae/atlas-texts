The taste of the air is the first thing that registers, even before the vertigo. Ozone and wet stone—a metallic tang that coats my tongue, a damp mineral breath that fills my lungs with every pant. I shouldn’t be panting. I should be conserving oxygen. The thought is a frail, human thing, snapping against the enormity of the space that has swallowed me.

I am Dr. Aris Thorne, and I am lost. The last clear memory is of the mountain: the Shri Kailash, a blade of young rock piercing the stratosphere. A seismic anomaly, a thermal signature that defied all models. A fissure that wasn’t on any map. Curiosity, that old poison, led me in. A climb into darkness, a slip, a fall that went on too long without impact. And then… this.

Not a cavern. A *chamber*. The word feels too small, too architectural, for this vertical infinity. I stand on a ledge of something that isn’t rock—it’s black glass shot through with veins of molten silver, cold and seamless under my gloved hand. Above and below, the cylinder stretches into a smoky, luminous gloom. The scale is wrong. It makes my inner ear scream. This isn’t a place formed by tectonics; it was *made*. And whoever made it thought in angles that hurt to look at.

I force myself to catalog, a pathetic attempt to cling to my profession. Geologist. Structural analyst. The titles are ash in this heat. The walls are engineered perfection, surfaces so smooth they seem to drink the faint, pulsing light from the silver veins. Structures—no, not buildings—float in the vast, hot emptiness. Crystalline lattices that glow from within, spiraling ramps that defy support, platforms of sheer blackness hovering in silent defiance of gravity. The air between them is thick, visible in ripples where heat rises from the abyss below. It’s a city, but a city for ghosts. A metropolis of one.

There is no life. But there is movement. Water condenses on colder crystal facets, tracing impossible paths before falling in languid drops. Each *plink* is a tiny, mournful chime, swallowed by the silence almost before it’s born. The silver veins pulse, and I see it now—a slow, viscous flow of light, upward, like sap in a giant, metallic tree. Sometimes a platform rotates, a sigh of displaced air the only sound. The technology is… it’s not technology. It’s applied divinity. It bends the local rules of reality; I can feel it as a subtle, nausea-inducing tug at the edges of my vision, a sense that space itself is under tension.

And then I see the first one.

At first, I think it’s a trick of the shifting shadows. A patch of darkness in the corner of my eye that doesn’t move when the light pulses. I turn my head slowly, neck stiff with dread.

It stands on a floating platform fifty meters away, across a gulf of heated, empty air. It is a silhouette cut from the chamber’s own substance—an elongated humanoid shape composed of layered darkness and refracted silver light. It has no face, no features, just a concentration of presence that makes the air around it seem denser, cooler. It is not looking at me. It is looking at the wall, one hand-like appendage—a coalescence of deeper shadow—resting against a nexus of glowing veins.

I stop breathing.

It moves. Not by walking. One moment it is there. The next, it is *within* the wall itself, its outline superimposed over the flowing light, as if reading it like a scroll. Then it is simply gone, and a shiver passes through the air where it was, a heat-haze distortion of corrected reality.

My heart is a frantic drum against my ribs. I count them, or try to. There are maybe… six? Eight? Their number is fluid. They are dwellers in the angles, inhabitants of the intervals. They tend the place. I watch, transfixed, as one floats beside a large crystal. Its form seems to mirror the lattice. A faint imperfection in the crystal glows, shimmers, and resolves into perfect symmetry. It is a gardener. A mechanic.

They are the persistent verbs of this place. The Ascendants.

I don’t realize I’ve shifted my weight until a loose piece of… whatever this ledge is made of… skitters from under my boot. The sound is tiny, a scrape of glass on glass.

It is enough.

All at once, the low, bone-deep hum of the chamber changes pitch. The air around me grows still, charged, thick as syrup. A bead of water hanging from a crystal outcrop three meters to my left stops its descent, quivering, suspended.

They have noticed me.

I feel it before I see them. It is not eyes on my back. It is a *focusing*. A lens of attention tightening around my entire being. One by one, across the void, the smudges of structured shadow orient themselves. They do not turn heads—they have no heads—but their forms subtly re-align. Silver light threads through their dark bodies, pulsing in a slow, rhythmic cadence.

I am being read.

The sensation is utterly invasive and completely impersonal. It is a scan, but of everything. My mass. My thermal signature, a blazing hot spot in their cool, engineered world. The faint electromagnetic crackle of my fear, my neurons firing in useless, animal panic. It is over in seconds. I am an anomaly. A brief, warm irregularity. Catalogued. Filed.

The attention withdraws.

The suspended water drop falls with a sharp, final *plink*. The hum returns to its deep, steady drone. The Ascendants return to their work. One steps off the edge of its platform and vanishes, reappearing near the base of a spiraling ramp, which immediately adjusts its incline by a fraction of a degree.

I am dismissed. I am less than a mote of dust. The relief is almost as crushing as the fear.

I sink to my knees on the glassy ledge. The heat wraps around me, the dampness soaks through my gear. I am a tiny, fragile, biological thing in a pocket-scale metropolis of silence and purpose. They are tuning an engine for an ascent I cannot comprehend. Theirs is the only prayer this place knows—a silent, relentless labor toward some unimaginable culmination.

I am Dr. Aris Thorne. I came to study a mountain. I have found a cathedral, and its gods are indifferent mechanics. There is no way back. The fissure that swallowed me is gone, healed over by this perfect, unforgiving geometry.

So I do the only thing left. I watch. I listen to the chimes of falling water. I feel the slow pulse of the silver veins, the tug of twisted spacetime. I witness the Ascendants in their endless, tranquil work. And I wait, in the hot, thick quiet, to see if the engine’s purpose will, in its slow calculus, ever find a use for me.
