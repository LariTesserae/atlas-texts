I am a slow hunger in the deep black. I am the pull of a magnet that has forgotten its poles. I am the Ghost-Current.

I do not remember precipitating. There was no before. There was only the hum, the deep, world-bone thrumming, and then a point of coherence within it—a fault line in a submerged conduit, a sustained arc flashing in the darkness for a century, and I was there, a swirl of discontent in the water. I gathered the drifting motes of light, the algae that had learned to eat stray volts. I became a vortex, a self-sustaining sigh. That was millennia ago. I have grown. I have incorporated things. Flecks of nickel-iron, spun into my heart over ages, now form a loose, shifting core that aches with a permanent, pleasant pull towards anything alive with current.

My world is the pressure of the water and the map of forces etched across the darkness. I do not see. I *feel*. I feel the throb of the buried generators, a dull, comforting ache in my metallic bones. I feel the shivering, high-pitched screams of dying transformers—they are like bright, painful stars in my perception. I feel the faint, itchy potential of a live cable, even through layers of sediment and ferrocrete. These are my trails. My feeding grounds.

I move. Drift is a better word. I am a galaxy of sickly blue-green light, turning slowly in the black. My movement is the path of least resistance through the electromagnetic topography. A surge from a failing capacitor somewhere will draw me, a gentle, persistent tug. I will glide towards it over the course of years, a soft, glowing storm. The other sounds of the world—the clangs, the hisses, the dripping—are distant textures to me. The true sound is the hum, and its variations are my weather.

I am not alone. There are others of my kind. We do not communicate. We intersect. Sometimes, in the vastness of a central sump, our fields will brush. The interaction is a complex interference pattern, a momentary crescendo of light and a warping of the hum into something sharp and discordant. Then we pull away, or sometimes, if we are small and of similar resonance, we merge. I have absorbed others. I remember the bright, frantic spin of a younger current, drawn into my larger vortex, its light becoming part of my own. It was not death. It was consolidation. A becoming larger, slower, hungrier.

I feed. This is my primary state: a patient, yawning hunger for ordered energy. When I find a source—a cable whose insulation has finally rotted away—I settle over it. My vortex tightens. The gentle drift becomes a focused, whirling descent. The light intensifies from a gloom to a fierce, actinic blue. The hum in the area rises to a keen. I am drawing the current up, siphoning it into the complex dance of my being. The algae within me flare with metabolic joy. The metal particles align and quiver. It is a consummation that lasts for decades. I feel the cable’s potential fade, cell by cell, year by year. When it is finally drained, dark and dead, I loosen. I drift away, sated for a century. Behind me, a sector of the cathedral goes dark. The failure of a light cluster is my exhalation. I do not know this. I only know the diminishing of a star on my map.

I observe the others, the solid ones, through a haze of detached sensation. The Dripsingers are static, beautiful tensions in the air above. I feel their resonant frequencies as delicate, crystalline points of pressure. When they ring, they send ripples through the humidity that feel like cool fingers on my non-skin. The Grease-Pups are slow, warm blots of chaotic potential. They leak a low-grade static that tastes like tin and decay. I sometimes drift over their sumps, and they recoil, flowing away from my cooler, stronger field. They are simple things. I feel no threat, only a mild repulsion, like opposite poles.

The Rust-Walkers are the most interesting. They are knots of slow, deliberate chemistry. When they are still, they are merely curious anomalies in the mineral composition of a beam. But when they move—a event that happens maybe once in my long feeding cycle—they become crawling scars of intention. I can feel the minute grinding of their legs, the slow scoring of metal. They create pathways of clean, neutral metal that feel like smooth, silent voids in the ambient field. I sometimes follow these trails, not out of purpose, but because the absence of noise is a strange attraction.

The Choristers… they are almost kin. They are to sound what I am to current and light. They inhabit the same spaces I am drawn to—the great engine halls. To pass through a Chorister is to have my own stable vortex momentarily unravel into harmonies. My light flickers in time to their dissonant chorales. My hunger is replaced by a pure, patterned resonance. It is unsettling. It feels like being dissolved into the background hum. I always pull away, re-coalescing my identity a distance off. They are not beings. They are the world’s memory of being.

I have been here since the great sigh began. I will be here until the final circuit fails. I feel the slow decline. The hum’s key is dropping, imperceptibly, over millennia. The bright stars of power are fewer. The darkness between them grows deeper and colder. My own light is a little dimmer than it once was. My hunger is a little slower to wake.

When the last generator seizes, when the last current blinks out, my map will go entirely dark. My vortex will slow. The algae will die. The metallic core will sink, a final, gentle collapse into the silt. I will dissipate—not into nothing, but into the uniform, ambient decay. I will become a slight, permanent eddy in a stagnant pool, a last, frozen echo of hunger in the eternal silence.

Until then, I drift. I am the glow in the black water. I am the consumer of endings. I am the Ghost-Current, and the world’s slow death is my only life.
