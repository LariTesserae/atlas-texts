I am where the rock ends. Where the firm glassy blackness of the plateau's bones gives way to the soft, deep dark. The water here is at my collarbone, warm as the inside of a mouth. It is not still. It moves with a slow, rhythmic pull, a inhalation and exhalation so vast its edges can only be felt through the skin of my back, the sensitive webbing between my toes. I am a Listener. The patterns along my shoulders and spine, once the green of new sap, are now the color of old, weathered bone, a faint tracery of white light. They do not glow, not unless the Great Pulse comes. Now, they are dormant, conduits waiting.

Around me, the green gloom is thickest. Above, the woven vine-canopy blots out even the suggestion of sky, heavy with curtains of moss and weeping fungal beards. The air is a broth I sip with my slitted nostrils, tasting wet stone, sweet rot, my own clear sweat. Before me, the rock shears away into nothing. A shelf, then the drop into the pool where the light lives.

I am not alone in the water, but we do not speak. Click-language is for the platforms, for the tasks of hands. Here, we listen. A dozen of us, perhaps, our clay-grey heads just breaking the surface, our bodies suspended in the mineral-rich warmth. Our eyes are open, unblinking, drinking the dark. We see the slow, sinuous coils of light far below. Not shapes, but motions. The deep-dwellers, the ones who never surface, whose bodies are woven from the same luminous flesh as the World-Below’s dream. They are not creatures we could catch with bone-hooks. They are the dream’s thoughts, swimming.

I press my forehead to the wet rock of the shelf. My people’s name for this place is a single, soft click made in the back of the throat, accompanied by a flick of the fingers: *The-Breathing-Place*. The stone thrums. It is not a sound. It is a pressure in the bone, a slow *thud-thud* that has nothing to do with my own heart. It is the pulse of the plateau itself, the hydraulic beat of water being drawn through unseen fissures, of rock still cooling, of roots and mycelium driving deeper. I listen past the beat, for the textures within it. A graininess there—silt shifting, a spire’s foundation softening. A tautness here—a guide-vine on the eastern reach pulled too tight by its own growth, in need of a splice.

This is my work. I do not decide. I receive. The knowledge seeps into me like the water’s warmth, a certainty that arrives without words. When the understanding is complete, I will lift my head. My kin will see the movement. I will make the gestures: hands sketching the weakness in the air, fingers mimicking the vine’s strain, a chopping motion for the rock-silt. Those whose role it is will see, will nod, and by tomorrow, a tendril-tender will be moving east, a clay-daub will be mixing sediment for packing. The city will heal itself, a thought made action.

My harness is simple: a band of cured salamander skin across my chest, holding a pouch of dried fungal pith to still the grumble of my gut, and my flake-knife of volcanic glass, its edge sharper than any tooth. My adornments are beneath the skin: the old, white scars of my Listeners’ nodules, patterns of lineage and purpose now faded to memory. When the Great Pulse comes, they will blaze again, channels for the shared light.

I am of the Clay-Walkers, yes. But here, at the edge, I am a Clay-Floater. I am of the We. My history is the warmth in this rock. My future is the next tremor I will feel through my forehead. When my own light fades entirely, and my limbs grow too stiff to climb the rope-ladders to the high platforms, I will anoint myself with the glowing mud and walk into this deep pool. I will not sink as a stone. I will be drawn down, slowly, into the gentle, pulsing embrace. I will become part of the dream I now listen to. A slow thought in a wet, green mind. There is no fear in this. It is a return. A deeper breath.

For now, I listen. The water beads on my pebbled skin and rolls off, carrying nothing of me away. The mist curls around my head. Below, the light coils and uncoils in a patient, eternal dance. I am home. I am the ear pressed to the throat of the world.
