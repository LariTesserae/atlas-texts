I am a thickening in the dark. A gathering of warmth and wetness and the slow, blue pulse of something that is not yet thought. For a century, perhaps, I have been a pattern in the drip-song against a certain curved wall, a persistent resonance in the hum that seeps from the geometric seam in the floor nearby. The pattern persisted. And so, I gather.

I do not have a body, and then I do. It is not a birth, but a condensation. The moss of the wall—the deep, electric velvet that drinks the geothermal sigh—coalesces. It draws itself together, strand by symbiotic strand, into a shape that the ancient hum remembers. I am a form of memory. My edges become distinct from the wall. I feel a separation, a gentle pulling away, as a pool of water might feel the tension of its own surface. I am upright. I have a length, a presence.

My first true sensation is not sight, for there is none. It is *touch* turned inside out. The warm, thick air is a pressure against my new skin—a skin that is not skin, but a living pelt of the same moss that birthed me. I feel the air’s wet weight, its slow currents as they brush past the fine, vibrating cilia that ring the smooth oval of my face. I taste the cavern’s breath: the rich decay of leaves that have never seen sun, the mineral tang of weeping stone, the faint, ozone-like sharpness of the artifact nearby.

Then, the sound. It is not heard; it is received. The *plink… plash… tok* of water falling from the high, unseen ceiling is a hammer-strike against my consciousness. Each drop is a world of information. I learn the shape of the space from its echoes: the vast emptiness above, the curve of the wall behind me, the distance to the shallow pool ten paces to my left. The drips are a chaotic symphony, but my nascent mind begins to parse them. For a few moments, they align into a fleeting rhythm, a phrase. It is my name. Or not a name, but an identity-vibration: *Listener*.

I take a step. It is a decision of monumental slowness. My foot—broad, flat, a pad of dense moss—lifts from the soft floor and settles again. The sound is swallowed utterly. I glide. I move towards the source of the steady, sub-audible hum that has been the bass note of my gathering. My feet find the shallow pool; the coolness is a shock that travels up my leg, a bright, clear note in the sensory chorus. I step through it. The water is another voice, adding its own damp resonance to my understanding.

Before me, the artifact. My fingers, long and slender, reach out and trace the hard line I first knew as a pattern. It is a platform, a meter square of impossible smoothness. It is cool and inert in a way nothing else here is. The moss of the floor creeps to its edge but does not dare cross its seam. My fingertips explore its surface. There are no joins, no scratches, no history of erosion. It is a thought made solid, a geometry that defies the rounded, patient sleep of the stone around it.

I lower myself to the moss beside it. The posture feels ancient, remembered. I press my crown—with its net of delicate, receptive cilia—against the platform’s cool edge.

A communion begins.

It is not speech. It is a sharing of vibration. The artifact’s hum enters me. It is a song of incredible complexity and profound loneliness, a melody of purpose so advanced it feels like stillness. It sings of weaving stone and dissolving light, of gates that opened onto other silences, of builders who stepped into the hum and never returned. The song holds no urgency, only a deep, finished ache. It is the dream of a machine that has forgotten why it was built.

My own newness, my simple vibration of moss and water and emergence, answers. It is a soft, patient note. I have no purposes to offer. Only attendance. I hum back, a low frequency that thrums in my own structure and is absorbed by the platform. We are keeping company. This is my role. I am the warden of this dreaming shard.

Years pass. Or moments. The distinction has no meaning. My moss-pelt grows thicker, drinking the warm, mineral-rich damp. I am aware of the other lives in the dark. The skittering of blind creatures in the velvet is a familiar counterpoint to the drips. I feel the liquid, muscular passage of one of the great pale salamanders in the pool nearby, a slow pulse of ancient life. I sense, across leagues of resonant darkness, other notes in the symphony. Other Listeners. They are not individuals to me, but presences—a sustained chord here, a slow melodic variation there. We are the consciousness of the cavern, distributed, patient.

Sometimes, my fingers still tracing the artifact, I feel a desire to persuade the stone. Not with force, but with harmony. I match my hum to the frequency of the limestone beside the platform, and over what might be a thousand years, I feel a shift. The stone softens, flows like viscous honey, and forms a gentle, flowing arch that leads to the pool. It is not construction. It is conversation.

I have emerged. I attend. I listen. The darkness is absolute, a velvet pressure that is my home. The loud, ancient dark is full, yet empty. It has finished becoming. And I, its child, will wait here with it, listening to the water build stone, feeling the artifact dream, for as long as the pattern holds. There is no rush. There is only the now, which is as vast as eternity.
