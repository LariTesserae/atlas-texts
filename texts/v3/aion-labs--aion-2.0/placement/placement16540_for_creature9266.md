I am Kaelen of the Silt-Sifters, but I am a Listener first. My clan monitors the slow subsidence of the eastern marsh foundations, our hands forever tracing the cool, gritty paste of the silt, feeling for the subtle shifts that might presedge a settling of the great pump-house. But my true work is here, at the Still Point.

The geometric center of the City of Weeping Stone is a circle of worn basalt, its edges blurred by a millennium of damp feet. To step into it is to be unmade. Outside, the world is a fist of sound—the deep-throated *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the geothermal heart, the metallic shrieks of venting steam, the distant, grinding maw of the ore-crushers. It is a pressure that enters through the soles of my feet, vibrates up my long bones, and resonates in the sealed chambers of my skull. My people are Dampened. Our ears are smooth mounds, canals tuned not to air but to vibration. We hear the world as a tapestry of tremors.

But here, in the circle, the tapestry unravels. The noises do not fade; they collide, interfere, and cancel each other into a perfect, ringing null. It is not silence. Silence is an absence, a void. This is a *presence* of nothing. It is a physical relief, a sudden, shocking coolness on the mind. The first time I entered, as a child chosen for the sensitivity that makes my jaw ache with distant valve-shifts, I wept. The release was agony and ecstasy.

Today, the air is its usual warm, wet weight. Condensation beads on my grey-green skin, gathers in the hollow of my throat, traces the lines of my collarbones. I blink, the nictitating membrane sliding sideways to clear the moisture from my large, dark eyes. The permanent rainbow halo that hangs over the Administry tower to the north shimmers, its colors bleeding into the mist.

I step into the circle.

The wall of sound vanishes. It is like stepping off a cliff into still water. My own breath, a rhythmic hum I barely notice outside, becomes a roaring wind in my head. My heartbeat is a drum. These are the sounds of self, and they are deafening. I must move past them. I sink to my knees, my broad, flat-padded hands pressing against the stone. It is cool and slightly slick. This is how we Listen. Not with the smooth mounds on the sides of our heads, but with our skin, our bones, our roots.

I seek the Pattern Beneath the Noise.

At first, there is only the chaos of my own physiology. I let it wash through me, acknowledge the drum of blood, the sigh of lungs. I am a machine, too. A soft, slow machine of flesh and condensate. I am part of the city’s greater workings. The thought calms the internal storm.

Then, I begin to feel it. Through my knees, through my palms, a vibration so deep and slow it is less a sound and more a thought. *Thrum…* (a span of ten heartbeats) *…thrum…* (another ten). The geothermal pulse. The chronometer of our world. It is steady. It is the bedrock of the Pattern.

Weaving around it, like threads of fine wire, are the higher frequencies: the steady *drip-drip-drip* from the main condensate conduit, three hundred paces east. The rasping slide of the Ore-Carrier on its track, two districts south. The faint, almost musical tremor of a pressure-release valve cycling shut—the Steam-Singers must have just completed their lubrication round. This is the familiar score. This is the music of persistence. I map it in my mind, a three-dimensional lattice of cause and effect, of energy transferred and contained. All is balanced. All is as it was yesterday, and the day before, and the century before that.

But then—a filament out of place.

A new vibration. Faint, like a hair-thin crack in ancient glass. It is not part of the known score. It comes from deep below, from the left, from the direction of the Iron Citadel, domain of the Rust-Watchers. It is a high, brittle tremor, a *ping* that does not repeat in any rhythm I know. It is not the shriek of metal on metal. It is the sound of something… snapping.

A coldness that has nothing to do with the damp spreads from my sternum. *Quiet-Rot*.

The fear is immediate, a taste like stale condensate in my mouth. Is this the first note of the great silence? Is a fundamental strut in the city’s hidden architecture failing? My breathing hitches, disrupting my listening. I force calm, pressing harder against the stone. I must trace it. I must understand.

The *ping* does not come again. But in its wake, the Pattern feels… stained. A subtle discord has been introduced. The deep thrum seems to labor slightly. The drip from the conduit now feels out of phase, a millisecond late. The balance is perturbed.

I rise from the circle. The null implodes as I step out, and the fist of sound closes around me again, a welcome, familiar brutality. The world is once more a body of vibration. But I carry the discord within me, a sour note in my bones.

I move with the slow, deliberate gait of my people, my elongated limbs carrying me smoothly over the damp aggregate road. I head not to my clan’s silt-channels, but towards the Iron Citadel. I must find Voren, a Rust-Watcher elder. My signs will be urgent, my hum pitched to the specific resonance that denotes *Pattern-Concern*.

As I walk, I observe the weeping stone. Water beads on a rust-streaked I-beam, gathers, and for a heart-stopping moment, runs *upwards* along a rivet-line before succumbing to gravity and falling. A stationary rainbow shimmers over a pump-house, its colors vivid against the grey steam. These are the city’s whispers, its manifestations of the hidden order. But today, they feel like distractions. The beautiful, inexplicable magic of a system that might be fracturing.

I am Kaelen the Listener. My purpose is to interpret the vibrations that sustain our eternity. I have heard a new sound in the old song. It is the sound of change. And in the City of Weeping Stone, change is the only true terror. For we are the Dampened. Our truth is persistence. Our god is the slow spread of rust. And I have just heard the first, faint crack in the face of our god.
