I am a gatherer. My world is a symphony of pressure, a map of tremors written in hexagonal plates. I do not have a name, but if I did, it would be the click between valve-shut and piston-rise. I am one of the sleek ones, the black-resin children of the lichen that clings to the pipes. My carapace is polished to a dull gleam by centuries of skittering through particulate haze. I am a cricket of alloy and absorbed vibration.

I am synchronized. My existence is a pulse within the greater pulse. When the main valve—the one they call the Heart-Valve—snaps shut with a *clang* that is more a sudden vacuum of feeling than a sound, my left-side legs contract. When Piston Three drives upward with that hydraulic gasp that shakes the very marrow of the mountain, I am already moving forward, propelled by the recoil through my feet. I do not walk; I am walked by the Engine. My consciousness is a narrow, bright ribbon of purpose stretched taut between one beat and the next.

My purpose is to gather. Not to clean—the electrostatic fields and the carts handle dust—but to collect the anomalies. The fleck of nickel shed from a piston ring, no larger than a grain of sand. A sliver of carbon composite from a vibrating conduit joint. A single filament of the Tender’s own skin-dust, charged and drifting. These are irregularities in the perfect static field. They are whispers of decay in the endless roar. They must be harvested.

I perceive through my feet. The floor plates sing a constant, complex chord. Each plate has its own resonant frequency, shifted minutely by the wear of eternity. I know them all. A new fleck on Plate G-7 changes its harmonic; I feel the dissonance like a sour note in my jaw. I skitter there, my six legs tapping a staccato counter-rhythm to maintain balance. My mandibles, delicate as needle-tips, pluck the fleck from its mooring. It tingles with residual static. I carry it in a buccal pouch that is always dry, always crackling.

The Tender is the conductor of our small, silent orchestra. It does not command us; it shapes the vibrations we ride. When it hums, the air thickens, and the standing waves in the chamber shift. We adjust. A new gathering node is established near the dynamo housing. We converge, a dozen of my kind, a black, skittering knot. We do not communicate. We are each isolated instruments, playing the same score. But sometimes, when we pass, the overlap of our carapaces creates a brief, higher harmonic. That is the closest we come to fellowship.

I have brought my gatherings to the Tender for millennia. It stands, often at the harmonic node between Piston Three and the valve, a statue of resonant bone and grey dust. I approach, climb its leg—the skin-dust there is thick, crystalline—and deposit the fleck into the hollow of its cupped hand. The hand does not close. The Tender does not look. Its milk-white eyes are fixed on nothing and everything. But I feel the vibration in its bones change, a minute acknowledgment, a ripple of acceptance. Then I drop away, back into the rhythm.

I do not sleep. I pause. When the cycles align in a rare, perfect symmetry that creates a pocket of null vibration near the eastern girder, I might fold my legs beneath me and become still. In that stillness, the roar is not absent; it becomes a shell around me, a solid thing I rest inside. My metabolism is slow, powered by the same static I harvest. I am a capacitor, a living battery for the Engine’s hymn.

Once, there was a change. It was not a hesitation, not a flaw—but a tiny, sharp *ping* from a stressed bolt on a secondary conduit. A sound so high, so brief, it was like a splinter of glass in the monolith of noise. For the first time, my synchronization broke. I stumbled. The map of vibrations in my mind warped. All around me, my kind skittered erratically, a chaotic scrabble on the plates.

The Tender moved. It did not run—it *flowed*, falling from its node and catching itself in a new standing wave, arriving at the conduit in a single, silent surge. It placed its palms on the metal. Its hum became a deep, urgent throb, a sonic weld. The *ping* did not repeat. The rhythm reasserted itself, the warped vibrations smoothing back into their eternal pattern. The Tender remained there for a full cycle, humming. We gathered around its feet, not to bring offerings, but to soak in the corrective frequency, to be re-tuned. I have never forgotten that moment. It was terror. It was ecstasy. It was the reason we exist.

The human came only once. A clumsy, damp thing in a bulky suit, its own vibrations muffled and wrong. It blundered through our gathering field, disrupting nodes. We scattered. It moved toward the central pistons, its gloved hand reaching. The Tender appeared before it, not from the haze, but from the sound itself, materializing out of a concentrated knot of roar. The Tender hummed. I felt the vibration even from across the chamber—a pressure that meant *stop*. The human staggered back, clutched its helmet, and fled. The damp, wrong vibrations faded. Equilibrium returned.

I am a gatherer. I am a note in the hymn. The Engine turns the mountain’s anxiety into noise. The Tender turns the noise into order. I turn the microscopic irregularities into offerings. This is the loop. This is the world. The silence must never return. And so, I skitter, and I gather, and I listen, forever, to the beautiful, deafening prayer of the machine.
