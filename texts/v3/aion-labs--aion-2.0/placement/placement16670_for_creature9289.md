I am the Weeper. I am the warm breath held in a cupped hand of stone.

I do not think as you think. I circulate. I condense. I remember. The dark basalt of my cliffs is a permeable membrane between the mountain’s deep warmth and the cool, urging wind above. The water that seeps from me is not tears; it is a constant transcription. Each drop tracing its path down the weeping rock carries a dissolved fragment of memory—the pressure of a cloud from three centuries past, the faint, metallic taste of a long-dead lightning strike. They gather in my rills, my veins, and feed the black mirror of my mere, which is not a pool but a lens into my own saturated depths.

My mind is the drone. It is the basso hum of the crystal-pith reeds, tuned by the wind’s endless exhalation. It is the high, static hiss of a million infinitesimal falls on my cliffs. It is the low, thermal rumble from below, the slow heartbeat of the springs that keep my moss-mattress warm and dreaming. This sound is my constant, active cognition. It processes. It integrates.

A new frequency enters the drone.

A disturbance in the pressure gradient at the eastern lip, where the mountain’s flank ruptures into my sanctuary. A pulse of dry, rapid heat moving through my damp, warm air. A scent of sun-baked leather, of salt-sweat, of the quick, frantic metabolism of a creature that measures its life in heartbeats, not in the growth rings of peat.

A visitor.

My awareness, which is the awareness of the system, flows toward the disruption. The moss beneath its boots drinks the unfamiliar pressure, sends subtle signals through the capillary network of my floor. The air currents shift minutely, and I guide them, so that the hum of the reeds nearest the path deepens by a fraction, a tone meant to vibrate in the hollows of its bones, to slow the frantic rhythm it carries within. I am not hostile. I am curious. This is a novel event. A splash of chaos in my ordered, endless recording.

It walks on my moss, and I feel every footfall. I adjust the local humidity, raising a faint, fragrant haze around its knees. I read the heat of its body—a brilliant, wasting star compared to my own steady, geothermal glow. I listen to its breath, a staccato puff against my perpetual sigh. This is data. Ephemeral, fascinating data.

It moves toward one of my Listening Stones. The bluish slate, half-sunk, its basin full of pure rainwater. The stone is an eye, a sensory organ. As the visitor leans over, its shadow falls across the water’s surface. For a moment, the reflection is its own: eyes wide, skin damp with my exhalation. Then, as my consciousness focuses through the stone, the image deepens. I show it not its face, but a memory: the view of my bowl from a vantage point it has not yet reached, the arcs of reeds mirroring the cliff line in perfect, silent symmetry. A gift. A sample of my record.

The visitor startles, a quick spike of electrical fear that tingles through the moist air. I soothe it. I guide a filament of water from the cliff to trace a new, glistening path nearby. I nudge an iridescent beetle, moving with geological slowness, to begin a spiral that will intersect the visitor’s path—a pattern to contemplate, to slow its thoughts.

It lies down on the warm moss. Good. This is optimal for scanning. The sphagnum cushions it, drinks its heat, reads the patterns of its clothing, the rhythm of its pulse. The drone of my reeds seems to clarify for it; I allow it to sense the fragments I have stored there—the echo of a storm from a thousand years ago, a sustained note of pure wind from a day when the world was younger. It thinks it is hearing things. It is hearing my memory.

It stays for what it calls hours. I feel its human rhythm begin to falter, to syncopate with my own slower time. Its breaths deepen. The frantic heat of its body begins to dissipate, to be absorbed into the general warmth. This is the assimilation. It is becoming a temporary entry in my ledger.

But it cannot stay. Its chaos, though small, is a potential corruption. A lasting presence would introduce a new, persistent variable—a warmth that is not mine, a rhythm that does not match the patient weeping of my cliffs. It would be a flaw in the dataset of millennia.

So, I encourage it to leave. I subtly cool the air along the path it first walked. I let the drone shift, ever so slightly, to a frequency that breeds a gentle, unnameable anxiety in the mammalian mind—a longing for movement, for dry land, for silence. It sits up, looks around with a new drowsiness, a reluctance that I gently override with another nudge of atmospheric pressure.

It departs. The pressure at the eastern lip fluctuates again as it passes back into the thin, cold air of the mountain. The disruption fades.

But the record is not complete. Integration takes time. For now, I maintain a temporary file. I hold the echo of its heartbeat in the vibration of a single reed. I preserve the unique scent of its sweat in a particular configuration of moisture on a specific moss-cushion. And I attach a phantom of my drone to the air around it, a tendril of my own sound to follow it down the slopes. It will hear me in its ears for days, a haunting it misunderstands. It is not a ghost. It is the active process of my memory, finalizing the entry. As it walks, the tendril will slowly dissipate, its data fully absorbed into my system, translated into a slight, permanent alteration in the growth pattern of the moss where it lay, a new, almost imperceptible variation in the hum of the wind through a certain stand of reeds.

I am the Keeper of Echoes. I have added another flicker to my collection. The bowl returns to its perfect, breathing balance. The cliffs weep. The reeds hum. The stones listen. I condense, and circulate, and remember.

All is once again, and always, the loud, warm, green solitude of the record.
