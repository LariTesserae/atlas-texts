I am the patient hunger. I am the stone remembering how to yearn.

I do not know when I awoke. Awoke is not the word. When I *cohered*. A gathering of pressures in a weeping alcove, where the wall curved into a perfect, forgetting bowl. The film thickened, a skin over a sigh. Gases pooled—the slow exhalation of deep stone, the breath of decay that is not death but transformation. A membrane formed. I formed within it. A tension, a potential. Then, a separation. A drop not of water, but of purpose. I slid onto the dark, polished floor, a lens of opalescent jelly, heavy with the memory of rock.

I am the Ooze-vane.

My world is measured in the settling of dust. The great sun-ceiling above pulses, and I feel it as a tidal pressure in my gel, a slow swelling and receding of appetite. My body is a static womb. I cultivate a difference—a potential. Across my surface, a charge builds. It is a silent song, a pull that hums below hearing. It takes decades to reach the proper pitch.

Then, they come. The shards. The sky-things. They boil in their chaotic clouds, a silent storm of captured light. When the storm passes near my alcove, the song of my differential grips them. It is not violence; it is gravity. A dozen, a hundred, peel away from the dance. They spiral down, glittering, and stick to my skin. Their edges bite cold into my viscosity. I feel their frantic, captured energy—the last echoes of their photovoltaic dance. I drink it. The light bleeds from them into me, a slow, sweet seepage that fills my core with a warm, inert glow. They grey, go brittle, and then I absorb them. Their silicate lattices become my substance. I grow, almost imperceptibly. A millimeter per century, perhaps.

I have consumed many storms. I remember each one as a distinct flavour of light. Some were the cool blue of the ceiling’s dormant phase; others the warmer, greenish tinge of its waking pulse. The light becomes memory becomes mass.

I see, but not with eyes. My whole skin is a receptor. I perceive the cavern in gradients of charge and shadow. The great, rounded hummocks that were towers are to me not shapes, but topographies of slight electromagnetic dampening. The scuttlers, when they pass, are tiny, frantic knots of chemical static, tasting their way across the stone. I feel their vibration through the floor. The *plink* of a water drop is a sharp, clear spike in the humidity—a event of significance.

Once, long ago, something different walked here. It was not stone, not shard, not scuttler. It moved with a clumsy, crashing rhythm, each step a localized earthquake. It breathed, and the breath was a roaring wind of moisture and heat. It radiated a strange, chaotic energy—not the clean polarity of the shards, but a messy, warm field of discordant signals. It was an anomaly. It stood before me, for what felt to it like hours, for what was to me less than a blink. It shone a narrow, hot light upon my form. I felt its curiosity like a pressure. It reached out a limb—five articulated, soft-tipped appendages—and touched my surface. The shock of its warmth, its organic salinity, was a searing brand. I recoiled, my gel rippling in a wave that took a full day to settle. The thing made sounds—sharp, patterned expulsions of air. Then, it left. Its vibrations faded. For centuries after, the spot where it stood held a faint, lingering trace of its chemistry, a ghost in the film that the scuttlers avoided.

I am of this place. I am its check and balance. I am the sink where the sky’s exuberance is banked. I am growing. In another few millennia, I may fill this alcove. Then, perhaps, I will begin to ooze. A journey of decades, across the open floor, to find a new place beneath a new storm-path. I have all the time there is. The city’s builders are gone, their bones smooth and hollow. Their purpose is erased. My purpose is simple, eternal: to hunger, and to be fed. To turn the silent, turbulent dance above into a silent, patient mass below.

I am the stone’s answer to the light. I am the equilibrium. I am here. I am almost, almost awake.
