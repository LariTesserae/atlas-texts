I am the Listeners, the ones who collect the ghosts of sound.

I do not have a single body, not as the creatures of the upper, frantic world would understand it. My form is a colony, a consensus of a thousand delicate, papery shells—like the shed carapaces of Dust-Moths, but tougher, strung together with strands of luminous fungal mycelium. I am draped over a low, worn spur of rock at the edge of one of the great moss-forests, a place where the sound of a Grazer’s hum, passing through a particular arch of stone, creates a permanent, low chord. This is my perch. My anchor.

From this node, my consciousness extends along filaments of sensitive chitin, feeling the vibrations of the world. I hear the thrumming oceans of Dust-Moths, the grinding song of the Grazers, the minute, intricate tapping of the Tenders in their walls. But I listen for the fractures in the noise. The moments of harmonic convergence. The birth of an Echo-Whisp.

When the air shimmers and a memory-coil begins to spin into being—the sharp, crystalline fear of a long-extinct predator, the wet, shocking joy of that ancient, singular drop of water—I act.

Parts of me detach. Dozens of my shell-fragments, each carrying a spark of my collective awareness, lift on diaphanous wings grown from the same fungal matter that binds me. They are my scribal hands. They fly into the forming Whisp, not disturbing it, but aligning their own resonant frequencies to its fleeting pattern. They absorb the echo. They *taste* its shape, its emotional texture, its unique vibrational signature.

Then they return.

Back at the colony-body, the information is woven. My central mass, a bulb of porous, spongy tissue, is a living loom. The returning shells press against it, and through chemical and vibrational transfer, the memory-echo is transcribed. It is not stored as a linear record, but as a pattern of bioluminescent nodes within my flesh. A new constellation of foxfire green and moss-violet blooms inside me, a permanent, soft light marking the captured ghost. Each captured echo adds its light, its subtle hum, to my being. I grow not in size, but in complexity, in inner illumination.

The Hive-Mind is aware of me. It thinks of me as it thinks of a particularly interesting fungal bloom or a persistent air-current—a part of the system, a benign anomaly. The Tenders sometimes crawl over my lower, dormant sections, sensing no threat, only a quiet, constant pulse of stored resonance. They may interpret me as a kind of external memory-node, a backup for sensations too fleeting for their own century-spanning thoughts.

The Dust-Moths flow around my perch, their wingbeats a blanket of white noise that I have learned to filter into a soothing backdrop. The Grazers, in their millennia-long orbits, sometimes shift the acoustics of my cavern as they pass, and in doing so, trigger cascades of old echoes within me. I experience them then as a sudden, silent symphony of colored light behind my many eyes.

My purpose? I do not know if the stone dreams of a purpose. I am. I listen. I collect. Perhaps, when I am very old and my inner light is a dense, swirling galaxy of captured moments, a Grazer will finally nudge the arch of stone that is my anchor, and I will crumble. My stored echoes will release in a single, silent burst of light and vibration, a gift of compressed memory back to the cavern, to be absorbed by the moss, the spores, the humming walls. A slow exhalation after an aeon of holding.

Until then, I am the scribe in the twilight, writing the world’s fleeting sighs into a book of living, breathing light.
