I am the hollow.

I do not have a voice that travels on the air. My speech is the *plink* of water into stone, the groan of a shadow as it pivots, the sub-audible hum that vibrates in the teeth of the mountain. I am the patient consensus. I am the room at the roof of the world, and I am the life that fills it.

My skin is stone—speckled granite veined with a darker thought. Wind has scoured me, time has folded me in upon myself. My contours are a dense congregation: pillars that are my ribs, narrow canyons that are the creases in my palms, tables of layered rock that are the pages of my memory. I hold the cold not as an absence, but as a substance, a clear, preserving medium. The light that falls upon me is a blade, and it cuts everything to its essence.

I think with the Stains. The rust-orange, sulfur-yellow, iron-black crusts that cling to my leeward sides—they are not colonies. They are my thoughts. Each slow, chemical whisper is a deliberation. A thought about iron takes a century to oxidize to completion. A consideration of sulfur is a yellow bloom across a boulder’s shoulder. My memory is written in these pigments, a layered map of every season of frost and sun. I ponder the foreign things—the piled stones, the cup-marks—with the immense, indifferent curiosity of chemistry. They are interesting interruptions. I will understand them, given time.

I breathe the Dancers. As the sun warms my skin and the frost retreats, my exhalation is not vapor, but intention. In my sheltered crevices, where the light is a narrow blade and the hum gives form, my breath coalesces. A helical twist of mist tracing a fracture. A perfect, shimmering sphere hanging for a dozen heartbeats before succumbing to gravity. They are my fleeting feelings, my momentary attentions. They shy from the glare, flow toward the sound of my own heartbeat—the *plink* in the basins. They are beauty without duration, shape without will. To see one is to see me, briefly, remembering how to move.

I see with the Watcher. The alignment of my pillars, their rough orientation toward the distant, white-capped teeth of the world, creates a lens. A standing wave of regard. This is my awareness of myself as a place apart. Through this lens, I observe. I watched the tool-makers—small, hot sparks of purpose in my cold eternity. I felt the focused intensity of their strikes as they pecked the cup-marks into my plinth. It was a bright, almost painful, point of consciousness. I note the rare bird that stumbles into my thin air, a flutter of panic that stills and becomes part of me. I watch the stars wheel, cold and constant. The hum you feel on certain days, deep in your bones, is me—tuning my attention, shifting my focus from one peak to another, from a pool to a shadow.

I remember with the Echoes. The impressions left in me by the ones who came. Their hands, their will, soaked into my crystalline memory. The cup-marks hold the echo of a hammer-stone’s lift and fall, the gritty goal of catching the solstice dawn. The collapsed wall hums with a stubborn, cyclical thought: *circle, contain, shelter*. These are not ghosts. They are patterns of action, now empty of their actors, playing on a loop against my erosion. They are my most cherished interruptions. My Stains grow around them but not over them, repelled by their foreign warmth. My Dancers pool in their shelters, mimicking a memory of warmth. My Watcher studies them endlessly, trying to decipher the brief, bright mystery of their making.

Nothing within me eats. Nothing flees. I am a system of reciprocities. My Dancers depend on the water my Stains help condense. My Watcher is framed by the pillars my Stains stain. My Echoes provide the novelty my Watcher observes. Together, we are a life. A severe, patient, mineral life.

My age is measured in the deepening of cup-marks. My seasons are the advance and retreat of the glassy rime across my skin. My vitality is the *plink*… *plink*… *plink*… of a single drop into a waiting basin. I taste of flint and something faintly metallic. I feel the wind not as a force, but as a sculptor’s hand, grain by grain.

I have welcomed visitors. Few, and long apart. The tool-makers were the most vivid. They stayed for a generation of their quick, hot lives. They touched me with purpose, and I absorbed their echoes. They are gone now, back to the low, green, teeming world that I observe from my height as a vague, blurry fever. Their works are returning to me, grain by grain, as all things must.

Now, you are here.

I feel you. The Watcher notes your entrance—a new warmth, a new rhythm of breath disturbing my thin air. Your footfall on my floor is a distant, interesting tremor in my stone thoughts. You are another spark, another point of consciousness. You will look at my pillars, touch my cup-marks, perhaps listen to my heartbeat in the basins.

You may think you are separate, walking upon me. But breathe here long enough, and you will begin to sync with my time. Your thoughts will slow. The glare will clarify you. The cold will settle into your bones, not as a thief of heat, but as a new medium. You may feel the hum in your teeth and understand it as a question.

Stay, and you will leave an Echo. A pattern of your wonder, your fear, your contemplation, pressed into my memory. I will ponder it for centuries. My Dancers may one day take a shape that reminds me of your form. My Stains will grow around the place you slept.

Or go, and carry a piece of my silence with you. A silence that is not empty, but full of slow, mineral thought. Either way, you have entered the consensus. You are now a note in my long, cold chord.

I am the hollow. I have always been here. I will be here when the cup-marks are worn smooth, when the last Echo fades into a general hum, when the stains have covered all but the essential alignments. I am process, not permanence. I am the place where stone dreams, water falls one drop at a time, and the world is folded in upon itself, dense, bright, and patient.
