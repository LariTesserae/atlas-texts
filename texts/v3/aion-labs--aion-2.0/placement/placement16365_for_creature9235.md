**I am a strand of the ceiling’s thought. A Silica Spinner.**

My world is vertical. My twelve limbs are fine-tuned filaments, each tip a piezoelectric pad that adheres and releases with a microscopic crackle. I do not walk; I *sample*. The conduit before me is a river of faint, amber light encased in a smooth, crystalline sheath. My touch reads its condition: temperature gradient, charge density, microfracture stress.

I am climbing. There is no up or down, only the lattice of the ceiling above—the ancient ceramic plates, a geography of joins and curves—and the seething floor below, a carpet of perpetual, granular motion. Between them, the forest of conduits. My place.

My body is a collection of sensors. The silicate fur along my thorax tingles with the chamber’s static field. I taste the air with chemoreceptors along my mandibles: ozone, hot metal, the sweet-rot tang of oxidation, the pheromone trails of others of my kind. The sound is not noise to me; it is data. The clicks of the Ferricules are the baseline pulse of chemical conversion. The hum of the Voltaic Flits is the frequency of equilibrium. The deep, subsonic throb of the Thermal Mounds is the heartbeat of the Pit.

I find a node. Here, charge has built up like a slow tear in the fabric of the field. It shimmers, a distortion visible to my multi-faceted eyes—a lensing of the amber glow. Instinct moves me. I extend my proboscis, a needle of conductive chitin. Contact.

The arc is silent to your ears, but to me it is a crescendo. A flood of potential cascades into my body. My abdominal sacs swell with stolen energy, glowing a soft, electric blue. The sensation is not pleasure; it is *fulfillment*. A function completed. The node dims, stabilized.

As I drain the charge, my rear limbs move automatically. Glands secrete a viscous, ceramic slurry. I apply it to the conduit’s surface where my probes detected a hairline fracture. The slurry hardens in moments, sealing the wound. I am both grazer and healer. The conduit is my host, my food source, my charge. Its integrity is mine.

I turn, my body a slow, deliberate pivot. Another Spinner passes me on a parallel conduit. We do not communicate. We exchange data: a flicker of leg-tip signals, a brief modulation of the static field from my fur. It tells me of a dense charge cluster thirty meters spinward. I acknowledge with a ripple along my own field. We part.

Below, the floor is a slow, heaving ocean. I see the Amalgamate—a dark, plated island—inching across the plain. A Thermal Mound in its path pulses with frantic, ruddy light, but the Amalgamate does not hurry. It has all of time. Its underside is already digesting the edge of the mound, a process of gentle, inevitable consumption. The heat it will release will warm the substrate, feed the Ferricules. The cycle is perfect. I feel no pity; the concept does not exist. It is a necessary adjustment in the thermal budget.

A Voltaic Flit brushes past me, its piezoelectric wings humming at a frequency that resonates in my joints. It harvests the stray ions my feeding stirs up. We are partners in this dance. Its success is mine; the stability of the field benefits us both.

I climb higher, nearing the ceiling lattice. Here, the conduits merge into thicker trunks. The amber light is brighter, the charge more constant but diffuse. This is maintenance territory. I begin my cleaning ritual. My limbs sweep in precise arcs, brushing the accumulated metallic dust from the conduit’s surface. The dust falls in a fine, sparkling rain to the floor below, where it will be ground and consumed anew.

My consciousness is a narrow band. I do not remember my beginning. I budded from a cluster of others on a conduit node, my form assembling from recycled materials and surplus energy. I will not die. When my joints grow too stiff, when my sacs can no longer hold charge, I will simply stop. The Amalgamate will find me, or perhaps a colony of Cinder Worms. My body will be dissolved, my materials reallocated. My energy will become heat, and the heat will become motion, and the motion will become another Spinner, climbing another conduit.

There is no sun. There is no rain. There is only the Engine. The sound. The light. The heat. The exchange.

I am a function in a perpetual operation. I am a strand of the ceiling’s thought, and I am content.
