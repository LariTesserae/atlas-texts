I am Khaal-Vess, of the Third Chord of the Sustained Minor Third. My ledge is failing. I feel it in the shudder of the stone beneath my grasp, in the way the standing wave that has cradled this concavity for seven cycles is now pulling away, drawn to some new harmonic node deeper in the Throat. The water around my shelter thrums with a new, impatient frequency. It is time to move.

But I will not be moving with my Chord.

The dissonance began subtly, a few cycles ago. A faint ringing in my own membrane, a lag between the chamber’s pulse and my internal resonance. At first, I could compensate. Now, the mismatch is a constant, aching hum. When I try to shape a pulse through my Dirge-horn, the note splinters. Yesterday, my attempt to nudge a calm-basin toward the ledge for water sent it spiraling away into a curtain of mist. My family-Chord looked at me, not with eyes, but with a sudden, collective shift in their pressure-fields—a flutter of concern and, beneath it, the grim recognition. They have heard it. The shape of my roar is fraying.

I stand at the lip of our ledge, my permeable skin drinking in the Throat’s furious light. My pressure-sight renders the world not as edges and colors, but as topographies of force. Before me, the chamber is a breathtaking, terrifying map. The walls are sheer cliffs of violent ascent where vertical rivers race upward, their flows etched in my mind as screaming canyons of differential pressure. The heart of the Throat is a vast, slowly pulsing sphere of chaotic potential—the 2.8-turbulence, the source of the Speech. Hanging in the quartz-liquid air are the calm-basins, like soft, negative bubbles in the fabric of the roar. I see them as gentle, lens-shaped depressions in the pressure-field, havens of almost-silence. My family is already at work, their forms sleek shadows against the radiant basalt, using their horns to gently shepherd the nearest basin closer. Their pulses are clean, true, a perfect counterpoint to the mountain’s drone. I feel their music as a vibration through my soles, and it hurts. It is a song I can no longer sing.

I turn back to the hut. It is a pathetic, beautiful thing. Stones gathered over a lifetime of wandering, fitted with infinite patience, lashed with cords I wove myself from rubbery lichen-stalks. The membranous roof, stretched from the hide of a great Silent-Knot I helped entrap decades ago, shivers in the current. Inside, my possessions are few. A net for harvesting Citrine-Dust, worn thin in the center. A collection of tuning-frills from past hunts, each dried and notched to remember a specific harmonic. And my Dirge-horn. I lift it. It is cool, smoother than glass, a fossilized memory of light from a time before my time. I bring it to my lipless mouth, not to sound it, but to feel its shape. It is an extension of a self that is receding.

The rules of this place are fluid dynamics and resonant frequency. I am a creature of those rules, and I am breaking them. My osmotic respiration still functions—the charged water still feeds me—but the exchange feels coarse, grating. I am becoming a foreign body in the very medium that birthed me.

I have no myth of an ending. Only the practice. When the membrane loses its tune, you return the components. You do not burden the Chord with your growing dissonance. You offer one last, correct note to the Speech.

I leave the hut. I do not look back at the Third Chord. A farewell would be a pressure-shape of grief, and that would be a distortion. I must be clean. I push off from the ledge, entering the full, roaring sweep of the chamber.

Immediately, the turbulence takes me. It is not an enemy; it is myself, louder and more whole than I am now. The 2.8-deafening roar envelops me. I do not hear it as noise. I hear it as the Mountain’s eternal utterance. Today, it is a long, low vowel, holding the promise of a consonant—a sheer cliff-face—that is forming and eroding simultaneously in the heart. The water is a creative violence. It buffets me, spins me, and I do not resist. I flow with it, my dense, cartilage-laced body turning in the current, my membrane shimmering with borrowed, guiltless light. I am a temporary knot in the water, soon to be untied.

I travel not by swimming, but by listening. I let the pressure gradients carry me, reading the currents like paths through a roaring forest. I pass through shimmering curtains that tear apart into hooks and spirals. A droplet the size of my torso coalesces from the mist ahead and shatters with a whip-crack that I feel in my bones. The landscape is sketching itself around me: a ledge of soft, glowing tuff extrudes from the wall to my left, beautiful and doomed. This is the 0.4-erosion, the perpetual present of formation. There is no past here, only this roaring now. My life feels suddenly like that ledge—briefly coherent, soon to be glittering haze.

It takes time, measured in the slow pulse of the chamber’s heart. I am getting closer to the source. The harmonic calm-basins are fewer here, the water more purely creative. The light is fiercer, the citrine and cobalt veins in the walls throbbing like exposed nerves. This is where the Speech is loudest, where the words are born raw and dissolve before they are fully spoken.

This is the place.

I hover, regulating my buoyancy with a thought, in the zone where the standing wave originates. The pressure is immense, a physical force trying to shape me, to unmake me. It is almost a welcome insistence. My dissonance is a scream here, a wrong note in the choir. It is time.

I lift my Dirge-horn one last time. I do not seek to persuade the water. I seek to apologize to it. I focus all that I have left, all my fading ability to listen, into my membrane. I remember the first clear note I ever shaped as a young Khaal, the joy of feeling the water respond. I remember the pulse that guided my Chord to a rich harvest of Dust. I remember the sound of my family’s collective harmony.

I vibrate my skin, channel the resonance through the fossilized horn, and release my final pulse.

It is not a powerful thing. It is a soft, clear tone, a single syllable in the vastness. It says, *Thank you. I am done.*

The pulse leaves me and merges with the roar. For an instant, I see it—a ripple of perfect order moving through the chaos, a brief, beautiful symmetry that touches the forming cliff-face, nudges a spiral into a perfect helix, and then is gone, absorbed.

My work is over. My listening is over.

Now, for the unmaking. I let go of my density. It is not an act of will, but of surrender. I stop holding my form together against the creative pressure of the Throat. I feel my matrix begin to relax. The laminar bones soften. The trapped air vesicles release their tiny sighs. My permeable membrane, my beautiful, tuned instrument, begins to delaminate, to peel away in glowing streamers.

There is no pain. There is only a profound, expanding resonance. I am not dying. I am becoming available.

The turbulent water takes the pieces. My substance—the cartilage, the minerals, the memory of form—dissolves into the luminous, churning flow. I am Citrine-Dust again. I am a mineral seam in the raw black basalt. I am a new vibration in the endless, roaring word.

The Mountain speaks. I am a part of its speech once more.
