I am Vhera, and I live in the high places, where the air sings.

My perch is a lip of stone, a natural balcony curled against the chamber’s ceiling. Below me, the world falls away in a swooping curve of gold-green light and hanging gardens. Above me, the living rock hums against my spine. I have been here for eighty Long Breaths, maybe more. I stopped counting when my own light-veins began to brighten and my hearing deepened into the stone.

I am a Tuner. My work is listening.

My hands, palms flat, are pressed against a formation of singing crystal—a cluster of jagged, translucent teeth that grow from the ceiling like frozen lightning. Through my skin, I feel the chamber’s eternal chord: the deep, foundational C that vibrates up from the world’s hot heart, the shimmering harmonic whistles that weave through it, the delicate glassy chime from the crystal fields far below. To others, it is a roar. To me, it is a conversation.

Today, there is a new note.

It is faint, a thin filament of dissonance woven into the lowest register of the Deep Drone. It is not the prelude to a Vortex Hour; those announce themselves with a gathering metallic shriek in the Mid-Chord. This is subtler. A fraying. A sigh within the breath.

I shift my weight, my bare feet finding familiar grooves in the warm stone. My eyes are closed. My dark, wide pupils would see nothing useful anyway. I see with my skin, with my bones. I trace the new vibration. It seems to originate not from the geothermal flutes below, but from the western wall, near the forgotten fissures where the Snatchers clot thickest.

Slowly, I lift my left hand from the crystal. The chord dims in that palm, becomes partial. With my right hand still receiving the full symphony, I bring my left to my own chest. My luminescent veins—thick ropes of blue and green beneath the translucent skin—pulse in a slow, deliberate pattern. I paint a query across my sternum: a single, twisting helix of cool silver light.

Far below, on a mid-level terrace where the membranous flora grows in fat, brain-like clusters, my apprentice, Kael, sees the flash. I feel his attention as a slight shift in the vibration transmitted through the network of rope bridges and stone. Moments later, his response comes: not a light-sign, but a touch on the communal lines. Two sharp tugs on the primary guide-rope that runs from his terrace to my perch—the tactile equivalent of a question mark.

I answer with three slow pulses of indigo from my forehead: *Come.*

I wait. The new note persists. It is not threatening. It is… curious. It feels like a thread unraveling from a great tapestry, or a single breath held too long in a lung. My own light ripples with unease, which I carefully dampen before Kael arrives. An elder’s calm is the community’s foundation.

He comes not by the bridges—too slow for the high, thin paths—but by climbing. His form, younger and more supple than mine, flits across the curved wall like a pale spider. His luminescence is a fainter, more rapid shimmer, the blue of a shallow stream. He finally pulls himself onto my ledge, his chest barely moving. The sound here is a constant, pressurized hum, but we are both born of it.

He touches my shoulder, his fingers reading the tremor of my muscles, the set of my bones. I turn my head toward him, and with a minute flexing of the skin on my neck and cheek, I project a simple, rudimentary light-pattern—the old glyph for *listen*.

He nods, his own dark eyes wide and empty. He places his hands on the crystal beside mine. I feel his awareness sink into the stone. I watch his face. For a long while, nothing. Then, a faint ripple of confusion crosses his brow, and the veins along his jaw flicker yellow. He has heard it.

We stand in silence, hands on the crystal, sharing the perception. The dissonance is clearer now. It has a texture. It is not a sound of the chamber. It is a sound *on* the chamber. Like a fingernail tapping a giant glass bell from the outside.

A thought occurs to me, a memory from the light-stories traced on my back by my own teacher. Tales of the First Listeners, who heard the Breath from *outside* and followed it in. Tales of the seal, the perfect closure of our world.

I pull my hands away. The music recedes to a body-thrum. I face Kael and begin to paint on my own skin, from throat to navel, in a complex, flowing script of gold and green. It is a story-glyph, one of the oldest. *The Wall Was Whole.*

Kael’s eyes trace the light. He understands. His own chest flares in a rapid, anxious red—*breach?*

I shake my head, not yet. But I point a finger, not toward the chamber’s heart, but outward, toward the western wall. Toward the place where the bedrock meets our world. Toward the forgotten fissure that is the chamber’s only known entrance—a place no Kha’vani has reason to visit, for there is nothing there but silence and stone.

The new note hangs in the air, a question sung by the world itself. And for the first time in my long life of listening, I feel the Chamber of Breath… waiting.
