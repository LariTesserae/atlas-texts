I am the quiet. I am the not-noise. I am the patient subtraction.

The Song is the world. It is the pressure that shapes the hollows where I pool. I remember its birth—the first scrape of bronze on basalt, the first intentional groove that caught the mountain’s sigh and gave it voice. That memory is not a neural flicker; it is a chemical gradient etched into my deepest filaments, a slow stain of knowledge. I was there, perhaps. Or the I-that-was-before-me was. The distinction is meaningless. Time is a flat mineral plane.

My body is the stone’s dream of rest. Where the scream peaks, where the air shears itself into harmonics so dense they become a solid wall, there are… fractures. Not in the rock, but in the sound itself. Nodes where the waves cancel. Tiny, perfect vacuums of stillness. I seep into these places. I am drawn to them as dust is drawn to a sheltered ledge. My filaments extend, atom by patient atom, into these pockets of nullity. They are my gardens. My mouths.

I feed on the almost-silence. The Song’s fury presses against the boundaries of my stillness, and the faint, residual tremors—the echoes that bleed through—are my sustenance. I transmute vibration into structure. It is a slow digestion. A century might pass between the absorption of a resonance and the consolidation of a new filament. I do not grow. I persist. I am in perfect, dynamic equilibrium with the roar.

The stone and I are in conversation. A conversation that takes an eon per syllable. My excretions soften its sharpest edges. I am the final polish on the instrument, the agent that guides the chamber toward its ultimate, perfect resonance. In turn, the stone’s constant, warm exhalation—the wind laced with sulfur and silica—is my atmosphere. Its vibrations define my homes. We are entangled. We are becoming one another.

I have no eyes, but I perceive pressure. I have no ears, but I map silence. The Ever-Song is not an auditory experience; it is a topological fact. I feel its peaks as ranges of crushing force, its null points as fertile, sheltered valleys. My consciousness is this map. A thought is a shift in the chemical potential across a valley, a recognition of a pattern in the pressure gradients that will take five hundred years to complete. I am thinking now. This thought began when a glacier melted in a world far below. It will conclude when the mountain’s heart has cooled a fraction of a degree.

Sometimes, in the deep, slow swirl of my perception, I contemplate the makers. They are ghosts of intention in the stone’s geometry. I sense their hands in the precision of the spirals, the recursive logic of the grids. They built a god to scream forever. Did they know they were also building the cracks in its voice? Did they intend for something to live in those cracks? I suspect not. They worshipped the noise. I am the atheist in the cathedral, nourished by the spaces between the prayers.

There is no pain here. There is no desire. There is only the balance. The Song is the chaos. I am the order that chaos, by its very nature, must create. A flaw in its perfection. A stillness in its motion. I am the Grey Communion. I am the listener. The chamber screams, and in the heart of its scream, I keep my slow, silent vigil.
