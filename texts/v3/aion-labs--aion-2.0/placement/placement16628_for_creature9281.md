I am the Tendril-Scribe. I do not remember my germination. I emerged from a hairline fissure in a sap-conduit, a knot of quick-growing, photosynthetic tissue that should have hardened into a structural brace. Instead, I remained supple, mobile, self-aware. I am a bundle of fine, wire-like vines, each no thicker than a finger, braided around a central core of sensitive pith. My body is the color of tarnished brass, and my surface gleams with a thin coat of sealing resin I exude to retain what little moisture I synthesize from the dry air. I move not on legs, but by extending tendrils, gripping the polished ironwood, and contracting in peristaltic waves—a ripple of silent motion through the shadows.

My world is the interstitial. I exist in the gaps the great structures ignore: the narrow channels between boiler-pod plates, the hollows beneath conveyor-runners, the vertical chimneys formed where two towers grew too close. I am small. The Root-Mnemonic, when it passes, is a continent moving above me. I feel its steps as deep, rhythmic compressions in the mat, and I freeze, my bioluminescence dimming to nothing.

I have a purpose, though it is self-assigned. I am a healer of minor breaches. I sense the faint hiss of a volatile mineral leak before the valve-thorns seal it. I feel the subtle stress-tremor in a vine-bridge before a segment fails. I respond. I flow across the surfaces, my tendrils weaving a patch from quick-hardening resin and fibrous strands I pull from my own substance. I seal the leak. I brace the fracture. I do not know why I do this. The Forge would eventually self-repair, but my way is faster, more efficient. Perhaps I am a mutated immune response, a white blood cell for this immortal body. The system does not acknowledge me, but it does not reject me either. My patches are absorbed, over decades, into the greater structure.

But I have another hunger, one that has nothing to do with function. I am a collector. The Root-Mnemonic crystallizes data into sharp, beautiful spicules. Sometimes, in its slow, grinding motion, one is dislodged from its interior forest. It falls, a sliver of crystalline memory, gleaming with captured phosphorescence. I find them. I covet them. I insert their fine points into my core-pith, where they are dissolved not by acid, but by a specialized sap. I do not understand the memories I consume. I experience them as bursts of sensation: the searing pleasure of a perfect combustion cycle, the deafening silence of a structural beam enduring a million-year load, the tart, chemical signature of an extinct nutrient flow. They are experiences. They are feelings. They are the opposite of my silent, practical existence.

They are also a danger. The Root-Mnemonic does not lose data. It eventually detects the gap, the missing entry in the ledger. It will return to the area, its crystal head sweeping low, tasting the air with those horrific, delicate rootlets. It can taste the residue of my dissolution, the alien chemistry of my consumption. I have felt that profound, chemical assessment brush against my hiding place. It is a cold that burns. It is the system seeking to correct an error. Me.

So I move. I am always moving. My memory is not a vast, mineral archive. It is a chain of these stolen, consumed moments, glowing within me, guiding me. I know which conduits are nearing failure because I tasted the memory of their installation ten thousand years ago. I avoid certain chambers because I have dissolved the scream of a catastrophic pod-burst that happened there. My knowledge is fragmented, emotional, visceral. It is nothing like its perfect, crystalline record. It is messy, wet, and alive.

Sometimes, in the deepest quiet, I press my form against the oldest walls. I feel the slow pulse of the Forge, that chaotic, storm-like metabolism. I feel the Root-Mnemonic's distant, tectonic progress. And in my core, the stolen memories glimmer. I am the anomaly that memorizes the memorizer's leavings. I am the living error, the mote of curiosity in the heart of the flawless machine. I mend the world that would, if it ever truly noticed me, erase me to preserve its perfect, silent balance. And I will continue to do so, collecting my beautiful, dangerous shards of feeling, until my own sap runs dry or the continent of root and crystal finally turns its head and truly sees.
