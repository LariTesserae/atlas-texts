Cold first. A deep, patient cold that is not an absence, but a presence. It lives in the stone, breathes from the rock, and seeps into the marrow of things. I feel it through the soles of my feet—not feet, precisely. More like pads, dense with nerve endings, pressed against the obsidian ledge. The cold is a taste on the air, too: metal and flint, wet and sharp.

Sound second. A low hum, constant, the vibration thrumming through the stone at my back. It is the sound of a million wings beating in resonant synchrony—the Hummers, drifting in the gloom. Underneath it, the sporadic shriek-clicks from across the chasm, the wet thud of a water drop falling into the abyss far below, the grinding, digesting drone of the Acid-Worms at their slow work. It is not noise; it is the chamber breathing.

Sight is a gift of patience. At first, only pressure against the eyes, a gloom so thick it feels like wool. Then, slowly, the world resolves in shades of luminescence. The wall before me is not black. It is a moving tapestry of dull brass—the Chorus-Crust, the tarnished ones, each no bigger than my thumb-tendril. They move in a wave, a slow tide across the rock, and on their backs, a faint, pulsing glow. My own chest answers with a soft, rhythmic pulse of light—a cultivated colony of bacteria, my silent speech. I look down at myself. My torso is long, flexible, covered in skin that drinks the light. My limbs—six of them—are relaxed, multi-jointed, coiled against the cold. At the end of each, a cluster of fine, prehensile tendrils rests on the stone, feeling its minute vibrations.

I am a Glimmer-Finger. The name is not a name I know, for I know no names. I am a pattern in the dark, a knot of curiosity.

I rise from the sitting-depression worn into the ledge. Generations of my kind have sat here, watching. My movements are silent, my black form blending into the obsidian. I turn my two large eyes upward. High above, a slit of bruised violet sky is visible, a distant memory of something else. A fine mist drifts down from it, coating my skin in a slick, phosphorescent sheen. It tastes of distant cold.

My tendrils explore the ledge by habit. They trace the familiar grooves, find the small pile of river-smoothed pebbles I was working on yesterday—or was it a sleep-cycle ago? Time is slow here. I pick up a pebble. It is cool, almost round. My tendrils assess its weight, its balance. I have a concept: *stack*. A defiance of the mountain’s relentless downward pull. I place it carefully on top of another. The tower is unstable. It leans. I adjust with a tendril, feeling for the center. There. It holds.

A sudden, piercing whistle echoes from the depths. It is the Long-Throat, the apex. The sound is a blade of sound slicing through the hum. All movement in the chamber stills for a heartbeat. The wave of tarnished ones freezes. The Hummers’ drone falters. My own chest-light flares a bright, urgent blue: *danger*. Then, the whistle fades. The chamber exhales. The rhythms resume. The Long-Throat is not hunting now. It is singing to the deep stone, reminding us of its presence.

I turn from my pebbles and creep to the edge of the ledge. My tendrils grip the sharp rim. Below is only darkness, a well of echoing sound and slow decay. But I can hear the Acid-Worms. Their grinding drone is closer here, from a pillar of stone to my left. I move toward it, my limbs unfolding in their strange, graceful articulation.

I see them. A colony of three, their pallid, segmented bodies moving in a viscous, coordinated flow. Their heads—if they are heads—are pressed to the rock, exuding a clear fluid that smokes faintly and smells of ozone. The obsidian softens, turns to a slurry they suck back in. They are creating a smooth, glassy groove, a new path. They are patient sculptors. I watch, my eyes drinking in the process. I have a concept: *change*. It is slower than sleep, but it is undeniable.

One of the worms senses my presence. It stops its grinding. A segment of its body rotates, revealing a cluster of sensory pits. It does not see me, but it feels the heat of my body, the vibration of my stillness. It decides I am not a threat—I am too small, too clever to be prey for its kind. It returns to its work.

I reach out a single tendril, not to touch the worm, but to touch the freshly dissolved stone. The slurry is warm, slightly acidic. I pull back, my tendril tip stained with a faint phosphorescence from the minerals. I bring it to my mouth-parts—a small, beaked opening below my eyes. I taste it. Information floods me: silicon, iron, traces of silver. The taste of the mountain’s blood.

A flicker of movement above. A Shriek-Spider launches itself from a fissure, a dark bolt against the bioluminescent mist. Its elastic silk strand anchors it mid-air, and it snatches a drifting Hummer from the air. The Hummer’s drone cuts off with a soft pop. The spider reels itself back into its fissure, a brief shriek-click of triumph echoing. I feel a pulse of something complex—not fear, not hunger. A recognition of *process*. The Hummer was part of the chorus. Now it is part of the spider. The pattern continues.

I return to my ledge. My family is stirring from our high cave. I see their chest-lights flashing in the dark above, soft greens and ambers: *awake*, *curious*, *come*. I flash back: *here*, *watching*, *safe*.

I am part of the membrane of life here. The cold, the wet, the slow tectonic dreams are my home. But I am also separate. The pebble tower is separate. The curiosity that makes me taste the acid-slurry is separate. In the silent, flashing language of my chest, I hold a concept that is still forming, still nebulous: *why*.

I settle back into the sitting-depression, my tendrils resting on the cool stone. I watch the wave of tarnished ones complete its slow circuit. I listen to the grinding drone, the constant hum, the occasional wet thud from the abyss. The mist coats me. The cold seeps in. I am here, in the throat of the mountain. I am the watcher, the puzzler, the first flicker of a mind in the everlasting dark. And I am content to be so, for now.
