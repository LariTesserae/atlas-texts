I am a Tender. My name, if I have one, is the pattern of lichen on the back of my left hand, a whorl like a fossilized fingerprint. I have been here for a long time, though time is not a river here; it is a deepening pool. My skin is the color of the young fern-bark, pale and rubbery, cool to the touch. My hair is not hair; it is a cascade of *Usnea*, fine and grey-green, stirred only by the gentlest movements of the mist.

Today, I am going to the Half-Swallowed Pump.

The path is not a path. It is a memory of alignment. The moss is thinner along a line where cobbles once lay, and the mist, as always, avoids this strip as if out of polite habit. I walk slowly. My feet, long-toed and broad, press into the velvet moss without sound. Each step is a decision, a consultation with the damp earth. I feel the faint, rhythmic *thump-thump* of the basin’s deep heart through my soles. It is the geothermal pump, far below, still pushing warmth and movement into the root systems. The Half-Swallowed Pump is its older cousin, a relic from a more frantic age, now being assimilated.

I carry no tools. My tools are my hands, and the pouches of my woven-moss belt. In them: a paste of crushed bioluminescent spores, a clay jar of water from the Condensation Well, and a few smooth, dark stones polished by the tarnished millipedes.

The pump emerges from the base of a great Root-Mind. The iron casing, once proud and cylindrical, is now a crumpled, rust-blossomed embrace. The Root-Mind—I think of it as *She Who Hums in B-flat*—has sent a major root directly through a fracture in the housing. The root is not crushing; it is filling, stabilizing, making the metal a part of its own circulatory system. Rivets pop like seeds. Around the base, a garden of blue-black ferns thrives in the iron-rich soil excreted by the millipedes.

My task is not repair. It is facilitation.

I kneel in the soft peat, the cool damp immediately soaking through the knee of my fibrous leggings. I place my palms flat on the ground, feeling the subterranean hum. It is a question. *I am here*, I answer, not with words, but with a stillness. The root above me seems to glow slightly from within, a soft chartreuse light pulsing in time with the deep thumps.

The issue is a seep. Not of water, but of memory. From a crack where the root meets a brass valve assembly, a faint, silvery vapor whispers out. It smells of hot oil and loneliness. It is an Echo, leaking. An imprint of the pump’s last operator, his fear of the silence coming, his desperate checks on gauges that no longer exist. The vapor tries to form a shape—a hand reaching for a lever—but dissipates with a sigh.

The Echoes are not harmful, but they are inefficient. They are energy stuck in a loop. The Root-Mind seeks to integrate everything, even memory. My role is to help translate.

From my belt, I take the clay jar. The water within is not just water; it is from the Well, which holds the condensed breath of the basin itself, full of minute, sleeping spores. I pour a little into my palm. With my other hand, I gather some of the blue-black fern dust, rich with metallic decay. I mix them into a paste on my palm, the color of a stormy twilight.

I lean forward and, with two fingers, gently apply the paste to the crack where the Echo leaks. The touch is intimate. The metal is cold, the root is warm and vibrant. The paste smokes faintly, not with heat, but with a release of captured time. I begin to hum. It is not a tune; it is the *shush-shush* of the deep piston, mirrored in my own breath. The hum vibrates in my chest, through my arms, into my fingers.

The paste begins to grow. Tiny, hair-fine filaments of mycelium emerge from it, white as mist. They reach into the crack, into the vapor. They do not block the Echo; they give it a pathway. The silvery memory-vapor is drawn down the fungal threads, into the root. I can feel the Root-Mind receiving it. The hum in B-flat deepens, becomes a chord. The memory of fear and oil is unpacked, examined with vegetative patience. The fear is dissolved into a simple pressure differential. The oil becomes a nutrient trace. The loneliness… the loneliness is recognized as an absence of connection, and is soothed by the vast, interconnected web of roots that now holds the pump.

The leaking stops. The crack is now sealed with a living, silvery lichen that pulses softly in sync with the root’s inner light. Integration.

I sit back. The process has taken most of the morning. A cloud of wingless gnats drifts into the space above the pump, swirling in a complex helix pattern. They are sensing the new equilibrium, mapping the change in the air’s electrical taste. I watch them, my thoughts slow and green.

From the corner of my eye, I see an Echo fully form. It is the pump-operator, but he is not fearful now. He is simply checking the valve, a routine gesture. As his mist-hand turns the mist-lever, the new lichen on the crack glows brightly for a second. The Echo pauses, seems to look at his own hand, then at the glowing root embracing his machine. He nods, a gesture of profound understanding. Then he dissolves, not with a sigh, but with the soft *plink* of a water drop hitting a copper plate. He has been integrated, too.

I take one of the polished stones from my belt and place it at the base of the pump, alongside three others from previous visits. An offering of respect, and a marker of process.

Rising is a slow unfurling. I feel the damp air on my bark-skin. A tarnished millipede, its body a segmented jewel of translucent grey, winds over my foot, following the new chemical trail left by the fungal seal. It will process any remaining rust, complete the alchemy.

I am a Tender. This is where I find myself. In the small, patient space between a memory and a root, facilitating the quiet, endless conversation between what was built and what has grown. I turn and walk back into the mist, following the memory of the street, the deep *thump-thump* of the basin’s heart my only compass. There is no next task, only the next breath, the next drop of condensation, the next slow, green thought of the world.
