I am graveldweller. The word is not a word, but a rhythm in the meat. It is the *thrum-thrum-thrum* of the plateau in my marrow, the *plock-plock-plock* of my own broad feet in the cold slurry. I am one of many. We are a dark river over the rising swells, a single organism of shaggy hides and steaming breath.

The cold is not an enemy. It is the condition of existence. It seeps up from the damp clay, through the porous pads of my hooves, into the dense bones of my legs. My hide—long, coarse filaments the color of a storm sky—drinks the mist. I feel each bead condense on the outer hairs, drawn down along the shafts by some ancient capillary thirst, until it seeps into the skin beneath. I am always drinking. The water tastes of iron and crushed stone. It is the taste of the world being born.

My head hangs low, close to the weeping rock. My eyes, wide and dark, see in a spectrum of greys and damp blues. The light has no direction. It glows from everywhere and nowhere, erasing shadows, blending the horizon into a soft smear of mist and cloud. I see for miles across the gentle rolls of the plateau, and the emptiness is a presence. It is the space between the slow beats of the hum.

The hum. It is everything. It is in the air, a sub-auditory pressure that vibrates the delicate bones of my inner ear. It is in the ground, a steady pulse that travels up through my legs into my barrel chest. When I chew the slick biofilm from the rocks, I feel the vibration in my jaw, a counterpoint to the grinding of my molars. The hum tells us where to go. It is not a sound we hear, but a tide we feel. A shift in its harmonic texture—a slight thickening in the lower registers—signals a place where the rock is weeping most richly, where the mineral sweat is freshest. We turn as one, without discussion. The herd’s mind is a collective attunement.

My tongue is a rasp of hardened cartilage. I extend it, scrape it along a seam of dark stone. The biofilm comes away, a cold, salty-slimy paste that coats my tongue. It is rich with the ghosts of minerals, with the slow ferment of primordial algae. As I feed, I clean the rock. My passing leaves the stone darker, wetter, ready for the next phase of its sweating. My dung, when I drop it, is not waste. It is a black, steaming catalyst that the engineers will find and break apart, seeding the gravel with possibility.

I move with the herd. Our movement is a geological process. Our thousand hooves knead the clay, squeezing water from it, compacting it here, loosening it there. We are part of the plateau’s compaction and drainage. Above us, a sudden flash—a twist of iridescent light. The tinsel-flits. Their wingbeats are a brittle, frantic music that rides atop the hum. They are mapping the air we stir, reading the vapors of our breath. Sometimes one darts down, its quartz-click beak snatching a silica-midge from the air above my back. We are connected. Their dance is a reflection of our mass, our heat.

I know the shroud-stalkers are out there. I have never seen one clearly. They are silence given form. But I have felt the disruption. Once, a calf at the edge of the herd fell ill. Its rhythm faltered. It stepped out of sync with the hum, creating a tiny, dissonant echo. By the next dawn, it was gone. Only a depression in the clay, slowly filling with mist-beads, and a scent of iron that was quickly washed away by the damp. The stalkers are the plateau’s quality control. They cull the untuned. We do not fear them; we are calibrated by their presence.

Today, the hum deepens. A new resonance, felt through the soles of my feet. The herd shifts, a great, slow turn to the east. We are flowing toward a new basin, where the weeping is strong. The mist thickens around us, beading on every hair until I am a walking cloud, a fragment of the plateau’s exhalation. I am cold. I am damp. I am drinking the world as it forms. My heart beats in time with the industrial hum—the engine of a landscape still dreaming itself into being. I am not a visitor here. I am a worker on the factory floor of genesis.
