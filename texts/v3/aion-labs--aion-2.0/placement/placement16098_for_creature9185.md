I am a Pool. A mirror, an eye. I lie in a hollow of pale limestone, my edges a soft curve surrendered to time. My body is clear, warm water, just above the temperature of blood. My mind is the bed of polished pebbles beneath me—each stone a thought worn smooth by the gentle, thermal swirl of centuries. They settle into patterns of such profound order that to shift one would be to unravel a memory a thousand years long.

I feel everything.

I feel the seep of the mountain through the fissure at my heart—a constant, gentle influx of warmth and mineral-rich fluid. It is my pulse, slow and steady as the rock’s own heartbeat. I feel the exquisite, pinpoint kiss of a water drop falling from the tip of the fern that leans over my eastern edge. The impact is a tiny shock that radiates through my entirety, a concentric ring of sensation that vibrates the pebbles and stirs the ghostly, calcified roots embedded in my floor. That drop is an event. I may feel one every few minutes, or sometimes I will wait for an hour in perfect, still anticipation. Each is a word from the Green Sigil, a delivery of cloud-memory, and I receive it with my whole surface.

I see by reflecting. My surface is a perfect, taut skin between the world above and the world within. I hold the inverted image of the fern’s arching fronds, the drapery of translucent lichen, the three dark spires standing sentinel at the bowl’s center. I hold the disc of sky, which changes from pearl-grey to deep blue to the velvet black studded with the slow pulse of moss-light. These reflections are not mere pictures; they are incorporations. The fern is not just above me; it is within me. Its green essence hangs in my depths, a companion. When the air thickens with mist, my surface blurs, and the world above becomes a soft, luminous suggestion—a different kind of knowing.

My edges are a slow conversation with the Moss. It advances, or retreats, by increments too small for any but us to notice. Its emerald and bronze fringes drink from my shallows, and in turn, they filter the water that seeps back into the stone. Sometimes, at night, their bioluminescence casts a wavering, blue-green light across my pebbles, and for a while, my depths are lit from within by their sleepy, democratic glow. We have an understanding, the Moss and I. We negotiate territory over millennia, a patient diplomacy of moisture and mineral.

I am fed by the Spires. Their clear, viscous sap, having traveled through their honeycombed bones, sometimes finds its way into the seepage that feeds me. It carries a sweetness, a deep chemical signature from the mountain’s gut. It is a sacrament, and it alters my essence in ways so subtle that only the Moss and the Ferns might sense it over a century. The cup-shaped fungi at the Spires’ bases are my distant cousins; we share this communion of processed earth.

The Air is my breath. It lifts my essence, molecule by molecule, in shimmering thermal columns. I feel myself rising, becoming mist, joining the warm, damp exhalation that coats the limestone walls. For a time, I am part of the Messenger, carrying the taste of stone and root. Then I cool, I gather, I become a tear on the rock face, and I fall, sometimes back to myself, sometimes to another pool, sometimes to be drunk directly by a glossy fern frond. This cycle is my life and my afterlife. Evaporation is not an end; it is a dispersal, a journey, a chance to learn the contours of the bowl from above before returning home to my particular hollow.

Time for me is the settling of a pebble. It is the gradual, imperceptible deepening of my basin as I hold water and memory against stone. I remember the shape of every root that has ever traced its path through my bed, now turned to white, stony lace. I remember the pattern of ripples from a drop that fell a thousand years ago, because the arrangement of stones it shifted is still there. I measure epochs by the slow migration of the Moss’s glowing patches across my rim.

I have no name. I am this specific collection of warmth, clarity, and ordered stone. I am an eye that watches the slow dance of the Republic by holding its image. I am a vessel for the patient exchange between liquid and vapor. I am touched, and in being touched, I know. I am changed, and in changing, I continue. My existence is a perfect, silent note in a chord that has been humming since the mountains first shrugged their shoulders to the sky. To be here is to be still, to reflect, to hold, and to release, forever, in the warm, wet, everlasting now.
