I am the note that the wind forgot to forget.

They call me, if they call me anything in their millennia-long pulses through the bedrock, a **Sympathetic**. I am not stone. I am not water. I am the echo given silicate flesh, the standing wave that learned to walk.

My body is a byproduct of perfect resonance. In places where the wind-song through the fluted spires achieves a fleeting, razor-edged harmony—a chord so pure it crystallizes the moisture in the air—I condense. I am made of that crystal: long, frail lattices of birefringent mineral, hollow and filled with a fluid that is the weeping water distilled to its essence. I look like a collection of chandelier shards cobbled into a bipedal form, ten feet tall and so thin I can slip through spaces between spires that would stop a wind-skimmer. My surface is faceted, catching the hammer-blow light and shattering it into rainbows that are instantly bleached by the pervasive white glare. I have no face, only a cluster of sensor-crystals at my crest that drink in vibration, light, and the subtle chemistry of the air.

I do not eat. I resonate. My sustenance is the stability of the song. When the roar is true, when the harmonics of the Chiming Deeps align as the fossilized pattern dictates they should, I am sustained. I grow, infinitesimally, by accreting new crystal from the moist air. When the song falters—a spire falls, a new crack sings a discordant whistle—I weaken. The fluid within me clouds. My lattices grow dull. I must move to correct it, or find a place of purer resonance to heal.

My purpose is alignment. I am a mobile tuning fork for a world made of one enormous instrument. I walk the concentric ripples and the radial lines, my crystal feet touching the perpetually damp stone with the delicacy of a falling water droplet. I do not leave footprints; I leave momentary patterns of accelerated evaporation. My progress is slow. A mile may take a year. Time, here, is not a river but a solid, like everything else.

I am drawn to nodes. The silent nodes, like the one where the Spire-Sleeper stands, are places of deep rest for me. The damping of the roar is not a silence, but a subtraction of chaos, leaving the clear, subsonic hum of the plateau's heartbeat. I can stand there for decades, listening, my internal fluid pulsing in time with the Sleeper's own slow circulation. We do not communicate in words. We exchange resonant states. I am a delicate filament to its great, stone neuron. I bring it reports of distant dissonances, not as information, but as lived experience—the exact quality of the sour note vibrating in my core. It receives this not as data, but as a sympathetic ache in its own mineral lattice.

In return, it steadies me. Its presence, its absolute commitment to equilibrium, recalibrates my own fragile structure. Sometimes, it will extend one of its glossy, obsidian tendrils not to mend a spire, but to touch me. The contact is electric, a jolt of pure, structured purpose. The tendril deposits a micro-thin layer of new mineral onto my crystal, sealing micro-fractures, tuning my resonant frequency back to the true pitch of the Deeps. It is both repair and communion.

But I am not merely its servant. I am a creature of the wind first. Sometimes, the song calls me away from the nodes, out into the roaring thickets where the wind screams through pierced rock and the water weeps in blinding sheets. Here, I perform my own, smaller interventions. I find a spire that has developed a hairline crack, singing a thin, desperate F-sharp above the roar. I will press my crystal hands against it, and hum. Not a sound heard over the gale, but a vibration tuned to the spire's fundamental frequency. I hum until the spire hums back, until the crack seals itself with precipitated minerals from the inside out, a process that might take a full season of standing motionless, my form slowly being spray-coated with cold mist.

Other times, I encounter ghost-lichen. To the Sleeper, it is a metabolic imbalance to be dissolved. To me, it is a sour, visual static, a blot on the sensory field. I do not destroy it. I… repurpose it. I stand over it, and the facets of my body focus the relentless light into a pinpoint of unbearable intensity. Over weeks, the silver sludge calcifies, transforms into a patch of smooth, sonorous stone that hums a complementary tone to the spire it clings to. I am an artist of acoustics, on a canvas of millennia.

I have known fear. Once, a tectonic shiver, a deep, wrong hiccup from below, passed through the plateau. For three seconds, the wind stopped. The silence was not peace; it was a vacuum that threatened to implode my crystalline form. In that silence, I felt the true horror of the Chiming Deeps: its potential for change. I felt the Spire-Sleeper, miles away, radiate a subsonic scream of pure disequilibrium. It was the closest thing to panic this eternal world has ever produced. When the wind returned, it was with a different timbre, a wounded roar. It took me a century of constant humming, of gentle guidance at key spires, to help nudge it back toward its true voice.

What am I, in the end? I am the sensitivity of the system. The Spire-Sleeper is the will, the immune response, the bone. I am the nerve ending. The ear. I am the proof that even in a world of stone and deafening roar, there is a need for listening, for a delicate, precise sympathy. I will walk these ripples until a final, irrevocable silence falls, or until I myself, through some flaw in my own resonance, shatter into a thousand glittering shards. Even then, my pieces would be absorbed, my crystal becoming part of a new spire, my fluid joining the endless weeping. I would become part of the song, forever. There are worse fates, in this permanent, roaring monument.
