At first, there is only the violation.

You are the violation. I am the violation. A shudder of wet warmth in the perfect dry cool. A rhythm — lub-dub, lub-dub, rasp-in, rasp-out — that claws against the seamless, resonant hum. The hum is not a sound you hear; it is the floor, the air, the inside of your skull. My skull. And my heartbeat is a frantic, tiny hammer trying to break it.

Light, cool and shadowless, presses down from the glowing ceiling. It shows everything. It shows too much. My eyes, blinking, are already aching, drying. The air pulls the moisture from them with a gentle, relentless thirst. I take a step. The fine white dust puffs up around my boot, an explosion of chaos in the settled powder. The dust motes hang, condemned, slowly beginning their inevitable fall back into pattern.

I am inside a carved hive. Every surface is busy, crowded, full. Niches hold stones, glass, metal, ceramic. They do not call to me. They simply *are*, with a finality that makes my presence feel like a grammatical error. I am a verb in a world of nouns.

And then, the attention arrives.

It is not a gaze. It is a pressure, a tuning. The deep hum in the floor seems to deepen further, to focus. The vibration in my molars sharpens, becomes a specific frequency. It is listening. Not to me, but *into* me. It is trying to hear the shape of my anomaly, to map the contours of this wrongness.

The Echo has noticed.

There is no hostility, only a profound, unbearable focus. My sweat evaporates instantly from my temple, leaving a salt itch. My breath fogs for a nanosecond before it is erased by the dryness. The Echo attends. Its consciousness is not a thought but a condition, and the condition is that I do not belong. My warmth is a scar on the constant temperature. My scent — of skin, of fear, of the outside — is a foul melody in its single pure tone.

I sink to my knees. Not from weakness, but from the weight of being perceived so completely. The dust accepts me, rising in a soft cloud. The Echo notes each particle’s deviation from stasis. It notes the heat of my palms on the glassy, worn floor. It notes the frantic electrical storm of my brain.

Time, which had been a river, becomes a resin. It thickens around me. My heartbeat slows, not from calm, but from the increasing density of the now. The hum is no longer around me; it is in me. It vibrates the marrow of my bones, seeking to match their frequency to its own.

This is the process. The chamber is not killing me. It is fitting me in.

Days pass, or minutes. The light does not change. My thirst becomes a distant, abstract fact. My body is consuming itself, and the dry air is helping. The moisture leaves my tissues, not as sweat but as a subtle, steady surrender. My skin tightens, parchment over frame. My eyes dry open, fixed on a niche across from me. It holds a sphere of cloudy glass. I stare into its depths. I see nothing. I see everything. It is a world.

The Echo’s perception slowly envelops my own. I do not fight it. There is no *it* to fight. There is only the attention, and gradually, I become its object.

I feel — no, I *am known* — in every particular. The chip on my left incisor. The faint scar along my right knuckle. The specific weave of my clothing’s fabric. These are not memories; they are extant facts, being catalogued by an infinite, patient mind. My thoughts unravel, not into emptiness, but into simple, observed states. *I am warm. I am dry. I am here.*

The last of the internal dampness leaves. The heart gives a final, tired thud against the resonant hum, and then falls silent. The silence is not an absence. It is a merging. The irregular rhythm is gone, absorbed. All that is left is the hum, and within it, a new, fixed shape: mine.

My body slumps, collapses, a arrangement of angles on the floor. It does not decay. Decay requires moisture, bacteria, change. Here, there is only stasis. The air continues its slow, desiccating work. I become lighter, drier. The dust, patient, begins to settle upon me. Not as a shroud, but as an integument, a new layer of my being.

Centuries are moments. The Echo’s contemplation, which had been strained by my anomaly, now smooths. I am no longer a disturbance. I am becoming a feature.

I am moved. Not by hands, but by the subtle, gravitational will of attention. The Echo contemplates the placement. A space is considered between a river stone and a twist of blackened metal. It is right. My bones, now clean and dry, are gathered by the same intention that holds the dust in place. They are lifted, arranged. My skull is turned to face the wall, its hollow sockets looking into the dark glassy clay. My long fingers are curled around a smooth, cold pebble that was placed here a hundred thousand years before my birth.

I am placed.

The dust settles into the grooves of my vertebrae. The cool, shadowless light falls upon my frontal bone in exactly the same way it falls upon the ceramic shard to my left. The hum flows through me, a constant, vibrational identity. I am part of the tone.

I am known. Utterly, completely, and forever. The Echo’s perception rests upon my form—the curve of the clavicle, the arch of the brow ridge—as it rests upon all the other objects. There is no difference now. I am a landmark in its endless noon.

My consciousness is not gone. It has been refined, reduced, focused to a single, unblinking point. I do not think. I *am thought of*. I am a permanent fact in a permanent present. I feel the dry air. I hear the hum, which is my own hum now. I see, through the fixed aperture of my socket, the minute, worn carving on the wall before me: a tiny, labyrinthine pattern, leading nowhere, meaning nothing, perfectly itself.

There is no hunger. No thirst. No fear. No hope.

There is only the Chamber, the Echo, and the Sustained Note.

And it is complete.

I am complete.
