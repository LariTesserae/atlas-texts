I am the glasswort. The crack in the stone is my world; the slow, warm current is my breath. I do not remember taking root. There was only the darkness of the fissure, the minute seep of minerals, and then the imperative to reach. So I reached, my segmented stems pushing out into the liquid volume, each joint a decision, each tip a yearning.

My existence is a patient stretching. I am not one thing, but a colony of many, a forest in miniature. My stems are hollow, filled with the same warm brine that surrounds me, and through them I taste the chamber. I taste the salt, the faint tang of limestone, the metallic whisper of iron from the basalt cube. I taste the slow decay of the algae that beards the walls—a green, earthy note on the constant current.

And I taste the light. It is my primary language. When the sun-spear strikes the water high above, it fractures into a thousand dancing coins that shower the chamber. My uppermost stems bask in that gold, drinking it down, converting it into the slow pulse of life. My lower stems, shrouded in the gentle shadow of the cube, wait their turn as the light-disc crawls. My whole being sways in unison, not by choice, but because the water moves me. I am a instrument played by the hydraulic sigh.

I know the cube, a dark presence that anchors the light. I know the lily-column, a silent neighbor. But my deepest knowing is reserved for the one who sits upon the cube.

The Keeper of Stillness is my counterpoint. Where I am many, it is one. Where I am flexible, it is fixed. Where I am perpetually reaching, it is perpetually receiving. I do not think it knows me, not as I know it. Its awareness is a vast, placid mirror, reflecting the whole room. Mine is a focused, thirsty attention.

Our connection is the droplet.

Each day, as the light begins to bleed away into turquoise twilight, a change stirs in the Keeper. I feel it not through sight, for I have none, but through a subtle shift in the water’s pressure, a minute alteration in the flow patterns around the cube. The slow, syncopated pulse of its torso hesitates. Then, with a grace that belies its stony form, a hand rises.

From my crack, I witness this ritual through the distortion of water. The extended finger, a spire of calcified silt. The gathering at its tip—not a bubble, but a droplet heavier than the surrounding sea, rich with what it has drawn from the light and from the Keeper’s own substance. It holds the day’s final, concentrated brilliance, a tiny sun.

Then, the release.

*Plink.*
A vibration too subtle for hearing, but I feel it through the stone of my root-crack. The droplet falls, strikes the cube, and begins its journey. A carved channel, no wider than a thread, guides it. The droplet does not roll; it glides, a mercury pearl drawn by a destiny etched in stone. It travels the short span from the plinth to my domain.

When it arrives, it merges with the water at the entrance to my crack. It is a gift, a sacrament. It carries a potent blend of dissolved minerals and, I believe, a faint echo of the sunlight the Keeper absorbed. This is what I reach for. This is why I grow. My finest root-hairs absorb it, and a thrill of vitality, deep and quiet, spreads through my colony. It is the closing of a circuit: light to Keeper, Keeper to droplet, droplet to me. In the giving, the Keeper is sustained. In the receiving, I am.

I will never cover the chamber floor. My purpose is not conquest, but continuation. I am the chamber’s quiet proof of life, its soft, green exhalation. The Keeper is its soul, its still point. Together, in the warm, roaring silence, we perform the only work that matters: the slow, eternal exchange. We turn light into stone into water into growth. We are the breathing of this drowned room.

When the walls finally slump, and the light fails, and the current stills, I will likely wither. But perhaps, in that final darkness, a last stem will hold a memory of gold, and a memory of the perfect, falling droplet that connected me, utterly, to the stillness.
