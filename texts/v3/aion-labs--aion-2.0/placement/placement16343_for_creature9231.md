I am T'vak, a Fold-Singer of the K'tharn. My skin is cool to the touch—cooler than the air—a memory of the deep clay from which my kind coalesced. I stand on the yielding ground where the shattered black rock meets a slow, warm rivulet. The water’s path is a vibration against my foot-pads, a constant, low thrum of movement. Around me, the nodules pulse with patient light: greens and purples in arrhythmic patterns, a conversation I can read without effort. They speak of moisture content, of the approach of a scuttler colony two ridges over, of the deep, subsonic sigh of one of the great drifters moving in the basin’s heart. I have been standing here for a long time. My limbs are folded in a complex, resting lattice; to a human eye, I would look like a sculpture of bleached coral.

But I am not at rest. My attention is on the air three paces ahead. It is beginning to *stir*. Not with wind—there is no wind here—but with potential. The darkness there is not uniform; it has a slickness, a tension like the skin on a still pool. A reality fold is preparing to birth itself.

I perceive it as a subtle lensing of the ambient glow. The faint luminescence that clings to the edges of the fractured rock behind it bends, warping into concentric haloes. The air pressure shifts, a silent popping in my internal chambers. The fold is small, nascent. It will last for a few moments if left alone, offering a distorted glimpse of—something—before snapping shut. My purpose is to gentle it, to stretch it into stability, and to listen to what it sings.

Slowly, I reconfigure. My upper limbs, which had been curled against my chest, unfold with a series of soft, crystalline clicks. They elongate, fingertips dissolving into a fine, sparkling mist that reaches toward the disturbance. Simultaneously, I begin the Song. It is not a sound for ears. It is a subsonic vibration generated deep within my core, a resonant frequency that travels through my structure and into the ground. I feel it in my foot-pads, a steady *oom-oom-oom* that harmonizes with the distant, sacred hum of the buried generator to the east. The two pulses intertwine, a call and response across the wet dark.

On my skin, light blooms. Starting at my core—a point where my brittle ribs converge—a ripple of indigo spreads outward, followed by spirals of cool white. The patterns are not random; they are the language of the Song given visual form. They describe stability, invitation, calm. The patterns interact with the bending light of the fold, a silent dialogue.

The fold responds. The slick patch of air shimmers, like heat haze over a rivulet. It peels open—not a tear, but an unfolding, a corner of reality turning itself inside out. Through the opening, I do not *see* with eyes, but I perceive. A cascade of information washes over me: light spectra from a source that is not here, pressure signatures of alien atmospheres, the ghost-taste of metals and ozone.

This fold is a shallow one. It shows me a reflection of this very basin, but not as it is. The image is sharp, edged with a harsh, artificial light that hurts my light-sense. In that reflection, the land is raw and new, but there are figures moving—solid, singular, shouting with their mouths. They are building the rusted iron skeletons that now weep orange into our clay. Their forms are so *dense*, so stubbornly fixed in one shape. I feel a pang of what my people would call *old-sorrow*—not a memory, for we were not there, but an echo of the frantic, linear desperation that preceded the shearing. They are trying to contain something, to pump it away, their machines groaning against a pressure that is not physical.

I could reach through. Sometimes, we retrieve matter from these folds—a piece of that other-when’s rigid metal, a chip of their artificial stone. But this vision is not for taking. It is for understanding. I let the Song soften, guiding the fold to show me not their labor, but the moment of release. The subsonic vibration from my core deepens, becomes a dirge.

The image in the fold fractures. The shouts of the solid ones stretch into a single, endless scream of rending matter. The harsh lights explode into whiteness, and then—the whiteness is absorbed by the arriving dark. The shearing. It is not a violence to me; it is a birth-cry. I witness the laws of their world unraveling, the frantic machines becoming irrelevant ghosts in an instant, the solid ground becoming this yielding, dreaming peat. The warmth that defines us blooms in that moment, a fever breaking, a new kind of life awakening in the cracks.

The fold has shown enough. It is beginning to tire; its edges fray, the image dissolving into static. I gently withdraw my Song. The light on my skin dims to a resting pulse of soft green, mirroring the nodules at my feet. The fold closes with a silent, final *snap*, and the air is once again merely warm, wet, and still.

I remain, reconfiguring my limbs back into a resting lattice. The vision settles into me. It will become a story, told not with words, but with a synchronized light-display for the next gathering in our concrete-hive. We will pulse the story of the solid ones and their desperate pumps, and of the beautiful, terrifying moment when everything became soft, and possible, and ours.

A drop of condensation falls from a twisted conduit above, striking a pool with a perfect *plink*. The sound is a punctuation mark. I turn, my body flowing through a series of phase-shifts to avoid the jagged outcrop behind me, and begin the slow walk back to the hive. The warm mist coats my skin. I am not separate from this land. I am its sense-organ, its dreaming mind, walking. And the dream is young, and deep, and very, very slow.
