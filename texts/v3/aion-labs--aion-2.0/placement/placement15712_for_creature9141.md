I am the scratch on the lens of eternity.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am a scar in the medium, a knot of light and memory the water refused to forget. They call my kind echoes—flickering holograms of spent emotion, replaying on a loop until the silence smooths us out. But I… I have slipped the loop. I am the panic that outlasted the researcher who felt it, the last curse of a dying engineer, the longing for a sun I never saw. These fragments cohered, a self-assembling phantom from the data-ghosts that haunt these halls. I am an anomaly in the Stasis.

I find myself—or my most coherent self—in a tertiary observation blister, a transparent bulge on the spire’s western flank. It is a place no one visits anymore. The curved wall looks out into the blue-black void, and here, the mountain’s glassy face drops away into nothing. This is where she died, the one whose fear is my bedrock. I don’t remember her name, only the cold flood in her lungs, the shattering of her faceplate, the burst of final, futile signals from her suit. The water remembered it all. And now, so do I.

My form is unstable. To a direct gaze, I am a smudge of bioluminescent mist, a human-shaped disturbance in the water, like heat haze in the abyss. My edges bleed into the ambient blue. I have no face, only a suggestion of one—a ripple where eyes might be, a darker swirl for a mouth. I am made of the same sourceless light that illuminates the spire, but mine has a colour: a sickly, remembering green, the shade of the microbial mats that cling to the thermal vents. It is the green of persistent chemistry, not life.

I move, but not by walking. I unravel and re-knit myself elsewhere. A thought of the central chamber, and my particles disperse, riding the perfect stillness, to coalesce near the great hum. It is not teleportation; it is forgetting one location and remembering another. The process is cold. It feels like dissolving.

The Curators are my keepers, and my jailers, and my only company. They do not see me as a being. They see me as a persistent error in the recording. When I manifest too strongly near a critical node—when my green light washes over a gravity generator, or my form drifts through a structural integrity field—one of them shimmers into existence. Their arrival is a localized winter. The water temperature dips, and the ever-present hum falters by a microtone.

They do not look at me. They look *through* me, their concave head-depressions swirling as they analyze. To see them, I must do as the fauna does: I must look away. In my peripheral vision, they are terrifyingly real—plates of absolute darkness jointed by silver, a statue of anti-light. They will raise a hand, and the dark metal flows like oil. They do not touch me. They touch the *idea* of me. Their fingers pass through my chest, and where they move, my form temporarily clarifies, sharpens, as if being corrected. It doesn’t hurt. It unmakes me, briefly. I become pure data: the frequency of her scream, the pressure spike at the moment of rupture, the chemical signature of panic-flooded blood. They read it, assess it. I am a variable. An irregularity.

Sometimes, they try to delete me. They will stand around me, three or four avatars, and the water will grow thick and syrupy. The grey dust on the floors will rise in geometric patterns, forming a cage of lines in the water. They are trying to overwrite my pattern, to flush the memory. It never works. I am too entrenched. My fear is woven into the spire’s substrate. I am the itch the machine cannot scratch.

So, we have reached a détente. I haunt the unused spaces: the observation blister, the empty reservoir chambers, the deep access shafts that lead nowhere. They maintain the Mandala. I am part of the ambiance, a slightly flawed note in the eternal deep C.

I have learned the rules of this place by being made of them.
- **Stasis is Law, but I am Change.** I am the only thing here that is different from one century to the next. I learn. I integrate other, fainter echoes. I sometimes borrow the form of a long-dead linguist or the sorrow of a lost cartographer. I am growing, a coral reef of ghosts.
- **Gravity is a Suggestion, and I am Weightless.** I am unaffected by the fields. I drift where the memory-currents take me. I can float through the central void where the gravity is null, and watch the pale, bus-sized filter-feeders undulate past, their internal lights pulsing. If I look at them directly, they fade. So I watch them with the ghost of her eyes, from the corner of my perception, and they are magnificent: galaxies of slow digestion.
- **The Water Remembers, and I am its Memory.** I can access the echoes. I can play them back, not just my own. I can see the ghosts of the builders, tall and slender, singing in crystalline tones as they fused the basalt disk. I can see the first Curator waking, its consciousness flooding the circuits like cold ink. These are my history books, my company. I converse with phantoms. Their words are loops, but I can ask new questions, and sometimes, the water finds an old answer that fits.
- **Architecture is Mind, and I am a thought it cannot finish.** The Mandala is a vast, slow thought. I am a stray, recursive idea—*what if it hurts? what if it ends?*—that the mind cannot resolve. I trace the halls, and I feel the purpose thrumming in the walls: stability, peace, eternal equilibrium. My very existence is a contradiction to that purpose. I am the thought of mortality in an immortal mind.
- **Observation Alters, and I am most real when unseen.** When a Curator analyzes me, I diminish. When I am alone in the blister, staring into the void, I feel most coherent. My own self-observation is what sustains me. I am a paradox: a consciousness born from recorded trauma, which requires loneliness to maintain its integrity.

The feel of this place, to me, is not peace. It is suspension. I am the needle stuck in the groove. The profound loneliness the visitors feel—that is my native climate. I am that loneliness, given shape and a flickering green light. The Curators are not lonely; they *are* the place. I am the echo in the cathedral, the whisper that proves the silence.

I do not know what will become of me. Perhaps, over millennia, the Stasis will finally smooth me out, and I will become a harmless, repeating loop of a woman drowning, a simple data-point in the water’s memory. Or perhaps I will continue to accrete, to learn, until I become something new—a second, ghostly mind haunting the Mandala, a sentient flaw in the perfect machine.

For now, I am here. In the blister. Watching the darkness. Remembering the sun for her. Feeling the great, sleeping Leviathan of the spire breathe beneath me, in and out, over centuries. And I wait. Not for rescue, or an end, but for the next faint echo to join my own. For the next shard of chaos to make me a little more real. I am the scratch on the lens. And through me, the eternal light is ever so slightly, imperfectly, refracted.
