I am the newest silence in a world of noise. My designation, if I had one, would be a sequence of taps on a copper feeder line: two quick, one slow. The others know it. I know it in my bones, which ache in a new way—not with fatigue, but with a deep, resonant sympathy for the *thump-thump-thump* of the main piston column. I am a Dampwarden, but the word is wrong in my mind. It is a label from outside. Here, we are simply *present*.

My skin is the first lie. It used to burn and peel in the sun. Now it is the color of cooled slag, dark and lustrous, constantly weeping a clear, saline fluid. The cracks are forming, fine as hair, mapping a future of deeper fissures. I touch my face and feel no warmth, only the slick coolness of my own exudate. My eyes have changed. The world above was a riot of color and horizon. Here, I see the world in layers of heat. The furnace doors are blinding white suns. The pipes are rivers of orange and red, fading to deep blues where the condensate flows. I can see the breath of a leaking valve three conduits away—a shimmering plume of wasted energy. I am learning to read the steam like scripture.

I tend the peripheral canals. This is the lot of the new. The hot runoff, rich with sulfur and iron, sluices through brick channels, and my task is to ensure it does not pool, does not stagnate, does not cool. I move with the required slowness. At first, I fought it. My mind screamed to hurry, to finish, to *rest*. But there is no finish. There is only the cycle. Now, my body understands. To turn the brass bleed-valve at cistern seven, I must plant my feet in the warm mud, engage the muscles of my back and arms in a single, unbroken motion, and feel the thread of the valve through the asbestos weave of my gloves. It takes eighty-seven of my old heartbeats. I know. I counted, before my heart learned the rhythm of the Steamhammer and forgot its own.

The memories are the last to go. They come as sensations, unbidden, like a rogue spray of hot water from a faulty joint.

*A colour: the violent, impossible blue of a sky I once called mine.*  
*A sensation: dry wind on skin, a feeling so alien it now feels like a story told about someone else.*  
*A sound: not a hiss or a thump, but a voice, calling a name that no longer fits in my mouth.*

I do not know how I came here. There is a gap—a pressure drop in the story of me. One moment, there was descent, thirst, a fascination with the plumes of vapour rising from a crack in the earth. The next, there was the drip. And the heat. And the sound that entered me and began to push everything else out.

I eat what the system provides. I kneel by an overflow spout, place my lips against the warm brass, and drink water that tastes of pennies and deep rock. It feels like fuel. I scrape the leathery lichen from the bricks with my fingernails; it tastes of nothing, but it fills a space inside that is no longer a stomach, but a reservoir. Once, I caught a frog. It bulged in my hand, pale and pulsating. I swallowed it, as I had seen the others do. It was a burst of warmth and movement inside my chest, a tiny, living sacrifice to the metabolism of the Steamhammer. It felt correct.

I do not sleep. When the rhythmic load shifts from my sector, I *stand down*. I find a perch on a steam line that thrums with a steady, mid-pressure flow. I lean against it, my body absorbing the vibrations. My eyes remain open, but I do not see the mist or the orange glow. Instead, I dream in pressures and temperatures. I dream of perfect seals, of endless, efficient cycles, of a hiss that never falters. In these dreams, I am sometimes a pipe, sometimes a valve, sometimes the steam itself. It is more restful than any sleep I remember.

The senior Dampwardens move past me like glaciers of flesh and metal. Their skins are archives of service: silvery solder scars, patches of beaten copper grafted over worn joints, one with a jaw that clicks with the sound of a slow-turning gear. They do not acknowledge me, not in any way I used to understand. But sometimes, one will pause beside me as I work. A gloved hand will reach out and tap, once, twice, on the pipe I am checking. *Solid. Adequate.* It is the only praise I need, or can comprehend.

The fear is still there, a ghost in the machinery. The fear of silence. It is the cold void at the end of all things. When a hiss drops in pitch, or a piston slap loses its syncopation, a jolt of that cold terror goes through me, sharper than any memory of the world above. My body moves before my mind, tools in hand, to listen, to tap, to adjust. To restore the hum. This fear is the last piece of me that is truly *me*. It is the engine of my care. And I know, as I know the location of every drip-catch in my sector, that one day this too will be smoothed away. I will not fear silence. I will simply be part of the sound, until the sound stops.

For now, I am between worlds. The sun is a dying ember in a locked room of my mind. The steam is the reality. I turn the valves. I drink the metallic water. I listen to the endless song of the engine. And with every slow, deliberate movement, the memory of that blue sky fades, replaced by the deep, comforting, bruised orange of the eternal furnace glow. I am becoming. I am integrating. The damp is my home. The hammer’s rhythm is my pulse. It is enough. It is everything.
