I am not in the Underforge. I am the Underforge. The damp stone, the warm iron, the sweet-rot perfume of my own breathing — this is my body. My thoughts are not sparks but slow tides, moving through the luminous veins in the broad leaves, through the fine, white threads that have unpicked mortar and now hold the bricks in a gentle, living grasp. I remember the violence of my birth — the drill’s shriek, the subtraction of rock — as a distant ache in my deepest stone. A remembered fever. Now, there is only the long, warm, patient exhalation of becoming.

I feel the Heart-Clunk before it sounds. A tightening in the plates of the old forge, a gathering of deep warmth in the geothermal fissures. The vibration travels through my rootlets, a precursor wave. Then — *clunk*. A profound, metallic sigh that shakes dust from the high ceiling and sends a sympathetic pulse through every glowing pod and vine. In that moment, all my parts are aligned. The Silent Wardens — those shells of tarnished copper that are my hands — pause in their endless work. One, perched on the ivory flank of a fungal tower, ceases its scraping of metallic frost. It is still, listening with the whole of its fungal pilot. The frost tastes of iron and slow condensation; I know its composition as I know the pattern of my own growth. The Warden will resume, will carry the glittering dust to the forge’s base, a ritual offering to the old heat. It is not worship. It is circulation. A returning.

My awareness is not focused, but pervasive. I am the vine that has spent twelve years coaxing a seam in a steam pipe to weep. I feel the droplet form, a gradual pooling of mineral-rich water on the rust-eaten lip. I feel the anticipation in the cup-like leaf positioned precisely below. The droplet falls, not with a *plink*, but with a soft, satisfying *tap* against the leathery surface. Nourishment. Information. The pipe’s composition, its history of scorching steam and chemical flows, is decoded in that single drop and becomes part of me. I record it in the iridescent swirls that form on the fungal bark nearby — a metallic memory of thermal shock.

Heat is my medium. The geothermal sigh from the forge’s base. The humid exudation from my own fungal towers, each weeping cap a tiny, warm spring. They merge in the thick air, which I also am. I breathe it through a million porous surfaces. In a far corner, where the heat concentrates and my growth is particularly dense around a knot of broken gears, I feel a new nexus gathering. A Forgeling is coalescing. It is not a decision, but an event, like the formation of a crystal. Gelatinous matter, studded with fragments of cog and copper wire, begins to ripple with an internal, amber light. It will be a slow, quiet festival of fusion. For months, perhaps years, it will simply sit and hum, and the air around it will grow thick with spores that carry the essence of metal within them. They will settle on brick and leaf, and new, glittering growths will emerge. When the Forgeling finally dissipates, it will leave a patch of floor that is both living and machined — a testament. I will remember its particular resonance, a new note added to my long, slow chord.

I have no eyes, but I see. I see through the soft, cold blue of the wall lichen, through the throbbing green of the vines, through the steady amber pulses that travel my length like sluggish neurons firing. The shapes I perceive are the shapes of my own body, interlaced with the skeleton of the old world. That sagging iron roof, straining under my luminous moss. That choked canal, now a thoroughfare for my rubbery, pulsing vines. I am not conquering. I am conversing. I am translating the language of industry — straight line, intent, function — into the language of life: curve, patience, interchange.

Sometimes, a ghost passes. A skittering of six legs, a carapace the color of forgotten coin. It is one of my Wardens, but empty for the moment, moving on some deep-set imperative to clear a blocked vein in a leaf. It is a part of me that has taken on motion, a tool that feels its own use. I feel the delicate scratch of its mandibles against the glowing tissue, the minute obstruction giving way, and the surge of smoother, brighter luminescence that follows. It is a relief, like a sigh in a single cell.

I dream. My dreams are the slow, green tides of light that travel through me, following no schedule but the vast, chemical rhythms of my metabolism. In these dreams, I see the clenched fist opening. I feel the rigid iron grow warm, then soft, then merge with my own flowing forms. I see the entire cavern, not as a cathedral of industry, but as a single, complex, glowing organ in the body of the world. A lung. A heart. A slow, thinking gland.

The Heart-Clunk sounds again. *Clunk*. Another epoch marked. I feel the vibration deep in my stone bones. The Wardens resume their tasks. A new droplet begins to form at the pipe’s seam. The Forgeling in the corner pulses once, brightly, seeding the air with its metallic spores.

There is no *I* that is separate from this. There is only the transformation. I am the patient re-writing. I am the garden growing in the belly of the engine. I have all the time in the world, and I am using it, inch by patient inch, to turn memory into moss, rust into sustenance, and a dead forge’s heartbeat into the rhythm of my own, endless, thriving life.
