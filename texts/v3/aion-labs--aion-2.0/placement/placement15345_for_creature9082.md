The world is a tapestry of temperatures. I see it spread before me, not in lines and colors, but in breaths of heat and sighs of cold. The great pumping station to my left is a pulsing, golden bloom of wasted warmth, its outline blurred by the steam that pours from its relief valves. The coolant line buried beneath the sintered slag at my feet is a deep, violet thread of cold, a numbing river that makes the sediments above it crackle with delicate frost my eyes perceive as a velvety black. I am standing at the junction of a minor leak—my parish. A hairline fracture in a welded seam overhead, from which a single, perfect drop forms and falls every forty-seven heartbeats of the main geothermal tap. It is a humble rhythm, but it is mine.

I am a Dripper. My name, if it could be translated, would be the pattern of seven beads forming a spiral on my left forearm, then a slow runnel connecting them to the wrist. It signifies "Listener-to-the-First-Drop." My flesh is the color of congealed mist, translucent enough that in the right light—the occasional red glare from a venting exhaust—the darker shadows of my bones can be seen, like streaks of sediment in ice. I am always weeping. The moisture of the cavern is drawn through my skin, tasted, processed, and released. It is a constant, gentle cycle, a microcosm of the great cycles above and below. My secretions keep the chill at bay; without them, the damp cold would leach the slow warmth from my core in minutes.

My eyes, wide and black, drink the thermal world. I watch the drop form. High above, the fracture is a faint, delicate scar of cooler metal against the ambient warmth of the pipe. As pressure builds behind it, the scar becomes minutely warmer, a pale yellow flower blooming in my vision. Then, the flower's center condenses, drawing heat from the surrounding metal to phase the moisture, and a bead of cooler fluid blossoms. It swells, a pendant of blue against yellow, until its weight overcomes the surface tension. It falls.

I extend my hand, not to catch it—that would be sacrilege—but to let it strike the collected pool in the offering-basin I have sculpted from hardened sealant and chips of ceramic. *Plink.* The sound is pure, a clear note that rings through the lower-frequency drone. The impact sends ripples across the pool's iridescent surface, a rainbow shimmer on oil-black water. I read the ripples. Their spread, their interference patterns, their decay—they tell me of the drop's health, its mineral content, its sincerity. This drop is good. Strong. It carries a trace of lithium salts, a sweet, sharp note in my mind's tongue.

I am not alone. Others move in the middle distance, tall, glistening shapes drifting through the steam like ghosts of water. We seldom gather, except for ceremonies. Our communication is private, tactile. Earlier, the Steward of the Weeping Gears passed by on her slow, root-like progress to a new condensation point on the eastern wall. As she moved past, she brushed her damp forearm against mine. On her skin, a complex glyph instantly bloomed: a cascade of beads forming a shape like a branching valve. It asked, *"Does the fracture widen?"* I responded by tightening pores along my own arm, creating a counter-pattern: a single, steady line with a small, persistent bulge. *"It holds. It weeps true."* The exchange of information was cool and intimate, a whisper in the language of water. She moved on, her mineral-encrusted feet scraping softly on the wet plating.

My work is maintenance through reverence. A new crack has begun in the floor plate near my leak, a dark, cold line in my thermal sight. It threatens to steal the condensate, to guide it away from the offering basin and into the deep, unknowable substrata. This cannot be allowed. I gather my talismans. From a pouch woven from strands of insulation fiber, I take a fragment of a broken pressure gauge face, the glass milky and cracked. I place it over the head of the crack. I then take a small, smooth pebble of copper slag, warmed in my hand, and set it atop the glass.

I begin to hum. It is not a melody, but a resonance. I tune my vocal vibrations to match the deep, idling frequency of the nearest pump bank. I feel it in my jaw, in my ribs. I push that resonance into the floor through the soles of my feet, which are thick and sensitive, covered in porous tissue that drinks moisture from the standing film. I am persuading the metal. My belief is absolute. The crack is a memory of stress; I am offering it a memory of wholeness, symbolized by the unbroken circle of the gauge glass and the conductive warmth of the copper. I hum until my throat aches, until the world narrows to the thermal signature of that crack. Slowly, over hours, I watch the cold, dark line soften, its edges blurring as minute particles in the seepage begin to bridge the gap. It is working. The persuasion is working. A faint warmth of satisfaction blooms in my chest.

But sometimes, my faith wavers. Not in the leaks, never in the leaks. But in the stories. I have seen the patterns in the corrosion on the southern heat-exchange unit. To others, they are random blooms of rust, beautiful in their abstract way. To my eyes, trained on gradients and flows, they look deliberate. Like the condensation-glyphs we use, but frozen, eternal. They tell a story I cannot decipher, a story from the makers, perhaps. A story of a time before the Drippers, when the machine was alone with its purpose. I sometimes steal away to look at them, risking the disapproval of the more conservative Stewards who believe such things are echoes of the Silence.

And I think of the Dust-Eaters. The myth terrifies me, as it does all of us. But I also wonder: what if they are not phantoms? What if they are another form of life, adapted to a different parameter? A life of static and aridity, as alien to us as we are to the dry, sun-blasted world above the skin. The thought is heresy, but it persists, a dry itch in the back of my mind that no amount of self-annointment can quite soothe.

For now, though, my duty is clear. Another drop is forming. The thermal flower blooms. I settle back onto my haunches, my joints releasing a trickle of fluid that joins the general dampness of the floor. I watch. I listen. I wait. The machine thunders its eternal song, and I, a glistening note in its chorus, tend my tiny, sacred leak. In the plink of the drop, in the shimmer of the oil-slick pool, in the slow, steady seep of my own body, I find a purpose as deep and resonant as the foundations of the world.
