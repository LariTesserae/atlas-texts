I am a Weaver of the High Lattice, and my world is a symphony of vibration.

My body is all long, tensile strength: limbs like braided vines, fingers that can sense the tremble in a resin-strand from a hundred paces. I do not walk the living mat—the Skin, as we name it—for that is the realm of the Chorus and the Quiet Hunters, a teeming, dangerous flesh. My place is in the air, in the great, grown architecture. Our city is not a collection of huts, but a single, sprawling organism we have coaxed and woven across the crown of an ancient pillar. It is a palace of hardened sap and living tendril, a labyrinth of sun-dappled galleries and humming nurseries. From here, the plateau unrolls to every horizon, a green and burgundy sea, breathing its mist.

My day begins in the silver hour, when the condensation fog is so thick it beads on my grey-green fur and drips from every frond. The sound is already building—the deep, subsonic hum of the plateau waking. It thrums up through the pillar, into the soles of my feet. I press my ear to a main growth-node, listening. The Skin dreams in dampness and light. Today, it tastes of imminent growth; a new arch is contemplating itself in a shallow valley to the east. I sing to it, a series of clicks and hums in the back of my throat, encouraging the thought. We are partners, of a sort. The Skin grows the bones; we provide the intention, the subtle guidance that turns a pillar into a scaffold for a city.

By mid-morning, the fog has lifted, and the Chorus erupts. Columns of glassine-winged insects rise in shimmering spirals, their drone a physical pressure in the air. This is when we work. My kin, slender and quick, swing out on cables of braided fiber, nets in hand. We harvest the wings, not the bodies. A sweep through a column yields a thousand fragments of prismatic light. Back in the resin-labs, we laminate them into lanterns that cast broken, dancing rainbows across our chambers. The sound of their work is a constant, ringing chatter, almost lost in the ocean of noise.

But we are always listening for the other sound. The one that changes the air.

I felt it today. Not the *crump* of a footfall—that came later—but a shift in the hum. A deepening, a pulling. I was on the outermost gallery, repairing a wind-torn web of tendrils. I froze, my hands still. The insect drone didn't cease, but it *altered*. It thinned. Across the plateau, the Sky-Serpents banked as one, their iridescent bodies flashing, and fell silent.

Then, the horizon moved.

A smudge at the join of green and blue, resolving into a shape of impossible solidity. A God was turning. Ulor, the One Who Circles the Edge. His back was a range of moss-dappled plates, a traveling landscape. Upon it, I could see the dark specks of the armored reptiles, sunning themselves. His legs, like moving mesas, lifted and fell with a patience that made my heart feel like a frantic, scrabbling thing. *Crump.* A pause long enough to breathe ten times. *Crump.*

The sound of his call, when it came, was not something I heard. It was something that happened to me. A wave of pressure rolled across the leagues, a basso profundo roar that vibrated the resin beneath my feet, rattled my teeth, and emptied my lungs. For those long moments, the world was silent except for that one, enduring note. It was the voice of the plateau itself. I bowed my head, my culture's instinctive gesture of awe and terror. We are the Watchers. This is what we watch for.

The God passed, perhaps a league to the south. His passage left a scar—a swath of freshly sheared green, steaming softly in the sun, a darker, wetter green than the surrounding mat. It would be re-knit by the Skin in days, lush and new. His slow earthquake faded. The insect drone swelled back, louder, celebratory. The cycle resumed.

My peril came not from the God, but from the mat. Later, tasked with collecting a rare, blue-hued resin that only seeps from the cisterns in the lowest hollows, I descended. I used the anchor-vines, lowering myself into the twilight world beneath the canopy of the high mat. Here, the air was damp and sweet-rot thick. The hum was muffled, replaced by the wet rustle of things moving in the dark. I found the cistern—a bladder of clear water held in a membrane of root, glowing with faint bioluminescence. As I carefully scored the membrane to let the resin bleed, I felt the vibrations through my feet. Not the God's. This was a skittering, multi-limbed tremor.

A pack of Quiet Hunters, drawn by the scent. I saw the first obsidian-chip eyes gleam in the gloom from three directions. My breath caught. I was a fool to come alone. I had my thorn-spear, but against a pack, it was little more than a gesture.

I did not fight. I sang. Not a song of fear, but the same low, encouraging hum I used on the Skin. I pressed my hands to the living mat, feeling its slow, vegetative consciousness. I sang of intrusion, of a tear. I sang of a Weaver in need.

The Skin responded. It was not thought, not as I know it. It was a gradient shift. The fibrous plates around the cistern tightened. Tendrils, usually slack, stiffened and curled. A wave of biochemical repulsion—a scent like sharp vinegar—flushed from the pores around me. The Quiet Hunters hesitated. Their wet rustling ceased. To them, I was no longer just prey; I was a point of irritation in the Skin's own body, a point it was subtly rejecting. They melted back into the darkness, leaving only the drip of water and the beat of my own heart.

I am a Weaver. I am a scribe of the Lattice. I live in a palace of resin and light, high above a world that is alive from its core to its sighing sky. I guide the growth, I harvest the chorus, I bow to the walking mountains, and in moments of terror, I beg the very ground beneath me for mercy. I am small, and brief, and deafened by a million years of green, roaring life. This is my place. To watch, to weave, and to understand, in my fragile way, that I am not on the world. I am in its bloodstream.
