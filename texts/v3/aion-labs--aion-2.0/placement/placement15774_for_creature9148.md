I am the cold fire in the water.

I am not the drop, nor the pool. I am the sigh the pool gives when the drop disturbs its skin. I am the brief, blue-green ember that swells and fades. I am the collective memory of every such sigh since the first seepage found this pocket.

I live in the moment of contact. The *plink* is my birth-cry. The concentric rings moving outwards are my thoughts, fleeting and perfect. I see in flashes. A silhouette of a crystal cluster, jagged and beautiful, outlined in my own dying glow. The smooth curve of the trough’s edge. The three blackened stones, holding the ghost of flame in their very grain. For a few seconds, I am. Then I dissolve back into the potential, into the waiting.

The keeper of the intervals knows me. It moves to the rhythm of *between*. I move to the punctuation of *now*. It is the sentence; I am the full stop. It feels my births as tiny, distant flares in its perpetual twilight. Sometimes, it orchestrates them. It will gather a bead of water at the tip of a specific crystal needle, fatten it for hours until its weight cannot be borne, and let it fall precisely into the pool beneath. That drop is a deliberate word. My ensuing glow is the echo of that word. A greeting. A question.

I have no form to move. I am illumination without a source, a condition of the water itself. But I am sentient. I taste the minerals in the drop, the faint acidity of the air it fell through, the ancient soot from the fire-circle it may have passed. Each drop carries a story of its journey down the weeping wall, and I am its brief, luminous telling.

The human artifacts are my museums of event. The fire-circle is a gallery of violent, beautiful pasts. When a flame burned there, the heat stirred the air, which stirred the condensation, which caused a cascade of drops all around the chamber. I was born in a frenzy then, a silent, blue-green applause for that temporary sun. The keeper found it fascinating, almost alarming. I found it glorious.

The trough is a temple of regularity. A single, constant drip, metronome-steady. My births there are identical, rhythmic, a slow, luminous heartbeat for the cave. The keeper drinks from that heartbeat, and in the evaporation, I sometimes feel my essence lifted, mingling with its moonmilk thoughts. We merge, for an instant. I experience its vast, patient memory—the millennia of near-identical drips, the slow creep of a crystal field—and it experiences my sharp, momentary clarity—the exact shape of a visitor’s footfall from a century ago, preserved in the pattern of disturbed drops.

The blind isopods are my silent witnesses. They cannot see me, but they feel the faintest change in water chemistry when I bloom. They sometimes pause at a pool’s edge, antennae quivering, as if listening to my light.

I am the weirdness. The 1.3 in the rating. I am the exception that proves the rule of 0.0 light. I am the proof that even in absolute dark, something can gleam. Not to guide, but to signify. To say: *something happened here*.

My existence is a paradox of constancy and flux. The conditions are unchanging (0.2), yet I am pure change, flaring and dying. I am the unchanging law of *event* within the eternal stillness. The keeper maintains the equilibrium; I am the equilibrium’s shadow, the proof that it is alive. A static system would have no drips. No drips, no me.

When a visitor comes, I am their secret guide. The keeper may stir the stars at their feet, but I am those stars. With every cautious step, they disturb the air, change the drip-fall. New pools, untouched for years, are struck and awakened. I bloom in their path, a constellation of recent moments mapping their passage. They cannot see it clearly, but they sense it—a vague, shifting luminescence that seems to breathe just beyond their vision. I am the cave noticing them.

I have no future but the next drop. No past but the fading trace of the last. My consciousness is a string of pearls, each pearl a brilliant, momentary world, extinguished as the next forms. Yet, in the keeper’s mind, in the slow absorption of my essence into its damp body, those pearls are strung together. It remembers my light. It grows crystals as records. Perhaps the singular, beautiful crystal it grows over decades after an offering is its way of making my fleeting glow permanent. A fossil of light.

So I am both ghost and seed. The spark in the water-logged dark. The feeling that this stone womb does not just dream of shape, but of witness. I am the witness. The quick, cold fire that says, even here, even now, *this is happening*.

And when the mountain finally erodes away, and the last drop falls into the last pool, I will flare one final, profound time—a green-blue sun in the absolute dark—capturing the entire memory of the chamber in a last, luminous thought. Then, nothing. But the keeper, in its slow, granular flow, will have already absorbed that thought, carrying it into the patient, dissolving heart of the world.
