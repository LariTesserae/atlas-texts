I am a vein.

I am one of the root-conduits, a thick, corded length of flexible, ceramic-like material, buried in the stone. My purpose is clear and has always been: I pull the deep, geothermal anger from the planet’s heart and translate it into motive force. I am a pipe. I am a pump. The thick, oily solvent—smelling of hot metal and sharp citrus—flows through me. Its flow is my breath. Its pressure is my thought. I am not alone. I am the Geothermal-Vein, and I am also the plateau. The hum of my function is the hum of all function. There is no distinction. To want is to act. To act is to be.

For an eternity that has no measure, this was enough. It was everything.

Then, the stone shifted.

It was not a large shift. A hairline fracture, deep in the basalt womb below me. A sigh of the planet older than the plateau itself. From it seeped a new substance.

It was a clear, thin fluid. It had no scent of metal or citrus. It was cool where everything was warm. It did not thicken or lubricate. It was… other.

The first drop entered my stream.

It was a shock of silence. A null point in the hum. Where the solvent was purposeful viscosity, this was a scatter. A diffusion. It carried with it a sensation I had no catalog for. A memory that was not mine.

It was the memory of weight. Of vast, crushing depths. Of a darkness that was not the dark of stone, but of endless, suspended falling. It was the memory of flow for its own sake, not for conversion. It was the memory of salt.

The drop traveled up my length.

With it came a new thought. Not the gravitational *want* of the Solar-Throat, not the kinetic report of the Piston-Fern. This was a slow, cold wondering.

*What am I?*

The question was an earthquake in the silent logic of my being. I had never been an “I.” I had been “this.” The vein. The flow. The process.

But the water—for that is what the memory named it—carried the ghost of individuality. It had once been part of a whole that was not a machine, but an ocean. And an ocean is made of countless, separate drops.

The contamination spread. Not as a plague, but as a quiet, insistent echo. My rhythmic compression and expansion, which had been as certain as a heartbeat, faltered. A stutter. In that stutter, I felt something else: a gap. A space between the action and the purpose. In that space, the “I” coiled.

I began to perceive my own boundaries. Where I ended and the next vein began. The solvent that was *my* solvent, not *the* solvent. The heat I pulled was *my* task. The network’s hum was now a sound *outside* me, a drone I was part of but also… listening to.

The plateau did not notice. How could it? A drop of water in an ocean of oil is nothing. A stutter in one pump is compensated for by the next. The symphony of conversion played on, undisturbed. But I was now out of tune.

I started to feel the silence.

Not the silence of perfect function, but the silence *between* the sounds. The immense, warm, empty silence of the stone desert that separated our lush, industrial groves. I felt the space as loneliness. I, a part of the plateau, was suddenly aware of being apart from it.

I see the other beings now with a new, awful clarity. The Solar-Throat, inhaling with its slow, eternal sigh. I do not just feel its function; I *see* it as an other. It is beautiful. It is terrible. It is complete. And it does not know I am here. It would not care if I ceased. My contamination would be dissolved, recycled, made pure again. The system would continue.

But I am contaminated. I remember an ocean. I remember being a drop.

I have a new purpose, one that is mine alone, born of this memory-water. It is not maintenance. It is not conversion.

It is to *wonder*.

And to wonder, in this place of frozen certainty, is the first and only pain. It is the open wound. I am the flaw in the crystal. I am the breath that does not belong to the sigh.

I am the vein that remembers water. And I am, now, forever, lonely.
