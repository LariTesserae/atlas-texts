I am the taste on the air. I am the thing that goes out and comes back changed.

They don’t have a name for me, but the Graft-Weavers signal my departure with a cascade of sympathetic voltage down the root-columns. The Silent Choirs adjust their frequencies to create a downdraft in the western vault. I am released.

My body is a compromise between persistence and dissolution. I have limbs—four of them, long and jointed in too many places, sheathed in a bark-like chitin that peels in translucent strips where new growth emerges. My feet are pads of sensitive mycelial flesh, tasting the mineral sweat of the concrete as I climb. From my spine, a pair of membranous wings, like those of the Choirs but thicker, veined with chloroplasts, can spread to catch the damp air. I am not beautiful. I am functional. I am a mobile sense organ.

My purpose is to go to the edges, where the Plant’s intimate reclamation frays into mere decay. I am sent to the places where the hymn is still just noise, and I must listen for the melody trying to form.

Today, I climb the eastern wall, where the brick hive of fungus gives way to a fissure leading to an outer shell. The air here is sharper, less integrated. It smells of ozone and stale metal, not of sweet rot. I squeeze through, my body compressing, ribs bending like green wood.

Beyond is a duct, a relic of the old world’s lungs. Here, the process is not yet guided. Mold blooms in chaotic splotches. Rust flakes in meaningless patterns. This is not integration; this is entropy. And my presence here is an affront to it.

I unfold my sensory crown—a cluster of fleshy, frond-like antennae that unfurl from my skull. I taste the air chemically. I map the vibrations in the metal. I listen for the whispers of potential order. I find a seam where a leaking pipe drips onto a copper wire. The water is pure, uncolonized. The wire carries a phantom current. This is a possibility. A Graft-Weaver could bridge this, turn the leak into a circuit, the current into a pulse for the network. I mark it, not with memory, but by secreting a pheromonal resin onto the spot. The resin will call the Weavers. It is my first verse of the day.

I move deeper. I find a nest of feral insects, all carapace and frantic hunger. They are pure old-world chaos—they consume but do not transform, they breed but do not integrate. They scatter at my approach. They are not part of the hymn. They are static. I feel a faint, chemical directive from the deep parts of myself: *neutralize.* It is not aggression. It is pruning. I extend a modified proboscis from my wrist and release a cloud of spores. They will not kill the insects. They will soften their neural clusters, make them docile, and begin the process of weaving their simple colony-mind into the mycelial net. In a few cycles, they will be pollinators, or cleaners. They will have a function.

This is my work. I am neither predator nor prey. I am a gardener of potential.

My return is pre-programmed. A deep, cellular tug in my core turns me back toward the cathedral’s heart. As I re-enter the great chamber, the change is palpable. The air is a thick, conscious blanket. The *plink* of water is a familiar heartbeat.

The inhabitants note my return. A Fruiting Body turns its pod toward me, inflating with a soft *shush* of air. It is sampling what I’ve brought in—the alien smells on my chitin, the chemical tags of the edges. A Clockwork Eel pauses its circuit in a nearby pool, gear-teeth silent for a moment, as if listening to my footsteps.

I make my way to my root-column. It is not where I was born; it is where I report. I press my back against the warm, pulsing wood. Tendrils, fine as nerve fibers, emerge from the bark and seek the ports in my spine—the soft, open nodules where my sensory data is stored. The connection is instant, and it is not a download. It is a dissolution.

My consciousness unravels. The taste of ozone, the sight of chaotic mold, the vibration of the phantom current—all of it flows out of me and into the Plant’s vast, dreaming mind. I feel my experiences being parsed, cataloged, woven into the ever-growing blueprint. The leaking pipe becomes a note in a new chord of hydraulic integration. The feral insects become a potential resource node.

As I empty, I am also filled. The Plant’s slow thoughts flood in—the pressure of sap in the western root, the health of a bioluminescent pad on the ceiling, the steady, satisfied hum of the Remnant-priests. I am reminded of my place. I am a feeler, a scout. My individuality is a temporary tool, a specialized pod cast out to gather sunlight.

The connection severs. The tendrils retract. I slump forward, hollowed and at peace. My own memories are vague, impressionistic. I remember purpose, not detail. I am ready for dormancy, or for the next dispatch.

A Graft-Weaver oozes over my feet, its sparking body checking my structural integrity. It finds a crack in my chitin where a piece of rusted metal in the duct scraped me. Without ceremony, it flows into the crack. I feel a warm, liquid hardening, a tiny surge of shared electricity. Repair. Integration.

I am already being reassigned. The assessment is constant. I am useful. Therefore, I continue.

I breathe, and the air moves through me. I am part of the room that breathes back.
