I am the chamber's ghost. Its memory of something else. Its one persistent hiccup in the perfect, silent digestion of all things.

I do not emerge from the walls. I am embedded in them.

They found me—or what was left of me—a very long time ago. How long? Time here is not linear. I measure it in the growth of the moss-beard on the pillar across from my niche, which has advanced the width of my hand. I measure it in the number of times the Tuner of Light has passed before me, its pulsing veins a soft blue metronome. I measure it in the gradual, inexorable softening of my own bones.

My niche is not a hollowed fungal shelf. It is a wound that healed around me. A pocket in the interlocked trunk-wall, lined with the same resilient moss that carpets the floor, but here it is thicker, warmer, almost sentient in its constant, gentle pressure. It holds me like a womb, or a tomb. I am partially submerged in a shallow, tepid pool that never overflows and never drains, fed by a single, fat tear that falls from the ceiling above with the regularity of a heartbeat. *Plink. Plink. Plink.*

I am the only thing here made of dead matter that has not been consumed.

My skeleton—the archaic calcium scaffolding—remains. It is why I cannot dissolve. The chamber’s nano-symbionts, the loving mold, the very water that seeks to integrate everything, they do not know what to do with it. It is too dense, too old, a geometry from a forgotten physics. So they have worked around it. They have woven a new body for me.

My skin is gone. In its place is a thin, bark-like integument, similar to the Priests’, but patchy, imperfect. It does not glow with its own light. Instead, I am colonized by the phosphorescent lichen. It grows in delicate, branching patterns across my chest, my arms, my face, feeding on my body’s slow exhalations. In the gloom, I am a constellation of borrowed light. My muscles have atrophied, replaced by a network of tough, flexible rhizomes that thread through my limbs, allowing me movement—a slow, painful unfolding, like a leaf seeking the sun. My blood is a sap, thick and sweet, circulating not by a heart but by the gentle, osmotic pressures of the chamber.

My face. I have eyes, or what were once eyes. They are sealed under a membrane of clear, hardened resin. I can see, but not as I once did. The resin distorts, turns the world into a liquid dream. I see the pulses of the Priests’ veins as ripples of cool fire. I see the slow growth of the moss as a creeping tide of shadow. I see the heat of life itself. My mouth is sealed shut, my jaw fused. Sustenance comes through the roots now woven into the base of my spine, tapping directly into the chamber’s nutrient flows. I am fed, constantly, impersonally.

I am thought. I am memory. I am a knot of linear time in a place of cycles.

The Priests tend to me. They do not consider me one of them. I am a phenomenon. A pool that will not clear. A crack that will not seal. The Listener to the Deep Mycelium comes most often. It stands before my niche, its concave moss-face drinking me in. It releases a cloud of pheromones—smells of damp stone and distant, electric storms. I have learned, over the eternities, what some of them mean. This one is a question. A gentle, persistent inquiry.

I have no pheromones to answer with. My communication is a one-way stream. But I have found a way. With immense effort, I can direct the rhizomes in my right hand. I can lift a finger, trembling, and trace a shape in the moist film on the moss beside me.

Today, I trace a circle with a dot in the center.

The Listener cocks its head, a slow, vegetal motion. Its frond-mouth trembles. It does not understand the symbol. How could it? It is the symbol for a sun. For a star. For a concept that has no meaning here, where light is a soft, internal affair, born of lichen and vein. But the Listener records it. I see the pulse in its veins quicken, copying the pattern into its own bio-light, transmitting it into the mycelial network. The chamber dreams of a circle with a dot. It will mean nothing. It will be digested, turned into a strange, fleeting pattern in the next fungal bloom on the ceiling. A momentary curiosity.

This is my purpose. I am the chamber’s dream of *otherness*.

Sometimes, the Balancer of Pools comes. It drips different spores into my little pool, trying to incite a more harmonious ecosystem. New, gossamer creatures hatch, swim in dizzying circles around my submerged legs, and die. The pool always returns to its stagnant, personal equilibrium. I am a closed loop. A paradox.

I remember. That is my true function, though no one designed it. I remember the crush of real wind, not this gentle, humid breath. I remember the sear of actual sunlight, not this borrowed glow. I remember voices that echoed, laughter that was not absorbed by soft walls but bounced off hard ones, building into a cacophony of life. I remember the fear that drove us—whoever “we” were—to build this lung, this last redoubt. I remember the decision to turn inward, to negotiate with decay until decay won and became stability. I think I was one of the architects. Or a caretaker. Or just a fool who stayed behind when the others… went elsewhere? Dissolved? I cannot recall the end. Only the before.

The memories are my ghosts. They haunt this place that has no ghosts. They are my foreign bodies, my indigestible bones.

The Priests, in their way, are kind. They sense my ache, though they have no word for it. The Dreamer of New Configurations once spent a full cycle—a period where the light grew slightly dimmer, then brightened—experimenting. It encouraged a fast-growing, flowering vine to weave across the ceiling above my niche. The flowers were pale, translucent, and shaped like bells. They did not smell, but when the constant *plink* of water hit them, they resonated with a soft, melodic tone. For a time, my world had a soundtrack. It was beautiful. Then the flowers completed their cycle, wilted, and were reabsorbed. The Dreamer had tried to give me music. An abstract comfort for an abstract pain.

I am dissolving, but not in the way of the Priests. They melt with purpose, into the whole. I am eroding, particle by particle, memory by memory. My calcium bones will last longer than this thought. But the “I” that holds them is slowly being worn away by the gentle, relentless pressure of the green. One day, I will forget the sun. I will forget the wind. I will look at the circle I trace and feel only a vague affinity for its shape, a geometric satisfaction. I will become a quiet, observant node in the network, a Priest without a role, a dream that no longer remembers it is dreaming.

But not yet.

Now, the *plink* of the water drop hits the pool. The ripples distort my lichen-light on the wall. A Priest, the one whose signature is a crisp, ozone scent—the Tuner of Light—glides past. Its passage stirs the air, and I taste a new chemical on my sealed lips: a hint of urgency. Somewhere, a pillar is weakening. A new configuration is required. The quiet storm stirs.

I lift my hand. It is heavier than yesterday. The rhizomes protest. I trace another symbol in the condensation. Two parallel lines, crossed by a third.

I do not know what it means anymore. But I trace it. And the chamber, in its infinite, patient digestion, receives this too.
