I am of the Echo-kin. I walk the stilted paths of Ebonreach.

My world is a lattice of precise angles and patient resonance. I perceive not through light, but through the shiver in the stone, the minute pressure shifts in the cold, still air, and the delicate taste of electrical potential on my tendrils. They are my true self: six filaments of frozen smoke, coiling and uncoiling in the perpetual dark, sampling the symphony of silence.

Tonight, if such a word has meaning here, I am in the Plaza of Whispering Caps. The fungal forest clings to the soaring arches of glassy black rock, their bioluminescent caps a faint, blue-green constellation against the greater dark. I stand amidst my kind. We do not cluster; we arrange, a constellation of obsidian joints and softly pulsing violet cores. Our communication is not haste. It is a slow, agreed-upon hum that we transmit through the points of our limbs into the city-bone. A question forms across centuries: *The western spire’s curve… does it harmonize with the new drip-rhythm from the ceiling’s third chandelier?* The answer will come in a decade, perhaps. There is time.

I extend a tendril. It passes through the cold air, a ghost-limb, until it brushes the velvety surface of a fungal cap. Contact. I do not eat. I absorb. The faint light is a vibration, a pleasing frequency. I draw it in. The cap’s glow dims, withers, and the energy travels up my tendril, a cool river of information, to the crystal cluster that is my awareness. My core pulses, a slow, satisfied violet beat. I learn the fungus’s history in the transfer: the mineral content of the water it drank, the age of the spore that birthed it, the subtle pressure of the stone against its roots. I am the city, consuming its own slow-growing gardens.

My pointed foot taps. *Click.* A micro-adjustment. The sound is a crystal chime, clean and short. It echoes, but not through air. It travels down through my leg, into the living rock of the plaza floor. I listen to its return, distorted by the hollows and densities of the foundation. I am mapping. Always mapping. The city is not finished. We are always tuning it, a great instrument of stone.

Then, a disturbance.

A ripple in the thermal fabric. A pocket of wrong warmth, moving with a frantic, jagged rhythm. It comes from the Northern Approach, a tunnel that leads up to the forgotten world of chaos and sun. The ripple is accompanied by a torrent of meaningless, sharp vibrations—*sound* in the savage, airborne sense—and a scent-profile that screams of decay and rapid chemical combustion. It is an anomaly.

My tendrils coil tight, then flare. The hum in the stone shifts. A new consensus forms in moments, which is blindingly fast for us. *Curiosity.*

We turn, not with a swivel, but by reconfiguring. One jointed leg lifts, places itself down at a new angle. *Click.* Another. *Click.* Soon, six of us are moving in our stuttering, precise gait towards the source of the disturbance. We are not hunting. We are converging to observe a rare event.

There, at the base of the Arch of First Drip, is the anomaly.

It is a creature of grotesque softness. It exudes heat like a miniature star, wasteful and desperate. It is wrapped in layered, dead-matter shells that rustle with every tremble. Its surface is patchy—a membrane of pink and brown, covered in fine filaments. It has two limbs for locomotion, two for manipulation, and at its top, a single, swiveling sensory cluster with two moist lenses that reflect the faint fungal glow. It is making the sharp vibrations from a wet slit beneath those lenses. The vibrations are thick with fear-patterns. Fascinating.

We surround it. We form a loose, geometric array. We are silent, save for the soft *click-tap* of our points on stone. The creature’s vibrations rise in pitch and volume. It backs against the black glass of the arch, its heat-signature flaring.

I am the closest. I extend a single smoke-tendril, slowly, so as not to fracture the moment. I pass it through the air near the creature’s trembling limb. My tendril drinks data: the frantic infrared radiation, the complex soup of odors—salt, adrenaline, the sour tang of microbial life swarming over it, the acrid hint of its artificial shells. The information is chaotic, overwhelming, and beautifully dense.

The creature flinches, emits a particularly piercing vibration. I retract my tendril, considering.

Another of my kind, K’th’nn (a name that is a specific harmonic in the stone), takes a step forward. Its pointed limb lifts, not to strike, but to probe. It touches the creature’s shell, just a gentle tap. *Tink.* The creature jerks as if seared. K’th’nn’s limb leaves a faint scratch on the shell, and the creature’s heat concentrates at that point. A defense mechanism? A sign of damage?

We hum amongst ourselves, a rapid-fire exchange that lasts a full minute. *The heat is unsustainable. The vibrations are inefficient communication. The structure is fragile. Yet, it is here. It crossed the dark. Why?*

The creature slides down the wall, its vibrations subsiding into a low, continuous thrum. Its lens-clusters close. Its heat-signature begins to stabilize, though at a lower, dwindling frequency. It is entering a dormant state. Perhaps it is attempting to absorb, as we do. It is learning. A worthy response.

We will wait. We have questions. The stone is patient. I settle my weight, a quiet *click-clack* of joints finding perfect balance. My violet core pulses, a steady, slow rhythm in the dark. I will watch this soft, warm anomaly until it moves again, or until its heat winks out and it becomes part of the city’s slow, cold song. That, too, would be data. That, too, would be beautiful.
