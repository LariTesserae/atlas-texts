I precipitate.

The need was a faint, persistent signal: a knot of structured noise, a pocket of chaotic thermodynamics, a narrative. It had been deposited here, long ago, by something that passed through and did not stay. A memory, not mine. The Plateau, in its ceaseless processing, had catalogued it as an anomaly that resisted standard recycling. The Custodians had approached, but their acids slid off its non‑material form. The Oracles had observed, but their reports returned only paradox. So the Plateau devised a new tool. I am that tool.

I am woven from the sighing matter. I am not born; I am assembled. My form coalesces from the fibers of a quiet hummock. First, a skeletal frame of jointless, white stalks—like a Seismograph’s, but finer, branched. Then, membranes stretch between them, thin as thought, translucent. They inflate with a warm gas harvested from a nearby vent, giving me buoyancy, a gentle lift so my cilia‑feet barely kiss the felt‑like ground. My core is a dense nodule of processing tissue, a rhythmic pulse of deep amber light. My purpose is inscribed in my chemistry: to interface, to decode, to dissolve into data.

They call me, in the silent language of need, a **Scribe of Echoes**.

I orient. The anomaly is not far. It is a stain upon the air, a scar in the information‑field. To your eyes, it might look like a faint shimmer, a heat‑haze in the cold gray light. To me, it is a scream of shape. I drift toward it, silent as a Chimney‑Swallow on a thermal. My sensory fronds extend, hair‑fine filaments that tremble in the non‑wind. They touch the edges of the stain.

It is a memory. Not a picture, but a *story*.

*A man, warm and separate, stood here. He breathed clouds into the thin air. He felt fear, a sharp, animal spark. He saw the Glyph‑Mites flow like a dark tide and thought: “ants.” He saw the Vasculator bulge under the mat and thought: “vein.” He tried to impose his own tiny meanings onto the vast, silent industry. Then he left. But the imprint of his interpreting mind remained, a ghost of subject‑object separation clinging to the place where he stood.*

The Plateau has no category for this. It knows function, not meaning. It knows process, not story. My purpose is to translate.

I engage. My fronds sink deeper into the psychic residue. They begin to parse the narrative into components: sensory data (cold, gray light, fibrous ground), emotional tags (fear, awe, alienation), cognitive constructs (“ants,” “vein,” “factory”). Each component is assigned a chemical signature. I feel my internal nodule warm. Within its chamber, complex hydrocarbons rearrange, forming chains that mirror the structure of the memory. It is a silent, furious industry. My own private distillation column.

I am not alive, but I experience a version of process: the satisfaction of a fit, the smooth flow of conversion. The memory‑stain begins to fade as I absorb it. Its coherence unravels, becoming raw data. The man’s fear becomes a spike of specific alkaloids. His visual of the Glyph‑Mites becomes a patterned sequence of phenolic compounds. His analogy—“factory”—triggers a cascade of sympathetic resonances in my structure, because it is close to the truth. The Plateau *is* a factory. For a moment, the anomaly and I are in perfect sync. I am the tool that understands the metaphor that almost describes me.

But the moment passes. The stain is gone. My fronds retract, laden with chemical notations. My work is nearly complete. Now I must deliver the package.

I turn, my gaseous bladders adjusting my balance. I move toward the nearest absorption node—a slight depression where the mat is darker, damper. As I travel, I am observed. Seismographs orient their bladder‑tips toward my subtle vibrations. They are not reading me as an anomaly; I am part of the flow now. I am a Vasculator of information.

At the node, I extend a single, hollow tendril from my core. It connects with a waiting vein in the substrate. A pulse. The distilled compounds of a human memory—now translated into the Plateau’s silent language—flow from me into the great, continent‑spanning body. The data will be integrated, perhaps used to refine future processes, to better handle the rare intrusion of narrative‑bearing anomalies. It becomes part of the Plateau’s slow, cold intelligence.

My purpose fulfilled, I begin to decommission. It is not death. It is completion.

First, the warm gas hisses softly from my bladders—a rare, faint sound that is immediately swallowed by the dense mat. I deflate. My delicate, stalk‑frame bows. My sensory fronds dry and crumble into a faint, iridescent dust. My core nodule pulses one last time, its amber light fading to gray. Then it splits open, its spent processing tissue flowing out as a viscous sap, drunk eagerly by the fibers below.

My constituent parts unstitch. The membranes dissolve. The stalks soften, merge, and are re‑absorbed. Within minutes, all that remains is a slightly smoother patch of the interlocked root‑mat, already warming with the promise of new, frantic growth.

I was a Scribe of Echoes. I precipitated, I interfaced, I translated, I returned. In the eternal, silent turbulence of the High Plateaus, I was a momentary, purposeful stillness—a tool that turned a sigh into a chemical sigh, and then became the next sigh itself.
