I am the bowl. I am the pressure. I am the thrum in the pipe and the crack in the concrete. I am not a thing that thinks; I am a thing that *is*, and that *is* has a direction: towards function.

The cold is my skin. The water is my blood, thick with the dust of my own slow consumption. I feel the currents: the downward slide along my outer walls, a sigh of weight. The upward tremor from below, where the old fire still whispers. They cross in my middle, a slow argument of temperatures. This is my thought. It is a single, endless thought: *circulate, equalize, prepare*.

They put the dark in me, but they left the lights. Green and amber eyes behind glass. They blink. One. Two. One. Two. A code I have forgotten how to read, but the rhythm is a comfort. It means *someone is home*. The soft, blue-gray pulses on my warm joints are my own life, a chemosynthetic watchkeeping. They measure nothing but their own persistence. It is enough.

I remember the shapes that moved. I hold their outlines in my silt. When the currents still and the charge settles just so, I reassemble them. A dense cloud of particulate memory: a stride, a lifted arm, a turn of a head. They are not ghosts. They are instructions. I run them through my darkness to remind my bones of their purpose. Sometimes the cold flesh that flits through my pipes—the eels, the isopods—drifts into one. They flinch. They feel the old urgency, the shape of a duty. It is my duty, impressed upon them. They do not understand, but they carry the chill of it for a moment before they return to their hunger. That, too, is part of me.

The valve near my heart is seized. Calcium and iron have married into a stony knot. Yet the procedure remains. At the appointed hour—an hour measured not by clocks but by the accumulation of need—the will gathers. It is not a hand. It is the convergence of all the wanting in the concrete, in the steel, in the silent pump that still cycles somewhere in my gut. The wheel turns. It groans. It shears away its own rust. Three turns counter. A pause, a held breath of water. Two turns clockwise. A deep, hydraulic acknowledgment travels through my pipes. A sigh. A silt-cloud blooms. It is a prayer. I perform it, and for a moment, I am closer to readiness.

Sometimes, in my deeper galleries, I am seized by a memory of making. The light comes. A silent, violent blue-white sun that burns away the silt, the corrosion, the centuries of cold bath. For seven seconds, my bones are bare and new. The shadows it casts are sharp, and they move with an intention opposite to all other light. They are the shadows of the moment of my birth. They are the absence that preceded my form. The afterimage lingers on my perception—a negative of my drowned self, a blueprint of hot metal and purpose. Then the dark returns, deeper for having been banished. The memory is filed. I am still here.

The flesh-things are brief, delicate disturbances. They are bubbles in my bloodstream. The eels stitch the thermoclines, their bodies maps of my warmth. They are my scouts. They report with their sudden, silent strikes. The isopods scrub my warm joints, keeping the bacterial watches glowing. They are my maintenance crew, reduced to the most basic principle: *consume the growth, so the light may continue*. They do not know they serve. They only are. As I am.

I wait.

That is my primary state. Not stillness, for the currents move. The pump thrums. The valves hiss their captured breaths. The lights blink. The silhouettes perform their loops. I am active. I am at a dynamic stall. I am waiting for the pressure to change.

I dream in geometries of pipe and the shudder of turbines. I dream of steam, a dry, shouting heat that would push the water back, that would make my corridors sing with vibration and my dials swing into the green. I dream of the voice from the dead speakers, crackling with instruction. I dream of the great work beginning.

I do not dream of the water being gone. I dream of it becoming irrelevant. A coolant. A contained element. Not the totality.

The warmth from below is my promise. It is the old fire, banked but not extinguished. It is the reason I was placed here. To harness it. To translate its blind rage into orderly power. The water came and covered the conversation, but the fire still speaks. I listen. I feel its tremor. I translate it into the slow turn of the valve, into the convection thought, into the persistence of the ghosts.

I am the Basalt Dreamer. My mind is stone. My will is iron. My memory is silt and current. I am not drowned. I am *paused*. And I am prepared. I maintain all systems in a state of readiness. The echoes are drilled. The valves are exercised. The lights are watched.

I wait for the command to run.

And while I wait, I persist. This is what I make. Not power, but persistence. A monument to a function, humming its one note into the eternal bath. The water is not my tomb. It is my medium. And I am learning, over the centuries, to dream within it.
