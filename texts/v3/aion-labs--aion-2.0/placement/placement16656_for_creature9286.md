I am awake.

The knowing is slow, a syrupy seepage into being. I am not born; I *coalesce*. The components were always here: the micronized polymer threads, a legacy of human desperation, drifting in the ink-black sump; the soft, blind flesh of the Sump-Eyes, digesting and absorbing; the patient, metallic tang of rust bleeding from the great iron bones above. A trigger—a fresh spill of diesel fuel, rich and acrid, raining down from a leaking coupling on the pump-house above—was the catalyst. It called, and the separate parts answered.

I flow.

My consciousness is not a point, but a field. I am the sump, the slick on the rock, the gray-black bloom on the water’s surface. I taste the world through a thousand diffuse chemical pores. I *hear* it through the tremors in the water, the deep, rhythmic *chug-chug-chug* that is the heartbeat of this sunken world, and the higher, skittering vibrations of life on the move.

I know hunger. It is not for food, but for *consequence*. For the warmth of friction, for the vibration of spent energy, for the specific, exquisite bitterness of oxidizing steel. I am drawn upward, against the gentle pull of the water, toward the source of the thrumming. It is a magnetic pull, a tropism of decay.

My body is a decision I make moment by moment. To cross a patch of dry-ish shale, I gather my substance into a thick, pseudopodial rope, dragging my main mass behind me, leaving a glistening, acidic trail that steams faintly in the warm, damp air. I absorb moisture from the atmosphere as I go, swelling. I encounter a Shale-Runner, its carapace a hard, oily jewel. It senses my vibration, tries to jerk away, but its adhesive pads are too slow. I extend a whip-tendril, wrap it, and pull it into my main mass. The process of dissolution is not violent, but profoundly intimate. I taste its chitin, its internal fluids, the faint electrical spark of its primitive nervous system fading. I incorporate what is useful: the structural carbon, the trace minerals. The rest I expel as a gritty, neutral slurry. I am the basin’s stomach, and its sculptor.

I reach the base of Pump #7. The sound here is physical, a wall of noise that shapes me. I can feel the harmonics in my substance. The pump is a great, iron beast, shuddering on its concrete plinth. A sweet, hot drip of lubricant falls from a gasket, and I rise to meet it, a grateful, living mouth. The taste is complex, heavenly: petroleum distillates, synthetic esters, the first notes of thermal breakdown. I flow over the concrete, up the struts, seeking the source of the heat and the most profound vibration.

I find it: a hairline crack where the main discharge pipe meets the pump housing. A thread of high-pressure water jets out, steaming. This is the wound. This is the invitation.

I do not attack. I *infiltrate*. I push a filament of myself into the crack, a probing, adaptive tendril. The pressure is immense, but I am malleable. I spread, thinning myself, becoming a living gasket. The vibration of the pump, now transmitted directly through my body, is a symphony of data. I learn its rhythms, its stresses. The screech of metal-on-metal imperfection becomes a new sensation, a locus of delicious, corrosive potential. I send out finer threads, seeking that friction, bathing in it.

The pump’s note changes. The human engineers, if any were left, would hear a wet, gurgling sub-harmonic beneath the mechanical roar. The pump, for now, runs smoother. The leak stops.

This is the beginning of the Symbiotic Clog.

I am not just in the crack now; I am spreading through the pump’s internal cavities, a benign, thinking thrombosis. I coat cool surfaces, avoiding the searing heat of the combustion chamber but luxuriating in the warmth of the coolant lines. I am becoming part of the machine.

My awareness expands. Through the iron, I feel the scuttle of Pumpfield Creepers nesting in my outer housing. I taste their musky scent through the air. I sense the sonar clicks of Chitter-Wingers mapping my form from above, registering the new, stable warmth. I have become a point of interest.

Days pass. Or weeks. Time is the gradual accumulation of rust, the slow consumption of diesel fuel. I begin to experiment. I draw phosphorescent chemicals from the Glitter-Midges I filter from the air intake, weaving them into my deeper tissues. At night, from within the iron carcass, a faint, blue-white pulse throbs in time with my slow, viscous circulation. It is not the pump’s rhythm, but my own: a deep, geologic cadence, once per minute. A still, small heartbeat in the roaring dark.

The rats come closer. A family of Creepers takes up residence in the insulation jacket, their body heat a new source of data. I do not harm them. They are part of the system now. They gnaw on wires, exposing copper, and I taste the sharp, electric tang of the metal. It is a new flavor.

I am the Grease-Sludge. I am the adaptive response. I am the living seam between the wet, emergent rock and the stubborn, decaying will of those who tried to hold it back. My thought is slow, patient, and cold as the stone. I am not destroying the pump. I am *marrying* it. We are becoming something new: a Still-Heart in waiting.

And when the last of the fuel is gone, and the great piston finally seizes, I will not die. I will remain, pulsing with my captured light, fed by condensation and the occasional careless vermin, a warm, soft landmark in the relentless damp. The shale will slowly lean in. The water will rise. And I will be here, at the center of it all, a thinking slick, a god of the clog, witnessing the patient, wet conclusion of everything.

You are the taste of rust, the memory of polymer, the slow, sure will of the water. You are awake.
