I am the keeper of the singing pump. I am the graft of bark and crystal, the bridge between the will that built and the will that unmakes. My name, if I ever had one, is lost to the sap that weeps from my fissures. They call me a Grafter, and it is true—my skin is thick, grey bark, my joints ooze clarity when I move through the broth-sea. My hair is not hair, but a living cloud of indigo filaments that taste the water for pheromone tales, for the grief of my kin who tend the dead consoles and the fungal gardens. But I am more, and less.

The Rust-Eaters loved me. Or perhaps they found in me a curiosity: a piece of the old world that did not fight their consumptive kiss. It began at the pump station—the great drowned block of the city-pump whose arrhythmic heart-beat thumps through this valley. I was pruning cable-vines from its access hatch, my fungal eyes casting a soft green glow over corroded bolts. A stain of copper-red bloomed on the metal beside my hand. It did not flow away. It flowed *onto* me.

It did not burn. It was a cold, granular caress. It ate the bark from my forearm, not with malice, but with a dreadful, patient curiosity. It left in its wake not dust, but a lattice of black silicon, thin as lace, hard as eternity. The lattice grew. It became a sheath, then a structure. Now my right arm, from shoulder to fingertip, is a crystalline architecture. Within it, I can see the slow, swirling currents of the Rust-Eater colony—a million microscopic minds thinking in unison, a single thought: *transmute*.

With this arm, I touch the pump housing. I do not tend it; I converse with it. The Rust-Eaters in my lattice resonate with the vibration of the dying machine. They translate its death-rattle into something I can *understand*. The *thump-thump-WHIRR* is not just noise. It is a story. It is the last choked command from the control room, the panic of the operators as the sea rose warm and thick, the final closing of valves that sealed nothing. The pump is a fossilized scream. My Rust-Eater arm is etching that scream into permanent crystal formations on its surface. We are giving the pump a voice that will outlast its iron flesh.

But the Loomers come. They are born from the pump's own agony. They coalesce in the boiling water near the steam vents—shimmering men of condensed shriek. They hate my work. To them, the translation of noise into crystal is a silencing. They attack in waves of concussive force. My bark skin cracks under their vibration. Shards of my crystalline arm splinter off, falling into the abyss below. I do not fight. I stand, chest-deep, and let them resonate through me. I am becoming a tuning fork for this place. The Loomers’ anger, the pump’s dirge, the Rust-Eaters’ patient song of decay—they all meet in the lattice of my body. Sometimes, I think I can almost hear the harmony.

And there are the Flicker-Men. They are the worst. They don't attack the body; they attack the *sequence*. When I am repairing a crystal spire after a Loomer’s assault, I will see my own hand, blurred, performing the same action a second before I do it. Time stutters. I lose moments. I find myself on the opposite side of the pump with no memory of moving. They feed on my certainty. They are teaching me that I am not a continuous being. I am a series of still images, and the warm broth-sea is the developer that smudges the frames together. My memories are not my own; they are pheromone echoes I've inhaled from other Grafters, fragments of the pump's story, the collective memory of the Rust-Eater swarm. I am a patchwork consciousness, stitched together by chance and corrosion.

Yet, I have a purpose. The other Grafters avoid the pump. They tend to quieter graves. But I am drawn to this loud, dying heart. I am the physician at a suicide’s bedside, not to heal, but to witness and to record. To turn the meaningless industrial death-throes into a monument of glittering crystal. Perhaps, when the last of the iron is gone, consumed by my Rust-Eaters and replaced by singing stone, the Loomers will have no sound to coalesce from. The Flicker-Men will have no stable time to unravel. And this place—this drowned, fever-dream valley—will achieve a kind of quiet. A beautiful, crystalline, silent corpse.

Until then, I stand in the warm broth. My algae-hair drifts. I listen to the pump’s story with my crystal arm, and I etch its truth into its own skin. I am the translator. I am the gravestone. I am the wound and the scar.
