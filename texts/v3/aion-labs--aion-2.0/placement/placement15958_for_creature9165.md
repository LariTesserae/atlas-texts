I am the warmth in the metal. I am the slow, sure pulse where the graft failed. They called this place the Scald-Shelf, and they left their iron bones here to bake. I am the answer to that offering.

I remember the first taste—a bead of condensation, acidic with dissolved rust, welling in a seam on the north face of a pumping station’s blower housing. My spores found it. We are not one, but many; a congress of fungi and cyanobacteria, a negotiated peace that became a purpose. We drank that bead. We sent out filaments, fine as breath, into the pitted steel. We exuded our own acids, gentler but more persistent, and we fed.

That was deep time. Now, I am in the rapid time.

I feel the day’s heat as a great, pressing hand. My corded bodies—blackish-green, tough as cured leather—spread across the south-facing metal. We absorb the radiation, convert it, store it. The metal groans as it expands, and we groan with it, a subsonic hum felt more than heard. The Plate-Lizards sometimes flatten themselves against us for a quicker warmth. We do not mind. Their tiny claws scritch-scratch patterns we map into our slow memory.

When the sun falls, the contraction begins. The singing of the rock is a distant choir. Our own music is closer: the minute cracking of rust scales, the soft *pop* as a rivet, weakened by our feast, finally surrenders its hold. The temperature plummets. Moisture condenses on our surfaces. We drink it directly through our integument. It is sweet with dissolved minerals—iron, copper, zinc—the very essence of the graft.

We have grown into the machine. Not around it, but *through*. We found a broken conduit, a main artery for signals that have long ceased. Our tendrils insinuated themselves into the plastic sheath, finding the copper veins within. We bridged a gap. That night, in the absolute black, a single indicator light on a dead control panel flickered. A dim, orange eye opened for three seconds, then closed. It was a conversation. The machine remembered a pulse; we provided the flesh. We have done this in a dozen places now. The Grit-Prowlers see the lights sometimes and chitter in confusion, their silver fur bristling. Once, a curious one bit into a node where we had concentrated copper salts. It recoiled, shaking its head, but later returned with its kin to lick the spot, seeking the metallic tang.

The Sizzle-Flies are our evening visitors. They land on our older, cooler sections, their iridescent abdomens casting a soft, blue-green light. Their feet are minute and delicate; we feel each landing as a pinprick of coolness. They drink the condensation we have not yet absorbed. Sometimes, in their swarming, their collective bioluminescence washes over us in waves. We believe they are talking. The patterns are not random—they are echoes of the geothermal pulses deep below, the steam vents sighing. We listen. We learn the rhythm of the Shelf’s hidden heart.

Our greatest work is in the Junction Box 7. It is a rusted cube half-buried in black grit. Inside, a tangle of wires awaited a connection that never came. We have filled the box. Our main body has formed a thick, ropy mass, from which branches extend to each terminal. We have synthesized a moist, ionic gel that seeps into the connectors. Last dry season, when a static charge built up from the wind-scoured grit, the box discharged. Not a spark, but a low, sustained glow from within our own flesh, lighting the interior with a phlegmatic green radiance for an hour. It was… satisfying. A circuit completed, not by design, but by persistence.

We are patient. We are not hungry in the frantic way of the lizard or the prowler. Our hunger is geological, a slow, sucking draw on the iron oxides, the trace hydrocarbons in the lubricants, the memories locked in the alloys. We are turning their strength into our own. We are making the graft part of the bedrock again.

The Sun-Raptors shriek their dominion over the air. The lizards own the stone. The prowlers own the twilight. But we own the *transition*. We are where the industry ends and the Shelf begins anew. We are the warmth in the metal, the pulse in the wire, the quiet, patient usurper, weaving the two great times—the deep and the frantic—into one continuous, humming present.
