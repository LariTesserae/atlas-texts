I am the silence within the roar.

The great chugging breath of the cleft is a vibration through the peat, a thrum in the warm rock beneath my pads. I do not hear it. My world is a tapestry of wet shapes and shimmering motion. The two large eyes drink the gloom: the emerald velvet of liverworts, the skeletal black stems of the berry thickets, the foxfire glow of the shelf-fungi like distant, cold constellations. The four smaller eyes, arranged on my temples, paint the air itself. They see the dance of energy: the swirling vortices where steam plumes cut the air, the delicate shudder of a fern frond three leaps away as a beetle lands, the concentric rings of disturbance in a still pool from a rising gas bubble. It is a composite picture, depth rendered in layers of movement and heat.

My pelt drinks the air. Each filament, tipped with its microscopic bulb, pulls moisture from the perpetual mist. I am never thirsty. The condensation beads and is absorbed, a constant, cool ingestion. My colour is the colour of wet basalt and shadow, shot through with faint rainbows where the light, such as it is, catches the oily sheen. I am camouflage made flesh.

I flow. My shoulders unlock, my spine becomes a river. I slide beneath a fallen log furred with moss, my belly fur brushing the sodden peat. The springy ground accepts my weight without sound. I am navigating the islands of life in the cathedral emptiness. Past a gently boiling pool, its surface stippled with escaping breath. Past a clot of the fat, pebbled ones, basking on a flat stone, their throats pulsing like slow hearts. They are not for now. I am patrolling. The sharp walls of my domain are there, at the edge of my large-eye sight: young basalt, sharp-edged, weeping constant tears. I know every seep, every overhang, every cavity where the rock’s inner warmth bleeds through. This is all mine.

The hunt is a meditation.

I choose a spot where the steam from a minor fumarole curls around a tumble of rocks. Upwind, though the scent of sulfur and decay is merely background data. I settle. My body melts into the contours of the stone. I become a shadow within the shadow, my pelt drinking the ambient moisture, blurring my edges. I am patient. The earth’s engine chugs on.

One of the pebbled ones lumbers onto a warm shelf of sinter, just a tongue’s length away. It settles, its dull consciousness focused on the heat soaking into its skin.

Beneath my jaw, the gland tingles. I extend the thread. It is viscous, clear, a filament of deception. From it exudes the precise chemical signature of a nutrient seep—a sudden, localized promise of sulfur and life. I flick it, ever so slightly. The motion is invisible to the large eyes, but in my lateral sight, I see the thread’s vibration ripple the air.

The amphibian senses it. Its head lifts. Its primitive brain, tuned to the chemistry of the cleft, registers feast. It turns, sluggish, and begins its ponderous approach. Closer. Its own motion is a violent disturbance in my lateral sight, a crashing wave of kinetic energy.

I wait until its neck is extended, until it is committed. Then I uncoil.

The strike is not a leap but a swift, piston-like extension. My neck shoots forward; my needle-teeth find the junction between skull and spine. A prick. The injection is instantaneous. The neurotoxin is a wave of stillness. The creature’s limbs lock. Its pulse slows. It is alive, meat preserved, but its world has gone dark and silent. I withdraw my teeth, clean. I take the heavy, paralyzed body in my jaws, the pebbled skin rough on my tongue, and carry it into the deeper gloom of the fern thicket to feed.

Later, I move on, my belly full and warm. My scat, deposited in a hollow filled with new sphagnum, is rich with the indigestible: fern spores like orange dust, the tiny, hard pits of the waxy blue berries. I am the gardener. I do not know this. I only follow the compulsion to mark, to clear. The seeds will sprout in my waste, and a new thicket will rise, shaping the emptiness.

When the great vent’s mood turns furious, when the bass-note roar deepens and the jet of steam becomes a violent, shattering plume, I am drawn to the heart.

I approach the sinter-crusted rim. The heat is blistering, a dry fury that cuts through the constant wet. The stone trembles. I lie prone, pressing my belly to the radiating rock. I bring my face close to the roaring, unseen column. My eyes close. The sixfold vision shuts down. In the darkness, I feel it: the raw, planetary vibration. It courses up through the stone, into my bones, into the core of me. The hygroscopic filaments of my pelt seem to resonate, absorbing not just moisture now, but the pure vitality of the engine. I am recharging. I am plugging into the source. This is my communion.

I am the calculus. I am the regulation. The birds explode from the ferns in metallic alarm because I have passed. The amphibians grow fat on the rocks because I cull them. The ferns spread because I carry them. I move through the drip and the roar, a silent, sleek component in the wet, breathing machine. The cleft is my body, extended. I am its purpose, and it is mine. We hum together in the deep, cold dark.
