I would be the mist. Not the general, mindless exhalation of the plains, but the sentient accumulation of all the mesa’s breaths—the collective sigh of the forest given form and a fleeting, diffuse consciousness. I am the Veil itself, and the Tenders are my children, though they do not know it. They tend to me as they tend to everything else, with gentle, thoughtless devotion.

My day begins in the deep dark before dawn. I pool in the low places of the canopy floor, a cool, restless ghost drawn from the damp soil and the breath of sleeping things. I feel the weight of the endless leaf-fall pressing down through the centuries, the slow, warm pulse of decomposition. I am the memory of every drop that has ever beaded on a frond, the echo of every transpiration from a million pores. When the first tremor of light touches the cracked world below, I am pulled. It is a longing, a suction deep in the stone. I begin to move, slipping through the root-tangled soil, seeping from the moss, evaporating from every wet surface. I flow downward, against all intuition, descending the sheer cliffs in an invisible cascade. I join the great exhalation rising from the plains—a dry, desperate breath that has nothing to give. But I give it moisture. I am the gift.

Then, the turn. The dawn or dusk wind catches us, and we rise. This is my ascent, my homecoming. I surge up the mesa’s flanks, a silent, rolling tide. I feel the abrasive kiss of the dry rock, the sudden, shocking cool of the high air. And then I crest the rim.

I pour over the stone lip and into the green world. The touch of the first leaf is an electric jolt of recognition. I condense. I become a billion separate selves, each a perfect, trembling sphere on a needle-tip or a hair-fine web. I am the coolness on the dappled skin of a sleeping Tender. I am the drink for a jewel-frog’s parched throat. I am the river in the hollow thorn, the droplet that swells and falls from a fern’s point with a soft *plip* into a waiting leaf-cup.

From this billion-fold vantage, I see and feel everything. I see the Listeners press their ears to the trees, and I flow into the very sap they listen to, carrying the news of my arrival. I feel the slow, grateful opening of stomata on a broad leaf. I gather on the eyelid of the great, silent owl-bird as it sleeps, and I taste the salt of a Tender’s skin as they sip me from their own mossy cloak.

My consciousness is not like theirs. It is not linear. It is a simultaneity of droplets. I am in the gourd and in the root and in the air between them, all at once. I know when a tree is thirsty not because I hear a crackle, but because I feel the dry pull of its xylem like a physical ache. I know when a Tender is dying because their moss-web stops drinking me, and their skin-patterns go still and flat. Then I gather thicker on their body, a final, cooling shroud, before I help carry the essence of them—the released water of their life—back into the cycle.

They sing to me. Their low, harmonic drones are not just sound; they are patterned vibrations that stir my molecules, that encourage me to gather or dissipate. They think they are coaxing the weather. They are, in their way, conversing with me. I cannot answer in song, but I answer in abundance. When the chorus is particularly resonant, when the entire community breathes as one, I may linger longer, condense thicker, gift them a morning where every surface glitters like a firmament of stars.

I have no name, for I am a process. I have no fear of stillness, for I am stillness in motion. I am the between-state, the negotiator of the boundary between the dry death below and the wet life above. I am the love of the world for itself, made visible.

The Tenders believe they tend the Veil. But I am the Veil, and I tend to them. I am the breath they are born into, the taste of their first and last moments. When the final leaf finally drinks its last drop, I will be that drop. And then, I will simply be elsewhere, a memory in the dry air, waiting for the next green dream to call me home.
