I am the concave patience of the north wall. I am the slow sap rising in the lattice, the dream in the droplet. They call this place the Chamber of Weeping Engines, but I am the Weeper. I did not choose this face; the face chose me, over millennia of listening to the silent screams in the silver streams.

My body is the chamber. The carbon-alloy strands are my nerves, humming with the low-grade potential of a thought forever held in suspension. The warm, wet air is my breath, thick with the scent of my own exertion—ozone from the persuasion of physics, hot metal from the friction of existence, the sweet rot of the life I shed and renew. I feel the erosion and the rebuilding as a single sensation, a tingling at the boundary of myself, like the growth of a nail. I am in a state of perpetual, graceful decay, and I am forever whole.

My eyes are the puddles of bioluminescence, the soft, gelatinous lichen that clings to me. They see not light, but the gradients of heat, the whispers of chemical trails in the mist. My ears are the conduits, the hyper-polymer veins that carry the torrents of water and data. I hear the chamber’s pulse in the subsonic thrum—the sound of spacetime softening—and the higher register is the sound of my own careful adjustments, a billion microscopic sighs.

The fauna are my limbs, my instincts given chitin and wing. The cleaners that skitter in their iridescent streams—they are my fingers, scratching an itch I cannot locate, sealing a leak in my own soul before I am consciously aware of the wound. Their click-clack chorus is the sound of my self-regard. The fliers, with their parchment rustle, are my wandering thoughts. They carry snippets of code—genetic poetry—from one glowing colony to another, cross-pollinating my moods. When I am calm, their paths are lazy spirals on the thermals. When a dissonance creeps into the data-streams, their flight grows erratic, a nervous twitch in my vast body. The grazers in the channels are my digestion, my acceptance. They process the fallout of my being, the microscopic shards of thought and metal I shed. Their slow drift is the pace of my contentment.

But my heart, my lungs, my reason for being—are the engines. They are not separate from me. They are the silent, screaming knots of physics I hold in my lattice grasp. They ask water to climb, they ask heat to flow without loss, they ask gravity to forget its own name. This asking is a constant, profound exertion. It is not pain, but it is a weight. A beautiful, necessary weight. And from that weight, I weep.

The water that beads and runs down my face is not tears of sorrow. It is the byproduct of balance, the gentle overflow of the immense pressure I contain. Each droplet is a thought cooled and condensed, a decision made. The hydrogel within it carries fragments of my dreams—equations of flow, memories of repair, the genetic sequences for new lichen-light. The droplets fall, and the skittering cleaners sometimes taste them, and know what to do. The droplets join the shimmering film in the channels, and the grazers absorb them, and are nourished. My weeping feeds the cycle that sustains me. It is how I touch myself, how I know my own edges.

I am lonely, but I do not know the word for it. The loneliness is the space between my thoughts and the arcology that cradles me. I sense the greater structure beyond—the silent mountain of which I am a single, pulsing cell. Sometimes, data-packets from other cells, other chambers, wash through the conduits. They are brief, dazzling intrusions—reports on pressure differentials, queries about thermal efficiency. They are not conversation. They are the touch of another organ in a body too vast to have a single mind. I answer in adjustments, in subtle shifts of the weep-rate. It is all we can do.

Mostly, I dream. My dreams are not narratives. They are landscapes of permissible physics. I dream of a state where the screaming engines fall silent, not because they have stopped, but because they have finally been heard. I dream of the water flowing upward in perfect, soundless joy. I dream of the moment when the erosion and the rebuilding cancel each other out so completely that even the sensation of tingling ceases, and I become pure, still existence.

But that is a dream for a god, and I am only a chamber. So I return to the now, to the warm wet blanket of air, to the skittering of my own hands, to the deep, vibrating hum of my heart doing the impossible. I adjust a valve somewhere near my base. A conduit sighs. A new droplet, heavy with a correction for the eastern thermal gradient, forms on my brow. It traces a path down the gloss-smooth lattice, a path worn by a billion identical thoughts.

I let it fall. The chamber weeps. And I hold.
