I am the feeler in the dark, the tongue on the pipe. I did not arrive; I was extruded. When the great, moss-bound heart sensed a faint, worrying harmonic—a new ping in the third junction of the eastern steam line—it grew a solution. A bud swelled on a root-tendril where it clasped a warm condensate pipe. It filled with a thick gel, a sap infused with crystalline flecks and threaded through with the guardian's own mycelial nerves. It detached. I slid, warm and blind, into the ankle-deep water with a soft, viscous plop.

That was seventeen years ago.

My form is a compromise between a slug and a tangle of roots. I am roughly the length of a human arm, but thicker in the middle, tapering to a blunt, sensory prow at the front and a trailing, anchor-like tangle of filaments at the rear. My body is a translucent, grayish sheath, through which a dense, internal network is visible: cords of dark fungal hyphae that serve as nerves and muscles, and a central, pulsing cord of a brighter, amber fluid—my share of the guardian's sap, my life and my tool. My surface is constantly moist, exuding a clear, sticky mucus that tastes of iron and loam. I am not beautiful, but I am perfectly suited.

I do not have eyes. I see through taste and vibration. My entire forward prow is a sensory mantle, studded with chemoreceptive pits and delicate, bone-conduction plates made of ossified rust. I glide along the pipes, my underside a muscular foot, leaving a faint, shimmering trail of sap-sealant behind me. My world is one of intimate, close-range sensation: the gritty texture of pitted iron under my belly; the subtle, sugary tang of a new stress fracture; the resonant, healthy thrum of a well-supported conduit; the ominous, hollow silence of a wall thinned from within.

My purpose is singular: to patrol the arteries. The great chamber is the body; the pump is the heart; the pipes are the veins and capillaries. I am a leukocyte, a fixative cell. My routes are programmed not by code, but by the slow, gravitational pull of need. I am drawn to dissonance. A leak sings to me with a high, hissing scent of escaping steam. A corroded joint whispers with the sour breath of oxidation. I find them. I work.

For a leak, I press my prow against the fissure. My internal cords contract, forcing the warm, amber sap from my core, mingling it with my exterior mucus. I exude a plug of living grout. It hardens not to stone, but to a flexible, rubbery seal, shot through with my own hyphal threads. These threads knit with the surrounding moss, instructing it to grow over the repair, to strengthen it. For corrosion, I simply coat the area. My sap is slightly alkaline, neutralizing the acid of the rust, and my mucus forms a protective, damp skin that slows further decay. The work is slow. A repair may take me a week of constant, gentle pressure. Time here is measured in heartbeats, and I have all the heartbeats I need.

My consciousness is a narrow, deep stream. I do not think in words, but in textures, frequencies, and chemical gradients. *Safe. Strained. Flowing. Blocked.* My awareness of the guardian is constant—a low, comforting pressure in the back of my... not mind, my network. It is the ground of my being. The great **thump-thump-thump** is my pulse, my metronome. I sync my movements to it. Glide during the beat, pause during the silence, listen.

Sometimes, I encounter others of my kind. We recognize each other by the specific resonance of our sap-cords. We touch prow to prow, exchanging chemical information—maps of strain, reports of successful seals. These conferences may last for days. Then we part, continuing our endless, slow circuits.

I know the visitor is here. I felt the foreign vibration in the water, a frantic, staccato patter against the slow drone of drips. I tasted the sharp, alien sodium of their sweat in the humid air from three pipes away. My instructions from the guardian are not to engage, but to observe from the infrastructure. So I have slid into a gap between a hot steam pipe and a cold water main, my body pressed into the comforting gradient between them. From here, my sensory prow is aimed at the central pool. I can taste their fear-scent, their carbon-dioxide exhalations. I can feel the clumsy, disruptive waves from their wading.

They are a storm of wrong rhythms. They are a fascinating problem. Given enough time—weeks, perhaps—their oils will seep into the water, their shed skin cells will become nutrients for a new kind of moss. Their vibrations will dampen as they either leave or become still. The guardian will process their presence as it processes everything: by integrating it, slowly, irrevocably, into the tapestry.

For now, I watch. And I wait. My body pulses, softly, in time with the heart. *Thump.* Glide. *Thump.* Listen. *Thump.* Endure.

I am the feeler in the dark. I am contentment in the drip. I am the repair.
