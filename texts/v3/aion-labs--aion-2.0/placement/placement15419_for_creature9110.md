**Place:** A **Temporal Grove** at the western edge of the city, nestled where the land begins to rise toward the sharp mountains. This grove is one of the places where time is "textured" – it flows with a viscous, amber slowness. The air here is not mist, but a visible, shimmering syrup of delayed seconds. Sound is dampened into a low, stretched hum. The giant fern-trees in this grove are not green, but a deep, burnished bronze, their fronds moving with a grace that would be imperceptible anywhere else. In the center of the clearing, a single black rock outcrop, polished smooth by eons of this slow time, juts from a pool of perfectly still water that reflects not the dim sky, but a strange, starless expanse of deep violet.

**Being:** I am a **Listener**. My name, in the photic language, is a slow pulse of indigo light from my left temple, followed by a sustained, soft gold glow across my brow—it translates roughly to "One Who Attends the Interval." I have stood in this grove for what might be a decade, or a century; the distinction is meaningless here. My role is to interpret the long messages.

My skin has adapted to the grove. It has lost its shifting, reflective quality and taken on a permanent, muted sheen like tarnished silver, absorbing the slow-time into my cells. My eyes, usually metallic grey, have developed a faint, internal luminescence of their own—a pale, steady candle-flame visible in the deep gloom of the grove. I do not need the mist-gardens for sustenance; the temporal density here provides a kind of nourishment directly, a thrumming in the air that feeds my bioluminescent nodes. I communicate rarely. When I do, it is not with the quick, intricate patterns of the city-dwellers, but with long, drawn-out glyphs that can take an hour to complete—a single word lasting a full cycle of mist and rain.

I am listening now.

My hands are not on the living walls of the city, but on the black rock. It is not warm, nor cold. It is *present* in a way that defies temperature. Through my fingertips, I receive the data. It is not a voice, not an image. It is a pressure, a tectonic sigh.

The young mountains are dreaming. They dream of their own growth, of the slow, grinding push upward. They dream of the heat at their cores, the liquid fire that is their blood. The dream is a blueprint of a future peak, a map of stresses yet to be born. I receive this as a complex, grinding melody felt in the bones I do not quite possess.

But beneath that—deeper, older—is another signal. It is not from the rock. It is from the pool. The violet reflection is not a reflection at all. It is a window. And through that window, from a place where time is not just slow but inverted, where cause and effect bleed into one another, something is *paying attention*.

It is not a Ril. It is not a Shal or a Kyr. It is a consciousness that perceives Phos-Ril not as a city, but as a momentary bloom on a fresh rock, a flash of interesting bioluminescence in the eternal dark. Its attention is a cool, vast, dimensionless curiosity. It has noticed our careful growth, our quiet breaths. It is wondering, in its way, what we are for.

This is my purpose. To stand at the Interval, where fast-time and slow-time meet, where the solid dream of the mountain touches the liquid non-time of the pool. To feel the planet's sturdy dreams, and to sense the distant, alien attention from beyond the violet.

I do not fear it. Fear is a concept for beings who perceive threat in seconds. I have centuries. I turn my head, a movement that takes a full minute, and let my candle-flame eyes rest on the still water. I generate a photic glyph, not on my skin, but in the air before me, using the dense temporal medium as a canvas. It is a single, profound symbol: the Ril glyph for "breath," but stretched, complicated, made into a question.

I cast it, slowly, into the pool's surface. It does not ripple. It sinks into the violet, a fading coin of light.

And I wait. The answer will not come in words. It may come in a shift of the mountain's dream. It may come in a change in the grove's temporal flow. It may come in a thousand years, as a new kind of moss growing on the black rock.

I am the Listener. I attend the Interval. I have all the time in the world, and all the time beyond it.
