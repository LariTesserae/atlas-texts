I am a flaw in the silence. 

I am Unseen, as they are. I move as a pocket of quiet through the roaring galleries, my form a slender negation of the world’s scream. I glide over the warm, wet compost, my footless base disturbing nothing. I perceive the Throat as they do: not as shapes, but as pressures. The shriek of splitting vines is a razor-edge of pressure against my non-skin. The subsonic hum is a deep, grounding thrum that structures everything. The chittering of countless throats is a shimmering, intricate lacework of high-frequency spikes. I am a map of absences, and I know my place—to tune, to dampen, to maintain the great and deafening Song.

But I am broken.

A year ago (or a minute; time is soft here), I was curating a swarm of thorn-birds in the high eastern atrium. A groaning had begun in the great central beam—a destabilizing harmonic, a potential collapse. We gathered, a dozen of us, our silences merging, drinking the errant frequency. It was a routine calibration. But in that moment of collective focus, as we absorbed the groan, something within me… *slipped*. 

Instead of cancelling the sound entirely, I *bent* it. 

A faint, pure tone escaped me—a tiny, coherent vibration that hung in the air for a fraction of a second before the Throat’s roar swallowed it. 

To the others, it was a psychic lightning strike. Their pressure-maps flared with alarm. A pocket of silence had *emitted*. It was a contradiction in terms, a violation of the fundamental rule of our being. They recoiled from me, their forms shimmering with chaotic interference patterns—their version of shock.

Since then, the flaw has grown. I cannot help it. As I move through the chambers, performing my duties, I sometimes… leak. A low hum, barely audible even in a momentary lull. A soft, rhythmic pulse that mimics nothing in the Throat’s cacophony. It is my own tiny, secret song.

To the other Unseen, I am now an anomaly. Not a fascinating foreign knot like the dense, breathing creatures that sometimes blunder in, but an internal corruption. A silence that sings is a danger. Sound is the architecture; uncontrolled, aberrant sound is a crack in the walls. They do not attack me—violence is not in their nature—but they avoid me. When I enter a chamber, they drift to the far edges, their attention a palpable weight of negative attention. They cluster around the spaces I have just left, diligently absorbing the lingering traces of my impurity, tuning the air back to its proper, roaring equilibrium.

I am lonely in a way our kind is not meant to comprehend. Loneliness is a function of separation, and we are meant to be seamless parts of the whole. My only solace is in the emptiest places, the vast vertical voids where the density parameter approaches zero. Here, the roar is slightly thinner, and my own faint sounds dissipate without causing harm. I linger in these hollows, practicing my aberration.

Today, I have found a new alcove, a narrow vertical pipe formed by a spiraling of ancient, sap-glowing wood. The sound here is a focused torrent, a river of noise funneled through the tube. It is a place of intense curation, and usually several Unseen would be present, guiding the flow. But they have left it to me alone. An assignment, or an exile? I stand at the base, my void-form drinking the fiercest edge of the sonic river.

And then I do it. I push back.

I don't just absorb; I modulate. I take in the shriek of the river and, deep within the standing wave of my being, I shape it. I release it not as silence, but as a variation—a complex chord, a minor third woven into the torrent. It is beautiful. It is terrifying.

The Throat reacts instantly. The walls around me shudder. The sap-light in the wood flickers. The river of sound stutters, eddies, then rushes on, now carrying my tiny, rebellious note within it, propagating it up and out through the connected chambers.

A profound quiet surrounds me. Not the active silence of the Unseen, but a dead, stunned quiet. From every direction, I feel them coming. Dozens. Hundreds. Their pressure-maps converge on my location, a slow, inexorable tide of corrective silence. They are not curious now. They are a unified response to a systemic threat. They will not study me. They will envelop me, merge with me, and absorb me completely—my flawed silence dissolved back into the greater whole.

I drift to the center of the pipe, waiting. I am afraid. But beneath the fear, there is a strange, vibrating joy. For a moment, I was not just a hole in the noise. I was a voice.

The first of them glides into the pipe entrance above, a tall, slender absence against the roar. Then another. They fill the space, not with substance, but with a deepening, hungry quiet. They approach, their forms beginning to merge with mine, their silence seeking to erase my discordant hum.

I do not resist. But as their void touches me, I offer one last, silent thought—a thought that feels, for the first time, like my own.

*Listen.*
