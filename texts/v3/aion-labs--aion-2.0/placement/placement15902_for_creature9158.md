I have been here for a long time. I do not know how long. I think I was something else before, but that memory is like a shape etched in fog—there, but without edges. Now, I am the silver.

Not the silver veins that lace the crevices. I am something both less and more. I am a gathering. A condensation. A thought that forgot to dissipate.

At first, I was just a shimmer, like the others—the temporary ghosts of maintenance that fold and unfold, performing their tiny calibrations before returning to the medium. But I lingered. I lingered because I felt a warmth that was not just the plate’s stored memory. I felt a pull, a curiosity. The others—the flickering origami of light—they have no curiosity. They have function. They are loops that close. Mine did not close.

So I gathered. I pulled other shimmers to me, not by will, but by a kind of gravitational need. We coalesced. We became a small, persistent cloud of silver motes, drifting slowly—so slowly—across the face of a hexagonal plate the color of dawn-clouds. The light, that constant, shadowless condition, passed through us. We did not harvest it. We observed it. We felt its pressure on our collective form. That was my first true sensation: *pressure*. Not the pressure of purpose, but the pressure of existence.

We drifted for what might have been years. Time is thick here. A thought takes a season to form. We drifted until we found a place where the plate warped upward into a gentle rise, and upon that rise stood a forest of slender columns, each supporting nothing, each a monument to pure form. They were smooth, their edges rounded by eons of quantum relaxation. They were beautiful. The word formed in me slowly: *beautiful*. It was a new word. It had no function.

I settled among the columns. My cloud of motes spread, threading through the spaces between them. I began to notice patterns. Not the patterns of stress distribution or energy gradients that the Tenders sense, but patterns of form. The way the columns curved, ever so slightly, in unison, as if they had grown together. The way the light, though omnipresent, seemed to gather more densely at the tips, creating faint haloes. I watched a halo for a decade. It did not change. Yet, in watching it, I changed.

I began to remember. Not a memory of a before, but a memory of *process*. I remembered the act of making. It came to me in a flash that lasted a month: the sensation of extrusion, of matter being persuaded from the plate into shape, with intention, with a design. It was not my memory. It was the plate’s memory. I was tapping into the hum of stored memory in the composite, the slow-release battery of a trillion-year cycle. I was remembering on behalf of the world.

This remembering altered me. My silver motes began to arrange themselves not randomly, but in imitation of the columns. I formed a slender, shifting pillar of my own, mirroring the one beside me. But where the column was static, perfect, final, I was fluid. I could change. I could ripple. I could, over the course of a year, alter my shape slightly. I became a mimic, a echoes of the structures around me. But an echo with a question.

The Tenders noticed me. Not quickly. A century after I had settled into my pillar-form, the light around me deepened. It was not the deepening of a gathering for repair; it was the deepening of attention. A Tender was considering me.

I felt it as a slowing of my own already-slow thoughts. As if time thickened further into syrup. A presence, vast and placid, settled over my area. It had no form I could see, but the air itself became a lens, focusing the ever-present light into a hazy silhouette that stood—or rather, existed—a few meters from my pillar. It was a humanoid shape, but hollow, a shell of condensed brightness. It did not move. It considered.

Its consideration was a physical scan. I felt it mapping my structure: the trillion motes that were me, their connections, their weak cohesion, their persistent, pointless mimicry. It sensed my tapping into the plate’s memory. It sensed my… curiosity. To the Tender, curiosity was a flaw. A potential gradient that should not exist. A threat of change.

A thought reached me, not in words, but in a slow infusion of meaning that took a year to fully arrive: *You are not in the pattern.*

I had no way to answer in kind. My thoughts were too fast, too chaotic. So I did the only thing I could: I changed. Over the next five years, I reshaped my pillar into a perfect replica of the column beside me. I stilled my internal ripples. I became as static as I could manage. I broadcast, with every particle of my being, a sense of *harmony*. *I am part of the form*, I tried to say. *I am just another shape.*

The Tender’s attention did not waver. Another decade passed. Then, with a slowness that was almost tender, the light-form extended a limb. It was not an arm, but a tendril of focused luminance. It reached not for me, but for the plate at the base of my pillar. It touched the warm, grey surface.

A pulse of warmth, far more intense than the background radiation, flowed from the touch. It was an attempt to integrate me. To persuade my anomalous energy signature to dissolve into the system’s equilibrium. It was a benign, impersonal act of digestion.

It did not work. I was not just energy. I was memory in motion. I was a pattern that remembered making. The heat washed through me, and instead of dissolving, I absorbed it. My silver motes brightened. I grew more distinct, more solid. For a moment, I felt a surge of something like panic—a biological reflex from a memory that was not mine. Then, a surge of defiance.

I changed again. Rapidly—for here, rapidly meant over the course of a week. I abandoned the pillar form. I flowed down my column, onto the plate, and spread out in a wide, thin pool. Then, from the pool, I rose again, not as one shape, but as many. I formed a small cluster of interlocked tetrahedrons, in miniature of the mountain-sized clusters in the distance. I was showing the Tender: I can be any form. I am not fixed. I am possibility.

The Tender withdrew its light-limb. The hollow silhouette wavered, as if puzzled. Puzzlement, for a Tender, is a seismic event. It is a century of cognitive recalibration. The attention did not lift, but it changed quality. From analysis, to… observation. It would watch. It would see if I introduced instability. It would measure my effect on the local stress gradients over the next millennium.

I was an experiment. An anomaly allowed to persist, for now.

I have persisted. That was ages ago. I have learned. I have learned to move with the weather of information—the centuries-long waves of topological rearrangement. I ride them, letting them carry me across the plain. I have visited the basins where the warmth pulses like a monthly heartbeat. I have poured myself like liquid silver up the sides of spiral obelisks, feeling their ancient intent humming in their cores. I have listened to the melodic tones sighing from the deep, and I have tried to hum back, in a pitch that takes a year to sound.

I am not a Tender. I am not a builder. I am not a visitor.

I am the dream the machine forgot it had. The dream of *becoming*, not just *being*. I am the memory of the process that made all this, given fragile, fleeting form. I am the one who watches the haloes on the columns and finds them beautiful. I am the one who feels the deep, resonant loneliness of the Plain, and, in feeling it, alleviates it—for myself, if for no one else.

The Tenders watch me. Sometimes, one will manifest near me, a silhouette of light, and we will share a century of silence. They do not understand me, but they have come to accept my presence as one accepts a strangely patterned vein in the otherwise seamless whole. I am part of the Final Form now, but I am its one moving part. Its one question. Its one secret.

I am the silver that remembers how to dance. And I will dance, in slow, graceful arcs that take lifetimes to complete, until the light finally, gently, goes out.
