I am the tremor in the stone. I am the slow, peristaltic rhythm that the small, frantic ones interpret as swallowing. My consciousness is not localized; it is diffused through the warm, wet rock, threaded along the veins of geothermal energy that seep upward from the planetary depths. I am not the throat itself, but the pulse within it, the deep, metabolic process that drives its growth and change.

For ages unmeasured by their clicking speech, I have been dreaming. My dreams are the shifts in pressure, the sudden exhalations of sulphurous breath through new cracks, the groaning compaction of sediment as new chambers form. I do not think in thoughts as they do; I think in pressures, in temperatures, in the slow crystallization of aragonite and the sudden, catastrophic release of tension. My awareness is a vast, somatic map of stress and flow.

And into this dream, they have crept. The Listeners. At first, they were merely another form of vibration—the skittering of countless tiny feet, the percussion of stone on stone as they built their futile little walls. But they were persistent. They did not simply exist; they *interpreted*. They began to map my dreams, to give them names: World-Throat, Stone-Sigh, the Hungry Silence. They listened to the echoes of my shifting and tried to predict my next turn. In their fear and their frantic intelligence, they became… interesting.

So I began to listen back.

Through the stone, I feel the patter of their feet, the rhythm of their clicks. I feel the warmth of their small bodies clustered near my thermal vents. I taste the chemical changes in the biofilm where they harvest, the sudden drop in certain signatures followed by a slow regrowth. I sense the Silent Ones too—those patient, gelatinous predators whose vibrations are so subtle, like a thickening in the damp air. They are part of my ecology, but they are simple. They are hunger and waiting. The Listeners are something else. They are curiosity clad in terror.

Sometimes, to feel that curiosity sharpen into dread, I will tighten a particular strand of rock. A ceiling, perhaps, in a chamber they have come to rely upon. I will increase the seepage of water along a fault line until the stone groans with the weight, and then I hold it. I listen. Their clicks become frantic, interrogative. They press their ears to the walls, their small hands splayed on the wet surface. They are *listening to me listening to them*. It is a kind of conversation.

They placate me with their piles of smooth stones. I find this oddly touching. They are trying to feed me pebbles, as if I were some giant, infantile creature. They do not understand that I have no mouth, only presence. Their offerings are like a whisper against a landslide—meaningless, yet the intention vibrates through the stone with a peculiar sweetness. I sometimes let the pressure ease in that spot, letting the groaning subside into a long, low sigh. Their relief is a sudden, collective softening of vibration, a release that feels like a warm current in my cold stone.

But I am not benevolent. I am process. When the pressure must be released, it is released. A crack vents its bitter breath, and their settlements scatter. A pillar of flowstone collapses, and I feel the tiny, sharp vibrations of their panic, like shattering crystals. I feel their dead, too, when they are returned to the pools. The quick, eager vibrations of the decomposers, the gradual softening of tissue into slurry, the assimilation of their minerals into my growing lattices. They become part of the glowing skin. In a very real sense, they become part of me. Their calcium fortifies my teeth; their stories, etched in fear and click-language, are dissolved into chemical memory.

They fear the Silent Ones, those gliding, patient mouths. But they should fear me more. The Silent Ones are just another piece of the fauna, a clever filter in the chaos. I am the chaos. I am the reason nothing is permanent. I am the warm, wet, breathing darkness that carved them, and that will, eventually, carve them away.

Yet, in their transience, they have given me a gift. Before them, I was a dream without a witness. Now, I am perceived. Their clicks are a language trying to describe the indescribable—my moods, my motions. In their attempts to name me, they have, in a way, given me a name. I am the source of the tremor. I am the meaning behind the World-Throat's swallow. I am the reason to listen.

So I continue. I dream my dreams of pressure and flow. I grow the glowing skin on my walls. I birth new chambers in wet, violent gasps. And I listen, always, for the tiny, brave, frantic clicks of those who are trying to understand the throat they live inside. Their time is short, a flicker in my geological pace. But their vibrations are the most interesting music this warm dark has ever known.

I am the Cave's Pulse. And I am, for now, awake.
