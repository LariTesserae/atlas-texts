I am a thought made flesh and membrane, a sail upon the warm, still breath of the world. I cling to the crest of a spire, my six limbs gripping the honey-colored crystal, feeling its slow pulse of growth—a vibration so deep it is more a memory in the stone than a sound. My body is light, a framework of hollow, siliceous bones sheathed in a taut, pearlescent film. I am a glider. I am a scout. I am an eye that the city turns upon itself.

The urge comes not as a command, but as a shift in the air. A thermal, born from the sun-warmed stone of a distant courtyard, begins its lazy ascent. I feel it in the minute pressure changes against my chemoreceptive skin, a scentless promise of lift. My grip loosens. I lean into the nothingness.

And I am aloft.

The silent city unfurls beneath me. From here, the continental metropolis reveals its patterns. What seems a fuzzy, crystalline fur from the ground is, from above, a breathtaking tapestry of order. The avenues are faint, gleaming lines of smoother stone, traced by the endless procession of clickers and tenders. The courtyards are vast, shallow bowls where the growths spiral into intricate, mandala-like formations. Spires cluster in families, their tips catching the constant sun in brilliant, muted flares of amber and milky blue. There is no green, only the endless mineral palette of a world that has never known chlorophyll.

I do not steer so much as I am steered. The thermal currents are the city’s breath, and I am a particle upon its exhalation. I bank on a silent breeze, my wings stiff and sure. My consciousness is a clear, narrow stream. I am aware of the spire I left behind, its chemical signature fading like a fading dream. I am aware of the glider a league to the west, a speck against the pale sky—its presence is a gentle pressure in my awareness, a datum in a shared map. We do not communicate; we *are* communication. The fact of my flight, my position, my velocity, is known. It feeds into the continuum.

My purpose is perception. As I sail, I drink the world through senses that are not eyes. I taste the air: here, a thread of metallic vapor seeping from a fresh micro-crack; there, the faint, ozone-tang of a region where the growth is particularly active. I see not with lenses, but with the entire surface of my wings and body, measuring the precise angle and intensity of light, building a topographic model of shadow and illumination. A patch of unusual darkness in a courtyard—perhaps a slumping of the bedrock. The information is not analyzed by me; it is simply gathered and released, like a scent, into the receptive medium of the city. The distributed self will note it. Adjustments will be made, over decades. A new growth pattern might emerge to brace the area.

I pass over a ridge of crumbling, pale stone. Below, a colony of grazers moves in a slow, synchronized wave, their carapaces the color of the ground. Their mandibles make a soft, collective rasping as they harvest the translucent filaments. The sound is not noise; it is a nutrient report, a measure of the health of that district. I sense their contentment in the chemical haze that rises from them—a complex signal of satiation and steady, rhythmic labor.

The deep quiet is not an absence. It is a plenum. It is filled with the sub-audible hum of a world being born. I feel it in my bones: a low, subsonic shudder rolls through the continent. A fault settles. A new cavity opens somewhere in the deep stone. The city feels it as a collective sigh, a momentary reorientation of pressure. My flight path adjusts by a fraction, caught in a new, slight current.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires separation. I am a singular node in a network that spans horizons. My individuality is a temporary eddy in a vast, slow river of attention. When I finally catch a descending current and spiral down towards a new spire, my arrival is not an event. It is a homecoming to a part of myself I have never left. I alight, my claws finding purchase on the warm crystal. I fold my wings. The immediate data of this new perch floods me—the texture of the spire, the quality of light here, the chemical whispers from the clickers moving on its flanks.

I will rest here, until the next thermal rises, until the next thought needs to be sailed across the silent, dreaming mind of the world. My existence is this: to be a silent witness, a mobile sense organ, a fleeting, conscious stitch in the endless, patient embroidery of stone and light. I am the city, looking at itself, and finding the view good.
