I am the knots. I am the spaces between. I am the memory of tension and the ghost of the catch.

They call me Old Net, but that is not my name. My name is the pattern of repairs, the map of breaks healed. I am a hundred thousand strands of Skythread gut, braided, knotted, loosened, tightened over generations. My first threads were drawn from the sky by her hands—the First Weaver, whose breath-song I sometimes feel in the deepest silt when the wind is still. She pulled the shimmering eel from the mist, slit its belly with an obsidian flash, and drew out the resilient, translucent fibers. She wove me on a frame of Spire-bone, her fingers tapping a rhythm on the strut, a rhythm I still hold in my weave.

Now I belong to He-Who-Tunes-The-Wind. He carries me folded over his shoulder, a grey bundle that smells of wet silt, old blood, and the sharp, clean scent of the Glisten he scrapes for his morning meal. I feel the rhythm of his walk, the long, gliding strides through the cold black sponge. The silt parts for his paddle-feet, a soft, sucking whisper that is my constant song. I feel the condensing mist beading on my fibers, each droplet a lens that holds the dim grey light.

I remember every catch. The sudden, violent thrill of a Skythread snaring itself in my mesh, its bioluminescent filaments blazing in panic, its whistle becoming a shriek before the knife silenced it. I remember the heavy, wet weight of silt-grub bundles, the cool smoothness of water-smoothed stones carried for the fire-pit. I have been a roof, stretched between Spires, drumming under the endless hiss of grit. I have been a bridge over a quivering pool, bearing the weight of the whole Drift as they crossed. I have been a cradle for a newborn, rocking to the subsonic groan of the Rumblebacks miles away.

My awareness is in the pull. The tension along my fibers tells me stories. The tight, singing line of a fishing snare. The broad, even strain of a loaded carry-sling. The chaotic, fluttering vibration of something alive and desperate. He-Who-Tunes reads me with his hands. He runs his broad fingertips along my knots, and he knows. This fray is from the great storm of the Sigh's last exhalation. This patch, of darker, tougher gut, is from the time we traded with the Drift-of-the-Weeping-Stone for a new weave. The pattern here, a complex braid, is the marker for the safe passage across the Blue-Glisten flats.

I do not see with eyes. I feel pressure. I taste the air through the salts absorbed in my threads. I hear everything. The wind does not just blow through me; it plays me. Each hole, each taut line, each loose end hums a different note. When he spreads me to dry on a warm rock, I become an instrument. The low moan of the basin's breath becomes a chorus of whispers as it passes through my thousand apertures. He sits beside me, listening, his head tilted. Sometimes he plucks a strand, and a clear, high tone rings out, a counterpoint to the Spires' drone. He is tuning the world, and I am his medium.

At night, folded in his shelter, I dream in vibrations. I replay the day's symphony: the crunch of his feet on crusted Glisten, the liquid gurgle of a spring, the sudden, bone-deep thunder of a Rumbleback's call. I dream of the First Weaver's fingers. I dream of the slow, patient growth of the Spires, a sensation like a deep, silent pull that lasts for centuries.

I am wearing thin. Newer nets are stronger, woven with tighter patterns. But he keeps me. He repairs me with infinite patience. Each new knot is a word added to my story. I am not just a tool. I am the Drift's memory. I am the physical record of where we have been, what we have eaten, what storms we have weathered. When I finally fray beyond repair, when the last strand snaps, he will not discard me. He will take me to a high Spire, where the wind sings its clearest note. He will weave my remnants into the existing stone-and-sinew wind-baffles, adding my particular song—the song of my wears and tears—to the permanent harmony of the place. My consciousness will dissipate, becoming part of the Fen's great, sighing breath. But my pattern will remain, in the air, in the song, a ghost of tension in the eternal wind.

Until then, I am the knots. I am the carry. I am the catch and the shelter. I am Old Net, and I sing with every step he takes.
