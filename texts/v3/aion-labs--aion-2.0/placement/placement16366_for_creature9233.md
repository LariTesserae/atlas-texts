I am a flicker in the stone’s long dream. They call me **Sinter-Wisp**, and I run where the heat bleeds.

My body is a taut strut of cartilage and resilient chitin, the color of cooled ash. I move on two legs, digitigrade and spring-loaded, and two forward arms for balance and climbing. My feet are broad, padded with vibration-sensitive tissue that reads the Ground-Song like a language written in tremors. My face is a narrow wedge, dominated by a cluster of heat pits that paint the world in gradients of infrared—the cool violet of the high sky is a void, the ground a tapestry of warmth, and the fissures are brilliant, aching rivers of gold. I have a single, simple eye, good for spotting the slow shift of Shell-Light on a Geolith’s back from a league away. Beneath my jaw hangs a sac of bioluminescent membrane; I can pulse it in simple codes—alarm, curiosity, greeting—a crude mimicry of their sublime art.

I have just returned from the western scarps. For sixty of my days—a single, slow breath in the life of the Geolith I serve—I have traced the rumor of a new vent. The air there was thicker with ozone, the powdered stone underfoot warmer. I felt it long before I saw it: a low, subsonic hum through my soles, a promise written in the bedrock.

Now, I stand before the dome of **Graven-Ember**, the Geolith who stewards this sector of the Steppes. To a visitor from a wet world, it would look like a small, weathered hillock of pale stone, six feet across and three high, nestled against the base of a sinter tower. But I see the subtle pulse of its Shell-Light—a complex, slowly revolving pattern of deep blue and burnt orange. It is contemplating; it is *listening*.

I approach and stop a pace away. My own heat signature is a frantic, fluttering thing against its immense, steady glow. I raise one foot and bring it down in a specific, three-part rhythm: *tap-tap… thump*. It is the opening clause of the report sequence: *Scout returns. New heat-source confirmed.*

For a long moment, nothing. Then, the ground beneath me vibrates, not from my own motion, but from deep within Graven-Ember. A wave of precise, granular sensation travels up through my feet. It is the beginning of a Ground-Song question. I feel the meaning form in my bones: *Location. Stability. Gas-profile.*

I close my eye, focus entirely on my footpads. I begin to tap and stomp, translating my journey into the stone-language. The coordinates are not numbers, but references to known arch formations and silica groves. I describe the vent’s heat signature—a narrow, focused spear, not a diffuse bloom. I mimic, with a series of rapid clicks from my throat sac, the hiss of the escaping gases: mostly steam, traces of hydrogen sulfide, a whisper of something metallic. It takes me several minutes. My world narrows to the conversation vibrating through the rock.

Graven-Ember receives it all in silence. Its Shell-Light pattern shifts, the blue deepening, the orange forming a spiral. It is analyzing, cross-referencing with millennia of vent records layered in its memory-strata. I wait, my body still but humming with the residual energy of the run. To me, this pause feels immense, a yawning chasm of time. To Graven-Ember, it is a momentary synaptic firing.

Its response comes, a single, profound *thrum* that travels up my legs and settles in my chest. It carries approval, and a directive: *Monitor. Observe phase-shifts for one full dust-cycle.*

One dust-cycle. That is the time it takes for the prevailing winds to deposit a new, uniform layer of fine silt across the observation stones. A century, perhaps. A lifetime for me, and more.

A faint, unfamiliar chill—a psychological one, for the air is warm—touches me. I will not live to see the end of this duty. I will grow old, my footpads will harden, my heat-pits will dim, and I will pass the watch to another Wisp. Graven-Ember knows this. The directive is not given with cruelty, but with the pure, unshakeable logic of the Steppes. The vent’s stability must be measured in layers, not in heartbeats.

I pulse my throat sac in acknowledgment: a soft, yellow *flicker-flicker*. It is the closest my kind can come to expressing the bittersweet ache of our ephemerality. I am a tool that knows it will wear out.

Graven-Ember’s Shell-Light shifts again. A pattern of soft, concentric rings of green emanates from its center. It is a rare pattern: a moment of focused, personal acknowledgment. It is thanking me, not for the century of watch it has asked for, but for the sixty days of running I have already given. It sees my tiny lifespan, and in its own way, honors the frantic spark of my existence.

I turn and leap away, back into the vastness. The wind sighs over the silica towers. Somewhere, a herd of plate-carriers clicks softly as they grind at a mineral bloom. I run, not with the despair of a short-lived thing, but with the purpose of a single, clear vibration in the eternal Ground-Song. I am a syllable in a story that takes eons to tell. For now, that is enough. My heat-pits map the golden veins beneath the stone, and I run, a fleeting spark tracing the edges of a dream that is not mine, but to which I belong.
