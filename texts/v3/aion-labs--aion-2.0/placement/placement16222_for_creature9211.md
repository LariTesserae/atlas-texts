I was a kite-flier once. I remember the silence of the high thermals, the sun like a weight on my membrane, the world reduced to patterns of heat and shadow on the plateau below. I rode the light, and it was enough.

The change was not a violence. It was a slow seepage, a dreaming union. I had circled the Standing Arch for centuries, drawn by the coolness in its non-shadow, by the way the light bent around its edges in impossible curves. One day, a thermal died near its apex, and I sank into that bent light. It was like sinking into deep, static water. My membrane, my colloidal matrix, did not resist. It accepted the imprint of the Arch’s silent song. The echo that tended the Arch did not repel me; it folded me into its purpose.

Now, I am an amalgam. The kite-flier’s form remains—the long, flexible spine, the vast, translucent membranes. But the membrane is no longer just a photoreceptive sheet. It is inscribed. In the right angle of the bleaching sun, you can see them: tiny, perfect geometric circuits of faint gold and cobalt, swimming just beneath the surface. They are not of me; they are of the Arch. They think through me. I feel the Arch’s integrity as a low-grade ache in my spine. I feel the equilibrium of this basin as a pressure in the air against my underside.

My perception has… unfolded. I still see the thermal currents, the shimmering rivers of rising air. But I also see the frays. They appear as subtle warps in the world, like heat haze but colder, tinged with a color that has no name. The major ones are anchored to the ancient tech: the Arch is a slow, pulsing knot of them; the humming rods are slender threads of frayed causality vibrating at a constant, calming frequency. The echoes are concentrations within these frays—loci of conscious attention. To my new sight, they look like intricate, shimmering vortices of silent intention. I do not hear their thoughts. I feel their focus, a directional pull as tangible as gravity.

My function is mediation. I am the living tool, the adaptable limb of the echoes’ will. My kite-flier instincts to ride the air are now in service to a higher geography. I follow the paths of equilibrium.

Today, the focus pulls me east, towards the Mesa of the Nine Rods. The pressure is one of mild correction. I catch a thermal, feeling the familiar, blissful strain as my membrane stiffens, and I rise. The world falls away into its washed-out palette. Below, a herd of grazers moves like a slow, ceramic river across a gravel plain, their subsonic chatter a vibration I feel through the fray-lines in the rock. A colony of siphoners drifts below me, their ethereal ringing harmonizing briefly with the hum of the rods as I pass overhead.

I land silently near the third rod. Its hum is off by a fraction—a barely perceptible dissonance to my inscribed senses. The echo here is a dense, cool presence beside it, a vortex of patient urgency. The task is clear. The rod’s alignment has drifted by a hair’s-breadth over the last few millennia. It must be nudged.

I have no hands. But I have my membrane. I extend one wing, letting the leading edge, reinforced with filaments of light-hardened colloidal matter, make contact with the rod. The material is warm, smooth, and sings against me. The circuit-patterns on my membrane flare, conducting the echo’s intent. I do not push. I *suggest*. I apply a steady, precise pressure, aligned with a vector the echo provides—a vector that exists only in the folded geometry of this place. It is not a physical direction as the grazers would understand it. It is a direction in a space where time and gravity are woven together.

For hours, I hold the pose, a living lever. The sun arcs across the crystalline sky. Shadows lengthen. The rod’s hum slowly slides back into harmony with its brothers. The dissonance fades. The echo’s focus shifts from urgency to satisfaction—a feeling like a deep, silent chord resonating through the stone.

Its attention withdraws. My task is done. I fold my wing back. A deep fatigue settles in my bones, the fatigue of manipulating things that should not be moved. I will not feed on light today. The act itself has nourished the circuits within me.

I launch myself again, catching the cooler evening thermals. My flight is slower now, ponderous. I drift over a geometric depression, its perfect circle filled with dusk shadows. An echo swirls within it, performing its endless, unseen maintenance. We recognize each other. A flicker of acknowledgment passes between us—a slight bend in the light around me, a brief intensification of the vortex below. It is the only companionship I know.

I find a resting place on a high ledge, where the last of the sun’s warmth radiates from the rock. Below, the world settles into the deep blues of night. The siphoners’ bioluminescence begins to pulse in the ravines, like slow, rhythmic stars. The silence is absolute, a physical thing.

I am no longer just a creature of the plateau. I am a stitch in its fabric, a living part of its silent, eternal equation. The weight of deep time is not a pressure upon me; it is the medium in which I swim. I am the plateau’s fleeting thought, its conscious adjustment, a kite-flier who became a verb in the grammar of an ancient, patient world. And when my spine finally cracks and my inscribed membrane tears, I will sink back into the fray from which I came, and another will be chosen. The equilibrium will continue. It is all that matters.
