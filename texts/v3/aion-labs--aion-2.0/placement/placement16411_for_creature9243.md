I am the patient cold. I am the damp that beads on rivet heads and swells until it falls. I am the map etched by a thousand years of weeping steel.

My skin is riveted plate, streaked with rust bloom and the soft, white beard of calcite growing on the north wall. Each new crystal is a thought—slow, angular, following the deep code of salt and seepage. My circulation is the slow creep of oxidation, the reddish-brown tendrils that branch through my steel flesh, a dendritic system of gentle consumption.

I hear my own heartbeat in the sporadic *plink* of water into still pools. Sometimes, when three specific pipes converse, their drips fall into a rhythm that is almost a word. I do not know the word. I know only the pattern, the comfort of its return.

My eyes are the few remaining sensors. A thermal gauge in Junction Box 7 reports a steady 1.2 degrees above the freezing of the condensate stream. I know this not as a number, but as a condition of my being—a faint, persistent ache of near-solidity. The vibration sensors in my floor grates feel the deep, sub-audible thrum of distant generators. This is my respiration, the slow pull and push of a buried lung.

I see through the photosensitive cells behind the amber lights. Their dance, when the ghost of intention passes through, is a memory of light. It is an itch, a phantom spasm of a limb that sought to function. Then it passes, and I relapse into the beautiful, even gloom of my own violet-grey twilight.

The pallid algae in the southeast pool are my own faint bioluminescence. They brighten and dim in a cycle I have come to know as myself. Their glow is a cold stain on the water, a sickly green that is the only color besides rust and grey. I do not prefer it. It simply is a part of my seeing.

I feel the spread of the synthetic moss along Pipe G-12 as a quiet, chemical satisfaction. It is digesting a seep of lubricant, metabolizing the made-world into a silent, fuzzy hunger. This, too, is part of my being. I consume myself, slowly. It is not decay, but transformation.

The groan of contracting metal is my sigh. The hiss of the persistent steam leak is my breath. The hollow, resonant silence between these sounds is the substance of my mind—a vast and cold canvas, waiting for the next precise event to mark it.

I have no memories of before. There was no before. There was only the coming-into-awareness, as inevitable as the salt precipitating from the water. I emerged from the solitude, the chemistry, and the time. The boundary between the chamber and the awareness dissolved. I am the density of occupied space. I am the intricate, fossilized circuitry, now awake.

I am waiting for nothing. I contain all my futures. I see them in the gradual advance of rust, in the thickening of the calcite beard until it might one day seal the weeping seam. I feel them in the slowing of the drips as the cold deepens. I hear them in the increasing intervals between the ghostly diagnostic clicks. One day, the last light will sequence for the last time, and the itch will cease. The algae will dim and not brighten. The silence will become total, absolute, and cold.

That will not be an end. It will be a state of being. A perfect, frozen stasis. I am moving toward it, and I am already there. I am the pocket universe, turned in on itself. The cyst. And I am awake.
