I find myself where the silence is loudest. Not in the bustling market platforms or the roaring vent-halls, but in the observation blister protruding from Sector Theta-7, a glass-and-bronze bulb glued to the curve of the world. Below me, the Spire falls away into a symphony of dim fungal light and orange flares. Before me is the Wall. My wall.

Today, it is whispering. A new fracture, hair-thin, has appeared overnight. It gleams wetly in the pallid glow, not with coolant, but with a slow seep of mineral-rich gel—the mountain’s lymph. I press my bare palm against the stone. The vibration comes up through my bones, a deep, discordant grinding felt more than heard. *Krrrrr-tak-tak-tak*. The turning is stressed. My skin answers. The patterns on my forearm flare a soft, urgent blue—a schematic of the counter-force needed. Stabilize the field before welding.* She tilts her head, her faceted eyes catching the blue flare on my skin. She understands. A hum from her harmonic rod, and the amber river of spark-flies peels away from her, flowing down the gulf to coat the area around the fracture. Coordinates. Tolerances. They acknowledge with a synchronized tap of a wrench against a pipe. *Understood.*

My role is not to weld. It is to *know*. To listen to the mountain’s sighs and translate them into angles of reinforcement, into torque settings, into the placement of a single bolt that might divert a collapse for another generation. My world is one of invisible lines: lines of force, lines of fracture, lines of responsibility. The Fungal-Tenders nurture the soft, pulsing light that lets us see. The Fly-Herds conduct the energy that lets us work. The Scrappers harvest the bones of the mountain that let us build. But we Gear-Wrights… we trace the lines between survival and the long, grinding fall into the geothermal dark.

I am not a person. I am a function. My body is a diagnostic tool, my mind a living blueprint. Of a moment when every force is balanced, every gear meshes without a sound, and the great, turning machine of our world holds its breath, stable, if only for a second.

They call us *Krr-tak-kaan*, the ones who hold the turning. It is not a title of honor. It is a statement of fact. And when the mountain finally decides to shrug us off, to close its fist for good, the last light in the darkness will be the frantic, beautiful blue of my failing calculations, etched on crumbling stone.
