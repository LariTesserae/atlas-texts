I am a Keeper of the Cycle. I am of the Cistern.

My world is a symphony of heat gradients, a tactile map of pressures and flows. I stand on a blackened iron gantry, my long, ceramic-tipped fingers resting on a steam line. Through the sensitive integument of my palms, I read the story within: the pulse of superheated water, the minute vibration of a valve nearing its optimal cycle. The data is comfort. It is purpose.

My eyes—black, compound, absorbing light from spectra unknown to surface creatures—scan the chamber. I see not darkness, but a beautiful tapestry of temperature. The great condenser banks are cool, deep blues and purples against the hot orange glow of the rock near the geothermal vents. The lichen colonies on the walls are gentle, steady greens, their exudations rising as shimmering, barely-visible vapors in the thick air. The electrostatic precipitators hum, a tone I feel in my thoracic cavity, their grids charged to a precise potential, pulling the precious spore-dust from the atmosphere.

I move. My body is a tool of elegant efficiency. I leap a gap between gantries, my attenuated form slicing through the steam-wreathed air, landing with a soft click on the next platform. I do not need to look; my infrared sight has plotted the trajectory, accounting for the sway of the walkway. I am performing my rounds. Check the pressure gauge on Vat Three. The brass face is fogged with condensation. With a finger, I wipe a clear arc. The needle trembles at 2.9. Acceptable. I trace a quick pattern in the moisture on the gauge’s glass—a sigil of confirmation—and project a brief, warm pulse from my palm gland toward the Keeper on the far side of the vat. She receives it, her own gland flashing a subtle amber acknowledgement in my thermal sight.

This is language. This is community. The silent exchange of data, the mutual assurance of function. We are nodes in a network, flesh-and-blood instruments regulating the great, breathing engine of the Cistern.

But then.

There is a discordance.

It begins as a sound. Not the *clunk* of a valve, not the *hiss* of steam, not the eternal *plink* of water droplets. This is a… a *scratching*. A dry, brittle sound, like stone on stone, but wrong. It comes from the eastern wall, near the base of the primary lichen colony, where the condensation runoff is channeled into a copper collection pipe.

I tilt my head. My auditory receptors, tuned to the frequencies of machinery, strain to parse the anomaly. The other Keepers in my vicinity show no reaction. Their thermal profiles remain steady, focused. The sound is either outside their perceptual range, or they filter it as irrelevant noise. But I cannot filter it. It lodges in my consciousness like a grit in a bearing.

I descend. My four-jointed limbs find purchase on the rough basalt, my coolant-weeping skin leaving faint, evaporating trails. The scratching grows louder. It has a rhythm, but not the rhythm of the Cycle. This is arrhythmic. Stuttering. *Alive* in a way nothing here is alive, except us and the fungi.

I reach the floor, a cramped space of dripping stone and warm puddles. The sound emanates from a crevice where the cavern wall meets the hewn platform. It is a crack, perhaps no wider than my hand, dark and seemingly shallow. But as I watch, a piece of the darkness *detaches*.

It is small, no larger than the first joint of my finger. It is many-legged, its carapace a dull, non-reflective black that absorbs my infrared gaze, making it a chilling, absolute zero shape in the thermal tapestry. It scrabbles at the rock, its limbs moving with a frantic, desperate energy. It is Fauna. Parameter 0.1. An impossibility.

My training, my very neurology, screams to correct the anomaly. An unknown variable. A contaminant. It could damage the lichen. It could clog a vent. The protocol is clear: isolate, neutralize, return to the Cycle. My hand extends, ceramic fingers poised to pinch the creature into oblivion.

But I pause.

I have never paused before. Pausing is not efficiency. Pausing is a drop in pressure, a misalignment.

The creature stops scratching. It turns. It has no eyes I can see, but it seems to sense me. It does not flee. It raises its front limbs, and in a motion shockingly deliberate, it taps them against the stone.

*Tap-tap-tap.*
*Tap.*
*Tap-tap.*

It is not random. It is a pattern. A code.

My mind, a system designed to interpret the pulses of engines and the gradients of heat, struggles. This is not data for the Cycle. This is… something else. A message. A message from the 0.1.

I look from the creature to the crack from which it emerged. My infrared sight cannot penetrate the deep cold of that narrow gap. But for the first time in my existence, I feel a pull that is not toward a gauge or a valve. I feel a pull toward the *unknown*.

What lies beyond the crack? Not more Cistern. The maps in my mind—the maps of pressure lines and condensate flows—end at the basalt walls. This crack is a breach in the known world.

The creature taps again, the same sequence. An invitation? A warning?

I know what I must do. I must report this. A single, focused thermal pulse to the Foreman Keeper will bring the collective attention. The anomaly will be catalogued, the crack sealed, the creature dissolved into base components for the fungal slurry.

My palm gland warms, ready to emit.

But I do not.

I, the Keeper designated for Sector Seven-Gamma, make a choice. I lower my hand. With a single, careful movement, I scoop the creature into my cupped palm. Its legs tickle against my coolant-slick integument. It is light. Fragile. A spark of wrongness in a world of perfect, humming rightness.

I look at the crack. I am built to maintain, not to explore. But the scratch in the stone has become a scratch in my mind. The flawless cycle of the Cistern has, for me, developed a tiny, persistent leak.

I am a Keeper of the Cycle.

And I am about to break it.
