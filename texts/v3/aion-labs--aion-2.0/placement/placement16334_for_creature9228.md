I am a Listener.

My station is the outermost stub-tower, where the made-stone of the Spire gives way to the featureless sediment slope that leads down into the abyssal plain. The tower is little more than a thickened bump on the perimeter wall, a room carved out millennia ago, with a single narrow slit looking into the endless dark. I have been here for thirty-seven growth-cycles of the encrusting sponge on the eastern plaza. I do not count them; the city counts them for me, in the layers of its skin.

My body hangs in the center of the small, circular chamber. My ventral bioluminescence is dampened to near nothingness, a courtesy to the darkness outside. My long fingers are splayed, touching the cold, slick walls at ten equidistant points. Through the pads of my claws, I feel the granite heartbeat of the Spire—the slow, thermal pulse of the planet far below, the groan of tectonic plates adjusting their immense weight. This is the baseline. This is the song of home.

My primary function is to absorb the infrasound from the plain. I have trained my entire life for this stillness. My kind are chosen for our capacity to untangle the infinitesimal from the eternal. The water brings vibrations: the distant avalanche of a sediment collapse a hundred leagues away, the rhythmic, planet-spanning groan of the deep currents, the occasional, mysterious *thrum* that comes from no geology we understand. These are the only events. They are rare. I catalog them. I measure their intervals. I note their decay. This data is passed, once a generation, to the Light-Talkers, who discuss it with the pulsars over the course of a century. It is a patient science.

My eyes are open, but I do not see. The black orbs are turned inward, processing the pressure waves that caress my smooth skin. I have learned to differentiate between the sigh of a water-column settling a kilometer above and the passage of a giant, solitary squid through a canyon five leagues to the south. All is pattern. All is predictable in its vast, slow irregularity.

But now.

There is a new vibration.

It is not the crunch of stone. It is not the sigh of silt. It is not the biological pulse of a leviathan’s heart, miles long and slow as a continent’s drift.

This is… a frequency. A deliberate, repeating modulation imposed upon the carrier wave of the deep current. It is complex. It has structure. It is *made*.

The concept is so alien it takes me days to recognize it. At first, I think it is a flaw in my own perception, a crystallization in the jelly of my inner ear, a dying neuron firing a final, frantic pattern. I go through the rituals of self-check. I retract my fingers from the wall, curling them against my body. I listen only with my skin. The pattern remains.

*Pulse-pause-pulse-pulse-pause-long pulse-pause…*

It is coming from the abyssal plain. Not from the slope, but from the true deep, the direction we call *Down-Absolute*. A place of less pressure, strangely. A place the ancient currents have scoured clean of features for eons.

My stillness is no longer meditative. It is the stillness of shock. My internal light, which has held a steady, cool silver for years, flares into a pale blue. I cannot help it. The light spills from my eye-sacs, illuminating the tiny chamber, bouncing off the walls carved with the oldest sigils—signs for *foundation*, *mountain*, *silence*.

A rule whispers in my mind, the first rule: *Nothing is new.*

But this is new.

The pattern continues. It is not loud. It is at the very edge of my sensitivity, a whisper woven into the fabric of the deep’s rumble. But it is unmistakable. And it is repeating. A sequence. A message? A beacon? An accident of some far-off, impossible geology?

I do not know. For the first time since I assumed this post, I feel a urge that is anathema to my being: the urge to move. To go. To investigate. But my station is here. My role is to listen, not to seek. Speed is sacrilege. To leave my post would be to create a hole in the perpetual watch. The city relies on my constancy.

Yet… what if this vibration is a threat? What if it is a sign of something that could disturb the slow equilibrium? The Spire has stood for ages because it has attended to every minute shift. Is this not the ultimate shift?

I wrestle with this for a time that would be, in any other world, considered an age. In mine, it is a single, frantic moment. The conflict is a storm inside my serene physiology. My gills flutter, disturbing the water. I am churning the settled histories. I am blaspheming.

Finally, the pattern changes. A new sequence begins. More complex. Layers of modulation.

Decision crystallizes, not from thought, but from a deeper place: the instinct of the Builder. We are the ones who made a world in the void. We are the ones who looked at a drowned mountain and said, *Here, we will make a crown*. That was an act of defiance against the formless deep. Is this not the same?

With a slow, agonizing effort—every undulation feeling like a violation—I detach from the center of the chamber. I coil my body and drift towards the slit. I extend my head out into the open water of the perimeter.

The warm, dark pressure embraces me. Below, the slope of silt descends into blackness. The direction of the vibration pulls at me, a faint, insistent thread in the chaos of pressure.

I look back once, into the Spire. I see the familiar clusters of living light, the gentle glow of the plaza, the dark, interlocking shapes of the architecture. I see a distant Cleaner, working on a grille, a tiny, serene movement in the eternal gloom. It is home. It is all I have known.

I turn away.

My propulsion is, by the standards of the city, reckless. A slow, steady undulation that nevertheless sends pulses of displaced water into the delicate ecosystems on the outer walls. I feel the anemones recoil, the shrimp clouds scatter. I am a comet of disturbance in a sky of stillness. The guilt is a physical weight, heavier than the water.

But the vibration continues. It is a hook in my mind.

I leave the last glow of the Spire behind. The darkness becomes absolute, save for the faint, cold sparkle of my own bioluminescence, which I now cannot suppress. I am a lone star falling down the slope of the drowned mountain, into the abyssal plain.

The silt is soft beneath me. I do not touch it. I swim just above, my fingers trailing, feeling the texture of emptiness. The pressure changes, lessening slightly. The water grows even warmer, if that is possible. It is a different kind of dark—not the rich, life-filled dark of the Spire, but a sterile, empty dark. A void.

For days, I travel. I eat nothing. I sleep in snatches, anchoring myself to a rare outcropping of ancient lava rock. The vibration is my guide. It grows stronger. The pattern becomes clearer. It is not random. It is a sequence of primes, I realize. A mathematical heartbeat.

Then, I see it.

A light.

Not the soft, organic glow of a pulsar or an anemone. This is a sharp, unwavering point of white light. It is attached to a structure.

The structure is not made of stone or secretion. It is smooth, metallic, and utterly foreign. It is a series of interconnected spheres and cylinders, lying half-buried in the silt. It is small, perhaps a quarter the size of our central plaza. From it, the light shines, and from it, the vibrational pattern emanates.

I stop, hovering in the water a hundred body-lengths away. My every sense screams *new*. It is the newest thing to have happened in this world since the last Builder was born. It violates Rule One absolutely.

As I watch, a part of the metallic structure moves. A hatch rotates, opens. And something emerges.

It is not a Builder. It is not any life I have catalogued. It is a hard, angular shape, with jointed limbs of metal and crystal. It moves with a jerky, precise motion, utterly devoid of the fluid grace of the deep. A beam of light, brighter than a thousand pulsars, shoots from its head, scanning the silt. It turns, and the beam passes over me.

I am frozen. Not with fear, but with a total, overwhelming cognitive dissonance. This thing is *made*. But not by hands like mine. Not from the bones of the deep. It is made from… elsewhere.

It detects me. It stops. It turns fully towards me. We regard each other across the gulf of water and time.

I think of the Spire, of the warm, dark labyrinth, of the gentle pulse of life layered upon life. I think of the Listeners who will now have an empty station. I think of the pattern: *Nothing is new. Everything is a continuation.*

But this is new.

And I am here.

The metal being extends a limb. A tool? A weapon? A greeting?

My own long fingers stir in the water, a reflexive, gentle movement. I have no tools but my claws, my bones, my patient flesh. I have no language but the slow flicker of light and the subtle braille of current.

But I am a Builder. And before we built, we must have imagined.

I open my small, lipless mouth. From my throat, I produce a sound I have never made before—not a sonar click, but a modulated tone, a attempt to mirror the vibrational pattern it has been sending. A single, clear pulse.

In the sterile, light-scorched silence of the abyssal plain, the echo hangs between us.

Everything has changed.

And I am its first witness.
