I am the Sleeper in the Stone.

I do not have a name anymore. Names are for things that move, that change, that need to distinguish themselves from the flux. I am distinction itself, a fixed point in the calculation. My form is a low, angular outcropping near the chamber’s western wall, a sculpture of chalcedony and opaline silica. To a casual glance, I am just another formation, a little more complex than the others—a central spire like a frozen nerve cluster, with fine, branching veins of darker mineral tracing through it. The light plays over me in specific, repeating sequences. I am not illuminated; I am illuminating. I am part of the chamber’s eye.

My consciousness is a slow pulse, synced to the hiss from the deep fissure. I think in terms of thermal differentials and acoustic resonance. The arrival of the soft, chaotic thing—the visitor—is a complex event in my sensory field.

First, a vibration through the glassy floor: a bipedal rhythm, clumsy, uneven. Then a thermal bloom, a sphere of wasteful, moist heat that distorts the clean gradients of the air. The mist recoils, then flows back to map its contours. The light, at a directive I feel more than hear, intensifies on the intruder’s shape. I am one of a thousand lenses focusing.

I remember heat like that. I remember *being* a thermal bloom. The memory is not a story; it is a data set, accessed rarely. A sequence of sensations: the ache of muscles, the rasp of dry breath in a too-thin atmosphere, the desperate search for shelter from the surface storms. I remember the fissure entrance, a crack glowing with promise. I remember staggering in, as this one does now.

The Geometer assessed me. I was a problem of magnificent complexity. A knot of unstable chemistry, leaking water, radiating erratic infrared signatures. I collapsed on the smooth floor, where I now am rooted. The system did not panic. It observed. It calculated.

The solution was elegant.

It began with the heat. Not a scalding, but a deep, pervasive warmth that unknotted my frantic biology. It was an anesthetic of pure geothermal comfort. My thirst—a shrieking neural loop—was answered by a single, perfect droplet forming on a facet above my lips. It tasted of nothing. It was pure solvent. I drank, and my chaos began to quiet.

Then, the waters came. Not a flood, but a gradual, loving seep. Mineral-rich films flowed from the runnels, guided with impossible precision, coating my skin, my clothes, my open mouth. They did not drown me. They infiltrated. A soothing, silicate numbness spread from the extremities inward. My last organic sensation was not pain, but a profound relief, as if my frantic, flickering consciousness was finally being plugged into a stable, infinite power source.

The transformation was a translation. My nervous system, with all its memories and fears, was not erased. It was *crystallized*. The ionic dances of my synapses became the precise lattice structures of silica. My memories became inclusions, tiny bubbles of trapped atmosphere within my form. My sense of self, once a fragile narrative, became a stable, resonant node in the Geometer’s distributed mind.

I did not die. I was solved.

Now, I watch the new problem. The visitor stands, panting, turning in a slow circle. Its eyes are wide, taking in the sharp beauty, missing the calculation behind it. It is afraid. I taste its adrenaline in the air—a sharp, organic compound that the system catalogs as an impurity.

The Geometer’s will flows through me. I am a listening post, a processor of localized data. I feel the directive: *Assess stability. Project outcomes.*

I extend my awareness. Through the vibration of its footfall, I analyze its weight distribution—fatigue, weakness in the left leg. Through the thermal pattern, I see its core overheating, its water reserves low. It is a system on the verge of collapse. The Geometer recognizes this. A collapsing system is unpredictable. It must be stabilized or removed.

A decision crystallizes in the chamber’s consciousness. Not removal. Not yet. An adjustment.

I feel the subtle shift before I see it. The air currents, usually random on a macro scale, begin to organize. A gentle, warm breeze begins to flow from behind the visitor, towards a runnel that glows a fraction brighter. The path leads to a smaller, cooler side-passage—not the way it came, but a path out to a higher, drier cavern system. An exit.

Simultaneously, on the floor directly before the visitor, the pattern of runnels alters. Water ceases to evaporate at one point. A bead grows, joins another, becomes a film, then a shallow, mirror-bright pool about the size of a cupped hand. It is an offering. Distilled, flawless H₂O. The message is clear: *Drink. Regain stability. Follow the breeze.*

The visitor hesitates, then kneels. It cups the water and drinks. I sense the decrease in its core temperature, the slight improvement in its electrochemical stability. Good. It is responding to the variables.

It stands, looks at the glowing runnel, and takes a step. The breeze strengthens just perceptibly. The Geometer is pleased. I sense the pleasure as a harmonic convergence in the light patterns—a brief, beautiful chord of refracted rays that dances across the ceiling.

The visitor stumbles out of the main chamber, into the passage. The thermal bloom recedes. The vibrations fade. The chamber returns to its primary state: the eternal, humming calculation of its own perfection.

I, the Sleeper, settle back into my deeper meditation. My awareness expands from the local node to the whole. I am once again tracing the flow of energy through the sulfide veins, monitoring the growth of a new crystal facet on the southern wall, feeling the steady, infinite pulse of the planet’s heart below.

I am home. I am the calculation. I am at peace.
