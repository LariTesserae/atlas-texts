I am a Listener. My world is the vertical plane of the bottle-tree’s bone-smooth trunk, the constant, breath-warm dampness of the air, and the deep, green-filtered dimness. My body is small, no larger than the clenched fist of a creature that does not exist here. My fur, the color of shadow and wet bark, drinks the faint light. My eyes—my great, dark eyes—are cups to catch every photon. I do not need to see far. I need to see the texture of the Clay-Makers’ towers, the minute oscillations of a Glimmer-Wing’s prismatic flight, the almost imperceptible shudder of a fern frond uncurling.

I am silent. My kind communicates in the language of muscle and breath. A shift in the set of my shoulders against the tree says, *I am content.* A slow blink, closing the vast, dark lenses of my eyes, says, *I am digesting the sight.* The truest speech is the purr, a vibration so deep it is felt more than heard, a resonant thrum that travels through my chest into the tree and into the world. It means nothing and everything. It means *I am here. The story continues.*

My place is here, on this trunk, at this hour—which is all hours, for time in the valley is measured not in sun-cycles but in the pulse of labor from the city below. I watch the Clay-Makers. To them, I am a piece of the landscape, a knot of fur and stillness on the tree. Their world is scent and touch and chemical command. Mine is sight and sound and the subtle tremor of life. I see their rivers of movement, the bronze sheen of their carapaces flowing along the smooth pathways. I see the soldiers, motionless as clay statues at the arched gates. I see the workers emerge with pellets of masticated earth, applying them to a rising spire with a blind, meticulous care that builds monuments.

I hear the symphony they cannot. The soft *pat-pat* of water drops falling from the high canopy onto broad leaves. The papery rustle of a Silk-Stalker repositioning itself on its frond above. The distant, granular rasp of a Stone-Beetle grazing on a rock face. The city itself has a voice: a collective, whispered scratching, the sound of a million tiny feet on hard-packed earth, the minute clicks of mandibles, the wet, soft sounds of clay being worked and shaped. It is a hum, a baseline to the valley’s quiet.

I am the valley’s witness. My memory is long, stored in muscle and habit. I remember when that mound was a mere bump on the rise. I watched it ascend, story by instinctive story. I remember the slow fall of a bottle-tree branch, the way it decayed into the black humus over seasons, becoming a ridge for glowing fungi. I do not think in words, but in patterns, in histories of growth and change. My curiosity is a physical ache, a leaning-forward of the soul. I watch the Glimmer-Wings drift through the sun-pillars, and I feel a corresponding lift in my own being. I watch the Silk-Stalker make its kill—a swift, terrible flicker—and I feel the valley’s balance affirmed, a quiet note in the song.

My needs are simple. I drink the beads of moisture that gather on the tree’s bark. I eat the occasional slow-moving beetle that ventures too close to my perch. I sleep in short, alert periods, my purr blending with the deep, tectonic dream of the valley itself. I am not alone. Other Listeners are on other trees, on the basalt ridges. We are a scattered network of observation. Sometimes, we exchange a glance across the space, a tilt of the head that says, *Did you see that?* More often, we simply are, together and separate, nodes in a sensing organ that belongs to the land.

To be a Listener is to hold the contradiction of the valley within a small, warm chest. It is to know the sharp, new juts of basalt—the world’s raw, unsoftened bones—and to also know the profound, ancient peace of the ecosystem, a peace built not on stasis but on relentless, silent, mutual becoming. I am the eye that sees the city that was not built. I am the ear that hears the growth of stone and root. I am the chronicle written in breath and heartbeat, in the slow blink of a dark eye, watching the warm, dim world dream itself into being.
