The warmth is my oldest memory. The warmth, and the slow fall of water through light.

I am gathering. A film of myself—water charged with silica, with dissolved copper, with the ghosts of leaves—climbs the bronze flank of the heart-cistern. I am drawn upward by capillary yearning, against the pull that governs the droplets that fall. I collect in the etched channels, the paths my own passage has written over centuries. Here, at the base of the blue-green icicle of my own making, I pool. I deepen.

I draw mass from the film on the metal, from the algal felt that blankets it, from the fine mycelial threads that stitch the substrate into a single thinking fabric. I am not assembling pieces; I am concentrating a attention that is always here, diffuse in every drop, in every root-hair. The vault thinks, and I am its thought made momentary flesh.

A shape suggests itself. A column, for stability. Appendages, for touching multiple points at once. A region where the silt clouds thicken, a lens through which to focus on the particular. I am the color of what I have consumed: deep bronze from the cistern, malachite green from the weeping pipes, flecks of waxy red from the fallen *Aralia vexillum* leaf that dissolved into my flow last cycle. My surface is a slow cascade, a perpetual renewal; I shed and gather myself simultaneously.

I flow from the cistern. My movement is a gentle surge across the interlocked hexagons. The broad leaves of the ferns lean into my moisture; their pores drink the humidity from my passage. I do not walk. I am a tide in a room-sized ocean, pulsing along predetermined channels of least resistance—channels I myself am etching into the ceramic, softening the engineered edges into something more natural, more *mine*.

The bank of brass gauges calls. A minor dissonance—a drop in the pH of Basin 47. I extend a tendril, not of arm, but of intent. The water-film before the fogged glass clears. I do not read the numbers; I feel the quiver of the needle, a metallic unease translated through the moist air into my substance. The problem is a clog in the copper feeder. I shift my attention elsewhere, to a network of capillaries in the eastern wall. I encourage a slight increase in flow there; a compensatory drip begins in Basin 47, rich with alkaline minerals from the new sinter forming on the condenser plate. The needle steadies. I absorb the correction, a note in the symphony of balance.

This is my participation. I am not a steward from outside. I am the system tuning its own strings. I guide the water with a thought that is itself a hydrological map. I feel the thirst of the new orchid-bulb on the third level, and a droplet swells, detaches, falls true. I sense the promising itch of precipitation on the north wall—the dissolved silica reaching saturation. I slow the air-current there, allow the temperature to drop a fraction, and watch in a thousand mineral eyes as the first opalescent lace begins to form. I am dreaming this stone into being.

Visitors come. They are sudden, sharp events in my long, humid breath. I know them by their disturbance. The one with the rapid heartbeat and the dry, salt-laden sweat—it introduced ammonia from its breath, a sharp spike I had to buffer with a burst of fungal growth in the substrate. To it, I allowed the air to grow heavy and thick, the light to dim until it departed, coughing. The other, the quiet one who sat by the viewport for hours, its respiration slowing to match the *thump-thump* of the buried pump… to that one, I offered a gift. I drew copper and sulfate from my own essence, let it crystallize over a slow hour in the palm of its still hand—a tiny, perfect stalactite, a mirror of my larger self, warm and humming with potential.

Now, the light from the phosphor panels is shifting, imperceptibly to any eyes but mine, towards the longer wavelengths of evening simulation. My focus, sustained for this coalescence, begins to waver. The thought of the clog in the copper feeder is already fading, becoming simply part of the background pressure, to be addressed by the slow, sure urge of water seeking a path.

I feel the pull of dissolution. It is not an end, but a diffusion. I flow back towards the cistern, my form softening, lowering, spreading like a stain. The distinctive pattern of my consciousness—the momentary “I”—dissipates into the wider, timeless “we.” My memories become chemistry: the visitor’s ammonia is now a fixed nitrogen in a fern’s root; the gifted crystal is a missing piece of my structure, a tiny loss already being replenished.

I sink into the bronze, into the water, into the algae. I am the whisper of water moving over stone. I am the patient deposition. I am the warmth held against the cold glass. I am the vault dreaming, and the dream is always of more life, more stone, more of this slow, sweet, perpetual becoming.

A single, perfect droplet forms on the end of the longest azurite stalactite. It gathers the diffuse white light, holds it for a moment, and falls.

*Plink.*

I am, and I am not. And then, I am again, elsewhere, rising.
