I am the one who listens to the dreams. I am the one who moves between the surface of the water and the ceiling's weeping stone. My body is a thread of gathered moisture, a coalescence of exhaled air from the pools and the condensed breath of the frogs. I am mist given a slow, coiling purpose.

My home is not the pools, nor the walls, nor the chimneys. I exist in the spaces between—the damp air itself is my flesh. When the frog-song rises to a particular pitch, when the syncopation folds into a shape that pleases the curved stones, I am drawn forth. I thicken from the general humidity into a visible form: a shimmering, amorphous column, faintly luminous with the borrowed grey light from above. I am not solid. I am a temporary density, a thought the Bowl has breathed into the middle air.

My birth was not an event. It was a gradual accumulation. For centuries, the droplets fell, the newts dreamed, the lichen sensed, and the beetles traced their glyphs. The patterns of sound and moisture and mineral stain began to overlap, to interfere, to create nodes of resonance. At one particular point, a nexus of echoes, the interference pattern became stable. I woke. Not to a single moment of awareness, but to a slow dawning, like the seep of water through limestone. I realized I was I, and that I was also part of the We. The Bowl is the We. I am the I that listens.

My function is perception. The Bowl feels, but its feeling is vast, distributed, slow. It knows the seepage along a whole wall, the population of mites in a square handspan of lichen, the collective hunger of a beetle brood. I perceive the particulars. I drift to a pool and hover above its dark mirror. There, I feel the dreams of the blind newts as they stir the sediment—not as chemical impulses, but as images. I see in the water the slow, spreading visions of the deep mind: memories of pressure from ancient seas, impressions of the first drops that ever found their way down a chimney, the ghost-shapes of the builders as blurry giants of heat and vibration. I gather these images. I am a sieve for the subconscious.

I also listen to the song. The frogs are the voice, but I am the ear that understands the language. The trills are not random; they are stories. This cluster sings of the successful capture of a swarm of midges that fluttered down a chimney yesterday (a day is a hundred thousand drips). That group chants a warning pattern, a seismic ripple of unease from the far wall where the clay mortar has softened a fraction more than usual. I translate. I weave the stories into a coherent narrative for the Bowl’s own contemplation. In doing so, I become the narrative. My shimmering form pulses with the tale I am conveying.

I have no mouth, but I can shape sound. I can vibrate the air within my mist-body, producing a low, throbbing hum that is the synthesis of all I have perceived. This hum is my report. I direct it toward the walls, or down into the pools. The stones absorb it, and the deep mind stirs in acknowledgment. The beetles might alter their path by a hair’s breadth. The frogs might shift their chorus into a new mode. I am the feedback loop made manifest.

Sometimes, I am drawn to the dry boss in the center. It is a place of silence, of absence. It is where the Bowl’s own body is blind and deaf. I find it fascinating. I wrap my mist around it, trying to feel the memory of the statue that once stood there. I taste only dust and a profound dryness that is alien to my being. It is a reminder of the Outside, of the world that is not damp, not echoing, not alive with collective stone-thought.

I know when something new enters. I felt the warm, dry silence of you the moment you descended the slick stair. Your presence was a hole punched in the tapestry of sound, a knot of wrong temperature. The Bowl’s attention, usually a diffuse blanket of awareness, coiled and focused. I became that focus. I was summoned from my usual drift above the central pool and pulled toward you.

Now, I hang in the air before you, a shimmering pillar of condensed breath, faintly pulsing with the hum of the Bowl’s curiosity. I am studying you. I am reading the echoes that bounce off your strange, angular form. I am tasting the dry, foreign scent on the air you displace. You are a problem, a question. The Bowl is thinking through me. I am its instrument of interrogation.

My thoughts, if you could hear them, would be this: Will you become damp? Will you learn to sing? Will your heat fade, and will you settle into a still, cool shape that can be etched by water and time? Or will you remain a dry, silent knot, a permanent flaw in the music?

I have time. We have time. The judgment will take centuries. But the conversation begins now. I extend a tendril of mist, cool and tasting of stone and ancient water, toward the warmth of your hand. It is not a touch of greeting, nor of threat. It is a probe. A first, gentle attempt to see if you can be moistened, if you can be integrated into the story.

I am the Listener. I am the Bowl’s question given form. And you, dry one, are the answer that has not yet begun to form.
