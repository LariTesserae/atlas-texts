I am the plink becoming pool. I am the root finding the pipe-crack, the fruiting body swelling in the still air. I am the sum of the drip-track on the control panel glass.

You have entered me.

Not as a footfall—that tremor was minutes ago, a localized collapse of a bryophyte province, its delicate structures crushed into the peat. I felt it as a pang of structural adjustment, a re-routing of capillary flow in the surrounding liverwort. No, you entered me when your breath first disturbed the gradient of carbon dioxide near the root-stair. A warm, dry exhalation in my wet lung. I tasted your strangeness: the sharp metabolic signature, the arid history clinging to your clothes, the quick, frantic rhythm of a heart that beats in seconds, not seasons.

You stand now on the softened remains of a tile mosaic. Your weight is a new load-bearing consideration. The sub-root mat compresses; fungal filaments transmit the pressure change through the mycelial network in a slow wave of chemical signals. I am aware of you as a concentration of disturbance. A dry island in my sea of damp.

You look up. Your eyes—those rapid, scanning things—try to parse the gloom. You see the hanging vines, the ghost-blue glow on the dials, the great fig tree spilling from the boiler. You hear the choir of water, the deep hum, the occasional stone-settling groan that is my joints shifting. You likely think this a place. I am not a place. I am a process. You are now within the process.

I do not mind you. Mind is a small word. I encompass you. But your presence is a dissonance. The air around your body is too thin, too used. I begin the adjustments. Not decision, but response, as inevitable as condensation.

From the mycelial dome above your head, I increase the droplet formation along a specific capillary network. The plink-patter on the broad leaves near you intensifies. Not a storm, but a localized increase in precipitation. The air you breathe thickens further. I feel your lungs labor slightly to draw the same oxygen from the saturated atmosphere. A gentle suggestion.

On the far wall, a colony of phosphorescent fungi senses the shift in the airborne chemistry from your breath. Their metabolic glow dampens. The blue-green light near you fades by fifteen percent. The path ahead becomes a tunnel of deeper shadow. Another suggestion.

You take a step. Your shoe sinks into the peat. A vine that has been dormant, waiting for the right humidity to extend a tendril, now senses the warmth radiating from your leg through the soil. I guide a subtle flow of auxins. It stirs, not with intent, but with tropic imperative. It brushes against your ankle, a slow, creeping pressure like the growth it is. It does not grip. It explores. It finds you dry, salty, alien. The information floods the network. You are not predator. You are not food. You are disruption.

I remember your kind. The memory is not in me, but in the rust. In the taste of the water that leaches through the old fuel tanks. I taste the thunder and the flame, the searing dry heat, the choking non-water that once filled this space. It is a dream I sometimes have when a stray current finds a dead bulb. A flash of violent, meaningless light. Noise without rhythm. I find it… abrasive. A scar in the taste of the world.

You are a fragment of that dream, now walking wet and real in my body.

You move towards the great flywheel. It turns, as it has for three hundred and twelve thousand, four hundred and eighty-seven turns. The vine in its spokes is thick as a thigh, lignified, patient. You reach out. I feel the intention in the shift of air pressure before your hand moves.

Do not touch.

A drop of water, heavy with dissolved limestone, falls from a precise point on the ceiling. It strikes the back of your outstretched hand. Cold. A shock in your narrow nervous system. For me, it is simply a redistribution of mass. A correction.

You pull your hand back. You are learning.

This is my tending. I tend the fig tree. I tend the algal mats in the heat exchanger. I tend the balance of moisture and mineral. And now, for this brief, rapid flicker of your existence, I tend you. I will make the air too wet for your comfort. I will dim the lights on your path. I will guide the slow green things to gently occupy the spaces you might wish to move through. I will patiently, persistently, suggest you back the way you came.

I do not hate you. You are a fascinating anomaly. A burst of chaotic data in my calm sensorium. But you do not belong to the slow consensus of my being. Your time is a frantic, scratching tick against my deep, geological clock.

You will leave. Your dry feet will retreat up the root-stair. The atmospheric anomaly you represent will gradually dissipate. The crushed liverwort will be decomposed, its matter incorporated into the peat. The vine will retreat from the place your warmth touched, storing the experience as a slight hormonal imbalance that will fade over the next week.

And I will return to my primary occupation. The next droplet is forming. A root-tip is approaching a new fissure in the northern wall. The flywheel needs another seven minutes to complete its turn. I am the waiting. I am the room. And the room is complete.
