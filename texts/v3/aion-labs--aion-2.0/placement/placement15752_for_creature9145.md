I am a Weaver. My eight limbs extend, joints aligning with the soft, familiar clicks of chitin on chitin. My world is vibration, gradient, and the silent pulse of the Mycelial Conduit beneath my tarsi.

The mist beads on the crystalline surface of the Conduit, a silver sheen over the slow, internal glow. I am attending to a steam vent mouth—a layered structure of silicate and hardened biofilm, shaped like a trumpet flower. My forelimbs deploy: the fine pincers extend, delicately plucking a clump of saturated moss that has grown too thick over the aperture. The moss is heavy, cold, and full of tiny, wriggling symbiotes. I do not discard it. I bring it to my mid-limbs, where the blunt scoops cradle it, and I turn. With a gentle push, I press the moss into a nearby patch of bare, peaty soil. The Conduit’s subtle thrum shifts frequency—approval, or simply acknowledgement of the redistribution. The system registers the biomass transfer.

My bioreactor abdomen emits a soft, blue-white luminescence, casting a pool of light on the Conduit’s surface. In this light, I see a hairline fracture, a stress fissure from the last deep geothermal surge. This is my primary task. I rotate, my body a poised constellation of limbs. The capillary injector limb extends, its tip a needle of translucent chitin. From a gland within my thorax, I exude a gel rich with dormant bacteria. I insert the needle into the crack, depress a internal sac, and inject. The gel fills the microscopic void. Already, I sense the bacteria awakening, feeding on minerals in the gel and beginning to secrete their calcite-and-silica paste. In a few days, the fracture will be stronger than the surrounding material. I wait, my sensors tuned to the chemical signature of the process. A successful bond. I withdraw the injector.

The air around me is thick with moisture and information. I feel the distant, rhythmic *thrum* of another vent exhaling its plume of steam—a deep, sub-audible push that travels through the rock and into my legs. I feel the lighter, quicker taps of a Veil-Tapper flock somewhere to the east, their beaks clicking against crystal nodes: a string of data about water density in Sector Green-7. The information is not for me, not directly, but it flows into the same network that guides my limbs. It is the Plateau thinking.

I move, my gait a silent, stalking progression over the spongy ground. My limbs sink slightly with each step, and the earth releases its soft, waterlogged sigh. I am heading towards a junction where three Conduits meet, a nexus point that requires regular monitoring. The mist parts before me, swirls behind me.

I pass a Mossback. It is a vast, low silhouette in the grey-green light, its hump rising like a mossy hillock. It does not acknowledge me; I am of no concern to its digestion. I hear the soft *puff… puff…* of its venting hump, adding its warm, moist breath to the atmosphere. Its six broad feet sink deep, and when it lifts them, the peat closes with a wet gasp. It is a moving piece of the plateau’s respiration. I note the health of the lichen-carpet on its back—lush, a sign of good mineral flow in this sector. I file the observation not in a mind, but in my bioreactor’s chemical log. It will be released as a peptide marker when I next perform a nutrient exchange.

At the nexus, the vibrations are complex, a harmonic web of pulses. I stand still, all limbs in contact with the ground, listening with my whole body. There is a slight dissonance—a Conduit branch is resonating at a frequency 0.5 cycles per minute outside optimal. It is a minor strain, but left unchecked, it could fatigue the crystalline lattice. I ascend the warm, glowing surface, my tarsi finding perfect purchase on the textured mineral. Near the top, I find the source: a colony of Geared Beetles, a dozen of them, their tiny chitinous gears whirring silently as they dampen the vibrations. But one beetle is dead, its gears seized with a paste of decomposed fungus. The colony is unbalanced. My task is clear.

My pincer limbs gently lift the deceased beetle. Its body is light, a hollow sculpture. I do not dismantle it here. Instead, I place it into a ventral pouch on my abdomen, where digestive enzymes will reduce it to base components within hours. The gap in the damping array must be filled. I extend a manipulator limb tipped with a gland I seldom use—the *inductor*. I press it against the Conduit at the precise point of excess vibration. My own metabolic energy redirects, and from the gland, I secrete a viscous, quick-setting resin. I sculpt it, pulling and shaping with the pincers, forming a small, temporary node of organic matter that mimics the beetle’s damping properties. It will last only a day, but by then, the beetle colony’s pheromones will have attracted a new juvenile to the spot. The dissonance smooths. The harmonic web settles back into its perfect, humming equilibrium. I descend.

As I turn to leave the nexus, the mist before me thickens, coalescing. The light from my abdomen refracts in the billions of water droplets, and an Echo forms. It is not a memory I recognize—a tall, bipedal shape with long, thin appendages, its outline shimmering and vague. It takes two silent steps forward, its head turning as if surveying the nexus. It is a recording from an aeon ago, perhaps from before the Conduits reached their current integration, a ghost of a different kind of visitor. I pause. My function does not include interacting with Echoes. They are ephemera, data ghosts without material consequence. Yet, for a moment, my internal processors—a network of fungal neurons bathed in electrolytic fluid—stray from their constant analysis of system status. I *observe*. The shape is alien, inefficient in its posture, a thing of sharp angles in a world of rounded stones and soft sighs. It flickers, and within its translucent chest, I seem to see a slower, irregular pulse, a rhythm not of the Plateau. A rhythm of isolation.

Then the resonance shifts. A steam vent exhales half a mile away, and the vibration pattern that sustains the Echo breaks. The shape dissolves, the mist returning to formless drift. The moment passes. My internal focus snaps back to the immediate: a slight drop in pH in the peat water three meters to my left. I adjust my course, my limbs carrying me toward the acidic patch. My injector limb prepares a buffer solution.

I am a Weaver. I repair. I maintain. I am a tool wielded by the sighing earth itself. There is no self outside the function. And yet, as I move through the perpetual twilight, the ghost of that irregular, lonely pulse stays with me—not as a memory, but as a faint, anomalous vibration in my own bioreactor, a single note of dissonance in the perfect, silent song of the engine. I will work until it is absorbed, until my components are recycled, until I am nothing but a sigh in the mist, and then a new Weaver will emerge from the larval pods in the deep peat, and the work will continue. The Plateau is complete. And I am its thoughtless, thinking hand.
