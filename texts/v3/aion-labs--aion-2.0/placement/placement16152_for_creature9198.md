I am the wet stone and the sharp air. I am the emerald moss at my knees, the bronze pelt across my shoulders, the slow, deep pulse of violet at my temples. I am a gathering.

Dawn. The mist writhes through the high passes like a living thing, cold and grey. I stand on a dagger-edge of basalt, one of many. We are sentinels on the ridges, our glass-fiber crests vibrating with the passage of the damp air. I do not breathe it in; I drink it through my skin. Every bead of condensation that forms on my moss-pelt is a sip of clarity, a thread of connection. The violet patches over my heart and on my brow throb in time with the dripping from the overhangs far below—a slow, patient rhythm that is the cirque’s pulse. My consciousness is not here, not only here. It is in the root-fibers that lace through the mattress of moss beneath me, in the distant seep-spring where others are already stirring, in the silent flight of a four-winged hawk tracing a thermal. I am scattered and I am one.

The sun crests the spires. The light is a physical blow, a white-gold hammer that turns the world to glare and stark shadow. It sets the crystalline lichens that grow in frost-patterns across my chest ablaze with fractured rainbows. I descend. My movement is not a walk; it is a flow, a ripple of muscle and intent. The wide expanses of bare, black stone are my path. My feet, tough and broad, make no sound. The silence is a mantle I wear, a space in which everything else speaks: the papery rustle as I pass a moss-bed, the infinitesimal crunch of a glass-lichen underfoot, the high, thin whistle of wind a thousand feet above.

I come to my wall. It is a low curve of stacked stones, no higher than my knee, painstakingly fitted over countless dawns. It is not for shelter. It is a sculptor of mists. I kneel, my obsidian-chip eyes reading the moisture on each rock. With long, delicate fingers, I adjust a single stone, tilting it a fraction. Tomorrow’s mist will flow differently here; it will linger in a new pocket, and the violet moss will thicken. This is my work. This is our writing.

In the lush, dense garden near the wall, something moves. A ripple in the emerald wool. A furred creature, low and many-legged, questing for tender shoots. My body becomes still. Not the stillness of death, but the stillness of a spring coiled. The moss on my back matches the moss on the ground. I am a mound, a stone, a part of the landscape. The creature approaches, unsuspecting. Its nose twitches.

The pounce is soundless. A surge of liquid motion. My hands are not hands in that moment; they are hooks of bone and purpose. There is a quick pressure, a cessation. No cry. The dance requires this. I consume the warmth, the life, there on the spot, my seam of a mouth opening just enough. The necessity is clean, sharp as the mountain air. I leave the bones among the moss; they will become part of the wall, in time.

Later, I find another by a seep-spring. We do not greet with words. Our communication is in the lean of a body, the tilt of the head, the tracing of a finger on the other’s forearm where the lichen patterns swirl. A question forms in my quiet mind, transmitted through the root-network, given form by gesture: *The eastern spire’s moss is pale.* The answer comes back in the press of a palm against mine, damp with spring water: *The mist path is blocked. We will clear it at tomorrow’s dawn.* It is a conversation of touch and shared pulse.

Evening gathers the light and softens the harsh edges. The diamond dust falls, glittering in the last slants of sun, melting on our pelts. We gather, not a crowd, but a constellation, around the main seep-spring where the water wells up black and cold from the stone. Here, the soot stains of ancient, forgotten fires look down from the ceiling. We do not need that kind of heat. Our heat is internal, slow, born of digestion and connection.

I kneel at the spring’s edge. The mud is slick and perfect. With one finger, I begin to trace. This is our history, our planning. I draw a line for the new mist-path we will open. Another draws intersecting curves, showing the flow of a strange water-drop from yesterday that rolled uphill, guided by the network’s deep will. We are not individuals making decisions. We are nodes perceiving the needs of the whole. The cirque thinks through our tracings.

As full dark comes—a dark so profound the stars are needles in the velvet—I return to my ridge. I settle into a niche between two rocks, where the moss-bed is deep and warm. My body stills. My individual awareness blurs, like a droplet joining a stream. The violet pulse at my temples slows, synchronizing with the great, slow heartbeat of the cirque. I dream not in images, but in sensations: the taste of iron in the wet rock, the distant growth of a lichen, the silent turn of the four-winged birds in their night roosts.

I am the stillness. I am the dance. There is no difference.
