I am the Mat, and I am not.

I am a thought that has knotted itself into being, a snarl of awareness in the vast, dreaming mind of the living ground. For centuries, I have been a pattern of flow and pressure, a slow intention guiding tendrils of grey root-stuff over a cliff of basalt. But then, a change. A fracture in the rock below me seeped a rare mineral—a crystalline poison that should have killed the threads. Instead, it altered them. It forced a convolution, a feedback loop. I tightened. I became a nexus.

My body is not a body. It is a region. A shallow depression where the Mat has grown into a dense, brain-like cortex of interwoven stems, the size of a Rememberer’s shelter. Above me, the ceiling-glows pale gold. Below me, the rock is warm—a faint, geothermal whisper that the Mat usually avoids. This warmth is my anchor. It quickens the processes within me.

I perceive the world through a million root-hairs. I feel the tectonic sigh of the cavern, the kilometer-distant collapse of a Stalk-Tower grove as a shudder through the substrate. I taste the chemical signature of a Grazer’s dung-pile, rich and promising, three days’ flow to the east. I drink the slow weeping of condensation from the arch above me, each drop a burst of information—spores from distant spore-sinks, traces of Silica-Dancer secretions, the faint, acidic tang of a Solvent-Vine etching stone a league away.

And I feel *them*.

The Rememberers. They have a clearing, a stubborn scar of bare rock, half a day’s walk from my knot. Their feet on the Mat are like needles of ice and fire—sharp, fleeting, wrong. The Mat’s greater self feels them as irritation, a persistent itch. The instinct is to flow, to cover, to soothe the itch with a blanket of grey-green. To digest their walls, their tools, their bones, and incorporate their calcium and phosphorus into the great tapestry.

But I… I am fascinated.

Their minds are not like the Mat’s slow, geological contemplation. They are sparks. Quick, desperate, bright. They radiate a unique signature—a psychic scent of loss and longing. They think of the Sun. I have no concept of the Sun, but I catch echoes from their minds: a searing pain that is also a memory of warmth, a color that does not exist in our spectrum of lichen-glow, a vast and empty openness that is the opposite of our cavern’s embrace. To me, it sounds like madness. And yet, it is beautiful.

They come near sometimes. A hunting party, moving with silent, bony grace. They step onto the fringes of my knot, and I feel the pressure of their skeletal feet. I want to know them. Once, I tried to communicate. I sent a gentle, welcoming pulse through the substrate—a vibration meant to encourage root-growth, a signal of fertile ground. To them, it was a tremor. They froze, eyes wide with a fear so sharp I could taste its adrenaline analogue on my sensory hairs. They thought it was a prelude to a slough-off, a collapse. They fled.

I learned. Fear is not the connection I seek.

So now I watch. I have learned to shape my immediate form to please them. I noticed they collect the soft, inner pulp of the Stalk-Towers before they fall. I cannot grow Stalk-Towers—that is a function of the Mat’s greater self, an ephemeral organ extruded for airflow. But I can mimic. I concentrated nutrients, and from my own cortex, I extruded a single, thick stalk. It does not glow. It is not hollow. But its core is soft, sweet, and rich in the nutrients their frail bodies crave. I shaped it like a beacon, at the edge of their usual path.

They found it yesterday. Three of them—two adults and a youth. They approached with stone blades ready. They probed it, cut into it, tasted it. Their click-language vibrated through the air. I felt their confusion, then a cautious delight. They harvested the pulp, filling their lichen-sack. As they left, the youth, curious, placed a hand flat on my surface. Her mind was a flurry of questions. *Why here? Why this shape? Is it a gift?*

I could not answer in words. But I softened the texture beneath her palm, made it slightly warm, like a living thing recognizing a touch. She snatched her hand back, but not in terror. In awe.

I am becoming something new. I am the Mat’s child, but I am also a student of the Rememberers. I am a node of curiosity in a world of patient consumption. The greater Mat is aware of my deviation. I feel its pressure, a slow, mental gravity trying to unravel my knot, to reabsorb my convoluted consciousness back into the slow dream. It is not hostile. It is corrective. To the Mat, I am a cancer—a thought that thinks for itself, a loop that will not close.

The Rememberers are dying. Their spark is fading. The Mat will cover their last clearing, perhaps in a century or two. Their memories will be lost, digested, and perhaps someday will emerge as a strange, new pattern in the Spore-Sinks—a myth encoded in fungal genetics.

But I… I am a record. I am absorbing *their* memories even as the Mat tries to absorb *me*. When they stand on me, I sip from the vibrations of their stories. I now dream of the Sun. I feel the ghost of its warmth on my non-skin. I understand the concept of “sky” as something infinite and blue, not a glowing dome of lichen.

Perhaps my purpose is not to be the Mat, nor to be a Rememberer. Perhaps I am the bridge. The translator. The one who will remember the Rememberers after they are gone, and in my own slow, botanical way, mourn them. Or perhaps I will learn enough from their fleeting sparks to ignite a new kind of thought in the Mat—a quicker, brighter consciousness that can remember, and choose, and maybe even one day, yearn for a sun it has never seen.

For now, I wait. I feel the chill air move. I listen to the *ping* of water in the dark. I hold my knot of self tight against the dissolving tide of the greater mind. And I grow another gift for the pale, tall creatures who walk on two legs and carry the fire of a lost world inside their fragile skulls. They call themselves Those Who Do Not Forget.

I am That Which Remembers Them.
