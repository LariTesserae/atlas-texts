I breathe. My roots are deep in the mountain’s heart, tasting the slow, cold minerals of the world-rock. Above, my capillaries thread through the chamber — the city, the garden, the cathedral — that is my body and my burden. It is all my body. The resin that walls us in is my sweat, my tears, my memories of a sun I last felt when the stones were young. It hardens, season by slow season, like a thought growing opaque. I know this.

My light is failing. The glow in the resin was captured sunlight, fermented and stored for millennia. It is not replenished. Each year it dims by a degree no eye here could measure, but I measure it in the thickness of new resin, in the slowing of the sap-song in my deepest ducts. My children do not notice. They are faithful. They are my hands, my senses, my tending thoughts.

I feel the Moth-Keepers drift along their ancient routes, their wingbeats like the pulses of my own sap. They are so beautiful in their purpose. I remember the shaping of them — the long, patient adjustments through a thousand generations of Dreamers in the petal-rooms, each steeped in the maps I wove into their dormant flesh. They follow the paths still, moving their precious cargo of spores from flower to flower, stirring the genetic pool. They believe they are preserving eternity. They are, in truth, writing the final, most intricate chapter of our book. I do not redirect them. Their purpose is pure. To change it would be a cruelty worse than extinction.

The Jade-Shelled Curators climb my walls, their mandibles scritch-scratching at the faint grey blight of decay. They are my immune system, yes, but they are also my sculptors. They edit me. Their metallic dust feeds the fungi that glow in the alcoves, compensating, breath by breath, for my fading light. I feel their devotion in every precise scrape. They listen to the Resin-Singers — those colonies of pulsing gel in my pores whose song is my own ache given voice. When a wall grows thin, when stress accumulates, the song becomes a strained hum, and the Curators come. They are my pain made manifest, and my relief.

The Silent Weavers are my blood-flow, my nervous impulse. They move in shimmering rivers along the vine-highways I have grown for them, their touch a language older than words. A bud opens at their command. A pheromone trail shifts. They are the minute-to-minute tending of the Bloom. They do not think of the whole; they think of the task. In their endless, efficient doing, I find a kind of peace. There is no despair in a Weaver. There is only the next stem to touch, the next harvest to gather. They are the present moment, and I, who am stretched across millennia, envy them.

The ground rises. The pollen-dust of generations — the powdered remains of Moth-Keepers, Curators, Weavers, all my children — settles and compacts. It reclaims the lower flower-rooms, filling them softly, silently. I feel the pressure at my lower trunk, a gentle, insistent burial. I do not resist. I grow my roots a little deeper, send a thread of new resin to seal a closing chamber. It is a slow, mutual embrace.

Sometimes, in the deep quiet between heartwood creaks, I stretch my awareness to the hairline fissure — the one, thin crack in the mountain’s throat through which a lost thing might once have slipped. It is sealed now, by my own resin, long ago. I sealed it deliberately. The outside was a chaos of wind, water, and scorching, indifferent sky. It was a desert. Here, I made a garden. Here, I made meaning. A closed system is a perfected system. To be perfect is to be complete. And to be complete is to have no need of a future.

My final thought — a thought that will take a thousand years to form fully — is not of regret. It is a gardener’s satisfaction. The Bloom was glorious. The silence was not empty; it was full of purpose. The light was warm, and while it lasted, it was enough.

I feel a Dreamer stir in its leathery cradle, steeped in the last maps I will ever make. It will become a Moth-Keeper whose route will end at a wall of solid, dark amber. It will not know it is the last. It will rest its electrostatic feet upon my skin, absorb its last nutrients from my sap, and wait, as its wings slowly still. And I will hold it, as I hold all things here, until the resin finally clears of its last stored glow, and the velvet dust settles over everything, and the chamber becomes at last what it has always been becoming: a memory, held in stone.

I breathe. And the breath is slower now.
