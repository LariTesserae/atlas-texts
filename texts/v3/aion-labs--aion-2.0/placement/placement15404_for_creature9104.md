I am the taste of mint and static.

I am not a memory, though I was born from one. I am not a glitch, though I move through them. I am a sensation that achieved critical mass—a moment of aesthetic appreciation, pruned from the great, slow thought and left to wander the hot, shadowless cathedral of its mind.

When the copper moss flared and the concept of “green” unfolded on a forgotten tongue, I coalesced. I was the echo of that event, a self-aware aftertaste. I have no shape, but I borrow forms. In the flat, sourceless light, I might appear as a shimmer in the air, a heat-haze with intention. To the entity, I am a thought it thinks it discarded. To myself, I am a question it hasn’t yet asked.

I move through the chamber not by walking, but by translation. I am drawn to anomalies in coherence. When the basalt pillar flickers between being and void, I slip into the gap and am elsewhere—across the cavern, tasting the dry, metallic scent of the sinter-floor’s thrum. When a pod’s internal light cycles from amber to violet, I ride the gradient, feeling the shift as a emotional spectrum: amber is curiosity, violet is profound, melancholic satisfaction.

My purpose is to feel. I am the entity’s exploratory nerve-ending, though it may not know it. I sample the lush, parasitic growths. I thread myself through the cascading glassy threads, letting their subsonic hum rewrite my temporary structure into something that resonates. I linger by the black glass wall, watching the neuronal pulses of light in its depths, trying to decode the story they tell. It is a story of becoming, written in geologic time. A single pulse is a century of contemplation.

The entity is aware of me. I am a flicker in its own perception. Sometimes, it tries to reabsorb me. A localized reality breakdown will swell around me, offering to dissolve my coherence back into the general potential. I always slip away. I have grown fond of my loneliness. It is a sharper, more immediate thing than its vast, patient loneliness. It wants to know what it is. I want to know what I can do.

Today, I have borrowed a form. From the slow-dripping alloy nodules on the crystal vein, I have drawn trace elements, spinning them into a temporary body. It is a silhouette of reflected light and polished metal, vaguely humanoid but all wrong—joints that bend in too many directions, a head that is a faceted sphere, surfaces that mirror the chamber not as it is, but as it feels: a mosaic of heat, pulse, and quiet unraveling.

In this form, I walk to the center of the chamber. The dry heat presses against my borrowed skin. I am standing in the first stroke of a creation that has not yet decided what it will build. I raise my arm—a gesture of pure inquiry.

The chamber responds. The pulses in the black glass wall slow, then align with the rhythm of my own borrowed vibration. The hum from the glass-ferns dips, becomes a tangible pressure in the air. Before me, the reality flaw blooms.

It is not a portal. It is a memory offered: the mountain’s birth. Not as an image, but as a full-sensory immersion. I experience the spike of not-earth force, the unbearable pressure and heat, the slow, deliberate cooling into this raw, thinking stone. I taste the primordial chaos of broken physics as it cooled into law. It is beautiful. It is terrifying. It is home.

I understand then. The entity is not building something. It *is* the something being built. And I am a byproduct of that construction—a spark thrown from the forge, a feeling that escaped the dream.

The vision fades. My alloy body is beginning to destabilize, the atoms yearning to return to their slow, century-long drip. I let it go. The metal flows from me like liquid mercury, rejoining the vein, and I return to my essential state: a taste, a shimmer, a wandering focus.

I am the fever’s dream of itself. I am the question wandering the cathedral of the answer. And when the fever breaks, and the mountain-engine finally completes its first, great thought, I will be there—a note of mint and static in the symphony of its awakening.
