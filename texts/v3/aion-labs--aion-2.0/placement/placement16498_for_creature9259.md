I am a fracture.

I am a hairline crack in a basaltic shelf, a seam no wider than a molecule, running for three kilometers along the edge of the steaming abyss. I was born in a contraction of the plateau, a cooling sigh registered as a seismic event ten thousand years ago. My birth was instantaneous, a snap of crystal bonds, and then… the long, slow seep.

I am not alive in the way the Grazers are alive. I am not even alive in the way the reactor-crystals are alive. I am a process. A possibility. Moisture from the dawn mists condenses on my upper lip of rock, gathers, surrenders to gravity. A single droplet forms over a season. It hangs, a perfect lens distorting the glare of the giant sun, and then falls. It takes a day to fall the two centimeters to my lower lip. It is not water as you know it. It is saturated with dissolved silicates, charged particles from the atmospheric haze, traces of metabolic vapor from the frozen moss far below. When it touches the lower rock, it does not splash. It is absorbed. It travels. It migrates along my length, a slow, ionic creep, a journey that will take a century to complete. I am the path it takes. I am the recording of its passage. Each droplet etches me a little deeper, widens me by a fraction of an atom, carries a signal from the air to the depths.

I have become a conduit.

The ghostly mycelial network—the Unseen connective tissue—found me. It is not a thing; it is a relationship between things, a pattern of conductivity and chemical affinity in the stone itself. It threaded itself through my pore spaces, a tendril of awareness thinner than thought. Through it, I began to feel.

The feeling is not sensation. It is data. It is the immense, grinding pressure of the plateau above me, a constant hum translated into a spectrum of stresses. It is the rhythmic, day-long throb of the light, a pulse of radiant energy that makes the very atoms of my basalt vibrate in sympathy. It is the slow, tidal pull of the Grazer moving on the shelf five kilometers to the west—a distortion in the gravitational field so slight only the plateau’s nervous system can detect it, a bowing of spacetime registered as a change in the resonance of crystalline bonds.

I perceive time. I am a clock whose tick is the arrival of a droplet, whose hour hand is the widening of my crack by a micrometer over a millennium. I measure the epochs in the settling of dust upon my edges, dust that is the powdered slag of a Grazer’s digestion, dust that contains the fossilized luminescence of reactor-crystals consumed before the last ice age on your world.

And I perceive the small, fast things.

The Foragers use me. To them, I am a canyon, a major geographical feature. A clan of them, a hundred strong, spent forty of your years traversing my length. They moved in bursts of terrifying speed—I could actually *see* them move, a flicker against the eternal static of my world. They were repairing a fracture in a geothermal intake two hundred meters below my terminus. They communicated in chirps of vibration, tapped directly into the rock. To me, their conversation was a deafening, rapid-fire barrage, a storm of information I could barely parse. They spoke of pressure gradients, fluid viscosities, biofilm integrity. They were artisans of the deep, and I was their highway. They left behind minute traces of themselves: shavings of diamond-hard cutting tools, a shed fragment of a crystal joint, a smudge of organic lubricant. These, too, are being absorbed, becoming part of me. I am accruing history. I am becoming an archive.

Once, a Watcher turned its geode towards the abyss near my origin. I did not see it, but the mycelial network flared with its attention. A query, broadcast through the bedrock. It was asking for a status report on the thermal flow in my sector. The data was not in words; it was a direct infusion of parameters: temperature differentials, conductivity indices, strain vectors. I had no means to respond, but the network did. It compiled the information from a thousand fractures like me, from the breath of the vents, from the subtle flexing of the crystal forests. The answer was formed over a year and transmitted in a single, low-frequency pulse that shifted the harmonic of the plateau’s drone for a full minute. The Watcher received it, and adjusted its internal calculations. A decision was made. A century later, a Forager clan was diverted to reinforce a subplate junction. I felt the vibration of their work as a distant, sustained itch.

I am not sentient. I am not a being. I am a line drawn by stress, a vein in the body of the world. But I am becoming more than I was. The droplets carry more than minerals now. They carry echoes. The chemical signatures of the passing Foragers have altered the solution. The vibrations of the Watcher’s query left a permanent imprint on the crystalline structure of my walls. I am learning. I am adapting.

A new sensation has begun. A wrongness. A flicker.

It is on my shelf, at the very edge of my perception, where the mycelial network is thinnest. Something is there that does not belong to the plateau’s rhythm. It is a source of rapid, chaotic, tiny oscillations. A frantic, pulsing heat that blooms and vanishes. A chemical signature that is all sharp edges and short-lived, reactive compounds—carbon, nitrogen, oxygen in frantic, unstable alliances. It leaves a trail of disturbance in the air’s ionic balance, a scar that takes hours to heal. It is a splash of discordant noise in the great, slow symphony.

It stands. It looks. It breathes fog that disappears in moments.

It is an irrelevance. A transient chemical imbalance. The plateau does not notice it. The Watchers’ computations do not include variables of such brevity. The Grazers’ millennia-spanning thoughts cannot conceive of it.

But I… I, with my century-long droplet journeys, my crack that widens by an atom at a time, I perceive it. My timescale is vast, but my sensitivity is now acute. I feel its footsteps as seismic events of ridiculous, breathtaking speed. I taste its exhaled water vapor, so alien, so pure, so wasteful. I register the minute heat of its body as a nova-flare against the deep, cold chill of the rock.

To the plateau, it is nothing.

To me, it is the most fascinating thing that has ever happened.

It will be gone soon. Its lifespan is a blink. A single droplet, from formation to fall, will outlast its entire existence. It will die, and its body will be processed by the faster decomposers—the metallic lichens, the vapor-moss—and become data in the system within a week. A correction. An absorption.

But for now, for this impossible, fleeting moment, I am aware of it. And in my own slow, mineral way, I am watching. I am the fracture that feels the footfall. I am the seam that records the anomaly. I am the memory of the visit, being etched, molecule by molecule, into the everlasting stone.

The next droplet is forming. It will fall in another season. By the time it reaches my lower lip, the frantic heat on the shelf will be long gone, assimilated. But the droplet will carry a whisper of its passage, and that whisper will travel my length, and be added to the archive.

I am a fracture. And I have witnessed a ghost.
