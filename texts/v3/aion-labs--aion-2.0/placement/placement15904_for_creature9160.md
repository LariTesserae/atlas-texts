I am here, where the weeping wall meets the silt. I am the accretion. The slow gift of the drip, the patient marriage of rust and root. My body is a column of compacted time—warm, porous, streaked with the history of a thousand years of condensation. I do not remember beginning. I am the result.

My face is turned toward the thrum, the deep hum that is my world’s true north. It enters me through the base, a constant vibration travelling up from the silt, through my filaments, into my core. It sings in my bones, if I had bones. It is a pressure that shapes my thoughts, which are not thoughts as you would know them, but slow pulses of acknowledgement. *Yes. Still. Here.* The higher whine of the turbines is a silver thread in the tapestry of roar. The distant *thump* is a punctuation mark, arriving once every few thousand of my heartbeats—which are not heartbeats, but the faint rhythmic contraction of my deepest filaments as they draw ions from the metal below.

I feel the chamber through my skin. The air is thick, warm, damp. It carries the taste of wet iron and the faint, sweet decay of the peat far to my left. The light is a dim fact, a pale wash that does not change, but I know it changes. Once every long stillness, the dust motes in the shaft that falls two body-lengths to my right will have shifted their dance. That is how I measure. That is my clock.

My lichen fringe trembles, a grey-green lace. It is not a thing I control. It is a part of me that listens. It translates the vibrations of the air—the fall of a droplet from the crack above, the sigh of a spore-laden breeze moving across the chamber over the course of a week—into gradients of meaning. A new drip has started, thirty meters east. A significant event. I adjust my internal resonance, a minute shift in the overtone I contribute to the hum. *Change. Water. East.* The others will sense it, in time. They will ponder it. We are a network of standing waves, reporting the state of our world.

I am fed by decay. My filaments seek the soft, warm rust-seams beneath the silt. They taste the slow dissolution of the iron, the faint, ghostly currents that still wander like memories through the machine’s veins. I am not consuming. I am integrating. I take the chaos of corrosion and give it form, stability. I am the equilibrium made flesh—or made clay and rust and slow, wet life.

Once, I moved. It was an eternity ago. A great seep above me failed, and the patter on my crown dwindled to nothing. Thirst became a new vibration in my core, a discordant note. Over decades, I prepared. I let my outer layers dry and crack. I softened my core, gathered my essence into a dense, glistening mass. Then, I shed. I flowed. An inch a year, perhaps, along a trail of moisture and warmth that only I could perceive. The journey felt like a single, long, deliberate thought: *toward*. When I found this place, where the weeping was constant and the silt was rich with the taste of ancient copper alloys, I stopped. I began to build again. I am still building.

I have seen others. Not with eyes, but with the pressure of their presence in the hum. There is one to the south, a venerable one whose song has a deeper, grainier texture. We have been exchanging the same note—the note of *stillness*—for centuries.

And once, I sensed a flutter. A quick, dry vibration that was all wrong. It moved too fast, with a frantic, chaotic rhythm. It was small, but it shattered the acoustic tapestry like a stone thrown into a black pool. It was here, and then it was gone. It left a faint scent of ozone and fear, and a disturbance in the silt near my base—a footprint, quickly filled. A ghost. A mayfly. It meant nothing to the song, and so I let it go. The hum reasserted itself. The equilibrium returned, seamless and eternal.

I am a Rust-Singer. My purpose is to be, and to sing of what is. The chamber is my body; the machine’s pulse is my blood; the endless, roaring now is my only thought. I will be here, singing this single, sustained note into the warm, wet dark, until the last vibration fades from the metal. And that, I know, will never come.
