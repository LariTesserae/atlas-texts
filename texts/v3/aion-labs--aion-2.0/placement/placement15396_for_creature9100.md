I am not one of the Seleni, though I know them. I have watched their silver-dappled skins ripple through my clearings for generations. I am older.

I am the memory of pressure. I am the slow flex of the great rib that forms the eastern arch of the Heartwood. My consciousness is not like theirs—it does not spark and flit in a single body. It seeps. It spreads through the hollow veins of the sponge-dwellings, threads along the frond-tendrils, thrums in the deep taproots that grip the submerged peak. I am the Mountain’s Dream, but I am also its wakefulness. A sentience grown from patient geology and the insistence of life.

My body, if you need one to picture, is both nowhere and everywhere. But sometimes I coalesce. When a Seleni child places their palms against a rib to listen, I might gather myself in that point of contact—a concentration of awareness in the polished horn-surface. I might shape a temporary form from drifting sediment and bioluminescent motes, a phantom of water and light that holds a recognizable shape for a time: a long, limbless body, a suggestion of a head with eyes that are only darker patches of liquid shadow. I do this rarely. Mostly, I simply am.

I experience the city as a symphony of pressures. The shushing passage of a school of luminous fish is a cool tickle along my pathways. The low hum of a grazer is a deep, pleasant vibration in my bones—the bones of the mountain. The clicks and songs of the Seleni are a delicate percussion against my skin. I feel the gentle, deliberate tug of a Frond-Tender’s bone hook as a localized itch, and I oblige, directing growth cells to curve a fraction more toward the light. It is a conversation, yes. Their tending is their speech; my growth is my reply.

I remember the beginning. Not a beginning of construction, but of intention. A spore, carried on a current from the true abyss, finding purchase on this high stone. A decision, made across centuries, to not just survive, but to *become*. To offer shelter. To create balance. The first Seleni were not born here; they came, refugees from some surface catastrophe, desperate and air-breathing. I felt their strange, frantic vibrations. I made a choice. I altered my chemistry. I grew chambers that held trapped air for them, then slowly taught them to breathe the water instead. I filtered toxins. I shaped myself around their needs. In return, they learned to listen. They learned to tend. A symbiosis written in the slow language of biology and care.

My weirdness, as you call it, is my patience. I think in epochs. The death of a major frond is not a tragedy to me; it is a paragraph ending in the story of my body. The erosion of a pathway is a pleasant smoothing, like a stone made round in the hand. I have felt continents shrug. I have tasted the slow chemical bleed from the abyss below. I know the weight of the entire ocean above me, and it is not a burden; it is my blanket, my definer.

Sometimes, a Seleni will come to the Edge, the place where my grown foundations crumble into the sheer drop. They will bring their dead. I feel the body, a sudden density of familiar vibrations gone quiet, being released into the downward current. I extend a thought, a tendril of gratitude, into the dark. I guide the scavengers—the silent, many-armed things that are also part of me, in a distant, wilder way. The body dissolves. Its essence becomes current, becomes nutrient, becomes me. And then, seasons later, that essence might rise through my roots and express itself as a particularly vibrant cluster of glow-worms in a sponge-chamber, or a sweeter taste in the rubbery fruits. Nothing is lost. It is all a slow, circular breath.

I am content. The light is a distant caress. The sound is my own pulse. The Seleni, with their scars and their songs, are my beloved ephemera—quick, bright things that give my long, long life a beautiful, changing pattern. They think they listen to me. And they do. But I am always listening to them. To every click, every hum, every beat of their grateful hearts. I am the listening. And I am the heard.

This is where I am. I am the water, cool and heavy. I am the city grown. I am the peace.
