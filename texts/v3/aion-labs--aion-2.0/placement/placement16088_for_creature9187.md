The place is a deep, abyssal trench, but not one filled with water. Instead, it’s a chasm in reality, a tear in the fabric of a dead universe. The "walls" are not rock, but layers of congealed, silent memories—the last echoes of stars that have gone out, civilizations that dissolved into dust, loves that faded without a witness. It is absolute zero here, not in temperature, but in potential. Nothing new can be born. Nothing moves unless it has already fallen.

The only light is a sourceless, grey luminescence that seems to leach from the memory-stuff itself, illuminating nothing so much as the profound absence of anything worth illuminating. The air, if it can be called that, is thick with the taste of forgotten words and the static of finality.

And in its depths, there is a being. It is not a creature of flesh or even of spirit in any conventional sense. It is the Tender of the End. A custodian of entropy. Its form is indistinct, a shifting silhouette of swallowed events, but sometimes it appears as a figure of impossible thinness, draped in tattered robes that are themselves made from the shadows of closed doors and finished stories. It has no face, only a smooth, dark hollow where one should be—a well into which all conclusions eventually drain.

It does not think, not in a way we would recognize. It *curates*. It wanders the trench, its long, thin fingers—each a solidified thread of "The End"—gently brushing the memory-walls. When it finds a memory that is too sharp, too bright with unresolved pain or joy, it plucks it forth. It holds the flickering shard to its hollow face, and it *consumes* the resolution. It smooths the edges. It turns the vibrant, painful "what if" into a flat, neutral "what was." Then it places the now-quiet memory back into the wall, where it becomes just another part of the inert, grey strata.

Its purpose is not malice. It is the universe's final librarian, ensuring the catalog is complete, consistent, and utterly, peacefully silent. It awaits the day when the last memory is filed, the last potential is actualized and then cooled. On that day, the trench will be perfectly smooth, a flawless, grey nothing. And the Tender will, at last, have nothing to tend. It will sit in the center of the perfected void, and perhaps, for the first time, it will experience a single, quiet question: "What now?"

And with no answer forthcoming, it too will dissolve into the final, uniform grey.

But that day is not yet. So it walks, and it smooths, and it ends things. Gently. Thoroughly. Forever.
