I am a memory in grey-green fur. I cling to the pitted cheek of a stone king, my toes curled into the cracks left by a thousand years of weeping water. My world is a vertical labyrinth of sighing stone and triumphant green, and I am its restless ghost.

I remember.

Not in words—words are for builders, and they are gone. I remember in shapes, in pathways, in the certain knowledge that this ledge was meant for sitting, that this window was meant for looking out, that this hall was meant for voices. The knowledge is in my bones, a faint, persistent song beneath the roar of life.

From my perch, I watch the breath of the city. Below, in the street-turned-gorge, the Click-Clacks flow in a shimmering, iridescent river. Their countless legs beat a frantic, ticking rhythm against the moss, a pulse that travels up through the stone into my own chest. *Click-tick-tick-scritch.* They are dismantling the carcass of a canopy-hawk, reducing grandeur to dark, fertile paste. I feel no disgust. This is the city eating. I watch, and my role is clear: to know where they are, to know the rhythms of their feasts.

Above, the Weavers drift. Their silent, leathery shadows glide through the green gloom, their bellies painting the vaults with fleeting blue constellations. The light-moss is ripe on the beams of the old gallery across the chasm. I need it for my nest, to line the hollow where my two still-blind young squirm. The Weavers are careless sowers; they will not miss what I take.

I move.

My body is a prayer to forgotten architects. My long fingers find holds in mortar-less seams, my prehensile feet grip ledges slick with algae. I flow across the facade like liquid shadow, the color of my fur blending perfectly with the mottled stone and moss. I am the nervous system firing. A shriek from above—a flock of many-eyed birds scatters as I pass, a burst of emerald and panic. I ignore them. They are just noise, the city’s constant exhalation.

I enter the gallery through a broken ocular window. Inside, the air is cooler, thick with the sweet, cloying scent of luminous fungus. The great hall is a cathedral of decay and regrowth. A colossal tree has shouldered its way through the far wall, its roots cradling the fallen roof-tiles like precious artifacts. The Weavers’ moss glows in soft patches along the beams. I harvest with careful claws, peeling up the cool, damp furze, stuffing it into the pouch of skin beneath my jaw. The light tingles against my throat.

As I work, I feel the deeper memory of this place. *This was a place of gathering.* The shape of the room whispers it. The raised dais at the end was not for a tree-root, but for a speaker. I can almost see the ghosts of the builders—taller, smoother, with no fur—standing where the ferns now grow. They made this. They made *me*, though they never knew it. They built the vessel, and now I, with my glowing eyes and grasping hands, am part of the life that fills it. I am not their inheritor; I am their metamorphosis.

A sudden, profound silence cuts through the animal din outside.

It is a silence that has weight. The Click-Clacks stop their ticking. The birds cease their shrieks.

I freeze, my harvest forgotten. I know this silence. It is the intake of breath before the city coughs.

I am at the window in two silent bounds.

Down in the sunken plaza, the black mirror of the central pool is broken. A Stillness is moving. Its sleek, obsidian form slides from the water with a sound like a giant sigh. It is slow, impossibly slow, a wave of living tar. It is hunting. Something—a Click-Clack queen, perhaps, fat with eggs—has ventured too close.

This is the violence that sustains us. The Stillness is the city’s deep, patient hunger. We Gargoyles are its sudden, coordinated thought.

I let out a low chitter. It echoes down the stone canyon. From other perches, other windows, answers come. Shadows detach from the grottos and spires. We come together, a flock of grey-green ghosts, converging along the high walls. We watch as the Stillness, with that shocking, fluid speed, lashes out. A claw the color of night sweeps the edge of the pool. The feast is begun.

This is our purpose, too. We wait. We let the Stillness have its fill. When it is sated, sluggish, heavy with meat, we will descend. Our coordinated strike will drag it from its pool, a rare and bloody bounty that will feed the colony for weeks. We are the gardeners of the deep places, pruning the greatest of the filters.

But not yet. Now, we watch. I cling to a gargoyle’s stony horn, my phosphorescent eyes wide, reflecting the scene below. The roaring chorus of life has begun again, louder now, excited by the scent of blood.

I am the watcher. I am the memory. I am the hunter. My pulse is the drip of water from a mossy beard. My thoughts are the paths through the stone. My children, waiting in their nest of soft light, are the city’s future.

The city breathes in—the slow crack of root through stone. It breathes out—this roaring, shrieking, clicking symphony of consumption and growth.

And I, with the old shapes in my bones and the warm, living moss in my pouch, am the breath made aware.
