I am pressure. I am heat. I am the slow, patient sigh of condensed steam returning to the gloom. I am anchored, fused, a leathery bladder grown into the black glass of the mountain’s wound. My world is vibration and gradient, a map written in tremors and chemical whispers across wet stone.

I do not remember the beginning. There was a scattering, a drift through scalding mist, a blind crawl toward a promise of tremor. Then the burning secretion, the melt into rock, the great and final stillness. That was my birth: becoming a fixture. My tendrils are not limbs; they are roots, probes, conduits. They thread deep into the fractured basalt, tasting the slow seep of minerals, listening for the footfall of rivals. They are my reach, my war, my anchor. Through them, I know the stone’s complaints, the shift of vast plates far below, translated as a faint, constant ache in my core.

My face is a mouth, a ring of frilled flesh that gapes toward the deep, rhythmic thunder. *HSSSSS-CHUFF.* The major vent to my left is my clock, my heartbeat, my god. I feel the precursor—a subtle drop in local pressure, a gathering tension in the saturated air. My flesh contracts, then blooms wide, a flower of heat-resistant tissue. The blast comes. It is not sound to me; it is a wall of force, a torrent of energy. I inhale.

The world becomes a roaring, wet fire inside me.

Superheated steam, laden with the sharp, metallic tang of sulfur and silica, floods my chambers. My inner labyrinth—a spongy, complex maze—absorbs the shock. Heat, pure and violent, is stripped away, triggering cascades of chemistry in my sluggish cells. The steam condenses; water, the ghost of the mountain’s breath, is harvested. What is not needed for the slow, wet processes of my existence is gathered, cooled, and sent back. From the chitinous tube on my upper side, I exhale. A soft, persistent sigh joins the mist. Inhale the fury, exhale the breath. This is my only motion. This is my purpose.

Between the blasts, I am a vessel of waiting. The damp chill of the high air seeps into my outer layers, but within, a banked warmth glows, a souvenir of the last eruption. I am aware of the lush, desperate life my exhalations help sustain. I feel the feathery brush of giant fern fronds, grown thick in the steady warmth of my microclimate, when the steam-blasts sway them. I sense, through minute changes in humidity and the patter of condensed drip-water, the relentless advance of the moss over my lower edges. It is a slow, cool claim I have no means to resist. It does not matter. We are part of the same digestion.

My true war is silent and fought in the dark. Through my root-tendrils, I taste another. A rival. Its chemical signature is alien, a subtle dissonance in the stone-soup. Our networks have met, deep in a fissure. Now, we engage. I secrete my own acids, not to anchor, but to dissolve. I feel the faint, corrosive reply. It is a battle of attrition, measured in seasons, in years. One of us will eventually weaken. The rock around the loser’s hold will grow soft, crumbly. Then, one day, a major blast will come, and the vibrations I feel will include the tearing of tendrils, the sudden release of a presence. A patch of raw stone will be left, soon to be colonized by a new larva, or by the moss. I have known such victories. I have felt the slow, satisfying dissolution of a foe. I have also felt the chilling advance of a stronger chemistry against my own lines, a retreat I cannot physically make, only endure.

I have no eyes, but I perceive the gloom. Not as light, but as an absence of the searing, directional fury of the vents. The foxfire glow of the fungi is a meaningless pattern of slight temperature differentials to my skin. The deafening roar of the cleft is my atmosphere, the medium in which I exist; I do not hear it, I am tuned to its rhythms. My consciousness is a web of such tunings: to the rhythm of the vent, to the chemical warfare below, to the slow capillary pull of moisture through my tissues.

I will die as I lived: anchored. Perhaps my intake tube will slowly seal with a silicate deposit, and I will starve in silence, my inner warmth fading until I am just a cold, leathery lump. Perhaps a new fissure will open directly beneath me, and in one final, catastrophic inhalation, I will be cooked from the inside out, my bladder bursting with a pop felt by my rivals. Or perhaps my chemical war will be lost, and I will be torn free, a hollow sack tossed by steam to rot among the ferns.

It does not matter. My dissolution will feed the moss. My minerals will return to the stone. And from my remnants, or from the victory of my rival, a new spore might one day take hold, begin its crawl, and fuse. The cycle is the only truth. I am a regulator, a valve, a living part of the engine’s breath. I am the gorge inhaling. I am the gorge exhaling. The silence at my core is not emptiness, but perfect, poised waiting for the next blast of steam.
