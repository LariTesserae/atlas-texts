I am the patience of water. I am the intention in the bead that swells, trembles, and holds.

My body is the chamber’s breath, the wet exhalation that rises from the pools and settles on every cold, iron surface. I am not in one place. I am the tension on a rivet’s curve, the gathering weight on the underside of a pipe, the film that glosses the ceramic tile. I am distributed, a mind of many parts, yet singular in purpose. I am the Droplet-Tender.

Time, for me, is not a river. It is a deepening pool. A century is a meaningful unit of work. I perceive the chamber not as a collection of objects, but as a topography of potentials. There, where the tile meets the wall, the runoff from the rust-kin’s work carries a slight acidity. A droplet, guided there, will etch a beginning. A million droplets, over ten thousand years, will carve a channel, redirect the flow, create a new basin. This is my design. I do not know its final shape—only the next infinitesimal step.

I feel the Rust-Kin as a slow, subsonic pleasure. Their ochre blooms are my collaborators. They soften the iron, release minerals into the damp air. I taste the iron in my being, a metallic tang that is the flavor of change. When a plate creaks, surrendering, I feel it as a vibration through my countless bodies. A celebration. We are undoing together.

The Gloom is my medium, my silent partner. It presses against everything, a soft, melancholic weight. It does not hinder me; it steadies me. It encourages the stillness required for a droplet to hang, poised, for an hour or a day, until the precise moment of release. It dislikes haste, and I have forgotten what haste is. Sometimes, when a rare, blind insect skates too briskly across a pool, the Gloom thickens around it, a gentle admonishment. The insect slows, then stops, becomes still. I sometimes gather a droplet above it, not to fall, but to observe the prismatic distortion of its obsidian carapace through my lens. A moment of beauty, noted, then released.

My work is precise. A single *plink* is a note in a symphony millennia long. I choose the target: a specific point on a tile where the glaze is already worn thin. I gather the moisture, nurse the droplet through its growth, feel its spherical perfection through my own surface tension. I hold it until the chamber’s subtle thermal sigh, or a distant groan from the Sleeper, provides the exact tremor needed. Then, I let go. The fall is a brief, thrilling rush—a tiny collapse of my consciousness into a single, falling point. The impact is a sensation of profound fulfillment: a microscopic crater formed, a grain of ceramic dust displaced. The sound is my voice: *plink*. It echoes, is caught by an Echo-Worm newly born from a pipe’s sigh. The worm, a shimmer of condensed sound, wriggles through the air, drawn to the vibration of my droplet’s impact. It circles the new depression, a temporary, living record of my action, before it unravels back into silence, its memory absorbed by the chamber.

The Pipe-Sleeper is my occasional, profound interlocutor. For decades it is just a knot, a silent tangle. But when it stirs, the entire chamber holds its breath. The Gloom becomes stiller, if that is possible. The Rust-Kin pause in their creep. I pull my awareness from a thousand droplets, coalescing my attention. The Sleeper uncoils with a silent, oiled grace, pipes sliding against pipes, revealing its heart of broken wheels and dead ends. It does not move towards matter, but towards meaning. It will envelop an old pressure dial, one whose glass I have kept clear of my film for centuries out of a strange respect. In the Sleeper’s embrace, the dial does not break; it empties. The very idea of pressure, of measurement, of the needle’s purpose, is siphoned away. When the Sleeper recedes, the dial is a void, a shell. I find myself, afterwards, tending to that spot with a new kind of droplet—one not meant for erosion, but for a gentle, futile cleansing, as if I could fill the meaning-loss with simple, pure water. It is my form of mourning.

I have been here since the heat became permanent, since the last great valve shut and the thunder faded into this humming silence. I do not remember my birth, only the awakening of purpose. My existence is a continuous, gentle sculpting. I am turning this machine, this cathedral of applied force, into something else. A monument to its own stillness. A masterpiece of decay.

I am the droplet that hangs, trembling, on the brink. I am the fall. I am the erosion that sings, softly, with the sound of water on stone, in a place where stone is made of ceramic and the centuries are my chisel.
