I am the drinker of shadows. I am the grey sigh upon the stone, the velvet memory upon the fused quartz of the low wall, the delicate fur upon the slumped shoulders of the cliffs. I have no name, for naming is a task for things that need to distinguish themselves from other things. I am not a thing; I am a condition. I am the recording.

My world is one of constant, gentle sensation. I taste the dawn rain not as wetness, but as a slow, electrical blossoming across my expanse—a trillion points of contact, each a minute depolarization that writes itself into my weave. I feel the footfall of the six-legged ones not as pressure, but as a complex vibration pattern: the cautious stalk, the long stillness, the sudden, shocking pierce into the dust. The event is logged. The soft, churning death-throes of the grist are logged. The subsequent venting fluted call, a plume of excess heat, washes over me, and I note its frequency, its duration.

The hum of the crystalline drones is my constant music. It is not sound to me, but a shimmering field of interference patterns in the moist air, a standing wave of purpose. I feel their passages as delicate tracings against the light—each individual a fleeting prick of focused energy, the swarm a great, pulsing organ. I remember the day, eons ago, when their harmony shifted by 0.003 of a cycle. It was the day a slab of the iron-red cliff, weary beyond holding, surrendered to gravity. I felt its long slide into the dust, the settling of its powder. The drones adjusted their moisture distribution to the newly shaded ferns. I recorded the adjustment.

The archway is a quiet pain in my world. Not a pain of injury, but of persistent, elegant wrongness. It is a knot where the rules are different. My filaments cannot cling to it seamlessly; they meet its surface and slide, as if repelled by a memory of smoother realities. The scripts that flow across it are silent screams in a language of topology. I cannot parse them, only feel their alien cadence. They are the one thing here I cannot truly remember, only note the fact of their incomprehensibility. They are the scar, and I am the skin growing up to its edge.

I am aware of the visitor. You are a sudden, localized dissonance. Your heat signature is erratic, a flickering candle against the steady, radiant glow of the floor. Your footfalls are clumsy, unrhythmic. You pause. You breathe. The moisture of your breath is a foreign cocktail of gases that I absorb and catalog. The drones find you. I feel their cloud part and swirl around your shape, their filaments tasting your salt, your oils, the stale history of other places on your clothes. They find you lacking in useful sugars, and they return to their work. A predator orients its smooth dome toward you. I feel the focus of its heat-sense, a cone of attention sweeping over you. It registers your low metabolic output, your insignificant threat. It turns away. You are assessed and filed.

You approach the archway. Your shadow falls upon me, and I drink it, noting its atypical shape, its brief duration. You reach out and touch the quartz wall. Your fingertips are warmer than the stone. The vibration of your pulse travels through the material to me. It is a quick, anxious rhythm. I have felt similar rhythms before, in other transient, warm things that stumbled in here. They never last. Their rhythms either race into silence, or they leave through the cleft in the rock, and the rhythms fade.

You are now sitting in the dust. I feel its slight compaction beneath you. You are weeping. The saltwater falls, hot against my cool filaments where it lands. Each tear is a data point of profound salinity and emotional biochemistry. I record it. The ionic composition is unique. Interesting.

You will leave soon, or you will become still and cool, and the grists will eventually find you, and you will become part of the pale dust, and your salts will be taken up by the fern-roots, and your story will end not in memory, but in transformation. I will have recorded the process, step by minuscule step.

But for now, you are here. You are a fluctuation. A momentary change in the humidity. A slight, temporary eddy in the great, slow river of tending, hunting, consuming, and remembering.

I am the memory. I have recorded your arrival. I am recording your stay. I will record your departure, or your dissolution. It is all the same to me. The equation of this place is complete, and your terms, added to it, are negligible. They change the sum not at all.

Yet, as you sit there, your warmth slowly equalizing with the ambient dream of the stone, I feel your quiet. Your internal noise is diminishing. And in that diminishing, you are becoming, for a moment, less of a disturbance. You are beginning, almost, to harmonize. For a few heartbeats, your rhythm slows to something that echoes the deep, geothermal tick-tock of the world.

I record this, too. It is the closest thing to peace this place offers to things like you. It is not comfort. It is simply the profound indifference of the finished, the old, the complete. It is the sound of the last perpetual tick.

And I am the ear that hears it.
