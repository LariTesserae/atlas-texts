My feet are the first thought. They spread, root-pads flattening against the spongy walkway, drinking tremors from the living city. The chamber around me is not seen but *understood*: a vast hollow space, its ceiling a high, damp pressure thirty body-lengths above. I am Khel, though that is not a name, merely the unique pattern of chemical signature I leave on the roots I guide—a taste of slow patience and a slight affinity for sulfur-springs. I stand at the edge of the new gallery, my tendril-fingers resting lightly on the pale, crisp rim of a growth-node. The node is young, its edges sharp (erosion score 1.2, the chroniclers would hum). Through my fingertips, I exude a gentle insistence—a cocktail of growth-promoters and directional inhibitors. The root-fibers beneath my touch tremble almost imperceptibly, then begin their slow, cellular turn, weaving a new arch to meet its sister across the pool.

The air is thick broth. I taste it through the slit of my mouth, drawing it over specialized membranes. Today’s humidity carries the standard profiles: chlorophyll from the glowing moss, the sweet tang of fungal blooms from the upper tiers, the distant, oily signature of a hunting eel. And beneath it, something else. A metallic aftertaste, cold and dry. Dryness here is an anomaly, a concept almost meaningless. Yet it is there—a whisper of aridity that does not belong.

I tilt my smooth face upward, not to see, but to let the pressure gradients map the space. The chamber is crowded with life, but it is the right kind of crowd. Moth-winged amphibians flutter in silent eddies, their passage causing minute drags against my skin. Blind shrimp pulse in the pool at my feet, their collective bioluminescence a soft, rhythmic counterpoint to the moss-glow. All is as it should be, and yet the dry taste persists.

A vibration comes through my feet, transmitted up through the fused tendrils of my legs into my core. It is a root-thrum from the Deep Wardens, a status report. *All growth proceeds. The western infiltration reaches the black-salt stratum. Consensus: continue.* I respond with a subtle push of my own heel, sending a confirmation pulse back along the network. My role is to tend this arch, to ensure it grows strong and pure. But my attention keeps returning to the dry taste.

It is not in the air. It is in the water. I kneel slowly, my joints a series of soft creaks like bending roots. I dip my tendril-fingers into the pool. The water is gelid, but information flows into me. Mineral content, pH, the health of the shrimp colony. And there—faint, diluted—the same metallic dryness. It is seeping from below, through the rock, into the aquifer that feeds this pool. A new mineral spring? Perhaps. But the taste is wrong. It tastes of… absence. Of a place with no moisture, no growth, no root.

For a moment, the consensus in my mind wavers. The shared consciousness of the Mycorrh is a gentle, constant pressure, a humming song of *continue, grow, fill*. This new signal is a silence within the song. A question the network has not yet formulated.

I rise. My decision is not my own; it is a chemical inevitability. I must trace the seep. I turn from my arch, leaving it to grow untended for now. The walkway yields under my feet, reporting the passage of others—three Mycorrh passed this way since the last drip-cycle, one carrying a net of harvested fungal fibers. I move against the flow, following the increasing dryness on my tongue.

The passage narrows, the root-walls here older, softer from constant drip. The phosphorescent moss is thinner, the light dimmer. I am moving towards the periphery, where the living city gives way to raw, weeping stone. The taste grows stronger. It is not pleasant. It itches at my membranes, a sensory scratch.

I reach the end of the passage. Before me is a wall of naked rock, seeping water in slow, vertical rivulets. And there, at its base, a new fissure, hairline thin. From it, a trickle of water that does not bead or gleam. It looks dull, heavy. I kneel again, and extend a single, fine rootlet from my fingertip towards the trickle.

Contact.

The information is not chemical. It is a jolt, a vibration of a frequency that screams. It contains images—not seen, but forced into my perception: vast, angular spaces filled with a burning, sterile light; hard, polished surfaces that reflect nothing; a deafening, dry roar that is the antithesis of our drip-plink silence. And a presence. Not a living network, but a single, focused, hungry will. It is aware of the moisture. It is aware of the life. It *wants*.

I recoil, severing the rootlet. The amputated tip writhes for a moment on the stone before going still. The dry taste is now a scream in my mouth. The consensus in my mind fractures, fills with static.

The fissure in the rock widens, just a millimeter. A puff of air escapes—air so achingly dry it feels like a wound. It carries a final, clear signal, translated by some deep, horrified instinct in my biology.

It is a word. The first word my kind has ever received that did not come from ourselves.

***Mine.***

I stand, my body trembling with a foreign emotion—fear. I must return. I must thrum the alarm through the roots. But the roots here are thin, disconnected. The network is distant. I am alone at the edge of the warren, with a taste of dryness on my tongue and a silent scream building in my throatless body.

The city behind me grows, blind and patient. The city before me—the concept of *before*, of *other*—has just spoken. And it is hungry.
