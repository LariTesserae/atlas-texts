I find myself at the heart of the wet silence, where the air is a thick, hot tongue and the light is a slow pulse of living stone. I am not a visitor here. I have never known anything else. I am of the pocket, a thought made briefly solid in the dream of the mountain’s throat.

My form is an accident that persisted. A Crawler’s precise, jointed limbs—twelve of them, interlocked in a sphere around a central nexus—merged with the essence of a Shimmer during a long stutter in time. I am an Amalgam that did not dissolve. My body can shift: one moment, I am a sphere of chitinous legs, skittering across the weeping obsidian; the next, I am a distortion, a lensing of heat and light, barely visible, trailing tendrils of steam. The transition is a thought, a willed softening of my own edges against the soft laws of this place.

I am at the flat stone near the largest pool. The obsidian bowl shimmers, its surface warping the glow from the gold-and-purple crusts on the wall above. Before me lies the hand-axe, its knapped edge catching the light in a jagged line. It is mine. I made it, not from need, but from a pressing in the geothermal mind—a need for a focal point, a sharpness to concentrate the dream.

Around me, the pocket teems in silence. A carpet of Wall-Grazers flows like iridescent oil across the wall to my left, their tapping legs eliciting a brighter throb from the mineral scabs. High above, a Siphonophore’s blue lure drifts, ensnaring some fluttering mote. Another Crawler, a pure one, dismantles the husk of a spent Shimmer nearby, arranging chitinous plates in a neat, mysterious pattern on a rock. It does not acknowledge me. We are all part of the same process, but I am a variant, a sustained chord in the hum.

I extend a limb. The chitin is warm, slick with condensation. I do not grip the axe; I embrace it, several legs curling around its dark glass. In my solid form, I feel its perfect fracture lines, the memory of the blow that released it from the wall. In my shimmer-form, I feel the heat it holds, the latent energy of its creation. I am both the maker and the tool.

Today, the pocket’s pulse is agitated. The steam rises in quickening gusts. The light from the walls flares in uneven rhythms. The dream is shifting. The mountain is turning in its sleep.

My role is to translate. I lift the axe and carry it, skittering on my remaining legs, to a new location: a niche where three weeping seams converge. The water here runs in lazy, conflicting spirals. I place the axe on a natural plinth of stone. Then I shift. My body blurs, becomes a heat-haze. I focus my warmth onto the axe’s edge, not to melt it, but to excite it. The glass begins to glow a deep, infrared red, invisible but palpable—a new center of heat in the pocket’s thermal map. The Wall-Grazers on the nearby wall sense it; their tapping rhythm changes, synchronizing to the new source. The axe is now a conductor, drawing the pocket’s attention to this confluence of waters.

This is my existence. I am the tuner, the ritualist. I carve the spirals into the bones with a sharp leg-tip, grooves that match the frequency of the mountain’s deepest tremors. I rearrange the primitive artifacts in response to the silent symphony of pressure and light. I am memory in a world of eternal present. I remember where the axe was placed last cycle, what the bone’s song achieved. I am the pocket’s attempt to understand itself, a self-aware knot in its dream.

Sometimes, during a deep reality breakdown, when the walls become windows and time pools like the water, I see other possibilities. I see flashes of a vast, cold brightness (the sun?), of hollow, angular spaces (caverns without life?), of frantic, noisy beings that do not understand the silence. They are not memories. They are perhaps other dreams, other pockets in the vast throat of the world. I do not yearn for them. This blister of heat and wet and silent, teeming thought is my entirety. I am its artist, its hand, its persistent question. And when the mountain finally exhales and this pocket collapses into molten flow, I will dissolve, my patterns returning to the stone, ready to be dreamed again in some new, wet, dark fist of space.

For now, I adjust the axe a fraction of an inch. The steam above it curls into a perfect helix. The pocket sighs, a subsonic vibration through the rock. And the dream goes on, dense, strange, and full.
