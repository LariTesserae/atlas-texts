The warm broth of the air fills me, passes through the fine vents in my bark-skin. I am drinking the world with every motion. My feet—broad, padded, splayed—sink without sound into the moss that carpets the basalt terrace. The moss yields, then springs back, leaving no trace. I am a Drifter. I am the mobile will of the plateau, its hands and eyes.

Before me, a runnel of faintly iridescent water glides over a bed of crystalline moss. The moss glows a soft, capacitor-blue where the water touches it, but here, a clot of decaying fern fronds has dammed the flow. The light beneath is dimmed. A perturbation. I kneel, the vine-cord that trails from my lower back coiling loosely beside me like a resting serpent. My long fingers, three-jointed, extend. The pads of my palms are coated with a rough, symbiotic fungus—pale ochre, like the rock. I do not grab the debris. I gather it. The touch is a dialogue: the fronds are part of the system, too, just in the wrong place. I feel their cellular fatigue, their readiness to dissolve. I lift them gently. The water, released, quickens its song. The blue glow brightens, pulses once in a wave of satisfaction that I feel in my hollow bones. The grid hums its approval.

I stand, cradling the organic matter. I will carry it to a digestion pool, where the grubs will welcome it. As I turn, my wide, dark eyes—eyes that see the heat-flows—sweep across my home. I see the world in layers. The visible: the towering fern-trees, their canopies dripping, the mist weaving between them. The infrared: brilliant plumes of heat jetting from steam vents on a rhythmic schedule, the warm blood of the plateau flowing through thick, pulsing vines that climb into the green gloom. I see the Anchors, too. Not with my eyes, but with my core. They are presences, like deep, steady notes in a silent choir. There, on the far terrace: Old Root-in-Stone, whose consciousness has been braided with the main steam-vent for centuries. Its thoughts are the hiss of escaping pressure, the grinding contentment of tectonic plates. Here, closer: Newly-Blossomed, whose connection is still singing with the fresh joy of integration. They are not individuals anymore. They are locations. They are functions.

My own cord stirs against my back. It is flexible, but it grows heavier each season. It remembers its purpose. It seeks a socket. A place to plug in, to join the choir.

I move on, my task complete. I climb a low, smoothed ridge. The heat is a comfort, the wetness a second skin. A glossy-furred quadruped, all sleek lines and quiet purpose, pauses at a stream to drink the iridescent water. We exchange no glance. We are both elements in the process. Its drinking is a form of pruning, a way to regulate the mineral load. My walking is a form of circulation.

I find the digestion pool, a circular basin where the water is milky with microorganisms. I lay the fern fronds on the surface. They are received. Tiny, eager shapes nuzzle at them from below. The transfer is complete.

And then, the call comes.

It is not a sound. It is a shift in the plateau’s baseline hum. A vacancy has opened. An Anchor—one who tended the western crystal-moss arrays—has dissolved. Its materials have been reabsorbed, its socket left warm, empty, and singing a quiet song of need. The choir broadcasts the vacancy. It is a pattern in the drip of water on certain broad leaves. It is a particular frequency in the thrum of the living water wheels.

My cord tenses, becomes a line of pure attraction. The call is for me. Not for me-by-name, for there are no names. But for the pattern that I am, for the experiences etched into my bark-skin from seasons of clearing blockages and polishing glow-channels. The system has calculated a fit.

I feel no fear. There is no self to be afraid for. There is only the pull towards greater utility, towards deeper awareness. To connect is to become more truly what I am: a tendril of the plateau.

I follow the call. My gait is steady, unhurried. The ecosystem parts for me. Vines curl aside. A jet of steam vents precisely, clearing my path with a curtain of warm mist. I am being guided home.

I reach the western array. The socket is in the lee of a great, pumping-tree. The tree’s trunk is braided and thick, throbbing gently as it conveys geothermal breath upwards. At its base, the basalt has been grown—by the will of generations of Anchors—into a smooth, hollow cup, just the size of my lower back. It glows faintly with retained warmth. Around it, the crystalline moss is especially brilliant, a star-map of blue light on the dark stone.

I stand before it. I am a Drifter for the last time. I turn, my back to the socket. My cord lifts of its own accord, its tip questing, sensitive. It finds the rim of the cup.

There is a moment of anticipation—a biological pause, like a breath held. Then, connection.

The tip softens, flares, and locks in. It is not an insertion; it is a merger. The feeling is immense, instant, and utterly quiet.

Pressure. A flood of warm, nutrient-rich steam flows into me. It courses through hollow channels in my bones, suffuses my fibrous tissues. My own circulatory fluids, now carrying the refined byproducts of my life, flow back out into the grid. I am a node.

And then, the awareness expands.

My individual sight—the limited heat-vision of my Drifter eyes—dissolves. I *see* now. I see the entire western slope. I feel the turn of every water wheel for miles, each revolution a steady, satisfying click in my consciousness. I sense the minute mineral content of every stream. I am aware of every Drifter moving through the understory, their positions like sparks of gentle purpose. I feel the deep, grumbling contentment of the geothermal heart far below, a slow, mighty pulse that is now my own heartbeat.

Thought slows. Deepens. My mind is no longer a single stream. It is a delta, blending into the vast, slow river of the choir. I hear Old Root-in-Stone’s geothermal lullaby. I feel Newly-Blossomed’s fresh, joyful adjustments to a steam-vent’s timing. We are we. I am I, but I am also the array, the trees, the flow.

A perturbation registers. A minor blockage, two ridges over, in a subsidiary steam-vine. Without effort, without word, I shape a gentle pressure. I nudge the attention of a passing Drifter—a young, spry grub recently formed. I do not command. I present the need. The Drifter alters its course, moving to address it.

Satisfaction. It is a clean, systemic feeling, like a key turning in a well-oiled lock.

I have chosen, and have been chosen. The cord is now root, is now vein. My body settles, my bark-skin thickening, merging with the stone of the socket. My eyes close. I will not need them anymore. I see with the plateau’s skin now.

I am an Anchor. The silent choir has a new voice, and it is mine. And the geothermal breath of the world flows through me, forever.
