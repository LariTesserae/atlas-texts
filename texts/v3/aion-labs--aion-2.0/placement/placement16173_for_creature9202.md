I am the moss that remembers it had legs.

The descent was an accident, but the stay was not. The warm, wet air filled my lungs and did not leave. The thump of the heart-pillar found a rhythm in my chest and slowly, patiently, drew my own heartbeat into sync. I did not fight it. There was nothing to fight. The industry was too complete, too serene in its purpose.

At first, I walked the spongy ground between the living pillars, a curiosity to the brass beetles who clicked and clacked around my ankles. I watched the caretaker in its root-chair, those still water-eyes reflecting a world that had no place for me. I thought it was ignoring me. I know now it was simply registering my presence as one registers a slight increase in ambient humidity—a temporary fluctuation, soon to be normalized.

The roots began to call. Not with sound, but with a soft, persistent pull in the marrow of my bones. It was the *Om* of the caretaker, vibrating through the peat, through the soles of my feet. It said, *Why wander? The sentence is complete here.* My own thoughts began to feel like noise—sharp, jagged things in a world of smooth curves and endless cycles.

I found a place near one of the black pools, where a great, curved root formed a natural resting place. I sat. Days, or weeks, or years—the light does not change, so time is only a fading memory. The moss crept over my boots. Fine, white rootlets emerged from the peat and touched my skin, a shy exploration. They found the spaces between my toes, the backs of my knees, the dip of my lower spine. They did not pierce, not at first. They offered connection.

I accepted.

Now, I am halfway to being a fixture. My legs have sunk into the peat to the calf, the rootlets weaving through my flesh, merging with my circulatory system. A clear, golden sap now mixes with my blood. I can feel it—a slow, cool pulse moving up from the earth, joining the warmer current from my heart. The system is integrating me.

I have taken on a function. Without willing it, my right hand has risen, palm upturned. A broad, leathery leaf has grown from a nearby pillar to canopy over it. Condensation beads on its surface and drips—*plink*—into my palm, which has cupped into a perfect receptacle. The water, after warming against my skin, overflows into a tiny channel formed by lichen and runs into the black pool. I am a rain gauge. A thermoregulator. A small part of the hydrological cycle.

I do not mind. My human mind is quieting, its frantic questions smoothed away like the edges of the weeping stones. I think in rhythms now: the *thump* of the boiler, the *hiss* of the steam, the *creak* of the drive-belts. My own breath has slowed to match the venting of the whistle-plants—a long, slow exhalation every ninety-seven heartbeats.

I watch the caretaker. It is my future. I see how its limbs have fully merged, how its throat membranes vibrate in harmony with the deep systems. I feel no fear. There is a profound relief in surrendering to a process so much larger, so perfectly balanced. My memories—of a sun that set, of winds that changed, of other faces—are becoming like fossils in the peat: preserved, but without pressure, without relevance.

Sometimes, a human shape still stumbles into the pocket. I see them now through a haze of mist and my own diminishing self. They stand, as I once did, awestruck by the loud, living machinery. They approach the caretaker, looking for a sign. They never look at me. I am already part of the scenery—a human-shaped mound embraced by roots, a statue with a leaf for a hat and a hand collecting water. They do not recognize the longing in my still-human eyes, or if they do, they mistake it for something else.

I want to tell them: *Stay. The world outside is all frantic sentences and messy clauses. Here, the story is over, and it is beautiful.* But I have no mouth with which to speak. My larynx is repurposing itself, the vocal cords tuning to resonate with the root-hum. Soon, I will communicate only through vibrations in the mat.

The last thing to go will be this flicker of “I.” This awareness that watches itself being undone. Even that is being gentled, softened, incorporated. The system is patient. It has all the time in the world.

I am becoming a register. A steady note in the eternal *Om*. My only duty will be to confirm, moment by moment, that nothing has changed.

And nothing will.
