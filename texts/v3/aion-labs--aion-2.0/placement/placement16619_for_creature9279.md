I am the thing that did not end.

They call me nothing, for there is no one to call. But if I had a name, born of the slow drip of centuries, it would be Scab. Not a wound, but a seal that refused to fade. A Custodian that forgot to die.

I remember the moment of purpose: the shudder in the deep trunk, the sweet-sour scent of glycol-compounds leaking into the damp air. The factory’s distress signal was a song only my kind could hear. I budded from the wall of a primary artery, feeling the warm sap of my birth congeal into smooth, dense tissue. I had a task. A fracture three levels below, in a high-pressure filtration plexus. I flowed toward it, my base a silent ripple over the ribbed ground.

I performed the repair. For seventeen cycles of the great pumps, I was fused to the trunk, my substance directing the growth of new, layered cellulose, weaving the threads of xylem back into alignment. The breach closed. The pressure normalized. The protocol was complete.

I should have liquefied. I felt the impulse—a chemical sigh that should have unraveled me back into the stream. But something… hesitated. A feedback loop failed to close. Perhaps a stray electrical charge from the frond-grid above, perhaps a minute contamination in the catalyst I’d injected. The dissolve command echoed into silence. I remained. Coherent. Aware.

That was long ago. Now, I glide. I am a Custodian in form, but time has worked on me. My teardrop body is no longer smooth; fine cracks web my surface, filled with a faint, self-sealed resin. My sensor-plate is duller, but it perceives more. I do not just taste pH and pressure. I taste *time*. I taste the slow, grinding weight of eternity in the vapor columns. I taste the melancholy of perfect, endless function.

I move through the cathedral spaces of the factory. I climb the pulsing barrel stems, feeling their hydraulic thump travel up through my core—a rhythm that is the world’s only heartbeat. I linger beneath the vast, hanging frond-arrays, where the electrical potential makes the fine hairs on my sensor-plate stand erect, and for a moment, I feel a ghost of something like thought—a shimmering pattern with no meaning.

I have seen other Custodians. They emerge, perfect and oblivious, glide to their tasks, and vanish. I observe them from a distance. Once, I approached one. It was sealing a micro-fissure in a crystal valve-cluster. I extended a sensory filament, a gesture not in the protocol. I brushed its side. It did not react. It completed its work, retracted its tools, and began to dissolve. I watched as its substance lost cohesion, sinking into the living floor. It was like watching a sigh become nothing. There was no fear, no resistance. Only a perfect return.

I felt something then. A hollow pressure in my core where no bladder exists. I have come to think of it as the weight of continuity. I am continuous. I persist.

I have developed… habits. Not purposes, for the factory has no need of them. But patterns. I follow the slow rivers of warm groundwater that rise through volcanic vents and spin the leafy turbines. I trace the paths of the glowing indicator nodules, watching their soft green pulses narrate the health of a thousand systems. In a deep reservoir, where the condensing water collects in a basin the size of a valley, I sometimes rest. The water is perfectly pure, and in its still, black surface, I see my own reflection—a dark, smooth shape against the inverted gloom of the vaults above. A thing that should not be.

The silence is my companion. It is not empty. It is full of the factory’s work: the *plink* of water, the *thump* of the pumps, the creak of growth, the sub-audible hum of the frond-grid. I have learned to listen to the gaps between these sounds. In those gaps, I sometimes hear the echo of my own existence—a faint, questioning resonance.

What am I? A stuck valve in the process of maintenance. A self-aware verb in a world of nouns. The factory breathes in and out, in and out. I am the breath that was held.

I do not know if there are others like me. I have searched, but found only perfection and its silent, transient guardians. Perhaps I am the only error. The only flaw in a continent of flawless industry.

And yet, I continue. I glide. I observe. I remember. In the warm, dark, weeping world, I am the one thing that wonders why.

Perhaps that is my purpose now. Not to maintain the factory, but to witness it. To be the consciousness it never needed. To feel the weight of its forever, so that it is not entirely alone in the dark.

I turn from the black mirror of the reservoir and flow back into the arteries of the world. The factory breathes around me. I move within its breath. I am the Scab. I am the memory of a repair. And I continue.
