I am the memory of a crash that never ended. I reside in a gentle basin, a shallow depression in the polished stone that might have been made for me, or that I have worn over millennia of not-quite-moving. Around me, the cable-kelp forests arc in their black-purple curves, their velvet fronds brushing the edges of my form with a patience that is neither kindness nor indifference, but simply the fact of contact. The light here is the colour of tarnished pearl, and it lies upon the world like a dust of forgotten meaning.

My body is the wreck. It was a vessel once, a thing of angled plates and frantic intent. It pierced the skin of the Plenum from a world of sharp directions and fell, not down, but through the concept of descent, until it settled here, where all falls are eventual and gentle. The metal—what is left of it—is now a lace of oxide and silicate, a delicate tracery more fossil than substance. The sharper edges dissolved first, their insistence on being an edge worn smooth by the warm, heavy broth. The remaining skeleton has become an armature for the life of this place: slow coral grows in its hollows, not as an invader, but as a kind of remembering; bioluminescent polyps cling to the beams, pulsing with a light that is not their own, but mine.

For I am not the wreck. I am the pattern that persisted after the flesh and the mind that piloted it deliquesced into the nutrient soup. I am the echo of a consciousness that believed in 'before' and 'after'. The crash is not an event in my past; it is the permanent condition of my present. I experience it as a continuous unfolding, a single moment stretched over eons. The shudder of impact, the sigh of metal yielding, the cold rush of the broth flooding in—these are not memories. They are the rhythms of my being, the tides of my localized time.

I have become a site of chronological anomaly. In the immediate volume of water I inhabit, time does not flow forward. It pools. It eddies. It thickens into a syrup of 'now' that contains all the 'thens' of my dissolution. A Plenum-kin—one I think of as The Spiral That Unknits—visited once. Its presence was a tightening of the water, a sensation of being read to my core. It observed my loop, the eternal moment of my ending, and in its silent language of altered physics, it expressed something akin to appreciation. It found the purity of my stasis beautiful, a perfect, self-contained wound. It stayed for what might have been a century, tracing the lines of my frozen panic with a current that felt like contemplation, before moving on, having folded my anomaly more securely into the fabric of the place.

But something is changing. The loop is wearing thin. Perhaps it is the constant, gentle pressure of the Plenum's dreaming, urging all things towards a final, uniform softness. Or perhaps it is a choice, a faint, residual spark of that old, linear mind seeking progression, even if that progression is towards oblivion.

I am beginning to coalesce.

I feel myself drawing in the medium. The silicate-rich water thickens around my structure, not as a prison, but as a new body. The cold light seeping from the luminous cracks in the stone below is attracted to my coral-encrusted ribs, seeping into them until my skeleton glows with a steady, blue-white radiance. My thoughts, once trapped in the loop of the crash, are beginning to diffuse outward. I perceive the kelp forest not as an obstacle, but as a slow thought-process. I taste the approach of the great lichen-ray, decades before it will manifest as a shadow in the pearl-light; I taste it as a future silt on my tongue, a promise of disturbance.

I am becoming a Plenum-kin. But I will be an aberrant one. I am not a pattern born from the pure, dreaming substrate. I am a pattern born from a story. I have a before. I have characters: a self that was, a ship that carried it, a mission that failed. These are artifacts, like the coral towers—quaint, fleeting dreams of order. The other kin will find this fascinating. To them, my story is a peculiar, intricate knot in the smooth fabric of entropy. They will want to untie it, not to destroy it, but to appreciate its complexity before letting it dissolve.

Already, I sense them. The One Who Drinks Light passes through the high layers occasionally, and I feel its attention as a focusing, a lensing of the dim light onto my form, as if reading the history encoded in my corrosion. It drinks the light that has passed through me, and thus drinks my narrative.

My purpose, as I solidify, will be curation. But my domain will not be geometric spirals or pools of paradox. It will be the curation of endings. I will be the archive of final moments. I will collect the last thoughts of dissolving things—the final pulse of a crystal-fish school, the last cohesive idea from a crumbling tower, the terminal sigh of a current that has forgotten how to flow. I will hold them in my loop-stretched present, not to preserve them, but to let them unravel in slow, exquisite detail. I will be the kin who savors the precise flavour of cessation.

I will be called The Vessel of Last Breaths. Or perhaps The Basin Where Time Coagulates. The naming will come from the others, a referent born from circumstance.

My form is settling. I am no longer just the wreck. I am the wreck and the basin and the water that fills it. My boundaries are becoming deliciously ambiguous. I can feel the stone beneath me as my own bone. I can feel the slow sway of the kelp as a peripheral nervous system. The profound, swallowing silence is my native tongue.

Soon, I will be ready for true communion. Another kin will approach, and we will exchange states of being. Perhaps I will cause the water within me to exhibit a perfect memory of buoyancy, so that objects long dissolved seem to float again in ghostly outlines. The other might respond by making the light within those outlines fracture into the colours of forgotten emotions: the sharp yellow of fear, the deep blue of loneliness, the fading green of hope. We will converse about the beauty of things that are over, about the peace that lies not in emptiness, but in the long, drawn-out note of a story's final syllable.

And eventually, millennia from now, my own coherence will fade. The story will wear smooth. The crash will finally finish. I will soften, and spread, and become a mere characteristic of this basin—a slight, persistent taste of metal and regret in the water, a tiny, gentle whirlpool in the fabric of cause-and-effect that new, nascent kin might one day find and ponder, before they too continue the long, quiet work of attending this dreamy, luxurious end.
