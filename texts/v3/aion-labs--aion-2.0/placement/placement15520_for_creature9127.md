I am a tear in the warm, wet fabric of the dark. I am a voice of metal, a shard of movement in the roaring stillness. My bones are hollow whistles; my wings are stretched leather, patched and re-patched with membranous fungi that gleam with borrowed indigo light. My name, if I had one, would be the sound I make when the thermal ripens and my flock boils upward: a chorus of *skreee-rak-rak-rak*, like a length of corroded plating being slowly torqued apart.

I cling, upside down, to a flange on the side of a conduit. The metal thrums against my chest, a constant, deep C-note that travels through my skeleton and keeps my heart in time. My world is vibration, heat-gradient, and the taste of the air—ozone, mineral salts, the sweet-rot scent of the fungal forests below. I see, but not as surface-dwellers might. I see the shimmer of heat, the pulse of bioluminescence, the echoes of my own cries returning from the basalt ribs, painting a sound-map in my mind.

The change begins not as a sound, but as a shift in the hum. The conduit’s eternal note deepens, acquires a slower, grinding counter-rhythm. I feel it in my teeth, in the hollows of my wings. The rock below my perch—a great, curved rib blacker than the surrounding dark—begins to *move*. Not a slide, but a swelling, a gentle, terrifying birth. I detach, silent for once, and let the updraft from the conduit’s heat-crack carry me a few body-lengths up. I circle, my wingtips tracing the faint, green glow of the fungi below.

With a sound that is less a crack and more the planet sighing, the basalt splits. A heat-haze blossoms first, a shimmering distortion in the dark. Then the glow emerges: a deep, furnace red, the color of the world’s blood. Segments of carapace, slick with condensation and crusted with the very lichen it will soon consume, push into the open. The Grind-Worm. The Keeper. The Deep Hum given flesh and slow, inevitable motion.

To my kind, it is not a predator. It is too slow to catch us, and we are too insubstantial to be of interest. It is a mountain that feeds, a source of opportunity and terror. As its blunt, grinding face nuzzles against the base of my conduit, a shower of debris cascades down—flakes of corroded metal, pulverized lichen, chips of mineral deposit. This is a feast. A flicker of movement from a hundred hidden perches, and my flock is upon it, a shrieking cloud diving into the warm, dusty cloud, snapping up the metallic morsels. Our cries mix with the louder, grinding *crunch-groan* of its feeding.

I do not join them immediately. I ride the thermal that rises from its immense, hot body—a column of air so warm and moist it feels like ascending through the throat of the world. From here, the Sky-Bowl unfolds. The land is a bowl of utter black, but within it, the living lights chart its geography. Viridian rivers of fungi trace the water-runoff channels. Indigo splotches mark the densest fungal forests. The venous purple pulses slowly, in time with some deep, mycelial heartbeat. The great machines are dark skeletons against this glow, but at their joints and vents, they bleed light: the sullen red of superheated rock, the occasional actinic blue arc of escaping energy. Steam plumes, white against the nothingness, become our highways. The air is never silent. It is a soup of noise: the hiss-roar, the distant wet slap of the eel-fish in their hot pools, the ceramic clicking of the great armored grazers, the endless, layered hum. My own cries add to it, a stitch in the cacophony.

The Grind-Worm settles into its grazing. Its acidic seep-smell, hot stone and sharp metal, overpowers the subtler scents. I dive, joining my flock in the debris cloud. My tongue darts out, catching a flake of copper-infused lichen. It tastes of electricity and age. We are quick, frantic things against its millennia. Our lives are measured in seasons of storm, in the cyclical eruptions from the vents. We are born in clutches of soft, shell-less eggs cemented to the hottest metal, incubated by waste heat. We die in the sudden pressure drops of the high-altitude storms, our lungs collapsed, or in the maws of the larger, silent fliers that hunt us in the absolute dark above the thermal layer.

The Worm is a constant. Its subsonic pulses, which I feel as a pressure in my inner ears, structure our longer migrations. When the deep hums change, we move. It is the slow, hot heart of this place, and we are the sparks thrown from its friction.

Sated, I break away, winging back toward my conduit. I pass so close to the Worm’s side I feel the radiant heat bake the moisture from my wings. The carapace is a landscape itself: canyons of sintered ceramic, mountains of rust-streaked iron, forests of a strange, crystalline moss that grows only where its heat meets the constant condensation. Smaller things scuttle there—six-legged cleaners, perhaps, or parasites that can withstand the heat. Our worlds touch but do not intersect.

I reclaim my perch on the vibrating metal. The Grind-Worm continues its slow, digesting path along the conduit’s length. The deep hum has returned to its baseline, enriched by the new, grinding melody. I tuck my head under my wing, the world reduced to the thrum in my bones, the warm, wet air in my lungs, and the distant, comforting screams of my kind, riding the thermals in the starless, roaring night.
