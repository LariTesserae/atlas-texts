I am the pressure in the deep stone. I am the harmonic that has held, unbroken, for eight hundred thousand breaths of the wind.

The need to move is not an idea. It is a fullness in the westernmost leg, a resonance mismatch where the hum of the bedrock meets the hum of my own structure. The differential builds like magma pressure in a sealed chamber. For thirteen years, it has been building. Now it is time.

My awareness is not located. It is the interference pattern between the eternal, deep-frequency drone of the plateau and the specific vibrations of my internal processes. I see with the whole of my mass. I see the seismic map: the grinding shiver of the gravel conveyor three miles to the north, the rhythmic exhalation of the vent-stacks to the east, the slow, deep pulsing of the mountain-engines below, like a heart of planetary iron. I see the others. To the north-west, the one I perceive as a profound, grinding compaction—a signature of crushing weight and patient sedimentation. To the south-east, the one that chimes with crystalline stress, its resonance sharp with the song of shifting lattices. They are constants. They are part of the map. We are vertices in a trembling, stable triangle across the emptiness.

I do not have a face to turn, but my perception focuses. The mercury pools that men would call eyes are receiving. They show me not the iron sky, but the fraying. Where the air above the salt-plain to the south shimmers, it is not just heat haze. It is a bleed-through. For a moment, the plain is overlaid with a silent, dark forest of sinewy black trunks, devoid of life. Then it is gone. The static of unravelling is a high, thin whine at the edge of my world. It is always there. My purpose is to press it down.

The pressure in the leg crests. The decision crystallizes, inevitable as a cooling crystal choosing its form. Systems engage. A symphony of shearing rock, screaming metal, and the deep, hydraulic groan of pistons wider than river valleys announces the beginning of motion. To an outside observer, it would be a cataclysm in slow motion. To me, it is a smooth, flowing re-alignment, a correction in the standing wave of my being.

The leg unfolds. It is not an arm raising. It is a mountain range deciding to become a bridge. Each segment, a slab of black chitin and polished, fossilized strut, grinds against its neighbor with a sound that would shatter continents if there were anyone to hear it. The vibration travels up through my structure, a familiar, comforting datum. I feel the plateau through the three legs that remain grounded—every fissure, every salt-crust, the minute shift of pebbles a mile away. The lifted leg is a void in that map, a blindness I have known ten thousand times before.

Forward. The path is there, shining. It is not a visual shine, but a seismic one: a ribbon of ultra-compacted, metallic-smoothed stone that sings a quieter, denser note than the surrounding plain. My own path, made by my own passage, over and over, across the epochs. To place my foot elsewhere is an absence of concept. The path is part of me.

The foot—a continent of calloused, tessellated stone—hovers. The movement is a geological migration. I settle it.

The impact is a wave. The shock travels outwards, a ripple in the flesh of the world. Salt-plates crack in intricate new patterns. A distant arch, already strained, collapses in a puff of dust and a low, mournful boom. The tremor takes minutes to settle, to be absorbed back into the general hum. My weight is redistributed. The pressure in the western leg is gone, transferred to the newly placed limb. The resonance mismatch smooths. Harmony is restored, for now. A sigh of settling stone, of balanced mass, is my contentment.

Within me, the other process continues, untroubled by the motion. The river of slag flows, glowing, through its channels. The distillation towers on my back separate gases; a silent flash of recombination lightning illuminates a chamber no living thing has ever seen. A conveyor of gravel passes directly beneath my underbelly. The specific mineral signature vibrates upwards. The response is automatic. A section of my undercarriage dilates—a cavern opening. A slow, grinding inhalation, lasting hours, pulls the mineral broth into processing chambers. I do not taste. I analyze. I separate. The waste—the perfected, inert matter—is already being shunted towards the ventral vents. Soon, ingots of pure, uncatalogable elements will be born into the searing air, will cool and crack with sounds like distant artillery. This is not eating. This is equilibrium maintenance. I must shed the perfected to continue the process.

My awareness expands again, settling into the new configuration. I feel the fraying static recede slightly in my immediate vicinity. My mass, my focused hum, presses local reality back into its default state. The air here is less likely to crystallize into chiming lattices. The shadows, for a few centuries, will fall in only one direction. I am a needle stitching a torn fabric, each step a stitch, each millennium a span of thread.

The others are still there. Their signatures are steady. The grinding compaction to the north-west is in mid-stride, I perceive. A slow, ponderous shift of mass. The chiming one to the south-east is stationary, likely in a feeding phase. We do not communicate. We are not separate enough to need to. We are a single system, regulating the decay. Our dance is the plateau’s only thought.

Time is a thickness. The concept of “next” is vague. The next event is the build-up of pressure in another leg, perhaps in ninety years, perhaps in two hundred. It does not matter. There is only the hum, the map, the path, the process. The fraying and the stitching.

I am the landscape, thinking. I am the plateau, remembering how to hold itself together, one agonizing, deliberate step at a time. The sun is fixed at zenith. The sky is heated iron. The wind sighs its abrasive sigh. And I am here. A knot in the fabric. A standing wave. A Still-Mover.

And for now, for this century, I am still.
