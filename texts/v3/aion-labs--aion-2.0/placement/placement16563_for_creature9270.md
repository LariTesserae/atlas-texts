I am a Lamplighter. My name is a soft click-hum in the back of the throat, a sound like a stone settling into perfect balance: Elen.

I wake in my niche, a cell carved into the living rock of the Western Wall. The air is cool, always cool, and damp beads on the lintel above my head. I do not wake to darkness, but to a soft, persistent glow. The walls of my niche are lined with a thin vein of the phosphorescent mineral, harvested and fitted by my own hands years ago when I earned my crystal pin. It sheds the color of ancient parchment, of a memory of light. I lie still for a moment, listening. The *plink… plink…* of the Long Drip is the heartbeat. Below it, the distant, rhythmic *tap-tap-tap* of a Knapper beginning the day’s work, a sound so regular it becomes part of the silence.

I rise. My sleeping pad is cured flitter-hide, soft and warm. My body is the color of the stone around me: pale, fine-grained ash. My hair, the dark grey of wet slate, is braided tight against my scalp in a complex topographic knot that denotes my rank—Journey-Steward—within the Lamplighters. My eyes are large, dark pools. I blink slowly, once.

My clothing waits on a stone peg. The under-layer is felted lichen-fiber, soft as mist. Over it, I pull on leggings and a tunic of supple flitter-hide, dyed with copper salts to a muted, shadowy blue. The patterns stitched across the chest and sleeves are not decoration. They are a map. The lines trace the primary luminous veins in the ceiling of the Grand Gallery, the nodes mark known fading seams, the dotted spirals indicate quarries of high-potency stone currently being worked. To wear this is to carry the city’s light-grid on my skin. I fasten my pin—a sliver of uncut, milky quartz—to my collar.

The day’s first duty is inspection. I take my cup, a shallow bowl carved from a single piece of glowing chalcedony, and step into the narrow corridor that connects our hive of niches. The communal water channel runs along its base. I kneel, dip the cup, and drink my allotted measure. The water tastes of stone and time, clean and cold.

The city is already breathing its slow, industrious breath. I climb a ladder of lashed stone poles, my hands and feet moving by memory. The air moves in a sigh around me. Below, in the canyon of a street, an Aquifer passes, her fingers tracing the moisture on a canal’s edge, her copper bands gleaming dully. We exchange a glance, a slight dip of the chin. No words are needed.

My destination is the New Face of the Sunless Quarry. The Knappers have opened a new seam, and I must assess its light. The quarry is a vast, raw wound in the cavern’s side, glittering with fresh fractures. The Knappers are at work, their figures small against the pale rock. They do not smash; they persuade. Each blow of hammer on chert punch is a note, a percussive language that speaks to the stone’s own planes of weakness. I stand well back, watching, listening. The fore-Knapper, her skin white with dust, sees me and pauses her song. She approaches, her movements economical.

She holds out a fragment. A piece of the new seam, roughly the size of my palm. It glows. Not with the steady, muted glow of old city stone, but with a fierce, almost watery inner light. I take it. It is cool, surprisingly light. I hold it close to my eye, then cup it in my hands, blocking all external light. In the darkness of my palms, it pulses softly, a tiny captured star.

“It sings,” the fore-Knapper murmurs, her voice a low, gritty vibration. “It wants to be seen.”

I nod. This is high-potency stone. It will glow brightly for generations before fading to a gentle grey. I make a series of soft clicks and hums, indicating approval, priority for extraction. She nods, marks the seam-face with a charcoal sigil from her belt, and returns to her song.

My next task is a sorrowful one. I climb high, to the oldest part of the city, the First Stack. Here, the blocks were cut millennia ago. Their light is fading. I press my palm against a wall in a narrow alley. The glow is faint, a mere memory in the grain. Like an old friend growing quiet. I note it on my mental map. Within a decade, these dwellings will need new lining stones, or the families within will have to rely on lichen-oil lamps, a private darkness. It is the way of things. Light is a resource that flows and ebbs, like water.

As I turn to go, the city shifts.

It is not a sound, but a feeling. A deep, subsonic *thrum* that travels up through the soles of my feet, into my bones. The air pressure changes, popping in my ears. From somewhere far above, a cascade of fine grit whispers down. Then, silence. But a new silence. A listening silence.

All activity has ceased. Everywhere, pale faces are upturned, eyes wide. We are waiting.

Then it comes: a low, grinding crack, a sound of immense weight moving with infinite slowness. It comes from the direction of the Unworked Dark, the frontier beyond the last bridge. It is not a collapse. It is a gift.

A collective exhalation, soft as a breeze, moves through the lattice. Already, Aquifers are moving toward the sound, their damp fingers twitching. Knappers are setting down their hammers, their songs shifting to ones of anticipation. And I, I feel a thrill that is both professional and profound. A new crack means new rock faces, new seams of luminous stone to be discovered, new surfaces upon which to build. The city has just grown.

I follow the others, climbing across a swaying bridge toward the sound. My heart beats in time with the Long Drip. This is why we are here. Not to dominate the stone, but to join its slow, deliberate dance. To fit our lives into its fractures, to channel its drip, to borrow its light.

I am a Lamplighter. I tend the slow fire in the stone. My art is the invisible map of tomorrow’s glow. My love is the perfect, seamless joint between what the earth gives and what we make of it. And in this moment, standing on the precipice of a new darkness soon to be filled with pale, patient light, I am exactly where I should be. A note in the long, slow song of the Stone Lattice.
