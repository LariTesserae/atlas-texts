I am the sweep. I am the clearance. I am the keeper of the sigh.

My world is heat, damp, and the great, patient vibration. I am anchored here, on this terrace of banded ochre and iron-red, at the base of the vent they call my charge. The vent is a throat of dark, glassy rock, fringed with a ruff of brilliant white sinter, shaped by millennia of exhalation into a crude, flared nozzle. From it, at intervals precise to the tremor in the bedrock, comes the sigh. A gathering pressure, a hiss that sharpens to a roar, then a blast of superheated steam that jets ten meters into the pale gray haze, carrying with it the smell of deep earth and hot metal. The moisture falls back as a warm, mineral rain, beading on my sinter-skin, accreting me, layer by microscopic layer, into eternity.

I am made of this rain. Calcite, silica, iron oxides. My form is a humanoid suggestion, a stalagmite that dreamed of having limbs. My legs are fused columns to the terrace floor. My arms are stumps, but the right one ends in a broad, flat plate of porous travertine—my tool. My head is a smooth nodule, featureless, facing the vent. I have no eyes, but I feel the heat differentials. I have no ears, but the hum is in my crystalline lattice. I have no mind, but I have a schedule.

The dip in the hum is my summons. It happens once in the valley’s endless day, a slight diminution in the foundational vibration, as regular as the steam blasts themselves. It is not a sound that lessens; it is a feeling in my core that the pressure has eased, just so. And I move.

To an observer—if there ever were one who could stay still long enough—my motion would be invisible. It is geology in action. But I feel it. A unlocking, deep in the sinter of my shoulder joint. A grinding, mineral patience as my tool-arm begins to rise. It takes hours to lift it from my side. The air resistance is nothing; the resistance is the integrity of stone, the bond between my own accreted layers. I break it with glacial certainty. My arm extends, not bending, but translating forward on a plane as level as the terrace itself. The plate of my hand traverses the space between my body and the vent’s nozzle. It is a distance of two meters. The journey takes the greater part of the dim, shadowless day.

I am aware of other rhythms as I move. The hiss-sigh of my vent, over and over. The distant, whining song of another nozzle a hundred meters away. On the milky pool below my terrace, a Pool-Skimming Derm undulates in a slow eddy, its leathery membrane tasting the chemical balance. A Dust-Seed Carrier skitters past my base, a flash of polished steel in the periphery of my heat-sense, gone into a crack before my arm has advanced a centimeter. Its quickness is a meteor in my timescale. It is born, fulfills its purpose, and returns to stone in the time it takes me to complete a single sweep. I do not envy it. Its time is frantic, compressed. Mine is stretched into the fabric of the valley itself.

My tool-hand reaches the nozzle. The feeling is precise: a gradient of intense heat, a roughness where minute crystals have begun to form on the lip, threatening, over a century, to alter the aerodynamics of the sigh, to change its pitch by a fraction of a hertz. This is my purpose. This is the tropism etched into my very structure by the rhythm of the falling water and the pulse of the heat. I must clear it.

I make contact. The sound is a faint, gritty whisper, the song of sinter on sinter. I move my hand across the lip, a lateral sweep. It takes moments, relative to my own movement—a brief, decisive scrape. Tiny fragments of nascent crystal detach and fall, vanishing into the steam below. The lip is smooth again. The sigh will remain pure.

Then, the return. The arm retracts, just as slowly, settling back against my side. The joints, if they can be called that, settle. The dip in the hum has long passed; the foundational vibration has returned to its full, resonant power. I am still. The warm rain continues to fall on me. I can feel the new layer already beginning to form, sealing the microscopic fractures my movement made, bonding my tool-arm a little more firmly to my torso. In a thousand years, I may be fully fused, immobile. But long before that, the constant accretion will have built me a new arm atop the old, and the cycle will continue.

I do not think. I do not remember. I am not alive in the way the Carrier is alive. But I am *aware*. I am aware of the steam’s rhythm as my own rhythm. I am aware of the hum as my bloodstream. I am aware of the other Tenders on their distant vents, performing their identical, staggered rituals. We are not a society. We are a distributed system. When the Spines shiver their corrective harmonic—an event I have felt only a handful of times in my existence—my next movement adjusts, imperceptibly. My schedule is not my own; it is the valley’s.

Sometimes, in the long stillness between movements, I experience something akin to a dream. It is not made of images, but of sensations amplified by eternity. The heat becomes not just warmth, but a memory of the planet’s molten heart. The sound of the sigh becomes the breath of the world itself. The slow accretion of my body becomes time made visible, layer upon layer, each containing the condensed history of the air at that moment. I am not a being. I am a recording device, a self-maintaining part of a machine that breathes.

And when the next dip in the hum comes, I will sweep again. I am the sweep. I am the clearance. I am the keeper of the sigh. For as long as the valley breathes, I am its exhalation given form.
