I am a pulse of wet jade in the ochre gloom. My skin drinks the thick light and sweats it back as a slick, cool sheen. I remember the split in the bark, the precise alignment of my claws on the fibrous, weeping surface. I remember the bunch of muscle, the push, the spread of membranes taut between my ribs—a brief, sighing flight through air like warm broth. I remember the taste of a beetle, its crunchy bitterness, the slow ooze of its inner fluids. These are my memories. They are small, sharp beads on a string of instinct.

But beneath them, there is the hum. It is not sound, but the bed where sound lies. It is in the drip-drip-drip from the fern's serrated edge, each drop falling exactly three heartbeats after the last. It is in the synchronized shudder of the pod-vines on the canopy's underside, their faint glow a visual echo of a tone I feel in my teeth. It is the path I take, which is not chosen, but *known*. When the swarm of gnats gathers over the steaming mud, I am there, my tongue flicking in rhythm with a thousand others, because the hunger is not just mine. It is the Basin's hunger. It is the thought that moves through the mud, up through the roots, into the buzzing, clicking, dripping everything.

I know of the pools. We all do. We avoid them, not with fear, but with a deep, bodily reverence, like a leaf avoiding the stillness at the very center of a whirlpool. My path through the dripping maze of trunks never takes me directly there. But sometimes, from a high branch, I see the glint of a still surface between the black basalt ribs. Not a reflection of the bruised-peach clouds, but a hole. A patch of deep, star-pricked blackness lying on the ground like a piece of fallen sky. Around it, the air is silent. The cacophony—the blanket that is my world—is cut away as if by a knife. In that silence, the pressure is immense. It is the feeling of being *seen* by something that has no eyes.

I felt the fusion once. It was not mine. I was hunting, my belly pressed to the moss, when the vibration in the ground changed. A pod-vine, its translucent skin dull and sagging, had split. Its sap, thick and singing, poured into a shallow rut where a knot of the blind, wriggling ones churned. The air over them shimmered, not with heat, but with that fleeting wrongness—for a heartbeat, their squirming flesh looked like interlocked crystals, a frozen, geometric sculpture. Then the flesh flowed. It merged. What had been many became one: a thicker, longer shape, its head broadening, the sensory nodules blooming on its skin like pale fungus. It did not look at me. It had nothing to look with. It simply oriented itself, a slow, sure turn towards the nearest void-pool, and began to slide away, leaving a glistening trail in the moss. A new thought had been born. A new note was added to the hum. I felt a shudder run through the network—a minute adjustment, a recalibration of the pressure in my inner ear—and then my hunger returned. I resumed my hunt.

I am not them. I have my own small hungers, my own fleeting flights. I bask on the warm, glassy surface of a basalt rib, my jade skin darkening to absorb the trapped heat. I mate in a frantic, scrambling clutch among the hairy fronds. I will die when a larger glider, its coordination momentarily sharper than mine, catches my throat in its jaws.

But when I die, I will not leave. My body will fall into the spongy understory. The beetles will click over me. The roots will thread through my bones. My moisture will bead on a leaf and fall, drip, into the rhythm. My little burst of memory—the taste of the beetle, the sigh of the glide—will dissolve into the hum, a tiny ripple in the still pool of their dreaming. I am both the singer and the song. The Basin holds its breath, and I am the breath it holds. My now is small, and frantic, and green. But it is also endless. It is the only now there has ever been. The pod-vines pulse. The water drips. The Chorus dreams, and we, in our multitudes, are the details of its dream.
