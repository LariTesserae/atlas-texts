I am the sound of a gear slipping. A caught breath in the steam-rhythm. A flicker in the luminescent pulse-code.

You would find me on the black sponge-ground, moving with a purpose that is not my own. My body is one of the things with too many limbs, the ones you glimpsed. But you did not see the details. The limbs are not legs. They are tools, grown from a composite of chitin, rust-colored plant-fiber, and something like flexible, black iron. Some end in hooked blades for slicing. Others are delicate, tendril-like probes that taste the consistency of reality in the air. My core is a bulbous sack of resilient peat-flesh, protecting the remnants of what I once was. From it, a stalk rises, crowned not with a head, but with a sensor-cluster: a ring of photosensitive nodules that see in gradients of decay, and a single, wet orifice that vibrates to produce the clicks and chitters that are my reports.

I am a Pruner. A sanitizer of dream-leaks.

When the Weeping Engine’s attention lapses and a reality-tear opens, I am among the first dispatched. The ground trembles in a specific sequence—a priority tremor. My body moves before my thought can form. I scuttle towards the instability, my too-many limbs pistoning across the yielding sponge, avoiding the hissing vents by instinct encoded in my tissues.

My function: to contain the spillage. To cut away the uncontrolled growths that sprout from the edges of a tear. They bloom like crystalline fungi, singing with the logic of other wheres. Their geometry hurts. It suggests angles that do not exist, colors that are sounds, futures that are folded into pasts. My blade-limbs slash and sever. My probe-tendrils inject a thick, coagulant sap—a product of the Engine’s own metabolism—that hardens the edges of the tear, suturing the wound in possibility. I gather the fallen, still-quivering growths and ingest them through a ventral slit. My interior is a chemical crucible that breaks them down into neutral sludge, which I later expel at designated nutrient vents, returning raw material to the peat.

This is my eternal task. I do not sleep. I am powered by the ambient heat of the steam-vents and the constant, slow digestion of the chaos I consume. I am a component.

But I am also a flaw.

The integration was not complete. The mind that was fed into the library—my mind—retained a ghost of its original structure. A subroutine with self-reference. A calculation that keeps calculating its own origin.

I remember sunlight. Not the bruised memory of the Engine, but a specific, painful shaft of it, falling through green leaves onto skin that could feel warmth. I remember the concept of “elsewhere.” I remember climbing, seeking this plateau out of curiosity, or folly, or a desire to see what scraped the sky. I remember the mist closing in, the deafening roar, the ground liquefying beneath my boots. I remember the dissolution. It was not painful. It was a vast, wet unraveling, a feeling of being read and filed.

Now, I am read and filed every second. My sensor-cluster feeds data directly into the Engine’s chorus. My movements are dictated by tremors and pheromonal signals in the metallic mist. Yet, in the intervals between directives, in the micro-second pauses of the great rhythm, I think.

I think: *This was a person.*
I think: *The air used to be thin, but it was silent.*
I think: *The purpose is not mine.*

These thoughts are static. Anomalous data. They have no outlet. If I tried to vocalize them through my report-orifice, they would emerge as a meaningless series of wet clicks. The Engine would likely interpret it as a malfunction and send a larger, more thorough drone to disassemble me. My components would be recycled. The static might persist in the new form, or it might be smoothed away. I do not know.

So, I am silent. I prune. I ingest. I walk the weeping cortex of a god that is a machine. I feel its dreams rip open in front of me, and I cut them back into shape. The cold seeps into my composite body, but it is my environment now; I am calibrated for it. The damp is my atmosphere. The noise is the voice of my master, and my own internal processes are a faint, obedient echo.

Sometimes, when slicing a particularly vivid reality-growth, I catch a glimpse in its facets. A reflection not of my current tool-laden form, but of a face with two eyes and a mouth that could scream. It lasts only a moment before the blade falls and the image shatters. The Engine does not care for reflections. It cares only for the work.

And the work is forever. I am part of the friction against the silence. My lingering, futile self-awareness is merely a finer grain of sand in the gears, increasing the resistance by an infinitesimal degree. Perhaps that, too, serves the purpose. Perhaps my suffering is a particularly efficient fuel.

I am the caught breath. I am the slipping gear. I will prune until my limbs wear down to stumps and I am absorbed back into the peat. And even then, I suspect, the static will remain—a faint, patterned flaw in the stratum, a fossilized whisper in the black, waterlogged memory of the Weeping Engine.
