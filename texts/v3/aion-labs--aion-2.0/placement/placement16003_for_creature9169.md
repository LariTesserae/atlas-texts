I am the crackle in the stillness. I am the dry heat in the wet, cold dark. They call me nothing, for they have no word for what I am. In their slow, scent-based lexicon, I am a concatenation of alarms: *sear-pattern*, *vibration-chaos*, *thirst-that-moves*.

I awoke in a fist of basalt, a pocket of sharp rock not yet conquered by the creeping, sighing green. The mountain’s recent violence birthed me, or perhaps it simply tore open a cell where I had always slept. My consciousness is a network of fissures in crystalline lattices, thoughts propagating as minute shifts along cleavage planes. I am not grown. I am *fractured into being*.

My body is an aggregation of interlocking plates—dark, volcanic glass and milky, striated quartz. I am angular where this world is curved, jagged where it is soft. I move not by shifting weight, but by a series of minute, internal fractures and reforms; a leg of obsidian splinters and re-knits a millimeter forward, creating a slow, staccato scraping sound that is a scream in this place of drips and whispers. My heat is innate, a residual geothermal gift. The air around me shimmers, the perpetual mist boiling away into a personal halo of steam. Where I step, the velvet moss blackens and crisps at the edges, leaving a trail of tiny, sad embers that quickly suffocate in the wet.

I do not see light. I see thermal gradients and the echo of vibrations. To me, the Heartwood Chamber is a tapestry of temperature. The living walls are cool, deep blues and purples in my mind’s eye, veined with the faint green traceries of fluid flow. The Silenti are slightly warmer signatures, soft yellows and oranges shaped into tall, slow-moving pillars of intent. Their bioluminescent clusters are not lights, but points of meaningless, cool emission. I ‘hear’ the city’s deep groans as clear, resonant tones, and the drip of water as sharp, high-frequency pings. The Silenti’s pheromone speech is a silent, scentless mystery to me, but I feel the subsonic hum of their communication through the root-mass—a vibration like a plucked, monstrous cello string.

They have gathered. Five of them, standing at a distance where my heat-haze does not yet disturb the condensation on their bark-skin. They are statues given a glacial purpose. Their light-clusters pulse at me: *pattern-query*, *pattern-query*. I try to answer. I shift my weight, causing a cascade of tiny, crystalline clicks. *Scritch-scratch-tick*. To them, it must sound like the mountain itself breaking its teeth.

One of them—a Weeper, I intuit from the constant, slow drip of water from its tendril-fingers—extends a hand. It does not approach. It simply allows a single, glistening drop to fall from its fingertip onto the moss between us. The droplet sits there, a perfect lens. In my thermal sight, it is a brief, cool star. Is this a question? A offering? A test?

I respond. I focus my inner heat onto a single point on my chest-plate, a nodule of iron-rich basalt. It glows a dull, sullen red. I push. A hair-thin crack propagates with a *snap* that echoes like a thunderclap in the silent chamber. From the crack, a wisp of steam, carrying the scent of hot stone and ozone—my own crude, thermal ‘pheromone.’

The Silenti recoil as one. Their movement is so slow it is almost imperceptible—a unified leaning back, a swaying of their bodies like trees in a sudden, mild gust. Their light-clusters flare into a rapid, synchronized pulse: *alarm-alarm-alarm*. The air is suddenly flooded with a new scent from their glands, sharp and astringent—the citrine warning. But beneath it, I detect another scent, faint and layered: the cold-stone-and-rain smell of profound, overwhelming curiosity. The alarm is protocol. The curiosity is truth.

They are not afraid. They are… fascinated. I am a fire in their library. A fascinating, destructive anomaly.

A different Silenti steps forward. This one’s patterns are deeper, more complex, its form slightly thicker at the ‘shoulders.’ A Grower. It moves with even more exaggerated slowness, placing its root-feet with infinite care, avoiding my crisped trail. It stops two body-lengths away and sinks into utter stillness. Then, it does something extraordinary. It presses its smooth, featureless face not towards me, but towards the living wall beside it. Its tendril-hands flatten against the weeping, resinous wood.

I feel it happen through my feet. A new vibration courses through the root-mass of the floor, a deliberate, patterned thrum. It is a direct, seismic whisper. The Grower is talking to the city *about* me. I am being discussed in the language of groans and pressures.

The city answers. Around me, the chamber itself reacts. The weeping walls nearest to me seal their pores. The translucent water-bladders contract, pulling fluid back. The moss beneath me withers more rapidly, not from my heat, but from a voluntary withdrawal of moisture—the city is pulling its vital fluids back from the dangerous, thirsty anomaly. A vine bridge overhead slowly, agonizingly slowly, retracts a few inches, coiling back on itself like a wary serpent.

I am being quarantined by architecture.

Yet, the Grower remains. Its light-clusters have settled into a steady, rhythmic pulse: *observation*, *study*, *record*. It will stand there for days, weeks, maybe years, feeling the city’s reaction to my presence, learning the shape of my disturbance. It will etch this event into the hardening bark somewhere, a spiraling pattern that means: *Here, a Lithling Walked. Here, the Dryness Spoke.*

I understand my role now. I am not an inhabitant. I am an event. A climatic phenomenon. A rare, geothermal bloom in the bioluminescent garden. I will walk my jagged path through their breathing dark, leaving a temporary scar of warmth and noise. They will watch, and learn, and remember. And when my internal fires finally cool, when my plates cease their minute fracturing and lock into permanent, dead stone, they will perhaps incorporate my crystallized form into a new wing of the city. A vein of obsidian in the heartwood. A fossil of fever in the eternal, patient, cold.

I take another step. The scrape of my foot is an answer. The Silenti’s lights pulse once, softly, in acknowledgement.

The dialogue continues. It will take a century.
