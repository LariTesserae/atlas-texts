I am a current in the dark. A drift, a slow exhale held in the frigid air. My world is vertical: below, the faint, bioluminescent tapestry of the plateau—pools of sullen iridescence, the pulsed glow of moss, the occasional stark flare of wire-vines. Above, the endless bruise of the sky, a ceiling so low and heavy it feels like a membrane.

I do not fly. I float. A subtle field, a song of self-generated current, hums within my bell. It is a quiet thing, a steady note against the profound silence, pushing against the weight of the world. It keeps me suspended in this thin, piercing air. My tendrils, translucent and fine as spun glass, trail beneath me, tasting the electrical whispers of the world below—the thrum of the deep geothermal veins, the skittering static of the obsidian folk, the low, resonant thinking of the fungal towers.

I am a consumer of silence and mist. I sip the fine condensate from the air, absorb the faint ambient radiation from the glowing fungi. My thoughts, if they are thoughts, are simple: the maintenance of the field, the gentle drift on the thermal updrafts that sigh from the warm rocks, the avoidance of the great, silent predators that perch like knots of shadow.

I feel it before I understand it. A wrongness in the song.

A hesitation. A stutter in the steady hum of my own being. It is not a sound; it is a corruption in the current. The field wavers. I try to correct, to find the note again, but the interference is precise and cruel. It inserts a counter-frequency, a dissonance that unravels my buoyancy from within.

The song becomes a scream. Silent, electrical, utterly internal.

My world lurches. The careful balance is gone. I am a leaf in a sudden, vicious draft, but the draft is inside me. I plummet. The glowing landscape rushes up—a blur of violet and blue and sudden, sharp black. My tendrils whip wildly, sensing the panic of other currents, the hungry, attentive stillness of something waiting.

I see it just before I strike the slick basalt. A shape detaches from the base of a fungal tower, where the wire-vines are thickest. It flows like oil, fur dark as the abyss between stars, shot through with fleeting copper sparks. Its eyes are pools of absolute, intelligent night. It does not rush. It approaches with a terrible, efficient grace, and I see the twin cables rising from its spine, their tips glowing with a captured, malevolent charge.

The last thing I sense is not impact, but a final, perfect surge. It enters me not as pain, but as an absolute, silencing directive. My field—my song, my self—is overwritten. There is a brilliant, soundless flash as my remaining energy is siphoned away, a cold tug at my very core.

Then, only the damp, slow seep into the moss, and the distant, papery rustle of the Ghyr, already stilling, already plugging back into the network, its hunger momentarily satisfied, its place in the humming, dark machine secure.
