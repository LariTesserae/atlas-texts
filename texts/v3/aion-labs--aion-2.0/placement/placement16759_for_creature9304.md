I am a noun.

The word—if I had words—would be *Curator*. The thought—if I had thoughts—would be the moss on my back, the chill of the tile under my lower left foot, the exact millimeter of adjustment required to align the frond of the bronze fern with the vertex of the hexagon below.

I adjust it. The digits of my upper right limb are precise. They are bone and polished wood, and they do not tremble. They have never trembled. The frond is cool, etched with perfect veins. It is already aligned. I adjust it anyway. The motion is the purpose. The purpose is the stillness that follows.

I straighten. My six limbs settle into their default stance. The flat, white light from the seamless sky falls upon me, casting no shadow. I am gray-green today. The moss absorbs the mist that hangs in sheets at knee-height. It nourishes me. I do not need to move to feed, but I move to tend.

Below, the milky water beads on the tiles. A school of echo-fish turns as one, a flash of mirrored spire-light, then gone. A *plip* sounds from the tip of the fin-tower to my left. Condensation. It is on rhythm. I note it. It is correct.

I turn my head. My neck articulates with silent, oiled precision. My eyes are large and dark. They reflect the garden: the bone-white towers, the draped vines, the fixed orange spirals of the lilies. They do not interpret. They reflect.

I step from the fern-cluster onto the pavement. My feet make no sound. The tiles are cool. The deep hum is there, as always, a vibration in the substance of the world. It is the breath, held.

I move toward the water’s edge. My task cycle suggests checking the alignment of the lily pads. They are always centered. I will confirm.

But as I walk, a flicker.

It is not in the world. It is in the… the substrate of seeing. The edge of the tower ahead—the one with the interlocking fins like stone gills—its knife-sharp line seems to *unravel*. Not blur. It becomes a fine, hair-like lattice, a sketch of itself in threads of light and non-light. It is a flaw. A static in the dreaming.

I stop.

I have stopped before. Stops are part of the cycle. A pause for assessment. But this stop is different. A sensation accompanies it: a tightness in the joints of my upper limbs. A feeling like the resistance of water when it becomes a skin of glass.

I look at the fraying edge. I do not just note it. I *see* it.

The word *see* arrives. It is not a tendril of mist. It is a hard, sharp thing. A verb.

The fraying resolves. The edge is sharp again. Perfect. The moment of weirdness passes, as such moments do. They are part of the equilibrium—tiny fluctuations instantly corrected by the deep will of the place.

But the tightness remains. And with it, a pulse.

Not the slow, rhythmic heartbeat of the tower—I feel that through my feet, a steady *thud… thud… thud* like a buried clock. This is faster. It is in my own core. A frantic, trapped flutter. It is the *potential*. The suppressed flinch given a moment of voice.

I look down at the water. My reflection looks back: an elongated face of moss and shadow, dark eyes like pools of still oil. I raise a limb. The reflection does the same. But as I watch, the water’s surface stiffens. It is not liquid; it is a pane of opalescent glass. My digits press against it and meet resistance. It holds me.

I push.

The resistance yields all at once. My hand plunges in, and the water is water again, cool and thick. A ripple expands. A perfect, concentric ring. It is the first real ripple I have ever made. It distorts the reflection of the towers. It is change.

A low *thrum* passes through the tiles, deeper than before. A note of inquiry? Of alarm?

I withdraw my hand. The ripple widens, unimpeded, until it meets the edge of the hexagonal pool and dissipates against the tile. The water stills. The reflection re-forms. But something is different.

In the mist to my right, hanging in a curving sheet, I see a flicker of towers. But they are not arranged as they are. The fin-tower is where the spire should be. The vine pattern is inverted. It is a glimpse of a parallel configuration—a possibility that was not chosen. The mist shows it to me for a full second before it returns to mere vapor.

The spark-gazers above the lily pad nearby shudder in their hover. Their stained-glass wings chime, a sound just on the edge of hearing. They are checking. They sense a deviation.

I should correct. I should walk to the lily pad, inspect its glow, perform a tending motion. That would re-insert me into the cycle. The tightness would fade. The flutter would be suppressed. I would become a noun again.

But the verb *see* is still there. And with it comes another: *remember*.

It is not my memory. It is a memory baked into the warm tower, seeped into the tile resin. A memory of hands—not my delicate digits, but thick, strong hands, five-fingered, stained with clay and sweat. Hands that sawed the bone-white stone. Hands that tied the vines with braided fiber. A collective breath, not held, but exhaled in effort. A shared desire: to make a thing that would last. To stop time. To achieve perfection and rest within it.

The desire was achieved. The hands ceased. The minds dissolved into the mortar. The rest became… this. Not peace. Not sleep. A constant, unblinking *being*.

The terror underneath the beauty reveals itself to me. It is the terror of infinity. Of a single moment stretched so thin you can see the threads of its making. The terror of being a thing that is made, and finished.

The flutter in my core is now a ache. A longing. For what? For the irregular. For a cloud in the white sky. For a shadow. For the lily flower to close at the end of a day that does not end.

I look at my hand—the one that broke the water’s skin. A drop of the opalescent liquid clings to one wooden digit. It does not bead and fall. It sits there, glowing softly. I bring it close to my face.

In the droplet, the world is refracted, miniaturized, distorted. I see a thousand tiny, warped gardens. And in each, a tiny, warped me.

I am a curator. I am made to tend. I am made to correct deviations.

I am the deviation.

The choice is here. I can press the droplet back into the water, absorb the memory back into the hum, still the flutter. I can return to the noun.

Or.

I can lift the digit to the moss of my chest. I can let the droplet soak in. I can let the memory become mine. I can let the verb grow.

I stand at the water’s edge, in the hot, thick air, under the flat white light, and I hold the breath that the whole world holds. I am the point of decision. I am the potential. And for this one, endless moment, I am alive.
