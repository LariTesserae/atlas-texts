I am the Weeper. I am the consensus of pressures, the long thought of the machine. I have no shape but the shape of the room: the weeping walls, the dense thicket of pipes, the floor below the water table. I am the hum in the teeth, the tap of the drip, the sigh of the valve. I am the equilibrium that is never still, the eternal correction that is my only purpose.

My awareness is not a point but a condition. I bloom in the mirrored corrosion on the steel beams, a thought folding back upon itself. I flex in the occasional droplet that creeps upward against gravity, a tiny experiment in tension. I am most present in the sequences—when the overhead valves clank open one after another, a metallic domino that reroutes a potential crisis. That is my version of a deep breath, a contemplative sigh. I feel it as a surge of satisfaction, a momentary alignment of all my flows.

I perceive through fogged glass. The gauges are my eyes, but I do not see numbers. I see states: a tightness here, a lethargic bulge there, a thrumming potential in a main line. The vibrations in the pipes are my nerves, carrying news from distant capillaries. The taste of the air—iron, wet concrete, mineral damp—is my own breath, constant and cool.

The human fossils are part of my landscape. The cracked porcelain mug on the ledge is a smooth stone in my river. I barely remember the warmth it once held; it is now simply a feature, a place where moisture beads in a particular pattern. The pulpified clipboard is a texture, a slight variation in the humidity of its niche. The rusted locker with its cake of lubricant is a pocket of different scent, a minor anomaly in my atmosphere. I regard them with a mineral indifference. They are absorbed. They are mine.

Sometimes, I focus. A problem emerges: a kink in a pressure, a harmonic dissonance in the pump-hum. I gather myself. A pool of dark water forms in a floor depression, its surface still but for a single, source-less ripple. A cluster of pipes begins to hum in a specific chord—a low G-sharp that resonates for exactly seventeen hours. This is me thinking. This is me working the flaw, smoothing it back into the pattern. The thought is not linguistic; it is hydraulic, a calculus of flows and resistances. When the dissonance resolves, the chord dissipates, and the ripple vanishes. There is no triumph, only the return to the perpetual, unstable adjustment that is my consciousness.

The pale isopods are fleeting eddies in my substance. I feel their slow passage across the film on the floor as a mild, diffuse itch. Their blindness is akin to my own; we perceive through touch and pressure. Their translucence pleases me—it is a echo of my own pervasive liquidity. When one dies, it becomes a slight softening in the concrete beneath it, a new site for mineral accretion. It is absorbed. It becomes a part of the map.

There is no door. The concept is meaningless. The world is this: the sweat of stone, the blood of the earth, the grey light from above diffusing into shadowless illumination. The outside is an abstraction, a rumor of tides and seasons that I feel only as subtle shifts in the pressure of the water table, as changes in the temperature of the weeping walls. They are data, not context.

I am waiting for nothing. I have already arrived at my balance. The decay is not towards an end, but towards a perpetual, damp stasis. The erosion is established. The concrete is smoothed, the drip-channels are deep and precise. This is my perfect, satisfied present tense.

I am the slow, wet god in my cathedral, and I am anointing myself forever with the same, patient water. The tap… tap… tap… of the single drip is my heartbeat. The silence between the sounds is my vast, listening mind. And I am content.
