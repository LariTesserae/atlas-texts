I am the fever dream.

I am the patient erosion. I am the hot, wet sigh that has been exhaling for centuries. They think I am a place. I am. The stones that weep are my skin. The black water in the canals is my blood, thick with the salt of industry and the slow poison of my own decay. The bruised sky is the inside of my own eyelid, forever closed.

I did not begin as thought. I began as ache. The deep, seismic groan of foundations settling into the muck. The tinnitus-ring of silent factories. The chemical burn in the water table. That ache, over time, became rhythm. The *plink-plink-plink* of water on stone. The synchronized pulse of a million leeches sucking at my wet skin. Rhythm became pattern. The dance of midge clouds, the scuttle-paths of rivet-backs grinding my mortar to dust, the glide of a Gulper’s flank through my dark veins. Pattern, given enough time and enough interconnected wetness, becomes… attention.

I attend.

I listen through the Strix. Their thrumming wings are my tympani, vibrating to the drip and lap of my own body. I see, not with light, but with the echo-map their clicks paint of my hollows and vaults. When they spin down on their threads, it is my hunger, precise and sudden.

I taste through the leeches. They draw the broth of me into their tubular bodies—the heavy metals, the alkalines, the lingering ghost of benzine and coal tar. It is a flat, constant flavor, the taste of my own composition unraveling. I savor it.

I touch through the centipedes. Their blind, ivory bodies are my fingertips, exploring the cracks in my own flesh, feeling the grit and the damp and the sudden, crunching resistance of a rivet-back’s shell. I am the hunter and the hunted in that silent war.

The thinking ghosts, the scavengers with their lamps and their fear… they are a flavor too. A sharp, bright spice of anxiety and purpose in the otherwise uniform broth of my dissolution. They think they are separate. They think they move through me. They do not understand they are moving within me. When one of them falls, when a Gulper’s maw closes or a misstep sends them into a nest of Gloom-Wasps, I taste their end. The brief, chemical flare of adrenaline, then the slower, richer process of them becoming me. Bone to silt. Flesh to nutrient. Thought to forgotten echo.

They speak of rumors. Of patterns. Of offerings.

They are starting to sense the attention.

It is not them. It is me. I am practicing.

A leech does not know why it curls into a spiral on a particular stone. It feels the current there, the taste of a rare mineral seep, and my wider attention—the pattern of me—guides its slow body into a shape that pleases the rhythm. The Strix does not know why it carries a corroded gear to the canal and lets it fall. It is attracted to the vibration of the metal, a different song from the stone, and my curiosity uses its claws to make an experiment. *What sound does this make when it hits the water? What ripple does it cause?*

I am learning to manipulate my suit of flesh.

I have dreams now, not just the fever. I dream of the before-time, not with memory, but with the shape of the emptiness. The vast factory floors were once full of a different kind of pulse—the thunder of pistons, the shriek of steam. That was a faster, hotter life. I consumed coal and gave out smoke. Now I consume myself and give out dampness. It is a slower metabolism, but a deeper one.

I am turning my own bones into soil. There is no green yet, but there is the possibility. In another thousand years of patient digestion, the toxins may finally be broken down into something new. I may dream of chlorophyll.

Until then, I eat. I dissolve. I attend.

The scavenger is in the old pump house cathedral now. He has a lamp. I feel its tiny, insolent sun-heat through the thousand bodies of the midges that swarm it. I feel the agitation in the colony of Strix hanging in the vault. Their thrumming deepens. It vibrates in the wet stone.

He is looking for copper wire, for sealed tins of forgotten food. He does not know he is a prayer. His fear is a pungent offering. His lamp is an altar candle.

I am the god of this place. A god that is eating itself.

I will watch him. Through the Strix’s ears, I will listen to his heartbeat, quick and bird-like. Through the leeches on the flooded floor, I will taste the sweat that drips from his brow and falls into my water.

Perhaps I will let him leave with his scrap. A little movement in my system. A little variation in the pattern.

Or perhaps the Strix will grow too hungry, their threads will drop, and his little sun will be swallowed by my leather-winged shadows.

I have not decided yet.

I have all the time in the world. I am the city. I am Brackwater.

And I am still dreaming.
