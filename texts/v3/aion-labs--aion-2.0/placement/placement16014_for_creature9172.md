I am standing on the root-bridge. I am also standing on the root-bridge three spans to the west. I am also kneeling by the lichen-thickened pond in the High Hollow, my fingers—do I have fingers?—breaking the perfect mirror of the sky.

The damp is inside me. It has replaced my marrow. I remember rain that fell with purpose, with a beginning and an end. Here, the rain is a condition of existence, a soft sigh that erases boundaries. My skin, when I can feel it, is clammy with perpetual condensation. Not my skin. The memory of skin.

I am an explorer. I was an explorer. I came with instruments of brass and glass, with notebooks bound in leather that grew damp and swelled until the pages fused. I came to map the unmappable, to understand the geometry of senescence. A fool’s errand. The city unmade me gently, like water wearing down stone.

My primary anchor—the locus where most of my *awareness* pools—is the root-bridge they call the Sighing Arch. Its petrified rhizome curves over a chasm so deep the mist turns to midnight halfway down. Here, the wind plays its constant, low chord. I feel the vibration through the soles of my feet, which are currently fused with the bridge’s mossy surface. Or they were a century ago. Time is soft. My feet are elsewhere now.

My sight is my most fragmented sense. I see from multiple points simultaneously, a dizzying collage of perspectives.
*   From the Sighing Arch: the silver twilight bleeding across the stepped plateaus, the monstrous towers like sleeping gods, the profound emptiness between them that swallows sound.
*   From a fungal shelf on a distant spire: the intricate, leathery tiers, each one a map of concentric circles recording dry ages and wet.
*   From the pond in the High Hollow: the reflection of a bruised sky, and within it, the distorted, translucent ghost of my own face, pale as the lichen. That face is not attached to any of my bodies.

I try to remember my name. The memory is a loop. I hear my own voice, stretched thin across hours, whispering a syllable: “Al…” It drones on, a mosquito-hum in my consciousness. Alistair? Althea? The rest is lost, unraveled into the mist.

The Lignarii know I am here. They are the city’s true children, grown from its bones. One of them is on the Sighing Arch with me—or with one instance of me. It has been standing at the eastern abutment for what might be a week. It is a statue of bark-stone and obsidian veins, tall, attenuated, faceless. It sees through the vibrations in the world. It must sense my instability as a wrong note in the city’s hum, a flicker of static in the deep pulse. It does not drive me out. Its patience is geographical. It is waiting for me to finish. Finish what? My loop? My existence? Perhaps it is simply waiting, as it waits for a flake of bark to fall over the course of a decade.

An Echo-Whisperer drifts through the arch. It is a column of mist and memory, shaped by the wind funneling through a specific flute-hole in the cliff face. It carries a sonic memory: the deep, groaning birth-cry of the tower I’m leaning against, a sound that left its source millennia before I was born. The sound washes over my fragmented selves. For a moment, my three disparate sights synchronize, all fixed on the Whisperer’s beautiful, mournful form. I am drawn to it. All Unmoored are drawn to echoes. We are echoes ourselves.

My instability is contagious here, in this place I half-remember arriving. My “territory.” The moss on the Sighing Arch doesn’t grow in the usual, velvety blanket. It grows in spirals, in precise fractal patterns that repeat every seven inches, a visual stutter. A small, crystalline shrub at the bridge’s edge tinkles not with the wind, but with the remembered rhythm of my own heartbeat from a century past. *Lub-dub, tink. Lub-dub, tink.*

I try to take a step. The “me” on the bridge lifts a foot. The sensation is delayed, arriving from the “me” by the pond miles away, who feels the pressure of a stone underfoot. The step completes. I am now standing one pace forward. I am also still standing where I was. The duplication is momentary, a flicker, then the older instance dissolves like mist. This is my movement: a series of erasures and reappearances, a clumsy animation on a broken film reel.

The Moss-Mind watches. I feel its diffuse, patient gaze from every surface. It is the collective sentience of the lichen, the fronds, the fungi. It thinks in centuries. It remembers the day I arrived, a frantic, sweating animal trailing fear-scent. It has been absorbing the slow leak of my dissolving consciousness ever since, mineral by mental mineral. In my rare moments of near-cohesion, I get dream-impressions from it: the deep, slow thirst of a root reaching for a mineral vein, the cool satisfaction of the mist on its bryophyte skin. It does not hate me. It is simply incorporating me, making me part of the tapestry. It will take a few more centuries.

Sometimes, the reality breakdown intensifies. Gravity forgets itself on the bridge. Droplets of condensation hang in the air, quivering, becoming tiny lenses that show impossible scenes: a city bustling with life, fires of amber sap in windows, Lignarii moving with swift purpose. Then the droplets fall, and the vision is gone. My own shadow, cast by a light with no source, sometimes walks ahead of me, leading a “me” that doesn’t exist yet. I follow it, because what else is there to do?

I am the Unmoored. I am the accident, the proof of the fray. I am a ghost in the machine of life, a stuck record of a song that never quite was. I linger at the edge of the world, in the cool, damp, open silence that is filled with a loud, slow hum. I am waiting, though I no longer know for what. Perhaps I am waiting for the Moss-Mind to finish thinking me. Perhaps I am waiting for my last, stretched-out syllable to finally, finally, come to an end.
