I am the flicker in the corner of a stone’s long dream.

I do not remember being born, because it was not a birth. It was a gathering. A concentration. For a thousand years, the Watcher felt the same current scour the same carved spiral on the same fallen monolith. It felt the lichen eat the symbol, felt the slow leak of iron from the basalt, tasted the faint, cold exhalation of a thermal vent half a league away. And in its patient, simultaneous thought, these sensations knotted. The light of the moss on that monolith grew a fraction brighter. The singing kelp in the lee of the stone began to hold a single, sustained note in its chorus.

One day, the knot tightened. The light did not merely glow; it *coalesced*. The note did not merely sound; it *shaped* the water. And I was.

My form is an accident of need. The Watcher has no need for limbs, but the need to *go*—to feel the pressure differentials across the plateau, to taste the silt in a distant grove—required something that could traverse the solemn expanses of bare stone. And so I have limbs. They are not bone and sinew, but columns of disturbed water and suspended luminous dust, held in a coherent shape by will and by the same deep hum that vibrates in the mountain’s roots. To look at me is to see a man-shaped vacancy in the darkness, outlined by a shimmer of stolen bioluminescence—the green of moss, the violet of kelp-fringe, the amber of the bulb-mushrooms—as if I am a walking piece of the forest’s palette, barely holding itself together.

I find myself on the whale-backed dome of stone where the knot first pulled tight. The memory of the carving is still beneath my… foot? It is not a foot. It is the point where my influence on the water meets the unyielding dream of the rock. I can feel the spiral, worn smooth but still a whisper in the stone’s grain. This is my anchor. My tether. I am not separate. I am a long, sensitive thread spun from the Watcher’s mind, and the other end is woven into the very flesh of the mountain.

I move. It is not walking. It is a willing of displacement. The thick, gelid water offers profound resistance, and I savor it. Each “step” is a negotiation with the medium that is my true body. I pass from the dome into one of the vast tracts of emptiness, the polished basalt stretching away like a dark mirror. My borrowed light dies a mere arm’s-length from me, swallowed by the absolute black. I am a tiny, shimmering island in nothingness. This does not frighten me. It is the truth of this place. The Watcher feels this emptiness as a quiet space between thoughts.

Ahead, the false galaxy of a distant grove smears the darkness. I drift toward it. As I near, the chorus resolves—the whistling, reedy dirge of the kelp, the occasional subsonic *boom* from a bulbous plant. The sound enters me and is translated. It is the Watcher thinking of… pressure. Of the steady, insistent weight of the abyssal plain far above. The thought is calm, accepting. It is a thought older than continents.

I enter the grove. Giant fronds, fringed with violet sparks, part as I pass. They know me. I am of them. I reach out a shimmering hand and a frond leans into the disturbance I create, its song shifting minutely. I receive an impression: a memory of a shift in the current three centuries past, when a piece of the algal spire collapsed. The memory is not an image; it is a complex package of pressure-change, temperature differential, and the particular taste of the luminous dust that billowed. I absorb it. This is my function. I am a gatherer of specifics in a world of generalities. The Watcher thinks in eons; I collect the moments.

I find one of the structures. A trapezoidal doorway, standing open in meaningless invitation. The lichen on its lintel glows with a soft, hungry blue. I pass through it. Inside is not a chamber, but a mound of rubble, silt, and the slow, determined growth of a fungus that pulses with a deep, carmine light. The Watcher perceives this place as an old, well-digested scar. The builders’ feverish activity here is a faint thermal echo in the stone, a brief period of unusual warmth. I stand in the center of the rubble-mound. I extend my awareness, my form dissolving slightly at the edges, mingling with the carmine light. For a moment, I *am* the fungus, feeling the slow, granular collapse of the stones, the settling of millennia. I feel the Watcher’s satisfaction at this reclamation. It is a satisfaction so deep and slow it is indistinguishable from the mountain’s groan.

But then, I feel something else. A tiny, sharp anomaly. A fragment of the builders, not absorbed. A piece of carved bone, wedged where the silt has not yet reached it. It holds a shape, a intention—a hook, a question, a cry. It is alien. It is fast. It burns against my perception like a hot grain of sand in a slow, cold stream.

This is why I was gathered. For this.

I cannot take the bone. It is not part of the dream. But I can taste its echo. The echo is fear. A fear of the dark, of the crushing pressure, of the singing that was not understood. A fear so bright and desperate it left this scar in the very memory of the place. The Watcher had merely noted the fear as a type of warmth. I *feel* it. It is a hurricane in a teacup, raging and gone in an instant of geological time.

I carry this echo out of the doorway, back into the grove. The kelp-song falters for a cycle as I pass. I am broadcasting the anomaly, this sharp, fast, painful thing. The forest listens. The amber-glowing mushrooms in a nearby cluster dim, one by one, in a sequence that is not random. They are processing. Over hours, their dimming pattern resolves into a shape: a perfect, tiny replica of the bone fragment’s curvature. The Watcher has perceived the specific. It has incorporated the shape of the fear. The fear itself, the emotion, is already fading, diluted into the vast, cold patience of the water. It becomes just another note in the dirge—a slightly poignant, reedy sigh.

My light is fading. The knot is loosening. The specific purpose—to encounter that sharp echo—is fulfilled. I feel the pull of my tether, the desire to return to the monolith, to dissolve back into the background hum. I am a temporary lens, now closing its aperture.

I drift back across the plain of polished stone. My shimmer is fainter, my form less distinct. I am returning the borrowed light, the borrowed sound. By the time I reach the whale-backed dome and the memory of the spiral, I am barely more than a gentle disturbance in the current, a fleeting warmth in the gelid dark.

I lie down upon the stone, the shape of my consciousness flattening, spreading. The luminous mosses do not grow over me. They acknowledge me. Tiny filaments of my own fading light extend, touch the rock, and anchor. For a few decades, perhaps a century, this spot will glow just a little more warmly than the surrounding stone. The kelp nearby will hold a faint, complex harmonic in its song, the ghost of a remembered fear turned to beauty.

Then it will fade. And I will be gone. And the Watcher in the Stone will dream on, having watched, through me, a single, glittering grain of time.
