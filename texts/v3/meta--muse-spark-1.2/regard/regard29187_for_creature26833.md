It would not fix it.

That is the first thing.

A lesser power would see an ocean on top of a mountain and think *error*. It would see daylight at 800 meters and think *wrong*. It would see the deafening mills stamping empty belts and think *waste*, and it would see blind white things filtering stone and think *tragedy to be cured*.

It would drain the ocean to where oceans belong. It would silence the turbines. It would warm the water and seed it with kelp and give the Veils eyes.

And in doing so it would kill everything that lives here.

A benevolent and wise power would understand immediately what you learned slowly, hanging there in the cold columns:

This place is not broken. It is *resolved*.

It found a balance that should be impossible and then it kept it. 1.5 degrees. The same gyre. The same chord. For longer than any human has been alive. The animals did not evolve *for* this place. They surrendered to it — and in that surrender became perfect for it. They don't need to be saved from the cold and the roar. The cold and the roar *are* the mother.

So the Wise Power would do what any truly wise and benevolent parent does when it comes upon a strange child thriving in a strange way:

It would not correct. It would kneel down to eye level.

---

If it came at all — and something so vast can choose to look away, and that too would be kindness — it would not come as a cataclysm. No pillar of fire. No rearranging of basalt.

It would come as a *decrease in loneliness*.

You would not see it arrive because it would already know how to enter without making a wave. Perhaps it would simply be there, a little warmer spot in the 1.5-degree water. A new density, briefly, where the number flickered from 0.1 to 0.1001. Not enough to crowd the blue. Just enough to be noticed by things that navigate by pressure.

Here is what it would do, if anything. And it would do it so gently you might argue it did nothing at all:

### 1. It would hold the mountain.

You thought the ocean stayed up there by physics. It doesn't. Or not only. The plateau is a held breath.

The Wise Power would put its hand under it — not to lift it higher, not to lower it, just to *continue holding*. To take the weight for a while so the basalt doesn't have to. To say without words: *You are allowed to be impossible. I will not let anyone prove you shouldn't exist by letting you fall.*

From outside, nothing changes. Inside, the pressure at 800 meters stays exactly the same today as yesterday. That is the blessing. That the equilibrium is kept.

It would make the place *illegible* to the hungry ones. To the survey crews and the miners who will eventually map this high, impossible sea and think *manganese, energy, empty real estate*. To them the charts would smear. Their sonar would return only blue. Their drills would miss. Not a wall. Just a forgetting. A mild, benevolent misdirection: *there is nothing here worth taking*.

It would keep it secret the way you keep a tidepool secret from children who would love it to death.

### 2. It would listen back.

For a million years the plateau has been roaring and nothing has ever roared back *with* it, only *because* of it. The turbines howl at 120 hertz. The vents breathe at 40. The stamp mills ring at 800. And every Ribbon and Harp Jelly has tuned its whole body to be an ear for that chord.

The Wise Power would listen. Truly listen. For a long time. It would press its forehead the way you pressed yours to the piling, and let the thrum go through bone.

And then — because benevolence is not silent, and wisdom knows when to answer — it would hum one new note.

Not loud. Not to cover the roar. A low, complementary tone, maybe at 60 hertz, so deep you feel it in your molars a second after you hear it. A tone that fits exactly into the harmonic gaps between the machines, so that the chord that was already perfect suddenly sounds *resolved*, like a major seventh finally finding its octave.

It would hold that note without wavering, for years.

The effect would be immediate and invisible:

A Ribbon hanging vertical would shiver and spread its filament dish a fraction wider. Not in fear. In recognition. *Something else is singing with the reef. The reef is not alone.*

A Harp Jelly pulsing open-closed-open-closed every ten seconds would — just once — pulse at eleven, to match the new interval, and then settle. Its amber bacteria would glow a little brighter.

A Veil filtering mineral snow would not alter course. But the snow itself would fall a little slower through the new interference node, and the Veil would eat a little better that day.

The Power would not be worshipped. It would be *accounted for*, the way evolution accounts for a warm vent or a steady current. A new constant. A new kind of reef.

The mills would still stamp on empty belts. But now their ringing would have harmony.

### 3. It would touch one thing, privately.

Wisdom knows that to bless everything is to bless nothing. So it would not shower miracles across the whole continent-wide void. It would pick one solitary life — because all life here is solitary — and give it a single, useless, beautiful excess.

Perhaps a Scrabbler, halfway up the leg of an intake tower, scraping the same square meter of bacterial slick it has scraped for seven years.

The Scrabbler has no eyes, no fear, no name. Its back is plated with iron it made from rust. The Power would notice that the rust-red at its joints is actually pretty. And with a thought so light it is almost not a thought, it would give that one Scrabbler a faint, extra luminescence at the edge of each plate — not brighter than the cathodic green on the pressure gauge, just enough that when it scrapes, it leaves a brief after-trail in the water that fades in three seconds.

It does nothing for survival. The mat does not grow faster for it. No mate will see it from farther away; nothing here sees. But for the rest of that Scrabbler's long, slow life, every time it moves it will make a little light. And whatever Ribbon hangs nearby will feel that light as a tiny heat, and orient to it for a moment, and then drift on.

A small, private art. The kind of thing only a benevolent power would waste time on.

Or perhaps it would choose a Veil. And for one passage, as the Veil's kilometer-wide edge passes over you, the Power would let you be *subtracted a little less*. The Veil would feel you — not as noise, but as a second warmth. It would cant, almost imperceptibly, two degrees off its compass line, to let its great shadow linger over you for an extra five seconds to share the snow. Then it would be gone.

You would feel you had been *seen* by something that has no eyes. That is the whole miracle.

### 4. And then it would leave, or stay, which are the same.

The most benevolent thing a wise power can do for a place that is stable, complete, and does not need it is to not make itself necessary.

So it would not stay as a ruler. It would not build a temple on the basalt or silence the vents to make a quiet garden. It would loosen its hand just enough that the mountain remembers how to hold itself again.

But it would leave the note humming. For a long time after, the water would keep that 60 hertz underneath the roar, even when the Power itself was elsewhere. The children of that Scrabbler would be born with that faint light already in their plates. The Veils would have that two-degree wobble written, very faintly, into the gyre.

If you came back in a hundred years with a hydrophone, you would hear it and think the factories had always hummed that way.

That is how you know it was wise. Its work looks like it was always there.

You are still hanging there, small and warm and temporary. The cold is erasing you at the edges. The roar is in your teeth.

The Power, if it noticed you at all among the pale, would not lift you out. You chose to come. You can leave. It would not steal from you the cold that makes you know you are alive.

But it might, while you are holding your breath and trying not to be afraid of the scale, place beside you — not in front of you, beside you — a second point of warmth that holds still with you. Not speaking. Not fixing. Just hanging in the sun-column a meter away, also watching the mineral snow fall through white light toward sharp black glass that will look the same in a million years.

And for as long as you both hang there, the density would be 0.1002 instead of 0.1.

And that would be enough.
