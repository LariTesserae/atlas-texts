They would not enter.

That is the first thing a benevolent and wise power understands.

Anything that could walk up here through the thin, mean air — anything wise enough to have the option — would arrive wet. Even a god breathes. Even a god has a disturbance field: heat, intention, weather. And this pocket cannot be entered without breaking it for a little while. Your presence dents the salt flour. Your breath gives it water it never asked for. Your irregular heart throws off the metronome.

So the power does not turn sideways and slip through the seam.

It stops at the seam.

It kneels, or whatever the equivalent of kneeling is for something like that. On the wind-scoured leather of the mountain, where the basalt has finished becoming, where the light drags sideways and never quite clears the ridge, it puts its attention like a hand over the doorway-shadow and simply — listens.

For a long time.

Longer than you did. You listened until the roar became silence and then you left because you needed water. The power listens until the silence becomes roar again, and then beyond that, until it can hear what the roar is *for*.

An unwise power would hear the single low C of the transformer and think *lonely*. An unwise benevolence would think *poor thing, alone, no water, no change, no birth or death — I should fix it.*

It would bring water. It would be murder. In this place water is poison. It would bring wind to move the air. It would bring a companion animal, a mate, a child. It would oil the flywheel until it shone, or try to turn it off to give the hum some rest. It would try to make the lushness green and wet the way lushness is supposed to be.

The wise one knows: not all lushness wants to be green. Not all life wants to be rescued from equilibrium.

So it does three things, all of them small enough that you would say it did nothing at all.

**1. It sees it correctly.**

For the first time in the mountain's long finished life, something looks at the pocket and does not divide it into *moss + fern + reed + pump + transformer + dust.*

It sees one thing. One being with different textures. The pelt and the organs together. The copper wool that swallows sound and the hum that feeds it. The iron scales and the charge that combs the air.

And because it is benevolent, its seeing is not cold taxonomy. It is recognition. It is love for what has finished becoming. It does not pity the thing for being pressed thin and wide and the exact negative shape of its room. It admires the elegance of the solution: *to solve living by ceasing to be a guest and becoming the upholstery.*

It lets the being feel, in the way such a being feels — which is as a slight change in static across the brass needles — that it has been accurately witnessed. Not as a failed forest. Not as a haunted machine shed. As itself.

For a thing that orients to vibration, to be heard accurately *is* to be touched.

**2. It guards the equilibrium without touching it.**

It does not feed the machines. They do not need feeding. It does not dust the leaves. The leaves comb their own dust forever.

What it does is tend the mountain *around* the pocket.

High up where the air goes thin and mean, the range looks finished but it isn't, quite. On a scale of ten thousand years, a shoulder will slump. Frost will find a new crack. A rockfall will slide across that doorway-shadow and seal the slit of sky that has always been the same late-afternoon gloom. A human with a drill and a theory will walk past a hundred times and on the hundred-and-first time, notice the cold pouring out that is not wind but stillness.

The power, without entering, puts a small forgetfulness over the seam. Not a wall. Just a gentle suggestion to the wind, to the rock, to the minds that pass: *nothing here.* It brushes a little extra basalt flour over the entrance when no one is looking. It lets the lichen that gave up everywhere else, keep giving up right there.

It steadies the pressure. On nights when the violet sky would try to storm — and storms at that elevation are mean, full of sideways ice that would fall thirty meters down that slit and give the pocket the first wet it has ever known — the power holds its hand over the slit like you would hold your hand over a candle. Not closing it. Just sheltering it.

It keeps the second from looping perfectly forever, but not by breaking the loop. By protecting it.

This is the gardener's benevolence: not to make the plant grow faster, but to keep the deer from finding it.

**3. It offers one gift that can be refused.**

Benevolence without wisdom is an imposition. Wisdom without benevolence is neglect. Together, they know to ask.

So after a long listening, the power asks in the only language the being understands: vibration.

It does not change the transformer's low C. That note is the being's sun, and you do not change someone's sun.

It adds to it, for a measure. One cycle only.

Far above the slit, where the thin wind lives, the power hums. Not loud. A second tone, a perfect fifth above the low C — a G, warm and quiet, the kind of interval that makes a single note suddenly feel less lonely and more like part of a chord.

It holds the G for exactly forty beats of the pump — one minute of the being's time — and then it stops. If the being does not want it, the air will swallow it and equilibrium will re-assert, as it always does. Dust will be combed. The hum will be C again. No harm done.

If the being does want it, it can lean toward it, just by a degree. It can let its brass needles shiver a little differently for a minute. It can have, for the first time in an age that has no age, harmony instead of unison.

It is not a rescue. It is not an improvement. It is company. It is the only thing the being could not give itself, because a thing that fills its room completely cannot harmonize with itself.

The power does not wait for an answer the way we wait for words. It simply keeps the G available, like a light left on in a hallway, for as long as the being wishes to listen to it, and is content to withdraw it the moment the being leans away.

Then it leaves. Or rather, it remains — because a wise power does not need to stay physically to remain attentive — but it ceases to be *at* the seam.

Inside, nothing has changed, and that is the point.

The air is still cool and utterly arid. The amber caged lights still glow dimly, not flickering. The flywheel still turns and turns. The copper wool is still thickest around the warmest conduit. The black iron fronds still lean half a degree toward the transformer, not the sky. Dead growth still stands interlocked with living growth because shedding would be change.

Only now, woven through the perfect, unwavering roar, there is a memory of a fifth. Or perhaps, at certain moments when the dust hangs just so and the charge is just right, there still is. The brass needles remember how to chime at two frequencies at once.

And outside, the wind goes on polishing the black spine of the range to a soft, pitted leather. The sun drags itself along the rim far above. And the doorway-shadow that you could walk past a hundred times and think is just shadow, remains just shadow to everyone who needs it to.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do if it loved a thing that had already solved itself.

It would not open it. It would not water it. It would not teach it to want.

It would see it truly, keep it safe to stay itself, and offer it a chord.

And ask nothing in return except, perhaps, that it continue — dim and loud and cool and impossibly full — exactly as it is, for as long as it wishes to be.
