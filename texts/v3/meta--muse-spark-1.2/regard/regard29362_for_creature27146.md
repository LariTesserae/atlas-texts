They would do almost nothing.

That is the first thing a wise and benevolent power learns to do. To not arrive.

Because anything that could see this place from far away - and see *her* - would know immediately what it was seeing: a perfection that is perfect only because it is on the edge of impossible. A bargain held together by thin air and exact restraint. The bowl catches only because it is empty. The mat glows only because it is starved for light and has learned to save it. She lives only because she has learned to make herself as small and as hollow and as patient as the bowl that holds her.

A clumsy power would come down like a second sun. It would make the air thicker so she could breathe easy. It would make a spring well up in the center and fill the basin. It would build her a real house with a real roof and tell her she no longer has to crawl on her knees. And in a season, all of it would be dead. The moss would rot. The spiral would be drowned. The pikas would leave. She would leave, or become someone else entirely.

So the wise one does not come down at all.

It stops on the far side of thin air and looks. And for a long time, just looks - the way you have to look at this bowl to see it at all.

It would notice her first, not the bowl. Or notice the bowl *through* her. From a great height, the green spiral would be just anomaly enough to catch an eye that is used to catching anomalies. But what would hold that eye is the other pattern laid over it: the daily circle, clockwise, knee-worn, hand-tended. The only animal up here that moves with that kind of deliberate, non-hungry geometry. The power would watch for a day, then another, and understand: this is not a woman living *in* a place. This is a place that has grown a woman the way it grows saxifrage - as an organ for tending itself.

And having understood, it would be moved the way the benevolent are always moved - not to pity, but to reverence. And reverence does not intervene. It attends.

What it would do, then, would be small. So small she would not be sure anything had been done at all. So small the bowl itself would barely register it, except as a good year.

**1. It would hold the rim.**

Not build it higher. Just hold it.

The world below is beginning to look up. There are eyes now that can see a green coin on a mountaintop from orbit. There are boots that can get anywhere, and drills that want the fresh, unweathered stone because it hasn't had time to hide its metals. There are other hungers.

The power would not put a dome over the bowl. It would not strike blind the climber who finds it. It would just make the bowl slightly uninteresting.

A little more wind on the approach ridge, a little more whiteout on the wrong afternoon. A sense, to the survey plane, that this summit is less stable than the next one over. A map error that puts the old trail half a kilometer to the east, where the scree is looser and people turn back. Not a wall. A forgetting. The bowl has stayed empty because people have had somewhere else to be. The kindest thing is to keep giving them somewhere else to be, for a little longer.

**2. It would tend the catching.**

It would not make more water. More would kill it. But it would make the catching more sure.

That southern gap where the cloud dies - it would keep that gap true. When the larger climate stutters and the afternoon cloud comes thinner or later, the power would coax it, just a breath. A nudge of pressure, a cooler stone. Enough that the rime still feathers thick on the quartz at night. Enough that the three-stone cairn still drips its cupful at ten. Enough that the moss still holds light after sunset.

It would do the same for her breath. Not give her new lungs. Just let each deliberate inhalation count for a little more. Let the thin, bright air give up its oxygen a fraction more willingly, so the pause between inhale and exhale does not have to be quite so long, so her heart does not have to hammer quite so hard when she darts. Let the sun hammer a little less on the afternoons when she lies flat and offers her dark, polished skin to it. Filter it, just one percent.

You would not see it. She would only feel it as an easing she would attribute to the weather. *Good cloud this week. Good sun. My knees did not hurt until evening.*

**3. It would ease what does not have to hurt.**

This is the benevolent part, and the hardest to get right.

It would not take away the cold. The cold is the bowl. It would not take away the solitude. The solitude is her shape. It would not cure age, or make her young, or make her stay forever. She knows she must go down before winter and that one year she will not come back up. To rob her of that going would be to rob her of the way she belongs to the place - lightly, seasonally, like the finches.

But it would take the *extra* pain. The grinding in the left knee where cartilage has worn to nothing from years of clockwise circling. It would lay a little cushion there, not new tissue, just a quieting of the nerve on the coldest mornings so she can still kneel to move that saxifrage dome. The frost-nip scar on her cheek that never quite healed - it would keep it from cracking this year. The loneliness that sometimes pools at dusk when the stones tick and the pikas fall silent - not remove it, but sit with it, so that it feels less like loneliness and more like the quiet that the bowl itself holds.

If it could, it would give her a longer autumn. Two more days before the rime stops melting at noon. Two more days of the bees droning heavy and improbable. Two more days for her to pull the windbreak stones down flat with care instead of hurry, to turn the quartz cap sideways, to scatter the ash, to walk down the spine slowly, breathing deliberately, without fear of being caught by storm.

And it would leave a small, physical kindness where she will find it without knowing it was left.

Not a tool. She has the tools she needs. Not food - the finches need the seeds more.

Maybe a single, perfect stone for the windbreak. One she has been looking for without knowing it: the right height, the right flat face, basalt that fits her hand as if it had been pried for her. She will find it tumbled right beside the southern gap on the morning she rebuilds the wall, and she will think, *good, this one.*

Maybe a quartz cap that is just a little clearer, so it rimes thicker. She will think the old one had been washed clean by the melt.

Maybe, in the year when her hands are finally too stiff to make the spiral true, a second set of hands. Not a person sent to replace her. A young one, from the valley, who got lost on the ridge and followed the strange green glow one evening and found the bowl and found her still there, tending. And who, instead of feeling the contempt or the urge to rescue that lowlanders often feel for such a life, felt the quiet *click* of a shape fitting a hollow. The power would not force that. It would just make the path visible for one day, and then leave the choosing to both of them. To see if the bowl wants to grow another gardener.

Mostly, though, the power would do what she does.

It would make itself small. Hollower. A trap for what is scarce.

It would not speak. The bowl eats sound. To speak would be to fill it.

It would crouch, somewhere just beyond the rim where the wind still scours, where she will not see it because she never looks beyond the rim - and it would breathe with her. In, hold, out, hold. Deliberately. Letting the thin, bright air be insufficient together.

And in that shared, insufficient breath, it would love the place without needing to improve it. It would keep the light lingering a minute longer at dusk, not because the moss needs it, but because the lingering itself is beautiful and deserves to be witnessed by something that can witness it without disturbing it.

When she goes down at last for the winter, and the bowl is empty again except for the pikas haying and the beetles frozen under stones and the spiral faintly glowing and fading, the power would remain a little while longer, holding the quiet.

Not as an owner. As a second, larger bowl around the first.

Catching, and holding, and being, for a little while, exactly the shape of what holds her.
