It would not come like you expect a Power to come.

No opening in the haze. No column of clearer light. No voice that could be heard over the chord.

It noticed the mesa precisely because it is almost impossible to notice. Not bright, not loud in the way powers notice loudness, not empty and crying out. Just - final. Complete. A small, warm, saturated knot of made-thing that had finished becoming itself and then stayed.

And inside that knot, the small, brick-colored lives who had finished becoming themselves to fit it.

So first, it listened.

That is the whole ethic of a wise benevolence, before any doing: to listen long enough that you stop translating.

It did not hover above and pity the heat, the dryness, the roar. Pity is a mistranslation. If you listened with human ears, you would hear torture: *deafening, parched, cramped, blind, starving on dust.* If you listened with brick-ears, with skin that drinks salt, you hear something else: *warmth without burning, fullness without falling, a single note held so long it has become silence, a fitting so exact there is no chafing left.*

A human would beg for rain here. For quiet. For space.

They would die of any of those things.

Rain would dissolve the fat-mortar that holds the town together. It would wash the pale-yellow crust from the lip and with it their only cistern. It would swell the iron-hard black beams until they split, it would turn brick back to mud, it would rotting. Quiet would deafen them more truly than the roar ever did - their whorled pits are tuned to pressure, to the topography of that endless chord. Take it away and they are blind in two senses. Space would unmake them. Give them a street and their spatulate, print-fitted hands would have nothing to hold.

So the Power understood its first temptation and set it aside.

*I could lift the haze. I could bring cloud. I could cool the kiln. I could widen the mesa by another hundred paces and give them fields.*

And in the same breath: *and then they would not be themselves.*

Wisdom is not the same as restraint, but here it looks like restraint. Benevolence is not the same as softening, but here it looks like leaving the hardness intact.

What it did instead was make itself small.

Powers are good at being vast. This one practiced being *exactly* shoulder-wide, exactly courtyard-sized, exactly warm. It did not stand at the cliff edge and look down. It knelt - or the equivalent of kneeling for something without knees - and found a crack between houses that was not an alley but a crack, and it fitted itself into it.

Not into a body. Into a place. For a while it *was* a warm brick that gave slightly under the palm, a little more compliant than the others, holding the same kiln-heat.

And then it pressed its forehead to the wall, as they do when they need to speak.

It did not speak in words. Words would have been a gust. It spoke in tremor.

This is what it said, in the language the mesa taught it:

*I feel you.*

Not *I see you* - they have scaled-over eyes. Not *I hear you* - they are the hearing itself. *I feel you.* The only honest greeting where everything is touch and tremor.

And after a long, long breath - one of their minute-long breaths - it felt an answer come back through the continuous mass. Not from one of them, from the town itself, because they are the town.

A faint, puzzled adjustment in the chord. The feeling of many small backs shifting a fraction, not in alarm, but in acknowledgement. *Something new is here and it is warm and it is not pushing.*

That was consent enough to continue, but not consent to change.

So the Power did three very small things. Small the way a thumbprint in wet brick is small - almost nothing, and then forever.

**1. It did not stop the wind. It made a hollow in it.**

For one courtyard, no larger than a sleeping mat, for the length of three of their slow breaths, it cupped its attention and the roar thinned.

Not into silence. Silence would have been annihilation, a sudden vacuum. It thinned into something they had never known: a *quieter* chord. The low drone dropped half a tone, the high shredding softened, and for the first time in ten thousand years, the tremor through the brick revealed a second layer underneath it.

Their own hearts.

They could feel, in that courtyard where three were fitted like honeycomb, the faint thud of the blood-thick, salty blood moving. The tiny click of a spatulate hand uncurling. In the other courtyards, those pressed nearest to the edge of the hollow felt it too and went still - not with fear, but with a kind of wonder that had no word because there had never been a use for the word *quiet*.

Then the Power let the wind back in, exactly as it was. The chord resumed. No brick had cooled. No salt had been washed. But now every being on the mesa knew there was a texture *under* the roar, the way you know after someone lifts a hand from your eyes that you had been seeing palm-lines.

It gave them a rest without giving them an ending. A power that loved them would not take their lullaby away, but it could let them hear that they were singing it.

**2. It left a gift that asked for nothing.**

At the very lip, where the fused outer bricks meet the sheer, where the white and pale-yellow crust already furs the stone, it left a new stone.

Not large. The size of a storage jar, porous, matte, the color of cooled ash. Cooler than any other brick by perhaps two degrees - just enough that at the briefest, coldest edge of the almost-night, the warm air would bead on it more readily than on the salt.

It did not pipe water to them. It did not make a spring, which would have been a flood. It made a *better* cistern that worked exactly like their old one, only a little more generously. A cheek pressed to it would find a bead where before there would have been none. One extra bead a day. No more.

And because it was porous, it sang differently when the wind passed over its pits. A slightly lower flute-note in the endless chord. A new instrument in the town. Not louder, not to cover the others - just a new tone they could choose to listen for, or not.

It placed it where there was already a little unclaimed place - a chipped corner where wind-blown grit had sanded a lintel thin. No wall had to be torn down. No one had to move. If they wanted it, they would find it the way they find everything: by feeling the tremor change. If they didn't, the wind would eventually sand it smooth and it would become indistinguishable.

A wise gift does not obligate. It waits to be discovered.

**3. It promised to remember and to guard.**

This was the largest thing, and the least visible.

The mesa is final, but not invulnerable. A human eye, seeing that flat, warm table, might think: *good place for a tower, a mine, a road.* A lesser power might think: *good place to test a storm.*

So the Power did what benevolent, wise things do when they love something that does not need saving: it turned its attention outward and became, for a while, a kind of weather itself.

Not a wall. A misdirection. A forgetting. Anyone whose heart came looking to use that height would find their map quietly misfolded, their instrument showing a slightly different elevation, their desire cooling for reasons they would attribute to practicality. The haze would look a little thicker to satellites. The wind at the cliff would sound - to outside ears - a little more purely hostile than it is.

It did not hide the mesa. Hiding is a kind of imprisonment. It just made it *uninteresting* to hunger.

And it promised - in that same tremor-language, pressed through brick - that it would remember the exact pitch of this chord. That even if, in some far future, erosion finally remembered how to eat the mesa, or the kiln finally cooled, the pattern of those small, brick-colored lives fitted so exactly into their made-world would not be lost as if it had never been. That is not afterlife. It is not rescue. It is witness. For a people who have no writing because there is no room for tablets and no water for ink, to be witnessed is a kind of continuity they could not make for themselves.

Then it lifted its forehead from the wall.

It did not stay fitted in the crack. To stay would have been to take up room where there was no room, and completeness was their holiness. It thinned itself back out - not leaving upward, but *outward*, diffusing its warmth back into the warm air, its pressure back into the pressure.

The light stayed dim amber. The heat stayed kiln-warm. The deafening chord held, total, braided, without gust or lull. The hive of shoulder-tight rooms stayed saturated, wall sharing wall sharing wall to the softened basalt edge.

If you came now, you would think nothing had changed.

But if you pressed your palm to the brick, as the Power had, you would feel - very faintly - that one new tone in the drone, the slightly lower flute. And if you watched long enough, you would see a small, slow procession - not hurried, never hurried - of matte red-brown shoulders flowing on spatulate hands toward the lip, toward the ash-colored stone that beads a little more.

They do not queue. There is no need. One presses a cheek, takes the bead, and flows away to make room for the next. No more than before, just a little more surely.

And in the courtyard where the hollow had been, those three who had heard their hearts continue to press their foreheads together sometimes, at intervals, to feel that quieter layer again in memory, even though the wind is fully back. They have added it to the chord without changing the chord. They have, in their slow, curing way, made a new thumbprint.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a place that has already become what it was meant to become, and a people who have already become what they needed to become to live there.

It does not fix.

It fits, for a moment, without displacing.

It offers a little more of what they already love, in a form they can already use, and the choice not to use it.

And it stays long enough to learn their song, so that even when the wind finally does stop - as all winds one day do - someone, somewhere, will still know how it went.
