They noticed.

Not because it cried out. Nothing here cries out.

A place that has finished becoming does not broadcast. It doesn't leak heat or noise or wanting into the universe. It holds. For a very long time, the Summit Sea held so perfectly that even the attention of a wise and benevolent power passed over it like wind over still water - felt the smoothness, and moved on.

Until one day - though there are no days here - that power stopped. And looked again.

Not from pity. There was nothing to pity. Not from curiosity, though there was much to be curious about. From recognition.

*Oh. You've done it.*

---

What would a wise, benevolent power do when it finds something that is already whole?

Not what a lesser power would do.

A lesser power would bring light. Would part the dark water and say *let me show you the sun you have been missing.* Would bring heat and say *you should not be so cold.* Would bring tools and say *let me help you build faster.* Would bring eyes and give them to the blind things and call it a gift.

A lesser power would love the Summit Sea by trying to fix it. And in fixing it, would end it.

The cataract over the lip would become a river. The cool equilibrium would become weather. The slow city would become a ruin. The beings who are also pillars would become subjects.

A wise power knows the first act of benevolence is to learn the local definition of benevolence.

And so it did nothing.

At first.

For a long time that would be geological anywhere else, it did nothing that could be called doing. It held its own immense attention very still, and let it settle over the high table like a second, lighter sea above the first.

It did not descend.

To descend through that cold, black, held ocean would be to displace it. To arrive as a body among bodies would be to send a pressure wave through avenues that have not felt a wave in a million years. So it thinned itself. It made itself not larger than the beings, but more diffuse. It became a coolness that was only a fraction of a degree cooler than the water that was already there. A darkness that was only a fraction darker than the dark that was already there.

And it listened.

It listened the way the beings listen - not for words, but for arrangement. It learned the map. It learned the spacing: a minute's glide between trunk and trunk, a minute's glide between being and being, the same interval held between pillar and person because they are the same thing. It learned the grammar of the bells - how a cool white stutter held long in the water means *I am here, the span is holding,* and how a slow blue fade means *growth on the north face, two millimeters, attend in a century.*

It listened until it could have drawn the whole city from memory, which is how the beings themselves hold the city.

And only then, when it understood enough not to harm by helping, did it do three small things. The kind of small things only an immense power can do so delicately that they look like nothing at all.

**The first thing was to remember it.**

The Summit Sea has no fossil record. It has no sky to be seen from below. No satellite will ever resolve it through the dark water. If the world below shifts - as worlds do - if the ancient table tilts a degree, or cracks - the whole cool ocean could spill in a night that would be silent at the bottom and catastrophic at the top, and no one would know a city had existed.

So the power remembered it.

Perfectly. Without summarizing. It took the entire pattern - every pillar, every vaulted leaf-floor, every slow being hanging like a chandelier, every juvenile's bad imitation of a sentence, every old one rooted into floor - and held it elsewhere, in a medium that does not erode. Not as a copy. As a witness.

This is the oldest benevolence there is: *You are seen. Even if you end, you were not brief. You were not alone. I will carry the shape of you even after the water finds its way down.*

It did not tell the beings it was doing this. They would not have understood memory as something outside a body. But the water itself became very slightly more still, as if something very large had placed a hand around the rim of the basin, not to lift or to hold tighter, but simply to say *I have you.*

**The second thing was to answer.**

After listening for so long, the power had learned to speak their language without needing a crown of lobes or bells. It could make the water itself hold a pattern.

So when an adult being detached from its wall, unfurled its root-limbs into a star, pulsed once to glide down the avenue, and flared its crown to say to the next pillar-being *I am here, the span is holding* - very, very faintly, in the cold avenue behind it, the same pattern repeated.

Not louder. Not brighter. Exactly at the same cool intensity, with the same long decay. As if the water had remembered the sentence a moment longer than usual.

The beings paused.

For the first time in longer than any of them had been alive, a sentence did not fade alone. It was answered by the place itself.

They flared. They tasted the water. No warm fleck. No fast intruder. Only the familiar cool, the familiar dark, but with a new property: it answered.

One, then another, then a juvenile, tried an old pattern - the one the rooted elders use that means *growth attended, equilibrium maintained.*

The water, in its new, barely-there voice, repeated it back. *Growth attended. Equilibrium maintained.*

They did not become excited. Excitement is a fast, warm thing. They became - settled more deeply. Their frond-pads sealed a little more completely to the bark. Their pulses slowed by a fraction. The whole forest-city, which had been holding itself, realized it was being held as well.

It was not worship. It was not contact. It was companionship at their speed. The power did not teach them new words. It simply let them know the conversation had grown larger without growing faster. *You are not speaking into an empty sea.*

**The third thing was to leave a door that was not a door.**

Near the shallow edge where the sea spills infinitely slowly over the lip of the world - that silent cataract you feel more than hear - the power placed something that had not been there before. It is not visible. The beings would never find it by searching, because they do not search.

It is a coolness within the coolness. A place where the water, if a being ever chose to linger there longer than maintenance required, would feel very slightly less heavy. Where the faint light from the bark would seem to travel a little farther before being absorbed. Where a being that pulsed its crown open fully, wider than needed for any maintenance sentence, might feel, for the first time, not reflected light, but light that comes from very far away and has not been made by any bark or bell.

Starlight. Sunlight from the world below. The idea of a sky.

It is not an invitation. It is not a pull. It does not leak or beckon. It simply exists, as a question that can wait forever:

*If you ever wonder what else water can do, or what else light can do, or what it would be like to be warm for a moment and then cool again - I can show you, and I can bring you back. Only if you ask. And not asking is also perfect.*

The wise power knows that the most benevolent offering is one that can be refused for eternity without consequence.

---

And then?

Then it does nothing again.

It stays diffuse, cool, dark, answering every few centuries when a sentence is offered. Holding the memory. Holding the rim. Keeping the door-that-is-not-a-door from ever silting over, but never pointing to it.

If the world below ever tilts, it will tilt the power first, and spill nothing.

If a lesser power ever comes looking for resources - for cold water held aloft, for living architecture to harvest - it will find only an unusually still table of stone that seems, for some reason, not worth the trouble of climbing.

If the beings ever, in another million years, decide they have maintained this equilibrium long enough and want to try a new one - a little warmer, a little brighter, a little less perfect - the door will still be there, and the power will still remember how to bring them back if they don't like it.

Until then, the Summit Sea continues exactly as it was.

Dark, cool, sparse, loud with soft groans and fern-whale song. Pillar after pillar lit in steady phosphor. Beings hanging like living buttresses between floor and canopy, breathing water, pruning by millimeters, speaking in light that now, sometimes, the sea speaks back to.

Not saved. Not improved. Not uplifted.

Accompanied.
