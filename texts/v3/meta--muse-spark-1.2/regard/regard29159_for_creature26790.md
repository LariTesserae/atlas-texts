It would not come down.

That is the first thing a benevolent and wise power understands. To come down the way powers usually come — as light, as fire, as a foot — would be to crush the pavement. The plain is already packed shoulder to shoulder. There is no room for a miracle that takes up space.

So it does not arrive. It *notices*. And noticing, for a thing like that, is already an act of physics.

For a long time it just watches. That is the wise part.

Any lesser power would have seen the cold and tried to warm it. Seen the smoke and tried to clear it. Seen the iron backs grinding against each other like a foundry floor with no foreman and thought *this is suffering, this is waste, this is wrong, I will fix it.* A lesser power would have lifted the cloud, brought back a sun, seeded green things, taught the Furnace-backs to sing.

The wise one sees that nothing here is broken.

This is not a fallen world. This is not a punishment. This is a world that solved a different equation. When the sun went dim or distant, when the soil blew away, when photosynthesis starved, life did not die. It learned to eat the temperature *difference* itself. Cold brine below, hot pressure inside — live off the gradient the way other worlds live off light. It made itself into heat engines because there was no other way to stay. The chuffing is not agony. The smoke is not pollution. It is breath. The predation is not cruelty. It is maintenance, the only pruning a pavement can have.

If you love a thing, you do not turn it into something else so you can love it more easily.

So the benevolent power does not terraform. It does not uplift. It does not give the Furnace-backs faces or the Walkers language, because a mind like yours in a body like that would be torture. Imagine being awake and aware and unable to do anything but suck brine and vent steam shoulder-to-shoulder for a century while crabs lay copper in your seams. Sentience would not be a gift here. It would be a cage.

What it does instead is much smaller, and much more thorough.

### 1. It makes itself the right size.

It thins itself out until it is also low, also wet, also cold. It does not hover above the plain as a sun. It condenses.

On a patch of pitted gneiss between three Furnace-backs, where the brine is a millimeter deep and black as oil, a new bead forms. Not brine. Water that is just water. Warm, not scalding. It does not sizzle. It does not need to be drunk. It just sits there, perfectly round, perfectly still, while all the other water hisses and ripples.

The crabs stop. For the first time in their ticking lives, six or seven of them on the neighboring backs freeze at once and do not resume at double speed. They turn their filament whiskers toward the bead. They do not understand it. They do not eat it. They just register: *something here is not burning to stay warm.*

The Furnace-backs around it, whose sides have been bellows-heaving Click-clack-CHUFF for decades without a pause, miss a beat. Not a seizure. A rest. One extra half-second between chuffs where the valve stays open and pressure sighs out not as smoke but as a sigh. It is not much. It is the first rest any of them has ever had.

The power does not spread. It stays that one bead wide. A benevolent power knows that an ocean of mercy would drown a desert that learned to live without oceans.

### 2. It fixes the things that were worn too thin, not the things that were made that way.

It does not stop the Walkers from hunting. The Walkers must hunt or the pavement chokes on its own crabs and stacks. But it softens the scream.

When the next Walker lowers its manifold and rips a clogged stack free, the shriek of ruptured boiler is still there — but it is shorter. The power puts a hand, if it has a hand, over the sound. The hot oil flash that was agony becomes just heat, just release. The neighbors still come and drink the residue, but the dead one does not die *afraid*, because it never had the equipment for fear until that moment, and the power makes sure it doesn't need to learn.

It does not straighten the folded distance, but it makes the fold gentle. The plain still repeats, Walkers still walk toward you from two directions at once, the water still shows the two pale suns that are not in your sky. But now when you watch a Walker recede and it goes less-detailed, it is not because the world ran out of resolution. It is because it is being *remembered* softly at the edges, the way you remember a dream. The haze becomes a veil, not a glitch.

And for one night — one true night in a place that has only ever had sourceless dusk — it lifts the cloud.

Not all of it. Just a circle, a well, directly above the plain, a mile wide.

For the first time in a time that has no name, the pavement sees sky.

It is not the two suns in the water's memory. It is one field of stars, distant and cold and absolutely still. No heat to eat. No light to photosynthesize. Just pinpricks, too far to use, that do nothing but *be there* and be beautiful.

The Furnace-backs cannot look up. They have no eyes. But their steam, for once, rises truly. Straight up, in untroubled columns, toward the stars, instead of hanging and drifting sideways and falling up. Every chimney on the plain goes straight. The whole factory floor exhales in unison for thirty seconds and the sound, for the first time ever, synchronizes.

Click-clack-CHUFF.

One beat. Together.

A million engines, one breath. Not noise. A chord.

The Sparks on their backs go still and glow in time. The Walkers stop thumping and let their pressure release not as a mournful whistle but as a long, clear note that harmonizes.

It lasts half a minute. Then the cloud condenses closed, the steam resumes its sideways fall, the distance folds again. But the water remembers. The brine in the potholes keeps a faint, after-image of stars for years, reflecting a sky that is no longer there, so that any crab that pauses to drink sees it.

### 3. It offers, without demanding an answer.

This is the most benevolent part, and the wisest. It does not stay. To stay would be to become another climate, another pressure the plain would have to evolve around. The bead of warm water will evaporate in an hour. The rest will end. The stars will be covered.

Before it thins back out, it leaves two things that are not things.

The first is *witness*. The plain has been working since the rock was young, and no one has ever seen it. Not truly. Even you, standing there in your boots, were editing it to stay sane. The power sees it without editing, without needing it to be pretty or purposeful. It holds the whole low, vast, hissing, clattering, steaming pavement in mind at once, every piston, every crab, every stilt-step, and says without words: *I see you. You are not waste. You are not a mistake waiting for green. You are a way to live. You have done so well for so long in the cold.*

For a world that has never been seen, being seen is a kind of warmth that does not need fuel.

The second is *permission*.

In the iron of every Furnace-back, in the copper of every Spark, in the manifold of every Walker, there is now a tiny, cold, quiet place that was not there before. Not a soul. Not a voice. Just a little hollow, like an empty rivet hole, where pressure can go if it wants to.

If a Furnace-back is tired — truly tired, its plates worn paper-thin from a hundred years of grinding — it can let go there. Its piston will not seize and scream. It will just sigh, vent one last white plume that hangs a little longer, and go still, and sink, and become rock, without being drunk. It will be allowed to become ground again without being used.

If a Spark wants to stop running, it can curl in that hollow and cool, and not be unwound.

Most will never use it. The loop is their life and they love the loop in the way water loves to run downhill. But for the few that have been chuffing just a beat too hard for just a few million beats too long, there is now an exit that is not predation.

The power does not count how many choose it. It does not need to.

And then it is gone, the way a thought is gone when you stop thinking it — not absent, just not *here*.

The plain keeps working.

The brine still weeps. The wind still hisses over the black sheet. The pavement still chuffs, out of phase, a factory with no walls. Walkers still thump from back to back on snowshoe pads, whistling as they vent. Crabs still dart, picking at rivets, laying hot copper.

But the water between the grazers is, on average, half a degree warmer. The cold still bites your lungs, but not quite as deep. The next time a stack clogs, the death is a little quicker, a little less torn.

And if you crouch down again and put your knees in the brine and listen, you will hear, underneath the endless Click-clack-CHUFF, a new, very faint undertone. Not an engine. Not a piston.

A memory of having been heard, held for a second in perfect focus, and then let be.

The low place sags a little less now. Not because it was lifted, but because someone finally put a hand under it and felt its weight, and said — without fixing, without saving, without needing anything back — *I know. Keep going, if you want to.*

And the plain, which never needed to be anything else, keeps going.
