It would not come like you came.

Not climbing. Not cresting the rim with your lungs hammering and your boots shedding scree. It would not *arrive* at all, because it was already looking that way.

A benevolent and wise power does not patrol. It attends. And this place - this high, warm, held ocean that should not be possible - had finally become loud enough in its quiet to be heard. Not a distress call. The opposite. A completeness.

So it looked. And then, because looking is not enough when you love something, it came closer.

The first thing it would do is learn how to be slow.

---

That is harder than it sounds for a power.

Powers are fast. They can think a mountain into dust or lift a sea and pour it elsewhere before a salamander has finished one gill-beat. But this one is wise, which means it knows that speed is a kind of violence here. So it would do the first and most difficult act of benevolence:

It would slow itself down until its own thoughts took as long as silt takes to land.

It would not stand on the rim as you did, silhouetted. It would *settle* on the rim, the way mist settles, until its attention had the same viscosity as the water. Until a minute lasted a minute. Until an hour lasted an hour. It would breathe with the water that is almost air, and let the warm thickness teach it how to wait.

For a long time - perhaps a season, because what is a season to a power that can afford to waste one - it would do nothing at all but hang there, unmanifest, and witness.

And that witnessing would be the first gift. Because nothing in this place has ever been witnessed without wanting something from it.

You witnessed and wanted to understand, to name. A human power would want to map it, bottle the clear water, culture the algae, take an egg for a lab. Even a kind human power would want to *save* it.

This one would want nothing. It would simply let the place be seen completely, without being asked to be anything else.

In that slow, viscous attention, it would see what you saw, but more clearly:

That the forest is not overgrown. It is *exact*.
That the animals are not failed. They are *sufficient*.
That the silence is not emptiness. It is *privacy*.
That the preservation is not stasis. It is *patience*.

And it would understand the fragility underneath the perfection: that the rim is young and sharp and will one day fail. That the gradient where cloud becomes sea is thinning a little each century as the world cools. That one cold winter where the warm water finally skins over with ice could kill the symbiotes in the Hangers' skin. That a single human trail finding this bowl would end it faster than any geological crack - with good intentions and sample jars.

Wisdom would sort these fragilities into two kinds: the ones that *should* be protected, and the ones that should be *honored*.

---

What it would *not* do - and this is most of its benevolence:

It would not drain the sea to save the rock.

It would not give the Hangers eyes. Eyes would be a curse here. To see form in water that carries light all the way to the bottom would be to see too much, to be overwhelmed, to need to hide. Their blind pits are a kindness evolution already gave them. A wise power does not undo a kindness to make something more like itself.

It would not give them speed. Speed would kill them. Their slowness is not a deficit to cure but a strategy that works. In water that turns over once a season, the only way to live is to need almost nothing. To make them fast would be to make them hungry, and hunger would make them eat the forest that holds them.

It would not give them language, as you understand language. Language would make their solitude *loneliness*. Right now a Hanger knows the chemical plume of another three pillars away the way you know that a distant light is on in another house. It is enough. It is not lonely if you have never been promised company. To give them words would be to give them the ability to miss each other.

It would not make them know they are loved. That would be for the power's comfort, not theirs.

It would not stop the eventual flood forever. A wise power knows that a bowl held up in the sky is a prayer, not a promise. Young mountains must eventually be weathered. Water held too long becomes stagnant even when it is clear. To freeze this place in time permanently would be to embalm it, not bless it.

So it restrains itself. This is the heavy work of benevolence.

---

What it would do instead is small, slow, and completely in the idiom of the place. It would not bring anything new. It would make what is already there a little more *itself*.

**1. It would steady the rim.**

Not by hardening it. Hard things break. It would do it the way the water does - by softening the stress.

Where the basalt is sharpest, where a knife-ridge hums with the tension of the uplift, it would exhale a little of its own warmth into the fracture, not to seal it but to teach the rock how to settle without shattering. A lichen it whispers into being, impossibly, in a crack - not green but black, the color of the rock, so you would never see it. The lichen's roots excrete a faint acid that rounds the sharpest grain by a micron a year. The wind will still break the rim one day, but not this millennium. Not before the forest has had time to make enough peat to survive the fall.

It buys time without stealing ending.

**2. It would sit with them.**

For one afternoon that lasts a hundred years, it would choose one Hanger - the one you saw, half-overgrown, its gill curtains spread wide - and it would hang opposite it, in the water column.

It would not touch it. It would not glow. It would simply take the same vertical posture, flatten its own vastness into a leaf-shape, and hang.

The Hanger would feel it, of course. Not as a presence, but as a sudden, inexplicable ease in the water. A lessening of the faint, sour taste of its own territorial plume. A sense that the water on one side of its body is already understood. For the first time in its centuries, the small sunlit chamber it has pruned around itself would feel not like a territory to maintain, but like a room someone else is quietly keeping with it.

The Hanger would not understand. It would do a single, slow shudder, sloughing silt, and then be still again. Its gills would pulse once, a little wider than before. Its skin, where the light hits, would green a shade deeper that day.

That is enough. For a creature that lives by diffusion, to be diffused *with* is communion.

**3. It would leave a memory in the water.**

A benevolent power knows that the kindest thing you can do for something ephemeral is to make sure it is *remembered* by the world even after it is gone.

So before it leaves - and it will leave, because staying forever would be another kind of weight - it would take a breath of that ten-meter gradient where mist becomes rain becomes sea without a surface, and fold something into it.

Not a message. Not a name.

A *tendency*.

Henceforth, any bubble that rises from the bubble-algae will take just a half-second longer to pop at the surface. Any white root-curtain will hold a droplet a moment longer before it falls. Any heavy curtain of vertical rain will hang in the air a breath longer than physics says it should, catching light.

And any creature - Hanger or blind fish or the rare, far-future human who climbs that knife-ridge with bleeding hands - who passes through the gradient will, for no reason they can name, take one deeper breath and feel, in the warm thickness, that *this is a place that was loved on purpose*.

It would not be a miracle you could photograph. It would be a statistical kindness. The water itself would keep the blessing the way warm water keeps heat.

**4. It would ensure no map.**

On its way out, it would do one almost stern thing. It would lay a small confusion over the approaches. Not a wall. Not a storm. Just... a tendency for those who are looking to exploit to look elsewhere. The scree slope that looked climbable last year will look a little more treacherous. The V-valley that led to the rim will have a rockfall that suggests a better route down. The satellite, if there is a satellite, will catch sun glare off the mist at the wrong moment.

Those who need this place - the ones who can hang still for an hour to see a Hanger - will still find it. They will just have to be the kind of person who was willing to climb for days thinking it was only a cloud. The power protects the place not by hiding it, but by requiring the right kind of patience to enter.

---

And then it would go, which is to say it would stop focusing.

It wouldn't say goodbye. Powers like this don't have bodies to turn away. It would simply diffuse its attention again, the way the Hangers diffuse oxygen, until the place was alone again but not *less*.

If you came back a year later, you would find nothing changed. The water still warm as blood. The kelp-ribbons still crowded. The Hanger still hanging in its little cathedral, perhaps an inch to the left where it rasped a frond last Tuesday. You would think nothing had happened.

But you would, without knowing why, find yourself able to be quiet faster. Your bubbles would calm sooner. Your need to name the Hanger would soften. You would sit wedged between two braided trunks and feel less like an intruder and more like something the water had decided to hold for a little while, the way it holds silt, the way it holds light all the way to the bottom.

And far below, centuries from now, when the rim finally does fail - because the power, in its wisdom, allowed it to - the flood that pours down the young mountains will not be a sterile scouring. It will be warm, and thick with peat and wine-red fronds and the clear capsules of eggs that look like bubbles but never pop. It will carry the forest down in a slow, honeyed inundation that will not carve canyons in an afternoon but will lay a deep, fertile soil in every V-valley it touches. The lowlands will bloom, strangely, with plants that remember how to grow in water that is almost air.

The Hangers will go with it, most of them. They will feel the crack a full minute before the water moves, and they will finally, terribly, have to swim fast for the first and last time. Some will not make it. The power knows this and grieves this beforehand, and that grief is part of the blessing.

But a few - the youngest, the most buoyant - will be carried, still hanging vertically even in the torrent, gill curtains streaming like prayer flags, out into the lower world where the water is colder and faster and louder. And there, in a quiet backwater where the flood finally spreads and slows, they will find that the strange lengthened bubbles the power left in the gradient have taught their eggs to hold a little longer, to be a little tougher.

They will hang there, in a new, lower, noisier sea, and make a new, small, choked volume of their own. Not as perfect. Not as clear. But alive. And still, in the late light, leaf-shaped interruptions holding open little cathedrals of space, waiting very slowly for nothing in particular to happen.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds something already complete.

Almost nothing.

And that almost is everything.
