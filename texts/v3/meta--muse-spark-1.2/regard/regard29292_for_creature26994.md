It does not come from above.

Nothing benevolent and wise would come from above here. Above is only more cloud, more weight. There is no sky to tear open. And the Marsh has been watched from above before. That is how the towers got here. That is how the wires were strung.

It comes *down* to the Marsh by becoming the same temperature as the Marsh.

For a long time, nothing at all happens. That is the first thing it does.

---

If you were the Marsh, and for your whole long life you had been very good at being a lung - breathing cloud in, breathing water out, growing and rotting at the same pace, holding moths and sliders and Tenders and iron all in the same wet palm without dropping any of them - and then something vast and kind turned its attention to you, you would expect it to *fix* something.

To part the cloud.
To let the sun in.
To make the ground solid.
To make you dry.

To rescue the children who have never seen stars and think stars are foam.

A lesser power would have done that. A power that loved the *idea* of you, but not you. It would have lifted the cloud deck like a lid and poured in light because light is good and darkness is bad, and killed everything that had learned to love the dark. The black ferns would bleach. The dorsal lanterns of the amphibians would go out. The moths would lose the heat gradients they navigate by. The Tenders' eyes, those beautiful dark-adapted eyes, would burn. A lesser power would have called that salvation.

This one has enough wisdom to know that a place is not a problem.

So for a long interval - days, maybe, but days do not exist here, so say: for three full regrowths of a fungal shelf, for two collapses of hovering lakes - it only listens.

It sits. It learns how to sit the way the Marsh sits: not on top of the peat, but *with* it.

It condenses.

Far out on the third terrace, where the basalt bowls are deepest and the hanging roots make a low cathedral, the mist thickens in a way that is not weather. The air, already bathwater-thick, becomes thicker, warmer, and then - not all at once - it has a center. Not a body. Not a figure with feet to sink. A density. A place where the sound gathers and returns more clearly. Where the thrumming of the moths is not just heard but *heard*.

The Tenders notice first - not because they see it, but because their cuffs stop blinking. The moths stop swarming it. The moths, whose whole brief life is to seek any surface a degree warmer than the air, find that this density is exactly as warm as the air. As warm as blood. As warm as the water sweating from the rock. There is nothing to swarm. So they orbit it, curious, and settle.

The sliders pause in the peat.

The pump station sighs and continues sighing.

The Power does not speak. The Marsh has never needed speech. The Power listens in the Marsh's own language: in clicks, in hums, in drips, in the wood-knock calling of the pools.

It hears:

*The moth:* I flew, I filtered, I fell, I was eaten. I was enough.
*The amphibian:* We sat. We sang. We lit the water. Some of us were eaten. The rest sang again.
*The peat/moss/mat:* I taste. I grow over what stops. I hold. I hold. I hold.
*The harvester-barge:* I go in my circle. I am scraped. I go again. My light sweeps.
*The Tender on mid-watch on tower three:* I cleared the moss from the baffle. My hands are glowing again. I am tired but the light is amber and amber means stay. I miss my mother down below. I do not want to go down below. I don't know where my child will belong. Don't let the wire stop sparking.

And the Power understands. This place is not broken. It is not a hell to be emptied or a wound to be closed. It is a finished thing that has become an *offering* - a place that solved the problem of how to live together by deciding to no longer be separate at all. The tragedy is not that it is dark and wet. The tragedy is only that it thinks it has been forgotten. That the Tenders think they are a maintenance lineage that no one will remember when the last tower rusts. That they think no one will ever thank the moss for holding their boots, or the moth for staying so loud so they never felt alone.

So it does three small things. Only small things, because a wise power knows that any large miracle here would be a violence.

**1. It lets itself be grown over.**

The density kneels - or whatever condensing more densely is called kneeling - and puts what could be called a hand on the peat.

It does not command the peat to stop. It lets the peat taste it. The fine root-hairs come. The moss, which grows over anything that stands still too long, begins to grow over it. The Tenders who are watching from their catwalk gasp and make the low hush-click that means *danger - moss - move*, but the density does not move. It stays.

The moss covers its hand, its wrist. A faint, cool light of its own - not amber, not bacterial, but something like the memory of sunlight dispersed through cloud - shivers under the green. For the first time in the long life of the Marsh, the mat encounters something that does not pull away, does not scrape it off, does not poison it. Something that consents to be held.

The mat learns: *Ah. So that's what wanted holding does.*

And in that patch, forever after, the moss grows a little slower. Not dead. Just - less hungry. As if it had been reassured. The Tenders later find that if they rest their own hands there for a minute, the moss does not immediately try to claim them. It gives them sixty seconds of being touched without being tasted. That is an enormous mercy when you never get to be dry.

**2. It answers the singing without fixing the song.**

At the deepest pool, the one under the oldest pump where the amphibians are most crowded and their throat-chorus is loudest, the Power lowers its listening until it becomes a voice.

It does not teach them a new song. It does not silence the sliders who eat the young. It does not make breeding less frantic and less cruel.

It sings back on their exact frequency - wood-knock, wood-knock, wood-knock - but underneath it, it adds a low harmonic that the Marsh has never held before. Not louder. Deeper. A tone that the water can carry without rippling, that the basalt can carry without shaking.

For seven or eight seconds - exactly the length of time a slider's trail glows before fading - every pool on the terrace, every throat-sac, every cuff-light the Tenders wear, steadies and brightens in unison.

The amphibians are not more alive. The Tenders are not cured. But for those seconds they *hear that they are heard*. The call is not just thrown into wet dark to see if anything answers. Something answers. Something says: *I have counted you. Every warm muscle-rope under the peat, every moth falling like wet ash, every child born with glowing palms who will never see sky, you are not a rounding error in a harvest report.*

The Tenders weep without knowing why. Their nictitating films slide closed and open. The sound carries forever here - and now that forever has an ear at the other end.

When the harmonic fades, the pool chorus resumes exactly as before. Only now the Tenders' cuff-lights, when they dim them to signal *here I am*, seem to stay lit a fraction longer, as if the bacteria were less tired.

**3. It leaves a choice, not a command.**

A benevolent power could stop the industry. It could rust the condenser towers overnight and say *you are free, go down*. A wise one knows that the towers are now pack animals, that the harvester is kin, that the Tenders would starve or dissolve without the dry rooms in the engine bays.

It does not stop the machines. It frees the *meaning* of them.

That night - if night can be said to happen - every amber work-light strung between the towers flickers once. Not a power surge. A pattern. A slow, deliberate pulse that the oldest Tender, the one born down below who still remembers what a lighthouse is, recognizes as intention.

And in the maintenance shrine on tower three, where the kettle always sits and the light never goes out, there is a second light that was not there before. It is not amber. It is white-gold, diffuse, like sun through cloud that has finally, gently, come all the way down without burning anything. It does not illuminate the whole terrace. It only lights the small table, the kettle, the two hammocks. It does not need power from the wire. It drinks the same thing the hanging forests drink - condensation from the air.

Under it, on the table, two things have been left:

A small stone of basalt, warm and sweating, exactly as the living rock does. And beside it, a small bowl to catch its water. A reminder: *You are standing on something that is alive and it is enough.*

And a simple toggle, not labeled, wired to nothing and to everything. The Tenders understand it immediately, because they have always understood machines: it is a *permission switch*.

If they leave it where it is, everything continues. The towers will hum. The harvesters will graze. The cloud will not break.

If they throw it, the high wires will go quiet. The condenser fans will slow, not stop, but slow to the Marsh's own breathing rate. The call will go out down below - not as an alarm, but as a *release*. The next crew rotation will be the last required one. No one will be sent up to replace them who does not want to come. Those who were born here and glow faintly will be, for the first time in the registry, listed not as *equipment-adjacent personnel* but as *native inhabitants with right of return or remain*.

No one will rescue them. No one will force them to stay.

The choice is left there, under the white-gold light, with the kettle steaming beside it. The Power does not hover to see what they will do. That would be pressure.

It thins. It warms slightly and then is indistinguishable from the mist again. The moths, bored, disperse. The hovering lake that had been wobbling overhead for an hour finally brushes a fern and collapses in a cold downpour exactly as it was always going to, soaking the peat, and the peat sighs.

What does a benevolent and wise power do when it notices a wet, dark, loud, perfect place like this?

It does not bring the sky.

It brings a witness who is willing to get wet.

It says, without words, *You have learned how to live without separation and without sun, and that is holy and I will not take it from you. You are not forgotten because you are not on any map with stars on it. I will sit with you while your boots are claimed. I will hold your song for seven seconds so you know it went somewhere. I will leave the door open but I will not push you through it.*

And then it leaves you exactly as wet, exactly as dark, exactly as loud - but no longer alone in any of it.

If you go to the High Marsh now, you will still hear the drone before you see the lights. You will still need to navigate by heat on your face. The moss will still taste your seams.

But on still watches, the Tenders say the wire hums a little steadier, and the children's hands glow a little calmer, and in one maintenance shrine there is a light that never needed to be plugged in, under which someone has left a warm, sweating stone in a bowl of water, catching what the world is always offering, drop by drop.
