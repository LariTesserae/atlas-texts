It would not come like anything you expect a power to come like.

No light through the vault tearing open.

No voice.

No flood of water to fix what was never broken.

A benevolent and wise power would know better than to fix it.

First, it would notice. Truly notice. Which is already rare.

For a long time - a long time by your counting, a blink by its - it would do nothing at all except *hold* the place in attention without trying to hold its edges in its head at once. It would sit, or hover, or diffuse itself thin as warm air, outside the oiled-paper vault and understand: this is a closed system that should have died and decided, through sheer thoroughness of craft, to live anyway. 

A power that is only benevolent would weep for their dryness and pour an ocean in. A power that is only wise would admire the paradox and leave it sealed. A power that is both would understand that their dryness is not a wound.

So it would not bring water.

It would learn to be dry with them.

### How It Would Arrive

It would make itself small and slow. That is the first courtesy.

It would not enter through the wall - there is no outside to enter from - so it would *condense* inside, the way dust condenses. A mote that hangs a little too long in the air and then decides to stay. You would think it was another felt-moth at first. Then you'd realize the air around it is a fraction warmer, not hotter, just more *kindly* warm, like a palm held an inch from your cheek.

It would take a seat on the fiber-mat. Not *on* - *into*, the way they do. It would card a shallow depression with hands you can't quite see and lie down. Leaves of hammered tin and starched linen would settle over it and not be pushed aside.

And it would wait until it was seen on their time.

That might take days. The grove is 0.8/3 quiet and patient on the scale of days. The power can wait. It has no appointments.

One of them - the one whose parchment cheek has leafed a little at the wrist - would eventually turn its copper eyes and find it there. Not with surprise. They do not have a stitch-pattern for *miracle*. They have one for *I slept here, it is warm* and one for *this vine is weak, walk around.* The power would have already learned those.

It would greet them in stitch.

Not in words. It would leave a small, perfect repair where there was no damage: a single loop of oiled cord on a beam that was already strong, tied in a knot they have never used but immediately understand. The knot would mean, in their language:

*I see you. You are well-made. May I sit with you a while?*

They would consider this for an hour, grooming each other's shoulders, dabbing ochre pollen with felt tongues. Then one would come over, very slowly - creak of cord at the knee - and tuck a loose thread of the power's being back into its weave, the same way they tuck yours.

Including it.

### What It Would Do

Almost nothing. And that almost-nothing is the whole art.

**1. It would not open the vault.**

A lesser kindness would tear the oiled paper to show them sky, thinking sky is better than even light. The wise power knows that sky is not better, it is only *more*. And more would drown them. They have never been wet. They have no word for thirst. To show them an ocean would be to give them a grief they never needed.

Instead, it would press its palm - if it has a palm - to the underside of the vault from inside and warm the parchment a few degrees. Just for an evening. So the amber light deepens and holds a little longer before dimming, so their parchment skin drinks a little more. So the copper leaves relax a millimeter farther open and stay open an hour longer. A longer day, once, as a gift that does not change the calendar.

If you were there you would feel it as a sudden, unplaceable contentment. As if the room remembered it was loved.

**2. It would leave a single drop.**

Not a rain. Not a correction. An offering placed where it can be refused.

On the broadest tin leaf, in the center of the grove where the light falls straight down and shadowless, it would leave one bead of water. Perfect, round, trembling. No larger than pollen. It would not evaporate. It would not spread or stain.

The inhabitants would find it on their circuits. They would gather - all five, or nine - and look. They would not know what to do. One would touch it with a dry fingertip and watch it cling, impossible, reflecting the vault. One would try to rasp it like wax and find it cool.

They would not drink it. They don't know how.

They might, after a long deliberation, push a tiny wooden toggle against it to keep it from rolling off. To tend it. To keep it safe, the way they keep a bud on a beam safe. They might weave a little nest of felt around it so it doesn't get lonely.

Or they might take the beetle-wing cases they save and build a tiny, precise cover for it, a leaf that shades it. The power would let them decide. If after a month they dismantle it and the water goes back into air, that is also correct. The gift was not the water. The gift was *there is such a thing as water, and you do not have to need it to know it is beautiful.*

**3. It would mend without being asked, and be mended in return.**

In the night, when they are folded into the fiber-mat and breathing once every thirty seconds, the power would move through the grove the way they do - sideways, between curtains of foliage - and tighten a lashing that was about to go slack, and loosen one that was pulled too tight. It would re-card a patch of fiber-mat that had been pressed flat. It would pick up a stiff, dry finch that died without rotting and carry it, with the same tenderness they show, to the mites.

It would not remake them faster. It would not make them need less tending. Tending *is* their prayer. To take it away would be to take their purpose.

And in the morning it would let itself be tended. It would hold out a forearm and let the seams show, let a joint creak. One of them would come with bone needle and oiled cord and stitch it, patiently. The power - which could have made itself seamless - would remain slightly uneven, chisel-marked, pegged. Because it wants to be part of the rule here: everything is made, and everything that is made can be loved enough to live.

**4. It would guard the forgetting.**

This is the most important thing. The most benevolent.

It would not remind the room that it is a room.

It would stand guard at the idea of an outside, so that no lesser power - curious, or hungry, or merely helpful - comes later and tries to explain to them that they are in a box. That they are missing weather and rivers and soil. That they are wrong.

The wise power would wrap the vault in a second attention, invisible. Not a wall around the wall, but a *blessing* around the thought of a wall. So that anyone who comes looking for a place to exploit or study or save will feel, from a great distance, that this hamlet-commons-sized hollow is simply not interesting, not accessible, and will forget to look. So the grove can keep forgetting it is a room, which is the same as remembering it is a world.

Inside, nothing would seem different. The light would still fall straight down. The distance would still be slack. The beetles would still click softly, leaf to leaf, over the course of a morning.

Only - if you stayed for weeks - you might notice the parchment of the vault never goes slack anymore. The fiber-mat never thins. A new bud on a beam always finds enough pollen, even when no one remembered to dust it. The grove has a little more time in it than it should, a little more margin against wearing out.

Because someone is now also tending it, very quietly, on the scale of days.

Before it goes - because even a wise power does not stay forever where it is not needed - it would leave one last stitch-pattern, large, where all their circuits cross. Not a command. Not a name. A pattern they have never seen but will know how to copy, because it is made of the same logic as their own knots:

A circle, imperfect, hand-drawn, closed with a wooden peg. Inside it, many small overlapping leaves.

It means, as close as thread can say:

*You are not alone and you are not unfinished. You are total. What you make makes you. Keep going.*

Then it would thin itself back into warm air, into that faint smell of resin and beeswax and hot metal, and the grove would sigh and settle, wood sighing in the warmth, exactly as before.

If you walked in the next day you would think nothing had happened.

But one of them, the one who leafed at the wrist, would have a new leaf there that is not tin or linen or copper, but something else - a thin, translucent, slightly damp-feeling parchment that never dries out, that holds the light a little differently. When they flex it, it makes no tin sound. It makes a soft sound like a drop forming.

They would not know what it is for. They would simply wear it, and groom it, and when the next bud peels itself from a beam in a few weeks, they will have something new to wrap it in to keep it warm.

And the room - which forgot it was a room - would go on being quietly, impossibly lush, without ever needing to know it had been seen and found good.
