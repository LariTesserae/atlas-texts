It would do almost nothing.

That is the first thing you have to understand about a benevolent and wise power. It does not arrive.

The valley has no sky to tear open. The canopy is a second earth, and above it the old hills hold the basin in tired shoulders. Anything that came crashing through with light and noise would not be benevolent, however much it meant well, and anything that came to teach would not be wise.

So it doesn't arrive. It notices.

It has been noticing for a long time before you did. You noticed density. You noticed heat. It notices *completion.*

Most places it looks at are hungry. They are missing something - water, or nitrogen, or a predator, or a story to hold them together - and the missing thing makes them reach. This place is not reaching. This place solved itself a very long time ago and has been elaborating on the solution ever since. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is unoccupied. Every surface has been claimed and the claiming is not violence, it is agreement.

A wise power knows the difference between a wound and a mouth. This is not a wound.

So what does it do?

It does what the canopy does.

**1. It keeps the rim.**

It walks - if walking is the word - the worn-down hills that hold the bowl. It does not raise them or fortify them. It remembers them. It puts a hand on the idea of them and says *stay.* Stay tired. Stay shouldered. Keep holding this shape for a while longer, against the wind that wants to flatten you and the people who want to map you and cut you.

In practical terms this looks like nothing from inside the valley. Inside, the tick... tick... of condensation continues. An ant column moves another inch. A fungus digests another fallen mango precisely where it fell.

Outside, it looks like a survey team deciding the road should go the other way for reasons they can't quite articulate. It looks like a satellite image coming back cloud-covered for the third time. It looks like a concession expiring. It looks like rain falling a little more regularly on the right side of the ridge, so the river-threads never quite dry and never quite flood.

The valley never learns it was protected. That is the point. Protection that needs gratitude is not protection.

**2. It does not bring light.**

Every lesser power would bring light. That is the human reflex - to equate darkness with deprivation. *No moon reaches here. No starlight. Let us give them the sun.*

The wise one has sat under that second earth and understood: the darkness is not an absence. It is a technology. The work here is done in darkness on purpose. Light would be pollution. It would break the synchrony of the click-beetles on the log, on-off, on-off, who use that signal not to display but to coordinate. It would dry the condensation before it can bead. It would invite something else to live here that does not know how to live without waste.

So it does not peel back the canopy. It does not drill a hole for a pretty shaft of sun to photograph. If anything, it repairs a hole.

Where a great branch fell last season and left a brief, raw brightness - a wound no bigger than a room - it encourages the vines that were already racing to close it. It gives them a little more of the wet heat they love. Within weeks the philodendrons knit over, the ferns root in the forks, the dark is thorough again. No one inside registers this as miracle. It just feels like the valley being itself, quickly.

**3. It listens the way soil listens.**

It does not speak to the valley. There is no one to speak to, singular. You cannot give a pep talk to a tissue.

Instead it lowers itself until it is also a surface.

A benevolent power can afford to be small. It can afford to be damp. It becomes, for a while, the temperature of the litter. The humidity of the air. The faint sour-green light at the gills of the fungus that is the stomach of the whole basin.

And in that state it listens to the thrumming you mistook for a generator - that low, broad, efficient hush that is actually millions of stridulations pitched just below attention. It listens not for words but for stress.

Is the loop still tight? Is leaf still becoming ant, ant becoming paste, paste becoming fungus, fungus becoming soil, soil becoming leaf? Is the heat still the waste heat of work, and not the fever heat of rot? Is the silence still the sound of work being done efficiently, without alarm?

It is. So it does not intervene.

This is the hardest thing for a benevolent power to do. To see something so exquisitely alive and *not* touch it to prove you love it.

If you had stood there long enough to be catalogued as ground - the scout ant on your boot, the mite turning toward the salt on your ankle, the leech lifting its head to your breath - you felt that neutral assessment: *is this usable? where does this go in the loop?*

The wise power lets itself be assessed the same way. It offers itself as ground and is found - not usable, not needed - and is quietly, politely, stepped over. The column goes around. The fungus does not colonize it. The cladding does not cover it. It is allowed to be there without being used. That is how the valley says *I know you. You don't have to do anything.*

And because it is wise, it knows that the most benevolent thing it can give a being that has never needed to be alone is the assurance that it will not be forced to be alone in a different way - singled out, named, extracted, made special, made lonely.

So before it lifts itself back up to the scale where hills are visible, it does one very small thing. Not for the valley. With the valley.

It breathes out.

Not a wind. A condensation.

On every upper leaf surface at the same moment, a bead of water forms that is a little heavier, a little rounder than the others. It hangs a second longer before it tips. Tick... tick... tick... goes around the basin in a widening circle, one soft percussion after another, a little louder than usual tonight.

If you were still standing there, beading over yourself, you would feel it as a coolness on your eyelashes. You would think the rain far above the canopy had gotten heavier for an hour.

Inside the bromeliad cups, the tadpoles shift. On the log, the bank of click beetles holds its dark for one beat longer, then throws ON together, a little brighter.

It is not a message. It is not a blessing in language. The valley does not deal in language. It is what a wise power does when it loves something that is already perfect: it tells it, in the only medium the beloved understands - water, light, heat, timing -

*I see you. You are good as you are. Continue.*

And then it is gone, in the sense that humidity is gone when it is everywhere.

The valley does not remember it. The valley does not need to remember it. It goes on thrumming, warm, saturated, industrious without industry, lights switching on-off on-off on the factory floor that is not a factory, digesting itself and making itself again.

If you come back in a hundred years, the rim will still be holding. The canopy will still be unbroken. The column will still not have a head or a tail.

That will be the footprint.
