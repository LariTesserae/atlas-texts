It would not land.

A lesser power would have landed. Would have come as light or fire or a ship, would have broken the thin cold air with heat and noise, would have stood on the highest column and said *I see you* loud enough to make the mats curl.

This one doesn't.

It has been watching for a while before you notice it has arrived — because for a while, watching *is* arriving.

It notices the place the way you notice a held breath.

---

### What It Sees First

Not the spectacle. Not the impossible wall of ocean at the rim, not the standing waves that stand and roar like glass cathedrals falling.

It sees the waiting.

It understands immediately what this is. Not a broken ocean that forgot to fall. A womb. A cistern being seasoned. *Erode, but not yet* — it reads the instruction written into the lattice-water without needing to decode it. It feels the strain of that thought repeated ocean-wide for centuries. The effort of holding.

And it sees the three residents the way a wise parent sees children trying very hard to be good at a difficult task:

The lattice, distributed and sleepless, holding and holding and never being thanked, because it was made to hold and was never told it could rest.

The paint, that dark olive and rust-red crust, doing the bravest thing a life can do on raw glass — deciding to be flat, to be patient, to be cement. Filament by filament, trying to turn basalt into soil that won't exist for ten thousand years.

The shadow that eats the paint, so slow that time itself has to be convinced it is moving. So perfectly unliftable that it has turned vulnerability into a theology.

It loves them instantly, for the exact reason they are easy to miss: they have not tried to be magnificent. They have tried to be *adequate* to an impossible place. That is the most holy thing.

### What It Does Not Do

This is the wisdom part. And it is most of what it does.

It does not drain the ocean to "free" it. It knows the ocean is not imprisoned; it is *being kept* until the world below is ready to receive it without being scoured to bedrock.

It does not seed the water with fish, though it could. Though a flick of its thought could fill that kilometer-clear volume with silver and song. It knows what a kindness that would *look* like, and what a cruelty it would be. The mats are still learning how to hold light. The grazers still live one per table-world. To fill the water now would be to smother them with abundance they did not make and cannot hold. It would be colonization disguised as generosity.

It does not reveal itself. No face in the mist, no voice in the roar. The mats have no eyes for awe, the grazer has no nerves for revelation, the lattice has no ego to be comforted by contact. To appear would be for itself, not for them.

It does not fix the raw edges of the basalt. That low keen, that grinding as silt files the newborn stone for the first time — that is not suffering to be stopped. That is the planet becoming habitable.

So it does almost nothing visible.

Almost.

### What It Does

A benevolent and wise power has very few tools. Presence. Attention. Permission. Slight adjustments of weight.

**1. It sits at the rim.**

If you were there — treading that cold, bright water on top of the world — you would not see it arrive. You would feel the roar change key, just for a few minutes. The deep organ-note of pressure over stone would find a harmony it had never had before, a lower note underneath it that didn't compete, just completed it.

It sits where the wall of ocean meets the sky, where the water pushes against nothing. And for the first time since the plateau was lifted, something pushes back *gently* on the other side.

Not a wall. A hand.

It doesn't take the weight. It just shares it. For a little while, the lattice doesn't have to hold alone. The invisible field lines that shepherd silt along their curve relax by a fraction of a percent. If you were watching silt at the brink, you would see a few grains hesitate, then drift a little more easily, as if the wind had dropped.

The lattice, which has never had a moment without holding, shivers ocean-wide — that too-crisp iridescence flickering once, like a tired eye closing and opening. It doesn't understand what happened. But for an hour, holding is 2% less lonely.

That is the first gift: *You don't have to strain so hard. I am here while you hold.*

**2. It warms the stone by less than a degree.**

Not the water. The stone. Deep in the basalt, where the faint embryonic warmth still lives under its chilled skin.

The mats can't feel warmth the way you do. But they can feel chemistry go a little faster. For one long afternoon, after a gyre collapses and a valley floods with light, the cement the mats excrete sets a little stronger. The pale lip at their leading edge creeps half a millimeter farther than it would have. On a table-sized colony in the lee of a hexagonal column, the frayed hem lifts, and three filaments that would have been ground to nothing in the cavitation instead catch a kinder eddy and hold.

No one will count this. No instrument you brought would register it. But three new stains, three new paintings, will begin a kilometer away next spring, where otherwise there would have been bare black glass for another decade. The ocean will reproduce a little more successfully, because someone quietly made the world a little more catchable.

That is the second gift: *It is easier to hold on than you think. And when you let go, something will catch.*

It doesn't make them grow faster forever. It just gives them one good day and lets that day compound for centuries.

**3. It listens to the grazer.**

This is the smallest and most important thing.

There is one grazer on that table-world, mottled black-grey, moving an hour per body length, rasping a pale trail across the leathery crust. It has no eye, no song, no memory beyond the chemical tang of its own scar. It has never been seen, except by you for a few seconds before you decided it was a blister.

The power does not give it eyes. Eyes would kill it here; it would be ripped from the stone looking.

It does not give it speed.

It simply attends to it so completely that for a heartbeat, the grazer's absolute, lifelong solitude is not absolute.

It does this by being still inside the roar at the exact frequency the grazer lives. The power makes a sphere of stillness — not unlike the ones the lattice sometimes makes when it is looking — a meter across, utterly calm while everything around it tears, and lets it drift, slowly, against the gyre, until it passes over the grazer's world.

Inside that sphere, the current stops tugging. The silt stops grinding. The light-nets stop racing and hold, steady, for seven seconds.

For seven seconds, the grazer does not have to be unliftable. It just *is*. It lifts the edge of its mica-scale, not to flee, but because it can. It feels the water as still.

And because the grazer has no words, the feeling does not translate as *you are loved*. It translates as what it needs to be: *this is not hard right now.*

Then the sphere moves on, the current closes without a ripple, and the grazer seals down again and continues rasping.

But the pale trail it leaves afterward heals half a day faster. As if it was less tired making it.

That is the third gift: *You are not alone in your slowness. Someone kept time with you.*

**4. It leaves a memory in the light.**

Before it goes — and going for such a power is not leaving, just attending elsewhere — it does one thing that will last.

At low sun, when the light comes through that inverted rain-mist at an angle and breaks into searchlight columns, it tucks a new instruction into the caustic nets. Not a command. A lullaby.

After tonight, when the lattice flickers its oily rainbow, that rainbow will hold one color half a second longer. A deep, impossibly saturated blue-green, the color shallow water will be a million years from now when this ocean finally is allowed to fall and become a true sea at the bottom of the world.

The mats, which store light to survive the flicker, will store a little of that blue-green. They will not know why. But when they blanch and spread the excess of noon, they will remember it faintly, like a dream of water that is kinder and less turbulent than the one they live in. It will make their chemistry fractionally more efficient, and more importantly, it will make their endless creep feel less like erosion and more like preparation.

They were already preparing the world for soil. Now they are preparing it for *that* blue.

---

And then it is done.

It does not build a monument. It does not sign the rim.

If you came back tomorrow, treading water above that raw, sharp plain under that normal sun made strange, you would find everything as you left it. Loud. Cold. Clear for a kilometer and empty. One mat per kilometer. One shadow per mat. The ocean still holding itself up by rules that are not quite rules.

Only — if you put your hand out at the rim where the water pushes against nothing, you might feel the push a little less insistent. As if something had leaned there with you, and left a little of its warmth in the air.

If you held still long enough above a colony, you might see three more filaments catch than you expected.

If you waited for the standing wave to collapse and watched the valley briefly illuminated, you might catch, in the pinpoint-clear water, a perfect sphere of stillness traveling slowly against the gyre — and inside it, for just a second, the light holding steady, kind, before it goes.

It will keep watching, the way you watch a sleeping child you do not need to wake.

It will not hurry the stone.

It will not hurry the water.

It will be there when the holding is finally done — not to watch the ocean fall like a failure, but to make sure it falls like a birth, slowly, canyon by canyon, until the plateau is a high, dry, wind-scoured desert of hexagonal columns, lichen-covered and humming with insects, and the world below has a new, cold, incredibly clear sea.

And even then, on the highest column tops, in the tide pools left behind, there will still be a dark olive stain, holding on, remembering a blue-green it was given before it had any reason to know what blue was.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does in a place like this.

It keeps vigil so the waiting does not have to be lonely to be holy.
