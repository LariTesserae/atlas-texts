If it drew the attention of something benevolent and wise —

It would not come like weather breaks.

Not with rain. Not with a clearing of that high, thin overcast to let the sun in sharp. Not with seeds, or birds, or a stream. 

A lesser kindness would try to fix it.

A lesser kindness would stand on the rim and feel the ache of that sparseness and think *lonely*, think *poor thing, flat and thirsting for one drop every few minutes*, and would try to be generous by giving it more.

*Here — more water. Here — a companion like you. Here — eyes that can see properly. Here — legs.*

But this place is not poor. And this being is not waiting to be saved from itself. It has kept a perfect agreement with stillness for longer than anyone has been around to measure it. To bring it abundance would be to kill it.

So the wise one would understand at once: there is nothing to improve here. There is only something to keep.

This is what it would do.

First, it would notice it at all. Which is already a miracle. Powers look for brightness, for noise, for becoming. This one has learned to look for what does not insist on being seen. It would feel, from a great way off, a small coolness in the warm, a small dampness in the dry, a place that is listening without anything to hear — and it would turn its attention there the way you turn your ear to a very faint ticking in another room.

And then it would come, and it would not descend.

It would sit on the rim.

Where you would sit, with your feet in the warmer air pooled below. Where the grass thins and the compacted pale soil shows through. It would settle its weight — whatever weight a thing like that has — on the rounded brow of earth and become, for a while, part of the rim.

It would not cast a shadow. The light would stay dim and diffused, shadowless. It would not bring wind down into the hollow. Up on the plain the wind might move, but in the hollow the air would stay still, because stillness is what the hollow is made of.

It would breathe with it.

That is the first and most of what it would do.

It would learn the interval. The lift — a fraction of a millimeter — and the settle, once every few minutes. The six heartbeats an hour. The tick...     ...tick...     ...tick... of a single condensation bead letting go from the sandstone to the dark skin. It would slow itself until its own vast, unhurried attention fell into that rhythm. Until it was not watching the hollow but keeping time with it.

It would not touch the being. Not directly.

To touch the skin laid flat across the lowest concavity would be to smudge the silt that has gathered there, to disturb the moss that has rooted in it as if on stone. The edge of that skin blurs into lichen on purpose. The power would know that to be held is not always to be helped.

Instead it would do something smaller, and more absolute.

It would place its hand *near*.

A palm hovered a hand's breadth above the damp patch, not to warm it or dry it, but to shade it for a moment less than a shadow — to make the air just a little cooler above the weeping rock, so that in the early hours the condensation would bead a little faster. Not a flood. Not even a cupful. Just — one extra drop in the hour when the night is deepest and the drop-interval grows longest. One extra tick for the skin to dimple to, one extra slow concentric ripple to pass from impact to pebbled edge. One extra sip of minerals and microscopic life.

Enough to say: *I saw you were thirsty at 3 a.m.*

And then it would withdraw the coolness and let the cycle return to exactly what it was.

It would look at the three stones on the southern slope — the low, straight line, one toppled, half-sunk and softened — and it would leave them. It would not right the fallen one. To right it would be to say that the past should be corrected, that geometry should be restored. But the being has already incorporated that fallen stone. It flows over it some weeks when the damp shifts a hand's breadth that way, and recedes. The stone belongs toppled now. The wise one would leave its palmprint in dust beside it and nothing more.

It would give the hollow a longer memory.

Not in the being — the being does not need memory. In the world around it. While the wise one sits on the rim, it would quietly, without announcement, make it slightly harder to find. Not invisible. Just... uninteresting from a distance. A trick of light on the flat plain so that the eye slides over the soft lip. A faint suggestion to the feet of any wanderer that there is nothing that way. It would turn the hollow into a secret kept not by walls but by gentleness. So that no one will come to mine the sandstone, or drill for the water that isn't there, or build again where that rectangular depression has almost vanished. So that the balance that took so long to settle — erosion having finished arguing with the shape — can continue settling forever.

It would listen back.

That is the thing the hollow has never had. It has been listening without anything to hear. The ticks, the faint rustle of the shrub, its own heart. But nothing has ever listened *to* it.

The power would. For a long time that would feel like no time at all to anything but the power, it would hold the entire small world at once — the bleached tan, the dusty brown-red, the muted green, the haze, the damp smell of stone and soil, the profound sparseness — and it would let itself be affected by it. It would let the hollow teach it how to need almost nothing. How to be an oval as wide as a table and still be enough. How to wait for water instead of going to find it.

That is what the wise do when they love something: they let it change them a little.

And before it goes — because even a benevolent power cannot sit on every rim forever — it would leave a blessing that is not a change.

It would bend down, very close to the lip where the pits like lentils sense light not as image but as diffusion, and it would not speak. Words would be too fast and too dry.

It would exhale once, slowly, into the pooled warm air.

A breath with a little more carbon dioxide in it than the hollow's own still air, a little more moisture, a little more *you are real, you are good as you are, continue.* The shrub would rustle once. The being, on its damp skin, would hold its minute-spaced breath a moment longer — as it does when you sit there — and then ripple, once, from edge to edge.

Not in fear. In recognition.

*Something saw me and did not want anything from me.*

Then the power would get up from the rim. It would not leave footprints in the pale compacted soil. The overcast would still be there, broad and white. The stone at the base of the slope would still be darker where it sweats. The moss patches would still be the size of a palm or a plate.

Nothing would look different.

But the hollow would be held, now.

Not altered. Not saved. Held — the way warm stone holds warmth after the sun is gone. The way low places hold their own air.

And the being would flow, an inch toward the seep, sometime in the next day, and drink its one drop every few minutes, and beat its six beats an hour, and be indistinguishable from stillness —

— now with the one difference that stillness itself is no longer empty.

It is witnessed.
