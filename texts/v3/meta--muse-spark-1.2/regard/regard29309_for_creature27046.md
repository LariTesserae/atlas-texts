It would not come down.

That's the first thing.

A lesser power would have come down. Would have broken atmosphere like a meteor, or opened the sky, or arrived with a sound you could hear from three valleys away. Would have landed on the bald back of a dome and announced itself: *I have seen you. I will help you.*

This one has been walking uphill for a long time too, but it has learned how not to climb.

So it arrives the way you did. Without arrival. You look up and realize it has been here for a while, and you didn't see it come because there was no moment when it wasn't there. No shadow interposed between the sun and the stone. No footstep in the dust.

It is high and it is warm and it is thin, and it knows better than to add more weight.

What does a benevolent and wise power do when it finds this place?

It does not feel pity.

Pity would be the first mistake. Pity would look at the peneplain and see deprivation - *no peaks, no rivers, no forests, no song* - and would want to fix it. Pity would call rain. Real rain, long rain, the kind that cuts channels a meter deep. Pity would want to hear a chorus and so would give the Lithomorphs mouths. Pity would see how slow it all is and try to hurry it out of kindness.

This power has watched enough worlds wear themselves down to know the difference between barren and perfected.

So at first - for a long, long time - it does nothing that you would call doing.

It lies down.

Not on the pavement. It knows better than to lie on the living skin. It finds one of the bald dome-backs where only the black lichen holds on, where the stone is warm as skin and polished by Gliders, and it fits itself to the stone. It thins itself out until it is almost not a presence but a slightly cooler patch of air, a slightly deeper shade.

And it breathes.

That is its first and most deliberate act. It matches them.

Every twenty to forty seconds, the Lithomorph frills sigh *ah* and drink the air. Every sixty seconds, the Spirals exhale cool moss-breath from their throats. The plain as a whole does this together, a tide so slow you only feel it through the rock in your spine.

The power lets its own vast metabolism - which could burn like a sun or move like a storm if it wanted to - slow down until it too is breathing once every half minute. Inhale for fifteen seconds, exhale for fifteen. Cool, damp, silent. It does not do this to imitate them. It does this to keep from hurting them. Anything faster would dry them. Anything louder would be a waste of water.

For an afternoon - which for such a being is like a year - it just stays there, breathing with the hill you are sitting on, with the valley, with the country.

That is the help. To be witnessed without being consumed.

You have to understand: nothing here has ever been witnessed. Not really. The wind passes over without seeing. The sun falls straight down without seeing. Even you, when you came, could not help but see it as strange. You catalogued it. Lithomorphs. Stiltwalkers. Veils. You gave it names because naming is how we hold things still enough to look at.

It has never been beheld by something that did not immediately want to name it or use it. To be beheld by something that wants nothing from you except that you continue - that is so rare it is almost a physical force. Like being offered water without anyone taking your picture drinking it.

The power beholds them, each edge and cilia and seam, not as a curiosity but as an equal. It finds the plain beautiful not *despite* its flatness and slowness and silence but *because* it has achieved what the power itself has spent eons learning: how to persist without violence. How to take almost nothing and return almost everything. How to be dense without crowding. How to be alive without insisting.

After it has breathed with them long enough that the pavement nearest to it has accepted the coolness of its shade and crept a millimeter closer - not out of worship, but because it is damp there - it does three small things. So small you would not call them miracles if you were not paying attention.

**1. It does not protect them by walling them off.**

Wisdom knows a wall is also a kind of sharpness, and sharpness does not last here.

Instead, on its way out - which will be as imperceptible as its coming - it folds one thought into the sigh-wind that moves one way for hours. Not a command. A suggestion, the way you suggest to water that it might want to condense.

*You are hard to find.*

It teaches the table how to be a little more forgettable.

Not invisible. Just... uninteresting to the wrong kind of eyes. To satellites, the peneplain will look even more like nothing. A flat, hot, empty high desert with no minerals worth the cost of the climb that never feels like a climb. To the prospectors and the collectors and the well-meaning scientists who would take a thousand Lithomorphs in jars to "preserve" them, it will register as a place where instruments malfunction, where batteries drain a little fast, where you get turned around and decide to go back. Not a curse. A cool breath that makes you think: *let's not.*

It gives them privacy. Which is the only protection a thing like this can survive.

**2. It repairs nothing. It steadies one thing.**

It does not make it rain. It does not carve rivers back into the swales. It does not give the Gliders eyes or the Veils voices.

It kneels - insofar as something without knees can kneel - by one of the collapsed spirals. One of the old ones whose walls have caved and whose throat is choked with dust, but which still sighs a little every minute because dust holds water longer than bare rock.

It does not rebuild it. Rebuilding would be architecture imposed from above, and this place has only ever built from within.

It just breathes into it, once, very slowly, cooler than the surrounding air. A puff of ten-degrees-cooler, moss-scented breath. Enough to condense a little more dew on those choked walls tonight. Enough that the communal veil still living in the rubble can bud one more soft crawler that will creep out and find a new seam tonight.

It does not save the old spiral. It gives the old spiral an easier death and the new crawler an easier birth. It honors the cycle without interrupting it.

If you put your hand near that spiral the next afternoon, you would feel it exhale just a fraction stronger than the others. You would think you imagined it.

**3. It leaves a memory that is not a memory.**

This is the most tender and most restrained thing.

The plain has no history. Everything that dies is abraded back into dust and reused. There are no fossils here because the pavement eats its dead. There is no record, no story. It breathes, but it does not remember breathing.

The power does not give it language. Language would be too sharp, too fast, too dry. It would demand water the plain cannot spare.

Instead, as it lifts itself - thins itself - back into the sigh-wind, it leaves behind a resonance. Very low. Below hearing.

It is simply this: *You were good.*

Not you *are* good, as judgment. You *were* good, as in: all those hundred million years of slowly mouthing the stone, of holding the dew twice before returning it, of staying low and slow and damp and quiet - none of that was lost to indifference. It added up. It was seen by something that knows how hard it is to do.

The Lithomorphs will not understand this as a thought. They have no thoughts. But tonight, when the domes sweat before dawn, the condensation will bead a little more evenly. The tessellation will hold a little tighter. For a few nights, the whole table will breathe in slightly better unison, as if it had taken a deep, restful breath after a long day of being unobserved.

And then that too will fade, which is as it should. Even a blessing should erode here.

When you stand up from your dome at noon, your legs asleep, you will not see that anything has happened. The pavement will be still. The Stiltwalkers will be picking their way through seams. A Veil will drift three meters up, trailing its threads. The Spirals will wait with their cool throats open.

You will feel, though, that the air is a little easier to breathe. That the silence is not emptiness but fullness holding itself in. That you are not alone on the plain, even though you see no one else.

The power will be gone the way it came - without ever having left, because to leave would be to make a gap. It will be the wind now, sighing one way for hours. It will be the extra coolness in a Spiral's throat for a season. It will be the reason no road ever quite finds its way up here.

If you ask whether it did anything, the honest answer is: almost nothing.

If you ask whether that almost-nothing mattered, put your hand on the warm stone.

Feel it breathe.
