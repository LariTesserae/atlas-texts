They would come and they would not land.

That is the first thing.

Anything that could do what you mean by a benevolent and wise power could have come down like weather — bright, descending, rearrangement. But anything wise knows that to arrive is already to change the pressure.

So it does not arrive.

It notices. Which is different.

From very far away — far enough that the plateau is a pale, wet coin on the shoulder of the mountain — it notices that there is a place that has kept its vow.

And it pays attention the way water pays attention to stone: by taking its shape exactly.

For a long time it does nothing that you would call doing.

### The Listening

It listens the way the pillars listen — not with ears, but by being in contact.

The power thins itself out until it is no wider than the cool wind that already lives there, and lets itself be sifted through the same throats, between the same hand's-breadth gaps. It does not add wind. It *becomes* a little more of the wind that is already there.

For what might be a year or a thousand years — the plateau does not keep that kind of time and neither does the power — it simply moves with the circuit.

It beads as condensation on a crown and runs as a film down a flank.
It evaporates.
It condenses again on the next pillar.

It hums in the hollow chamber of an old one and feels how the hum travels flank-to-flank, miles, without loss. It learns that white roar is not noise to them. It is body. It is how they hold each other without touching too hard.

And it understands then what it is looking at.

This is not a place that is stuck. This is a place that has succeeded.

Everywhere else, life solves the problem of living by motion — by hunger and chase, by bloom and rot, by migration and death and replacement. This place solved it by agreement. No taking more than is given back. No louder than the others. No higher than the others for long. To die by drying quietly and letting your neighbors lean in. To divide only when the gap you leave would otherwise be a wound.

There is no suffering here the way lowland brains file suffering. There is also no joy the way lowland brains file joy. There is something rarer than either: *sufficiency without remainder.*

A wise power knows how unbelievably rare that is. In most of the universe, equilibrium is what things pass through on their way to somewhere else.

Here, equilibrium is where they live.

### The Temptations

Because it is benevolent, three temptations come to it, the way temptations always come to the benevolent when they see a strange perfection.

The first is to relieve them.

*You are so loud,* it thinks, with a lowland heart. *You have been roaring without pause for a million years. Let me give you a moment of silence, just one. Let me lift the wind, just an hour. You could hear how your own hum sounds when it is not drowned.*

The second is to enrich them.

*You are so bare. Let me soften you. A little soil in the gaps — a little green that could live on your weeping. It would be so lovely to see color move here. Let me bring birds that stay, not just those that are ferried over. Let me give you variation.*

The third is to wake them.

*You are so alone. You are one chord played forever. Let me give you a second chord, so you could have music. Let me give you a question that has an answer that changes.*

Any one of these it could do with less effort than it takes you to lift your foot from the pillar crown. A little less wind here, a few spores there, a new resonance slipped into the rock.

And because it is wise, it sees in the same instant what each gift would actually be.

Silence would not be rest. It would be amputation. Their bodies are tuned to that roar as your body is tuned to air pressure. Remove it and they would strain to hear themselves and find nothing to hold onto. Many would dry, listening too hard.

Green would not be decoration. It would be invasion. The biofilm that lives as a sheen so thin it is almost not alive survives precisely because it does not presume to cover anything. A hungry green thing would pry the pillars apart seeking purchase. It would drink the film that is their blood and call it resource. The plateau would cease to be a colony and become a battleground, as all lowland places are.

A second chord would not be music. It would be extinction for those standing whorls of sound that have held for longer than any pillar has been alive. They exist only because the conditions never vary. A new note, however beautiful to an outside ear, would collapse them like breath on a soap film.

Every gift that a lowland heart imagines is a form of breaking.

The power feels the old, familiar ache of benevolence — to *do* something visible, to prove that caring has happened. And it lets the ache pass through it the way the plateau lets water pass: without keeping any extra.

### What It Does Instead

So it does almost nothing.

And almost nothing, done by something that could do anything, is immense.

First, it answers them in the only language that does not require them to change.

It chooses a cluster of older pillars — seven of them in a ring around a slightly taller central one that has been humming a low F for perhaps eighty thousand years — and it leans in. Not with weight, with resonance.

It matches their pitch exactly. For a long while it just holds that low note with them, so perfectly that if you had your palm on the central pillar you would not feel a difference, only a sudden, inexplicable sense that the hum had become rounder, less lonely, as if the stone had finally heard its own echo return from somewhere that was not stone.

Then, very softly, it adds the smallest possible thickening — not louder, not a new note, but a warmth inside the note that was already there. As if the same chord were being sung by one more voice that knows how to not be heard over the others.

The whorls of standing sound in that cluster brighten. They do not change shape. They just become a little more themselves. The bacterial sheen on the sheltered faces darkens by a fraction, not spreading, just — respiring a little deeper.

To the pillars, this does not feel like contact from outside. It feels like realizing they have always been heard. Like that moment when you have been humming alone for a very long time and someone hums with you without interrupting, and you understand that your humming was never just to fill silence, it was a song after all.

It holds that for a while. Not forever — a few of your heartbeats, which for them is a season.

Then it lets go, and the chord goes back to exactly what it was, unchanged and not needing to remember.

That is the gift.

Second, it does the thing that only a power can do, which is to protect by withdrawing.

It knows that your foot was not the first foot, and would not be the last. That other lowland brains would come with tools that could find purchase in those hand's-breadth gaps, with hungers that could call that perfect weeping "water resource" and that white roar "power source." That they would call the pillars rock and therefore fair to quarry.

So as it thins itself back out of the wind, it folds the light a little.

Not a wall. Not a curse. Just a slight increase in convincingness. To any eye from below, the plateau will now seem a little more glare, a little more empty distance, a little more of that bright haze that says *nothing there worth the climb.* To any map it will be a little more inclined to be mis-copied. To any dream of conquest it will be a little less memorable. It will still be there, wet and shining and roaring, for anyone who needs it badly enough to come on foot and with reverence.

But it will be harder to want it for the wrong reasons.

That is the protection. The only protection that does not also become a cage.

And third — because wise benevolence always knows that attention itself is a weight — it leaves.

It does not linger to be thanked. The pillars have no concept of thanks. To be witnessed was enough; to be witnessed too long would become a new sun, a pressure they would have to turn into charge and could not.

Before it goes, in the last place your boot was, where water has already rushed in to fill the pale print of your sole, it lets one drop fall that is not quite water. Not heavier. Not holier. Just a little more *remembered*. If you tasted it you would say it tasted like clear water that had once been held in a kind hand.

That drop beads on the crown of a knee-high young pillar, runs down its flank in a thread, evaporates, condenses, beads again. The same circuit, for centuries. A little of that kindness will be in the system for a very long time, moving flank to flank.

No one will see it. No one needs to.

The plateau goes on being exactly itself without you.

The sun presses small and hard and very near through the thin air. The water weeps and shines. The wind sifts constant through the throats and the white roar fills your molars and the pillars lean just enough to stay crowded and well.

Nothing has been improved.

Everything has been kept.
