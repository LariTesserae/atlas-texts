It would not come.

That is the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power does not *arrive*. Arrival is what meteors and colonists and gods who want to be worshipped do. It would have already been looking in that direction, and then it would look *more carefully*, and that more-careful looking would be the whole of the intervention.

Because what it sees, when it really sees this place, is not a problem.

There is nothing to fix here. Nothing to save. No tragedy to correct, no scarcity to relieve, no loneliness to cure. The temptation for any power - even a kind one - is to misread strangeness as suffering. To think: *it is too cold, too dark, too thin, too slow, too alone. I should bring it light. I should bring it company. I should bring it purpose.*

A wise one knows that this cold *is* the purpose. That the darkness is not a lack of light but a different kind of light. That the slowness is not delay but metabolism.

It would understand within a single long breath that this plateau is not a failed earth. It is a successful something else. A factory that never needed a maker because it learned to make itself. A prayer that learned to answer itself.

So it would not terraform. It would not uplift the furnace-beetles into speech. It would not give the herd a shepherd or the predator a name. It would not cut a road so others could come and admire it, because to be admired by many would kill it faster than any ice age.

What would it do, then, if it is truly benevolent? If it is truly wise?

It would do four very small things and one very large thing. All of them would be almost invisible. The plateau would not notice them as events at all. It would just, over a long time, feel slightly less alone in the universe. The way you feel when someone in another room is awake with you at 3 a.m., not talking, just awake.

### 1. It would learn its tempo before it touched anything.

It would not speak in human time. Human time is frantic, it wants answers now.

It would sit - if a power like that sits - on the edge of the basalt table and breathe when the mats breathe. Inhale for an acre. Exhale for an acre. It would watch a whole frost cycle, wool-thick at dusk, gone without melting by dawn, *plip* by *plip* into the peat. It would watch a beetle finish a single braid of black glass, cooling, ticking, frosting over. It would watch the moth-birds row the thin air for a night and not call it silence, but call it singing at a frequency it had not yet learned to hear.

It would ask permission without words, the way you ask an old forest permission by being still long enough that the forest resumes around you.

And the plateau, which has never been asked anything, would answer by continuing. Which is consent.

### 2. It would draw a veil, not a wall.

The greatest danger to a place like this is not cold or hunger. It is being *found* by a clever, hungry elsewhere. By eyes that see a warm anomaly on a satellite map and think "geothermal potential." By minds that see iron slag braids and think "resource."

A wise power would not need to fight those eyes. It would simply make the plateau uninteresting to them.

Not invisible - wise powers do not lie. But *unremarkable*. A slight fold in attention. The satellite passes over and its algorithms log: basalt, thin air, no biomass, no profit. The prospector dreams of it one night and wakes having forgotten the dream. The map-maker's pen slips around it. It would become one of those places that every chart agrees is there but no one quite remembers to visit.

It would keep its terrible, beautiful secrecy. Because secrecy, here, is not hiding. It is integrity.

### 3. It would mend the small fraying underneath.

Even wise places wear thin. The geothermal veins that make the warm eyes, that keep the orange velvet alive, that keep the catchers from freezing shut - they wander. On the scale of mountains they are already dying, cooling by a fraction of a degree per thousand years.

The power would not drill. It would not stoke. It would put its hand - if it has a hand - very lightly on the warm place under the basalt, the way you put your hand on a sleeping child's forehead not to wake it but to make sure the fever hasn't risen.

And it would give it a little more time. Not forever. Nothing benevolent gives forever. Just a longer exhale. A few hundred thousand more years of *chuff... chuff...* from the fumaroles. Enough that the furnace-beetles can finish the field they are smelting. Enough that the predator can take its long steps between terraces three million more times.

It would not be felt as heat. It would be felt as the absence of a premature ending.

### 4. It would keep company with the one who is alone on purpose.

You felt it - the three-toed absence. The regulator. The one who is spaced apart so far that spacing becomes its nature.

Everything else on the plateau has a kind. Mats with mats, pillars with pillars, beetles with distant beetles. Even the herd has its single-file line. But the predator has only itself, by design. That is lonely in a way no human loneliness can touch. Not sad-lonely. Structural-lonely.

A wise power would not give it a mate. That would be cruelty, to double a thing that exists to be singular. It would not tame it.

It would simply *witness* it, completely, when nothing else does. When it moves between terraces in the blink you missed, the power would see. When it lies still for days under a frond the size of a door and does not steam, the power would know it is there and not call it empty. When it kills - cleanly, rarely, without waste - and then lies beside the cooling body while the slime does its quiet work, the power would not look away.

To be seen accurately, without wanting to change you, is the rarest form of love. It would give the predator that. And through the predator, it would give it to the whole plateau.

### 5. And the large thing: It would remember.

This is what benevolent powers are actually for. Not to intervene, but to remember on a scale that outlasts stone.

The plateau will not last. Nothing does. The thin air will thin further or thicken. The basalt will finally cool. The catchers will close for the last time and not open. The last warm eye will skin over with ice that does not melt. The last beetle's amber will go dark.

This is not tragedy. This is completion. But completion without witness is a different kind of cold.

So the power would do what the peat does, what the slag braids do, what the orange velvet does - it would keep a record not in writing but in itself. It would take the exact frequency of the mats crackling blue in dry air, the exact way frost sublimates without melting, the exact amber pulse of a furnace gut, the exact peripheral glow that disappears when you look, and it would hold it.

Not to bring it back. Not to clone it or archive it or display it.

Just so that once, somewhere, something lived like this: lush in darkness, industrial without industry, abundant without crowding, patient beyond patience, making its own warmth in a place the sun had abandoned.

And that once something was *known* while it lived, loved precisely for what it was and not for what it could be made into.

---

If you went back after the power had done all this, you would find nothing has changed.

The air would still be a cold pane. The chuff would still come every forty seconds from a mile off. The black-purple fronds would still be warm underneath and still glow only when you don't look. A stone you dropped would still fall a little too slow.

You might, if you stayed long and held your breath long, feel that the silence is a fraction less empty. As if the darkness at noon were listening back, not hungry, not wanting, just - with you.

You would kneel by a warm eye and the orange velvet would still be there, breathing. And for a second you would have the absurd, certain feeling that the water knows you are kneeling.

That is all a wise benevolence ever does, when it really loves something wild.

It does not improve it.

It keeps it safe from improvement.

And it stays, at a respectful distance, keeping the kind of quiet company that lets the wild remain wild - only now, the wild is no longer unseen.
