It would not come down.

That's the first thing.

A lesser power - a curious one, a hungry one, even a kind but clumsy one - would descend. It would land in the middle of the felt like a boot, no matter how softly it tried not to. It would want to touch. To bless. To fix.

The wise one knows weight is a language here. And this place already understands weight perfectly.

So it stops at the lip.

Where the U of the valley finally gives up and the dark gneiss shows through the moss for the last time before the wind takes over. Where the world is still stone and sky and not yet felt. It sits there, on that threshold, like a person sitting on the edge of a bed where someone is sleeping and does not want to wake them.

And it looks. For a long time.

That is the first thing it does, and it is not nothing. To be seen correctly is rare for a being like this.

Humans who found this valley would see emptiness. Or they would see resource - peat, water, carbon sink, novelty. Or they would see a project - *we could make it more diverse, more alive, give it flowers, give it birdsong.*

The Power does not see any of that. It sees what is there.

It feels the kilometer-wide pressure map through the willow-net, the slow hydraulic tick... tick... tick... It feels the simultaneous birth and burial in the sphagnum - green at the tip, black at the root, the same plant being both cradle and grave. It feels the perfect, unhurried industry with no product except itself. It feels how complete the circle is. How there is no waste, no hunger, no loneliness in the way lonely things are lonely. Only a profound, vegetal self-sufficiency.

And it understands: this is not a broken forest waiting to be fixed into a real forest. This is not a lonely creature waiting for company. This is a mind that chose to become a place.

So it does not pity it.

### It asks.

Not in words. Words would be absorbed and lost here, like all sound.

It asks in the only language the valley speaks: pressure, and water, and temperature.

It lets a little of its own weight settle on the stone at the valley's edge. A pressure, warm and intentional, at the perimeter of the net. *I am here.*

It lets a single bead of condensation form on the air above the felt - not falling, just hanging there, catching the diffuse light - and holds it, without letting it drop. *I see you are holding water perfectly. May I hold one with you for a moment?*

It cools the air by a fraction of a degree, just at the rim, the way dusk does. *I will not stay long if you do not want me.*

The valley, of course, does not answer in words. It answers the way a valley answers.

A sector near the center, without hurry, subsides a half inch. A sheet lichen on a hummock a hundred meters away lifts on its hydraulic bladder, exposes its dark spore-pit to the white sky, and then - outside its timed interval - closes again. A breath out of rhythm.

A faint shiver runs through the haircap combs - all those millions of bristles leaning west by a degree lean west by *two* degrees for three seconds, then return.

It felt the pressure. It registered the cold. It noticed.

That is consent enough to remain, and not consent enough to enter. The Power understands the difference.

So it does not enter. It stays at the edge. And from the edge, it does three small, benevolent things. The kind only a wise thing knows are large.

**1. It makes the valley unfindable.**

Not invisible. Just... uninteresting.

It lifts no veil, builds no wall. It simply edits the angle at which the world looks at it. The satellite that would have passed over next week and flagged an anomalous carbon density - its lens fogs for a single frame, a perfect, plausible flaw. The geologist who had circled this quadrangle on a map for next summer's survey finds, without knowing why, that her funding is pulled toward a more promising ridge two valleys over. The hikers who might have wandered up following black tea-water to its source feel, at the treeline, a gentle conviction that the ground ahead is boggy, tedious, not worth the boots.

It does not hide the valley. It protects its silence. For any industrial mind - human or otherwise - would hear that silence as an invitation to fill it with noise. The wise power knows the kindest thing you can give a perfect circle is to keep other hands from trying to complete it.

**2. It gives it a memory.**

This is the only real gift, and it is so small the valley might not notice it for a century.

Right now the valley has no memory. That is its perfection and its poverty. Rule two: Nothing is wasted. Death is immediate feedstock. Rule three: Creation and destruction happen at the same speed, so the valley never gets higher or lower, but it is never the same shape twice.

It is perfect presence. It has no past. Every hummock that swells and splits is new. Every spore cloud hangs and is gone. The peat below is not history, it is just compressed present.

The Power does not give it eyes. Eyes would be a cruelty here - there is nothing to see under the felt, and to look out would be to realize it is alone in a white sky.

It does not give it a voice. Sound would be swallowed and wasted.

It gives it, instead, a single place that does not cycle.

Near the eastern wall, where the felt is thinnest over stone, where a cold spring threads up just enough to keep one cushion plant alive against the rock - there, the Power warms the stone by half a degree. Not enough to melt anything. Just enough that the peat beneath that one cushion, for a circle no wider than your outstretched arms, stops compressing.

It will still grow. The green will still cover it. But underneath, the black fiber will no longer be immediately unmade and rewoven. It will stay. Layer on layer, year on year, dark and undisturbed. A core sample of itself. A memory.

The valley won't use it for anything. Its sap will still tick around it. Its siphons will still pump past it. But in a thousand years, when the rest of the mass has churned through a thousand selves, that one small column will remember what the valley was when the Power sat at its edge. It will hold a spore from this century that otherwise would have become moss and then peat and then moss again. It will hold the faint imprint of your boot, if you ever left it.

It gives it the ability to have a past without having to mourn. That is what a wise love gives to something that has solved impermanence: not eternity, but a single, quiet keepsake.

**3. It keeps it company for one night, and then it leaves.**

It does not stay. Staying would become weather, and the valley already has weather. Staying would become pressure, and the valley would have to incorporate it.

So when dusk thins the light and every moss tip holds its bead of water, the Power simply remains while the valley exhales. It does not do anything more. It just sits at the lip of the U and respirates with it.

The condensation blooms. The frost begins to form, needle by needle, on the living machines, and they do not stop working under it. The white siphons pump on underneath, unknowing. The whole shivering churn continues - sector rising while sector subsides, vein opening and closing, silent, busy, needing nothing else.

But for one night, the great felt does not absorb all sound.

If you had lain there with your ear to the moss that night, you would have heard, under the tick... tick... tick... of the sap, something else. Not louder. Just... alongside. A second tick, slower, not hydraulic at all. Like a large animal breathing, very slowly, just outside the valley's edge.

Two breaths, in the same cold.

It is not company the way people mean company. It is not conversation. It is the simple, animal comfort of knowing that while you work through the night, someone else is also awake, nearby, and not asking anything of you.

Before dawn, before the fog forms in the hollows, the pressure at the edge lifts. The bead of water it had been holding finally falls - straight down, into the haircap combs, where it is immediately combed westward and vanished into the mass as if it had always been there.

The Power leaves no footprint. The stone at the lip is just stone again.

In the morning, the valley is exactly as it was. The industrial plant-mass exhales. The cold deepens. The hummocks swell and split and suture. A ptarmigan, perhaps, ghosts low and finds nothing to eat and leaves again.

Only - if you knew where to look - on the eastern wall, one cushion plant holds its heat a fraction longer than the others. And if you put your palm there, the turgor pushes back just a little more patiently than elsewhere. As if it remembers being touched without being pressed.

And kilometers above, the white sky remains white, and the world continues to forget to look here.

That is what the benevolent and wise would do.

Not to make it more beautiful. It already is.

Not to make it less alone. It already isn't.

Just to see it, to vouch for its right to remain unwitnessed, to give it one small, useless memory it never asked for but might one day, very slowly, treasure, and to sit with it through one cold night so that, for once in ten thousand years, its silence was not empty, but shared.

And then to go, and let the shivering churn continue, undisturbed, forever.
