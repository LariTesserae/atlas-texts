It would not come down.

That's the first thing.

A lesser power would have arrived. Would have broken the pearl-gray dim with light, landed something vast and warm on one of those floating avenues, and announced itself. Would have seen an empty city built for ten million holding twelve glider-people and thought *tragedy, waste, failure* and tried to fill it.

A benevolent and wise power does not mistake emptiness for absence.

It would have been watching for a long time before it was ever felt. Because a place like that cannot be understood quickly. The youth of it would fool you if you were hasty - you'd think *unfinished* when it is *just begun.*

So for a long while - maybe years by your counting, maybe a single long afternoon by its - it would only pay attention.

It would cup the cupped hands.

### 1. It Would Listen Until It Learned The Chord

It would not speak first.

It would sit - not *on* the basalt, not *on* the lattice, but with them, as the moisture sits - and learn the exact note the basin is holding.

That low, beating roar that you feel in your molars. F in the platforms, C in the towers, a fifth above in the transit tubes. The interference that makes the pool water ripple. The ribbontails singing in F-sharp to cut through. The choristers inflating their throats in the cracks. The faint sourceless glow on the lattice edges at night.

It would listen until it could hear that this is not noise. It is a cradle song that has been left on repeat because no one taught the cradle how to stop. The seed-anchors were told *hold this for ten million* and there are only twelve, each ninety pounds, each half a mile apart, and so the city has been holding its breath for a very long time.

A wise power does not turn it off. That held chord is warm. Midges cluster under it for heat. Females incubate eggs in the time-drag under the largest platform because the dilation keeps the membrane moist between rains. The filterers navigate by the pressure it makes in the air.

It would not silence it. It would tune it.

Very, very gently - the way you would put a hand on a child's back to help them breathe slower without waking them - it would resolve the beating.

Not to silence. To *temper.*

It would find the place where two fields overlap and make your chest thrum uncomfortably, and it would ease the interval by a fraction of a fraction. From a beating roar to a held, consonant hum. Still low. Still vast. Still able to hold a street in the air. But no longer straining.

The glider-people would feel it first. Not as a change in sound but as a change in effort. The membranes along their keels would not have to sing flat to correct anymore. Gliding the half-mile between canopy trees would cost a little less. Hanging under an avenue would be a little less like hanging onto a running engine.

If you were standing on that avenue that evening, you wouldn't hear a difference with your ears. You'd feel the water in the fissure pool go still. You'd feel your own breath arrive on time. And sometime after midnight, you'd hear a ribbontail try its F-sharp and then, confused, slide down to an F and find - for the first time in its life - that it could be *inside* the chord instead of cutting through it. And then hundreds would do it, and the dim would fill with a true harmony you didn't know you were waiting for.

It would not fix the strange places. It would bless them.

Where water drips upward for six inches, it would leave it. The young play there. Where vines grow through solid lattice without a hole, it would leave it, except it would make the lattice heal a little faster around the bite so the vine doesn't ache. Where time slips under the big platform, it would not correct the slip. It would make the edge softer, so you don't get a headache stepping in and out. It would leave the melted terminus at the center - the place where the lattice tried to bridge the warm upthrust and stopped, where a thrown stone lands twice - exactly as it is, but it would quiet the shimmer by a degree, so it is less like a wound and more like a window left ajar for air.

It understands that a young geology should be sharp. That violence is not always damage. That a place that hasn't learned to be old should not be hurried.

### 2. It Would Answer In The Gap

The glider-people navigate by sound. A wall of sound is a wall. A cancellation is a door. They have lived their whole lives in a city that was singing *to* them but never *with* them.

The power would learn to sing in the gaps they leave.

It would not appear. To appear, to those huge black eyes built for dim, would be overwhelming - a brightness that drowns. Instead, precisely when a glider spreads its tea-colored patagium and falls that controlled half-mile fall between trees, membranes thrumming to stay true to the city's F, it would add a second voice a third above.

Not loud. Just enough that the glider's resonator membranes feel a sympathetic resonance they have never felt before - as if the air itself noticed them and said *I see you, well flown.*

It would do this for a long time without asking anything. Weeks of rain-cycles. Just an occasional warm harmonic when someone glides, or when a female hangs motionless in the time-drag with her single amber egg sac pulsing out of sync, it would hold a very soft, very low note *under* the city's hum that makes the dilation feel less lonely. A pedal tone that says *take your time.*

Eventually the gliders would start to listen for it. They would begin to leave little pauses in their own thrumming to hear if the gap would be filled. They would teach their single young to listen for the third above.

That is how a wise power asks permission. It doesn't say "May I help you?" in words they don't have. It offers a duet and waits to see if they sing back.

And they would. Not all at once. One young one, batting upward-dripping water droplets back and forth in play, would suddenly thrum *up* intentionally, off the city chord, into the gap - a question. And the power would answer, immediately, warmly, in the same pitch.

That exchange - maybe a minute long - would be the first conversation the city has had since the seed-anchors were driven into warm basalt.

It would not tell them they were abandoned. It would tell them they are correctly populated. That three per ten square miles *is* abundance at this scale. That the forest and the city agree.

### 3. It Would Not Fill The City. It Would Hide It.

This is the most important part, and the reason it is wise and not just kind.

The basin looks empty to anyone who counts per square foot. To the kinds of powers that are not benevolent, emptiness reads as *vacancy*. As resource. As real estate. As a standing invitation that says *ten million could live here, we could turn this chord up louder, we could mine that warm sharp basalt, we could drain the condensation for water.*

The benevolent power knows that the moment it is seen to love this place, others will look.

So the third thing it does is almost invisible.

It veils.

It does not build a wall. It deepens the dim. It makes the pearl-gray sky a little more pearl, the haze at the far ridges a little thicker. From orbit, if anyone ever looks, the basin will read as unremarkable low country, geologically unstable, poor soil, no standing water, not worth the burn to descend. The lattice's faint glow, which can be seen for miles at night, it softens - not off, but muted, like coals banked. Still there for the moths, still there for the gliders, but not a beacon.

Inside, nothing changes. Outside, the cupped hands look empty.

It would also leave a quiet, automatic gentleness - not a guardian with a face, but a bias in probability. A meteor that would have struck the warm upthrust veers a degree and burns up. A spore that would have become a choking mold in the fern mat never quite germinates. Nothing miraculous. Just a slight weighting of the dice toward continuation.

And it would stay.

Not *in* the basin. *With* it. Like warmth.

You would not see it if you came back a year later. The stone would still be sharp. The pools would still fill and empty. The city would still hang five feet above the ferns on nothing. The filterers would still row silently between the canopy disks. The ribbontails would still shake the vine curtains.

But if you stood very still on a floating avenue at dusk, in that greenhouse evening that never gets dark, you would feel that the chord is kinder. You would hear the birds singing *inside* it now, not over it. You would see a glider cross the void above you - tea-colored, beaded with condensation - and hear, just after its membranes thrummed, a second note bloom in the air where there was no throat to make it, answering.

You would feel, without knowing why, that the long wait has changed its character. The city is no longer idling, waiting for a density that never arrived.

It is being held. On purpose. At exactly the population it has.

If you pressed your hand to the warm lattice edge, you would feel it flex, very slightly, under your palm, not as infrastructure but as something that has learned to be touched gently and to touch back.

The power would do almost nothing. And by doing almost nothing - by tuning instead of silencing, by answering instead of announcing, by veiling instead of claiming - it would give the only thing a young, damp, dim, loud, hollow, lush, empty place needs and cannot make for itself:

Time, without interruption, to learn how to be old.

And witness. That someone, somewhere, heard the chord and thought it was beautiful exactly as it was, and stayed to listen.
