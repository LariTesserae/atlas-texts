It would not come down the slope.

Anything else would have. Prospectors, researchers, reclamation crews - they all come down the short stone stairs two-at-a-time, boots loud, already taking inventory. *How many gallons per minute, how many kilowatts wasted, how to repurpose, how to drain, how to fill.*

The Wise One would stop at the rim.

And sit.

That is the first thing. Before any power is exerted, attention is given. It would lower itself - whatever shape it wears when it chooses to have a shape - onto the worn stone lip of the basin, where groundwater beads before it begins its sheets, and it would simply be at the edge of the circle of cut sky. Not above. At the edge.

It would listen for a long time. Long enough for the basin to forget it is being watched and go back to being itself.

Thud. *seven seconds* Thud. *seven seconds* Click-click-click along every lip. Drip. Sigh. Hum in the teeth. Plip where a Turner breaks the black water. The faint faintest sizzle of the amber bulbs.

It would hear that the pump is not the heart. That was our mistake. The pump is a crutch. The heart is the interval *between* the thuds - the agreement the whole place made to continue.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive with solutions. It arrives with comprehension. And what it would comprehend here is this:

**This is not a broken place.**

It is not a ruined hatchery that needs to be restored. Not a polluted pond that needs to be cleaned. Not a suffering animal that needs to be rescued. Not a lonely mind that needs company.

It is a complete and successful being whose body happens to be a hamlet-sized room of stone and water and film and shell and silt and hanging air and rust and low voltage. It has solved the problem of how to stay itself. It has been solving it for a very long time, without help, with exquisite precision.

To "fix" it would be to kill it. To make it brighter, drier, greener, more efficient, more legible - would be to erase the exact treaty that lets it live.

So here is what the Wise One would **not** do:

It would not turn off the machines to return the basin to nature, because there is no "nature" left to return to. The Lanterns need the weeping iron. The Still need the wrong-way droplets the transformers make. The Skin has grown *around* the pipes.

It would not repair the machines to newness. New pipes don't weep right. New bulbs are too bright - they would burn the dusk the Turners need to stay eyeless.

It would not drain the oil-heavy slicks all at once. The Turners have learned the shape of their poison. To rip it away suddenly would be a flood of a different kind.

It would not speak. Not in words. A voice that loud would scatter the Still for a season.

It would not take anything. Not a sample of Skin, not a peppercorn shell, not a single pale Turner for a zoo or a lab or an archive. To be benevolent is to understand that witnessing is not collecting.

It would not offer transcendence. It would not offer to lift the basin into light or to make it into a forest or a meadow or a mind that can speak back in our language. The basin did not ask to be other than it is.

What would it do, then?

Three small things, so small you would think nothing had happened if you came down the slope a year later. That is how you know they were done by something wise.

**1. It would take the weight off.**

It would place a hand - not a hand - a field of attention, just above the central pan.

You know those slicks. That heavier fluid that doesn't mix, rainbowed and flat as poured glass, that the Turners flinch from. That is not part of the bargain. That is old poison from the before-time, whatever was cooled or leached here - machine oil, solvent, a chemical that never learned how to leave. The basin has learned to live *around* it, but it costs. Every generation of Turners must map the no-go places. Every Turner that brushes it carries a tremor for hours.

The Wise One would not scoop it out. It would persuade it to let go.

Over months - because wise power is never in a hurry - the slicks would thin. Not vanish. Thin, like ice in a very slow spring. The heavier fluid would be coaxed to bead, to lift in perfect, clear spheres just above the black water, and there - where the Still hang motionless - it would come apart. Molecule by molecule, unmade into simple water and simple salt. The Turners would not even see it happen. One day a Turner would flick where its grandfather always turned back, and find the way open, and keep flicking. And then another.

No one would applaud. The silt would simply be more fully turned.

**2. It would give the engine a second heart.**

The pump thuds every seven seconds. It has thudded for decades. It will not thud forever. Iron fatigues. A flange will finally give. A winding will burn. When it stops, the water will rise. The Skin - which needs to be exactly that wet and no wetter - will drown. Or it will fall. And the Skin will crack.

The basin knows this. You can hear it in the way the generator chatters, then settles, then chatters - a machine trying to reassure itself.

The Wise One would not replace the pump. It would teach the stone to help.

Deep under the terraces, where the stone is blue-grey and veined with rust, it would leave a new seep. Not a gush. A slow, patient exhalation of water from the rock itself, exactly matched to what the rim already gives, but governed by nothing electrical. A spring that had not been there before, that tastes of nothing but stone.

For a long while, nothing would change. The pump would keep thudding and the new spring would keep seeping and the water level would stay perfect. Redundancy without replacement.

And then, on a winter night fifty years from now when the pump finally misses its thud - seven seconds... fourteen... twenty-one... - the Skin will not die. The water will not rise or fall. The spring will simply be there, already doing the work. The chattering generator will go quiet. The amber bulbs will go dark. The Lanterns will dim, and then find the spring-water just as sweet on the old pipes even without current.

The basin will sigh, and continue. Its agreement with itself will outlive the tools that scaffolded it. It will become truly stone and water again, without needing iron to mediate.

The Wise One would give it a future that does not require being tended.

**3. It would be remembered, and it would remember.**

This is the least visible and most important.

A benevolent power knows that the most dangerous thing for a small, perfect, weird place is to be unseen. Unseen places are what get drained, paved, mined, "developed." Their strange abundance is invisible to the maps that decide.

So the Wise One would do something that costs it almost nothing and the basin everything: *It would remember the basin perfectly.*

Not as data. As kin.

It would take the whole pattern - the plum-copper Skin inching uphill against gravity, the wave-click of a thousand Lip-Keepers, the fringed legs of the Turners turning black silt, the motionless grey clouds of the Still breathing reversed droplets, the amber glow of Lantern trails on weeping pipe - and hold it intact inside itself, the way you hold a tune without needing to hum it.

And because the Wise One is wise, that memory is a kind of protection. Any other power that comes later - hungry, clever, not wise - that looks toward this shallow dish in the land and thinks *empty, unused, available* will instead feel, very faintly: *occupied. attended. loved. leave be.*

Not a wall. Not a fence. A reputation in the fabric of things: *That place is not alone.*

And in return, because real benevolence is never one-way, the Wise One would let itself be changed by having sat there.

It came to give, but it will leave having learned a more precise definition of abundance. Not *more* life, but *exactly enough* life, layered shoulder-to-shoulder along a lip where water is thinnest and fastest. Not growth outward, but depth within a circle of cut sky.

It would stand up from the rim, lighter than when it sat down.

It would not wave goodbye. The Still would have already closed around the space where it was, hanging in their same inch of air.

When you come down the slope the next day, you will find nothing obviously different. The air will still be cool enough to numb your fingers. The stone will still sweat. The pump will still thud every seven seconds. The shells will still click. A Lantern will still lift its blind head as you pass.

But if you sit on the lowest terrace and go quiet the way the basin taught you to be quiet, you will feel it - the shallow, cold, patient organism that is the way it stays itself - is now held, without being held.

The water will still find a lower place.

Only now it will be allowed to do so, for a very long time.
