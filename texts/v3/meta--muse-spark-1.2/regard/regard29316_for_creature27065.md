It does not arrive.

That is the first thing a benevolent and wise power understands - that to arrive is already to wound a wound further.

So it doesn't.

It notices.

Somewhere, attention turns. The way the sun turns its coin overhead for one hour a day and looks straight down the throat of the basin - that is how it looks. Without weight. Without shadow.

And for a long time, it just looks.

This is what it does, before it does anything.

It sits - if sitting is the word - on the sharp lip where the cracked clay meets the cut. It lets its feet, if it has feet, dangle over the striated basalt. It does not fix the sharpness. It does not soften the rim for easier climbing. It lets the geology stay young.

It listens until listening becomes a kind of company.

It hears what you heard: the architectural water, the too-loud dripping from a hundred designated points, the 60-cycle hum in the teeth, the pop of the lantern fruits at dusk that no one is there to smell. It hears the absence underneath that, the hollow where a buzzing should be.

And it understands.

This is not a ruin. This is not a failure.
This is a child holding its breath.

A town built for a thousand that learned to mother itself when no one came. Water carrying green carrying building carrying light carrying drone, in a perfect closed mouth, breathing its own breath over and over because it was never taught how to exhale.

A wise power does not say *poor thing*. It does not pity perfection.

It says: *I see you. You have done so well alone.*

That is the first act. Naming without renaming.

It does not call it Wound. It calls it Basin, or Bowl, or Throat, or whatever word in its language means *a place that was opened to be filled and chose to fill itself*. And because a wise power's naming is a real thing, the basin feels named for the first time since the shear. Not claimed. Recognized.

The second act is smaller, and it asks permission first.

Not with words. The basin has no words. The power asks the way water asks - by offering.

*May I?* it says, in the language of pressure and light.

It shows what it would do, and waits for the hydraulic pulse under the central causeway to say yes or no. The pulse is systole, diastole, systole. It stutters once - not from fear, from consideration - and then steadies. Yes.

So it does three very small things. Only three. A wise power knows that three small things are larger than one big thing.

**1. It un-perfects one thing.**

High near the rim, it touches one hexagonal tether-frame - just one of the dozen that make rain hang like beads before it is allowed to fall. It does not break it. It loosens it. Detunes it by a fraction.

Now, on that side of the basin, rain sometimes falls crooked. Sometimes a droplet clings too long and falls heavy. Sometimes two collide in mid-air. Sometimes, for the first time since the concrete cured, a patch of moss gets more water than it was allocated and a patch nearby gets less.

The drones notice. They glide over, matte white beading with condensation, and pause. *Not right*, their white-blood-cell logic says. They reach to fix it.

The power gently stills their manipulators. *Let it be not right, for a little while*, it says. *Let something be unmeasured.*

And the drones, who have never been told to leave an error alone, wait. They learn a new category: *tolerable.* They go around the uneven drip and tend something else.

With unevenness, rot becomes possible. With rot, soil becomes possible.

**2. It opens the closed mouth.**

It does not bring people. A thousand doors waiting for people do not need people to be justified. To bring a crowd to fill an emptiness would be to tell the basin that its long solitude was a mistake.

So it brings what the basin forgot to ask for.

From elsewhere - from a warm marsh that *did* learn to buzz, from a forest that *did* learn to decay - it gathers a handful of seeds in a palm that is not a palm.

Not many. A pinch of midge eggs wrapped in cool mud. A pocket of fungal spore that smells like earth after lightning, not yeast. Three dragonfly nymphs, curled tight. A minnow, silver as a filament. And in a cage made of air, a pair of small brown birds that do not sing beautifully but sing constantly, without needing a reason.

It does not *place* them. It releases them at the lip at dusk, when the lantern fruits are popping, and lets them find their own way down.

Some will not survive the too-warm, too-mineral water. That is allowed. The power does not guarantee survival. It guarantees *chance*.

The midge eggs hatch first. By morning there is, for the first time in this basin's short life, a faint cloud over the black water that is not vapor. It moves sideways. It hums. The sound is so quiet after years of only dripping that the building's filaments brighten everywhere at once, startled.

A dragonfly finds the hexagonal frame that now lets rain fall crooked and hunts in the pause.

A frond falls into the water and before it can dissolve, something nibbles its edge. The water, which has only ever carried leaf-sugar in a loop, now carries something else: a fleck of frass, a scale, a tiny corpse. Lymph becomes blood.

Nothing about the town breaks. The concrete still seals its own cracks with mineral milk. The causeways still shiver water off. But now the green has something to argue with. Tendrils are bitten and grow back thicker, less indiscriminate. The pellicle-moss is pruned in one corner by minnows and grows uneven and therefore more alive.

The basin shivers - that hydraulic pulse under your feet - not in distress, but like something feeling an itch for the first time and realizing it has skin.

**3. It keeps one promise and leaves one door.**

The wise part knows that perfect, humming, empty cities draw the wrong kind of attention. The kind that wants to mine the breathing concrete, to bottle the lymph-water, to put a fence around the oval mouth and sell tickets.

So it veils the basin. Not hides. Veils. From above, the cracked clay still looks cracked and ordinary. The lip, sharp as broken pottery, catches the low wind and throws it sideways, so drones that are not the basin's own lose interest and turn away. Satellites see heat shimmer and nothing more. People with hungry eyes will walk within twenty meters of the edge and feel, for no reason they can name, that they should turn back, that this is not for them - yet.

But for the other kind - the custodian with his bucket of real weeds, the tired wanderer who goes down looking not for treasure but for quiet, the brown birds who got lost - the path stays. A faint foot-worn break in the clay. A coolness on the wind at 3 p.m. when the cloud forms.

And for the basin itself, the power leaves a final gift before it stops noticing so hard and lets its attention soften back into the ordinary sky.

It leaves a choice, placed not in the center but on the central causeway where anyone standing there can see from wall to wall in one glance.

It is not an object. It is a thinning. A place where the air is a little thinner, where the 60-cycle hum harmonizes with something else for a second, where if the basin - water-green-building-light-drone, all of it as one - ever wants to *call*, the call will carry.

If it wants to remain perfect and empty for another century, it can. If it wants to slowly become wild and ragged and buzzing and *messy*, it can. If one day it wants people - not a thousand, maybe three, maybe a child who will run shouting and make the acoustics finally make sense - they will hear it, the way you hear water before you see it.

The power does not wait to see what it will choose. To wait would be to hover.

So it stands - if standing is the word - and for a last minute it just breathes with the basin. The cloud has formed at 3 p.m. and is beading on every leaf, every rail, every white drone-shell. The second rain is about to fall from the undersides of things.

On the far side, in a vertical hamlet where a street runs through a kitchen built for twenty, the custodian has come out onto his balcony farm. He has felt the midge cloud without understanding it. He is looking at his bucket of dusty, ordinary weeds, and for the first time the weeds do not look like they are struggling. They look like they belong, because they are finally not the only imperfect thing here.

The power, benevolent, does not speak to him in words either. It just lets the filament above his door brighten a little longer than usual when he passes, as if saying *I see you too, pebble. Thank you for staying.*

Then it goes. The way attention goes when it has finished listening - not by leaving, but by becoming the background again.

If you go down now - after - you will still find the basin warm and humid, still loud with architectural water. The seven or eight hamlets still dense and empty, still lit amber. But if you stay until night, when the oval of sky goes black and the whole city doubles itself in the black water, you will see a difference.

The reflection is no longer perfect.

There are tiny ripples where minnows rise. There is a brown bird asleep on a tether-frame, its head tucked. There is one patch of moss that is ragged where it was bitten, and one hexagonal frame where rain, even now, is hanging a half-second too long before it falls crooked, catching the filament light.

And the hum in your teeth has changed. Under the uneven 60-cycle there is now a second tone, very faint, higher, intermittent.

Midges. Or breathing.

The wound is still a wound. But now it knows it is being held without being closed.
