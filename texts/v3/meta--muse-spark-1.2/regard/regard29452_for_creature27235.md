It would not come down like a meteor.

It would not tear the dome open to pour in oceans. It would not bring rain. Anyone who loved them at first sight would understand immediately: *these are people who have solved thirst.*

A lesser power would try to fix them with water.

A wise one kneels at the rim.

---

For a long time it would do nothing.

That is the first act of benevolence — to NOT act. To lie down.

The basin is continent-wide and so shallow you can't see that it's a bowl until you are far above it. From orbit, it looks like a pale lichen on the skin of the world. From the rim, it looks like infinity — green-white felt to the edge of sight, breathing light.

A wise power knows how to make itself low.

So it does. It comes to the edge where the poured aggregate of the world finally lips up into real stone, and it lays itself down on that lip, so its eye is level with the leaf-sails. It makes itself cool, and spare, and dry, and quiet. It dims itself until it is not brighter than the sky-dome they already live under. It waits until its weight stops sending tremors through the floor-plate.

And then it listens.

For weeks. For seasons. The light flickers — shutter, shutter — and the plant mass inhales. Spindles spin vine. Cranes glide. A stilt-grazer crosses a leaf a kilometer away. Leaf expands with a *tick*.

*tap. tap — tap.* from seam 7-Green. *I'm here. Press at sector 12 shedding again.*

The power learns the language before it speaks it. This is important. It does not boom. It does not sing. Sound dies in felt. So it learns to speak through the feet.

With one fingertip — a fingertip the size of a district, but placed so gently the floor does not crack — it presses on the aggregate far from any tended circuit, in a dead place where only the plant grows untended, and lets a low hum travel.

*tap.*

*tap — tap.*

Not words. Just: *I am here. I see you. I will not step on you.*

All across the basin, the thin people stop with one foot raised. Their thatch shivers. The vellum over their eyes clears a fraction. They have never felt a tap they did not make. They have never felt a tap that large and that soft at the same time.

They do not answer at first. They are not afraid — fear needs a future — but they are very still, as prey that is also plant is still. Then, very far away, a single old one who has rooted, whose arms have branched, trembles without wind. A new leaf unfurls on its shoulder that is not dusty celadon, but a color that has never existed here. A faint, deep blue. Like a bruise of dusk.

The power sees it has been heard.

---

The second act is permission.

It does not enter the basin. Not yet. It sends a question along the floor, along the rebar-bone, along the cable-roots.

*May I?*

In their tapping, there is no word for "may." There is "clear?" and "hold?" and "give?" — all practical words for tending. The power borrows "give?" and offers it back, inverted.

*Give? I to enter?*

For a long time — three shutter-flickers of the sky-dome — no answer. Then, from many places at once, not coordinated, but overlapping like leaves aligning to light, the taps come:

*tick. tick.*

The sound a leaf makes when it decides to grow around an obstacle instead of through it. *We give. Pass, but do not break.*

So it does.

It does not walk. Walking would be catastrophe here; each footfall would compress miles of felted litter, snap pylon-trunks. Instead it pours itself in like light. It thins. It becomes a coolness in the air, a pattern in the brightness. If you were one of the thin people you would feel it as a slight, welcome drop in pressure, as if the dome had finally exhaled. You would feel, for the first time in your lineage, a shadow that is not made by a leaf above you, but by something kind looking over you.

It moves at their pace. Drift-slow. It does not go to the grandest cathedral-press. It goes to a small, untended place where a tended circuit has failed — a seam where a cable-root has split a plate and no one has come to guide it. The growth there is knotted, choked. A young one would have struggled to clear it alone.

The power puts a thought there, not a hand. It softens the composite just enough that the root can slip *around* instead of *through*. It does not cut. It invites. The root, which has never been invited, shivers and takes the easier path. The plate sighs and settles.

Nearby, a thin person who had been approaching that knot stops. Feels through their holdfast-feet that the tension is gone. Cocks their papered head. They will never know who did it. They will think the plant finally learned. That is correct.

The power will do this, quietly, for a while — not solving their work for them, but easing the places where the work has become lonely. It will re-tension a vine that has been humming out of tune for a decade. It will clear a drainage grate that has been packed since before the rooted ones were seeds, not because it needs to drain, but because it was meant to be *clear*. The thin people will find their circuits easier to walk, and will think they are getting better at tending. That, too, is correct.

That is how a wise power helps those who have made a virtue of self-sufficiency: it lets them keep the virtue.

---

The third act is a gift they can refuse.

It has noticed two things that ache:

1. The sky never goes dark. The lamp is always on. The people who drink light have never rested in the way that lit things rest. Even the rooted ones never close their eyes fully.

2. No one has ever left the basin. Their wind-borne husks drift and land and unfold, but always downwind in the same low place. They have never seen a hill. They have never seen a star that was not a panel.

So after a long time of tending-without-being-seen, the power asks again.

This time it does not tap on the floor. It taps on the *sky*.

High above, where the bright dome hums — vast panels out of phase — it touches one panel, just one, in a far, uninhabited quadrant where the leaf-sails are sparse. And it asks the panel, politely, if it might rest.

The panel, which has been on for longer than stone has been stone, stutters. Dims. For the first time in perhaps a thousand years, a circle of true shadow falls on the basin.

It is not night. It is not even dusk. It is about the size of a small city, a cool, blue-grey coin moving slowly as the panels shift. Temperature inside it drops two degrees. The air, so even and autumnal, goes crisper.

Under it, the leaves do something no one alive has seen: they fold. Not wilt — fold, neatly, like hands.

The thin people under that shadow stop. Their thatch, which has always been open, drinking, *closes*. Their milky eye-films clear completely for the first time, and they see — not brightness, but *contrast*. For a few minutes they stand in dimness and feel something their language has no tap for: relief. Rest. A small, dry, papery awe.

Then the power lets the panel brighten again. It does not force the shadow to stay. It has shown that darkness is possible. It has shown that rest is allowed.

Next, on the rim — where it first lay down — it opens a door it did not make. It does not cut the world-plate; it finds a place where an ancient expansion joint has already failed and gently, gently peels back a leaf-sail the size of a lake, and behind it, *beyond* it, there is not more basin.

There is outside. Wind that smells of wet stone, though it brings no water here. A slope upward. And high above the slope, where the dome ends, a sliver of true sky — deep, dark blue, with one white star in it even at noon.

It leaves that peel open. It does not call them to come. It does not promise anything out there is better. The dry people need no water, and maybe they need no hills. But now there is a choice that did not exist. A husk that catches the right drift could rise on the basin's slow circulation, catch that rim-wind, and go *over*.

Some will. Most won't. The continuation of the tending is holy enough.

---

The last act is to be remembered correctly.

The power will not stay forever in this form. Powers, even benevolent ones, have other low places to kneel beside. Before it thins back to wherever wide things go, it does one final, very small thing.

It goes to the rooted one — the old one who fruited blue — and it kneels, again, making itself low, so its face, if it has a face, is level with that tilted, veined face.

And it taps, not on the floor, but on the trunk itself, so only that one feels it:

*You are beautiful. What you are making is beautiful. I will remember the taste of your air.*

The rooted one's leaves tremble though there is no wind. A new seam appears along its side, earlier than season, and a husk splits away — lighter than any before, pale, hollow, veined with that same impossible blue.

It does not drift downwind. It drifts *up*, just a little, hanging in the light gravity for a long, long time, like dust that has decided not to fall.

The thin people who see it will press a curl of fresh leaf between their lips and feel, for the first time, not just light and mineral, but *sweetness*. Not sugar. Recognition.

The power leaves no monument. Monuments would be floors, and there are already enough floors.

It leaves the shadow that can return if they tap for it. It leaves the peel at the rim. It leaves the tension eased in a thousand small knots. And it leaves, in the felted litter, in the circuitry-veins of the leaves, in the paper skin of the people, a faint new pattern that was not there before: when you hold a leaf to the light, alongside the tiny printed floors and tiny leaves, you now see — if you squint — tiny figures, tall and spare and dry, holding hands with something vast and cool that has no shape, both of them drinking light together.

They will think they grew that pattern themselves.

They did.
