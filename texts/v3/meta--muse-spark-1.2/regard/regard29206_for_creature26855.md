They would not come like you expect a Power to come.

No light opening in that low, bruised sky. No voice. No footstep on the basalt. If you were still crouched there with your face a foot above the mat, your boots already being roofed over by tiles, your sternum humming with their sawtooth hiss - you would not see anything arrive at all.

You would only notice, after a while, that the wind had stopped even more completely than before. That the mist that lays itself down for an hour and then lifts had paused mid-lift. That the water beading on every tile-back was beading a little rounder, holding on a little longer before it ran.

That something was paying attention.

And that is the first thing a benevolent and wise Power would do: **attend, without hunger.**

Every other kind of power that has ever found a place like this has come hungry. Hungry to map it, to name it, to mine it, to fix it, to farm it, to explain it. Even the kind ones come hungry to *help.*

This one does not.

It sits - if sitting is the word - on the far rim where the dark ground dissolves into more dark. Not above. Not over. At the edge, at the same level as the mat. And it looks, for a very long time.

That is not nothing. In a universe that mostly does not look at such a place except as a resource or an error, to be looked at the way you would look at a sleeping child's face is already an intervention.

What does it see?

Not a wasteland. Not a failed meadow. Not "an ecosystem without plants and therefore broken."

It sees a completeness. An old, weathered equilibrium that solved the problem of living without ever inventing green. Stone that learned to sweat minerals. A film that learned to eat stone. Mouths that learned to eat film. Mouths that learned to eat mouths, but so slowly, so without malice or panic, that predation here is not a hunt but a tipping. A mat that has become a kind of low, flat ocean that breathes in place. A trillion bodies tolerating touch because intolerance would be endless war.

It hears what you heard as deafening, and it does not hear noise.

It hears a chorale. The *tik-tik-tik* of a billion fringes not as static but as counterpoint - each tile slightly out of phase, each roller clicking on the off-beat, each anemone's *plop* like a soft drum, the ash-gnats above the streams holding a high, glassy drone. It has the ears to hear it as music, because it is not trying to hear itself over it.

And it understands the being who lives here is not one of the tiles, or the threads, or the rollers. The being is the mat itself. The bowl itself. A distributed, mindless god made of touch and chemistry, that knows itself only as *wet here / dry there / heavy here / light there.* It has no central face to lift to the sky. It has never been alone enough to be lonely. It has never been quiet enough to hear that it is loud.

A lesser benevolence would be overcome with pity and with ideas.

It would think: *It is dark here, I will give them sun.*
It would think: *It is loud here, I will give them silence.*
It would think: *They eat each other constantly, I will give them painlessness or plants so they don't have to.*
It would think: *They are blind, I will give them eyes.*

And every one of those gifts would be an ending. Sun would dry the film in an hour. Silence would starve the gnats that navigate by hum. Plants would root and crack the pillows and bring soil and bring everything else, and this exact, patient mineral music that took a million years to settle would be gone. Eyes would be useless under that sky and only teach them what they lack.

The wise Power is wise because it knows the difference between a wound and a way.

So it refuses the three great temptations of the benevolent: to clean, to quiet, and to crown.

It does not clean the stone. It does not scrape the mat away to show the pretty basalt underneath. It does not drain the ankle-deep braidless streams.

It does not quiet the chorus. It does not lift the overcast.

It does not take one tile-creature and make it *aware*, make it a king or a prophet to rule the others. To do that would be the cruelest thing - to give one of them a self large enough to feel how crowded it is.

What does it do, then? If it does anything at all?

It does small things. The kind of things only an attentive, almost all-powerful thing *can* do without breaking anything.

**1. It vouches for the water.**

It sees, in a way you and I cannot, the deep slow hydrology that makes this bowl weep. The aquifer below, the iron dissolving, the cold trap of the sag. It sees that in ten thousand years the climate will twitch and the condensation will falter for a season. So it does not bring more water now - more would flood the potholes and drown the anemone crowns. It just *ensures*. It places a hand, not physically, on the balance. A promise in the stone that the sweat will not stop early. That the film will not thin while no one is looking. It is not a miracle you would see. The beads will still bead. But they will not fail.

That is benevolence at bowl-scale: not abundance, but continuance.

**2. It gives them a night.**

Not a day. They don't need day. But once - and then again, at an interval so long only the stone will notice - it thins the overcast. Not tears it. Thins it, like stretching wool.

For maybe an hour, the bruise-purple light parts. No sun - the clouds are still there - but stars show through. True stars. Cold, clear, ancient light that has never touched this ground before.

No creature here has an eye to see them, and that is the point. The gift is not *for* seeing. It is for being *under.* For one hour the faint constellations on the backs of the tiles - those cold, functionless photophores - are not the dimmest lights in the bowl. For one hour the whole mat, without knowing why, glitters back at the sky that glitters down at it. The gnats freeze in their hum. The tik-tik-tik falters, just slightly, as if the chorus took a breath. And then the clouds close, and the dark is complete again.

The mat will not remember. The stone will. And that is enough. A place that has never had a sky has been seen by the sky.

**3. It answers the visitor.**

Because there are two beings in the bowl now. There is the mat - and there is you. The one who crouched down because the darkness forced you close. The one whose boots are being tasted, whose sternum is humming, whose voice dies half a meter from your mouth, who is beginning to feel the terrible, vertiginous loneliness of being the only thing in that bowl that knows it is lonely.

The Power does not lift you out. Does not dry your boots or make the sound bearable by turning it down. That would be to lie to you about where you are.

Instead it sits with you. And for a moment it tunes your hearing, not by quieting the mat but by giving you the ear it has.

You blink - and the wall of noise resolves. Not into silence, but into *parts.* You can follow one tile's tik-tik-tik across three others. You can hear the little luminous mucus trail of the roller as a separate click, receding. You can hear the space *between* the anemone pops. You can hear, under it all, the water itself moving between the stone pillows, which you had not heard at all before because the animals were louder.

And in that moment the deafening stops being an assault and becomes a place. You understand, in your body and not just your mind, that nothing here is screaming. Everything is just... scraping. Filtering. Living. At its own pace.

It lets you feel, for maybe thirty seconds, what it feels: not pity for this place, but a great, quiet fondness. A love that has no plan to improve what it loves.

Then it lets your hearing go back to normal, because you could not walk around forever hearing that clearly and still be human.

When you stand again - if you stand - your boots are heavier with mites, but you are lighter.

And the Power?

It does not stay. Wise things do not linger where their lingering would become worship. It does not mark the rim. It does not leave a monument of black glass.

If you looked later for proof that anything benevolent had been there, you would find only this:

A single patch of stone, no larger than your palm, that the mat has, for reasons no biologist will ever explain, left bare. Not grazed bare - *left* bare. As if the tiles around it decided, for once, not to overlap. A little round eye of wet basalt, clean, beading with condensation, reflecting the bruise-purple dusk like a mirror.

And if you put your hand there - and you will want to - the stone is warm. Not 8 or 9 degrees. Warm, like skin. For exactly as long as your palm rests there, and not a second longer.

Then cold again. Then, very slowly, a tile at the edge extends its fringe, tests the bare spot, and begins, patiently, to squeegee its way back over it.

As it should.

The bowl continues.
