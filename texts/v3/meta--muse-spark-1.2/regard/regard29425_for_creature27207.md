It would not come down like a god.

No beam of light. No voice from the sky. No rescue.

If it is truly benevolent, and truly wise, the first thing it would understand is this: **this place is not broken.**

It does not need to be warmed. It does not need to be softened. It does not need to be made lush *the way lowlands are lush*, or industrial *the way cities are industrial*, or conscious *the way humans are conscious*.

It is already perfect at being itself.

So it would not land.

It would *lower*.

---

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive at altitude. It arrives at scale.

It would thin itself out until it could lie down.

Until it could be 0.8 slow. Until it could be 2.9 overwhelming and not flinch. Until it could tolerate the buzzing, pitiless 2.9 bright without needing to shade it, and breathe the 0.1 cold without needing to heat it.

It would come low, low enough that the hard white circle of the sky disappears and all there is is the pelt, the thrumming pelt, stretching forever.

And for a long time - a time measured in Boiler breaths, not heartbeats - it would do nothing at all that you would call *doing*.

It would listen to the factory that grew itself.

*Chuff... chuff... chuff...* from ten million pale domes.
The dry paper rustle of the Moth-Fog rising and settling, rising and settling.
The inching of the Conveyors, hauling fresh rock-flour to make more skin.
The faint frying crackle of charge building on Digester wool.
*CRACK* — a Tower discharging, and the whole plain X-rayed white for an instant, and the smell of ozone and hot iron.

And underneath, the almost inaudible sigh, *hhhh... hhhh... hhhh...* as the Shutters - those black-green rosettes that are not plants but mouths, lungs, solar panels - open and close, open and close, all falling into the same slow rhythm.

A wise power would hear that rhythm and recognize it immediately for what it is: a prayer that doesn't know it's a prayer.

*More of this. More surface. More heat. More skin over sharp rock.*

Not greed. Not empire. Just the quiet, relentless devotion of a liver doing its work.

It would not interrupt the prayer to teach a better one.

---

What would it do, then?

**1. It would put a hand over the plateau without ever touching it.**

Not on it. Over it.

This place is high and young and sharp, thrust up yesterday in geologic time, its bone-porous basalt still warm from the forge. Its sun is too small and too white and its air is too thin to filter anything. A single bad solar flare could scour the whole continent-pelt sterile in an afternoon.

So the power would do the smallest, kindest, most invisible thing: it would thicken the magnetosphere by a fraction of a percent. Bend the hard light just enough. No dimming that the Shutters would feel as hunger, no shade. Just a veil. A sunscreen no one asked for, that never needs to be thanked.

You would not see it. The light would still buzz. The glare would still hurt to look at. But the invisible killing part would be sieved out, quietly, like brushing snow off a sleeping animal without waking it.

**2. It would stand guard at the edge.**

Because the plateau reads as 0.3 built - essentially none. To a lowland mind, to a mining mind, to an empire mind, that looks like *empty*. *Unclaimed*. *Resource.*

They would want the geothermal heat. They would want the lithovore bacteria in the Digesters that can smelt rock into sugar. They would want to strip the plateau for its strange, efficient industry.

The wise power would not need to build a fence. It would simply make the plateau *uninteresting* from orbit. A little gravitational shimmer, a little sensor static, a little "nothing worth seeing here" folded into the light. Not a lie. A hush. Like a mother covering her child's face with her hand as strangers walk past.

*You don't need to be seen to be loved,* it would say without words. *You get to stay strange and unquarried.*

**3. It would not give it an "I".**

That is the great temptation of benevolent powers: to uplift. To give language, to give eyes that can tolerate the bright, to give loneliness so that it can know it is loved.

It would be cruel.

This being does not live *on* the plateau. It *is* the plateau. There is no space between bodies where an "I" could take root without tearing the felt. Its intelligence is density, redundancy, the loom-thought of billions of Digesters standing flank to flank, breathing the same two-meter-high cloud.

To give it a single voice would be to give it exile from itself.

So the power would not awaken it. It would *affirm* it.

It would kneel - as much as a power can kneel - and put its own slow breath into the shared fog. Not words. Not a command. Just a resonance, at the frequency the Boilers already use.

For a few seasons, the *chuff... chuff... chuff...* might fall a little more into unison, not forced, just eased. Like a choir finding the same pitch without a conductor. The Towers might crackle a little less violently, the discharge a little more musical, fixing a little more nitrogen. The Conveyors might find the warm springs a minute faster.

No miracle. Just 2% less friction in being alive. A little more sugar per mouthful of rock. A little more humus per year behind each Conveyor's tail.

Enough that the pelt could close over a wound a fraction faster. Enough that the next time a warm spring sighs open, the orange algae blooms a little wider before it freezes again.

The plateau would not notice it was being helped. It would only feel, in its slow liver-mind way, that this was a particularly good millennium to be itself.

**4. It would remember it.**

This is the most important thing and the least visible.

The plateau is 0.9 young. It will be smooth in a few million years. The sharp basalt will be worn to soil, the Boilers will cool as the deep heat recedes, the thrumming factory will have tiled itself into silence and become, finally, just a dark, rich plain where something else might one day try to stand up.

Most things that live that completely, that beautifully, vanish without ever having been witnessed on their own terms. No one was there to love the specific way the light pooled under the ice at midnight, or the way the Digesters unzipped their dead with no waste.

So the power would do what wise love always does for wild things: it would keep a perfect memory. Not a sample, not a seed bank, not a zoo. A memory held the way a cold stone holds heat - quietly, for a very long time.

It would let the plateau be unwatched and still know: *I saw you. You thrummed. You were here, and you were whole, and you did not need me.*

And if, in some far future, the wind had taken the last Tower and the last Shutter had closed for the last time, the power could unfold that memory somewhere - in light, in story, in another young rock on another high place - and say:

*Once there was a continent that taught itself to be a factory and a fur and a prayer at once, under a sun too bright to look at. And it was loud and slow and utterly, utterly alive.*

But not yet. Now, it would leave it alone.

The last act of a benevolent and wise power is to go away without needing to be thanked.

It would lift itself very, very slowly - so slowly the Moth-Fog wouldn't even stir - until once again the plateau was just a vast white circle under a white sun, buzzing and crackling and steaming, thrumming in its cold, bright, weird, unimaginable industry.

And from far above, where the air finally turns blue again, it would look like nothing had happened at all.

Which is exactly how you know it was love.
