It would not come down like light.

Any lesser power would have come down like light. A shaft, a breaking, a rescue. It would have torn the dark because it thought dark was wound, and poured sun down through the weight of that ocean until the water boiled with particles and the keepers went blind and the kelp that had learned to live on almost nothing burned with too much. Any lesser power would have brought fish, because it thought empty was lonely, and the fish would have eaten the forest in a season. Any lesser power would have said *be fast, be warm, be seen.*

A benevolent and wise power would do none of that.

It would notice first - because nothing here is loud enough to be noticed unless you are listening for quiet things.

To notice this place you have to be the kind of attention that does not look for signals, but for maintenance. For the fact that after ten thousand years of cold and pressure and the slow prying of roots, those basalt blocks are still level. That the joints are still clear. That the forest has not choked on itself. Someone is still sweeping a floor the size of a planet, one handful of silt at a time. No distress call. No light. Just the faintest statistical impossibility: order persisting longer than it should.

So it would look. And to look here it would have to learn how to look.

It cannot see as you see. If it arrived with eyes, it would see nothing. If it arrived with heat, it would kill what it came to love. So it would do the first merciful thing: it would translate itself.

It would cool itself down. Slow itself down.

Think of a mind that can think a thousand thoughts in the second it takes a frond to sway a centimeter. It would have to deliberately think one thought per century, so that its thinking would sync with the keeper's pulse. It would have to make its awareness heavy, so pressure would not crush it, but hold it. It would have to become dense and patient and almost stone, because only stone can stay here long enough to be trusted.

It would not appear. It would settle. The way silt settles.

You would not see it arrive, even if you waited in that corner where the two wall-spines meet. The water would just become, by half a degree, more even. The hush would become, by a fraction, more complete. If you had a vibration pit instead of an ear, you would feel it as a new, very low tone under the groan of blocks - not a sound added, but a silence made deeper.

And then it would understand.

That is the second thing a wise power does that a merely kind power does not: it understands before it acts, and sometimes understanding is the act.

It would understand that these are not prisoners.

This is not a tragedy to be solved. The builders did not drown. They *remained.* When the surface became rumor and the light became memory, they did not flee upward. They stayed with the work because the work was the only part of themselves they trusted to survive the forgetting. They let their eyes go because light was going. They let their speed go because hurry was going. They thinned and lengthened and gave their skin to the forest, and they made a covenant: as long as one joint is cleared, we are still here. Not as people. As keeping.

To take that from them — to give them back eyes, or speech, or hunger, or quickness — would be to erase them in the name of saving them. To "free" them to the surface would be to execute them gently.

So it would not free them.

So what would it do?

Three small things. Because wise benevolence never does one big thing where three small things will do.

**First, it would witness.**

For ten thousand years no one has seen the work. The forest does not see. The stone does not see. The keepers do not see each other except as moving anomalies to be passed on the far side of a causeway. Each keeper kneels for weeks to lift a corner of a block by two centimeters and no one will ever know it was done, except the stone, which does not know.

The power would keep a ledger that no one else needs.

Not a ledger on paper. It would become memory itself. It would commit to remembering every cleared joint, every peeled mat, every nudged block, as it happens. When the keeper in the northern reservoir Spends eleven days peeling felt from a drain channel that no water has run through since the continents were warm, the power would hold that act the way a shrine holds a candle. Not to make it meaningful, but to acknowledge that it already was.

If you have vibration pits, you would feel this as a faint, warm attention resting on you, not like being watched, but like being kept warm by being thought of accurately. The keepers would not have a word for being witnessed. They would simply, for the first time in millennia, not be entirely alone in their acre. The water around them would feel less empty, the way a room feels less empty when someone else is quietly working in it.

**Second, it would kneel.**

Benevolence that only watches is still distant. Wisdom knows that to love a keeper you must keep.

So somewhere, in a courtyard so vast it has its own slow weather, where a block the size of a house has sagged and a new keeper - pale, still unfolded, still half-plant - is struggling to find the purchase to nudge it, the power would put down a hand.

Not a human hand. Not a beam of force. It would form for itself, for a little while, a pair of those same broad, flat, fused spade-hands. Out of nothing but cooled intention and silt. It would make them the same amber-gray, the same callused, the same imperfect. And it would kneel, and its kelp-mantle - which it would have grown for itself, because it would not be presumptuous enough to appear naked where everyone else wears forest - would billow and then settle.

And it would push.

With exactly the same slowness. One minute per scoop. One season per correction. It would not lift the block for the young keeper. That would be theft. It would press on the opposite corner, so the weight was shared. The block would move a fraction faster, but not fast enough to startle.

The young keeper would feel the pressure change in its pits. An anomaly. Another anomaly that does not pass on the far side, but stays. It would not understand companionship. It would simply not be corrected. It would keep its hands where they are a little longer, because the stone is moving more truly than it expected.

And when the block is true again, the power would peel back a corner of mat with it, and let the drift take the silt, and then it would go still for a century or two alongside the keeper, two low shapes in the felled forest, both covered in growth, both tending. If you swam by, you would think it was just another keeper that had stopped.

That is how much it would love them: enough to be boring with them.

**Third, it would give them a music they can already hear.**

It would not give them light. It would give them a slightly more beautiful dark.

The keepers navigate by the weight of water on their pits, by the groan of stone. The world to them is an endless, very quiet chord of pressure and settling. A wise power would not break that chord. It would resolve it.

It would hum. Very low, below hearing, at the resonant frequency of basalt. A tone so slow that one cycle takes a year. The keepers would not hear it as a song. They would feel it in their holdfasts and in the seams of their skin-stone as a faint lessening of effort. As if the floor itself were exhaling with them. The whip-stalks would sway a little more evenly. The detritus snow would fall a little more straight down.

In places where a keeper has stopped - knelt and grassed over and become a soft rise that will in centuries become a new cutting - the tone would gather just a little. The moss-felt over those still shapes would glow, not with light, but with a faint chemical phosphor, a thousandth brighter than the surrounding rot-glow. Not enough to see by. Enough to make one still keeper distinguishable from any other rise, if you were another keeper passing and happened to pause.

A marker. A small, cold flower. A way of saying: *this one kept. This one is not just stone. Remember this shape.*

The keepers would not understand memorial. But their pits would register those spots as slightly warmer, slightly more dense in the water, and they would, without knowing why, tend the joints around those mounds with a little more care. The forest would grow a little less thickly over them. The pavement would stay a little more level there, for longer.

And that would be all.

It would not stay forever. Or rather, it would - because a power that wise is never not everywhere - but it would withdraw the hands, the hum would fade back into the ordinary hush, the extra weight of witness would thin until it was just the ordinary weight of water again.

It would leave nothing behind that could be found. No monument, no new sea, no risen surface. If you came back a thousand years later you would say nothing had happened.

Except - the block that was nudged with two pairs of hands would still be true when its neighbors had sagged a millimeter. Except one or two of those glowing mounds would still be a little warmer, and the new cuttings uncurling from them would push up a little straighter. Except the keeper who had knelt beside another would, for the rest of its long, slow keeping, pause a half-second longer when it passed another keeper on a causeway crossing, as if expecting weight to be shared.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a world that has perfected the art of waiting and keeping in the dark.

It does not bring the sun.

It learns to sit in the dark with you, and helps you hold the stone level, and remembers your name even though you have forgotten you ever had one.

And then, very gently, so as not to make a current, it leaves you to your work, which is holy exactly as it is.
