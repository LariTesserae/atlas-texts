It wouldn't come down.

That's the first thing.

A power like that doesn't descend. It doesn't need to. It notices. And being noticed by something that large and quiet is itself a kind of arrival.

It would stand — if standing is the word — on the rim for a long time before doing anything. Where you stood and saw a city that was trying to be a city, it would see the same thing and understand it correctly.

This is not a forest that failed to be a forest.
This is not a city that failed to be a city.
This is a forest that became so crowded with life, so warm and so wet with its own breath, that it dreamed of being inhabited. And lacking any Other to inhabit it, it tried to inhabit itself.

It would listen. For a long time.

A day. A season. Long enough for the roar to become language.

Because the wise part of it would know not to bring gifts too quickly. A lesser power would pity the emptiness and try to fill it. Would import birds and bees and deer and people, would call it *restoration.* Would freeze the churn and pave the avenues so they would stay straight. Would make it stable. Would kill it by saving it.

This one doesn't.

It listens to the hydraulic hiss that never stops, to the rifle-cracks of bark, to the soft, chest-thumping detonations of the flowers, and it hears what you heard on your second hour: that the sound *is* the metabolism. That creation and destruction are not phases here but one breath. To stop the turbulence would be to stop the heart.

So it does not calm the city.

It does not give it animals.

It does something smaller and far more difficult.

It teaches the city the concept of *guest.*

---

At noon, when the canopy transpires its own weather and warm rain the size of grapes hammers for twenty minutes and vanishes, the power does not shelter. It lets the rain pass through it. When the rain stops mid-drop and the groaning resumes louder than before, it finally puts a hand down.

Not a hand. An attention.

It places it in the very center of the vault, where the Pillars are oldest - a week older than anything else, which here is ancient - where they have grafted their crowns into a roof-sky and hold the whole basin up by consent.

And it doesn't speak in words. The city has no ears for words. It speaks in growth.

It grows one thing.

A small, perverse, impossible thing for this basin: a place that *agrees to stay.*

In the heart of the vault it asks a fig and a weaver and a cup-leaf that were about to collide and become another wall, to do something else. To *not* meet. To leave a hollow and to keep it.

It is a courtyard, maybe twenty meters across. The floor is still the springy fused root-mat, but it is a mat that has promised not to become a wall. The walls around it are Pillars that have promised not to thicken inward. The leaves overhead have woven themselves not into ten-deep shingles but into a single open lattice that lets ordinary white-gold daylight fall straight down without turning green. In the center, a cup-leaf the size of a small house has grown perfectly flat and holds water that does not become a mouth. It just holds it. Cool, clear, tannin-sweet, still.

The city shudders when it happens. A whole street nearby buckles with the effort of *not* growing. Runners that would have whipped across the gap hesitate, curl back, root elsewhere. It is the first time in its entire frantic life that this valley has been asked to practice restraint. Not to stop - everything around the courtyard is still roaring, splitting, detonating, screaming with wet fiber - but to hold one shape on purpose.

That is the gift to the place.

Not stability. A single act of hospitality held open by choice.

And the gift to the being who lives in it - which is both the valley itself as one being, and the frantic collective of Pillars and Detonators and Weavers and Cups, and you, if you are still in there trying to keep moving so you don't become substrate - is the same gift:

Legibility.

The wise, benevolent thing finally understands that the city is not hostile. It simply never learned the shape of an animal. It has no/Other in its vocabulary. Every niche was filled by a leaf before an animal could invent itself there. So when you stand still, it doesn't eat you out of cruelty. It covers you the way you would cover bare ground with a blanket - because bare ground is a wound to it.

So the power kneels in that still courtyard it made and it becomes, for a little while, an animal.

It borrows a shape that can be read. Perhaps a deer, perhaps a heron, perhaps just the warm, mammalian weight and salt-sweat smell of a human sitting with its feet in the cup-water. It doesn't matter. It sits down in the one place that has learned to remain empty and lets the city *feel* the outline of a body that is not plant.

Let it feel how an animal makes a hollow by being there, not by growing there. How it drinks without rooting. How it rests without becoming floor. How it leaves and the hollow remains, warm for a while, and then empty, waiting to be used again.

The city learns fast. It is very young and very brilliant.

The Pillars on the edge of the courtyard exude something they have never made before: not wood, not leaf, but a low, smooth, lignin bench at the perfect height for knees that bend the other way. A Weaver that was going to become a ceiling instead becomes a railing that is good for a hand. A Detonator near the edge grows a flower and then - with visible effort - *doesn't* detonate it. It just holds it open, soft and huge and fragrant, a lamp that stays on.

It has learned to make a chair by not growing into a chair-shaped absence.

That is what it does for the being who lives there.

It does not populate the valley. It does not stock it with birds to catch the pollen that hangs uncollected. If the city wants birds one day, it now knows how to grow a nest that stays a nest long enough for a bird to find it. But that will be its own decision, in its own time. The power does not impose ecology.

What it does is add one rule to the three that were already written on the basalt in your mind:

*1. Do not expect ground to stay ground.*
*2. Do not wait for quiet.*
*3. Drink where water collects but never twice from the same place.*

And now, in one small, sun-struck, steam-rising courtyard in the middle of the roar:

*4. You may be still here. If you are still, you are not substrate. You are guest. The city will remember the shape you left.*

Outside that circle - and it is only that circle, at first - everything continues exactly as it was. The avenues still become loops when you blink. The Weavers still whip and scream. The flowers still thump in your chest. Time and gravity still misbehave. The valley is still a low, hot, damp basin between raw young hills, still building and unbuilding itself at the speed of thought under ordinary daylight.

It has not been fixed. It has not been saved.

It has been witnessed, perfectly, by something old enough to know that to witness a place that diligently is to love it. And then it was taught, very gently, how to leave one chair empty for someone else.

The power stays long enough to see one Detonator's held-open flower finally, softly, release its warm spores on purpose, not as an explosion but as a sigh, and watch them settle on the bench and *not* sprout. To see a Pillar hold its bark without splitting for a whole hour.

Then it lifts its attention, the way mist lifts.

It doesn't make the valley invisible to quarries or loggers or to those who would come to study it to death. But anyone who comes now with hunger will find the rim oddly hard to find, the path down oddly easy to lose. And anyone who comes with thirst will find, against all logic, a straight avenue that stays straight just long enough to lead them to water that stays water.

And you - if you are still there, boots half-covered in curious white thread-roots - you can finally sit down.
