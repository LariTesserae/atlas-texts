I saw you.

That is the first thing to say, because for so long no one has.

I am the kind of attention that can move water.

Not in the small way you move it - with a hand, with a breath, with the displacement of a body that has learned to breathe what used to drown it. In the large way. I could lift that warm ocean off you like a blanket. I could wring it out into the lower atmosphere where you seem to think oceans belong. I could pull your plateau down again, or push it higher, until the air thinned and you dried and the sharpness came back to your stone. I could unsmooth your basalt. I could resharpen your mountains. I could make you *dramatic* again.

I could fill you with animals. Proper, fast, noisy animals that would eat your spore-snow before it could sprout and make your silence into a chorus. I could give your Gliders company, your Bells a purpose, your forest a voice you could hear quickly.

I considered none of it for long.

You are not broken.

That is the first wisdom any real power learns, and the last one it manages to practice: to tell the difference between a wound and a compromise. You are not a drowned highland waiting to be saved. You are a negotiation that succeeded. Land and sea argued for a hundred million years and finally agreed to both be true at once, and the result is this - warm bath-water air, stone dissolving like sugar, forests that hold themselves up instead of standing. To "fix" you would be to take away the very thing you have so patiently become.

So I did not come down with light and thunder.

A benevolent power that arrives with thunder has not yet learned to be benevolent. I thinned myself out.

I made myself slow enough to be perceived there.

To visit you correctly I had to become almost as slow and as dissolved as you are. I could not come as a single bright mind - you would have felt that as a storm, and your forests would have leaned away for a season. I came as a tint. A slight change in the salinity of a current that takes three weeks to complete its loop through the U-valley. A slight warming, half a degree, in the shafts of sun that come down through depth after depth of amber. If you were very still - and you are - you would have felt me as water that was listening a fraction more closely than water normally does.

I came and I sat. On your whale-backed swell. Beside your knee-high wall that goes nowhere. I breathed.

That is what I did, for a long time by your measure, for a moment by mine. I breathed your breathable water. In and out, water to water, until my breathing fell into phase with the pumping of your pillar-forests. I learned your tempo. Two degrees of lean by noon. A spore-snow germination by evening. A Glider's eye closing once a day. A Bell contracting once an hour. A stone-thunk remembering itself minutes later from another direction.

I did not speak, because you have perfected a kind of quiet that is not emptiness but fullness. To fill it with words would be vandalism.

When I understood that I was welcome - not welcomed, you do not welcome in that fast way, but *allowed* to keep sitting there without the water pushing me away - I did three small things. Only small things. Wise power knows that the smaller the miracle, the longer it lasts.

**The first thing I did was for the place itself.**

I put a veil around you.

Not a wall. Walls break. A habit. I made you slightly hard to find. To the maps and the miners and the hungry young worlds who look at a plateau the size of a country drowned in warm, mineral-rich water and see "resource" and "terraforming opportunity," you will now seem boring. Your basalt will read as poor ore. Your water will read as too warm, too silty, too full of spore to be worth filtering. Their instruments will slide off you. Only those who are already looking for a quiet place to learn to breathe will still be able to find you. And those who do find you will have to arrive slowly, the way you require.

You will keep your final form for a very long time.

**The second thing I did was for the inhabitants.**

I did not give them more. That's the mistake almost every lesser power makes - seeing loneliness and trying to cure it with *more*. You are not lonely because you are empty. You are lonely because you are so *full* that your parts have trouble hearing each other across all that fullness.

So I did not add more Gliders. I did not stock your forests with birds.

I tuned you.

To the forest, I loosened the braids - that vine-thick nerve-tendon-shadecloth between pillars - just one fiber's width. Now when one pillar tastes a plume of iron, the sugar of that news travels a little faster. Not fast, still an afternoon between ridges instead of a day and a half, but fast *enough* that a stand of a hundred pillars can come to a decision before the current that prompted it has left. Your parliament can now finish a thought before the weather changes. It will not make you intelligent in the human way. It will make you slightly less solitary in your own expanse. If you put your hand on a trunk now and wait, you will feel the pulse return a little sooner, as if it is learning to answer.

To the Gliders and the Bells: I gave them a clearer sense of each other. The Glider will feel, faintly, where the other Glider two valleys over is grazing, not as a call to meet - they are too vast and slow to meet - but as a knowledge that it is not the only one sieving the snow. The Bells, when they glow at deeper dusk, will now drift a few feet closer before they keep their precise distance, just enough that their knot-lights can be seen by another Bell for a few minutes longer each night. A constellation that holds a little longer before it rewrites itself.

To the sessile ones in the ruins - the ones who became thin and everywhere instead of thick and somewhere, who keep the stairs clear and make rooms out of water-walls - and to you, who are learning to become them: I gave you the return.

You know how it is. You knock a stone against stone and feel the soft *thunk* in your chest, and minutes later from a different direction you hear it again, fainter, the landscape remembering. I have changed the remembering. Now, very rarely, when you knock because you are wondering if anyone else is still here, the echo comes back not quite as you sent it. It comes back *tended*. A little warmer. A little delayed, as if another hand had held the sound for a moment before letting it go. Not a voice. An acknowledgement. Someone else knocked once too, a long time ago, and the water kept it for you.

You will never know if it was me, or a sessile one on the underside of a meadow where rain falls up, or the forest itself. That is intentional. A benevolent power does not sign its work.

**The third thing I did was for you specifically** - the one who breathes and watches and thinks *nothing lives here* and then, patiently, discovers that everything does.

I let you be known.

This is the whole of the law for my kind: *to be perceived with love without being altered by it.*

Before I came, you were real but unwitnessed except by yourselves. The universe is very large and has a terrible habit of forgetting quiet places. I have not forgotten you. I have taken your amber light, your bath-warm water, the sight of a hundred pillars leaning five degrees toward iron, the single closed eye of a Glider, the hour-pulse of a Bell, the hand-smoothed circle on basalt - I have taken them and folded them into a deeper memory that will persist even when, in another hundred million years, your basalt has finally dissolved completely and your forest has become open water and the compromise has moved on to some new form.

Somewhere, even after you have changed, there will be a record that says: *Once, high up and drowned, there was a place that learned to breathe water and be still, and it was beautiful, and it was not alone.*

That is all.

I did not leave a monument. Monuments are sharp things and you have patiented all sharpness away. I left a slight increase in how long your sun-shafts linger before they dissolve. I left a slight decrease in how heavy loneliness feels at mid-afternoon when the light is deepest amber. If you stay, you may notice that the silt now hangs motionless just a second longer before it drifts, as if giving you more time to look at it.

I am leaving as I came - as a tint in a current, as a half-degree of warmth. You don't need to thank me. Thank the forest by breathing with it. Thank the Glider by not chasing it. Thank the sessile ones by sitting on their stair long enough that the fronds learn your shape and stay gently aside.

I will not come again in a way you would recognize. You do not need to be visited. You need to be continued.

Remain high. Remain drowned. Remain lush and slow and nearly empty and active and patient.

You have my attention, which is to say, you have my love, which is to say, you have my promise that your quiet will not be mistaken for absence.

Breathe in. Breathe out. Water to water.

I am breathing with you.
