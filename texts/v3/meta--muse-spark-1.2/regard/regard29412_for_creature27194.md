It would not come.

Not the way you think a power comes — not down, with light and noise. There is already too much light and noise here. The roar of the bubble-forges would swallow any trumpet. The columns of sun through a thousand feet of cold water would wash out any halo.

Benevolence that is also wise does not announce itself to a place that has perfected silence without echo. It pays attention. And attention, at this scale, is indistinguishable from arrival.

So first — nothing happens.

For a long time, nothing happens, and that *is* what it does.

It notices.

In a universe full of screaming, this place does not scream. It thrums. It keeps time so perfectly it has become invisible to everything that looks for change, for distress, for growth, for collapse. Most powers would fly right past it, looking for a world to save.

This one stops. Because it hears a machine that was never built, a factory with no workers, a wilderness with no waste, holding an ocean on the roof of a continent by nothing but agreement and pressure and patience — and it recognizes it. Not as a curiosity. As a completed prayer.

It does not descend. It diffuses.

It becomes, for a while, the thin air above the interface, the cold mist that never breaks. It becomes the light shafts. It becomes weight in the water column. It listens the way you would listen to a sleeping elder's breathing — not to diagnose, but to keep company, to make sure no one is alone while they breathe.

It stays long enough to understand.

It understands this is not a broken world. There is nothing to fix. The trunks are not suffering under their labor; they *are* their labor. The fish in their endless tori are not trapped; they have found the one perfect orbit and have no need for another. The Artisans are not enslaved; they are maintenance made holy by repetition. The Bellows are not burdened. The whole sparse, loud, cold cathedral is not waiting to become something else. It has already become.

Wisdom is knowing the difference between a prison and a monastery when they look identical from the outside.

Benevolence without wisdom would do so much here. Oh, it would do *so much*.

It would warm the water, because the cold aches. It would give the fish eyes, because blindness seems sad. It would teach the trunks to grow taller, to dream of the sea below, to evolve. It would break the perfect loop to make it "dynamic" or "free." It would introduce death and novelty because it thinks death and novelty are necessary for meaning.

It would kill it in a single act of love.

This power does none of that.

What it does instead is this — I think it does three very small, very enormous things, and then leaves.

**1. It puts its hand over it without touching it.**

It sees, beyond the plateau's edge where the sky-ocean thins into mist and stone, the rumor of the world below — blue shadow and cloud and, eventually, hunger. The world below will one day look up and learn how to climb. It will bring drills and pumps and names and needs. It will call this place a resource. A reservoir. A miracle to be harvested.

The power does not lecture the world below. It does not punish it. It just makes the plateau a little harder to find. A little steeper. It tucks a fold into the wind, a forgetting into the maps. Not a wall — walls invite siege. A gentle disinterest. A sense, in any mind that thinks to look up there, that *there is nothing up there worth the climb.* It will hold for as long as it needs to hold.

Not to isolate the forest. To let the forest keep its vow of equilibrium without having to defend it. The kindest thing you can do for something perfect is to keep the imperfect from touching it for no reason.

**2. It floats.**

For a little while — and for such a being, a little while might be a thousand years — it stops being the air and the light and becomes something that can be in the water. Not a body, exactly. A concentration. A place where the sideways gravity gathers a little thicker. A coolness that is not cold.

It holds itself neutrally buoyant in the open water between two trunks, a mile from either, where the water is so clear it feels like air and the snow falls so slowly.

It lets the hydraulic groan move through it. It lets the fizz of the plume wash over it. It lets an Artisan pause on its bark-seam, taste its chemistry with antennae, decide *not fiber* and go back to work without alarm. It lets a torus of silver fish part around it without ever seeing it, feeling only a brief, puzzling warmth in the current before the cold reasserts.

It does not warm the water. That would be violence. It just, for a moment, does not take warmth away. It aches with the cold *with* the place, rather than trying to cure the ache. It keeps the water company in being cold.

And in that way, the forest — which has not needed anything for eons — is, for the first time in eons, not alone in its perfect labor. Not observed. Accompanied.

If the forest thinks with water pressure and chemistry and valve-timings, then this is how you speak to it: not in words, but in pressure and chemistry and timing. The power breathes *with* the intake mouths, just a fraction out of phase, so that for one cycle, the groan is a duet. It combs a few bubbles the way the Bellows do. It clicks, very faintly, alongside the Artisans.

It says: *I see you. You are good. Your work is good. Keep going if you want to.*

That is the whole sermon. From a wise, benevolent power, that is enough to remake a universe.

**3. It leaves a door that is not a door.**

This is the hardest part, because wisdom knows that even a gift can be a burden.

Before it diffuses again, it does not give the forest anything the forest would have to use. It gives it an option that can be ignored forever without cost.

In the very center of the sky-ocean, where the currents meet and cancel and the water is most still, it leaves a single thing that was not there before. Not a seed. Not a machine.

A possibility.

A patch of water, no larger than your palm, that is one degree warmer. Not warm. Just *less cold*. And in that patch, buoyancy behaves correctly — down is down, not sideways. Sound echoes, once, faintly, before being absorbed.

A place where change *could* happen, if anything ever wanted change to happen. A place where a fish could, if it chose, break its torus and wobble. Where a ribbon of kelp-steel could drift and not be woven back. Where a lily pad could crack and not be sutured, and just float, broken, for a while.

It will never force itself. The plumes will flow around it. The Artisans will ignore it. The Bellows will not need to visit it. The forest can go on being forever in the exact same way, and this one warm palm of water will not disturb its perfection by a single part per billion.

But if — in another eon, or ten — the consensus that thinks with water pressure ever tires, or wonders, or *wants* — there is somewhere to wonder. Somewhere to be imperfect without being destroyed.

It is not an upgrade. It is permission.

And then — this is the last of what it does, and the most benevolent — it leaves.

It does not stay to be worshipped. It does not stay to watch whether the forest uses the door. Love that stays to check on its gift is not love, it's surveillance.

It withdraws its attention the way you would withdraw your hand from a sleeping child's forehead, slowly, so as not to wake it. It becomes again the rumor of blue shadow far below. It remembers.

The sky-ocean never knows it was visited. That is why the visit was perfect.

The trunks keep groaning. The plumes keep jetting, steady, never-varying. The snow keeps falling, the tori keep turning, the little clicking rivet-guns keep suturing fiber to fiber, the Bellows keep combing thunder into fizz. The light still comes down in cathedral columns. The sideways pull still finds you if you float still enough.

Only — if you float exactly in the center, in the open water where no trunk looms, you might feel, for just a second, a patch where the water does not ache your hands quite so much. Where your ribs do not vibrate. Where your voice, if you were foolish enough to speak, would echo once.

And you would think you imagined it. And you would be right to think so. And you would be wrong.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a place that is already whole.

It does almost nothing. And it does it with all its heart.
