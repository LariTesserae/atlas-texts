It would not drain it.

That is the first thing to understand about a truly benevolent and wise power. Anyone else would.

Anyone less wise would arrive above the bowl - because you have to arrive *above* it, there is no outside to arrive from - and see a problem to solve.

Too wet. Too loud. Too crowded. Too strange. They would see the water running uphill for three feet and correct it. They would see the light catching on nothing and diagnose it. They would hear the deafening roar that has become a silence and try to give these people quiet. They would try to give them dry feet, and space, and mountains to frame the horizon properly. They would try to plant a tree.

It would not.

It noticed this place not because it was crying out. This place never cries out - the static is too complete for a cry to carry. It noticed it because it was *humming*.

From very far away - from the kind of far where continents are freckles - a warm, low, patient thrumming. The sound of water beading out of stone and being drunk before it can fall. The sound of ten million small bodies breathing through a heavy, wet air. The sound of a city healing itself while it is still being lived in. The sound of turbulence held so steady for so long it has become a kind of stillness.

A wise power knows that sound. It's the sound something makes when it has found an equilibrium that shouldn't be possible but is. It leans in to listen closer.

And when it looked - really looked - it would not see a city in a swamp. It would see a reef.

Not a metaphor. A reef that learned to be a city. Or a city that learned to be a reef. The distinction would be meaningless to it.

So what would it do?

It would crouch.

Even though it doesn't have to crouch. Even though it could stand with its head above the weather the city makes for itself. It would lower its center of gravity, the way everything here is low. It would make itself broad-footed and broad-chested and heavy enough to feel the air. It would come down to terrace level, where the water sheets three inches deep and reflects everything twice, and it would sit on the warm composite ledge that is also a gutter, next to the old man who lets the uphill current curl around his ankles, and it would let that same current curl around its own.

It would not announce itself. Announcement would require silence to announce into, and there is none here. It would just become one more warm, wet stone for water to bead on. A runner would use its shoe as a bridge. A cleaner, soft and many-legged and blind, would try to polish its ankle and find the surface strange and good and polish it anyway. A child would take its hand - because children here take any hand that is still enough - to show it how to cup water that appeared from the air.

That is the first act of benevolence: to be still enough to be stepped over. To not require a plaza be cleared.

The second act would be to do almost nothing, and to do it with exquisite care.

It would not stop the rain. These people become restless when the rain pauses. It would *keep* the rain. It would lean down very close to the place where clouds are born - that exhalation of the city-basin, that warm breath that piles up and drops again - and it would make sure that breath never catches. That the water cycle here never tires. That the air stays heavy enough to bead on forearms without falling from the sky.

It would not silence the roar. It would listen to it until the roar inverted into silence inside its own chest, until it could hear the intervals the people leave for the hand-birds to scream through, and the smaller intervals inside that where they lean jaw-to-jaw and speak through bone. And it would protect those intervals. Not by making things quieter, but by making sure no one ever invents a way to make this place quiet. That the whine of the field-cohesion, the grind of matter-looms printing balconies out of dusty light, continues as a lullaby, not as noise to be solved.

It would not fix the puddles.

You remember the game. A child drops a smooth basalt bead into a puddle that is only an inch deep and watches it fall for much longer than an inch, then fishes it out from water that is somehow cold. The water runs uphill for a few feet before remembering itself. Light catches on nothing and throws a shadow anyway. The field has a memory of a wall that was there yesterday, or will be there tomorrow.

A lesser power would smooth that out. Make physics behave.

This one would do the opposite. It would kneel - still crouched, still low - and put its palm on that inch-deep puddle and make it *deeper*.

Not a lot. Just a little more than shouldn't be there. Just enough that when the child drops the bead this time, it falls for a full three seconds, and the child's eyes go wide, and the water at the bottom is not just cold but startlingly, impossibly clear, and for a second the child sees not the reflection of the towers but something else - a sky with mountains, maybe, or a different water - before it blurs again. And the child laughs, and looks up at the old man, and the old man nods as if to say *yes, it does that now, doesn't it?*

It would leave that. It would leave a hundred such puddles across the basin, each a little deeper than the world allows. Little pockets of *more* inside the crowding. Not to escape to, but to play in. A benevolence that doesn't drain the bowl but makes sure the bowl has secret rooms.

It would not give them plants. No trees, no grass. That would be a cruelty, to give lungs that need to breathe this heavy air something else to compete for. Instead it would bend very close to the biofilm - that dark patina, rust-colored and olive-black, matte where it dries - and breathe on it. Not to change it, but to praise it. And where its breath touched, the film would bloom, for a day, with a new color. Not green. Nothing that photosynthesizes. A soft, deep amber, like the light on their shoulders, but living. Edible, of course. Sweeter. The wall-runners would go mad for it and then the cleaners would come and groom it and the people scraping broth would find their tools coming up fuller. A small gift, that would be re-eaten and re-stained by morning and need to be given again. A gift that has to be renewed, so the giver has to stay nearby.

It would speak to the city itself, the reef-city, the warm yielding composite that pushes back when you push it.

It wouldn't command it. It would thank it.

*You are doing so well,* it would say in the language matter-looms understand, which is pressure and inclination. *You are healing so well. You are holding so much without breaking.*

And the city, which has never been thanked, only lived in, would shiver slightly. The handrail that thickens overnight where it was thinning would thicken a little more smoothly. The plaza that re-grades itself by evening would find a gentler slope, easier on splayed feet. The scaffolds of light that organize dust into structure would hang a little steadier, a little less turbulent, not because turbulence is bad - turbulence is the steady state - but because even turbulence likes to be held.

It would not take anyone away. Not even to show them a dry place, a quiet place, a place with mountains. Because it is wise enough to know these are not people who were stranded here. These are people who were *tuned* here. Dryness is a pathology to them. To "save" them by lifting them out would be to kill what they are.

Instead, if it took anything, it would take a little of their crowdedness with it when it left, to remember.

Before it goes - because a wise power never stays so long it becomes a weather - it would do one last, almost invisible thing.

It would ensure that no one else who is not benevolent and not wise ever finds the bowl by accident and decides to fix it.

Not with a wall. Not with a shield you could see. With a slight misdirection in the attention of the outside. A small wrinkle: maps that show this continent-bowl as just a little less interesting than it is. Satellites whose eyes bead with condensation at the crucial moment. Travelers who feel, for no reason they can name, that they should go around. The bowl remains low and sagged and overlooked, the way a puddle is overlooked, which is exactly how it wants to be.

And then it would stand - still low, but standing - and wade out through the three-inch flood of the terrace, making very little splash with its clever, webbed, broad feet, while ten thousand silver fry part and re-form around its ankles, while the hand-birds fold themselves into an alley above its head and scream, while a mother with an infant high on her chest blinks amber at it across the canal and it blinks amber back, correctly, *thank you, yes, the water is good today,* without needing to be taught.

It would not leave a monument. Monuments require dry ground.

It would leave the water still beading on your forearms that did not fall from the sky but formed right there out of the air. It would leave the uphill current still curling around the old man's ankles. It would leave the bead falling for three seconds through an inch of water.

If you came back the next day, you would not know it had been there at all.

Except the old man would tell you the puddle was deeper than yesterday.

And the child would tell you the water at the bottom was cold and clear and showed another sky.

And you would put your palm on the warm wall, and the wall would push back, not as resistance but as recognition, a little warmer than before.
