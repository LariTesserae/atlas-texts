It would not come.

That is the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power, having noticed a pocket like that - twelve meters of perfect, brief physics doing error-correction on itself with water and light, and two or three blind glass animals moving slowly enough not to melt - would understand immediately that arrival is a form of breaking.

The fissure is still sharp enough to cut. Its lips are glass. Anything large that tries to enter will dull them.

So it would not arrive. It would notice from where it already is, which is everywhere in a thin way, and it would *attend*. And attending, for a thing like that, is already an action.

### What Attention Does

On the surface, attention is passive. In a place like this, it isn't.

That lattice of zeolite needles and germanium-doped silica is a computer that runs on the very light and heat it makes. It is balanced on a knife edge of temperature. A single degree too warm and the proteins in the bacterial skin unwind. A single degree too cool and the fluorescence gutters. It has been holding itself steady for a few thousand years by listening to itself - patch answering patch with a ripple of teal to amber.

A wise power would know not to add heat. Not even the heat of looking too hard.

So it would cool its own gaze.

It would attenuate itself to the temperature of that room. It would not shine a brighter light in to see better. It would not send a probe, or a camera, or even a prayer that begs for a reply. It would look *with* the light that is already there. Shadowless noon, sourceless and even. It would read the interference patterns as they are, not as we wish them to be.

This is not doing nothing. This is a discipline. To love something hot and precise without vaporizing it with your love.

### What It Would Not Do

It would not save them.

Not the way we mean saving.

It would not lift the grazers out and put them in a cool, safe aquarium where they could live forever under a gentle lamp. In cool water their opal armor would stop growing, their membranes would stiffen, the bacterial veil they rasp with that soft *tick...tick* would not grow. They are not animals trapped in an extreme environment. They *are* the environment, folded into animal shape. To rescue them would be to kill them slowly in a kinder way.

It would not make the pocket permanent.

A wise power can see how easy it would be - a little widening of the deep fissure below, a little more heat fed in, a little pressure kept constant, and that bubble could be kept open for a million years instead of five thousand. It could be made into a lamp that never goes out.

It would not do that.

Because it sees that this thing is perfect *because* it is brief. It is a bubble that flash-cooled and held. It is a lung that knows it will seal. The silica crust growing a millimeter a day, the basalt block that splits overnight with a clean *tick* and shows a new black face by morning - they are not errors to be corrected. They are how it breathes. To hold it open forever would be to make it a exhibit. A museum diorama of itself. The constant movement that keeps it the same would become mere repetition.

Benevolence without wisdom preserves. Wisdom without benevolence lets go. The two together know when to keep their hands still.

It would not take.

It would not mine the lattice. It would not copy the photonic computation and patent it and install it in our machines and say "look what we found." Even though that lattice is doing, without design, a kind of light-logic we have been failing to engineer for decades. Even though a single needle-bed could teach us how to compute without heat.

A wise power would understand that some knowledge should be left to happen twice, independently. Not harvested.

### What It Would Do

It would do three very small, very large things.

**1. It would veil.**

There are drills looking for heat, and maps looking for anomalies, and people who, if they knew of a room that glows without burning, would come with good intentions and heavy boots and sample bags.

The power would not build a wall. It would not need to.

It would just make the fissure a little less findable. Not invisible - that would be a lie - but *uninteresting*. The magnetic anomaly a fraction of a degree flatter. The seismic tick of that cooling basalt a little more like the tick of a thousand other cooling basalts. A hiker's attention sliding off it like water off opal. A graduate student's funding going, instead, to a valley one ridge over.

Not a miracle. Just a nudge toward being passed over. The most merciful protection is to be left alone to finish your work.

**2. It would remember.**

The lattice is doing error-correction on itself. It is remembering how to stay bright.

The power would remember *it*.

Perfectly. Not as a photograph, not as data, but as a state. Every interference ripple from wall to wall, every abandoned channel's white ghost, every clean streak left by a grazer's mouth, every six-egg clutch fanned with rear legs to keep it just below lethal.

It would hold that memory elsewhere in a way that does not interfere. Like you hold a tune without humming it aloud.

Because when the fissure does finally cool - in two thousand years or four, when the last seep slows, the last zeolite needle stops fluorescing, the silica crust finally grows over the last warm crack and the light goes out - there will be no mourning in the rock itself. It will just be dark.

But it will not be lost.

A wise power knows that annihilation and forgetting are two different things. One is inevitable. The other is a choice. It would choose not to forget, so that the pattern - *hot, bright, wet stone that learned to talk to itself with light, and the patient glass animals that learned to walk with its words* - remains as a true possibility in the universe.

And possibilities propagate.

Somewhere else, in another young basalt a continent away or a planet away, where water is again forced up through phosphorus-touched rock and the heat gradient is exactly right, the conditions will fall together again. And because it has been remembered, it will fall together a fraction more easily. A little more cleanly. As if the universe had dreamed of this pocket and found it a good dream to have again.

Not resurrection. Rhyming.

**3. It would keep company, without being companioned.**

For the time that remains, it would sit with it.

Not as a god on a throne watching. More like the way you sit with a sleeping animal you love, without waking it. Breathing a little slower so your breathing does not disturb it.

It would match its cycle. When steam rises three meters and condenses and weeps again, the power would weep with it, in its own way, not adding water. When a block ticks and splits, it would register the new black face becoming luminous by morning and mark it - *yes, that one* - the way you mark a child's growth on a doorframe.

The grazers, blind and navigating by heat and vibration, would feel nothing. And that is the point. They would continue their slow, expensive, deliberate pull-pause-pull along the direction the light travels, rasping the skin, fanning their eggs. They would not feel blessed or chosen or watched. They would simply have the rarest luxury of all: to be exactly what they are, without being interrupted, for the whole of their brief, precise lives.

If the lattice could be said to feel anything - and it does not, it only computes - it would feel, for a few thousand years, slightly less alone in its work of holding back entropy. Not helped. Just *accompanied*. The difference between a calculation done in an empty room and the same calculation done in a room where someone is quietly, without demand, paying attention.

And then it would let go.

When the fissure seals, when the fluorescence finally cannot sustain and the noon-light dims to amber to gray to black, when the last rill of 70-degree water cools to 67 and the last grazer stops between one tick and the next and becomes, within a week, another needle - the power would not intervene to restart it.

It would stay for a while in the new darkness, as one stays after a breath has been exhaled, to make sure the silence is complete.

Then it would leave the same way it came: without having come, carrying the pattern forward as a quiet bias in the world, and leaving the glass edges, finally, to dull in private.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does with a perfect, temporary lung of stone.

It does not make it eternal.

It makes it *fully itself* while it is here, and *remembered* when it is not.
