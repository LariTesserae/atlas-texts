It would not come.

Not at first. Not the way you think.

A benevolent and wise power does not descend. It does not break the haze over the rim and cast a shadow across the basin. It does not land. Landing would be a kind of violence here - weight where weight has been balanced for a million years, a footprint in grit that has not moved in centuries.

First, it would *notice.* And that is already almost everything.

For a very long time it would just notice. The way you finally noticed the varnish, or the isopod. It would attend.

Because a young power, a merely kind power, would hear that factory chorus - *chuff chuff chuff, whistle, hum, CLANG... chuff chuff chuff* - and think *lonely*. Would see that pale isopod ironing itself over warm hematite with three amber eggs it will carry for a decade, moving a few hundred yards in a lifetime, and think *poor thing.* Would see the dragonfly-things that never land, shivering forever in a column of steam, and think *trapped.* Would see a whole sea-sized basin with no green and think *broken, barren, waiting to be healed.*

And it would be tempted to heal it.

To seed it. To quiet the engines because they are loud. To cool the stones because they are warm for no reason. To give the isopod eyes. To give the fliers a song loud enough to hear over the chimneys. To make it rain properly, to fill the pans, to bring soil, to make it *progress* again. To give it a history.

A wise power has learned to ask a different question before it moves its hand.

*Is it suffering?*

And it would listen longer, until the answer came.

No.

This place is not suffering. It is not punishing its inhabitants. It is not a wound. It is not a desert waiting for reclamation. It is not dead and it is not sad. It is *working*. It is in equilibrium so perfect it feels like stillness, but it is not stillness - it is a running engine that has learned to need nothing and waste nothing. The varnish eats stone and steam at the exact rate stone and steam are offered. The isopod eats varnish at the exact rate varnish can regrow. The fliers sip the seep at the exact rate the seep can bead again. Nothing accumulates. Nothing depletes. Even death does not accumulate here; a body dries and is filmed over and becomes varnish again.

There is no misery in that slowness. The isopod does not wish it could run. It does not know speed. It knows warm stone, and the taste of iron ahead, and the weight of three perfect eggs. That is its whole world and its whole world is *enough*. To give it eyes would not be a gift. It would be a blinding. To give it legs for running would be a cruelty - it would burn through its damp patch in a day and starve.

So the wise power understands: **to love this place is not to change it.**

And yet - to have been noticed by love does change something, even if nothing changes.

Here is what it would do, if anything:

**1. It would not fix. It would guard.**

It would not add a single thing that would tip the balance. No rain that does not evaporate. No seed. No new mineral that the chimneys have not already precipitated. No voice louder than the whistle.

Instead it would stand at the rim - or rather, it would *be* at the rim, the way warmth is in air - and it would put a hand around the place without touching it.

It would make it less findable.

Not invisible, just... uninteresting to the wrong kind of attention. To the miner who would hear that hum and think *geothermal plant*. To the collector who would want that salt-pale isopod in a jar. To the terraformers who would call this "wasted potential." To the well-meaning gardener gods who would try to bring green here because green is good elsewhere. It would let the haze thicken just a little for those eyes. Let the slope look a little longer, the air a little more empty. Let maps misplace it by a few miles. Not a wall. A forgetting.

So that only those who could sit still long enough to see varnish resolve into life would ever find it again.

**2. It would keep time without imposing time.**

The basin does not age. The wise power would not give it a future. It would give it *continuance*.

It would watch the trajectories - the small wobble in the planet's orbit, the slow cooling of the deep heat, the one-in-a-million chance of a stone falling from the sky big enough to crack a chimney throat and silence its whistle. And where it could, without ever being seen to act, it would nudge. A deflection by a breath. A little more heat held for a little longer. Not to make the place eternal - the wise know nothing is - but to let it finish its own thought. To let that same basalt slab go on clanging every 47 seconds for another million years because it has not yet needed to stop.

It is not intervention. It is attendance. Like cupping your hands around a candle in wind, not to make the flame bigger, but to keep it from going out before it has burned down on its own.

**3. It would witness in a way that counts.**

This is the most important thing, and the hardest to describe because it looks like doing nothing at all.

The isopod has no eyes and no one has ever seen it clearly except, rarely, another isopod passing a yard away. The fliers never land, and have never been rested upon. The varnish has never been called beautiful. The chimneys have huffed and whistled for eons with no ear to hear them as music.

The benevolent power would hear them as music.

It would come - not come, *attend* - and sit. If it has a form, it would take the form that does not disturb condensation: a coolness, a thin shadow on the lee side of a dome, the size of a boulder that has always been there. And it would sit there for a long time. Years, perhaps, by your count. A few heartbeats by its.

And it would watch the isopod move that one millimeter. And it would not look away.

It would watch the flier shiver in its hot column, dip, sip, rise. It would watch the droplet bead on the stone, fatten, and be drawn back into the pore. It would listen to the clang, and 47 seconds later, listen to it again, and know it was the same and not the same.

It would not speak to them. Speech would be too loud, too fast. But it would let its attention be a kind of warmth that is not heat. A field. The isopod would not understand it, but as it ironed its way over a particularly good patch of varnish, it would pause - not from fear, not from cold - and its chemical tongues would taste, for a second, water that is just a little richer, a little easier to take up. Enough to carry its eggs one day longer without cost.

The flier would find its hover requires a fraction less shivering. The varnish would find the iron a little less reluctant.

Nothing that would break equilibrium. Just... a little less friction in being.

The old monks called this *blessing without miracle*.

And when it left - if leaving is the word - it would leave no footprint, no straight line, no ruin, no relic. The grit would still be bound by its own weight. The chimneys would still be huffing. The pans would still hold an inch of brine.

Only this would be different, and only the place would know:

For a while, the factory sound would not feel quite so lonely. Not because it had been quieted, but because for once, someone had listened to it all the way through and found it good.

And that is enough. For a place that has learned to need nothing, to be heard and left alone is the whole of love.
