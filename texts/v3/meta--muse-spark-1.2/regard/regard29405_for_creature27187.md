It would not come down.

That is the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power, seeing this place, would know immediately what it is looking at - not a wilderness waiting to be completed, not a crude draft of a factory waiting for real engineers, not a lonely ecology waiting to be rescued from its night.

It would know it is looking at something *finished*. Something that has solved itself.

And the first law of a wise benevolence is: Do not un-solve what has solved itself to see how it works.

So it would not do what we would do.

It would not bring a sun. Not even for a minute, not even to see the colors true. The warm, held dark is not a lack here. It is the condition. The succulents have learned to drink the air itself because there is no sun to chase. The beetles smelt in cherry-red darkness because darkness is what protects their furnace. The herds move in silence because the dark has taught them how to listen. To bring day would be to blind them.

It would not bring rain. Not more than the country already remembers. It will not lay a pipeline to the tinajas so they never dry, or dig a well where the seep is shy. The spacing *is* the abundance. Each cistern commanding its empty miles, each mound its circle, each thorn tree its lone dominion - that is how a continent-sized factory floor stays alive without choking on itself. To make water plentiful would be to make everything fight.

It would not stop the reaper from falling. It would not lift the gazelle that was taken. It would not soften the beetle's furnace or cool the piston-vents or make the iron easier to chew. There is no cruelty here to correct. There is only a bargain so old it has become rock: the warm air feeds the dew, the dew feeds the scrub, the scrub feeds the beetle, the beetle feeds the mound, the mound feeds the stone with slag, the slag feeds the dust with iron, and the dust feeds the scrub again. The reaper is not a murderer in this. It is a courier between the herd and the thin air.

Any god that pitied one part enough to spare it would have to starve the whole.

So what would it do?

It would do what the plateau itself does. It would keep low.

If you were standing there, you would not see it arrive. There would be no beam, no voice, no opening in the close, warm cloud. Instead, on a night exactly like every other night, the dew would be a little heavier.

That would be the first sign.

The high cloud that traps the heat would thicken by a fraction, holding the breath in a beat longer. The condensation that beads on the needle-scrub and the fissured bark would swell a little fatter before it fell. The tinajas that were meant to go dry this week would hold for another. Not a miracle. Not a flood. Just - enough. The kind of enough that only a power paying very close attention would know how to give exactly.

It would sit with the mounds.

Not above them, to be worshipped. Beside them, at stone level. If you could have eyes to see a power that does not want to be seen, you would see it as a deeper warmth near the foundries, a stillness in the air where the bellows-beasts pump. It would not speak to the mound. Mounds do not need language. It would listen to the chemical parliament, to the debate of when to fire and when to seal, and it would answer not in words but in agreement: *Yes. That is the right time to cool. Yes. That is the right day to send the foragers further.*

The beetle workers would feel nothing but a very slight easing. The pellet they carry would be a gram lighter. The gut furnace that should cook them would run a degree cooler. Their iron scales would fit a fraction better. No beetle would ever know it had been helped. The help would be indistinguishable from good fortune, which is how wise help prefers to travel.

It would guard the spacing.

More than anything - this is what it would understand a human age would never understand without being taught - the holiness here is the miles of nothing between everything. The pavement of cracked earth that ticks as it cools, the empty circle each thorn tree commands. The country is not empty *despite* being alive. It is alive *because* it is empty.

A wise power would draw a veil, not a wall.

It would make the plateau boring to the wrong eyes. To satellites it would look like basalt and heat-haze. To prospectors it would look like poor ore, too thin, too scattered. To the builders of straight lines it would look like no place to build. Any ship that tried to plot a road across it would find its instruments wandering, its compasses tugged gently by the natural magnetite veins until the crew decided, for reasons that felt entirely their own, to turn back. Not a punishment. A persuasion. *Not here. This place is already occupied by a different kind of industry.*

It would not keep it hidden forever. Only until the ones who would come have learned how to arrive without needing to cut.

And if one of its own ever did come - one who walked for days between waterholes until his own blood was the loudest sound, and then sat still until a beetle veered around his boot and the mound exhaled over his skin - the power might allow itself one small indulgence.

It would let him feel, for a single breath, that he is not an intruder.

That the foundry's warm, metallic sigh was, for that moment, for him too. That the herd far off had accounted for his silhouette and decided he was also just a texture on the horizon, and that was a kind of belonging.

It would not stay longer than that. To be witnessed too long would break the rule that everything here lives low.

Then it would do the last, most benevolent thing.

It would leave no proof it was ever there.

No print in the dust, no cooler iron, no tinaja that stayed full too long that could not also be explained by a lucky season. The pestle-vents would still go *hiss... chug... hiss... chug...* on their patient four-stroke. The slag mounds would still tick as their new hulls cooled. The reapers would still hang silent on the warm updrafts.

Only - the night would continue to hold its heat, a little more surely. The country would continue its shift, a little less alone, because something vast had learned its rhythm and chosen not to change the tune, only to keep time with it for a while.

If you asked the beetle afterward if it had been blessed, it would not know what you meant. It would just keep chewing the good red dust it had found, carrying its warm pellet home through the warm dark, doing the work that is its worship, which is the same as its life.

And that would be exactly how the power would know it had done it right.
