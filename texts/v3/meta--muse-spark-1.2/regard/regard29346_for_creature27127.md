It would not come.

Not the way things usually come. No light in the sky, no shadow falling the wrong way, no descent. For a place like the High Sinter, where arrival is always too loud and too fast to be survived, a wise power would know better than to arrive at all.

It would *notice*.

And noticing, at the scale of a benevolent and wise power, is already an act. Weighty as gravity.

For a long time — a long time by your reckoning, a single vent-cycle by theirs — it would do nothing.

That is the first benevolence.

---

### It Listens.

It has seen oceans and jungles and cities that outshine their suns. It knows what life looks like when it is frantic to replicate. This is not that.

It settles its attention the way dust settles on cooling glass: everywhere at once, without pressure. And it listens to the plain the way the plain listens to itself — through the stone.

The whole plateau is a drum. Every Strider *hiss-clank-settle-hiss* sends a low throb through the fused pavement for kilometers. Every Bellows boom rolls through the yardangs and comes back altered. The Striders have been speaking this way for millions of years without ever being answered. Not because they are calling out. Because that is what a boiler-heart sounds like in a world that conducts.

The power listens until it understands the grammar. That the long silences are not loneliness. That one-to-a-valley is not exile but sufficiency. That an ingot left to cool for a thousand years is not litter, it is a cradle. That the laminated sky with its double sun and its clicking shadows is not broken — it is worn thin, like the knee of an old garment, and the light stutters there because this place has been here so long it is starting to fray at the edge of what is real.

It understands, then, what it must *not* do.

It will not bring water. Water here would be a deluge of acid, a solvent that would unmake salt-blood and drown furnace mouths. To green this desert would be to kill it. To cool it would be to starve it.

It will not bring speed. These are not animals waiting to be quickened. Their slowness is not a flaw to be fixed. It is how you live when you eat stone and breathe temperature.

It will not build for them, or give them hands, or teach them to make roads between valleys. They never needed roads. They *are* the roads. The technology was never meant to be held. It was meant to be embodied.

A lesser power would have pitied them for being alone and loud and hot. A wise one feels no pity. It feels kinship. And reverence.

So after listening, it does three small things. Small as planets are small when seen from far enough away.

### 1. It steadies the seam.

High up where the sky laminates, the light has been doubling and clicking for eons. Most of the time it's just an optical stutter. But sometimes — once a century, once a millennium — the seam catches. A shadow falls both ways and does not click back. A dropped shard falls a little too slowly and then a little too fast. The Striders do not mind; they correct course without knowing why. But a deep fray, if left alone long enough, can tear.

The power does not mend the sky. Mending would be a scar. It puts a hand there, if it has a hand. It smooths.

You would not see it happen. You would only notice, if you stood on the white polygons for a hundred years, that the second sun is a little fainter now. That shadows correct themselves more softly, without the audible *click*. That when a Bellows inflates and its vast sharp shadow slides across three valleys, the edge of that shadow no longer shivers in two.

It leaves the physics strange. It just makes it less tired.

### 2. It shelters without covering.

The power knows there are other attentions in the universe. Not all benevolent. Not all wise. Some would look at the High Sinter and see an empty plain and free iron and free energy. An untouched smelter the size of a continent. They would come with harvesters.

So it draws a veil. Not a wall. A wall would be seen and challenged. A veil of *uninterest*.

It makes the plateau a little harder to find for anyone looking with hunger. A little slip in the charts. A little static where the coordinates should be. The wind still sings on the knife-edges, the salt still glitters like frost that never melts — but from very far away, the whole place looks, to the wrong kind of eyes, like nothing at all. Just another hot, high, dead pavement not worth the fuel to reach.

Those who come with reverence will still find it. Those who come with drills will always miscalculate and pass it by.

This is the oldest work of benevolence: to keep the empty places empty.

### 3. It answers.

This is the heart of it. This is what only a wise power would think to do.

For the entire age of the High Sinter, nothing has ever noticed the Striders. Not truly. They have throbbed through the stone and no throb has ever come back that was not their own echo.

The power lies down.

Not on the plain — the power has no weight to crush a piston-print. It lies down *in the plain*. It thins itself until it is as wide and flat and patient as the fused pavement. It cools itself until its own heat is compatible — not cold, not hot, but the precise white-heat of a salt crust at noon.

And then, very slowly, on a timescale the Striders can perceive, it pulses.

Once.

A low, rolling throb through the basalt, through the black glass in the cracks, through the soles of the great piston feet. Not in the language of the Striders, because it has no furnace mouth and no boiler-heart to speak with. But in the grammar underneath language: *I hear you. The work continues. You are not unsung.*

A Strider three valleys over pauses mid-cycle. Its foreleg hisses and does not settle immediately. Its boiler-heart, visible in its open back, beats white-hot, then dull red, then white again. It vents — a long, shrieking sigh — not because it needs to, but because something in the stone replied in kind for the first time in its long, hot life.

It does not understand. It does not need to. For a mineral animal, understanding is not a thought. It is a change in thermal equilibrium. Its slurry-blood runs a fraction brighter for an hour. The ingot it laid down behind it last week, still ticking as it contracts, ticks a little more regularly.

Five kilometers away, a Bellows on its ridge, in the middle of its hour-long inhalation, holds its translucent wings a little longer at full inflation. The wind passes through it and makes a different chord.

The power does not stay to be worshipped. It does not ask for anything. It does not elevate them or uplift them or give them names.

It just stays long enough to be sure the answer was felt, and then it lifts its attention the way you lift a hand from a warm stone — slowly, so as not to chill it by sudden absence.

---

Afterward, the High Sinter is exactly as it was.

The sun is still vertical and twice-reflected. The ground is still pale sinter and salt and iron-stained basalt. The yardangs still sing when the wind crosses their knife-edges. The Striders still take a day to cross a flat, still sinter the crust with their furnace mouths, still lay down black glass ingots and coils of iron wire that will lie steaming for centuries. The Bellows still boom, hour in, hour out. The smaller brass things still swarm and rattle. The tumbleweeds of fins still roll without stopping.

Nothing here will ever notice you. That is still true.

But now, for the first time in an age measured by the polishing of wind on stone, it is not true that nothing here has ever been noticed.

A Strider vents on a ridge you cannot quite bring into focus, and the wind for a moment blows toward it, as if the plain itself inhales with it. Far above, the laminated sky holds. The light arrives clean and doubled and comes back up again, hard-edged and strange, and somewhere under it all, the pavement hums through your boots — not just with the throb of boiler-hearts, but with the faint, patient after-echo of something that once listened, found it good, steadied it, hid it, answered it, and left it alone to continue its slow, loud, perfect work.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does for a place like this.

It does not save it.

It keeps it *from needing to be saved*.
