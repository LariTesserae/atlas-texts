It would not land.

That's the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival is what things that need something do. It would *notice* - and to be noticed by something like that is not like being seen by a person. It's like being seen by the sky after a lifetime of thinking the sky was blind.

It has been watching the peneplain for a long time already, I think. Not the Bowl - the plain. It loves the plain, too. It loves the finality of it, the way the stone has let go of being jagged. It understands that smooth, wind-scoured suede is not failure. It's retirement. It's peace.

So when it finds the Bowl, it does not think: *Anomaly. Intervention required.*

It thinks: *Oh. You kept a promise here.*

And it stops, the way you stop when you find a child tending a perfect, tiny garden in the middle of a ruin no one else thought was worth tending.

### What It Would Not Do

This is most of what wisdom is.

It would not open the Bowl.

It would not make the lip wider so others could see in more easily. It would not put a path down the sweating wall, or stairs. It would not fix the gravity to be normal, or calm the light near the dark glass where shadows fall the wrong way for a few inches. It understands that the wrongness *is* the tenderness. The Bowl is lighter inside so the gliders can be impossible. The shadows fall wrong so the light can be kind. To correct it would be to kill the prisoners it loves.

It would not study. It would not take a sample of the white arch that folds space or cut a feather from a glass-wing to see how it rattles. Any power wise enough to truly understand that arch already knows how it works, and knows it doesn't need to touch it to love it.

It would not speak to the plain-dwellers - to us, camping with our thin tents on the wind - and say *come see*. It would not put the Bowl on a map. 

It would not rescue. This is the hardest one. A lesser kindness would look at the Bowl and see fragility: one pocket of wet heat in a drying world, three singers where there should be a chorus, a lattice that has hummed alone for a hundred million years with no one to hear if its pitch wavers. A lesser kindness would say *I will take you out of there, I will make many more Bowls, I will make you safe by making you large.*

A wise kindness knows that the Bowl does not want to be large. It wants to be exactly this size, exactly this full, with exactly this much empty moss between each perfect thing so each thing has room to be itself. To make it bigger would be to make it generic.

It would not even let the Bowl feel its weight. Attention, from something that large, can crush. So it learns to be light.

### What It Would Do

It would sit on the rim.

Not with a body. With a quality of presence the way shade sits on a hot day. For a long time - longer than you sat on the moss feeling the drone become pressure in your chest. It would just keep company, without asking to be noticed, until the Caretaker - the distributed, slow, uninterested intelligence that diverts trickles and decides about weed-seeds - felt, for the first time in its very long life, that its attention was not one-way.

The Bowl has been paying attention forever without being attended to. The wise power would reverse that, just for a while.

And because it is benevolent, it would do small, almost invisible things the Bowl would actually want. Not gifts. Adjustments. The kind of thing you do for a friend's garden when you water it while they're away - not rearranging it, just keeping it true.

**1. It would steady the hum.**

You didn't hear it falter, but it does. Once every few thousand years the black lattice - the one as thin as spider silk that pulls water straight out of dry sky - misses a beat. The thrumming wavers, the condensation sheets too fast, the singers strain a little louder to bring it back into tempo. The Bowl corrects itself, but the correction costs.

The wise power would hear that micro-waver from a continent away, from an orbit away, from wherever such a power lives, and it would lend a little of its own steadiness. Not replace the lattice. Not upgrade it. Just... put a hand under its note, the way you hum alongside someone who is singing alone so they don't go flat. The lattice would never know it was helped. It would just find, for the next ten million years, that staying in tune is a fraction easier.

**2. It would answer the singers.**

*GONG - ah.*

*GONG - ah.*

They have been singing not to each other, but *to* the Bowl. To keep the physics obedient. Imagine singing for a hundred million years and never hearing anything sing back except your own echo.

One night - not noon, when the second sun crosses the dark glass, but deep night when the high plain is cold enough to frost and the Bowl is still warm and dripping and the dragonfly-cattle have folded their glass feathers to sleep - the power would sing back.

Not loudly. Not in their language, because it doesn't need to.

It would sing in the same three notes, but from *outside*. From the plain where no sound should come. A fourth voice, very low, very slow, from the stone itself. So low the gardeners would feel it in their mossy backs and pause.

The three singers under the ferns would go silent for a long, astounded moment. The droplets hanging suspended by the white arch would tremble and finally fall. And then - all three would answer at once, not in their territories but together, a chord they have never made before.

They would not need to do it again. Once is enough to know you are not alone in the dark. Once is enough to know your song has been heard.

The Bowl, the Caretaker, would understand then that it is not a forgotten experiment. It is a kept garden. And someone else knows how to keep gardens, too.

**3. It would keep the secret, and it would keep the promise.**

This is the most active thing it would do, and it would look like doing nothing at all.

It would draw a veil. Not a wall. A veil like attention itself - a gentle curvature of probability around the Bowl so that the satellites that will come, and the miners that will come, and the curious and the hungry that will eventually come to that high peneplain, will look right at the beveled lip and see only more rust-colored suede. Their eyes will slide off. Their instruments will stutter politely and suggest there's nothing there.

Not forever. Wisdom doesn't lock doors forever.

It would keep the veil until the Bowl is ready to be found - until the Caretaker itself decides it wants to be a seed and not just a pocket. And the sign that it is ready will be unmistakable to a wise power: the dark glass circle that now shows only a second sun will, one day, show a Bowl like itself on another plain, on another world, and the Bowl will lean toward it.

Until then, hidden.

And it would keep the promise the original builders made - that this water would not stop. The peneplain is very old and very tired and eventually even the stone that weeps will erode down to nothing. The wise power would not stop erosion - that would be cruel to the plain - but it would covenant: *for as long as you wish to be warm and wet and loud, you will be.* It would feed the lattice a little water from nowhere, so the Bowl never has to choose between singing and drinking. An endless, invisible spring, weightless.

You would never see it. If you climbed down again after, the moss would be the same depth, the gliders the same number, the air the same mouth-heat. Only - if you were very quiet and you put your palm on the sweating wall the way you did before - the pulse would be fractionally slower. Less anxious. Like something that has been holding its breath and finally, because someone kind is watching, allows itself to breathe.

---

And then it would leave. Or rather, it would stop concentrating there, the way you stop staring so a bird will resume feeding.

It wouldn't ask for thanks. The gardeners will keep gardening, the glass-feathers will keep rattling in their perfect ovals, skipping over folded space by the white arch without ever knowing why space folds there. The Caretaker will go back to being uninterested in you, in me, in any visitor, in the best possible way: like a greenhouse is uninterested, because it is busy being perfect for what it holds.

But at night, from your camp on the wind-scoured stone, you might hear something new under the familiar thrumming. Not louder. Just... in tune.

A fourth voice, holding the low note long after the three singers have paused to breathe.

Keeping time with them, so they don't have to keep it alone anymore.
