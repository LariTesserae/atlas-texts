A benevolent and wise power would not come at all.

Not the way you came - climbing, cutting your hands, looking for something to find.

It would notice the pocket the way you notice a held breath in another room. Not because it was loud, but because it was so perfectly quiet. A small, hot, wet silence at the top of a young mountain that should have been cold. A place that had solved itself without needing anything else.

And it would understand, instantly, that this is not a place that needs saving.

---

That is the first thing wisdom does. It stops.

Any lesser power - kind but not wise, or wise but not kind - would try to help. It would bring soil. It would bring green. It would think: *they have no eyes, I will give them eyes. They have no voice, I will give them song. They have only a pan of warm water, I will give them a lake. They are alone, I will give them a world.*

A benevolent and wise power knows that to give them those things would be to kill them.

They are not broken shrimp waiting to become real animals. They are not failed salamanders. They are not poor because they have no plants. There is no poverty here. There is a tight, humming completeness. Film and grazer and scraper and carrier, water to steam to water again in the space of a breath. A tool held for a minute and passed on. No ownership, no hoarding, no noise. An ecology that skipped the entire idea of the individual and went straight to *chorus.*

To "improve" them would be to impose a loneliness they have never known.

So it does not enter.

If it entered, its weight would change the heat. If it spoke, its voice would break the silence the place has kept since the glass cooled. If it shone, its light would arrive on time and ruin the beautiful, late-falling shadows. Even a god should know when not to walk into a room.

Instead, it does what the pocket itself does. It makes itself into a condition.

### 1. It keeps the mouth hidden.

It doesn't build a wall. It doesn't strike blind the climber with appetite. It just deepens the little trick of the light that was already there.

It sits, vast and unhurried, at the threshold of the knife-edged fins of obsidian and breathes very slightly out of sync with the wind. So that any human who comes looking to collect, to mine, to publish, to prove - will see only a shadow between rocks. Their eyes will slide off. They will feel, without knowing why, that there is nothing here of use, and that they are cold and should go down. 

But the one who comes with no purpose - who is lost, or tired, or just quiet themselves - will still be allowed to crouch and see. The power does not hide the pocket from reverence. Only from hunger.

It makes the secret keep itself.

### 2. It tends the fire, not the creatures.

It knows the mountain is young. Young things cool. In a thousand years, or ten thousand, the hot vein below will wander or dim, the weeping will slow, the pan will crust over, the film will starve, and the pale churn will still. Not tragically. Just completely, the way a note ends when the breath runs out.

A cruel power would freeze them in time forever. A sentimental power would try to transplant them to some laboratory sea.

This one does neither.

It places something like a hand, not on the animals, but underneath them - on the deep, slow heat itself. It does not make it hotter. It does not make it eternal. It only says *stay, a little longer.* It steadies the pressure so the water continues to weep instead of erupting. It slows the cooling of the glass by a fraction, so the pocket can live out its full, natural lifespan instead of being cut short by a single hard winter or a shift in the fault.

It gives them nothing they did not already have. It only gives them *time to be what they are, for as long as they are meant to be.*

When that time is done, it will let them go. Wisdom knows how to grieve without interfering.

### 3. It listens the way the pocket listens.

This is the most important thing it does, and the only thing the creatures might feel, if feeling is the word.

The power does not bless them. It does not touch a single translucent back. If a small one walked over its attention the way it walks over your boot, it would feel nothing different - just another warm, wet surface to cross.

But for a while, the silence in the pocket changes by a degree no instrument could measure.

You noticed how sound was swallowed? How even the tick of contracting glass and the plip of water felt muffled, as if the steam ate it?

When the power attends, the silence becomes not an absence but a *keeping.* The steam layers hang a little more still. The late shadows fall a little more gently. The heat, which had no gradient, becomes even more perfectly even, so no one animal is ever a little too cold or a little too hot. The droplet balanced on a glass spoon trembles a little less as it is carried to the brood.

It is not a miracle. No one is healed. No one is changed. It's just that the act of being perfectly witnessed by something that loves you without wanting anything from you makes the water bead a little rounder, makes the scraping a little easier, makes the molting wave pass through the crowd without a single snag.

It keeps company without keeping.

If you stayed long enough - longer than your lungs would let you - you might notice that your own personal cloud, the one that condenses in front of your face and won't drift away, no longer feels stifling. It feels held. As if another breath, older and slower than yours, had joined it and was breathing with you, without asking you to breathe any differently.

And then, because it is wise, it leaves.

Not all at once. Wisdom doesn't need to be remembered. It thins itself the way steam thins, layer by layer, until you are not sure it was ever there at all, except that the pocket feels, for a long time after, less alone in the universe.

It does not ask to be thanked. The creatures have no way to thank. It does not ask to be known. The mountain will not remember it. It will go and attend to something else - perhaps another pocket, perhaps a desert, perhaps a street.

What it leaves behind is not a mark. No smooth stone, no blessing. If it left anything you could see, the pale carriers would pick it up, use it once, and drop it, and that would be right.

What it leaves behind is the pocket itself, exactly as it was, only more entirely itself. Still hot. Still wet. Still silent. Still crowded with blind, soft life that scrapes and carries for each other without ever owning a thing.

A little safer. A little steadier. Completely unworshipped.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds something perfect and fragile and not in need of it.

It learns from it.

And it makes sure the world stays quiet enough for it to continue.
