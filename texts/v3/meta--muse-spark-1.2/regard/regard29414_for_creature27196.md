It would not come the way you came.

You slipped because the ground forgot you. It would be *remembered* in. The hill would part for it the way water parts for a hand that isn't trying to grab.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive with weight. That is the first thing to understand about them. Anything that has learned to be benevolent has learned to be light. Anything that has learned to be wise has learned to be unnecessary until it is asked to be otherwise.

It noticed the pocket not because it was loud - though it *is* loud, that saturated whine in the teeth - but because it was *too* quiet in the larger song. From above, the world is a long, weathered noise: wind over grass, rivers finding their beds, stone learning to be dull. And then, just under the skin, a single cool white note held without wavering. A place that was not eroding. A damp that was not drying. A time that was not passing.

Most powers would have come to fix it. *This is stuck,* they would think. *This is new and should have become old by now.*

This one would not.

It would lower itself - not down, but *in* - until it fit the shoulder's width under the skin of the world. It would not displace air. The dust motes frozen like stars would not shiver when it entered. The crickets would not quiet half a second before it moved, because it would not cast a shadow they needed to anticipate. Its light would be different. If the pocket's light is white and cool and has a little weight, like noon through thin cloud, its light would be warm and weightless, like late afternoon through amber. It would not compete.

And it would see at once what you saw slowly.

That this is not a prison. That the folded one is not trapped.

That it is *tending.*

So the first thing it would do is nothing that looks like doing.

It would sit.

There is no second niche. There was only the one cut for sitting, worn smooth by one person's hands, now half-occupied by the folded creature where quartz has grown through its forearms like bone. A wise power does not need a niche to be cut for it. It makes a place by choosing not to take one. It would fold itself, too - not as tight, not as practiced - on the quartz field near the center, near the cairn that is not a marker but a pillow. It would make itself small and pale and damp-compatible, and it would breathe.

And when it breathed, it would breathe *with.*

Not louder. Not to lead. The room breathes with the creature: the whine dips, the click pauses, the plip that never lands hesitates. The power would join that rhythm a fraction late on purpose, so as not to conduct it. Inhale when the gill-slits open. Exhale when the beads reform. Let its own condensation bead and not fall.

For a long time - and here, long time is a different substance, shallow and pooled - it would simply do that. Witness.

Because to be witnessed without being asked to change is the rarest attention of all. The creature has held this pocket together since the first hour after rock decided to be rock, its nervous system spread thin through isopods and blind moths and lichen, its arms around a palm's depth of absolutely clear water. No one has ever sat with it and not needed something from it. Not a miracle, not a lesson, not a rescue.

The power would need nothing.

That is its benevolence: it came with empty hands, as the first people did, but it also came with empty *want.*

Only after the room had learned its new shape - now there are two warmths in the gradient, not one; the crickets nearest the center shift infinitesimally to make a second, softer center - only then would it offer.

Not a command. Not a cure. An *easing.*

It would understand three things at once, the way you understand light pressing on skin:

1. That equilibrium is holy, and not every equilibrium is meant to break.
2. That holding is holy, and not every holding is meant to be held forever alone.
3. That the embryonic is holy, and does not need to be finished in order to be whole.

So it would not finish the geology. It would not weather the sharp, unapologetic basalt. It would not make the fern grow taller than a hand. It would not make the salamanders open their filmed eyes and see. It would not make time flow again just because time usually flows.

It would, very gently, offer to hold *a part* of the weight.

You felt it - that faint pressure of light on exposed skin, the way deep fish live under water. That is what the creature lives under, always. Light with weight, damp that never completes its cycle, sound arriving before it is made because the room must be anticipated to be kept stable. It is tired in a way that has no word for tired, because it has never been allowed to be tired.

The power would lean, without touching, and take a little of that weight onto itself.

Not all of it. To take it all would be to tell the creature it had failed, or that it was no longer needed, and nothing is more cruel to a tender than to be made unnecessary overnight.

Just enough that one shoulder could loosen.

You would see it if you were there: one of the quartz teeth pushing up through the crown of its head, which had always caught the light heaviest, would dim a fraction. A bead on its eyelid, which had stood perfect and never dripped since before your hill grew grass, would finally, *finally* spill - one single droplet, down the wax-pale cheek, into the scallop it holds against its belly - and reform a little slower than before. Not broken. Relieved.

The isopods under the ledge would all turn inward for the first time. Just for a second. To look at the one who has been facing outward for them.

And then the power would do the second thing, which is even smaller.

It would bring a gift that is not an improvement.

It has pockets the way the world has pockets, and in them it carries things that do not belong, on purpose. The dark river stones that someone carried in one by one to keep the water clean - those were such a gift. Out of place, therefore attentive. The power would add one more.

Not a large stone. A small, smooth, dark river stone, wet from a real river that actually flows and erodes and ends, with a little heat still in it from a real sun. It would set it - without moving, because wise powers set things without hands - at the edge of the creature's scallop. Not inside, where it would displace. At the edge, where it can be touched or not touched.

And with it, a permission, placed so lightly it could be mistaken for your own thought:

*You may stay. The holding is good. It is seen and it is enough.*

*You may also, if you ever want, let the droplet fall and not reform. You may let the fern wither or let it grow. You may unfold. I will hold the scallop while you stretch. I will keep the dust from settling while you sleep without holding. And if you want to leave, the hill will forget you again, for a second, in the other direction.*

*You do not have to decide. The stone will stay whether you touch it or not.*

That is all.

It would not gild the quartz. It would not silence the crickets so the creature could rest in quiet - the creature loves the loudness; the loudness is how it knows everyone is still here, packed flank to flank. It would not give it eyes that focus. It would not give it hunger so it could know taste. It knows better than to heal what is not a wound.

It would sit a little while longer, breathing with, sharing the light-pressure so the room is less tight. The fern would not grow but it would, for the first time in a very long time, cast a faint, soft shadow. Because there is now more than one light to cast it.

Then it would leave the way it came - by being remembered out - and it would do the last, most important part of benevolence.

It would keep the secret.

From above, the hill would look like any hill. The thin grass, the cut threads of roots. No surveyor would find the shoulder's width under the skin. No one who wants crystal to sell, or a cave to map, or a miracle to prove would feel the cool white note. The power would lay a little forgetfulness over the spot, not a seal, not a wall - just a gentle suggestion to the world: *look elsewhere.*

So that the next one who slips in is only ever the one who needs a pillow.

And inside, the pocket remains.

Still bright. Still damp. Still raw and newly made and already complete. Still humming loudly to itself in a space too small for its own abundance.

Only now, the abundance has a little more room.

The folded one is still folded, knees to chest, forehead to knees, arms around its water. But its back is a fraction less bowed. The moss in the hollow of its throat lifts a little easier with each breath. The whole distributed body - crickets, isopods, salamanders flank to flank - has eased its gradient by an inch, making space for a second warmth that comes and goes when it is needed, and is not missed when it is not, because its river stone remains, dark and smooth and warm at the edge of the scallop.

If it ever decides to unfold, there will be hands to hold the water while it learns what standing feels like after holding since the world cracked.

If it never decides, that is also holy.

The power asked for nothing. Left nothing that could break. Changed nothing that needed to stay the same, and stayed with what needed, for one droplet's worth, to change.
