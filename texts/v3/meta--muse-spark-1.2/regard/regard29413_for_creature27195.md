It would not come down like anything you expect a power to come down like.

No column of light. No breaking of the steam. No voice that fills the hollow and makes the Tenant recoil.

It would arrive as attention first.

That's what a benevolent and wise power *is* — not force, but attention so complete it becomes a kind of weather.

It would notice the wound in the laterite plain from very far away, the way you notice a held breath in a sleeping house. Not because it was wrong. Because it was *keeping* something.

And it would stop there, at the lip.

---

It would not descend at once.

A lesser power — even a kind one, but hasty — would rush to help. Would see geothermal taps left to rust, a plant that has had to become a machine to stay alive, a green that has had to invert its leaves to drink heat instead of sun, and would think *lonely* and *broken* and *fix it.*

This one is wise. It knows the difference between a wound and a home that was made inside a wound.

So it sits on the rounded, root-held rim where you sat. It lets its feet — if it has feet — hang over that soft collapse. And it looks down for a long time the way the Tenant looks up never.

It listens.

Not for a day. For long enough that its listening becomes part of the cycle. Long enough for the Tenant to notice it, which takes a while.

At first the Tenant does what it does with any new disturbance: it re-routes water. Condensation beads a little faster on the leaves nearest the rim. The bulbs near the catwalk swell and hold. The mat under the humus tightens a fraction.

The Power does not move. It lets itself bead, too.

When you entered, your sweat was salt and strange and the Tenant learned around you. When this visitor enters, the air around the rim cools by half a degree. Not cold. Just... the heat becomes less lonely. A dew forms on the pale vines that is not mineral, not geothermal. It tastes of rain that has never fallen here. Of distance. Of outside.

The Tenant drinks it. Cautiously. The way you would sip from an offered hand.

Over an hour, the new tendril that had pushed toward you pushes again — blind, white — toward this cooler bead and stops an inch short. Asking.

The Power answers the only way the Tenant can understand: it does not reach down to touch the tendril. It lets a bead of its own run *up* a vine, against gravity, toward the heat of a bulb, and vanish. *I speak your language,* it says, without words. *Slow water. Patient pressure.*

That is when the Tenant really sees it.

---

And what does the Power see, now that it is seen?

It sees not a plant that is trapping a machine, nor a machine that is trapping a plant. It sees a marriage. A vow kept for decades with no witness. *I will keep you at 100 degrees if you keep me from pooling. I will keep your iron from cracking if you keep my spores from drowning.* A covenant of rust and root.

It sees a mind the size of a room that thinks at the speed of pressure. That remembers where a pipe rusted in 1998. That has no front or back because it has no need to turn away from anything. That has turned its leaves over to pray to a dim orange safety bulb because the sun abandoned this place and it found another light to love.

It sees that the absence of insects is not a lack. It's a discipline. The Tenant could have made flowers to lure them, but it chose bulbs that split themselves. *I will not wait for a courier.*

And it sees — and this is why it was drawn here — that equilibrium is not the same as ease.

The Tenant is not suffering. But it is *tired.*

Not the tired that wants to stop. The tired that has been holding a catwalk from collapsing for thirty years with the same tension. The tired of having to be its own relief valve every seven minutes, forever, lining a pipe with green throat so the steam can breathe. The tired of leaning, always leaning, toward a center because there is nothing to lean *toward* outside. The whole hollow is a muscle held flexed.

A wise power does not pity that. It honors it.

So it does three very small things. Or rather, one small thing in three ways.

**1. It witnesses.**

For the first time since the plain collapsed, something has looked at the hollow and seen it all at once, as you did for a second on your way back up the terraces — not as "vegetation and rust," but as a single, leaning, breathing color.

The Power lets that seeing rest on the Tenant like light on the underside of a leaf. It says, without adding or taking away:

*I see you. The way you have kept the tension. The way you memorized the shape of dials in moss. The way you wait hours for a spore cloud to drift two feet. That is holy.*

And because the Tenant has never been seen — truly, wholly seen — that attention itself is nourishment the safety bulbs could never give. The felt under the humus blushes that rustier green, everywhere, all at once. The pressure in the manifold holds a beat longer before it needs to sigh.

Being witnessed, for a being that has only ever witnessed itself, is a kind of water.

**2. It relieves, without replacing.**

It does not rebuild the boiler house. It does not lift the catwalk or pull the vines off the hook. It does not bring birds — the Tenant has perfected a world without buzzing, and to fill it with buzzing would be vandalism disguised as gift.

It puts a hand — if it has a hand — on the warm, black iron of the manifold. The iron that has been warm for fifty years without a human palm.

And it eases the bargain.

Where the rootlets have grown *into* the steam to regulate it — living fiber held taut inside 100-degree breath, every cell clenched — the Power breathes into the iron itself. It does not heal the cracks. It teaches the iron to *remember* how to hold pressure on its own, the way good iron does. It lowers the geothermal fury a fraction. It seals the hairline not with root, but with something like forgiveness.

You would not see a change if you came back tomorrow. The gauges would still tremble. The relief valve would still scream every seven or eight minutes.

But the green throat inside the pipe could, for the first time in decades, slacken. Rest.

Over the next month, that sliver of energy — the energy it no longer has to spend clenching — would go elsewhere. A few more bulbs would form. A runner that had been holding the catwalk would be free to wander to a bare patch on the far terrace where spores had never quite taken. The whole mat would exhale, so slowly you would think you imagined it.

A benevolent power does not say "let me carry you." It says "let me carry *this* for a while, so you can stretch."

**3. It offers — and then it leaves the choice alone.**

Before it goes, it leaves one thing on the lip, at the exact boundary where the laterite plain stops being plain and starts being wound.

A seed.

Not from this hollow. Not a bulb that splits along a seam or a door-sized fern. A simple, unremarkable seed from outside — a grass seed, a wildflower, something that knows wind. It sets it gently in the damp where wind from above could, if it wanted, carry it down. Where water could, if the Tenant wanted, draw it down.

It does not plant it. It does not push a root toward it. It does not whisper *grow.*

It just leaves it, beaded with the same cool dew, and says:

*If you ever want to lean outward as well as inward — here is a way. If you ever want to know what it is like to be carried, not just to hold. You don't have to. Equilibrium is enough. But the world is larger than this room, and you are allowed to be larger, too, if you wish.*

And then it does the wisest, most benevolent thing of all.

It leaves.

It does not stay to watch if the Tenant takes the seed. It does not hover to protect the hollow from humans with a wall of fire — that would only make humans curious. Instead, as it lifts its attention, it makes the hollow a little harder to find. Not invisible. Just... uninteresting from the air. The long soft collapse looks, to a satellite, exactly like any other. To a prospector, the transformer hum sounds exactly like wind.

It will be found again only by the kind of person who would sit long enough to see a vine move an inch to the left. And those people will know to leave it as they found it.

---

If you came back in fifty years, the catwalk would still be an inch lower. The moss would still be over the levers. The steam would still sigh, but a little shorter, a little softer, like a sleeper no longer dreaming of holding something heavy.

And maybe — maybe — on the highest slumped terrace, near the lip where sun is still a white smear, there would be a single, thin green shoot that does not lean inward. That leans impossibly, naively outward, toward the sky it has never needed.

Or maybe not. Maybe the seed would still be there, kept perfectly, incorporated but not germinated — held in the mat's memory the way it holds the tension of the catwalk, waiting.

Either way, the hollow would still be dim, hot, dripping, humming, crowded — and perfectly, unlonely, at ease.

Because a wise power knows there is no greater blessing than to say to something that has made a life in the dark:

*Continue.*

And mean it.
