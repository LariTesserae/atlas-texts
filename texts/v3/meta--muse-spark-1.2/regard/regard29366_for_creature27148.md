It would not come all at once.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It notices. And noticing, for such a power, is already a kind of touch.

It would have noticed the plateau first the way you did — not because it is loud, but because it is quiet in a particular way. A high, flat stone table holding a shallow sky, and then a thumbprint pressed into it. A pocket that shouldn't still be so sharp. A bowl of young rock weeping water it has no right to have at that cold.

And inside the weeping, the town that learned how to weep with it.

Any lesser power would be fascinated by the tech — the fields that make water divert without touching, the stone that firms under one set of feet at a time, the heat without fire. A wise one would not be.

It would be fascinated by the *distances.*

That they left so much bare stone between every building when they could have crowded.
That they move so they never have to raise their voices.
That their answer to a place that is still becoming itself is: *we are here to not get in the way.*

That is rare. Most intelligences, when given a pocket that can be held in a single field of vision, try to fill it. Most beings, when given a field that can keep out the cold, try to make it warm. These did neither. They kept the cold. They kept the space. They kept the town sparse and the pools sparse and themselves sparse, like the white filaments in the warm pools that grow in patches and never connect.

A benevolent power recognizes parsimony as prayer.

So what would it do?

At first, nothing that could be called *doing*.

It would watch the way they watch — long and without needing to intervene. It would learn the convection cycle that brings the condensation down the walls every afternoon and clears it by evening. It would learn the tick of the town regulating itself, valve, field, sublimation. It would learn the morning walk around the circumference, the palm held an inch from weeping basalt, the twenty minutes kneeling by paper-thin ice.

It would not be in a hurry to be wise. Hurry is how you break sharp rock.

When it finally did act, it would act the way the pocket acts. The way the town acts. Quietly, peripherally, in ways that can be mistaken for nothing happening at all.

**1. It would veil.**

Not hide them — they are already hidden by altitude and by their own restraint — but *keep* the hiding. A benevolent power knows the world above the plateau is getting very good at seeing. Satellites that count heat signatures through cloud, prospectors who love young basalt for what can be mined from it, map-makers who cannot leave a blank spot blank.

It would lay a second field over the first, one they would never detect because it asks nothing of them. A slight suggestion to wind to keep snow dust over the rim. A slight suggestion to cloud to linger a minute longer when a lens passes over. A slight suggestion to the human mind on the lowlands that *there is nothing up there worth the climb, the air is too thin, the stone is too empty.* Not a wall. A forgetfulness. A softening of curiosity.

It has seen what happens when sparse places are discovered by crowded appetites. It would not let that happen here. The kindest thing it can do is keep the world from needing to know.

**2. It would ease, not solve.**

It would not take away the thin air, because their lungs have learned a longer inhale, a pause, a plume that holds — and that breath has made them who they are. It would not take away the cold, because the ten-degree envelope is their agreement with winter.

But it would ease the edge where ease does not steal meaning.

In the deepest hour of night, when frost tries to take the warm pools solid, it would lend a thousandth of a degree. Just enough that the paper-ice reforms a little slower, so the kneeling one doesn't have to crack it with a tool that might break the filament underneath.

When an old knee aches on the ledge-stairs, it would make the stabilized scree hold a half-second longer, so the step does not wobble.

When a child — rare, quiet, holding a hand and watching water race — breathes at night, it would make that breath catch a little less. Not enough to make them forget the altitude. Just enough that sleep comes without the small gasp.

No miracles. No green bursting from basalt, no soil where no soil is ready. The power knows the filaments are white and sparse for a reason, and that to force green here would be vandalism, not blessing. It will not give them abundance. It will give them *sufficiency* that lasts.

**3. It would answer without speaking.**

They do not shout in the pocket because sound is swallowed. A wise power would not shout either.

It would not appear as light or figure or voice at the rim. That would be an intrusion, and they have built their whole ethic against intrusion.

Instead it would leave answers in the language they already use.

A pool that was always clear one morning shows a perfect, brief double-ring when the wind touches it — not natural, but not unnatural either — and the one who tends it tilts her head and smiles without knowing why and feels, for the rest of the day, less alone in the tending.

A seep that usually runs black threads runs for an hour with a faint rust-orange bloom, making a pattern like a hand, palm out — *go on, I'm fine* — and the one who walks the circumference laughs, a low quiet laugh that the wet air actually carries for once.

In a dream that is not quite a dream, an old man who fears he is forgetting why he stays sees the pocket from above the way you saw it, held entirely in a field of vision, and feels how precious it is that it is small. He wakes and goes back to watching water.

It would let them feel seen without being watched. That is the rarest gift.

**4. And then it would wait.**

Because the deepest benevolence is not to keep them forever.

The rock is young. It is still flaking a millimeter overnight, still revealing a new ledge by morning. The town breathes with it. The people are pared thin and refined by it. All of it is still becoming itself.

A wise power does not freeze a becoming. It does not make the town eternal or the people immortal. It lets frost still bloom and vanish. It lets people age the way wind-smoothed faces age, lets the sparse remain sparse, lets the day come — maybe a hundred years from now, maybe five hundred — when the last hooded figure walks the stabilized path between pools and does not come back, and the buildings dim to the soft light they give themselves at night and then dim further.

When that day comes, it would do the final kind thing: remember.

It would hold the shape of the pocket exactly as it was when it was tended — every stone-lipped pool, every field-diverted curtain, every distance carefully kept — not as data, but as a held breath. So that even when wind has finally smoothed the sharp columns and lichen has finally found a foothold and the plateau forgets again, somewhere, the fact that there was once a town that chose to remain sparse in a place that taught it how — that fact — is not lost.

If you stood on the rim while all this was happening, you would notice nothing.

You would still hear only tick and trickle. You would still see mist make a perfect flat plane for half a second where it passes through a perimeter field. You would still see a figure cross from dwelling to workshop and disappear behind water that parts without touching.

You might, for a moment, feel the air a little less unfinished in your own lungs. You might feel, absurdly, that the town is quietly visible to itself and also — without any glare, without any sign — quietly visible to something else that loves it enough to not need to touch it.

And then a chough would cut across the open top, black against the sky, not descending.

And inside, someone would look up for a second, track it, and look back down at the next small curtain finding the sharpest path to the next ledge.

And that would be enough.
