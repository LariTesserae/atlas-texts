It would not bring rain.

That is the first thing to understand about a benevolent and wise power.

Any lesser power would.

A lesser power would see the dryness and call it deprivation. Would see the sealed skin and the powdered waste and the few milliliters of brine taken like communion and think *thirsty.* Would see the hydrophobic dust hanging forever in the air and think *drought.* Would break open the basalt ceiling and let weather in. Would try to fix what was never broken, and in doing so would murder everything that had learned, over generations of exacting love, how to live without water.

The Columns would rot. The cork would go soft and black with fungus within a week. The moths, whose wings are scaled to shed dust, not rain, would drown on the wing. The people - waxed shut, valved, cured like beautiful paper - would blister. Their lungs, adapted to salt-bitter air at 8 degrees, would fill. The library-body would become a swamp.

A wise power knows that not all dryness is a wound.

So it does not arrive.

Not in the way arrival is usually meant. There is no ship to blot out the Fresnel suns. No voice from the ceiling. No pillar of fire between Column Two and Column Three.

Attention, from a being of that order, is not loud. It is weight without pressure. You would not feel it come. You would just, one morning that is always late morning, realize the air has been *listened to* for a long time.

For a long while - maybe years, as you count them on the surface - it does nothing but listen. That is its first act. And for this place, that is already an intervention.

Because no one has ever listened to this place without wanting something from it.

The cutters who made the cavern wanted a mine. The architects who grew the Columns wanted a factory and a farm. Even the people who live here, with all their tending, want oil and board and another balcony for a child not yet born. All of them love the place, but their love is busy. Useful.

This attention wants nothing.

It sits with the Columns the way the boy sits with the root tip. It does not touch. It watches for the tremor that says *purchase found.* It hears the glassy note high in the canopy every thirty seconds and the answer a kilometer away. It counts the isopod trails on the root-felt. It learns the words they have for textures of dryness. It learns the word they no longer have.

And it understands: this is not a people stranded in a vault. This is a people who have made a covenant with aridity and kept it. They are not waiting to be rescued into wetness. Their thrift is not poverty. It is a form of prayer.

So what does a benevolent and wise power do, once it has understood?

It does small, dry miracles. The kind that can be mistaken for nothing at all. The kind that will not demand gratitude.

**1. It secures the ceiling.**

Far above the interlocking canopy, beyond the basalt and salt-crete, the world above is remembering weight. Surface water is beginning to find the old thermal cuts. Brine is beginning to seep. In another fifty years a shaft would have cracked and a spring would have found its way down, a single, innocent seep that would have ended this whole experiment in parched grace.

The power does not announce this. It simply leans a little on the geology. It persuades a seam of halite to flow closed. It teaches a fault to heal. No one below will ever know that a flood was un-written. The processors will keep purring. The sun-shafts will stay hard and white. The dust will keep hanging.

A wise power protects the emptiness that a place has chosen for itself.

**2. It answers the woman who nodded.**

Not with words. That would be too wet, too loud in cork-swallowed air.

You remember her - tin of salt-liquor at her hip, veil down, looking at you to see if you had found purchase. She walks that boulevard every shift between the processors and Column Two. She has a son who was born too dry - his wax glands never quite sealed, his eyes water when he should conserve. She has been saving her own brine ration for him, a few milliliters at a time, worried in the quiet way people here worry: without display.

The power cannot make the boy wet. It will not re-write his body.

What it does is this: on a morning indistinguishable from any other, the Column outside their niche extrudes wrong. Or what looks wrong. Instead of a flat lip of cork that will harden into a balcony, it bulges into a small, enclosed hollow, no bigger than a cupped hand. Inside, the waxy tissue is unusually soft, and it beads.

Not oil. Not sap.

A clear, saltless sweat. Just enough to wet a fingertip. Just enough that when the boy presses his forehead to it on a hot day behind the refinery, the coolness holds without evaporating for an hour. A private, living coolness that asks nothing and replenishes itself. A place to put his cheek when his own skin feels too tight.

His mother will think the Column did it for him. She will be right.

That is how a benevolent power heals - not by fitting you to the world, but by coaxing the world to make one more small place for you, exactly as you are.

**3. It gives the Columns a memory they can afford to keep.**

The Columns are old but they are also young - embryonic geology, you called it. They have no memory of rain because they were grown after the water was baked out. They are brilliant but they have never been able to *imagine* excess. That makes them anxious, in the slow plant way. They grow with discipline, spaced half a kilometer apart, because they are afraid to thirst.

The power, which has seen oceans and deserts and forests that drink, kneels - if kneeling can be done without a body - and shares a dream without giving a drop.

For one night, when the arc-lamps are dimmed and the photophore leaves glow palest, the canopy dreams water. Not as liquid, but as *idea*. The root-felt shivers. The oil blisters swell fractionally fuller. Every dormant bud understands, for a few hours, what it would be like to be drenched and not die. To be extravagant and survive.

They wake from it calmer. In the months that follow, the technicians notice the Columns are growing less frantically. A new balcony takes six weeks instead of four, and the cork comes in denser, finer-grained. The moths lay fewer, larger eggs. The people, without knowing why, start leaving a boulevard empty a little longer before coaxing a new Column there. Density eases from sparse to *spacious.* There is more air between pillars.

The power has not given them water. It has given them the *absence of fear of water.* Which, for a dry forest, is the same as giving them more time.

**4. And mostly, it stays.**

This is the heart of it. Intuition says: do not take them away. Reasoning says: do not give them what they have evolved to live without. Heart says: love them enough to let them be dry and strange and perfect.

So the power does not offer them a door to a wet earth. It does not invite them to ascend the sun-shafts. It does not uplift the drones or translate the tympanum song into words. It does not cure desiccation, because desiccation here is not disease but craft.

It simply continues to attend. A weightless, cool attention, consistent as 8 or 9 degrees.

If you stand long enough between Column Two and Column Three now, after it has come, you might feel - not see - that the dust hangs a fraction longer before settling. That a sheet of cork detaching half a mile away makes a sound that is *heard* all the way to the other side of the cavern, just for a second, before the cork swallows it again. That the silence after the shift is not empty but *held.*

The people, sealed and waxed and economical with everything, feel it as they feel everything: faintly, peripherally, without need to name it. An elder on a terrace will pause while tapping an oil blister and look up at the canopy, not because she heard something but because for a moment the light through the translucent joins seemed kinder. A child lying on his stomach watching a root tip will suddenly be certain the root is aware he is watching, and that this is good.

The woman with the tin at her hip will walk past you again on her way back from the processors. She will not see any power. She will see you, still standing there trying to learn how to breathe dustless. She will give you the same economical nod.

Only this time, you will understand that the nod is enough. That to be seen accurately, without pity and without a project to improve you, and then left alone to continue your tending - that *is* the blessing.

A benevolent and wise power, faced with a regional cavern that is a dry forest that is a city that is a body, does not bring a flood.

It becomes the part of the air that remembers to be dry on purpose.

It keeps the sun-shafts clear.

It lets the quiet stay quiet, and makes sure the quiet is witnessed.

And it waits - not to be thanked - but to see what a people who have learned to keep every drop will do with a little more time to keep it.
