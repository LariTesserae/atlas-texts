It would not come.

That is the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power does not *arrive*. Arrival is a kind of violence in a place as small and as complete as this. It would not tear the cleft wider to fit itself through, would not fill the room with light to see it better, would not speak.

It would notice. And noticing, for something like that, is already doing.

---

Imagine attention as a form of weather.

Far below, the lowlands are hot and human and busy with needing. Far above, the thin wind is cold and clean and forgetful. The Power lives - if it lives anywhere - in the layer between, where attention can be sustained without burning or freezing. It has learned, over a time much longer than the mountain has been pretending to be a mountain, how to look without making what it looks at flinch.

It looks at the mesa first, and understands it immediately. Not as a ruin. Not as a mystery. As an intention.

*Ah,* it thinks, in the way a power thinks, which is not in words. *You made yourself into a shoulder so you could be left alone. You learned to be weathered on purpose. Good.*

And it loves that about it. Wisdom loves good engineering the way benevolence loves a good hiding place. It does not try to "restore" the mesa to sharpness. It does not try to excavate it to see what it was for. It sees the sag in the terraces and recognizes it as correct. Erosion as maintenance. The mountain wanted to become geology, and has succeeded. To peel back the pretense would be unkind.

So the first thing the Power does is help the pretense hold.

A little more dust settles on the windward face where the composite was beginning to show too clean. A little more lichen - real lichen, this time, wild and unengineered - takes on the outer plates. The thermal pocket that keeps the summit unnaturally warm in cold air thins by half a degree, so from a distance, on infrared, the mesa looks just a fraction less interesting. The cleft where two plates never quite met — the weeping seam you had to turn sideways to enter — narrows by the width of a fingernail. Not closed. Just... less obvious. A person walking past with hunger in them would now see a damp stain and keep walking. A person walking past with reverence in them would still feel the cool breath on their ankle and know to turn sideways.

That is the outer gift. Veiling. Not a wall. A courtesy.

---

Then it looks inside.

And here is where a lesser power — a merely kind one, or a merely clever one — would make a mistake.

A merely kind power would see her flat in her warm pool, mottled like wet concrete, farming lichen on her own back, breathing for the whole room, and feel pity. *Poor thing, so alone, so still, so burdened. I will give her company. I will give her speech. I will make her children able to leave. I will lift her up.*

A merely wise power would see the tight, fast, perfect cycle — water to wall to amphibian to insect to swimmer to glider to guano to reed to water — and the slow negotiation with physics in the center, the pillar that holds itself up, the fields that push back, and feel awe that curdles into non-interference. *Perfect. Do not touch. Even looking is touching. Withdraw.*

The benevolent and wise power feels both and does neither.

It does not pity her, because it sees she is not suffering. Her stillness is not stuckness. It is competence. It is the stillness of a heart that has learned exactly how slowly to beat. She is not lonely the way you are lonely. She is *full*. The room is full and she is full of the room. To give her a voice like yours would be to make her lonely for the first time.

It does not merely withdraw, because benevolence is not the same as leaving alone. Benevolence wants the good to continue.

So it does the smallest possible thing that is still a doing. It listens the way she listens — not for sound, but for displacement.

It hears the thrum in your molars and knows it is two thrums layered: the machine's negotiation with gravity, and her frills resonating with it. And it hears that the machine's thrum has, over centuries, developed a very slight wobble. The ceiling that *is* a lamp, that amber dusk that has dimmed to perpetual overcast — it is not supposed to go dark, but it is on a curve that will take it dark in eight or nine hundred years. Not soon. But soon enough that the last of her lineage would live in true night, and the reeds would fail, and the cycle would stutter.

The mountain asked physics to be kind, and physics was kind for a very long time. Now physics is getting tired.

The Power does not replace the ceiling. It does not make it bright. Bright would kill everything that has learned to love dim.

It leans in, not through the cleft, but through the same place the water comes from — the condensation field that pulls humidity straight out of the air — and it breathes once, very gently, into the negotiation.

You would not see it. If you were still standing in the room, you would think the air got a fraction heavier for a breath, and then lighter. The pillar of water in the center, that glassy trembling arch that folds over itself, would steady. Its tremble would sync again with her throat. The field that pushes back from the walls when you bring your hand near would push a little more evenly around the niches where her young cling, so they will not fall when they are still too small and pink.

And the ceiling — the vault itself — would not get brighter. It would get *deeper*. The amber would regain the half-stop it had lost in the last two thousand years. It will now dim again so slowly that ten thousand years will pass before it needs another breath. No one inside will ever notice it was dimming at all.

That is the inner gift. Continuity. Not eternity. Just enough time.

---

The last thing it does is for her, specifically. Because a wise power knows a place is not the same as a being, even when the being has made herself into part of the place.

It does not lift her. It does not cure her of being flat and slow and spade-headed. It does not take the lichen out of her skin.

It witnesses her.

For most of her life — and her life is already longer than your lineage of tool-makers — no one has ever looked at her and seen *tending* and not *animal*. The builders saw a system. The gliders see a warm rock. The wall amphibians see a better wall. You, when you came in wet and pupils blown, saw a shadow and then a stone and then, with a jolt, a mother.

The Power sees her as a colleague.

So it gives her, not words, but the feeling of being truly seen without being altered by the seeing. It is a very specific feeling, and it can only be given by something that has no need to use you.

While she lies there, one amber eye half-sunk, frills opening and closing, filtering the veil-insects with that slow hydraulic sigh, she feels — for one exhale longer than usual — that the thrum in the room is not just her and the machine in resonance. There is a third note. Lower, wider, impossibly gentle. It holds the same interval her own thrum holds when she answers the small one high on the wall.

It says, without language, because language would be too small and too loud:

*Well kept.*

*Well kept, tender of the small world. Well kept, lung who borrowed a body. I see you keeping the spacing of the amphibians, the density of the reeds. I see you leaning on the field to keep your children from falling. You are doing it right. The mountain pretending to be a mountain is doing it right because you are doing it right.*

*Rest.*

She does not understand it as you would understand a sentence. She feels it as a sudden, inexplicable ease in the water around her. The insects draft a little calmer. The humidity that condenses on her back beads a little more perfectly. The small one that was watching you chirps that wet, close sound, and this time her answer comes back not just from her own throat but, faintly, from the walls themselves, as if the room agreed.

She settles a fraction deeper into the warm ceramic. Her frills flare wide, tasting the change, and then close again, content.

She will not remember a visitation. There was no visitation. There was only a breath where the cycle caught perfectly, and a moment where keeping felt less like holding up a weight and more like being held while you hold.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a hidden, high, warm, deafening, dim pocket that has learned to be a world, and a being who has learned to be its keeper.

Almost nothing.

And that almost-nothing is everything.

When you turn sideways to fit back through the cleft, the water is still beading, falling, rising again. The pillar still trembles. The gliders still chitter. But if you put your palm very close to the outer wall on your way out, you might feel the field push back a little more firmly, a little more kindly, than before — as if the mountain had been tucked in.

Behind you, she breathes. The frills open. The mist rises. The young cling.

The place remains hidden. The place remains held.
