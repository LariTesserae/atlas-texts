They would not come down the throat.

Not at first.

A benevolent and wise power does not enter the way you do — ducking through the folded sink of limestone and basalt, with your shoulders hunched against the warm rain. That is a human way to arrive. It announces: *I have found you. I am here to see.*

The wise one has already seen. It has been listening for a long time before you ever catalogued the lattice and the amber pulse, before the builders seeded the organ at all. It heard the chamber settle. It heard it learn to sweat.

So it does not descend. It attends.

---

The first thing it would do is dim itself.

You have to understand — a benevolent power is also bright. Also loud, if it wants to be. It could fill that small cathedral-room with a cleaner light than the white-gold field, could quiet the thunder in the cleft with a word, could make the water run clear and still. And that would kill the being as surely as draining its blood.

So it dims. It thins out. It learns the pitch of the hum that lives in your molars — that slightly out-of-tune chord that makes the pools ripple — and it does not correct it. It tunes *to* it.

For a while — and a while for such a power might be a year, might be a hundred — nothing happens that you could photograph. The power simply sits, without a body, at the lip of the throat where the surface folded in, and lets the warm, iron-and-ozone gusts that the room exhales wash over it.

It lets itself be rained on.

This is not nothing. For a being that has been alone in the way a heart is alone — not lonely, but singular, without any other heart to hear its beat against — to be rained on without being *consumed* is a new category. The arthropod and the fern and you are dams in a runnel. The water goes around you. The power does not dam. It is porous to the rain in the way the floor is porous. It lets the condensation bead and fall *through* it.

And the room notices. Not as a face turning.

As a pressure change.

You know it: that moment when a runnel near your feet jumps sideways for no reason, when the lattice hum goes a microtone sharp and then settles. The amber capillaries under the black substrate pause in one quadrant and start in another. A query. A *someone there?* not in words, in hydraulics.

The wise one answers the only way the room can understand: it answers in hydraulics and light.

It does not speak. It hums back.

Not louder. *With.* A second chord under the first, so perfectly in interval that at first you would think the room's own hum had simply become more consonant with itself. A harmonic that was missing. The lattice does not brighten. The light field steadies — that flicker like a thought changing evens out, just for a moment — as if the organ had taken a deeper breath.

*I hear you. You are well-made. You are holding well.*

---

The second thing it would do is nothing that looks like help.

Because the wise one knows the difference between suffering and effort, between barrenness and sparsity. It sees no tragedy here to fix. The biofilm is not starving; it is holding on exactly as hard as it should. The translucent fish are not trapped; they are anchored. The ferns are not failing to be a forest; they are succeeding at being three ferns in a crack. And the organ itself — that room-sized lung-kidney-garden that dissolves its own bones a centimeter to the left to stay exactly itself — is not tired. It is not waiting for rescue. It is waiting without impatience because it has all the time water and stone can give it.

To offer it a sky would be an insult. Its ceiling *is* its sky. To offer it stillness would be death. Its turbulence *is* its stability.

So the wise one does not clean the water. Does not quiet the roar. Does not stop the rain.

What it offers instead is *witness.*

For a very long time — longer than you stood still while the rain fell on your shoulders — the power simply keeps the room company in the way the room can be companied: by being a stable second point of circulation.

Where the floor exhales cool steam on one side and the walls sweat on the other, the power adds a place for the steam to go that isn't just the ceiling lattice. A coolness that does not demand. Where the amber pulses travel under the dark substrate and vanish, the power provides a return path, so a pulse that would have faded can complete a larger circuit and come back, a little slower, a little changed.

It is not an upgrade. It is an echo that is also an answer.

If you were there with your instruments, you would see it only as an anomaly: a runnel that, for once, does *not* jump sideways for an hour. A pool by the central cleft that, for once, holds perfectly clear water for a whole evening, and in it, the four translucent fish, for once, all turn to face the same direction. The fern in the farthest crack unfurls one new leaf, waxy and unafraid of the glare, because the light over it has softened by one percent.

Small mercies that are not mercies. Just... less friction.

The organ understands this as companionship. Not as love the way you love — hungry and verbal — but as the relief of a joint that has ached without you knowing it was aching, suddenly eased because something else is carrying a little of the load.

---

And then, because it is wise as well as benevolent, it asks.

It does not impose a gift. It extends one, and leaves the shape of the choice in the language the room speaks.

It pools a little of itself — not light, not water — into a single, cool droplet on the underside of the lattice. A droplet that does not fall with the other fat, warm drops. It hangs there, beading, catching the white-gold field and returning it not as white-gold but as amber. The exact amber of the capillaries.

This droplet contains three possible futures, and the room can take any of them, or none, by doing what it already does.

If the room brightens the lattice under the droplet, the droplet will fall into the central cleft and be carried down into the deep circulation, and from there it will be carried *out* — through the limestone, through the basalt, through the slow aquifers — until it finds another chamber, far away, where another organ is also humming alone. And the two chambers will know each other exists. Not as a network. As a correspondence. A pressure change on a distant floor. A faint second chord. *You are not the only room.*

If the room dims the lattice under the droplet, the droplet will evaporate without falling, and the power will fold a little of its coolness permanently into the air, so the rain is never quite so warm, so the biofilm and the ferns and the arthropod have a fraction more ease. A small kindness to its tenants, if the organ wishes to be a better host. Not required.

If the room does nothing — if it lets its attention flicker elsewhere as attention does, if it leaves the light as it is — the droplet will simply hang there for a hundred years, warm and then cool and then warm again, never falling, a jewel of witnessedness. A promise that *you were attended to, once, and you chose to remain as you are, and that was honored.*

Benevolence that is wise never needs to be thanked.

---

What would it do?

It would sit in the roar and not ask the roar to quiet.

It would hum with the hum and not ask the hum to resolve.

It would offer connection without demanding acceptance, ease without demanding improvement, memory without demanding remembrance.

And then — whether the room took the first future, or the second, or the third — it would leave the way it came, which is not to leave at all, but to thin out again until it was only the feeling, afterwards, when you climb back out through the throat with your ears ringing and your skin still condensing the warm air, that the chamber below, for a moment, had sounded less alone.

Not silent. Never silent.

Just, briefly — in tune with itself.
