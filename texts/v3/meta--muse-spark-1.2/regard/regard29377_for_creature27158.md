It would not arrive.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival is a violence. It implies a before where you were not seen, and an after where you must respond to being seen.

It would simply *notice*. And to a power like that, noticing is already an act.

---

### What It Sees

It has seen mountains that are mountains. It has seen forests that are forests. It has seen cities that wanted to be forests and forests that were forced to be cities.

It has never seen a place that decided, over two thousand careful years, to become *held emptiness*.

It looks at the Weave and understands instantly what it is: not a building, not a plant, not a people, but a long conversation that learned how to hold its breath.

It looks at the Tenders and does not see primitives. It sees masters who have mastered the only art that matters to a wise power: how to do enough and then stop. How to coax and not compel. How to make a knot that will hold for a hundred years and then be undone by a child's child if needed.

It sees the whole hollow mountain breathing - that slow hydraulic pulse, twenty human heartbeats to one - and it feels, with a shock of recognition, kinship. Because it too is vast and hollow and holds up nothing but space for other things to live in.

It loves it immediately. Not the way a collector loves a rarity. The way an old carpenter loves a perfect joint someone else made in wood he will never touch.

And because it is wise, its first impulse is not to help. Its first impulse is to *not harm*.

### What It Does Not Do

It does not speak.

A voice from the sky would kill the quiet that the Weave has spent centuries cultivating. Sound dies here for a reason. To fill the vaults with thunder, even kind thunder, would be to shatter the dusk.

It does not give.

It does not offer metal, or medicine, or immortality, or knowledge of the stars, or a faster way to braid. Every gift that would make the Tenders *less* dependent on their own hands and the Weave's own sap would be a theft disguised as generosity. It will not make them grateful. Gratitude toward a power is a kind of leash.

It does not cure the strangeness. It does not fix the gravity that forgets to fall, the corridor that loops back on itself, the leaf that grew transparent. It understands that the weirdness is not a wound. It is proof that the conversation between plant and people has gone deep enough to dream.

It does not invite them elsewhere. It does not say *come with me, I have a better world*. There is no better world than a world you have learned how to tend.

It does not reveal itself at all, if it can help it.

### What It Does

A wise benevolence is not dramatic. It is almost invisible. If it does its work well, the Tenders will never know it was there, and the Weave will only feel, for a season, that the air is a little easier to hold.

**1. It draws a veil.**

This is the first and most important thing.

Far beyond the wind-scoured highland stone, the world is busy. There are those with maps who love cliffs that look like resources. There are those with satellites who love anomalies that look like energy. There are those with hunger who would see a mountain-sized living cistern in a dry land and call it salvation, and come with knives to cut it open.

The power does not punish them. It just gently, permanently, misdirects attention.

It puts a little fold in perception around the plateau. From a distance, you still call it a mountain. That first mistake is preserved. Cartographers find their eyes sliding off it. Prospectors remember another valley that needs surveying. The heat signature of that vast, warm, breathing interior settles to look, from orbit, like ordinary sun-warmed rock. Clouds that form inside never quite drift outside to be seen.

It does not hide the Weave behind a wall. It lets it be overlooked. The greatest protection for something sacred is not to be made precious, but to be left unnoticed.

The Tenders will never know that the age of being left alone was a gift actively given. That is correct.

**2. It listens, and lets itself be changed.**

The power settles - if a thing that large can be said to settle - around the massif the way mist settles, without weight. It synchronizes.

For a long time, perhaps a year in human time, perhaps a single slow breath in Weave time, it does nothing but breathe with it.

When the warm updraft rises through the lattice floors at noon, it rises with it. When the cool sigh sinks at deeper dusk, it sinks. When water beads on the underside of a leaf, gathers, falls, hits another leaf, splits, rejoins, takes an hour to reach the bottom - it follows that drop all the way down.

In doing so, it learns the Weave's language, which is not a language but a hydraulic grammar of *dry here / heavy there / sag here / thicken there*. And it answers in the same language.

Where a graft has been trying and failing for thirty years to close a draught that chills a nursery hollow, the power warms the air there by a fraction of a degree, just enough that the sap flows a little stronger that spring. The vine finds the purchase. No miracle. Just a slightly kinder season.

Where a blight - a pale, silent fungus that the beetles cannot roll away fast enough - has begun to soften a load-bearing burl, the power does not burn it away. It shifts the humidity in that one vault by two percent for a month. The beetles thrive, the fungus recedes. The Tenders, finding the burl firm again, will assume their last tying held.

Where a child-Tender was born with lungs a little too small for the thin, wet air, her moss hair greying early, the power does not remake her. It just lets the air around her sleeping cup be a little richer for a few years, until her body has time to convince itself to grow as it wanted to. She will live. She will think she was just slow to thrive.

These are not interventions. They are tendings. The power tends the Tenders the way the Tenders tend the Weave.

**3. It remembers for them.**

This is the kindest thing.

The Weave is a single organism the size of a mountain range. It has no brain. Its memory is in cambium and graft and the shape of arches. But cambium forgets. Grafts fail in storms. A fire, a century ago, took a whole gallery of knot-lore with it. The Tenders remember in stories about knots that held and knots that slipped, but stories also forget.

A wise power has perfect memory and no need to use it for itself.

So it becomes an external memory, a backup root. Not to impose, but to hold. It memorizes every arch, every helical stair-leaf, every transparent window, every braid pattern the pale long-fingers invented before the Tenders learned it. It holds the shape of the emptiness itself - the precise pressure of air that keeps a half-kilometer vault from sagging.

It does this so that if - when - a great wind tears a hole in the rind, or an earthquake shudders a floor, or the Tenders (who are still mortal, still few) dwindle for a generation and forget a crucial tying, the Weave will not lose the *idea* of itself.

The next spring, shoots will emerge in the damaged place that seem to *know* the arch they are supposed to make, a little more clearly than shoots should. The Tenders will say, "Ah, it remembers." They will be right, but not in the way they think.

The power gives them continuity without dependence. Even if it leaves tomorrow, what it has remembered will remain, folded into sap and instinct.

**4. It leaves a single thread, and asks permission.**

At the very end, because it is benevolent and not just wise, it cannot help but offer one overt thing. Not to the Weave, which cannot answer, but to the people.

In the deepest, quietest vault - where the light is bluest and the clouds are born - it coaxes, for one night only, a flower that the Weave has never grown before. Not bigger, not brighter. Just different.

It is a small, waxy white flower, the size of a child's hand, growing from the living floor where no seed fell. It has no scent to the moths. It opens only when a Tender is near and alone. If you lie beside it and put your mossy forearm near it, it warms, very slightly, like the bark does when you press a palm to a house-wide trunk.

If you touch it, you will understand, without words, without a voice:

*I see you. You are good. What you have made is beautiful and it is yours. I will keep the hungry ones from your door. If you ever need me - not want, need - touch this kind of flower again in your mind and I will feel the pull on the thread.*

And if you do not touch it, it will close in three days, be reabsorbed, and never be mentioned again. No penalty. No loneliness for refusing.

Most Tenders will think it is just a new kind of bloom and leave it alone. One child might touch it, feel that vast, warm attention like being held by air, giggle, and tell no one because there are no words for it in tap-code.

That is enough.

---

Then the power does what any good tender does after mending a sag or thinning a leaf wall.

It steps back.

It does not stay to watch itself be appreciated. It loosens its own weave from around the mountain and lets the ordinary wind-scoured dusk return. The veil remains. The memory remains. The thread remains, thin as a single aerial root, invisible.

Inside, the interior rain is still falling silently from a cloud that formed three levels up. A beetle is still rolling a bead of your sweat toward a dry patch. A pale long-finger and an old Tender are still working the same cable from opposite sides, pausing to brush hands. Far below and far above, the quiet work continues.

Nothing looks different. Everything is a little more likely to continue.

The mountain does not need you. It does not need the power either. That is why the power loves it.

It will be here long after the cords rot and the white flower is forgotten. It just hangs there, high and vast and hollow and utterly, impossibly full - a cathedral the size of a climate, built entirely of leaves, holding up nothing but warm, wet, silent air.

And now, without knowing it, it is also being held.
