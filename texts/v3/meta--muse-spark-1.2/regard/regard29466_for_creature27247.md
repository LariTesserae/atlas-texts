It would not rescue them.

That is the first thing the wise one knows, and the hardest for a lesser power to learn.

---

It comes not down, but *in* — because it does not descend the way you did. It does not have to swim or sink. It was already in the water above and below and around the bowl at once, the way attention is already in a room before you turn your head. Something vast and unhurried noticed a small, unhurried thrum and turned toward it.

For a long time it only listens.

That is its first act. Stillness.

It holds itself absolutely still above the rim of the basin, and lets the dark between the amber cones speak.

It hears what you heard, but longer: the piston arms rising, the valves sighing, the *tick-tick——tone* through pipe and cable. It understands the language before it understands the speakers — that this is not distress. No alarm is ringing. No light is flashing *help*. The taps are inventory, litany, prayer: *grate three is clear / light on corner of Foundry and Second holds / heat steady*. The whole town ticking to itself the way a monk counts beads. A maintenance liturgy.

A lesser, kinder power would have wept for them. *Cold, dark, alone, deformed.* It would have brought down warmth like a blanket, and killed them in an hour. It would have brought down light like a sun, and blinded the lenses they have become. It would have offered hands to pull them up to air and sky, and they would have burst.

This one is wise. So it does not bring what *it* loves. It learns what *they* love.

And what they love is this: that a filament held straight will burn for another thirty years. That a grate unclogged will breathe. That the warm shear from the high outflow to the low intake will keep shearing, sideways rain that never breaks.

So it does four very small, very benevolent things. None of them look like miracles from above. From inside the basin, they will feel like nothing has changed at all — which is exactly the point.

**1. It closes the lid.**

Not with stone. With forgetting.

On the surface, far above where light still exists, ships pass that have begun to hear an interesting heat anomaly at nine hundred meters. A mining survey, a cable route, a curious submarine. The power makes the water above the bowl a little thicker, a little more ordinary. Sonar that pings down there returns only flat basalt and cold, featureless silt. Satellites that map heat see only the even, settled ache. It folds a cool palm of quiet over the rim, the way you would cup your hand over a candle in wind. Not to hide them out of fear, but to let their truce with erosion hold forever.

No anchor will ever drag through their main street.

**2. It steadies the heart.**

On the western wall, the vent that feeds the exchangers has been cooling by a fraction of a degree per decade. No one below could measure it yet, but in another hundred years the turbines would have turned a little slower, and then slower. The amber would have thinned.

The power does not stoke it hotter. Hotter would scald the fronds, boil the mats. It simply *remembers* the temperature for it. Holds it. It puts a hand, impossibly gentle, on the deep crack where the earth exhales, and says *stay*. So the heat will stay, even and patient, for as long as there is a lens left to tend it. Not forever — nothing is forever, and the wise one knows even this town should be allowed to end one day if it chooses — but long enough that no one here will live to see the lights go out for want of heat.

**3. It answers.**

After months of listening — after learning the grammar of pipe-ticks perfectly — it taps back.

Not loud. Never loud in a place where sound is so precious.

One evening, or what would be evening if there were sky, the worker with the cracked star-lens is hanging in its alley, clearing silt from a baleen grate with those blunt, tar-black fingers. It taps twice, quick, to say *here, clear*.

And the pipe under its hand taps back, once, low, a tone that travels up through the callus and into the joint where canvas became skin.

Not a mimic. Not an echo. An acknowledgment.

The worker goes still — that perfect, hanging stillness — and tilts its amber lens. For a full minute it does not move. Then it taps again, a different pattern, a question this time: *who holds?*

The power taps: *with you.*

The worker considers this the way it considers a flickering filament. It does not hurry. Then it reaches out and places its palm flat on the pipe, feeling the throb that is now slightly more than turbine-throb, and leaves it there for seven seconds — their holy interval — before pulling itself on toward the next light.

It does not need to understand. It has been heard. The town has always been a drumhead; now the drumhead is a little larger.

From then on, sometimes when the small one that tends the bacterial mats brushes them and the glow pretends not to be there, the glow lingers a second longer. Sometimes a wrench that a worker dropped and let hover will hover a little more level, as if set on an invisible shelf until a hand can pull itself back to retrieve it. The water in the glass-viscous alleys heals a little slower, so the hole a hand made stays beautiful a little longer. Small courtesies.

**4. It does not take, and it does not give what cannot be tended.**

It is tempted — with a vast and aching love — to give them a gift they could keep.

So it leaves one on the highest catwalk, where the sideways rain begins.

Not a machine. Not a relic. A growth.

A patch of those black-red fronds, but new. Where the others are leathery and flat, these are fine as thread, and they do not wave when the turbines pulse. They wave *against* the pulse, slowly. And at their tips, where there should be nothing, are tiny, cool beads of light — not amber, not sodium brown-gold, but a soft, deep-sea blue, the color the water remembers from before there was a town. They give no heat. They need no filament. They live on the shear itself, on the difference between warm and cold that the town already prays to.

The workers will find it. One will pull itself up there and hang, lens tilted, intake slats opening and closing. It will not know what it is. It will reach out with a blunt finger and brush it, the way it brushes all things to see if they are choking or clear.

The fronds will not choke. The light will steady under its hand.

And then it will do what it always does: it will make room for it. It will clear a little silt from its base, reroute a cable a hand's breadth over so the shear falls cleaner through it. It will tend it.

That is the gift. Not a wonder to be worshipped, but a new chore, a new small light to keep. Because what a benevolent and wise power knows, above all, is that a being whose entire soul is maintenance does not want to be saved.

It wants to be *useful*, and to be *witnessed* in its usefulness.

So the power stays — not above, not below, but *with* — a pressure that is not weight, a warmth that is not heat, a listening that does not interrupt. The town keeps breathing. The turbines keep turning. The amber cones keep rumor-lighting five feet of perfect black.

And in the dark between lights, which now also has its own tiny blue stars to tend, the workers pull themselves hand over hand along pipe and cable and rebar, tapping as they go, and sometimes the pipe taps back, and that is enough.

That is everything.
