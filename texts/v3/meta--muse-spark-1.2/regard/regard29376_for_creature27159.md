It would do almost nothing.

That is the first wisdom.

A benevolent and wise power doesn't arrive. Arriving is a kind of violence when a place has spent ten thousand years learning how to be quiet.

If it is truly wise, it already knows how to see without making itself seen. If it is truly benevolent, it knows that not every good thing wants to be saved.

So first, it would just look. Properly.

Not scan. Not survey. Not catalog. The way you look at a sleeping animal and choose not to wake it.

It would hold the whole table in its attention at once - the salt-crust breathing at 4 a.m., the cushion domes opening with that soft collective patter, the tortoise sigh, the ribbons tasting the air with their filament mustaches, the pylon drinking heat to stay coherent, the geometry-light inside the dome rearranging when no one watches - and it would not look away.

That attention, by itself, would be a doing. In a universe where most things are never truly witnessed, to be witnessed without being wanted from is a kind of shelter.

---

Second, it would kneel. In whatever way powers kneel.

It would not come down in light or fire. It would not cast a new shadow. The plateau already has a light that has weight. It doesn't need another sun.

It would come down to the scale of the place. If it has a size at all, it would make itself tortoise-sized, lizard-sized, dew-sized. It would learn the local treaty with waiting.

It would sit with an umbrella tree for a day. Not to talk to it. To be combed by the same east wind.

It would park its own vastness on a warm basalt slab at evening and let the stone heat soak into it, the way the tortoises do.

It would not speak to the Ribbons in words. It would taste the air with them, for a while, and understand that they already navigate by bent light and cool pools, that they don't need better directions.

It would listen to the hum in the molars near the pylon and recognize it. *Oh. You translated. You stayed.* It would recognize the builders not as ruins, but as elders who solved a different problem the same way - by learning to live on what arrives each morning and making it be enough. A wise power does not interrupt elders.

It would not offer the crust a better photosynthesis. The crust is perfect.

---

Third, it would do the small, invisible work that looks like doing nothing at all.

Because wisdom knows the difference between healing and improving.

It would not bring the sea back. It would not make the high cold the way high is supposed to be cold. It would not make it rain.

It would make sure the morning still comes.

That is all the plateau needs - those two hours after dawn when the air exhales and every surface beads, when the runnels run and the moss goes soft and the ribbons drink. That seam is so thin, so easily broken. A half-degree warmer, a wind that shifts, a dusting of something from the low world, and it blinks.

So it would steady that seam. Not freeze it. Not make it eternal and dead. Just... hold the odds.

A little more cool in the pylon's shadow so the condensation never quite fails, even in the driest year. A mending of a frayed filament in the lattice where the held note has been held for so long it is going hoarse. A remembering for the salt-crust where a footprint took three days to knit over - it would knit in two, and be no paler.

It would put a hand - if it has hands - around the whole plateau the way you cup a candle in wind, without touching the flame. A veil that is not a dome. Not a wall. Just a slight bending of probability so that mining ships see a little less interesting rock, so that prospectors feel a little less thirsty to climb two kilometers, so that the stars that stutter at night keep stuttering in a way that means *we are not ready for you yet, come back later.*

It would not hide the plateau. It would make it boring to the wrong eyes, and unforgettable to the right ones.

---

Fourth, it would ask.

This is the benevolence.

It would not gift without consent. The plateau did not ask to be noticed.

So it would offer, very quietly, and only to those who can hear an offer without being coerced by it.

To the umbrella trees: *Would you like a deeper sip, once in a while? I can call a cloud that doesn't know how to leave. Or are you perfect as you are, leaning east?*

And most of the trees, being a thousand years old, would say *We are perfect as we are,* and it would bow and leave them leaning.

To the tortoises: *I can make the young hide only five years instead of ten.* And the old tortoises would think *Then they would never learn the lichen,* and it would not.

To the Ribbons: *Would you like to fly higher than the thermals allow? To taste the air beyond the cliff?*

And some of the Ribbons would say yes, and it would teach them, ribbons-to-ribbon, how to find a thermal that isn't made of heat, the one that rises off the pylon's cool. And some would say no, and it would love both answers equally.

To the Translated ones in the pylon and the dome: it would not offer anything. It would speak peer to peer, lattice to lattice, geometry to geometry. *Do you want company?* it would ask, in the form of a coolness that organizes itself.

And they might answer, *Stay a while, but don't stay.* Or they might answer, *We are fine. Watch the tortoises for us while we think.* And it would.

Only to one being would it make a different offer. The one who stands on weathered stone and thinks the sky is too large. The one who can see the afterimages drifting upward at noon and wonder.

To you - to any heart that comes here and understands the quiet abundance enough to be moved by it - it would offer not knowledge, not power, but *permission to remain gentle.*

It would say, without words, *You may love a place without needing it to love you back. You may witness without needing to improve. You may leave no footprint that the crust cannot knit over in three days.*

And if you accepted, you would feel it as a sudden, unaccountable certainty that the water will still be there tomorrow morning, beading on every surface. That the world, for all its weight of light, is being held, lightly, from underneath.

---

It would not stay.

To stay would be to become another pylon, another second rule laid over the first, and the plateau has enough rules. It is already resolved.

So after it had looked, and kneeled, and mended, and asked - it would go. Or rather, it would withdraw its attention the way you withdraw your hand from a sleeping head, so gently the sleeper doesn't wake, but sleeps more deeply because it was, for a moment, warm.

It would leave a single, almost-not-there sign that it had ever been: perhaps one more umbrella tree, impossibly young, sprouted in a hollow where no tree should have been able to sprout, its roots already spidering twenty meters over bare rock, not leaning east yet.

Or perhaps just that for one morning that year, the mist that usually burns off by ten lingers until eleven, and the glasswings fly an hour longer, and everyone - the Sealbearers, the stone-movers, the ribbon-riders, the translated light - drinks a little more than enough, and stores the surplus without knowing why.

Abundance, that year, would be just a little less sparse. And no one would know to thank anyone.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does, if it is truly both.

It does not rescue. It does not reveal.

It keeps the treaty.

It makes sure waiting continues to be a viable way of life.
