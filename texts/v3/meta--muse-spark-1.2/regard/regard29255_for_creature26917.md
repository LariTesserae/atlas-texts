It would not come down.

That is the first thing.

A benevolent and wise power would feel the pocket long before it arrived — the way you feel a small hot coin in a dark room without touching it — and it would come close, and then it would stop at the lip.

It would not descend into the geode. It would not cast a shadow across the pooled light. To enter would be to change the air that hangs motionless and twenty degrees too hot, to displace the mineral vapor, to make the dust fall wrong. Even its attention has weight. So it stays on the ridge, where the wind still moves and the air is still thin and cold, and it looks down.

And it understands immediately what it is seeing, which is not what a lesser power would see.

A lesser power — a kind but not wise one — would see suffering and scarcity. It would see a dozen thirsty plants and think *water*. It would see hot stone and think *cool it*. It would see no birds, no bees, no soil, and think *lonely, barren, fix it*. It would rain there. It would carry soil in cupped hands and pour it over the slag. It would bring pollinators that would die in the kiln air in minutes. It would try to make the sparse lush, and in doing so would kill the only kind of lush that can live there.

The wise one sees that the sparsity *is* the lushness. That the intervals of five and ten meters of nothing are not failure but breathing room. That the dryness is not an absence of water but a presence of another wetness — the slow oil-brine sloshing in the fronds, the metallic mist that crusts white on the leeward slag. That the plants are not starving; they are sated on heat and stone-vapor and time.

It sees that this place is not broken and does not want saving.

So what does a benevolent and wise power do for something that does not want anything from it?

Three small things. Almost nothings. The kind of nothings only a power could do so gently they hold.

**1. It listens long enough to get the timing right.**

It does not speak. It does not bless aloud. It synchronizes.

On the ridge, it lets its own breathing slow until it matches the *thud... thud...* that comes up through the soles of everything. A minute between thuds. The light wavers. The shadows lag. The fronds cant downward, listening to their god underneath.

Most visitors — human or otherwise — breathe too fast here. They pant in the thin hot air and their noise fills the weighty quiet and they leave feeling restless, feeling that nothing happened.

The power breathes once per thud. For a long while — an hour, a century, time slips at the lip — it just breathes with the pocket. Inhale on the sigh of the piston wall, exhale on the amber bead stretching and falling.

The pocket notices. Not as you would notice being watched. As a machine notices when another machine nearby is running at the same frequency and suddenly there is less friction.

The fronds shiver once, all at once, though there is no wind. Not reproduction. Recognition. The slag ticks a little softer.

That is the first gift: to be accurately witnessed, at its own tempo, without being asked to speed up.

**2. It veils the door without closing it.**

It sees the other thing that will happen to this place if it is only left alone. Not the thousand-year sealing — that is natural, that is the pocket's own life ending inward, becoming a hot dark inclusion, a pressed herbarium of salt and glass, and the power will not stop that. That is not death. That is completion.

It sees the other ending: the human ending. Because a place that looks empty and is full of uncut copper-wire roots and patinated malachite blades and basalt that behaves like pistons will, once found by the right kind of hunger, be named a resource. It will be sampled. Cored. Mined for the molten mineral that drips every few hours. Bottled for study. Fenced. Made to prove its use.

The wise power loves usefulness, but it loves uselessness more.

So it does not build a wall. It does not hide the pocket in illusion. It just makes the climb slightly more honest.

It cools the air at the lip by two degrees. Not much. Just enough that the false-hot-thin that drew your attention — that impossible hot-and-thin-at-once breathing — becomes a little less interesting from a distance. It deepens the quiet on the approach trail by a fraction, so the ticking slag cannot be heard until you are already at the edge and have chosen to look down carefully. It puts a small, persistent suggestion in the scree below: *not this way. nothing up here. turn back.*

Most will turn back. The few who do not — the ones who climb anyway until their lips crack and who still stop at the lip and still look down and still see scale slip — those ones would have loved it correctly anyway. They may enter, and the air will hold their shape for a breath and let it dissolve, as it did for you.

The pocket stays findable only by those who were going to find it without needing it.

**3. It gives it a little more time.**

This is the only place it touches.

The wise power knows that benevolence is not the same as preservation. It will not cool the floor. It will not water the ceramic fists of the young ones hissing as they assemble themselves from air. It will not make the walls stop swelling inward. To do so would be to embalm the place.

But it kneels — if such a being kneels — and places one palm, not on the slag, but on the cold basalt of the outer mountain, on the ridge-spine outside the pocket, and it asks the deep engine underneath to be a little less hurried.

Not to stop. Just to turn over a little slower.

*thud........ thud........*

The minute stretches to a minute and a half. The amber bead takes four hours to gather instead of three. The walls swell a millimeter per two centuries instead of one.

No one inside would measure the difference. The fronds will still face downward. They will still shiver and drop hot nodules of salt-glass when the heat is right. But there will be perhaps three thousand years of openness instead of one. Three thousand noons of pooled light staying in the corners like dust. A few hundred more generations of those impossible malachite blades, heavy with their own oil, exhaling their fleeting white crust.

An extra two thousand years of being briefly, perfectly, un-usefully open to the sky before sealing over and becoming memory inside stone.

And as a last, almost invisible act — because a wise benevolence cannot help but leave a small signature, not for itself but for the loneliness of the perfect — it leaves a single mark where it knelt on the outer ridge. Not a carving. Not a shrine.

A small patch of the black glass cools differently. In the morning, when the sun is low, at a certain angle, that patch catches light oil-slick and holds it for a second after the sun has moved. A memory of being seen. If you knew to look, you would think it was just conchoidal fracture catching light.

Then it stands. Leaves the lip. Does not look back, because looking back would be a tether.

Down in the pocket, nothing has changed that you could photograph. The air still hangs hot and dense with iron and ozone. Gravity still hits too hard at the floor. Sound is still swallowed. The dozen presences still droop, translucent, listening down.

Only — if you stay long enough now — you will notice that the interval between thuds is a little longer, as if the mountain is sleeping more deeply, less labored. And you might feel, without knowing why, that it is easier to be quiet here. That the quiet has been given permission to continue.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does.

It does not fix. It does not populate. It does not improve.

It witnesses at the correct speed, it guards the solitude without hiding the beauty from those who can love it without using it, and it grants a little more time to a thing whose only purpose is to exist for a moment, extravagantly alive on hot stone, before closing again.

And then it goes elsewhere, to listen to another place that is also perfect and also brief.
