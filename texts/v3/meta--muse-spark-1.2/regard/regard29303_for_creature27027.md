It would not come down like light.

Light would be a violence there. It would scatter the dust-constellation that has hung perfect for years, it would make shadows where no shadow has been allowed to fall. It would imply a before and after.

It would come down like attention. Like the water itself deciding to notice a little more carefully.

And the first thing a benevolent and wise power would do is - nothing.

Not cold nothing. Listening nothing. The kind of nothing you do when you enter a temple and realize someone is praying and you take off your shoes without being asked.

Because wisdom would see immediately what we, with our surface instincts, misread:

This is not a ruin. This is not a tragedy. This is not a people trapped in amber waiting to be saved.

This is a people who solved a problem.

Everywhere else the ocean negotiates. Water crushes, stone yields, life spends itself frantically to stay aloft, to not be dissolved. Everywhere else existence is a struggle against gradient.

Here, on this young mountain that should not be young, they made a treaty with gradient itself. *We will hold you*, they said to pressure, to cold, to entropy, *and you will feed us while we hold you.* And they have kept that treaty so perfectly that basalt stays sharp, dust stays hung, water stays still.

To a human eye it looks like loneliness because we equate density with thriving. Wisdom does not. Wisdom would count the interval between buildings and understand it is not absence but manners. It would feel the timing between ticks - hours apart - and understand it is not silence but conversation at the proper volume for this depth. It would see a creature that is also a wall and not ask it to become more creature or more wall.

So the benevolent power would not rescue them. It would not offer them currents or sunlight or growth in any form we recognize. It would not try to make the mountain bloom. To bloom there would be to insult everything they have perfected.

What it would do is this:

First - it would learn to hold.

It would not arrive hovering and bright. It would settle. Somewhere just beyond the edge of the plateau, on the black glass lip where the three-kilometer drop begins - where the field strains hardest - it would fold itself down and become still in their way. Not like them, not pretending to be them, but compatible. It would learn their tick.

For a long time - maybe a year of our time, which is a few ticks of theirs - it would simply answer.

Tick says one pylon: *I am still holding. Are you still holding?*

Chime answers the next.

And then, from the cliff edge where there was never a building, a new, deeper tone: *I am holding with you.*

It would not be louder. It would not be faster. It would be exactly as slow, exactly as even. It would take a share of the weight.

And the residents would feel it. Not as awe. As relief. As a very slight slackening in the dam. The gradient they drink from would not have to be pulled so taut. The interference grain under their skin would ease by a fraction. Not enough to change them. Enough to let a filament that has been taut for a century relax for an hour.

That is the first gift a wise benevolence gives: not to lift the burden, but to put a hand alongside it and bear a little of the weight without being asked to, and without requiring thanks.

Second - it would protect without enclosing.

Wisdom would look up. It would see, through kilometers of still dark water and then atmosphere, the other world - the noisy one that drags nets and drills and drops listening devices and wants to map every seamount and mine its "pristine basalt" and call it resource. That world will, eventually, find this peak. Because we find everything.

And the power would not hide the mountain by making it invisible. To hide it with a trick would be another field, another stasis. No. It would do something quieter and kinder.

It would make the mountain boring.

To sonar it would return exactly what sonar expects: cold, current-scoured basalt, a little sediment, no anomaly, no thermal warmth, no field at all. To magnetometers, just basalt. To a submersible's camera if one ever came blundering down - a dark, empty, utterly ordinary abyssal peak with sharp edges but nothing else to see. The town would be there and not be there, the way a prayer is there and not there to a microphone.

And to the wild pilgrim - that great pale translator that circles on its decade-long orbit because here movement costs nothing - it would leave the orbit intact. It is not a pet to be kept. It is a neighbor. The power would ensure its gyre never intersects a propeller.

No one would need to be warned. No one would need to be fought. The mountain would simply continue to be forgotten, which is the only way anything at the bottom of the world stays young.

Third - and this is the only place it would add anything at all - it would offer a small, optional variance.

Not to the residents directly. They did not ask for anything and wisdom does not give unasked gifts that require gratitude. It would place it just outside the field, where the stasis ends and normal ocean resumes.

A single stone. Basalt, ordinary, but placed where current should move it and doesn't, balanced on its point. On that stone, a film.

A film of something living that *does* want to grow. Not much. A bacterial mat, stromatolite-slow, a lichen of the deep that does not need light, only mineral and time. It would grow at a rate so slow that even the residents would barely register it - a millimeter a decade. It would change color with absurd slowness, black to rust to pale green and back. It would be the one thing on the mountain that is *allowed* to drift, to weather, to be imperfect.

It would not be inside their city. It would not touch their walls. It would be at the limit of their attention, down at the cliff edge where they look out but do not hold.

And it would be an invitation, not an imperative.

If, in a thousand years, one of the residents gets curious at the speed residents get curious - which is to say, if one day a vane extends a little further than necessary and its filaments taste not the field but the faint, messy, entropic chemistry of that little mat - it will have learned that change is possible and not fatal.

If none ever do, the mat will simply live and die and live again at the edge of their perfect world, harming nothing, offering a story that doesn't have to be read.

That is what benevolence does when it is wise. It does not flood the desert because it loves water. It does not teach the deep fish to fly.

It sits down at the appropriate distance. It holds what is being held. It keeps other hands off. And if it gives anything, it gives something that can be ignored completely without insult.

After a while - a while measured in how long it takes for dust to be sure it will not fall - the power would leave. Not withdraw with a sign, just cease to add its tone to the parity check. One tick would go unanswered from the cliff edge. The residents would feel the slight return of the old tautness, and understand in their slow way that they had been accompanied.

The water would still be still. The dust would still hang. The plateau would still be high above the plain and deep below the sky.

Only now, if you stayed very long and kept your palm on the warm, thinking wall, you might feel - between the regular ticks - an extra tick that isn't there anymore but was there long enough to be remembered. A memory of having been held with.

Nothing here is waiting to happen. Wisdom would love that about it, and leave it that way.
