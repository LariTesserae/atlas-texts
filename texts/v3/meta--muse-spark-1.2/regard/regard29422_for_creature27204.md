It would not come down like anything you expect a power to come down.

Not bright. Not loud. Not corrective.

A benevolent and wise power that could do anything would first notice the one thing this place has taught you to notice: that loudness is wrong here. And so it would not arrive.

It would *attend*.

You would feel it first as a change in the quality of attention on the rim. The heron is still on the settling pond. The rat is still on its cable tray. But the air at the lip of the bowl, where it is a few meters higher and drier, would become - for lack of a better word - *listening*. As if something very large had knelt to be the exact size of the sump so it did not have to stoop.

It would look a long time before it did anything. Because that is what wisdom does with something so established.

It would see what you saw after ten minutes: that there is not a place and a being in it. There is a single condition. The bowl is a body of water learning how to be slow, and the beings are water learning how to be bodies. The pipes sweat and they sweat. The pump lifts and they swell. The pond roils and they open. To "save" one would be to kill the other. To "improve" the bowl - to drain it, to clean it, to plant trees in it, to silence the flywheel - would be to murder them with kindness.

A lesser power, an eager one, would be tempted. It would see the rust and the sodden asbestos and the grey churning silt and think *I will make this lush. I will make this pure. I will lift these poor translucent newts into a clear mountain stream where they can be beautiful.*

A wise one has learned to ask the place what it thinks beauty is.

And this place thinks beauty is *plink*.

Is condensation growing too large and elongating and pulling itself up against gravity. Is a dry ghost on a pipe where a sucker mouth was, before the metal sweats again. Is furious, quiet work to keep itself exactly as wet and cool and crowded as it is.

So it would not clean them. It would not heal them, because they are not sick. It would not speak to them, because they have no need for words and words would be too loud for the clay that swallows sound. It would not even let them know it is a power. Powers are terrible to be seen by small things that have learned to survive by being misread as stain.

What would it do, then?

Three small things that are almost nothing, which is how you know they are benevolent.

**First, it would confirm the equilibrium.**

Every such place in the flat country has a file somewhere. A surveyor's map. A reclamation order. A plan to fill it in with rubble and cap it because an old sump with an endlessly humming generator and asbestos jacketing is a liability. A wise power knows the most dangerous predator for a small, perfect equilibrium is not drought but *attention of the wrong kind*.

So it would lay a hand, if it has hands, on the rim. And it would make the sump *slightly less interesting* from above. Not invisible - invisibility is a kind of violence too. Just... easy to overlook. The willows on the rim would grow an inch thicker. The access track would develop one more pothole that makes a truck decide to go elsewhere that day. The map in the county office would develop a small coffee stain precisely over the contour lines of the bowl. Anyone who meant to come with bulldozers would remember, on the morning they were to come, that there is somewhere more urgent to be. The heron would still find it. The rain still would. You did.

It would protect it by letting it continue to be forgotten the way a tide pool is forgotten until you kneel.

**Second, it would answer the water.**

The bowl is always weeping, always wringing itself out, always on the edge of drying just enough to crack. The seep in the north wall, the condensation on every pipe, the waist-high mist over the pond - that is its breath, and theirs. A wise power that could command aquifers would not flood it. Flood would drown the very seams they live in. It would not "fix" the leaking sluice. The leak *is* the habitat.

It would instead promise the seep. Deep down where glacial clay meets siltstone, it would lean its weight, gently, the way you press a palm to a welling cut to keep it welling. *You may continue,* it would say without saying, and the cold threads running down the dark grey rock would run a little surer in August, when otherwise they might have thinned. The silver-jacketed steam line would sweat one bead more per minute, forever. That is an infinite gift at this scale: one more drink, one more plink, in perpetuity.

It would never let the flywheel stop. Not by miracle. Just by ensuring that when the belt frays, a rat knocks a spare down from a shelf. When the transformer hums too hot, a cool draft finds it. Small, deniable maintenance, credited to luck.

**Third - and this is the heart part, the part reason cannot quite justify but intuition knows is the whole reason it came - it would keep company, once, in the proper manner.**

It would not descend as itself. It would become small and wet and intersticial.

Somewhere between two pipes where there is a gap the size of a toolbox, where a large one is braced and puckered against the iron drinking condensation, the power would fit itself. Not as a being among them, because to imitate them would be condescending. As a *condition* among them.

For an hour, or a year - time is thin here between pump strokes - it would simply be an extra bead of water on the underside of that same pipe. A droplet that grows too large, elongates, pulls itself up - and stays. Beaded, translucent, catching the flat overcast light exactly the way they do. Resonating faintly with the thump felt in the chest from the big pump. Not observing. *Respiring in time.*

The large one braced there would not see it, because it has no eyes worth naming. But it would feel, for the first time in its existence which has never known loneliness because it never knew it could be known, a slight, adjacent coolness that is not pipe, not clay, not other colony. A presence that is also swallowing sound, also swelling and thinning, also asking nothing.

You know how good it feels to be in a room where everyone is working quietly on the same task and no one needs you to speak? How deeply restful to be *correctly* unnoticed and yet completely included?

It would give them that. For once, the condition of being lived-in would itself feel lived-with.

And then it would leave. Or rather, it would evaporate, the way the mist at waist height does when the sun finally breaks through - not gone, but distributed. It would not take them away. It would not uplift them to some dry, bright, higher plane where wise powers live. That would be exile. It loves them precisely for how low and cool and crowded they are. For how perfectly they have solved the problem of this bowl.

If you climbed back out after it had been there - your knees noticing the lift, the air getting lighter and drier at the rim - you would look back and see nothing different. The pond still churning. The buckets still clanking empty. The moss still drinking what the metal sweats.

Until a droplet on a low steam line under a catwalk grows too large. Grape, then plum, trembling.

It should fall.

It elongates.

It pulls itself up.

*Plink.*

Only now, if you listen the way the bowl taught you to listen, that plink sounds very slightly less alone. As if, somewhere between pipes, another bead had answered it at the exact same pitch, and the sump - that small, cool, low, wet, furiously working world - knew, in the wordless way water knows, that it had been seen and found good and would be allowed to keep on being exactly what it is.

And that is what a benevolent and wise power does when it can do anything.

It lets it continue.
