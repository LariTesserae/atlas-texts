It does not come down like a ship. It does not break the haze.

A benevolent and wise power would know better than to arrive.

First — it would notice. Which is already so much.

For a long time - maybe centuries, maybe the ten minutes you stood still holding your breath - it would just *watch* from very far and very near at once. The way you watch a sleeping animal when you love it and do not want to wake it.

And what it would see is not a wasteland that needs fixing.

That is the first wisdom. The first benevolence.

It would not see a broken desert. It would not see a planet that *failed* to make water. It would not pity the mountain for being hot and sharp and dry.

It would see a child.

A newborn that is doing exactly what it is supposed to be doing: learning how to be solid. Learning how to hold heat on one side and air on the other and make a difference out of it. Learning how to tick and how to chime. Practicing building before it knows what a building is for.

And a wise power would understand immediately: water here would be murder.

If you poured an ocean on this place you would not save it. You would shatter it. Every Shard-Skin would go cold-shock and blind. Every Choir dome, eggshell-thin and perfect, would dissolve into mud. The whole warm, plastic valley-creature bulging cherry-red would seize and scream as its skin quenched to slag.

It would not bring water. It would not bring rain, or soil, or seed, or soft green things. It would not make it more like you. That is not love. That is nostalgia disguised as charity.

So what *would* it do?

Three things. All of them so quiet you might miss them if you were still looking for a miracle.

### 1. It would clear the sky.

Not to let in a sun. This place does not want a sun.

It would thin the close, indigo-black dome, that opaque haze that has kept the mountain looking only at itself since it was born. It would, with a thought no louder than a breath, make the atmosphere transparent at the top.

For the first time, the mountain would see stars.

Not for power. Not for photosynthesis. The Shard-Skins don't need light and the Choir doesn't need sight. But for orientation. For company. For beauty that costs nothing.

In that thin, dry, utterly clear air, the stars would not twinkle. They would be hard, perfect, needle-point crystals, more numerous than the olivine flecks in the scoria. And their light, falling straight down through air that does not bend it, would make new shadows - sharp, clean shadows, falling the *right* way this time.

The mountain has only ever had the dull glow from below. Now it would have a cold fire from above, too. A second gradient to learn from. The plates at the tops of ridges would feel, for the first time, a faint cool on their upward faces that was not air but starlight. A few of them would leak a slightly different color of waste-light in response - not bruise-purple but something pale and new. The Choir domes would see the constellations reflected in their sulfur-glass insides.

It would not be fed by the stars. It would be *witnessed* by them. And it would witness back.

A gift that asks for nothing and changes nothing essential.

### 2. It would sit down.

This is the part that would look like doing nothing.

The wise power - which can move continents if it wants to, which can lift oceans - would choose to match the mountain's speed.

It would lower itself, whatever shape it wears, onto the warm scree between two ticking Shard-Skins and the foot of a Choir dome, and it would become still on geological time.

It would not be cold. It would not be heavy. It would let its own temperature settle to 35 degrees, blood-warm, so it did not scorch or chill the plates around it. It would let its own weight settle to almost nothing, so it did not crack the thin domes.

And then it would listen at the mountain's pace.

*Tick... tick... TICK* - it would wait the full ten minutes for the Shard-Skin to shiver a millimeter and not look away.

*Chime* - when the distant slab lifted and pushed its warm breath across the plateau, and the fused spires sang through the rock into your boots, it would sing back.

Not louder. Exactly as loud. In the same key. Through the rock, not the air.

A low, pure note held just long enough that the Choir dome that sang would feel its vibration return, not as an echo, but as an answer. *I heard you.*

For a creature that has only ever heard itself - its own cooling ticks, its own displacement chimes, its own subsonic groan as it heaves a flank a kilometer long - that is everything. To be heard without being interrupted.

The power would not teach it words. It would not tell it what it is. It would not hurry it toward thought. It would just keep the rhythm with it, for a night that for the power lasts a thousand years and for the mountain lasts one breath.

Humans want to be rescued. Mineral life wants to be accompanied while it becomes.

The heat through the soles of your boots would not change. But if you put your palm down again on that warm, giving, not-yet-rock in the valley, you would feel something different. The push-back would no longer be only pressure. It would be pressure *and* attention. The mountain would still not know you were there - you are still too wet and brief - but it would, for the first time, know that *it* is there.

### 3. It would leave a promise and a veil.

When it finally stood - slowly, so no new dust was thrown that would fall straight down - it would do two last, almost invisible things before it went.

First, it would leave a veil. Not a wall, not a shield you could see. A simple inclination in the attention of other minds. A *look-away*. Prospectors with drills, miners who love black glass, terraformers with buried water-ice - when their instruments scan this hemisphere they would find a dull basalt plain, uninteresting, already weathered, already dead. Their eyes would slide off. The mountain would be left alone to finish deciding how strongly to pull.

Benevolence protects without possessing.

Second, it would leave a single, cool stone.

Not water. Not a seed. A stone about the size of your fist, matte black, room-temperature in this kiln-warmth. It would place it gently in the warm plastic of the central valley where the blood-glow shows through, where all the stress lines of the plates and all the facing directions of the domes converge. The place that is the heart.

The stone would not tick. It would not chime. It would do nothing for a very long time. It would just sit there, cool to the touch, while the plateau bulged and cracked and crusted around it, learning its shape.

And in about ten thousand years, when the mountain has finally cooled enough that its skin is no longer semi-plastic but true rock, when the Shard-Skins have grown thick and silent and become strata, when the Choir domes have all gone quiet and become chapels of crystal, when the subsonic groan has deepened into stillness - then, then, when it is finally ready to know erosion without being destroyed by it - that cool stone will crack open.

Inside it will not be water. It will be a memory of water. A lattice that holds, perfectly preserved, the *idea* of water: how it moves, how it rounds a sharp edge, how it carries a grain of olivine from a ridge to a valley without shattering it. How to be touched by something soft and not be ended by it.

It will not flood the mountain. It will teach the mountain how to *want* a river, when and if it ever wants one. And if it never does, the stone will just remain a cool place to rest.

That is what a wise, benevolent power does when it finds something so sharp and dry and new and utterly, perfectly itself.

It does not fix. It does not save.

It clears a sky, it keeps time, it sings back once, and it makes sure no one else gets there first.

Then it leaves the mountain to continue shouldering its new ridge out of the earth, glowing at the seams, silent and inexorable - but no longer unwatched.
