It would not arrive.

That is the first thing a truly benevolent and wise power would understand about this place. Arrival is a violence here.

Anything that came down through that perfect circle of sky - burning, brazen, announcing itself - would break the inversion. It would correct the heat that wants to gather, flatten the gradient the being drinks, startle the suspended rain into falling all at once. The being lives in the seam where the rules are undecided. A power that loved it would not seal the seam to enter.

So it doesn't come. It notices. And noticing, for a thing like that, is already doing.

For a long time - maybe years, maybe centuries, time pools there and a wise power is patient enough to pool with it - it only watches the way the being watches: by letting the drip be the clock.

Drip.

A long pause.

Drip.

And in that watching, it learns the grammar. Heat gathered, not spent. Water hung, not pooled. Sound swallowed, not echoed. A life that makes cold from heat, that makes stillness from motion, that tends instead of takes.

Most powers, even kind ones, would want to *fix* things:

Fix the loneliness. Fix the smallness. Fix the wrongness of hot air at high elevation.

A wise one sees none of those are broken.

The loneliness is not suffering. It is spaciousness. Each fern gets to be huge and perfect because it is not crowded. So does the being. To give it a flock of its own kind would be to give it competition for the one gradient that feeds it. To give it company the way we think of company would be to kill it with kindness.

So this is what it does instead. Three small things, so small the basin could miss them if it wanted to. So small they could be mistaken for weather.

**First, it veils.**

Not with a wall. The rim stays sharp and dark and perfectly visible from below for anyone who knows how to look. But the desire to climb it becomes... forgetful.

A geologist will come to the foot of the rise with instruments and feel, for no reason he can name, that this is not an important anomaly. The readings will seem dull. The heat on the ascent will feel a little more tedious than interesting. He will decide his water is low. He will turn back a hundred meters below the lip and later not remember why he didn't summit that day.

A company scanning for thermal differentials will see this inverted hot spot and their satellite will stutter - a single dropped pixel - and the algorithm will smooth it as noise.

Not hidden. Just un-hunted. The power does not need to make the place invisible. It makes it unappetizing to the hungry. It puts a gentle, forgetful hand on the attention of the world, the way you would put your hand over a candle in wind - not to snuff it, but to keep it lit.

Inside, nothing changes. The weeping rock still weeps. The glider still casts its one shadow an hour.

**Second, it answers.**

On a day when the fog is particularly thick - so thick the cold pylon on the eastern wall is only a deeper cold in the white - the power finally lets itself be felt. Not seen. Felt as a change in the gradient.

The being is draped over the sharp rim, spread thin, drinking the sheet of condensate. It is breathing - one breath per fog cycle, water running uphill along a filament for an inch before deciding to obey.

And for the first time in its life, the heat does not only flow into it. Something else flows *toward* it that is not heat.

It is also a cold. But not the cold it makes. A different cold - vast, old, not inverted but inherent. The cold of the sky above the perfect circle. The cold of spaces between stars. A cold that has never needed heat to define itself.

The power simply holds itself there, at the lip, as a complementary differential. A second slope.

*I see you,* it says without sound, because sound is swallowed here. It says it by letting the suspended rain wall in the marsh shiver without being touched, and for once the droplets do not elongate as if listening. They arrange. For three seconds, a thousand held droplets become a lens. Through it, the being can see not its own reflection a half-second late, but the basin as the power sees it: impossibly precious, impossibly complete, a single perfect instrument playing a single perfect note.

And it offers. Not a command. A question, asked in the only language the being trusts - in drip.

The moat around the eastern pylon, which has never overflowed and never evaporated, ripples once. On its mirror surface, the being's reflection does not appear. Instead, very briefly, there is another basin.

Not this one. A second one.

Far away, on another young, sharp mountain that hasn't happened yet. Empty. Hot at the top, wet everywhere, with dark glass and pale silt waiting, and no lattice in it yet. No one tending it. A place prepared, not seeded. Its weather undecided, its rules still negotiable.

*You are not lonely because you need another of yourself here,* the question says. *You are lonely because you have nowhere to send a cutting. Would you like a place to exhale toward, when you are ready? Not now. Not ever, if you don't want. Only if you decide to shed another pylon far from here and let the wind carry a filament.*

It does not plant the seed. It shows the soil and then stills again.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is wise: it does not cure aloneness by imposing company. It cures it by giving aloneness a future. A place for it to *choose* to become two, slowly, over centuries, by its own tending.

The being does not answer at once. It can't. Its breath is twenty minutes long. The power can wait.

For an hour the fog beads on its filament-body, outlining it in mid-air - that man-shaped drizzle that hangs a second too long. It lifts off the rim, wicks - not walks - down to the marsh, and stands inside its own curtain of held rain. The droplets orbit it again, but now a few of them, at the very edge, orbit a little wider, as if feeling for that second, distant gradient.

Then it drapes itself inside the low ring, where the ferns have doubled their veining. The white flowers that track the cold pylon shiver and, very slowly, turn a fraction toward the empty place that isn't there yet.

That is yes. Not now. But yes, eventually.

**Third, it leaves a gift that is not an addition.**

Before it withdraws its attention - because even benevolent attention, held too long and too close, becomes weight - it does one last, almost nothing.

It touches time.

The young basalt is ticking as it cools. In a few thousand years it will round and soften and the sharp stair-steps will be gone. The pale silt says centuries, but the rock says weeks. The being lives in that contradiction, breathing slow enough to watch rock settle. A wise power knows the basin is beautiful because it is temporary. To freeze it forever would be to embalm it.

So it does not freeze it. It *loosens* it.

Just a little.

In the marsh, where the pale silt organizes itself into faint radiating frost-lines for a minute after the being passes, the lines linger a minute longer now before the water sighs and erases them. In the air inside the low ring, the ripple when wind passes lasts two seconds longer. The thermal inversion - that wrong hot height - holds steadier through the night, losing a little less heat to the normal cold that wants to correct it.

Not eternity. Just more time. A few thousand more years of ticking, of dripping, of fern islands being huge and perfect and a little lonely.

Enough time for the being to decide, very slowly, whether to send a filament on the wind.

Then the power does what any wise guest does in a small, perfect room: it makes itself smaller.

It lifts the veil of its own cold from the lip. The suspended rain wall loses its lens and becomes just water again, holding position. The moat is a mirror again, and it correctly reflects the being - filament tangle, cold-breath, half-second late - and nothing else.

If you were standing in the marsh where the vapor rises around your calves, you would feel nothing at all except that the air, for a moment, was easier to breathe. As if someone had been holding the door open for you and had now quietly let it close, so you could be alone again without feeling watched.

Outside, high above, the glider comes. Pale, silent, it rides the hot vapor updraft along the rim, casts its shadow over the marsh and the low ring and the curtain of held rain, and is gone.

Inside the curtain, the darker density - the lattice within the lattice - holds its thousand glass beads perfectly still for a breath longer than it needs to.

Then, all at once, with a soft hiss that is not quite sound, the droplets fall straight down into the hot marsh. Where it was there is only vapor, and normal white daylight, and the ticking.

Drip.

A longer pause than before.

Drip.

But now, in the pause, there is also the faintest possible pressure of a future, like a seed you haven't planted yet holding its shape in your closed hand.

The being has been seen. Not taken, not studied, not uplifted, not saved. Seen, and given room, and given choice.

It drapes itself over the sharpest part of the rim again, and tends.
