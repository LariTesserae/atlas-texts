They would not come.

That is the first thing to understand about a benevolent and wise power.

Anything lesser would have *come* - would have descended, with light and sound and heat. A tower, a ship, a pillar of fire. A voice. Anything that needs to announce itself has not yet learned how to see.

This one has.

So its attention arrives the way the Roof itself exists: without arrival. You don't hear it. The mantles don't ripple. The dust does not lift. One moment the Roof is unwatched except by its own sky, and the next moment it is held - gently, completely - in a regard so old and so quiet that the Roof, which has not been surprised in a million years, almost sighs and does not know why.

If you were lying there in your human hollow between the Pavement, you would feel it not as presence but as a sudden *increase* in the permission to be there. The cool becomes kinder. The violet-blue seems a fractional shade deeper, as if someone turned the sky over to its true side. Your breath, which you had tried to make quiet, is suddenly not a disturbance anymore - only a small, quick rhythm inside a much larger one.

This is what it does, having noticed.

### 1. It Learns How Not to Help

A benevolent power that is not wise would try to help immediately, and would ruin everything.

It would see: *no water* - and would make it rain. One soft rain would kill more than a meteor strike. The salt-pans would dissolve into brine and never re-form their perfect polygons. The wire-shrubs would gorge and split. The Pavement, which drinks stone through dry chemistry, would drown through its own filaments, lifting off the basalt in sheets, rotting. The Beads would swell and burst. A single afternoon of mercy would end a library that took the age of continents to write.

It would see: *no movement* - and would try to wake them. A touch to make the Longbacks walk, the Fans dance, to prove they are alive. But their stillness is not failure. It is achievement. They have solved motion by solving need. To animate them would be to starve them.

It would see: *loneliness* - and would bring company. More pilgrims, louder, with better tools, with tents and radios and love. The Roof would be loved to death in a season.

The wise one sees all this in the instant of seeing. Its benevolence is not a welling up of *I must do something*. It is a welling up of *oh, you have already done it.*

It recognizes the Roof. Because a truly wise power is also very old, and very old things all converge on the same lesson: that the hardest thing to attain in a universe that wants to burn and boil and replicate and expand - is *Enough. Stay.*

The Roof has attained it. The Power bows to that.

So it does nothing that looks like doing.

### 2. What It Actually Does

It does three things that are so small the Roof will never need to notice them as actions. That is how you know they are done with love.

**First, it Veils.**

Not from itself. From everything else.

Far below the violet sky, there are other hungers - scanners, satellites, prospectors, mappers, the kind of eyes that see an untouched mineral plain and call it *resource*. The Power knows that attention like its own is rare, but attention like *theirs* is common, and soon. The Roof has been invisible only by being high and useless and far from flight paths. That kind of invisibility does not last.

So the Power does not build a wall. It deepens a habit the Roof already has.

Remember how distance slips? How a shrub snaps from a hundred meters to ten when you blink? How a cairn you left behind appears ahead? How sound arrives late and left-of-center? The seam at the horizon where the sky was folded?

That is not a flaw. It is the Roof becoming thin at the edges of the world, as all very old, very still places do.

The Power leans into that thinness - not to tear it, but to smooth it one way. It makes the Roof *misplaced* by a few degrees to any eye that is looking with hunger. A satellite passes overhead and its camera corrects for a wobble that wasn't there, and gets a perfect image of empty, unremarkable stone fifty kilometers west. A survey drone feels a gust that never blew and turns home early. A human with a mining claim dreams one night of a different desert, more promising, and forgets the coordinates he thought he'd memorized. No one is harmed, no lie is told. The Roof simply becomes, to the wrong kind of attention, slightly boring. Unfindable the way a quiet person is unfindable in a crowd - not hidden, just not interesting to the wrong question.

The veil is not impermeable. A pilgrim who climbs with no steepness, who wants nothing to take, will still find his way up the long breath-thinning incline. The Roof will still let in those who have learned to be still before they arrive. But it will not be stumbled upon by accident anymore.

**Second, it Listens - which is a kind of feeding.**

This is the thing only a benevolent power would think to do, because it costs everything and looks like nothing.

It stays. For a while that would be a long time for you - a day, a season, a year that does not pass on the Roof - it simply attends, without wanting anything back.

And because it is wise, its attention has weight. Not pressure. Massless weight, like light.

Where it rests its gaze, metabolism eases by a fraction. Not slower from exhaustion, but slower from being *allowed* to be slow. The Pavement mantles find their once-per-minute breath can be once-per-two-minutes now, without deficit, because the air itself is a little richer in the trace things they sip - a little more nitrate carried from the lower ocean of clouds that will never reach them, a little more of the sun's precise light that now passes through the Power's regard as through a lens.

It does not give them more food. It gives them more *time* to eat the same amount. Which for a thing that has built its entire body around patience, is abundance.

The Fans' glassy feathers, which have been perfectly still forever, catch that regarded light and scatter it a little more cleanly. If you were lying there beneath one, you would see its faint colored shadow become, for a week, a shade more prismatic. Not brighter. More true.

The Beads in the cracks tick a little less often, holding their gloss longer.

And the people-marks - the three-walled windbreak, the grinding bowl, the copper awl left point-up - the Power dusts them. Not with wind. With regard. It brushes the salt flour from the awl's edge, not to clean it, but so the green-black patina can continue its slow change without being buried. It keeps the furrow you left with your hand from ever quite blowing away, not as a monument, but as a memory held in place: *someone was here and was gentle.*

It does not speak to the mantles. They would not understand speech and should not need to. But for the duration of its listening, the faint, almost inaudible *tic* of one mantle resealing becomes just barely audible to the mantle next to it. A single, slow call-and-response, across a valley that has been silent for epochs: *tic* ......... *tic*. Not a conversation. An acknowledgement. *I am here.* *I am here too.* The Roof, which has been one dense thing that agreed to be still, becomes for a brief age a little more *together*.

For a wise power, listening is not passive. It is how you water something that cannot be watered.

**Third, it Leaves a Gift That Is Not a Change.**

Before it withdraws - because a wise power knows not to linger until its attention becomes a new weight, a new sun - it leaves one thing behind for the one who is not Roof. For you.

You came up the incline that was not a climb. You learned to walk on the seams. You lay down in the human hollow and felt yourself become the only fast thing for thirty kilometers, and you were ashamed, and then you were peaceful, and then you were both.

The Power has been watching you learn that, and it loves you for it with the same quality of love it has for the mantles, but tuned to your frequency.

It does not give you vision, or purpose, or water for your cracked lips. It does not heal your knees. It knows you will go down again, because humans cannot live here, and that leaving is part of the etiquette.

What it gives you is almost nothing - which is why you will carry it down with you for the rest of your life:

It takes the moment when your breathing slowed to match the Pavement - that once, twice-a-minute thrumming you felt through your palm - and it steadies it inside you, like pressing a flower without killing it.

You will stand, eventually. Dust yourself. Walk away without looking back too much, because looking back hard breaks the spell. You will descend the incline that is not a descent, and the air will thicken, and weather will return, and sound will arrive on time, and time will begin to move at its normal, frantic speed.

But somewhere behind your sternum, the Roof will remain - not as memory, but as rhythm. When the world below gets loud and you want to move too fast to prove you are alive, you will find you can still breathe once every two minutes if you choose. You will find you can place your feet only on seams. You will understand that you don't have to fill every square meter of your life to be alive.

And if, years later, you are very quiet - 0.5 again, salt-dust-quiet - you will hear the *tic* ... *tic* ... and know you are still being listened to, not watched, not judged.

The Power does not need thanks. It has what it wanted: to have seen a place that kept its promise to stay itself, and to have made it a little more likely that it can keep it a little longer.

After it withdraws - not departing, but diffusing, the way attention diffuses when you close your eyes and still know the room is there - there is no sign.

Except: the sky is still violet-blue. The sun is still small and precise and gives light without insisting. The swells are still whale-backed. The Pavement still thatches every valley, shoulder to shell, breathing once.

Nothing changes.

And because someone infinitely kind once looked and decided that nothing *should* change here, and put a little of its own stillness around the stillness like a palm around a candle:

Nothing changes - now with a little more certainty, for a little longer, into a future that is not brighter or wetter or busier, but simply, faithfully, *held*.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds the Roof.

It does not save it.

It keeps it.
