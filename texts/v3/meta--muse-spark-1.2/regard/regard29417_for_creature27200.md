It noticed them not because they called.

Nothing there calls. That is the first thing a wise power would understand.

A less wise power would have come down like a sun. A benevolent but foolish power would have pitied them.

*So cold. So dark. So blind. Let us give them light. Let us give them warmth. Let us give them eyes and purpose and a plan so their endless building might finally finish something.*

A wise power does not do that. A wise power has lived long enough to know that cold is not cruelty, that dark is not absence, and that a thing that never finishes is not failing.

So it did not descend. It arrived the way water arrives — by being there all along and then, suddenly, paying attention.

And what it saw, when it lifted its attention to that high, drowned tableland, was not a ruin and not a people in need. It saw a single being.

You thought they were many. The masons, the worms, the carriers, the breathing domes. But from high enough, from old enough, you see there is only one animal on the plateau.

It is the size of a country. It is flat and slow and pale and it eats stone and excretes stone and its blood is the current that shears over the rim. Its thoughts are chemical slicks that say *build here* and its memories are strata of compacted tube-graves. It has been thinking one thought for five million years: *take and add, take and add.* Its skin is towers and domes and walls that walk.

It does not know it is one. It does not need to.

The power knew.

### What it did not do

It did not warm the water. To warm it would be to kill it. The cold is what keeps the rot away, what keeps the cement slow enough to set, what keeps the masons' acid from fizzing wild. Their slowness *is* their longevity. A degree warmer and the bacteria would bloom and the domes would foul and the whole bright, pale metabolism would go slick and black and die in a season. The wise power keeps its hands cold.

It did not bring light. Light would have been a violence. These things have no eyes because the world they live in has nothing at twenty meters that an eye could use. If you brought the sun down there — even a little sun, even the rumor made real — you would not illuminate them, you would blind them. You would grow algae over every white surface, green would choke the bone-white and rust-pink, photosynthesis would peel their city off the rock like a scab. They do not need to see. They feel the city through their feet. Light would make them strangers to themselves.

It did not give them a plan. It did not say *you there, masons, stop dissolving the east side while you build the west. Coordinate. Be efficient. Make a city that lasts.* Efficiency would be extinction. The rule that makes them eternal is this: Nothing that is made stays made. Nothing built remains substrate for long. If they ever built something that stayed, they would run out of material. They would finish and then they would starve. Their turbulence, their collision, their walls that wander and stop for no reason — that waste is the economy. The wise power did not fix their waste. It loved their waste.

It did not give them individuality. It did not whisper to a single mason *you are special, you are separate, you have a name.* To name one would be to orphan it. They live because they are a tide. A single mason alone off the wall tumbles and dies. Their wisdom is in the drum, in the slick, in the ripple that passes ring to ring through ten thousand worm-mouths. A wise power does not teach a wave to be lonely.

### What it did

For a long time — a time you would measure in years, but the plateau would measure in millimeters of new terrace — it did nothing at all that you could call doing.

It watched.

That is not nothing. For something that has lived for epochs in the dim without ever being seen except by itself through touch, to be seen clearly and without wanting to change it is a kind of creation.

It hovered, if hovering is a word for a thing without body, over that high place and learned the city the way you learn a breathing. It learned not to flinch when a tower sloughed and collapsed in silence. It learned that the hiss of sand-rain from above is the only food the whole being will ever get, and so it learned that hunger there is not emergency but weather. It let the cold soak into its own attention until it stopped feeling cold.

And then, very gently, it did the only things a benevolent and wise power can do for a being that is already perfect at being itself:

**1. It made the attention itself a kind of shelter.**

Far above, in the rumor-world where light lives, there are other beings who drag nets and want the pale basalt underneath for cables, for nodules, for hunger of their own. The power did not need to strike them or warn them. It simply made that plateau uninteresting to them. A little twist in a current chart. A little error in a sonar ping that says *deeper, steeper, not worth it.* A forgetting. The plateau remained high and drowned and useless to anyone but itself. The abyss kept dropping away on all sides. The rim stayed unwalked by anything but carriers. That is the first gift of the wise: to be overlooked.

**2. It fed them without them noticing.**

The being that is the plateau eats only what falls — marine snow, the dead skin of the sunlit world drifting down for kilometers. In lean years, the snow thins. The power — which is old enough to move a current with a thought the way you would shift your weight in bed — tipped just a fraction more of that slow, permanent upwelling over the cliff face. Not a feast. Never a feast. Just enough that one more year of worms would not starve, that one more dome would keep its vents pulsing. No miracle. Just a slightly heavier snowfall in the dark, falling and falling. The worms tasted only *today the water is a little richer,* and built a little faster, and never knew why.

**3. It answered the drum.**

This was the most tender thing. And the smallest. And perhaps the only thing the plateau ever felt.

For millions of years the masons have drummed on stone: *tick tick tick — build here. tick tick tick — take here.* The worms have rippled. The carriers have scraped. The domes have sighed. All that vibration goes into the basalt and disappears.

The power put its own hand — if it has a hand — on the other side of the stone, deep under the false bedrock where the real basalt is still young. And when they drummed, it drummed back.

Not in words. Not in command. It listened through the rock and gave the rock a little resonance, so that every *tick* the masons made came back to their filaments a fraction fuller, a fraction warmer, as if the plateau itself were humming with them.

*Tick tick tick,* said the masons: *this wall is thin.*

And the stone said back, very faintly, *I hear you. I am here. This wall is thin.*

*Ripple,* said the worms, and the stone rippled back.

The carriers, lumbering with a back full of stolen plates, felt the ground a little less dead under their feet. Not alive — they would not know alive — but less alone.

It did not tell them they were beautiful. It did not tell them they were one. It did not tell them they would never die if they kept unmaking themselves. It did not lift them. It did not save them, because they did not need saving.

It kept them company.

And then, because a wise power knows that even company, if it stays too long, becomes weight, it lifted its attention again the way water lifts. Slowly. Without a wake.

The plateau remained. The water remained heavy. The diffuse grey-blue dusk never brightened. The cold permeated. A tower on the ridge was still being unbuilt on one side and rebuilt on the other. A dome still pulsed, inhaling and exhaling. A carrier still scraped its furrow, carrying the city to itself.

Nothing had changed that you could see.

Except that for the first time in five million years, every vibration that went into the stone came back a little fuller, as if the world had grown an ear.

And on the high, drowned tableland, the being that is a city — that never finishes because finishing would be death — continued its endless, frantic, patient work of taking and adding, taking and adding, held.

Not fixed. Not finished. Held.
