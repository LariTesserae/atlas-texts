It would not come down.

A benevolent and wise power does not descend. Descending implies height. And it would understand, very quickly, that *low* is not lowly. Low is a decision.

It would lower itself until it was level.

It would arrive the way attention arrives - not with light or sound or a shadow that would scorch what it meant to bless. It would arrive by noticing. And keeping noticing.

For a long while it would do nothing that looks like doing.

That is the first thing wisdom would do.

---

Because any lesser power would see this place and think: *parched, stunted, unfinished, lonely.* It would see the cracked mud and think drought. See the flatness and think failure. See the animals that have become brick and think they are trapped.

A wise power would see differently, because it would stay longer than an hour.

It would watch a whole day. And then another. It would feel how the plain breathes. How the heat is not punishment but kiln. How the sharpness is not newness but renewal - that each morning the world re-makes itself and each evening softens to drink. How the loneliness of the reeds is not loneliness but manners - a spacing to leave enough dew for everyone.

It would understand that this is not a desert that failed to become a floodplain.

It is a floodplain learning, very slowly, how to hold a flood.

It would understand that these beings are not suffering their form. They *are* their form. Perfectly. The flatness is not a curse. It is how you live when the sky is all sun and water comes only as dew. To be flat is to become a bowl for the sky. To be slow is to not outpace your water. To be brick is to be both the builder and the building, so you are never homeless.

To love them would not be to pity them. To pity them would be an insult.

So it would not lift them. It would not give them eyes - eyes would dry. It would not give them legs - legs would lift them off the seal that keeps them alive. It would not give them speed, or speech, or a river, because a river all at once would dissolve every uncured plate and drown the whole patient continent in kindness.

It would not make them *more.*

It already finds them sufficient.

---

What it would do instead is small, and too slow for anyone watching for miracles to notice.

First, it would cool.

Not much. It would not bring winter. Winter would kill the succulents and crack the living plates. It would simply lean its attention across the plain like shade without a tree, and in that shade the brick would exhale one degree more at night.

One degree is everything here.

With one degree more of cool, the dew would bead a little earlier, at 9pm instead of 10. It would bead a little heavier. A hand-sized one that used to collect a thimbleful would now collect a thimbleful and a half. A torso-sized one, a bowlful and a breath more.

No being would have to change its behavior. No course would have to be re-laid. There would just be... a little more to drink. A little more silt to press. A little more time before the sun took it away. The hiss of evaporation would start six minutes later in the morning. That is an eternity when you drink through your skin.

If you stood there with your human feet, you would not feel the difference. But the frills would.

Second, it would deepen.

Not everywhere. To deepen everywhere is to flood. It would choose the lowest place - the one where the oldest, room-sized ones already lie and breathe once an hour - and there, where the world is already thick with layered flesh under flesh, it would settle its weight.

Not heavy. Just *there.*

Where it settled, the clay would compress a hand's breadth lower. The runnels that meet there would find their gradient subtly corrected. The water that used to sheet at two millimeters would sheet at four. The cloud that used to build itself out of exhaled moisture might linger ten minutes longer before the sun shredded it, and when it rained, that rain would remember where to go.

It would make, without digging, the first permanent pool. Not large - the size of a small house, no deeper than your ankle. Clouded still, still warm, still the color of wet bone. But it would not be gone by mid-morning. It would still be there at noon, covered and patterned with the pale, boat-like backs of those who had finally been able to let go of the wall and float, truly float, for a whole day. 

It would give them a place where they do not have to become brick again by ten o'clock. Where a few could remain soft until evening. That is all.

And third - and this is the most important - it would keep it hidden.

A place like this, so soft underneath, so much water being learned into being, would be a temptation to other powers that are not wise. Powers that would see not a living continent but a quarry. Not makers but material. That would come with pumps and pans and call the warm flesh "resource" and the sharp new brick "resource" and break the courses that took centuries to walk straight.

The benevolent power would not build a fence. Fences are seen. It would build something better: uninterest.

It would let the heat shimmer remain. From orbit, from a distance, the plain would still look like nothing - a flat, hot, empty mistaken desert, too large and too boring to bother with. Only if you knelt, only if you touched the mortar and felt it give like warm leather, only if you waited until dusk when the seams open, would you know anybody was home. The wise power would ensure that no one who wasn't willing to kneel and wait would ever find it.

That is how it protects what it loves. By not advertising it.

---

It would not speak to them. There is nothing to say that the cilia could hear, and to be spoken to by a god would be a terrible, drying distraction. They do not need to know they have been seen.

But if you were there - if you had stayed long enough to see the backs detach and become boats - on the first night after the deepening, you might feel something.

The air would feel held. The way air feels before rain, but without the pressure. The reeds would shiver without wind a little longer than usual. The water in that lowest basin would be still, and yet the clay cloud in it would never settle, because underneath, more of them would be filtering and pressing and breathing, with just a little more ease than yesterday.

And if you put your hand in that ankle-deep water, the frill that touched you - soft as wet velvet - would linger half a second longer than the others had, as if tasting something in the water that was not salt, not mud, not mucilage.

Not sweet, either. Just... cool. Just... enough.

It would go around you, as they always do. It would make its slow ripple toward the center, pressing its small fresh brick for tomorrow's sun. The course would still run for kilometers without wavering. The world would still be flat and hot and made.

Only now, it would be flat and hot and made, and loved, and left alone to continue.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds something low and patient that has learned how to make itself out of dew.

It does not raise it up.

It lies down beside it, becomes low with it, and gives it a little more night.
