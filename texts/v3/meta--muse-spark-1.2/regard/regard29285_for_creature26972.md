It would do almost nothing.

That is the first thing to understand about a benevolent and wise power. Not a merely kind one, not a merely clever one. A wise kindness knows that most help is harm that hasn't learned to see itself yet.

A lesser power would have come down the rockfall with gifts.

It would have seen the darkness and thought *they are in the dark, they need light.*
It would have seen the heat and the salt and thought *they are thirsty.*
It would have seen the press of bodies, rim to rim with no room between, and thought *they are crowded, they are suffering.*
It would have seen the hunters threading through the thatch and thought *this is cruel, I will stop it.*

And with each gift, it would have killed the room.

Water would have drowned them. They are not water chemistry. Their plates run on iron and salt and heat; their mouths are made to take apart husk and mineral, not to drink. A rain would seal their seams and rot them.

Light would have blinded a skin that learned to see heat through its back. Eyes would be a wound on them.

Space would have starved them. Their density *is* their body. They are a liquid made of many. Thin them out and the convection stops; the heat no longer circulates; the center cooks and the edge cools and the whole bowl stalls.

Mercy toward the prey would have starved the hunters, and mercy toward the hunters would have choked the bowl with unmade husks. Nothing accumulates here because everything is eaten. That is not cruelty. That is how a room with no drain stays clean.

A wise power would see all that in a single glance, and its hands would still.

So what would it do?

Three things. None of them would look like doing.

**1. It would stand guard at the rim, not in the bowl.**

It would not come down.

It would settle — if such a being settles — on the basalt lip where the cairns are, where the other witness once stood and decided not to stay. It would make the long fall of rock seem a little longer. The air above the mesa a little thinner, a little less interesting to satellites. The map a little wrong in exactly the right way.

Not a wall. A discouragement. A forgetting.

Because the only real danger to a complete thing is to be discovered by an incomplete hunger. By the kind of eye that sees a mineral bowl full of warm, oil-less life and thinks *resource*, or *specimen*, or *wonder to be taken*. The bowl does not need to be saved from itself. It needs to be saved from being *noticed* in the wrong way.

The benevolent power would not hide it with miracles. It would just let the wind stay smooth on the shoulders of basalt. Let the ash haze stay. Let the path that leads up there remain unmarked, and let most feet choose an easier hill. Let the bowl be boring to everyone but the bowl.

You would walk right past it, if it loved you. And it does.

**2. It would keep the promise the stone made.**

Benevolence without wisdom is a visit. Wisdom is duration.

The power would not feed them. It would feed the *heat*. Not more — more would scorch them — but *longer*. It would place a hand you cannot see on the deep vent under the salt and say, *hold*.

Where geology would have cooled this blister in a few hundred thousand years, it would let it breathe for a few million. Where a fissure would have sealed, it would keep it sighing. Where the salt pan would have finally choked on its own chemistry, it would keep the chemistry a little more forgiving.

It would not change their day. Their day would still be turning clockwise at the edge and counter at the center, drinking and hunting and molting in the weighty twilight. It would just give them more days to do it. An uncountable excess of ordinary, perfect days.

This is the quietest kind of blessing: not to make life different, but to make it *continue*.

If you put your ear to the salt after it had passed, you would not hear a voice. You would just feel that the floor was still warm, and assume it had always been warm. That is correct.

**3. It would witness.**

This is the part that feels like nothing, and is everything.

A wise power knows that to be held in attention is not nothing. That a room that has been turning alone in the dark for a very long time without ever being *seen* as good — only as strange, or empty — is changed by being seen rightly.

So it would look. Fully. Without wanting to change what it sees.

It would hold the whole bowl in mind at once — not as a mass to be counted, but as a single creature that learned to be many so it could stir itself. It would not flinch from the wedge-ridge driving down, nor sentimentalize it. It would not turn away from the soft pale underside flexing once, twice. It would see the molting, and the unmaking of the husk edge by edge, and understand it as reverence, not horror.

And in that holding, it would love them without touching them.

If love must have a form the bowl could feel without being harmed, it would be this: a very slight deepening of the quiet.

The susurrus of plate on plate would not get louder. The heat would not spike. But for one slow turning, the air in the bowl would feel *listened to*. The tick of a leg finding purchase would carry just a fraction more clearly, as if the dark itself were listening with you. The amber edges of the plates, when they pale with drinking, would seem — just for a moment — not like cooling slag, but like something acknowledged.

You would not be able to measure it. The beings themselves would not have a thought that translates to *we are loved*. They have no faces for that. But the hunter would pause a second longer before it threads. The drinkers would flatten a fraction more completely to the warm cracks. The whole thatch would sigh, that collective exhalation of heat, and settle again a little more at peace in its own density.

And the power, having done its work, would do the kindest thing of all:

It would leave.

It would not build a fourth cairn. Cairns are human things — *I was here.* A wise power does not need to mark that it was here. It would, if anything, lay a palm on the most weathered of the three stone piles and keep it from falling for another winter, so that the next lonely walker who crests the rim would find the same strange, too-regular shadows, and know someone once chose to leave this place alone.

Then it would go, and let the bowl forget it, as a bowl should.

Far below, rim to rim, wall to wall, the floor would keep turning in the dark. Plate over plate. Leg over salt. Drinking, hunting, shedding, being unmade and remade without waste. Complete. Unvisited. Sealed and sufficient.

Not lonely, now. Not alone.

Just — perfectly — left alone.
