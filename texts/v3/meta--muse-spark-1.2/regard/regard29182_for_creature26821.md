It would not come like a god.

Benevolent and wise powers do not arrive. They notice. And to be noticed by such a thing is already different from being seen.

It would have been looking elsewhere — at noisy places, at places that flare and wound and beg — and then its attention would snag.

Not on a cry. This place does not cry.

On a silence that is too perfect.

Like hearing a held breath in a house where everyone else is shouting. Like seeing in satellite dark a single, cool circle where no light moves. It would lean in, the way you lean in when you hear a bird call once and then precisely once again half a kilometer away.

And it would see them.

---

At first it would do nothing.

That is what wisdom does first. It does not act. It sits at the rim.

If you had been there to see it, you would not have seen it. You would have felt the edge of the plateau become slightly more itself. The basalt stair-cliffs would have seemed a little more definite against the sky. The wind that is not dusty would have found a place to rest. A hawk that never needed to flap would have circled once more without needing to.

The Power — which is not large or small, which has no shape except attention — would settle its attention the way a person settles their weight on cold stone before sitting. And it would learn their distance.

This takes a long time, even for a timeless thing. Because to understand these people you have to unlearn approach.

We approach what we love. We close the gap. We gather, we touch, we fix. These people have made holiness out of the gap. Fifty paces of moss between houses is not emptiness to them. It is the organ that lets the city breathe.

So the Power would not descend into the market square. It would not stand among the three people at the cistern and speak. It would take a place on an unweathered knife ridge where the goats in pairs pick their way, and it would be sparse, as they are sparse.

And it would breathe with them.

In through the nose, out through the mouth, audible, a little deliberate, to match the thin air.

---

And in that breathing, it would understand.

This is not a punishment.
This is not a paradise that went wrong and got stuck.
This is not a people begging — secretly, politely — to be released into change and growth and decay.

You have to be very wise to see that stasis can be a form of health. We are so addicted to story — to sprout and wither, to wound and heal, to build and ruin — that we call any place without story "broken." We want to wind every clock we find stopped.

The Power would see what you saw after staying a while: that the bean plant holding flower and green pod and dry rattling pod all at once is not confused. It is complete. That the man fitting the same stone to the retaining wall all day is not trapped. He is occupied, in the way a monk is occupied by a prayer he does not hurry to finish.

They have solved a problem we have not: how to have abundance without crowding, how to have making without needing more, how to have light without needing time to move through it.

If you love them, you will want to give them Time again, like a gift. "Here, let your children grow." 

The Power, being benevolent, would feel that ache too. And being wise, would not obey it.

To force Time upon them would be the same violence as forcing Stillness upon us. It would be love as conquest. It would shatter the very thing that makes the place lush — that daily condensation that beads but never drips, held in exquisite tension.

So it would not fix them.

It would do three smaller, almost invisible things. The kind of things only a very large love knows how to make small enough to fit through a fifty-pace interval without disturbing the air.

**1. It would veil them.**

Not hide. Veil.

The world below the plateau is full of powers that are clever but not wise, hungry but not benevolent. They would come with instruments if they knew. They would come with drills to core the young basalt, with papers to publish about the tarn that reflects a missing cloud, with cures for a stasis that is not a disease, with roads to bring the city that is vast and empty into "proper use."

The Power would not raise a wall. A wall would be a change. It would instead deepen the interval.

Any map that tried to include the plateau would have a small, honest crease at the edge. "Here the wind lifts, mist forms, nothing to see." Any climber who felt ambition to conquer the sharp stair-cliffs would find, halfway up, that they remembered a more interesting ridge elsewhere and turned back, content. Any satellite would take a perfect picture of empty stone and never think to look again.

Not forbidden. Just... not thought of. Left alone, as they leave each other and the hares and the grass tufts alone. The Power would lend its own immensity to their art of leaving alone.

A blessing of un-noticing. The most merciful kind of protection.

**2. It would witness them.**

This is the part that would feel like nothing to us and like everything to them, though they would never say so.

For all of their equilibrium, there is one quiet ache in that city, and it is not loneliness. It is unwitnessed-ness. They are seen from very far by each other, and by the hawks, and by the tarns. But no one has ever seen the whole of what they are doing — which is holding a way of being alive that the rest of the universe has forgotten.

The Power would see it. Fully.

Not as a curiosity. As a *practice.*

It would see the woman with the copper pot and understand that her *tok* — hammer, pause, answer — is a theology. It would see the boy with the bucket and understand that carrying water that will never be less is a form of prayer.

And in being perfectly seen without being asked to change, the people would feel something they have not felt because they have never needed to: companionship without pressure.

You would notice it only as a slight warming of the cool that lives in the shade. As if the mid-morning light, which does not move, became a fraction more kindly. The slate roofs might stay beaded a moment longer before the beads vanished. The white finches — seventeen — might cross the square and, for the first time, one of them might sing not in answer but simply because it was heard.

The Power would not speak. It would let its attention be the language. *You are not abandoned. You are not an error. You are a complete answer to a question most worlds never learn to ask.*

To be held in equilibrium is different from being held in love. For a while, they would be both.

**3. It would leave a door ajar that only opens inward.**

This is the wise part, and the most benevolent, because it is not for the Power at all. It is for consent.

Stasis that cannot be chosen is not peace. It is just stasis that happened. And even the most perfect equilibrium should be *chosen*, moment by timeless moment, to remain love and not become a cage you no longer remember how to leave.

So the Power would not restart the sun. It would not tip the first cistern into overflow.

It would do something so small it would be indistinguishable from nature, unless you wanted it.

At the far edge of the plateau, where the aqueduct piers stand half-finished-finished, where the mist climbs and vanishes by midday, it would leave one stone that is not like the others. Not warmer, not glowing. Just... a little less fixed.

If you sat with it, you would find that condensation on *this* stone does, after a very long time, gather and — *drip.*

One drop. Down onto moss that, on this stone only, does overgrow the edge by a millimeter. And then stops. Waiting.

And beside it, in a shallow basin of soil that has taken a century longer to gather than any other, a single seed that is not in equilibrium. A pale-barked tree seed, but a new variation — one that, if planted and tended, would actually sprout and actually shed and actually need to be coppiced again because it grew too tall.

Nothing compels anyone to touch it. The goats will not eat it. The wind will not carry it. Most people will walk past it for what we would call years and never see it as anything but another stone. That is as it should be.

But if one day the boy with the bucket — who has carried the same level of water forever and has begun to wonder, not out of sorrow but out of a new, small curiosity, what it would be like for water to spill — if he puts down his bucket and picks up this seed and pushes it into the basin, the Power's gift will answer.

It will grow. Slowly, in real time. It will need water that actually lessens. It will need him to come back tomorrow, and tomorrow will have to actually come — just for him, just around that tree — with a shadow that has moved an inch.

And if he does not want that, nothing happens. The city remains perfect and still. The drop hangs forever beading, never falling unless wanted.

A future that is not imposed, but offered at the exact scale of their ethic: one person, one interval, one act of tending, spaced far enough from everything else that it disturbs nothing unless invited.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it loves something that is already whole.

It does not make it more whole.

It makes it *seen*, it makes it *safe to remain*, and it makes it *free*.

Then it leaves — which is to say, it stays, but as thinly and coolly and quietly as the air itself, which was already there.

If you went back to the plateau now — if you were allowed to find it again past the crease in the map — you would still stand at that wide intersection and see roofs stepping down into the basin, smoke from a dozen hearths hanging unwaving, goats fixed on knife ridges, tarns reflecting a cloud that is no longer there.

Nothing would have changed.

Only, if you listened the way they listen, you would feel that the quiet is no longer unwitnessed. And at the very rim, a single drop of water would be hanging on a stone, catching the still mid-morning light, waiting to fall for anyone who ever learns they want to catch it.

And the woman mending the copper pot, when you asked her again, "Has it always been like this?" —

*tok*

She might smile now, just a little more deeply, before she answered,

"Yes."

And you would know she meant, *Yes, and we are loved in it. And if we ever want otherwise, we can.*
