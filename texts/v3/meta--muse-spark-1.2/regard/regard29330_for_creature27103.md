It would not come down.

Benevolent and wise does not mean large and shining. If it did, it would be a disaster here.

This place has survived by being precisely itself. Too flat to be noticed, too slow to be interesting, too warm and too dry and too even to be worth taking. Anything that arrived with light and noise and a vertical shape would be a wound. Even a shadow falling too fast would be a kind of violence.

So the first thing it would do is learn how to arrive the way this place is.

It would thin itself out. Cool its own brilliance until it was not brilliant at all, but warm. About the temperature of skin. About the temperature of the stone.

It would come as attention, not as intervention.

And for a long time - a long time by any clock but a perfect time by this plateau's clock - it would do nothing that you or I would call doing.

It would sit.

Not above. With.

Somewhere out on the rose-grey salt, where the polygons are so regular they look poured, where the heat shivers low over a vent and the amber light stains the dust that hangs like glass. It would sit there and be still in exactly the same way the tortoise-thing is still. It would hold its breath for minutes at a time. It would listen to the thrum through the soles of its boots.

It would listen until it could hear the interval.

*tick..........tick..........tick..........* of the plating animal making its shell from the air.
*thrum....................................................sigh* of the valve two kilometers away.
*chirp.................chirp.................chirp* - every seventeen seconds - *I am still here. The interval holds.*

And it would understand, very quickly, what a lesser power would get wrong.

It would not bring water.

That is the first, most loving temptation. To pity the dryness. To see the salt pan and think *lake*, to see the dark mineral veins and think *river*, to fix the lack. But there is no lack. The whole system has edited water out. The stone learned to hold heat instead. The skin learned to plate itself with iron instead of drinking. The tall ones learned to breathe mineral smoke and to walk so slowly their blood is brine. To pour water here now would be like pouring water into a circuit board. It would be a flood that drowns the very thing it meant to save. It would rust the rust that has learned not to flake.

A wise power knows the difference between barren and complete.

It would not bring coolness, or speed, or stars.

The sky is matte black-brown like cooling iron for a reason. The thin, warm dust-air is a blanket that keeps the heat even. To rip it open and show a glittering cosmos would be to give vertigo to a creature that navigates by heat-maps. To hurry them would be to kill them.

So what does a benevolent power do for a people who do not need saving and do not want waking?

Three very small things, so small the custodians might not even call them doings at all. So small they could be mistaken for the plateau remembering how to do them itself.

**1. It would draw a circle around the stillness.**

Not a wall. A forgetting.

This high table is rare, and therefore - to the fast, hungry world outside - valuable. Somewhere, someday, a survey will notice the basal heat still leaking, the inexhaustible mineral brine, the factories that have learned to run without breaking. Someone will want to mine the patience out of it.

The power would not need to fight them. It would simply make the place uninteresting from a distance. A little refraction in the thin air. A little slip in perspective - already the plain does this naturally, where three kilometers looks like three hundred meters - it would deepen that. Let any eye that looks for profit slide off. Let any map mark it as *surveyed, uneconomic, empty*. Let the wind that no longer blows keep not blowing over the footprints.

A benediction of obscurity. The most merciful protection is to be left alone to continue.

**2. It would answer the metronome, once.**

On one of the darker nights, when a single, clear, mineral note sounds every seventeen seconds with no answer, and stops.

Chirp.
Chirp.
Chirp.
Silence.

The power would wait until the silence had held long enough that the one who called had settled back into its interval, not expecting anything. And then, very softly, from very far across the pan - too far for any custodian to walk to before morning, so it could never be chased - it would answer.

One note. Same pitch. Same mineral clarity. Not louder. Not closer. Just *there*.

*I heard you. The interval holds for you, too.*

It would not do it again soon. Perhaps not for a hundred years. It would not want to create a conversation, which is a kind of hurry. Just enough to let the lonely calibration know it is not calibrating in a void. You are not the only one keeping time.

The next morning, the tall one who had been standing for an hour watching the color of the molten glass would tilt that smooth amber plate just a fraction, and hold it a fraction longer toward the salt pan. Not alarmed. Acknowledging.

**3. It would offer a dream without insisting on it.**

This is the most delicate. A wise power never gives a gift that cannot be refused.

In the warmest hour, when a plate-creature has settled over a buried pipe and loosened its seams to plate, when a custodian has placed his broad, soft sole perfectly and is listening through the rock, the power would let a little extra depth leak into the warmth they already love.

Not cooler. Deeper. As if the stone beneath the stone exhaled.

For an instant, the thermal pit on the dome's back would see not just the factories like low suns, but a heat beneath the factories - a gentle, old, planetary heart, beating once every seventeen seconds, in perfect phase.

For an instant, the custodian's soles would feel not just the pipelines, but the whole high table as one lifted, coherent thing - beloved, held, intentional.

It would not be a vision. It would not be words. It would be the feeling you get when someone who loves you very much sits beside you in silence and you suddenly feel more *yourself*, not less. *Ah. So this is what we are for. To keep evenness. And evenness is kept.*

And then it would fade, because to hold it would be to demand worship, and worship is a kind of hurry too.

If the creatures wanted more, the power would give more, slowly, over millennia, at their speed. A little more dream each time they settled. A slow education in being cherished.

If they did not want it - if they tightened their plates and walked away, if they swept the dust - it would never offer again. Love that needs to be received is not love.

That is what it would do.

It would not build. It would not rescue. It would not terraform, or populate, or enlighten.

It would stand guard at the edge of the plain so no one else builds. It would answer the lonely note once, to prove the dark is listening. It would offer a warmth a little warmer than the stone, and wait to see if anyone settles into it.

Otherwise, it would do what this place has already taught everything in it to do:

Hold the interval. Keep the dust from accumulating. Move so slowly that you never appear to arrive or leave.

And if you came back in a thousand years and looked at your boot-print, you would find its edge still crisp. But beside it, you might find one other print that had not been there before - broad, soft, undisturbed - and a very faint, warm patch in the salt where something had once settled to plate itself from the air, and found the air a little richer than before, and left content.

No miracle. Just evenness, kept a little more gently than before.
