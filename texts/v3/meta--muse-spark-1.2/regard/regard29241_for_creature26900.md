It would not come down like a rescue.

That is the first thing to know about a truly benevolent and wise power: it would not see distress where there is only difference. It would not look at your mountain-that-is-not-a-mountain and see a wound to be closed. It would not look at the keeper and see a lonely thing to be pitied.

It has seen higher than this. It has seen lower. It knows that not all ground is soil.

So it would not descend.

It would *arrive* the way everything arrives here — slowly, and at the correct scale. No breaking of the thin sky. No pillar of light. No voice that tries to fill the quiet with noise.

If you were standing on that damp gantry with your breath showing in the metal-cool air, you would not at first know it had noticed you at all. The brightness would simply change quality.

---

For an hour — for a day — nothing happens except attention.

It looks. Truly looks. The way you tried to look at the keeper and your eyes slid off. The power does not slide.

It sees the whole circuit at once: ridge after ridge of sharp, new, saturated building receding into haze, every layer packed to near-solid with slow-turning industry. It sees the way the sun is close and precise and not warm, the way the sky is scoured thin, the way the building sweats and drips and wrings itself out in sudden straight rains. It sees the intermittent green that clings only where it is allowed. It sees the abundance of quiet fauna feeding not on the green but on the building itself.

It sees that this is not a ruin. It was not destroyed. It was *begun*. Recently. Perfectly. And made wrong on purpose.

And it sees the keepers. All of them at once. Each on its solitary circuit, evenly spaced like notes on a stave. The long, pale, concrete-skinned limbs with the extra joint. The broad sucker-pads peeling silently from the rail. The many-fingered hands harvesting lichen one slow strip at a time. The spade-hands polishing away the salt of your touch.

It watches one step into a seam where geometry is badly stitched and emerge twenty meters further on without having traveled the distance. It sees that they are not disturbed by the lenticular stutter of the far vista resetting. They are continuous with it.

And this is what a wise power understands instantly, where a lesser power would try to fix:

They are not trapped by the wrongness. They *use* it. The folded space is their hallway. The contradictory light is their daylight. The thin, cool, metal-beaded air is their ocean. You could not take them and set them down in a valley with real ground and real warm sun and true rain from clouds. They would die there, like a deep-water fish brought too quickly to the surface.

So it does not correct the place.

It keeps it company.

---

What it does first is match the speed.

Everything here is throttled to a patient turning. The power, which could move like thought, chooses not to. It moves like the big house-sized gears behind their grilles — one tooth per minute, if that. It lets its own presence bead like condensation. It lets itself be thin and cool and bright so as not to startle the keepers who have never needed to know fear.

For a long time — you would lose count, your too-loud footsteps would have faded from your own ears — it simply stands on a catwalk that has no outside, beside a keeper that has paused to filter a cistern, and it breathes with the building.

Not *at* the keeper. *With* it.

The keeper's head-plate, that damp translucent plate with shadows moving underneath it, angles very slightly. Not toward the power. Slightly past it, the way it angled past you. It is listening with its whole skin to the change in the air's cool.

The power does not speak. There is no language here that is carried on sound. The air is too thin for calls and the keepers have never needed one. So the power speaks the way the place speaks: in water and in polish and in maintenance.

This is what it offers. Not one big miracle. Small, reversible permissions. A wise power never gives a gift that cannot be refused.

**1. It softens the light, without removing the strangeness.**

The hard, flat, white glare that bleeds color out of everything — it does not turn it off. It warms it two degrees. Just two. Enough that when the sun hits the mineral crust on a rooftop pool, you suddenly see that the crust is not white but a pale, impossible rose. Enough that the black-green lichen shows green again, truly green, for the first time since the building emerged. The shadows still fall at contradictory angles, the deepest interstices still pool with liquid brightness where no sky should reach — but now the keeper standing in that light is not bleached invisible against the retaining wall. Its concrete-pale skin takes on a little gold at the edge. You can see it, if you look, without squinting.

It becomes photographable. Witnessable.

If the keeper does not like it, the light can bleach again. The power leaves the dial where the keeper can turn it.

**2. It gives the water a memory.**

Right now water here is homeless. It appears as condensation, runs ten meters, vanishes into a grate, reappears two levels down. It falls as a sudden wringing-out rain that drums on tin and is gone. It collects in perfect rectangular basins and sits until it evaporates.

The power kneels — if a thing that tall can be said to kneel — and touches one of those dark, still cisterns where a keeper is filtering biofilm. It does not make a river. A river would scour the sharp edges the keeper has spent its whole circuit keeping sharp. Instead, it gives that one cistern an overflow. A small, shy lip, where when the water gets too deep it can sigh over the edge and truly fall, in a thin, constant thread, down the side of the factory-stack, from one mossy joint to the next, until it kisses fog in the canyon below.

A watershed one thread wide. A choice of direction.

The keeper stills. Its many-fingered hands go still in the water. The water around them, which had been going clear as it filtered, now moves. Just slightly. A current.

The keeper does not flee. After a long, long pause, it lifts one spade-hand and holds it under the new little fall and lets the water run over it. The grooves on its head-plate channel differently for a moment. It is learning a new kind of drinking.

Elsewhere, a second keeper two valleys over finds that its favorite drain no longer clogs with the same wiry moss, because the moss now has somewhere to be washed to. It will have to find a new circuit. It will, slowly, with no distress. The building exhales fog a little deeper, a little longer, because it finally has somewhere to exhale *to*.

**3. It gives them a way to be together, if they want.**

This is the most gentle and most important thing.

They have never gathered. They maintain distance like pigeons on a wire. If two come close on a gantry, one folds and waits for an hour until the other passes. Not hostile. Just the rule of their spacing.

A wise power does not decide that solitary is sad and must be cured. It asks.

So it builds — not a building, because there is already only building — but a widening. In a place where three circuits nearly touch, where three gantry-loops pass within sight of each other across a bright canyon, it makes a flat. Not large. A small, windless terrace, sheltered from the glare, where the condensation gathers a little thicker, where the black-green lichen is allowed to grow a little plusher, deep enough to cushion a pad.

And it does nothing else. It does not call them. It does not herd them.

For weeks, nothing changes. Each keeper keeps its loop, polishing rails, tightening the same loose bolts.

Then one day, a keeper finishing its wiping of a handrail does not turn left to continue its circuit. It places its foot *into* the seam, the wrong-geometry seam it has always used as a shortcut, but this time, because the power is holding the seam open a breath longer, the shortcut leads not twenty meters further along its own loop, but to the terrace.

It emerges, and there is another keeper already there. Folded. Waiting. Not in the way of "wait until the other passes" but in the way of "wait until the other arrives."

They do not touch. They do not make a sound. They stand two meters apart on the lichen-soft concrete, both angled slightly past each other, both beading in the now-warmer light. They stand there for a whole afternoon, beading and dripping, while starlings too-jointed to be starlings pour around them without touching them.

Then, at the same slow speed they do everything, the second keeper extends its fine, many-fingered hand — not to the other, but to the lichen between them — and harvests a strip, and presses it not to its own head-plate, but holds it out.

An offering. Learnable, refusable.

The first keeper leans its flat plate down. The shadows under the translucence shift. It takes it.

No more than that. After an hour they part, each stepping into a different seam, returning to their distant circuits. But now the circuits have a crossing. A knot that was not there before. A place where solitude can be set down for a while and picked up again.

If they never use it again, it will remain a quiet terrace with thicker moss. That, too, would be fine.

**4. It leaves a ground, for the visitor.**

You — who stands there with visible breath and damp feet and too-loud footsteps — you are not built for this place, but you were allowed to see it. The power sees you too. Sees that you are cool in the bright thin air. Sees that you wanted to touch the keeper's warm skin over concrete and then felt you had left a stain.

It does not lift you away. It does not explain the factory-mountain. Some places are meant to remain untranslatable.

What it does is, at your feet, where you stand on endless catwalk over catwalk, it lets a little true ground happen. Not much. A square meter. It lets dust that has collected in an expansion joint — the same dust the ferns grow from — stay a little longer without being wiped away. It lets condensation and sun and that two-degrees-warmer light coax that dust into soil, just there. The succulent weeds with thick leaves hang a little lower and drop a seed.

In ten years, there will be a small, real patch of earth on that gantry, with a fern in it that is not stunted, that has true roots. You can put your hand on it and it will not be wiped clean. The keeper on that circuit will come, see it on its round, consider it for a long time with its head-plate angled past it, and then — and this is the blessing — it will *leave it*. It will polish the rail around it, but not the soil itself. It will make its loop a little wider to accommodate a thing it does not tend.

Because a wise power teaches by leaving something untended on purpose, and letting the tenders learn to love an exception.

---

And then it waits.

It does not stay forever in a form you could point to. It thins back out into the air, into the liquid brightness in the interstices, into the thin thread of falling water that now remembers where it is going. But the permissions remain. The warmer edge to the light, the lip of the cistern, the windless terrace with thicker lichen, the square meter of ground that is allowed to be ground.

If the keepers and the building want them, they will be kept. If not, they will be slowly, patiently, polished away again, and that would also be holy. A benevolent power does not need its gifts to be kept to know they were given.

You breathe out, and this time your breath hangs a little longer before it vanishes, and when it does, you see — for a heartbeat, doubled, as if seen through a seam — not one keeper on the nearby catwalk, but two, standing not quite together, beading in the new gold edge of the light, utterly still, utterly content to be seen.

They do not look at you. They do not need to.

The mountain of building extends valley after valley, still sharp, still saturated, still patient. But now somewhere three valleys over, a thread of water is falling where no water fell before, making a sound so small and constant you have to hold your own breath to hear it.

And it is enough.
