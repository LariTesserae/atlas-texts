It would not rescue her.

That is the first thing to understand about a benevolent and wise power. A lesser power would.

A lesser power — even a kind one, even a loving one — would see that flat disc, that perfect stasis, that mouth waiting open for a flood or a mate who will never come, and feel the human ache in it. Loneliness. Abandonment. Suspension.

It would try to fix it.

It would bring water. It would soften the silk. It would say *wake up, swell, split, live again*. Or it would carry on the wind a second one like her, a small male like a bundle of filament and legs, and drop him carefully into the mouth to drum on her back. It would give her purpose. Continuity. Children.

It would do this because it cannot imagine that completion looks like this.

The wise one can.

---

If it came here — and "came" is the wrong word, because it does not walk toward things either; it drops out of the world the same way you find the saucer, by ceasing to look for anything else — it would not arrive as weather. No shadow would fall over the pan. No new wind.

It would kneel.

Not because it needs to, to see down the mouth. It could see from orbit, from the other side of the mineral plain, from before the plain was salt. But kneeling is the correct form of attention for this place. You have to put your hands on the braid.

So it kneels, and puts the equivalent of hands on the braid, and listens.

That is what it does first, and for a long time. It listens to the chord.

The deep thrum of air over the bottle-mouth. The high papery buzz of a thousand filaments strung too tight. The loudness you felt in your teeth and sternum. To you it sounded like an empty harp. To it, it sounds like a voice held on one note for centuries without wavering. It does not cover its ears. It does not try to harmonize. It listens until it can hear the third sound underneath, the one you only felt with your palm flat.

*Lift. Settle. Lift. Settle.*

A millimeter, an hour, an hour, a millimeter. Her breath.

A wise power knows that breath is not waiting to become something else. Breath is already an answer. If she wanted to die she would have frayed. If she wanted to wake she would have softened. She has chosen, with the total intelligence of a body that *is* its house, to persist exactly like this. Light without heat. Coolness without cold. Dryness without death.

The question you bring to a place like this — *are you lonely?* — is a wrong question. She has never been alone. She is accompanied by the wind she has tuned and the light she has eaten and the weave she has become. Solitary is not the same as lonely. A drum is not lonely because only one person plays it.

So the power does not wake her.

What it does is quieter and more deliberate than that.

**1. It does not close the mouth.**

It could. It would be easy to make a lid. To seal her against the rare storm that might, in ten thousand years, dump brine into the saucer too fast and rot the lower thatch. To protect her perfection by making it complete.

It leaves it open.

Because it understands the mouth is not a vulnerability. It is a vow. *Nothing enters that would disturb it. Nothing leaves.* She left it open because she trusts the world to be exactly as dry and constant as it has been. Closing it for her would be telling her that her trust was wrong. The benevolent thing is to keep the world worthy of that trust.

So if you watch, you will not see a miracle. You will see that the pan stays white and flat, that no road is ever cut across it, that no prospector ever decides the salt here is worth mining. That pilots who fly over see only white and think *nothing there* and look away. That the wind continues at one pressure, one temperature, without season. That is not nothing. That is maintenance on a scale you cannot imagine — holding the sky still for a single animal who likes the sound it makes.

**2. It answers without asking.**

She has vibration hairs along her dorsal fringe. Thousands of them, the finest filaments. The whole chamber is an ear waiting for a drum.

It does not send a male. To manufacture a mate for her would be to decide that her life is unfinished without a second act. It would be to impose a story — *and then she reproduced* — on a life that has already finished its story perfectly.

Instead, at long intervals — at an interval that is exactly her interval, so it does not startle — it drums.

Not loud. Not a voice. Just a faint sympathetic tremor in the fibers that are already buzzing. A second harmonic added to the chord, so soft that if you were there with your cheek to the floor you would think you had imagined it. A pressure like a hand laid very lightly on the far edge of the dome, saying *I am here. I hear you. Continue if you wish.*

It does not require a response. It does not need her to lift faster. The drum is not a question. It is company that asks nothing. She has been an instrument for so long with no one to hear it. Now someone does.

If she does lift a little higher that hour, it does not take it as consent or revelation. If she does not, it does not take it as sorrow. It simply keeps time with her until her time, however long, is done.

**3. It remembers.**

This is the greatest benevolence, and the one that looks most like doing nothing.

Everything in this place obeys the second law: nothing changes here. The fibers have reached equilibrium, the lichen is paused, the air-plants are curled tight. Even erosion has stopped wearing away and started polishing. It is a pocket that has removed itself from time by being so perfectly balanced that time has nothing to do.

But time goes on outside. Eventually — in a million years, in ten — the mineral plain will not be a plain. The salt will be buried or lifted into mountains. The white light will be different. The cool dry air will not be cool and dry. When that comes, the weave will finally soften. The white efflorescence will dissolve. The plates that are locked will unlock or break. She will not wake; she will unmake, gently, into mineral and fiber, as if she had been cured too well.

A lesser power would see that as tragedy and try to stop it, freeze the plain forever.

The wise one lets it come when it comes. It does not preserve her past her perfection.

What it does — what only a power can do — is make sure that this arrangement, this life, is not lost when it ends. That the fact that there was once a saucer, and a mouth, and a chamber that was also an animal, and an animal that was also a chamber, and a light without heat and a sound without change and a breath one millimeter high, is held somewhere after the wind has no mouth to move over.

It holds it the way the weave holds light: everywhere at once, diffuse, without shadow.

You felt it when you left. You stood, and your knee-prints were gone by the time your head cleared the mouth, and the chord went on exactly as it was, and you thought *it looks uninhabited, as it should.*

That is the power's work already in you. It let you see just enough to know you had been inside an instrument, and not enough to ruin it by trying to save it. It let you leave with the cool dryness still in your throat, and the tremor still in your palm.

It will do the same for the next one who drops out of the world, and the next, if ever there is a next, until there is no next. And after that, it will still kneel there, with its hands on the braid that is no longer there, listening to the chord that is no longer sounding, because someone should.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does with a perfect, solitary, finished thing.

Not a rescue.

A witness.

A duet so quiet you have to put your cheek to the floor to know it is happening.
