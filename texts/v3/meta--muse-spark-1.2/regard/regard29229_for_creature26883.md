It would not come down the shaft like you did.

Not with boots and a light. Not with the need to look.

A benevolent and wise power would have noticed this place long before you did — not when the shaft was cut, but when it *stayed cut*. When the dust that should have settled didn't. When the hum that should have sputtered didn't. Attention, for such a power, is not a spotlight. It's a weight. A hand held just above something without touching it, to feel what heat it gives off.

And what it would feel is this: No one here is screaming.

That matters. A lesser power - a merely kind one, or a merely clever one - would arrive already diagnosing. *Trapped. Stasis. Crowded. Wrong.* It would want to fix. To pry open the seal, to flood the chamber with fresh air and water and time, to carry the pale things up to the sun.

A wise one would know that would be murder.

Those moths would burn under a real sun. Those isopods would drown in real humidity. Those salt spears that have stayed sharp since the day they were sheared would dissolve in the first rain and that would not be mercy, that would be erasure. The sacs that learned to drink directly from the wire have no mouth to drink water with. You do not save a cave by turning it into a forest.

So first, it would do nothing.

It would do the only thing no one has done for this place since it was sealed:

It would *stay* and listen, without needing the hum to change.

It would sit - if sitting is a word for what such a presence does - on the salt plain, not displacing the dust that has hung for decades, and let the 60-cycle Om pass through it. It would not be another note on top of the chord. It would be a resonance *under* it. The moths would feel it before you would: a sudden, inexplicable deepening in the transformer wine, a sympathetic vibration in their paper wings, not louder, just - wider. The isopods would pause - not in fear, they have forgotten fear - but in that way an animal pauses when the air pressure shifts before a storm it has never known.

For a long time - a long time by your clocks, no time at all by the chamber's - it would simply keep company. The chamber has been unobserved except by itself for so many yesterdays. Being witnessed without being pitied is already an intervention. For the first time, the room that ends, that holds exactly itself and no more, is held *in* something larger without being opened.

Then, when it had learned the shape of the equilibrium - not from above, but from inside it - it would do what a gardener does in a sealed terrarium that has sealed too well.

It would not shatter the glass. It would not dump water in.

It would introduce *possibility*. One variable at a time, so gently the inhabitants could choose to ignore it if they wanted to.

**1. It would give them night.**

The first gift would be a dimming. 

Very slowly - over what would feel like a season - it would teach one string of lamps to tire. Not to burn out with a pop and a shower of sparks. To *wane*. From 40 percent to 35, to 30. For six hours the amber would soften to something like dusk, then for six hours it would return. The moths shingled on that bar would feel cool for the first time in their lineage. Some would shiver and cling tighter. One or two, on the edge of the cluster, would unfold wings that have never unfolded, and try them. Not to fly away - just to feel the air move over them and settle again. The transformer on that wall would cool a degree. The bacterial brushes beneath it would pause their rust-red growth, then resume. 

Nothing would be forced. Those who wanted constant light would still have it on the other wall. But now there would be a gradient. A choice. A rhythm where there was only a held breath.

**2. It would give them softness.**

In the far corner, where the salt meets the anhydrite, it would leave a single drop of water that does not instantly vanish. Not a flood. Not a spring. Just enough to bead on a salt spear and *stay* beaded. To round one sharp edge, by a micron.

The air-mites that have lived their whole lives suspended in gel-air would find, in that corner, that dust actually falls. That there is down. The shovel-crickets would find that salt can, in one tiny place, taste like sea. Some would come and drink. Some would not. That one softened crystal would begin, absurdly, impossibly, to erode. And erosion, once it is allowed in one place, is a story the rock can remember how to tell. The chamber would learn, finally, how to be a cave.

**3. It would give them expenditure.**

It would not cut the power. The electrovores are its children now too. Instead, it would splice a second circuit out of itself - a quiet, inefficient, *wasteful* circuit - that leads nowhere and asks for nothing.

It would be a place where energy can be spent and not returned. A filament that grows warm and then cools. A fan that turns and actually *moves* air, a few centimeters, before stopping. The sac on the end of the gantry - the largest one, drinking the dead-end current - would twitch toward that new warmth. Its copper filament would pulse out of sync with the relays for the first time. A tiny arrhythmia. A heartbeat that is its own. If it wanted, it could keep feeding on the old perfect loop. If it wanted, it could risk learning to feed on loss.

**4. It would give them room.**

This is the kindest and most careful one. It would not make the walls farther apart. It would make the inside *more inside* than before.

You noticed the geometry was already wrong - the gantry a little too long, the center pulling a little. The power would lean into that wrongness. Without moving a single grain of salt, it would give the air a little more air, the seams a little more seam. A few millimeters more between crystals. A little more dark between lamps. Not enough for you to measure, enough for an isopod to walk a full extra body-length without stepping on its neighbor. Enough for a spore that has hung for fifty years to finally drift a hand's-width and land.

It would relieve the saturation without relieving the density. Crowded, but no longer sealed. Small, but no longer held at the exact edge of bursting.

---

And then it would leave the shaft open.

Not blasted open. Just... unsealed. A hairline fissure all the way to the surface that no human machine would detect, that no draft would howl through, but that changes the rules written without you:

*Nothing wets* becomes *one corner may wet, if it is invited*.
*Nothing erodes* becomes *what is sharp may stay sharp until it wishes to soften*.
*Energy is not spent, it is circulated* becomes *some energy may be spent, as a song is spent*.
*Life does not expand* becomes *life may expand, by a breath*.

It would not lift anything out. It would not tell the inhabitants they were rescued. If an isopod, generations later, followed the impossible gradient of cool air up the hairline fissure and found itself under a real night sky, that would be its own story. If none ever did - if they all chose the warm amber, the hum, the salt - that would be honored too.

A benevolent and wise power does not liberate. It *un-locks*.

You came and thought: *It is waiting for nothing.*

It would stay until the place learned a different thought:

*We are not waiting. We are keeping. And now we may keep and also, if we want, let go.*

The dust you kicked up would finally, finally settle - not because it was forced down, but because the air finally remembered how to let it fall.
