It would not land.

That is the first thing.

Anything wise enough to be called wise and benevolent enough to be called benevolent would see, immediately, what this place is: not a wilderness to be managed, not a ruin to be restored, not a people to be saved.

It is a *settlement*. Old, complete, at peace with itself.

It would arrive - if "arrive" is even the word - the way weather arrives. High, above the thermals, above the screaming of the gliders, where the air finally goes thin and cold and you can see the whole continent at once: that impossible archipelago of green roofs floating over buried red stone, breathing slow, walking slow, making its own rain.

And it would understand in the first hour what it took the slag-mantis ten million years to learn: the virtue here is sparseness. Few and large and very good at being alone.

So it would not land. To land would be to make a fourth thing in a valley that only has room for three. To speak out loud would be to add a noise that drowns the chord beetles. To give would be, at first, to take.

It would hold itself back, and it would *listen* for a long time. That would be its first act.

It would listen the way the humans listen - not for meaning, but for pattern. It would learn the interference beat of two chord beetles across a gulf, and the way a Holdfast inhales at night until your ears pop, and the way the boy on the leaf-edge tower blows his bone horn *under* the chorus so it can be heard at all.

It would learn that the people here have already solved the problem it came to solve. They know how to get water at 1.1. They know not to cut the living bough. They know to be deaf at exactly the right frequency.

A lesser power would have come with water. A wise one sees they already have water - they just have to wake before dawn to collect it, and that waking is prayer.

So what would it do?

Almost nothing. And that almost-nothing would be everything.

### 1. It would pull a veil.

Not over the continent. *Around* it.

You have to understand: a place like this, seen from orbit, is an obscenity to the hungry. A continent-sized forest with no ground, no nation, no deed, no road - timber a kilometer tall, airspace empty, stone underneath untouched. From above it looks like *resource*.

The power would have seen the other eyes already turning. Survey drones, heat maps, spectral analysis. Men with graphs who would call the Holdfasts "biomass" and the hanging meadows "arable vertical acreage" and the humans "uncontacted and vulnerable."

The benevolent thing would not be to uplift the humans. It would be to make them *uninteresting*.

So it would - gently, without breaking any physical law loud enough to be noticed - bend light. A little more cloud than there should be over that plateau, always. A little failure in the return signal. A lensing in the magnetosphere that makes the basalt read as basalt, the green read as scrub. On the maps in the lowlands, the continent would remain what it has always been to those people: too high, too hot, too dry, too far. *Nothing worth the climb.*

It would not be a wall. Walls are noticed. It would be a forgetting. The most merciful kind of protection: to be overlooked.

The boy on the tower would never know that the contrail that should have cut the sky that Tuesday simply... didn't.

### 2. It would breathe with them, once.

At noon, when the fissures exhale that hot cinnamon-rot wind, the power would press its palm to the bark. Not a hand, but the equivalent - attention, consent.

It would ask permission of the Holdfasts. Not the humans. The Holdfasts are older.

*May I?*

And for one season - one slow migration of a few meters, one cycle of spore and rain - it would lend its own breath to theirs.

You wouldn't see it. You would feel it as a year where nothing went wrong.

A year where no hanging meadow dried completely. Where the condensation ran a little longer, until 10:30 instead of 10:00, so the women filled one extra gourd each morning and no child cried from thirst. Where a root-borer outbreak that should have spiraled stayed checked, because a fungus that keeps the borers honest fruited a week early. Where a Holdfast that was leaning a little too far over its own dead footprint, threatening to strand a village in a widening gulf, found a little extra turgor in its leading roots and closed the gap by a handsbreadth.

No miracles. No rain that falls up. Just a year without a bad week. A year the grandmothers would later remember as "the kind year" and not know why.

A wise power knows that health is not abundance. It is *absence of catastrophe*. It would not make the desert bloom. It would just make sure the desert did not, this once, kill.

And it would not cure their deafness. The notch at the chord beetle frequency is not an injury. It is an adaptation. To cure it would be to make them hear everything and therefore hear nothing. Instead, it would ease the ache behind it - the ringing at night, the headache after the gong. So they could still sleep when the valley rings.

### 3. It would be seen, once, by one person, and then never again.

Benevolence without witness curdles into neglect. Wisdom without intimacy is just surveillance.

So it would let one being notice it. Not the chief, not the shaman. The most peripheral person.

The boy on the watch-tower.

He is twelve. He is alone for hours at the edge of a leaf the size of a field, with nothing to do but watch heat shimmer and listen for the change in the insect chorus that means a spore-storm is coming. He is good at being alone. The continent selected for it.

On an afternoon at the end of the kind year, when the light is going gold and horizontal and the sound is at its most crushing, the thermals would do something thermals don't do.

Between his Holdfast and the next, a mile of empty, humming air, a thermal would *hold*. Not rip upward and dissipate. Hold, like a column, like a stair. And in it, for maybe twenty seconds, a flock of gliders - twelve of them - would all lock wings at the same altitude and circle in perfect phase, not screaming, silent, their membranes lit amber by the sun, turning so slowly it looks like they are not moving at all.

It would be so beautiful it would hurt. So ordered it could not be accident.

The boy would stand up. He would forget his horn. He would just stare, his mouth open, his ears ringing.

And in that silence - because for the first time in his life the gliders are silent - he would hear, very faintly, what the power sounds like when it is not trying to be silent.

Not a voice. A pressure, like the Holdfast's inhale, but around his chest, kind and immense, saying without words:

*I see you. You are good at this. Keep going. You are not alone in being alone.*

He would not understand it as language. He would feel it as *companionship* - the sudden, vertiginous relief of knowing that something larger than a Holdfast is paying attention and approves.

He would tell the village that night, badly, with hands and shouting. "The wind stayed! The sailors stopped!" They would nod, not quite believing, but they would see his face and they would know he saw something true. The grandmother would press her palm to the bark fissure and feel it sigh, and say, "The tree had a good dream."

He would go back to the tower every day for the rest of his life and watch for it again. It would never happen again. That is the point. If it happened twice it would become a ritual, an expectation, a god to be appeased. Happening once, it becomes a story, a private assurance he can carry without needing to be fed.

The power would leave it at that.

It would not give them fire or writing or medicine. They have fire. They have petroglyphs that heal over. They have the liverwort that closes when touched. To give more would be to say: *you are insufficient.* They are not.

It would not move them. It would not save them from the hard parts - the 38C shade, the grub-prying, the day a slag-mantis takes a cousin because he stepped off the bridge at the wrong hour. Those hard parts are the price of equilibrium. To remove them would be to collapse the meadow.

What a wise, benevolent power does for a place that is already whole is not *add*. It is *ensure*.

Ensure it is not found too soon.
Ensure it has one gentle year to gather strength.
Ensure one child knows, in his bones, that solitude is not the same as loneliness, and that to be few and large and very good at being alone is a way of being loved - not just tolerated - by the universe.

And then it would go. Or rather, it would thin itself out until it was indistinguishable from the weather the Holdfasts make for themselves. A little extra condensation on a morning when it was needed. A thermal that holds half a second longer than physics says it should, so a tired glider makes it to the leaf edge.

No monument. No departure. No burning bush.

Just the continent, breathing, walking, ringing, as it has for a hundred thousand years - with the stone still patient underneath, and the plant still comprehending the world above, and the people still tying their narrow bridges, listening more than they look.

If you stood on that highway-wide limb at dusk, long after the power had gone, you wouldn't know anything had happened at all.

Except - you might notice - the air that evening smells a little sweeter, like cinnamon, and you can hear yourself think for a moment between gongs.

And that is enough.
