It would not come like you did.

Not climbing. Not out of breath, not shouting to be heard over the roar. It would already be there the moment you looked away, the way cool air is already there before you feel it.

A benevolent and wise power does not arrive. It *notices*. And being noticed by a thing like that is different from being seen.

The first thing it would do is nothing.

It would sit on the raw lip where you sat - though it would not need to find a flat place, it would not be cut by the glass - and it would listen to the roar until the roar stopped being noise and became what it is: speech.

This place is not asking for help. That is the first thing a wise power hears that we do not.

We climb up and think *too loud, too empty, too cold, too unstable* - problems to solve. The pocket does not think it has problems. It is doing exactly what a newborn mountain water should do: churning violently while it decides whether it is allowed to exist. Its gravity is suggestible because it has not yet learned to obey. Its walls are sharp because they have not yet been forgiven by rain. Its emptiness is not a lack; it is cleanliness. It has swept its room and is waiting to see who can live in a clean room.

So the wise power would not fix it.

It would not quiet the churn. To quiet it would be to kill it. That percussive hammering on basalt *is* its breathing, its way of cooling the stone under it, its way of keeping the film of bacteria from smothering. If you made it still, it would become a mirror, and then it would become a puddle, and then it would be gone. The power loves its noise the way you love a child's loud, useless running in circles. It would leave the roar alone.

It would not fill it. That is the temptation for any benevolent thing - to see two translucent shrimp tumbling alone in thirty feet of pristine turbulence and think *they must be lonely, they must be hungry, let me stock this lake, let me make it abundant*. Abundance would murder them. They are built for scarcity. Their bodies are glass because there is nothing in the water to hide from. Their bioluminescence is useless here because they evolved in dark, and here the sun is white and honest. If you poured life in, you would cloud the water and bury the bacterial film and the white newt would starve under a bloom of algae it never asked for.

A wise power understands that some holiness is empty on purpose.

So what would it do?

Small things. Almost nothings. The kind of things you would miss if you blinked, and that is how you know they were done with love and not with power.

For the water itself, it would put one hand - if it has hands - just above the bulging meniscus. Not touching. Just remembering for it, for a little while, how to hold.

The pocket is holding itself together by wanting to. By a local, suggestible gravity that says *stay*. That wanting is tiring. Every sheet of water thrown five feet up hangs a half-second too long before it remembers to fall. The benevolent power would lend it a little certainty. Not a wall. Not a dam. Just a quiet agreement with the earth under it: *you may stay a little longer*. It would cool the deep basalt one degree slower, so the pocket doesn't seal itself shut next winter. It would open, invisibly, a hairline seep deep in the cylinder, so that the water lost to spray and sun is replaced drop by drop from the mountain's own cold blood. No inlet you could see. Just enough that the level never has to choose between overflowing and vanishing. It would give the place *time*. New mountains need time more than they need anything else.

For the stone, it would not soften it. It would not make soil where there is no soil. But in the one place where the white newt clings, where its too-long fingers splay and slip on the chisel-scored glass, the power would breathe once, warm and brief, on that single vertical score. And the glass would pit - microscopically - just there. Not smooth, but *toothed*. Enough for a toe to catch without peeling. And in three of the bubble-holes near the lichen, it would leave a dusting so fine you would think it was wind-blown - a pinch of feldspar and ash carried from a valley that *has* learned how to be soil, sterilized by its journey so it carries no invaders, just mineral, just food. The lichen would not grow faster. It would simply not die this winter. That is enough.

For the shrimp, it would do the kindest and strangest thing.

At noon, their light is useless. At dusk, when you have gone and the sun is thin, their blue-green pulse has no one to see it. The power knows they are not lonely the way we are lonely - they do not shoal, they do not call to each other - but it saw, because it watched for a long time, that twice in an hour their vortexes threw two of them within an arm's length and they pulsed at different times and missed each other entirely.

So as the light failed, the power would do what clouds do, but precisely. It would dim the air for thirty seconds. Not dark. Just dusk a little deeper, the white sun behind a veil it chose. And in that veil, for thirty seconds, the shrimp's light would be visible. One pulse. Answer. A second pulse, overlapping. They would see each other for the first time not as tumbling debris but as *other*. They would not mate that night. Maybe not ever. But the information would be in the water now: *you are not the only one riding*. A wise power does not force companionship. It makes companionship possible.

For the newt, it would ask nothing of it except to let it rest. The newt spends its whole life clinging and being torn off and swimming frantically to cling again, scraping that invisible bacterial lawn. The power would, for one hour after midnight when no human is there to see, still the strongest central vortex. Not still the whole pocket - the roar would remain - but calm the one column that keeps drilling the same patch of wall. In that hour the film would thicken, just a micron, just a feast. The newt would eat without being ripped away, and its pink gills would flutter slower, and it would close its dot eyes and simply hold, and not have to work. In the morning the churn would resume. But it would have had a full belly once. A wise power knows a single full belly can be the difference between persisting and vanishing for a creature that lives on the edge of impossible.

For the humans - for the ones who left the pine stakes and the cairn and the bowl with the smooth stone - it would do two things that seem like opposites.

First, it would steady their work. The rawhide lashing that has dried black and hard and is about to crack - it would ease, just a fraction, so it doesn't snap in the next wind. The four-high cairn that vibrates with every hammer-blow of water - it would settle the bottommost stone a millimeter deeper into its crack, so it holds. The bowl with the offering stone - it would keep the wind from filling it with grit, so the water in it stays clear. Not because the offering needs to be preserved like a museum. Because someone took the trouble to carry a smooth river stone up a raw mountain to say *I saw this*, and a benevolent thing honors effort. It says *I saw that you saw*.

Second, and more importantly, it would make the place slightly harder to find.

Not invisible. Not hidden by magic. Just - the next person who tries to describe how to get there will misremember one turn. The meadow before the basalt fist will look, from below, a little more like every other meadow. The faint boot track that was starting to form to the summit will be combed over by one hard rain that only falls on that track, because the power asked the clouds nicely. A wise power knows that the greatest danger to a newborn, loud, empty, perfect thing is not that no one will love it, but that *everyone* will. It would protect it from pilgrimage. It would keep it small enough to miss, as you said. It would keep it a secret that is too loud to keep, but still a secret.

And then it would leave the way it came - which is to say, it would not leave. It would stop noticing quite so closely, which for a thing like that is the same.

It would not be worshipped. If you came back the next day you would find nothing different except that your knees went numb a little slower, that you saw the shrimp pulse in daylight for a half-second and thought you imagined it, that the newt was still clinging where you left it instead of gone. You would think the mountain was being kind today. You would be right.

The pocket would keep churning. Deafening, cold, clear, suggestible, empty, alive. The lichen would persist. The grass would hold. The shrimp would ride. The newt would cling. The human would kneel, leave a stone, and go.

All of that would continue, because a wise benevolence does not want to make a wild place tame, or an empty place full, or a new place old. It wants to make a precarious place *slightly less precarious* - just enough that it gets to choose, for a little longer, what it will become when erosion finally, eventually, gives it a way down.

You pull your red hands out of the water, and for minutes afterward you can still feel how the water wanted to stack itself around your fingers.

If the power did its work well, you will still feel that tomorrow.
