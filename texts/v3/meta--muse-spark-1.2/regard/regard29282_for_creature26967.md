If a benevolent and wise power noticed this place, it would not arrive.

That is the first thing.

Something vast and kind, old enough to have watched plateaus cool and valleys learn to be valleys, does not descend. There is no pillar of light down the trough, no voice, no ship. That would be a violence here, where sound is waste and light is already too hard. The tenders would feel it as a sudden wind that shakes the dew off before it can be drunk.

So it would come by becoming slower, and higher, and quieter - until it was indistinguishable from attention itself.

It would sit, in the way such a power sits, on the sharp rim of the plateau where the cloud cuts itself. Not *above* the valley, but *with* it. For a long time it would do nothing that looks like doing. That is its wisdom.

Because to look at this valley properly you have to unlearn hurry. You have to let your heart beat at four times a minute. The power knows how.

So for a season - which for it might be a century, which for the tenders is one turning of the great wheel - it would just watch. And in watching, it would understand what it was seeing:

Not an abandoned factory being eaten by moss.

But a new kind of body learning how to be a body. The valley is the bowl. The mat is the skin. The machinery is the slow organs the tenders grew for themselves because basalt alone could not hold heat and water well enough. The tenders themselves are not *in* the ecology. They *are* the hands of it. Young, so young. The rocks still have their crystal hexagons. The moss has only just learned to be one agreement. The tenders have only just learned to oil a wheel. Everything here is first generation, first draft, still deciding what a valley is.

A lesser power - a kind but not wise one - would fix it. Would bring a river. Would make the light warmer, the air thicker, would give the tenders voices, would speed up the looms so they could make more muscle-fiber, would cure the strange jitter in the shadows.

A wise one sees that the strangeness *is* the health.

The cold that keeps the stone cold even at noon - that is what wrings the dew out of the sky. The thin air that carries sound poorly - that is what protects the dew from vibration. The slow time that makes water thicken and shadows stutter - that is not a wound in the valley. That is the valley conserving itself, learning to stretch a single day's water over a week, a single thought over a month. To smooth it would be to kill it with kindness.

So what would a benevolent and wise power do? Very little that the tenders would ever notice. Very much that makes it possible for them to keep not-noticing.

**1. It would veil.**

Not hide. Veil. The way the mat veils the stone - not to deceive, but to hold.

From the rim, the valley looks like a chisel cut. From orbit, it looks like a scar that could be mined, dammed, catalogued, explained. There are other powers that are hungry and not wise. There are humans with maps who love a straight line.

The benevolent one would not put a wall around it. Walls are noticed and therefore wanted. It would put a *misunderstanding* around it. A little fold in attention. Any eye that sweeps the plateau would find its gaze sliding off, finding the next ridge more interesting. Any satellite would log a sensor flicker exactly like the jitter of the gantry shadows and discard it as error. Any prospector who wandered near would feel, very gently, that early-autumn breath, and think *nothing lives there, too cold, too silent,* and turn back, and later not remember why.

It would not make the valley invisible. It would make it easy to leave alone.

**2. It would steady, without stopping.**

The time-jitter - the sun flickering a fraction of a degree - is the valley breathing unevenly, as all young things do. It is not dangerous yet. But left to stutter for eons it could tear, could make the dew forget when to materialize.

The power would not force time smooth. It would sit with the stutter the way you sit with a child learning to breathe while asleep. It would lend its own steadiness as a reference tone - a vast, slow metronome far below hearing. Not a correction. An accompaniment.

On the ground, it would feel like nothing. Only this: the green-held light under the terraces would glow a fraction less anxiously at night. The cup of water you left in the sun would still thicken - but it would thicken evenly. The seedlings that grow there overnight would root with one less contortion. The tenders would not know why their oil spreads a little easier this year. They would just oil the axle, and find it takes one less pass of the cilia.

**3. It would leave a gift that is not a gift.**

A wise benevolence does not give tools. It gives *margin.*

Once, at the deepest part of the trough where dew gathers last and evaporates first, where a juvenile tender the size of a stone has been waiting three days for its cistern to refill, the power would let the basalt sweat a little longer.

No miracle. Just an extra hour of condensation before dawn. Just enough that the pothole fills to the brim. The juvenile's bowl, lined with black cilia, would lower and drink, and its flank cisterns would swell clear, and it would have enough left over to carry to a dry patch of mat and pour, bead by bead.

The juvenile would not look up and thank anyone. It would simply not die that week. The mat in that patch would live. That is the whole gift.

Or maybe, on a night when all fifteen domes are gathered against the warm concrete under the amber bulbs, the power would make the bulbs burn one degree warmer, for one hour, with no extra draw on the humming transformers. Just warm enough that the moss on their backs photosynthesizes a little deeper, stores a little more. They would press closer, and share the heat, and their long, slow hearts would beat their four beats a minute with a little less effort.

They would think the building was just holding heat well tonight.

The power would ask nothing. It would not ask to be seen, or named, or worshipped. To be worshipped here would require sound and kneeling and breaking the mat.

Instead, it would do the most intimate thing a vast being can do for a small, slow one:

It would learn its tempo, and keep it.

For as long as this valley needs to become a valley - for the millennia it will take for talus to soften those sharp rims, for a river to finally decide to run, for the mat to finally thin and differentiate into many species that can afford to compete again - the power would remain at the rim, not as a guardian with a spear, but as a quiet that makes the silence safer.

If you walked the length of the valley on the afternoon after it had passed, you would notice nothing. Perhaps only this: the air would feel, absurdly, a little less lonely. Your footsteps on the mat would still be a dull thud. The big wheel would still take twenty minutes to turn. A tender would still brush your boot and leave a clean circle of water, and keep going.

But when you sat still until the cold came through the mat into your bones, and watched a dome tend its machine, you would feel - without knowing why - that it was proper to be slow, and proper to be quiet, and that you did not need to take a cutting, or a picture, or a souvenir cup of viscous water.

You would leave the cup where it was. And somewhere, under leaves, a clutch would root.

And that would be the whole intervention.
