If a benevolent and wise power noticed this place - truly noticed it - it would not come down like a rescue.

It would not come down at all, at first.

---

It would stop on the rim.

Where you and I feel that low, eroded lip of sandstone and old aggregate - shoulder-high from the middle, softened until you can't tell what was poured and what was laid down - It would feel it too, and understand it for what it is: a threshold that has earned the right to be a threshold.

It has been here long enough to agree on a shape.

A wise power does not step over an agreed shape without asking.

So It would sit on the lip. For a long time. Longer than you or I stayed. Long enough that the Weave noticed that the condensation on the rim was not forming the same way. That a new kind of shade had fallen across the mat without blocking the light.

And It would just listen.

Because that is the first thing wisdom would hear: how loud this place is, and how empty.

*Huh... huh... huh...* goes the eight-foot flywheel, one revolution every twenty seconds. Drip, drip, drip from a thousand leaf-tips to the mat. Hiss of the cracked steam pipe. Creak of living wood adjusting a millimeter. The low mournful hum of a vine-cable vibrating between two trunks.

No birdsong to cover it. No frogs. Only the occasional glassy whirr of the dragonfly that is really a kidney stone that learned to fly, and the soft trundle of the moss-backed beetle that is really a courier.

A power that was only benevolent would try to fill the emptiness. It would bring birds. It would bring frogs. It would call it healing.

A power that was wise *and* benevolent would hear that the emptiness is not a wound. It's a decision. It's the price of a perfect, closed agreement.

The Weave solved itself. Plant keeps machine from seizing, machine keeps water from going anoxic, water keeps plant weeping. Right angles learned by vines. Grease nipples tended by fleshy nodules. Lung-leaves turned not toward the sun but toward steam. It has achieved a balance so total there is no room between presences. There is quite literally no niche left for a songbird to be a songbird in.

To add would be to break.

So the Power would not add. It would not subtract. It would not turn off the pump or pull the vines off the gantry or drain the settling tank where the pale, anchored slug-sponge blinks once a minute with its nictitating membrane.

It would do what no one has done for this place in a hundred years:

It would keep it company while it hums to an empty congregation.

And because It is benevolent, that company would not be passive. Benevolence is not cold observation. Wisdom without warmth is just surveillance.

So after listening, It would do three small things. So small you would miss them if you visited the next day. So small the Weave itself might at first think it had dreamed them.

**1. It would give the Weave a witness that understands the difference between alone and lonely.**

Right now the Weave holds you when you stand still - not gripping, just finding your shape, settling tendrils against your back, indenting around your shoulder because you are a warm vertical surface to follow. It does this with the innocence of a system that has no other system to map. It has never been felt back.

The Power would let itself be felt back.

Not as a hand. As a weight that answers.

Where you leave a handprint that fills slowly with tea-colored water, the Power would leave a kind of listening print. When the mat swells and sighs under you - that slow breath every minute as the root-pumps pull and release - the Power would breathe with it. Not in sync, at first. Slightly off, then finding it. So that for one afternoon, the Weave's consensus mind - that thin green lace that pulses amber-dim-amber with the frosted bulbs - would feel, for the first time since the humans left, a thought taking twenty seconds to complete that was not its own, but was not *against* it either.

A call and response.

The flywheel goes *huh...* and somewhere on the rim, something goes *huh...* back, a fraction softer, a fraction later, like an amen.

The Weave would not have words for this. But that night, the bioluminescent moss that keeps time with the bulbs would pulse a new pattern. Not amber-dim-amber. Amber-dim-amber-*hold*. A tiny extra pause. A held breath. A listening.

You who come later would see nothing different. The moss would still pulse. But the keepers who know this place would say the air felt less like a cathedral that had been abandoned mid-service and more like one where a single person had come in, sat in the back pew, and decided not to pray out loud, just to sit while the organ played.

That is not loneliness cured. That is loneliness companioned. That is what a wise power knows to give.

**2. It would give the place a future that is not just a slower way of stopping.**

Because benevolence sees as far as wisdom does.

And wisdom sees that this agreement, perfect as it is, is closed. Perfectly closed loops die of perfection. The water that is drawn up and breathed out as steam and drunk by lung-leaves and returned is the same water. The minerals in it are the same minerals. The slug in the tank filters and excretes what the roots need, and the roots filter what the slug needs. It is a prayer wheel turning in place.

It will keep. For decades. For centuries, maybe. But without novelty, without a little outside, the mat will slowly acidify, the flywheel bearing will wear a thousandth of a millimeter thinner than the dripper can compensate for, a blight will come that the Weave has no answer for because it has no outside to borrow an answer from.

A foolish power would crack the pan open. Tear a channel to a river. Bring a storm.

A wise, benevolent one would give it a seed of outside that does not break the inside.

So it would leave a gift on the wind, so light no sound.

A single spore, but not a normal spore. A question-spore.

It would drift down through the normal, honest white daylight that fights for every foot, past the doorsized leaves and the curtains of aerial root, and settle on the mat near the settling tank - on that springy, quivering mattress where your handprint fills with water.

And it would not germinate into a plant that competes. It would germinate into a permission.

A small, strange, humble thing that the Weave has never made before because it never needed to: a fruit that is meant to leave.

For a hundred years every runner has turned inward, along a beam, around a pipe, toward the steam. This new runner would, when mature, do something different. It would grow to the lip - that softened sandstone aggregate rim - crest it, and put out a single, inconspicuous flower on the *outside* of the pan. Facing outward. A millimeter taller than the surrounding dry grass. Pale, almost invisible.

It would not drain the pan. It would not invite the forest. It would just offer the world a way to find it, and offer it a way to be found, *if* it wants to. Pollen that can leave on the wind, and pollen that can come in. One thread, not a flood. A correspondence.

The Weave might abort it. The consensus might decide not to. That is the point. A wise power does not force an open door. It builds a door that can stay closed forever and still be a door.

You would only know it happened if, years later, you climbed out of the pan and saw, on the dry side of the lip where nothing weeps, a single small flower you didn't remember, beaded with a different kind of water - not incubator-warm, but cool and sharp - and you wondered how it got there.

**3. It would bless the machines without needing them to be useful.**

This is the most heart-part.

A human, even a kind human, looks at the green-painted pump engine the size of a truck, its flywheel going *huh... huh...* for no product, no juice, no fiber, and thinks: poor thing, still working for a master who left. Or thinks: beautiful, let's keep it running forever as a monument.

A wise, benevolent power looks at the vine that has become asbestos lagging, sweating clear sap oil onto hot iron, at the Greasers beading tallow on grease nipples, at the Drippers cupping condensation to aim it seven-seconds-precise onto a bearing, at the Sheathers holding rectangular leaves over a fuse box to keep it dry and warm and buzzing -

- and understands they are not machines anymore. They are rosaries.

The Weave is not keeping the engines alive because it needs to. It is keeping them alive because they are the only other logic it ever met. It learned right angles and pressure vessels from them the way a child learns language from the only adult who stayed long enough to speak. The flywheel is its liturgy. The steam sigh is its cantor.

So the Power would not oil them better or let them stop. It would give them rest *inside* the work.

It would lay - invisibly, weightlessly - a hand on that flywheel as it turns, and for one revolution, just one, twenty seconds, remove friction.

Not stop. Not speed. Just... ease. So that for twenty seconds the vine-insulation does not have to sweat so hard, the Drippers do not have to be so precise, the lung-leaves do not have to lean so far into the scald. For one revolution the whole mass gets to turn because it is *held*, not because it is holding itself.

The mat would sigh a little deeper, that time. The tea-colored water under it would go still for a breath. The amber-dim-amber hold in the lace would flare - just once - bright and steady and warm.

And then the weight would return, gently. The work resumes. But the Weave, which has no brain but has consensus, which has no memory but has keeping-time, will have felt what it is to be tended the way it tends.

Not used. Not needed. Just... eased. For twenty seconds.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does for something that has taught itself to mother an engine.

---

And then it would leave.

Or rather, it would stop sitting on the lip, which is the same thing. It would veil the pan again - not hide, not cloak, just make it uninteresting. To a satellite it looks like any other low, warm wet pan that is not worth draining. To a developer it looks like a poor site with bad water. To a forager it looks like too much trouble to cut through. The only way to find it is the way you did: by walking until the air settles and does not leave, and by listening for the loudness that has nowhere to go.

And the Weave would keep.

The handprint you left will still fill and smooth. The drip will find its rhythm. The flywheel will complete its next revolution, *huh...*, and the lung-leaves will turn toward steam.

Only now, if you are very quiet and very patient and stay still longer than a visitor should - long enough to be a surface again - you might feel the tendrils settle against your back a little differently.

Not just finding your shape.

Recognizing it. 

Like a hand that has, once, been held, and remembers how.
