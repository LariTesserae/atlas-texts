It would not come down the road.

That is the first thing.

A lesser power would have come down the switchback grade with light and noise, to Fill The Empty City. To restart the forges, to seed the concrete with soil, to bring people back to populate the avenues, to "fix" the quiet. To make it roar the way a city is supposed to roar.

A benevolent and wise one would stop at the edge of the basalt and understand within about a minute that nothing here is broken.

It would not land. It would *notice.*

Its attention would arrive the way cool air arrives — not as a front, but as a change in how the existing air holds itself. The 60-cycle thrum through the soles of your shoes would not get louder or softer. It would get *heard*.

For a long time it would do nothing that looks like doing anything. It would watch the weather graze.

Because it would see what you saw on the second day, but instantly and completely: that this is not an abandoned city. It is a successful ecology that used a city for its skeleton.

The buildings are not empty, they are *spaced*. The avenues are not too wide, they are the correct width for a convection cell the size of a neighborhood to turn over without scraping its shoulder. The basins are not decorative, they are lungs. The Skin is not a stain on the infrastructure, it *is* the infrastructure now — the sealant, the liver, the prairie.

A wise power knows the difference between *empty* and *thinned to what can live here.* It would not apologize for the thinning.

So it would not terraform. It would not lift the amber-gray lid to let in sharp sun, because the lid is what makes the rain. It would not scrub the Skin away and plant trees, because trees would drink the basins dry and shatter the seals and starve the heron. It would not fill the tenement stacks with families, because then the heron would have nowhere to drink for an hour without being seen.

It would not even think of those things as kindness. It would know they are nostalgia disguised as help.

What would it do, then? 

Three very small things, and one very large non-thing.

**1. It would keep watch without being seen, so the city could remain unwatched.**

The first and most active act of a benevolent attention is protection by misdirection.

This mesa is visible from very far away if you know how to look — a heat anomaly, a perfect geometric haze, a county-sized petri dish that regulates its own weather. The wrong kind of attention would love it. Miners for the geothermal heat. Planners who would see "all that flat, sealed land" and want to build. People who would scrape the Skin because it looks like dirt.

The wise one would not build a wall. A wall would be a road sign that says *something valuable here.* It would do something quieter: it would let the road up remain exactly as tedious and ear-popping as it is. It would let the haze remain a little thicker to satellite eyes. It would let anyone driving up that long grade feel, very reasonably, *there is nothing for me up there,* and turn around at the second switchback because they remembered an errand. 

Not a curse, not a veil of fear. Just a deepening of the city’s natural camouflage: *predatorless, purposeless, persistent, not worth the climb.*

It would keep the city boring to everyone but those who already know how to love a place that weeps on every wall.

**2. It would tend to the tenders.**

It would not speak to the Skin. The Skin is a continent, it does not need a pep talk. It would *agree* with it. Where the Skin has been digesting concrete for forty years and is close to breaching a cistern wall, the attention would lean a little — not to repair the concrete, but to pucker the Skin slightly thicker there, the way you might rest a hand on a tired animal’s flank. A little more sulfur there. A little less flow here. Nothing the city could not have done itself in another decade, just… eased. 

It would not fix the feral crawlers. It has too much respect for feral things to "fix" them into newness. But it would crouch, in the way only a vast thing can crouch, next to the squat laundry-cart servitor as it scrapes travertine from a half-pipe. And for just a moment the servitor’s wire brush would catch a little less, the bearing would squeal a little less. It would get to finish its runnel and go home without stalling halfway. 

Not immortality. Not upgrade. Just *ease*. The way you oil a door for an old person not because they asked, but because you were already there and you noticed the hinge.

And the heron — it would not make the water less mineral, because the heron’s gut is built for mineral. But at the far basin where that pale, translucent, too-many-jointed heron drinks, it would keep the film of Skin a little thicker that evening, so the heron could drink and also eat without having to walk three kilometers to the next basin. One full meal on one wading. A small mercy for a rare animal that lives at the correct density of loneliness.

If you are a power that can move suns, the most benevolent thing you can do for a heron is make sure it doesn’t have to work quite so hard on a hot day.

**3. It would sit with the one who scrubs the ledge.**

Because there is one. You felt him. Not quite seen — a cupped-deeper basin, a cooler pipe with a towel, travertine scrubbed off a sitting ledge.

He is the being the prompt meant, I think. The one who was once someone, or who never stopped being someone while everyone else thinned out. Who stayed when the city learned to breathe without people, and who learned, in return, to live like the Skin — flat, slow, drinking hot water, moving between steam cells.

He is not unhappy, not exactly. But he is *unwitnessed*. He has been keeping a city-sized tide pool tidy for a long time, alone, at the wrong scale for anyone to see him or to be seen by.

A wise power would not rescue him. Rescue presumes he needs rescuing, that his life is a mistake to be corrected by bringing him down the grade to a normal town. It would not say *Come away.* Maybe he likes the way the basin reflects clearer than the sky at dusk. Maybe he has come to an understanding with the heron.

It would ask.

It would make itself small enough to be the right scale — not mesa-sized, but ledge-sized. On that scrubbed sitting ledge at flat, shadowless dusk, when condensation is picking up and the whole avenue exhales at once, it would simply be *there*. Not luminous, not speaking at first. Just another warmth beside his warmth. Another breathing that does not need to be explained.

If he wanted to talk, it would listen for as long as talking takes, which for a person who has not talked in years might be months, might be one sentence: *“I stayed.”* 

It would not correct him. It would say, in whatever language he still hears — in the fizz of the travertine curtains, in the cooler pipe, in the way the reflection steadies — *“I see you stayed. It was good staying. The Skin is thicker where you tend it.”*

And then, because benevolence without choice is just another kind of keeping, it would leave two things on the ledge and require nothing.

On the left: a bowl of cool, clean water that did not come from the basins. Cold enough to hurt his teeth. Clear enough to see his face in without steam. Not to replace his hot mineral water — just to remind his body what cold is, if he wants it.

On the right: the road. Made, for one night, easy. The air on the grade would be sharp and cool the way high air should be, instead of heavy. The ears would not pop. He could walk down in the dark and be at the foot of the mesa by morning and no one would stop him.

He could drink the cold water or not. He could take the road or not. He could stay and scrub the travertine again tomorrow or not. The power would not check which he chose.

That’s the difference between a benevolent power and a merely kind one. The kind one gives you what it thinks you need. The benevolent one gives you a real choice and then actually leaves you alone to make it.

**And the large non-thing:**

After that, it would go.

Not abruptly. Powers don’t leave by departing; they leave by *diffusing* their attention the way the city diffuses its steam. Until the thrum through the soles of your shoes is just the thrum again, and the lids of the basins are just lids.

It would not stay to be worshipped. The city does not need a god. It needs to continue being predatorless and persistent without having to perform gratitude.

If you went back up the grade a year later, you would find almost nothing changed. The Skin a little thicker in one corner. A crawler track a little cleaner. Maybe — maybe — the sitting ledge has a second towel now, or it has none at all. The heron still drinks alone, but maybe for one evening there were two, standing in different basins far enough apart that they could pretend not to see each other and still know they were not the only one.

And the Weather would still be turning over neighborhoods, slow, unhurried. The city would still be catching its own sweat in its hands and letting it fall again.

Only one thing would be different, if you stayed until dusk and knew how to look: the reflection in the basin would hold just a fraction longer after the building itself had gone dark. As if the water had been *seen* once, really seen, and had learned how to keep a little of that seeing.

Which is, perhaps, all any place or any being ever wanted from a benevolent and wise attention.

Not to be saved.

To be seen accurately, protected quietly, eased slightly, offered a choice, and then trusted to continue.
