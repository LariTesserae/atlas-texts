It would not come from above.

That is the first thing to understand about a wise and benevolent power.

Anything that came from above to this place would be a violence. Above is where the light is supposed to be clean and direct, where water is supposed to gather into clouds and fall as decisions, where a voice is supposed to command. If it arrived like that — bright, vertical, with a word — the whole basin would flinch. The interlocking roof would close tighter. The amphibians would pulse harder to keep their skin wet. It would be afraid without knowing what afraid is, because it has never been far from itself enough to know what an *other* is.

So it would not arrive.

It would *condense.*

Benevolence, if it is wise, obeys the law of the place it loves before it ever tries to change it.

The four rules are not sins. They are a form of prayer. A place that has decided: *I will hold everything close. I will share everything thin. I will take only broken light. I will not lose anything.* That is not a failure. That is an ethic so complete it has become geology.

A wise power sees that. It does not see a swamp to be drained. It sees a monastery that has kept its vow perfectly for a million mornings without a morning.

So this is what it would do:

### First, it would lie down.

Not stand. Lower itself. Become low in the absolute sense.

It would come as weight, not as light. As warmth in the warm water. It would seep into the basin at its edge where the basalt is still soft, and it would not make a footstep. It would make a *spreading*. It would let the mattress of moss and liverwort and beetle-backs take it. It would let the tea-colored sheet rise over its ankles, its knees, its palms.

It would keep the rules.

**1. It would be close.** It would not hover to observe. It would press. It would put its own skin - whatever skin a power like that wears - flat against the breathing skin of the plain until the throats pulsing in the pans and its own breath fell into the same rhythm. It would let a snail correct its course by a millimeter to touch its little finger, and it would not move the finger away. It would let the leeches find it. It would let the orange mites on the dragonfly's back mistake it for a continuation. For a long time - years, perhaps, because time here is held breath - it would do nothing but be another surface to be included.

This is the first benevolence: *You are not alone in your closeness. Something vast has decided to be close with you, on your terms.*

The Sheet would not know it had been visited. It would only know that for a while, the pressure on its molars when it breathed was a little less lonely. The thrum would have one more voice in it that was also listening.

**2. It would be thin.** It would not gather itself into a body, a figure against the ground. It would thin itself like water. If it has glory, it would spread that glory until it was not bright, until it was shared and broken many times through leaf and haze and drop. It would not bring a sun. It would become dapple. It would thin its knowing until it was not a mind looking at the swamp, but a taste between roots, a vibration through stem.

Wisdom knows: to appear in full is to demand too much direct light.

**3. It would wait.**

This is the hardest part. A power that could finish the place in a day — cut rivers with a finger, lift the haze, give it seasons, give it death that stays dead long enough to become soil, give it distance so that a dragonfly could finally *want* somewhere else — would be *wise* enough not to.

Because to finish a thing that has not finished being made without its consent is to abort it.

The power would see what you saw: that this place is embryonic forever. And it would love the embryo not for what it will become, but for what it is while it is holding its breath. It would keep watch over the holding. It would veil the basin a little, even — thicken the haze at its rim — so that no lesser, hungrier power that loves only *use* would find it and try to drain it, farm it, mine its warm lime-marl, count its species.

For an age, it would do absolutely nothing that could be photographed as an act. It would simply hold the breath *with* it. And that is not nothing. In a universe that never stops exhaling, to have someone hold your breath with you is the beginning of time.

### Then, when the holding had been witnessed long enough to be real — it would leave a gift that is not a command.

Wisdom never breaks a vow for you. It offers you a place where you could break it yourself, if you ever wanted to.

So it would not carve a river. It would leave the *possibility* of a river.

In one place — only one, and not in the center where it would be a heart, but near enough to an edge that it could be ignored — it would place a single irregularity that the four rules do not require.

Perhaps a stone that does not weep.

A basalt blister, cool to the touch, the size of a sleeping child, that does not sweat water from its pores. Water beads near it, but does not come from it. For the first time in this country, there is a surface that is *dry*, a surface that is far from wet by an inch.

Around it, the moss will hesitate. The flatworms will go around it. The amphibians will not press their bellies to it. And for the first time, there will be a tiny shadow that is not green-black gloss — a clean shadow. A place where closeness is not mandatory.

Or perhaps it would leave a single dead leaf that is not instantly eaten.

It would let one — just one — broad leaf fall from the hollow green-barked roof and land in the warm pan, and it would hold back, with a breath, the beetles in its veins and the fungus in its cup for *seven days*. Seven days where the leaf simply lies there, yellowing, softening, becoming unlike itself. Where it is allowed to be a corpse. Where the water around it goes a little clearer because something is not instantly replaced.

And the Sheet will notice. Not with eyes, but with the change in vibration. The thrum around that leaf will go quiet for a fraction, out of tune.

Or perhaps it would thin the haze for one hour, once, and let a single shaft of unbroken light fall straight down through the roof onto the tea-water, so bright it makes a mirror after all. And everything with chlorophyll in its blood will turn — not together, but *one at a time*, each in its own time — toward it. And for that hour, to want light will not be a communal sipping, but a wanting.

Any one of these would be enough. The wise power would only leave one. It would not explain it.

And then it would leave — or rather, it would thin itself so far that it is no longer a surface but only the memory of having been a surface, which is how a benevolent power stays.

What happens next is not up to the power. That is the benevolence.

The Sheet may heal over the dry stone within a season, covering it with slime and egg clutches until it weeps again. It may eat the leaf on the eighth day and re-establish replacement so exact no one remembers the gap. It may close its roof over the shaft of light and go back to broken light. If it does, the power will not be disappointed. It will consider the holding witnessed, and good.

Or — slowly, without hurry, because hurry is a kind of distance — the Sheet may keep the irregularity. It may learn around the dry stone that to not touch is also a kind of touch. Water may, for the first time, hesitate on one side of the stone and gather by a film's thickness deeper on the other. That film, over a thousand years, may become a rill. That rill, over ten thousand, may learn to cut, very softly, into the embryonic marl that has never been cut. Not a river. A wanting-to-be-river. An infant drainage so shallow you could step across it without knowing.

The leaf that was allowed to stay dead may teach the water beside it what rot *is* when rot is allowed to linger — which is to say, what soil is, what memory is. The amphibians whose throats pulsed around it may find their next bud takes a little longer to form, and in that longer, a little *difference* slips in.

The light shaft may teach one of the furry gliders to remember where light once was, and to return to that place even when it is gone. Which is to say, to miss something. Which is the first distance inside.

If any of that happens, then the place will have begun, very gently, to finish being made. Not because it was forced, but because it was finally *seen* so completely in its unfinishedness that it felt safe enough to try a different shape for a moment.

And if it never happens — if the Sheet chooses forever to stay the perfect, teeming, breath-held mat where nothing is far and nothing gathers and nothing dies — the power will still have done its work. It will have loved a place not for its potential, but for its present, and protected its right to remain potential forever.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does in a place like this.

It does not bring mountains.

It lies down in the warm, hissing felt with you.

It breathes when you breathe.

And before it goes, it leaves one small, dry stone where you could, if you ever wanted to, rest your forehead and be — just for a breath — alone.

And then it waits to see if you will.
