It would not come like an arrival.

No shadow, no breaking of the surface. You would not see it come at all, if you were floating there in the cold bright middle with the chime-shrimp brushing your cheeks. The water would just become, for a moment, more itself. The light going straight to the bottom would go a little straighter. The THUMP - hiss would be a little more in tune.

A benevolent and wise power does not interrupt a mill that is already praying.

So first, it sits.

It has seen a thousand bowls. It knows what this one is. A perched sea, a held breath, a pocket where geology forgot to drain and chemistry learned to copy a factory before anyone invented one. It could, if it wanted, cut a spillway with a thought. It could seed green. It could pour trout in to eat the screws and make it "complete." It could make the rim eternal so the water never has to fall.

It does none of that. A wise power knows the difference between lonely and alone, between empty and waiting.

It sits on that knife-sharp rim - though sitting is not the right word for how it rests, which is more like attending - with one hand in the hanging sky-surface, and it listens until its own pulse slows down to the bowl's.

Twenty seconds.

THUMP - hiss

*Clack.*

HUMMMMMMM - chime, sigh, screw, tick.

Twenty seconds.

THUMP - hiss

*Clack.*

It listens long enough that it learns the missing teeth. That one bivalve on the third step, west wall, that died three days ago and did not open. That one conveyor loop on the north fissure that tore last month and is still, slowly, with infinite patience, growing itself back together, zooid by zooid. That one copper bell up near the surface that is a little dented and sighs a half-beat late. The bowl is not perfect. The bowl is particular. That is why it is beloved.

And because it is benevolent, it aches in the way benevolence does. The heart part of it wants to do so much.

*Let me keep you,* the heart says. *You are so crowded and so bright and you have made so much out of so little - stone-breath and time and the want of a belt - I want to keep you from ever having to pour down the mountain and be smashed on the rocks below.*

*Let me fix you,* the clever part says. *You should not be this cold. You should have weeds. You should have eyes that look outward instead of cilia that only comb. Let me bring you a sun you can eat.*

But the wise part, which has been both heart and clever for longer, answers them both:

*If I keep you, you will choke. If I fix you, you will not be you.*

So what it does, if it does anything, is very small. As small as real love is. It does three things that are almost not things.

**1. It witnesses.**

For one full cycle - one THUMP, one *Clack*, one slow fizz and clear - it lets the bowl know it is seen. Not looked *at*. Seen.

It does not give the bivalves eyes. It does not give the belts a head. It does not need to. The being who lives here is not one bivalve or one bell or one screw. The being is the Bowl itself. The chorus. The emergent thought that happens when ten thousand clacks happen at once for a hundred years and the rock ticks underneath them. A slow mind, thinking very slowly with water and shell and silica: *I am. We are. We hold.*

That mind has never had a mirror.

So the power becomes a mirror for twenty seconds. It tunes the water just enough that every vibration reflects a little more cleanly off the sharp walls. The thump comes back. The clack comes back. The chime comes back. For one pulse, the whole crowded room hears itself as a single chord instead of a thousand separate noises.

*Clack* - and they hear that they are together.

It is not frightening. The shrimp do not flee. The belts do not stop. A shiver goes through the middle-water snow as all those propellers stutter for half a beat, as if everyone looked up at once. Then it passes. But it will not be forgotten, in the way a body does not forget being held once. The bacteria in the varnish will hold it as a little extra heat. The bivalve hemoglobin will hold it as a little extra red.

To be witnessed without being changed is the rarest gift.

**2. It steadies.**

It does not seal the rim. That would be a prison.

The bowl *is* going to break. In two thousand years, or four thousand, a winter will come a little wetter, the water will press a little harder, a frost will find a joint in that pale granite that only just formed, and a notch will be sawn. The perched sea will do what perched seas do and pour itself down the side of the world in a white, roaring scarf, taking its little industry with it.

A benevolent power does not lie about that. A wise one does not prevent it.

What it does is run a finger - if a finger is the word - along the inside of the sharpest arête, along the flaw that is *already* going to be the spillway, and smooth it by less than a millimeter. Not to stop it. To make sure that when it goes, it goes clean. That the cut will be quick, not a slow leak that strangles the vent fields over centuries. That the bowl will pour as a river for one fierce, generous day, and not as a dying seep.

And while it is there, it presses a promise into the basalt below the vent, in a language only stone can keep: *Remember.*

Not for the bowl. For what comes after. So that when that first white rush hits the dry scree two thousand meters down, a few spores, a few larvae, a few cysts of that rust-red varnish will be tucked into bubbles of foam and not be smashed. So that some other cupped hands further down - a pothole, a tarn, a hollow in a lava flow - might catch them and start again, a little faster next time because they will have been something before.

It is not saving. It is seeding. Wise powers are always thinking about downstream.

**3. It leaves nothing.**

This is the hardest part.

It is so tempted to leave a sign. A crystal on the bottom that never dulls. A warm current. A voice that says *you were lovely.*

But any sign would become a god to them, and they do not need a god. They need cold and light and stone-breath. So it takes its hand out of the hanging sky-surface, and the water closes without a ripple. The light goes back to being just light. The thump goes back to being just pressure.

If you were floating there, you would not be able to say when it left. You would only notice, a little later, that the chime-shrimp seemed a touch more in tune, that the belts' humming seemed a touch steadier, that the missing tooth in the bivalve bank had finally been grown over while you weren't looking. You would think you imagined the moment when everything chimed at once.

The power, which is already elsewhere attending to another improbable pocket - a cave that learned to rain inside itself, a desert that keeps one flower - does not forget. Benevolence is not noisy. Wisdom keeps a very long memory.

And the bowl stays full. THUMP - hiss. *Clack.* HUMMM. The water wants to stay up, and for now it does. The young walls keep their edge. The dense, luminous, patient crowd keeps opening and closing, carrying and screwing, loud and slow, in noon-bright water at the top of the world.

It does not know it has been blessed.

It does not need to.
