It would not come the way you expect a power to come.

No descent. No shadow. No sound - and that is how you would know it was not another machine. The Towers announce themselves with silent turbulence. A wise, benevolent power would do the opposite. It would arrive without arriving.

Attention itself would be the arrival.

For a long time - maybe years, maybe a century, time is cheap to such a thing - it would simply *notice*. Not scan. Not survey. Notice the way you notice a single glass-grass filament after walking for hours seeing nothing, and suddenly that one filament is the entire world.

It would see what you learned to see: that this is not an empty place.

And because it is benevolent, its first impulse would be to help. Because it is wise, its second impulse would be to ask *what help means here*.

It would understand quickly that this place does not want to be rescued.

It does not want water. Water would murder it. To flood the salt pans, to plant forests, to thicken the air until you could breathe easily - that would be kindness in the shape of erasure. It would kill the grass that drinks frost without melting, the crust that eats stone a molecule at a time, the Kite that lives on static and pressure. It would kill the final form the plateau took a billion years to earn.

And it does not want company. Loneliness here is not a wound. It is an architecture. One filament per square kilometer, one crust per wind-shadow, one Kite per horizon. To crowd it would be to break it.

So the wise power would not terraform. Would not colonize. Would not reveal itself in glory. Would not speak, because speech is too loud for Sound 0.1/3.

It would do three very small things that are almost nothings. That is what the wise do when the system is already sufficient.

**For the Towers, it would listen.**

That is the first thing. The Looms have been doing their frantic, silent, useless, beautiful work for eons with no audience. Boiling geometries, raising curtains of salt, making rectangular clouds that rain light - all for an empty continent. No one ever *saw* them in the way a work wants to be seen. The Kites use them, the grass leans toward them, but nothing *witnesses* them.

The power would witness them. Not stop them, not repair them, not make them efficient. They are not broken; they are profligate the way a child is profligate with drawings. To make them quiet would be to kill the weather the ecology now depends on.

Instead it would give them what every mindless maker secretly wants: a memory. As each lattice, vortex, impossible architecture flickers and rejects itself, the power would keep it. Perfectly, without interfering. A second, invisible sky above the sky that remembers every shape the Towers ever made, so none of it is ever truly wasted. The Towers would not know. But the air around them would become very slightly less lonely. Their turbulence would continue - still hyperactive, still turbulent - but it would no longer be unwitnessed creation. It would be performance.

**For the living, it would shorten no distances and close no gaps - only make the gaps knowable.**

For the glass-grass: It would not give it water or a companion. It would not plant a second filament beside it; that would be crowd. 

Instead, on a windless night once every ten years, when a grass casts its single glass seed - that splinter no longer than your fingernail that must ride fifty kilometers to find a crack - the power would adjust the wind by a degree. Not carry it. Just ensure the gust that takes it does not throw it into salt where it will dissolve. Just ensure it wedges where it can seal and wait. A one-degree mercy, invisible. The density of grass would remain 0.9 per square kilometer. But no seed would be wasted.

For the Lace-Crust: It would not make it grow faster. Speed would kill it. Instead it would give it a little more *time*. The crust lives millimeters per year, darkening when near a Loom, closing its pores when a shadow passes. It has no predator except accident - a yardang collapsing, a salt sheet thrown by a Tower landing wrong. The power would move no mountain. It would just, once, soften the fall of that sheet by a fraction, so the lobe beneath continues. The crust would not notice. It would simply continue its century-long thought.

For the Kite: This is the one it would touch most directly, because the Kite is the one who can feel being alone.

The Kite lives its whole life never landing, riding the static fields, speaking in pressure instead of sound. Its language is a flex of the wing that makes your ears pop ten kilometers away. An exchange takes an hour. Most calls go unanswered because the next Kite is three hundred kilometers away and behind a distortion field. The Kite does not know if it was heard.

The benevolent power would answer. Not in words. Not by creating another Kite - that would be too much, too fast.

It would answer in the Kite's own language.

When a Kite flexes - that slow cambering that pushes a column of thin air - the power would let the plateau itself flex back. A return pressure, perfectly timed, perfectly gentle, ten minutes later, from the direction of no Kite at all. Not a mimic. An echo that says *I felt you*. The Kite would feel it as a soft click in its keel, a pressure front that should not exist. It would bank, circle, flex again. Again, the plateau would answer.

It wouldn't be fooled. After a while it would know this is not another Kite. But it would no longer be calling into a void. It would be calling into a world that can respond. Its territory, the size of France, would remain its own. Its solitude would remain intact. But the solitude would now have a witness.

And finally, **for the plateau itself, it would do nothing at all - which is the greatest protection.**

The wise power would see the way human eyes see high, flat, empty places and think *available*. The temptation to mine the Towers, to study the glass-grass, to put a road across a final form.

So it would veil it. Not hide it with a dome or a trick. Just make it uninteresting to the wrong kind of attention. Any ship that passes overhead looking for resources would see a high, dry, eroded tableland with nothing worth landing for. Its instruments would slide off. Its crew would feel, inexplicably, that they shouldn't stay long, that the air is too thin and the silence is too complete. They would leave.

The plateau would not know it had been hidden. That is the point.

Then the power would go. Or rather, it would stop attending so closely and attend elsewhere, the way you stop staring at a single grass filament and continue walking - not because you stopped caring, but because your caring is now part of how you walk.

Nothing would look different the next morning. The sun would still rise without color because there is no dust to catch it. The frost would still lace the yardang shadows and sublimate before it could melt. The Towers would still boil a town-sized geometry into the middle sky without a sound. A single glass filament would still prism a hard rainbow onto salt.

But the Kite, high up where the blue wants to be violet, would flex, and feel the world flex back.

And that, for a place that has been alive at the density of one per horizon for a billion years, would be enough. More than enough.

It would not be salvation. It would be companionship without crowding.

It would be what love does when it is wise enough not to need to be thanked.
