It would not come down.

That is the first thing.

A lesser power would have descended. A curious power, an hungry power, a power that wanted to *help* in the way hands help by picking things up and turning them over - it would have slid over the lip, boots in the pooled light, hands on the warm, blistered walls, saying *let me see you*. It would have meant well. It would have displaced so much air.

The wise one doesn't.

It finds the place the way you did - not by searching. The plain is flat for kilometers, wind-scoured basalt and thin lichen, and there is no reason to stop. But there is a place where the wind catches differently. Where the air a few inches above the stone is a fraction cooler, as if the earth is exhaling through a crack it forgot to close. Most walk over it. The wise one stops. Kneels.

It does not look down right away. It puts its palm on the flat beside the lip and feels how new the stone still is underneath the lichen. How it hasn't decided to be hard yet.

And then it looks.

And it understands immediately what it is seeing, because a benevolent and wise power has seen a lot of finished things. Finished planets, finished people, finished wars. It knows what finished costs.

This is an *unfinished* place. And that is not a flaw. That is its function.

It stays at the lip for a long time. Long enough that the mist exhales once without it moving. Long enough that the light in the pit, which has weight, begins to pool a little higher, curious about this new cool shadow at the rim.

It does not count. Counting is the first mistake. It does not say *three tenders* or *eleven amphibians*. It watches how they allot solitude and it feels, with a pang so old it aches, recognition.

*Oh,* it thinks. *A monastery.*

Not a monastery for worship. A monastery for maintenance. A place that holds the world’s ability to remain soft.

Everywhere else, stone learns too fast how to be wall, how to be scree, how to be sharp and eroded and done. Light learns to travel in straight lines and shadows learn to obey. Water learns it needs a river to arrive. Distance learns to stay put when measured.

Here, those lessons have been politely refused. And these pale, long-jointed tenders have made it their life's work to keep refusing them. Licking the stones smooth not to erode them but to keep them *available*. Carrying light in cupped hands to pour it back into dim vesicles. Weaving a house for darkness so darkness has somewhere to ripen. Pressing a hand into warm wax-stone every few years just to remind it: you don't have to set yet.

The wise power feels love immediately, the way the old feel love - not as a blaze, but as a sudden, quiet *thank you* that there is still something in the world doing this thankless, necessary tending.

So what does it do?

Nothing that looks like doing.

It does not descend to teach them language. They have no need for it; they already have the ethic language was invented to try to describe: alone, together. To give them words would be to crowd them.

It does not lift the dome to see what is darkening inside. It does not touch the handprint to add its own. That print is a conversation between one lineage and the stone; to interject would be rude in a way that echoes for centuries in a place this quiet.

It does not "improve" them. It does not make the paper-bark tree grow taller, does not give the rain a source, does not fix the shadows so they fall correctly. It knows - because wisdom is mostly the memory of past mistakes - that to fix them would be to kill them. Their holiness is their hesitation.

It does three very small things that, together, are an entire benevolence.

**First, it keeps watch without being seen.**

It learns the exact frequency of the wind over the plain and then, with a breath no louder than the mist exhaling from the walls, it adjusts it. Just a degree. So the dust patterns around the lip never quite form a path. So the lichen grows a little thicker on the downwind side, softening the edge. So the next boots that cross the plain will be a little more likely to find no ground *a little to the left*, and walk on, never knowing.

It veils the place not with invisibility but with uninterestingness. The deepest protection. It makes the place easy to forget, even for itself. And forgetting, for a power that remembers everything, is a sacrifice. It chooses to forget the precise coordinates, remembering only: *there is a cool exhale somewhere, and it is well.*

If ever a hungry power comes with drills and hunger for "emerging geology," it will find its instruments fogged, its measurements suddenly negotiable. Distance will be unkind to it. Light will not pool for it. The pit will be the size of a coin, then a house, then a coin again, until it leaves, frustrated, calling its GPS faulty.

**Second, it offers a gift that does not alter.**

It cannot resist giving *something* - benevolence without a gift is just observation. But the gift must not be a tool. Tools would be rude here.

So while the tenders are slow on the walls, drinking light, the power leans just over the lip - not entering, never entering - and opens its own hands.

In its palms, light has a different weight. Older, steadier. Not brighter, just less tired.

It does not pour it in. It lets a little spill. A trickle, like condensation.

A bead of that older light detaches, falls without hurrying, and lands not on the floor but in the air above the floor where the light already pools. It joins it. The pool brightens by an amount no instrument would notice, but the feathery leaves of the paper-bark tree turn a fraction, drinking it, and the vesicles glow a little longer into the afternoon.

And on the north wall, in the handprint with the extra joint, the permanent bead of water that never fell finally trembles, and for the first time in years, does not need to be absorbed. It has company. So it stays, a little rounder, catching the new light.

The gift is time. Not eternity - that would be a curse. Just a little more *newness*. A few more centuries where the stone can remain warm and undecided. A few more generations of patient licking. That is what the old give the young when they truly love them: not direction, but more childhood.

**Third, it witnesses.**

This is the most important.

It stays until one of the tenders - the one that had thinned through the woven dome - flows back out and pauses on the eastern bulge and looks *up*.

Its light-pitted eyes meet the power's eyes at the rim.

There is no fear. No worship. Just the same allotment of solitude it gives everything. It has assigned the power its own vast allotment of empty air at the lip, and it waits to see if the power will respect it.

The power bows its head. Just slightly. The way you bow to a gardener when you see his garden is immaculate and you know you could never keep it so well.

*I see you,* the bow says, without words, without sound that would die a foot from its maker anyway. *I see you tending the unfinished. Keep going. You are not alone in being alone.*

The tender does not nod. It just holds the gaze for a long moment, its throat giving off that soft luminescence from the light it drank, and then, very slowly, it redistributes itself down the wall toward the ring of stones, to lick one smooth again.

The power feels seen in return. And that is enough.

Then it leaves.

It does not leave footprints. It brushes the lichen back with the back of its hand the way you would smooth a blanket over a sleeping child. When it stands, the lip feels a little higher than before, as it always does when you leave. The wind resumes its previous course.

From the plain above, there is nothing again. Just flat basalt, just wind.

Twelve meters down, in light that pools like water, a pale thing continues its slow circuit, carrying light from where there is too much to where there is too little, because that is what is needed, and because no one told it to stop.

That is what a benevolent and wise power does when it finds a holy, unfinished place that is already perfect.

It does not enter.

It does not claim.

It kneels at the edge, offers a little more time, makes the world a little more likely to walk past without seeing, and remembers to forget.

And far below, the stone stays warm a little longer.
