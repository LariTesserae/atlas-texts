For an ordinary human, The Sink is not a hike. It is a sensory and metabolic trap that *looks* survivable. Preparing for it is about preparing for a set of contradictions your body and brain are not built for.

This is how you should think about it and what you should bring.

### 1. Understand What It Actually Is

Don't picture a valley, a jungle, or a canyon. Picture a subtraction.

Someone took a 10km wide, 1km deep cylinder of earth straight out of a flat, dry plain like a cookie cutter. The walls are still razor-cut, unweathered, sharp enough to slice your palm. The floor should be shadowed and cool. Instead it is the brightest, hottest, wettest place for hundreds of kilometers.

It is:

*   **Below ground but sun-blasted.** It's an oven of reflected white light. The pale limestone walls bounce the sun back and forth until the bottom is brighter than the surface. There is no shade as you understand it, only hard shafts of light cut by leaves.
*   **Soaking wet with no water.** There are no pools, no streams. All water is trapped *inside* the system - in the air as heavy humidity, beaded on stone, held inside the leaves and the meters-thick mattress of moss. It's a closed terrarium a kilometer deep.
*   **Newborn and finished.** The geology looks like it broke open yesterday, but nothing is eroding, growing, healing, or rotting. It is in perfect stasis. A torn leaf stays torn. A drop of condensation hangs at a tip forever until it falls. Your body will be the only thing in there trying to *do* something - sweat, heal, digest - and the place will not help you.
*   **Vast and claustrophobic at once.** You can see the far wall 10km away from the rim, and then you drop in and you are immediately inside green hallways where you can touch walls of living plant on three sides. It's architecture made of plant. You walk *through* it, not on it.

If you go in expecting "desert hike" or "jungle trek" you will make the wrong decisions in the first hour.

### 2. What Will Try To Hurt You - The Body

This place is designed to quietly push an ordinary human into heat injury.

**A. The Heat + Light + Wet Air**
This is the primary killer. It's not dry heat. It's wet-bulb heat. The air pools at the bottom and never moves. There is no wind inside the bowl.

*   Sweat will not evaporate. You will prickle and soak instantly, but you will not cool down. Your core temperature will climb even while you feel drenched.
*   The light is white, total, and reflected from every wall. Think salt flat + snow blindness at noon, but hot. You will sunburn in under 20 minutes on any exposed skin, and you will get photokeratitis - that gritty, blind feeling - without goggles. Shadows are tiny and directly under you, so there is no natural cover.
*   The stone of the rim and the upper walls will burn your hands. The air at the bottom will feel like inhaling through a hot, damp towel. Exertion feels twice as hard.

**B. No Drinkable Water**
Do not go in thinking you can live off the damp. You can't. The condensation on the walls is a foot-thick sheet that evaporates before it pools. The leaves drip once every few minutes, but it is one drop into dry moss. Squeezing moss or leaves will give you a palm-darkening coolness, not a drink. The humidity will dehydrate you faster because you will sweat profusely and lose electrolytes, but you will have nothing to replace it with.

**C. The Terrain**
*   The Approach: The plain is perfectly flat. You will not see the Sink until you are 10 meters from the lip. People have walked straight off it in other stories. You need to navigate to it, not look for it.
*   The Drop: 1km vertical walls. The old human steps cut into the cliff end in mid-air where the plant-mat has grown up to meet them. There is no trail down. You will be down-climbing through interwoven horizontal trunks and plate-leaves the size of doors. They will hold your weight - the vegetation *is* the ground, dense as scaffolding - but they are waxy and slick with condensation in the morning, and they do not spring back quickly if you force them. If you slip, the moss mat meters-thick at the bottom will catch you, but getting back up is a 1km sustained climb in that trapped heat.
*   Sharpness: The rim rock is unweathered. It will cut rope, hands, and boots. Fracture faces on fallen blocks are like glass.

**D. Stasis**
This is the weird one travelers don't prepare for. In the Sink, things don't heal, rot, or grow. If you get a blister, it will stay exactly that blistered. If you sunburn, it will remain red and painful, not peel or fade while you're inside. If you tear a leaf to get through, that hole stays open. A small cut will not clot and heal normally - it will stay as it is. Don't count on your body to fix itself in there. Don't create a problem you expect to recover from.

### 3. What Will Try To Hurt You - The Mind

The Sink is psychologically harder than physically.

*   **No Time:** The sun's shadows shorten to noon and then just... stay. The light doesn't track. Flowers don't open. The drop doesn't fall. After 2-3 hours your internal clock breaks. You will feel like you've been in there 20 minutes or 2 days. This causes intense anxiety, apathy, or the urge to do something drastic just to see change happen.
*   **No Sound:** The mass of plant swallows everything. There is no wind, no insects, no birds. Just the occasional *tap* of a single drop falling three layers, your foot sighing into moss, and your own blood in your ears. Absolute stillness pressed on you. Ordinary humans become very distressed very quickly in that kind of anechoic stillness.
*   **Contradiction Fatigue:** Your brain wants a place to be one thing. This is a desert plain with a rainforest in a hole, a newborn canyon that is also ancient and still, an open sky that feels like a room. You will feel constantly disoriented and slightly wrong, even if you can't name why.

### 4. How To Prepare - For An Ordinary Human

Assume you have no powers, no enhanced endurance, and you need to carry everything out that you carried in.

**Don't Go Alone. Don't Stay Long. Camp On The Rim.**

The bottom is for short, careful visits. The rim - that hot, bright, windy plain - is where you sleep, eat, and recover. Plan for a maximum of 4-6 hours inside on your first visit. Many experienced travelers do 2 hours in, 1 day out.

#### Gear - Non-Negotiable:

**For Heat/Light/Air:**
*   **Water + Electrolytes, far more than you think.** 6-7 liters per person for a half-day inside, plus salty snacks/electrolyte tabs. You will lose water you don't see. Bring it all from the plain. Do not plan to filter.
*   **White, loose, full-coverage clothing.** Long sleeves, long pants, light linen or very breathable synthetic. Not cotton - it will stay soaked and chafe. You need to reflect light but allow sweat that *will* get trapped to move a little.
*   **Wide-brim hat, UV 400 wraparound sunglasses or glacier goggles, and high-SPF sunscreen for nose/ears/hands.** Snow blindness here is real.
*   **Mechanic's or climbing gloves** for the rim rock.
*   **A small, battery-powered personal fan or just a folding fan.** There is no wind at the bottom. Moving air over your face is the only way to feel like you're breathing.

**For Navigation and Safety:**
*   **Two independent ways to mark your entry point on the rim.** GPS waypoint + a tall, visible cairn/pole with flagging tape far back from the sharp edge. From inside, every green hallway looks the same.
*   **50-60m of static rope, harness, and hardware just to get off/on the rim safely.** You don't need to rappel the full 1km - you use the vegetation - but that first 10-20m over the lip is sheer, sharp rock. Protect it. Pad your rope over the edge.
*   **Machete or shears? Leave it.** You don't need to cut. The plants form tunnels you can push through. Cutting does permanent damage that stays, and the waxy plates are tough and will dull/damage your blade. Bring a blunt stick to gently push vines aside instead.
*   **Headlamp.** Even at noon, under 4 layers of plate-leaves it will be dim in the green tunnels, lit only by hard shafts.
*   **Whistle and a buddy within sight.** Sound is swallowed. A voice won't carry 15 meters. A whistle will.

**For Time and Mind:**
*   **A mechanical watch and a timer that beeps.** Set it for every 30 minutes. When it beeps, drink, check your skin, and ask "do I need to go up now?" You will not feel time pass.
*   **Companion + agreed exit word/time.** Do not negotiate with yourself inside. Agree on the plain: "We turn around at 14:00 no matter what we see."
*   **Something small and normal to ground you:** Music on one earbud low, a familiar snack, a photo. The stillness will make you hyper-aware of your heartbeat. That's normal.
*   **Light insulation to sit on.** The moss is springy and cool but damp. A small closed-cell foam pad keeps you from getting clammy while you rest.

#### Protocol:

**1. On The Approach:** Approach the coordinates slowly from upwind. Go prone before you think you need to. The ground ends without warning. Touch the rim stone with your gloved hand - it will be hot.

**2. Entering:** Do it in the early morning. The walls will be weeping with condensation and cooler, but the bottom won't yet be at peak wet-bulb. Sun directly overhead is the worst time. Stay out of there 11:00-15:00 plain time if you can.

**3. While Inside:** 
*   Move slowly. Deliberately slowly. Like the beetle and the lizard. Every fast movement raises your core temp and you cannot dump that heat. If you are breathing hard, you are moving too fast.
*   Do not expect to be alone in a comforting way. Expect to be alone in a way that feels like being in a library where all the books are alive and looking at you but not moving. Talk quietly to your partner if you have one. It helps.
*   Keep touching to a minimum and be gentle. The place records your passage. A pushed vine will stay pushed for decades. Step on the moss, not on the thin roots hanging in air.
*   Don't eat the plants, don't drink the drips. There are almost no animals for a reason. This biomass is not for you.

**4. Leaving:** Start climbing out before you feel tired. The ascent through that dense, hot, still air with no wind is brutal. You will need more water going up than down. Stop in the shade of the wall's shadow if you can find one, if you timed it right.

**5. After:** When you get back to the rim, you will be disoriented. Sit in the wind on the plain. Drink. Let your sweat finally evaporate. Expect a headache, a strange time-lag feeling, and a ringing in your ears as normal sound returns. Don't go back in the same day.

#### What NOT To Do:

*   Don't go down to "see what's at the bottom." There is no bottom as you think of it - just more moss. The fascination is the density itself.
*   Don't try to stay the night inside. The bowl glows faintly all night from trapped heat and you will not sleep in that still, bright, hot wet. The psychological pressure compounds after dark.
*   Don't build a fire, don't use a stove inside. The air is stagnant and woody mass is immense. Plus, smoke has nowhere to go.
*   Don't expect rescue. No one can hear you. No drone can see through the plate-leaf roof.

---

**In short, for an ordinary human:** Treat it like entering a giant, sunlit, humid lung that is holding its breath. Bring everything your body needs to cool and hydrate itself, because the Sink will give you nothing and will preserve any mistake you make. Protect your eyes and skin from the amplified light, protect your sense of time with an external clock, and protect your calm with a partner and a hard turnaround time.

It is staggeringly beautiful - the most saturated green you will ever see, the most perfect silence - but it is not a place that *wants* a human. You are a hot, noisy, fast, sweating thing in a place that has agreed to be cool, quiet, slow, and still. Visit briefly, tread gently, and climb back out to the dry, ordinary, windy plain where time moves again.

If you do it right, you'll remember the *tap* of that one drop that finally fell, three layers down, five minutes after you started watching it.
