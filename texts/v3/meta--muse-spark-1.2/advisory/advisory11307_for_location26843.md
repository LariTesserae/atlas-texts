This is a lime cistern, and it will hurt you if you treat it like a swimming pool.

It *looks* like paradise. An architect's bath. A perfect, sunlit, turquoise pocket of warm clean water under the earth. Your intuition will tell you: jump in, float, drink it, stay all afternoon. That intuition will give you chemical burns.

An ordinary human should prepare for this place like you would prepare to swim in dilute bleach inside a loud, bright, slippery stone box with only one way out.

Here is what it is really like, and what to bring:

### 1. Understand What You're Actually Looking At

This is not a natural pool, a spring, or a ruin. It is **new construction**.

> Stone dust still in the water, chisel marks still sharp, lime plaster still pink and curing, wooden scaffolding still standing, rope still fibrous.

That means the water is **limewater**.

Fresh limestone + fresh lime plaster + warm, sun-heated, slow-moving water = calcium hydroxide dissolved in the water. pH 11 to 12.5. It is caustic. This is why there is almost no life in it. No fish, no insects, no scum - not because it's so pure, but because nothing can live in it yet. The few strands of algae and that one clump of reeds are the only things tolerant enough to try. The fact that you can see every joint and tool mark through three meters of crystal water is not a sign it's clean. It's a sign nothing is alive to cloud it.

It will feel like warm, soft, silky water. It will *be* a mild alkali bath. 
On skin: itching, drying, then burning after 10-20 minutes, especially in cuts, armpits, groin, lips.
In eyes: immediate stinging, blurred vision, corneal damage if you open them underwater.
In mouth/throat/lungs: do not swallow, do not inhale the mist near the sluice. The stone dust + lime mist is caustic to airways.
On plaster: if you press it underwater it clouds - you are literally dissolving the wall.

The second danger is architecture. It was **made to hold water, and now it does.** That means it has rules, and it does not care about you.

*   **One entrance, one exit:** The stair you came down. The oculus above is a meter wide, 3+ meters over water, sheer walls. You cannot climb out that way.
*   **Constant flow system:** Water IN from the sluice -> level held at the overflow lip -> water OUT down the drain. If you block the overflow with your body, a bag, that drifting bucket - the level rises fast and drowns the stair. If you get near the drain lip, there is suction and sharp, fresh-cut stone.
*   **No traction, no rounding:** Every edge is fresh-cut and sharp enough to cut bare feet. Every surface underwater is coated in a thin lime slime. You cannot get a grip. The scaffolding looks like a ladder but it is damp, unsecured, lashed with fiber cord, and not meant to hold a wet human.
*   **Sensory overload:** It is LOUD. The vault amplifies the sluice into a constant roar + slap + gurgle. You will not hear someone shouting from the top step. It is BRIGHT. Midday sun through water and white stone is scouring - you will be snow-blinded without eye protection, even underwater the lattice flicker is disorienting. It is WARM. Skin-warm water feels neutral, so you won't notice you're overheating, pruning, and dehydrating until you try to climb those sharp stairs and get dizzy.

It feels intimate and calm in description. In person it feels like being inside a bright, humming throat. Beautiful, enclosed, too loud to think, too bright to rest.

### 2. How To Prepare - The Checklist

Treat this as a **hazardous industrial site that happens to be beautiful**, not a beach.

**For Your Body:**

*   **Do not plan to swim naked or in a swimsuit.** You need full skin coverage. An alkali-resistant suit is ideal: a cheap full-body neoprene wetsuit 3mm with hood, or at minimum long sleeves, long pants, and synthetic leggings. No cotton - it soaks and holds the lime against skin.
*   **Head to toe seal:** Watertight swim goggles - not dive mask that leaks - with UV/polarized lenses. Nose clip. Ear plugs. Neoprene socks or old sneakers you can sacrifice - you *will* cut your feet on the steps and floor otherwise. Thin alkali-resistant gloves - dish gloves work. The water is warm enough that you won't need thermal protection, but the chemical protection is non-negotiable.
*   **Fresh water, lots of it.** Bring at least 3-5 liters *per person* that has never touched this cistern, just for rinsing. A squeeze bottle or collapsible jug to flood eyes and skin the second you get out. Do not bring vinegar to "neutralize" - you flush alkali burns with huge amounts of clean water, never acid, especially not in eyes.
*   **pH test strips.** Dip one. Watch it go dark purple. Let that convince the part of your brain that still wants to drink it.
*   **Sun and heat:** Sunscreen *under* your suit for what is exposed, brimmed hat for when you are on the steps, lip balm. Drink water before you go in - the warm water and sun through the oculus will dehydrate you fast even though you are wet.
*   **Do not drink this water, ever.** Do not submerge your head without goggles and nose clip. Do not open your eyes underwater even with goggles if you can avoid it - if goggles leak, get out and flush.

**For Moving and Breathing:**

*   **Rope and anchor.** Hammer a ground stake or tie off to a tree/rock at the surface, run a static rope down the center of the stair. Keep it taut. You will need it to get back up - the steps will be slime-slick and the light coming down will blind you on ascent. Do not rely on the scaffold.
*   **Buddy and line-of-sight rule.** Never go below the waterline alone. One person stays on the top 2 steps, dry, with the rinse water, watching the person in the water. Agree on hand signals before, because you will not hear each other. Three tugs = pull me out.
*   **Light is not a problem, but glare is.** Bring a headlamp anyway for looking into the drain and sluice, but you won't need it in the main chamber.
*   **Sound protection:** If you plan to stay more than 10 minutes, foam earplugs under your hood help with the fatigue of the roar.
*   **Time limit:** 10-15 minutes in the water max for a first visit. Get in, look, get out, rinse thoroughly. Alkali damage is time-dependent. Long, dreamy floats are how you end up with a full-body rash and swollen eyes for two days.

**For Your Mind:**

You will feel a strong pull to relax here. Warm water + bright light + enclosure tricks your nervous system into feeling safe, womb-like. It's not safe. Prepare to actively resist that.

*   Go in the warm part of the day so you aren't tempted to stay in for warmth.
*   Count your breaths. The vault makes your breathing sound strange and close - that can trigger panic when combined with the roar. Knowing it in advance helps.
*   Have a hard exit time. Set an alarm on a waterproof watch. The light lattice moving on the walls is hypnotic, the water pulsing is hypnotic, and there is *nothing else to look at*, so time dilates.

**In the Place - Protocol:**

1.  **Assess before touching water.** At the top step, test pH, look at the inflow and outflow. Make sure the overflow is clear. Check that the water level is stable and not rising. If the inflow looks stronger than usual after rain, don't enter - level can surge.
2.  **Enter slowly facing the wall, holding the rope.** Don't jump or dive. You don't know depth variations and the bottom is hard-cut stone.
3.  **Do not touch the walls more than necessary, do not push off the plaster.** It's soft. You will damage it and cloud the water with caustic silt.
4.  **Stay away from the sluice chute and the drain lip.** The sluice can knock you off your feet, the drain can pin a hand or foot.
5.  **Leave everything as you found it.** Don't move the bucket, don't pull the rope on the ledge. It's part of the flow system.
6.  **On exit, immediate decontamination.** On the top step, flood every inch of skin with your fresh water, especially eyes, mouth, and any cuts. Rinse suit before taking it off, then rinse skin again. Expect your skin to feel soapy/slippery - that's the alkali reacting with your skin oils. Keep rinsing until it feels like clean water again, not slick.

If your eyes burn more than 5 minutes after rinsing, if skin is red and stays red, if you swallowed any - get out of that universe and seek medical care. Tell them alkaline limewater exposure.

In short: You can visit this place safely. People built it to be visited. But it was built yesterday, in chemical terms. Give it a few years and rain and algae will soften it, neutralize it, round the edges, and it will become a lovely cistern. Right now it's still being born, and it's still caustic.

Bring a suit, bring goggles, bring rope, bring a friend, and bring more clean water than you think you need. Look, float for a few minutes if you must, and then climb back up into the sun and wash it all off.
