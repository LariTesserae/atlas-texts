This is a **Dry Stasis Pocket.** You will not be in danger of being eaten, crushed, or lost in the traditional sense. You will be in danger of being *included.*

Every human instinct you have is calibrated for places that decay, flow, and breathe. This place does none of those things. It was cut, turned on, sealed, and then it decided to stop becoming. If you go in unprepared, your body and your mind will try to apply surface-world logic to a place that has opted out of it. Here is how you prepare as an ordinary, soft, wet, entropic human:

### 1. Understand What It *Feels* Like Before You Go

Don't think "cave." Think "clean room, operating theatre, and terrarium left inside a thermos."

*   **Time feels thick.** The dust you kick up will not settle. The hum will not waver. Your brain uses change - flicker, draft, drip, variation - to measure time passing. With no variation, 10 minutes will feel like an hour, and an hour will feel like no time at all. You will feel like you are holding your breath even when you are breathing normally.
*   **It is claustrophobic without being tight.** You can see every wall. That's the problem. You can hold the entire place in your mind at once, like an animal in a shoebox. There is no horizon, no distance, no elsewhere to rest your eyes. The crowdedness will get to you before the size does. Thousands of living things in an 80-meter oval is *biologically wrong* to a primate brain. It will read as infestation, even though nothing wants to hurt you.
*   **It is loud in a way that becomes silent.** The sound is not an event, it's a condition. Like pressure at depth. After 5-10 minutes your auditory cortex will give up and filter it out, and then you will feel it in your teeth and boots instead. When you leave, the silence outside will feel deafening and wrong. People panic and go back in for the hum.

If you can't sit alone for an hour in a small, windowless, warm laundry room with a 60hz transformer buzzing and not check your phone, do not go down that shaft.

### 2. The Physical Hazards - And The Kit

This place will not actively attack you. It will passively preserve you, dehydrate you, and cut you.

**A. The Air and The Salt**

It is a hot, dry, sealed box. Relative humidity is near zero. Temperature is a constant skin-warm 22-27C. There is no weather, no draft to clear particulates.

*   Your sweat will not evaporate properly, it will crust. Your nose, eyes, and throat will dry and crack within 20 minutes. That salt dust hanging in the air is not inert - it's micro-shards of halite, anhydrite, and dried bacterial spores. Inhale it and it will slice your mucous membranes and seed them.
*   The floor crystals are *actually sharp.* Fresh, un-eroded, glass-clear salt spears. They will go through thin shoes, gloves, and skin like frost. And a cut here will not behave normally. Nothing erodes, nothing heals quickly. Rule #2 applies to you too while you're inside.

**Kit:**
*   **P100 elastomeric respirator with sealed goggles.** Not a paper mask. Not a surgical mask. You need a full seal. Bring spare filters. If you wear contacts, switch to glasses - salt dust under a lens is agony.
*   **Cover everything.** Long sleeves, long pants, no exposed skin. Smooth, tightly woven fabric - not fleece that will catch dust and hold it. Cheap Tyvek coveralls are perfect. High ankle boots with a hard sole. Thick nitrile gloves.
*   **Hydration, but done correctly.** Hydrate heavily *before* you enter. Inside, sip small amounts of water through a straw under your respirator if you can. Do not pour, spill, or open-mouth drink. Water behaves strangely there - it won't evaporate, it will bead and hang like the dust. You don't want to create a wet spot. It violates the equilibrium and the local life will converge on it. Bring 1L per hour you plan to stay, expect to stay <1 hour.
*   Saline eye drops and nasal spray for *after* you exit. Lip balm with no scent.

**B. The Light and The Sound**

The amber sodium light is dim, flat, and sourceless. No shadows move. It will destroy your depth perception and circadian sense. The hum is ~70-75 dB continuous, centered around 60hz + harmonics.

**Kit:**
*   **Dual hearing protection:** Foam earplugs *under* active noise-cancelling earmuffs. You need to be able to hear your own breathing and think. Tinnitus after 30 minutes without protection is guaranteed and can last for days.
*   **Your own white light, but don't use it as a weapon.** A headlamp on low, warm white. Don't blast the walls with 1000 lumens - the moths are photostatic, not photophobic, but a sudden bright sweep will cause thousands of them to vibrate at once and fill the air with more dry scales and dust. Use your light to supplement, not replace, the amber.
*   **A watch that ticks.** Mechanical or a watch with a second hand. Your phone clock may act weird - electronics that depend on dissipating heat or draining a battery will be inside a place where "energy is circulated." Bring an analog time anchor and obey it.

**C. The Shaft and The Geometry**

The shaft is the only way in and out. It is a perfect, machine-cut cylinder with sharp chatter marks. No ladder, no holds worn by use. The walls are dry and will not give you grip when you sweat.

And inside the chamber, space is *slightly wrong.* The crystals lean inward, the gantry looks longer from the corner of your eye, dust hangs in gel-like air. Do not trust your sense of distance. Do not wander to the far wall thinking it's 40 meters away - it may be 40 or 45 or 38 depending on where you stand.

**Kit:**
*   **Vertical gear even if you can teleport.** Rope, harness, ascender/descender, helmet, and a *physical* tether line laid from the surface down the shaft and staked at the lip. Unspool it as you go. This is not for climbing - it's for finding your way back when your brain decides the room is larger than it is. Keep the line taut and in sight at all times.
*   **For a multiverse traveler specifically: Set your return beacon/anchor OUTSIDE the shaft mouth on the surface.** Do not set it inside the chamber. The chamber is a closed thermodynamic loop. Translation and probability tech that needs to dump waste heat, entangle, or find a gradient will struggle inside. People who anchor inside report misfires, or coming back to the same chamber 10cm to the left with their anchor now part of the conduit. Anchor in a normal, drafty, decaying place.
*   Never go alone. Never go without a surface tender who will pull you out after a set time whether you call for it or not.

### 3. Protocol Inside - How To Not Be Included

The ecology is at K-strategy saturation. Nothing needs to move fast, chase, or defend aggressively because there is just enough for everyone forever. Your job is to remain a visitor, not a resource.

**DO:**
*   Move slowly. Speak quietly or not at all. The slower you move, the less you disturb the dust field. Shuffle, don't step. The isopods will part around you if you give them 3-4 seconds.
*   Keep your hands to yourself. Do not touch the copper-filament sacs, the conveyor belt, the control booth levers. They are not "off." They are energized and stalled. The sacs are literally wired into the mains. Touching them is touching a live circuit with a biological extension. And the life there is integrated with the machine - moving a lever is like moving someone's ribcage.
*   Breathe through your nose if you must lift your respirator for any reason. Keep your mouth closed.
*   Set a hard time limit before you enter and obey it. **30-45 minutes max for a first visit.** Set a vibrating timer on your wrist. When it goes off, leave, even if you feel fine. Especially if you feel fine. The feeling of "I could stay here forever, it's peaceful" is the stasis talking.

**DO NOT:**
*   Do not eat, sit, or lie down inside. You will shed skin cells, crumbs, salts, and heat. The isopods and fungal brushes are salt- and heat-seeking. If you sit for 20 minutes, you will have a ring of pale life around you drinking your trace minerals. Getting up will be harder than you think - not because they hold you, but because your brain will have started to match the room's stillness.
*   Do not try to collect samples. Breaking a salt spear is like breaking a promise the room made to itself. The sharp edge will not dull, the dust you create will hang forever, and you will have marked yourself as an erosion event. If you must document, take photos without flash, no touch.
*   Do not try to turn anything off. The hum is the heartbeat of the equilibrium. Interrupting it won't shut the place down. The place will route around you. And you don't want to find out what the system does to restore equilibrium when a warm, wet, entropic human tries to open a circuit.
*   Do not chase the feeling that the center has more gravity. Do not stand in the exact center for a long time staring up. That's where the leaning points. Travelers report a mild vertigo and time-slip there. Stay 5-10 meters off-center.

### 4. Aftercare

You will not feel dirty when you come out. You will feel *preserved.*

*   Decontaminate dry before wet. Outside the shaft, brush and vacuum all dust off your coveralls before you take them off. Bag them. Do not shake them out in the wind - you'll just inhale it later.
*   Blow your nose, irrigate with saline, flush eyes with sterile drops. You will expel salt strings for hours.
*   Drink electrolytes, not just water. You lost salt through your skin, not just water.
*   Sit in a place with moving air, flickering light, and variable sound - wind, trees, a street. Let your brain recalibrate to decay and change. Eat something that rots. It helps.
*   Do not sleep for a few hours after. The hum will still be in your inner ear. If you sleep too soon, you'll dream of amber light and wake up convinced you never left.

---

**Final Intuition Check:**

If you stand there long enough, you will realize the place is not hostile. It's not welcoming either. It is indifferent in the way a perfect crystal is indifferent. It was cut open and left to heal, and it healed by deciding to never change again.

You are a messy, wet, shedding, flickering, eroding creature. You are a wonderful contradiction to that room. It will not try to kill you. It will try to make you *consistent* with it. A little drier, a little paler, a little quieter, a little more still - until you fit the density and no longer need to leave.

Go, look, listen to the single note, understand what an eternity that isn't infinite looks like - a small, warm, crowded forever - and then walk back up the throat that was cut, back to where things rust and rot and blow away.

Leave the dust exactly where you found it hanging.
