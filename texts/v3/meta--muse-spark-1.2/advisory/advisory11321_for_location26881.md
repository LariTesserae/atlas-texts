You are not visiting a mountain. You are visiting a *wound in a mountain that hasn't decided to be a mountain yet* - and then crawling into its only sealed blister. Prepare for two completely opposite environments with about two feet between them.

Think of it as an alpine desert outside and a dry kiln inside. Both will try to hurt you in different ways.

### 1. The Approach - High, Cold, Sharp, and Airless

**You are above the biosphere.**
There is no soil, no plants, no animals outside to tell you how high you are. There is just thin air and raw rock. You will feel it before you see it.

*   **Altitude:** You are above ~3,500m equivalent. Expect hypoxia. Headache, poor judgment, shortness of breath on the scramble. If you are an ordinary human, pre-acclimatize for 24-48 hours at a lower elevation, ascend slowly, bring supplemental oxygen if you can, and do not do this with a cold or a hangover. You will be crawling and squeezing - you need more air than you think.
*   **Cold + Sun:** The outside is classic high-altitude exposure. Air is thin, cold, and dry, wind is constant, UV is brutal. Full alpine layering: moisture-wicking base, insulating mid, windproof shell, sun hat, glacier glasses, SPF 50+, lip balm. You will be hot inside and freezing outside within seconds of each other - you need layers you can take off and put on in a space barely wider than your chest.
*   **The Rock Itself:** This is not old granite. This is fresh volcanic glass. It has not been weathered *at all*. That means:
    - It is razor-sharp. Conchoidal fractures, glass edges, frozen gas bubbles that pop under weight. Cut-resistant gloves, heavy kneepads, elbow pads, and thick canvas or softshell pants are not optional. One slip on the scree will fillet your hands.
    - It is unstable. Scree slides, flakes break off. Helmet, eye protection, and gloves for the crawl.
    - It gives you no handholds you can trust. Move slowly. Test every edge.

### 2. The Squeeze - The Seam

There is only one door and it was not made for you. A vertical pressure crack where the cooling skin split. No chambered entrance, no light coming out.

*   **You will have to turn sideways and exhale.** If you have wide shoulders, a large pack, claustrophobia, or a bad back/knees, this is your turn-back point. You cannot bring a big pack through. Everything you bring inside must be on your body.
*   **You will transition from kneeling to crawling to belly-crawling.** Bring a headlamp with a red-light mode, but expect it to be mostly swallowed. The walls are dark basalt and there is almost no reflection.
*   **Buddy and tether.** Never go in alone. Have someone outside at the seam who knows your time limit and can pull you out. A radio will not work well in a tight basalt pocket - use a rope tug code. Agree on it before you go in.

### 3. Inside - The Pocket

This is the inversion. Everything flips.

**Climate: Hot Dry Mineral Heat, Not Wet Heat**

Outside is alpine cold. Two feet past the seam the wind stops and the rock itself starts radiating. This is not a hot spring cave. It is a dry oven.

*   **No water.** Not humid, not damp, not dripping. Bone-dry air that was baked out long ago. Your sweat will evaporate instantly and then there will be no sweat. Your nose, eyes, mouth, and lungs will dry and crack in 15-20 minutes.
    - Bring 1L of water per person *just for a 30 minute stay*, plus electrolytes. Drink before you enter. Do not expect to find any inside.
    - Saline eye drops, nasal saline, and lip balm.
    - Cover all skin. Long sleeves, light breathable layers. The dry radiant heat will sunburn you without sun.
