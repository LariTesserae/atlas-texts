This is not a place that will kill you quickly. It will kill you politely, by drying you out, wearing you down, and taking offense at your wetness. An ordinary, water-based human is an obscenity here - a walking spill. You can visit the High Felt, but you cannot *be* from here. You have to go as a guest who has learned to contain themselves completely.

Prepare like you are going to walk on the exposed skin of a living continent that breathes electricity and hates water. Because you are.

### 1. Understand What You Are To This Place

You are 60% liquid water, salt, and heat.

The High Felt is 0% liquid water, cold, electrostatic, and anhydrous. Its chemistry runs on oils, ammonia, and charge gradients, not H2O. That means:

* **You are toxic to it.** A spilled water bottle, a sweaty palm, a bloody nose, a mouthful of spit, urine, even a deep exhale of humid breath will poison the felt. It will wall off the contaminated patch, kill it, and leave a white scar that the Lattice-Weavers will have to tend for decades. They will not be angry. They will be grieved.
* **It is toxic to you.** You cannot eat anything there. You cannot drink the gel from the house vanes - it's not water, it's an ammonia-glycol condensate that will blister your gut. The air itself will try to desiccate you from the inside out. The felt's spores and dust are silica and alkaloid - not deadly in small doses, but they will abrade your lungs if you breathe them raw.
* **You are loud in the wrong way.** The Felt navigates by vibration, not sight or smell. Your heartbeat, your footsteps, your voice are all signals. Everything that hunts or grazes in the felt will feel you coming long before it sees you.

If you internalize that - that your body is the contamination - all the other preparation makes sense.

### 2. Your Body: Pre-Conditioning

Do not go straight from sea-level Earth.

**Altitude & Air:**
The air is thin, cold, and utterly dry. 5-6C with constant wind means profound windchill even though it doesn't feel "freezing." That indigo dimness is real - the white dwarf sun gives you about as much useful light as heavy overcast at dusk, forever, with no direct heat.

* Spend 3-5 days pre-acclimating in dry, high-altitude air if you can. Sleep with a humidifier *off*. Get used to nosebleeds, cracked lips, and waking up with a throat like sandpaper.
* See a travel medicine doctor. Get checked for anemia, asthma, heart issues. You will be mildly hypoxic the whole time. You will get winded walking up a gentle swell that you could jog up at home. Plan for 30% less stamina.
* Start aggressive skin and mucous membrane prep 48 hours before: saline nasal spray every 4 hours, preservative-free artificial tears, heavy ceramide moisturizer, lip balm with no water content - beeswax/lanolin only. Hydrate well *before* you go, because you will not be able to hydrate normally while there.

**Hearing & Psyche:**
The Felt is LOUD in a way no recording captures. It's not wind. It's a sustained, layered drone - the low felt shushing like a drumhead the size of a country, the Towers moaning like organ pipes as wind is forced through their hollow cores, the dish-leaves thrumming at a high metallic pitch. You have to shout to talk to someone two meters away. It vibrates your molars, your sternum, the soles of your feet.

* Bring filtered musician's earplugs - or better, custom-molded attenuation plugs with a membrane that cuts 15-20 dB but *lets vibration through*. If you block sound completely with foam plugs or noise-canceling headphones you will be deaf to the actual navigation cues and you will miss predator movement.
* Prepare for sensory weirdness: dim, shadowless indigo light flattens distance. Everything looks closer than it is. The air is so clean you can see 10km to the next Tower but you have no haze to judge scale. People get quiet, anxious, or oddly euphoric after a few hours of the drone. Plan for short stays. An ordinary human does well for 12-48 hours. After 72, the dryness and the auditory fatigue erode judgment.

### 3. Your Suit: How To Contain Your Wetness

You cannot go in normal hiking clothes. You need a closed-loop, dry system. Think cleanroom meets desert Bedouin, not spacesuit.

**The Non-Negotiable Kit:**

* **Full coverage, abrasion-proof outer layer:** The felt fibers are glassy, silica-reinforced. They will cheese-grate exposed skin, especially palms and knees if you stumble. Wear a windproof, tightly woven softshell with no exposed cotton. Seams should be taped. Hood, high collar, neck gaiter that can pull over nose. Thin, tough gloves - you will need dexterity but you cannot touch the felt barehanded for long.
* **Soft, wide, low-pressure footwear:** Hiking boots with aggressive treads will tear the living mat. The Lattice-Weavers go barefoot or in wide, felt-sole slippers that spread weight. Bring soft-soled trail shoes, snowshoe-style over-soles, or moccasins with a flexible, flat sole. You want to *float* on the mat, not punch through it. It gives underfoot like a dense trampoline and exhales spores when you step. If you leave deep pockmarks, you're walking wrong.
* **Closed-loop breathing mask:** This is the single most important piece of gear. A simple N95 is not enough. You need a mask with exhalation capture - a respirator with a condenser/reclaimer that traps your exhaled moisture before it hits the air. The locals will notice a human breathing wet without one. It also needs to:
  1. Humidify and warm the incoming air just enough to keep your airway from cracking, but not leak humidity outward.
  2. Filter silica spores and dust - P100 level.
  3. Let your voice through. You'll want a mask with a speaking diaphragm because shouting through a filtered mask in a roaring organ-field is miserable.

  Practice wearing it for hours. Put saline gel inside your nose before you put it on. Bring spare filters - they will clog with cinnamon-smelling dust faster than you expect.

* **Eyes:** Sealed, vented goggles - not sunglasses. The air will dry your corneas to a painful haze within an hour. The dust is fine and electrostatic and loves to stick to eyeballs. Use the goggles even though the light is dim. Choose a lens that *boosts* low light - yellow or light rose - not a dark tint. You need every photon.
* **Skin and Water Discipline Kit:** All Earth water you bring must be in sealed, double-walled, waxed bladders - like the skins the houses offer. No open bottles. No hydration bladders with bite valves that drip. No metal canteens that sweat. Carry water inside your suit, drink through a closed tube. Same for food: no juicy fruits, no soups. Dry, calorie-dense, sealed bars, nuts, cured meats. Every crumb, every wrapper, every bit of saliva on a napkin comes home with you.

  Bring: unscented wet-wipes in a resealable bag for hygiene - but you must pack out the used wipes sealed. Bring a medical kit with self-sealing wound dressings, cyanoacrylate skin glue, not gauze that will shed lint. If you bleed, you must seal it *instantly* and cover it. Blood is water + salt + heat. The felt will recoil from it.

* **Electrostatic management:** The dish-leaves throw off huge charge. The air crackles. Your hair will stand on end inside your hood. Gliders ride those lines. Your synthetics will spark. Avoid highly insulating plastics that build charge. Ground yourself before touching a Tower or a transit filament - and *do not touch the filaments at all*. Those singing threads between Towers are levitated cargo ways. Touching one will discharge through you and may de-tension it. Ask before you approach any house filament.

**What NOT to bring:**
Open flames, combustion stoves, incense, cigarettes, vapes, anything that burns or makes free water vapor. No aerosol sunscreen, no perfume, no scented lotion. No drones with propellers - the Gliders will attack them and the charge will fry them. No speaker systems - you are already inside a speaker system.

### 4. How To Move and Behave

The Lattice-Weavers programmed the felt to grow houses. They can un-grow them. You are a guest in a living city that can decide to close its doors.

* **Learn to walk again.** Short steps, soft landings, no dragging feet. Let the felt breathe under you. If you run, the vibration propagates meters down into the compressed peat-coal layers and every twig-hunter and carpet-grazer will track you. If you need to stop, stand still for 60-90 seconds. The felt learns your rhythm and stops flagging you as an intruder. Locals stand like herons before they move. Copy them.
* **Speak low, listen lower.** Don't try to yell over the Felt's song. The locals wear sound on their bodies - ankle chimes, throat resonators, forearm drums - they communicate with taps, hums, and patterns felt through the feet. You won't master it, but you can learn to tap your sternum twice to say "I'm here / may I pass" and to crouch and place a palm *lightly* on the felt to signal "I'm not moving." Ask a weaver to teach you before you try to cross a dwelling threshold. The oval door only irises when it recognizes calm vibration.
* **Never, ever spill.** If you must spit, do it into a sealed wipe. If you must urinate, use a sealed container system - do not go on the felt, not even in a cathole. Pack it out. If you cry, wipe it inside your mask. This is not prudishness, it's chemistry.
* **Respect the Towers.** Don't climb them unless invited. Don't cut fibers. Don't try to take a dish-leaf as a souvenir. They are not trees, they are individuals. When twilight comes and all the crowns creak and align to the magnetic field, stop talking. Stand still. That collective alignment is navigation, prayer, and weather forecast for the city. Watch it.
* **Food & Shelter:** Sleep in a grown dwelling, not a tent staked into the felt - stakes tear it. The house will offer you light from its chemical filaments; don't try to rewire it. Accept the waxen skin of gel it offers but don't drink it - politely hold it, nod, return it. Eat only what you brought. Offer a gift that is dry and useful: fine silica polishing cloths, braided cordage made from non-biological fiber, a small, beautifully made dry tool. Do not offer water, sweets, alcohol, or anything wet.
* **Fauna:** The Gliders are not aggressive unless you flap or spark. Don't wave your arms at them. Their shriek is a sonar ping - if one screams directly over you, it has seen your heat shimmer or your charge. Crouch slightly, cover reflective surfaces. The carpet grazers and twig-hunters in the felt will avoid vibration-stable humans, but don't sit directly on the ground for long without a ground cloth - the hunters use the felt's surface like a web.

### 5. What It Will Actually Feel Like

For the first hour, you will be disappointed by how *soft* it is. You expected rock and got upholstery. The olive-black felt drinks light so completely that your eyes keep hunting for contrast. Then the wind shifts and a Tower half a kilometer away drops half a tone in its moan, and the felt under your knees thrums in sympathy, and you realize the whole valley you are in is an instrument and you are standing on its skin.

Your throat will go papery despite the mask. Your eyes will water, then stop. The air has a faint smell like old cinnamon and ozone, but after 20 minutes your nose blinds to it because there's no humidity to carry scent. The light never changes. Your watch says noon and your body says 6pm. The gliders are invisible until they bank and catch the white dwarf's thin light on their translucent wings, and then they are everywhere, silent except when one decides to scream - a sound like a glass plate snapping that cuts the drone and makes every hair on your neck rise.

If you are lucky, a Lattice-Weaver will invite you into their dome. Inside, the drone muffles to a hum. The walls glow where you touch them, a cold light that follows your hand without heat. The floor gives slightly under your weight, alive. You will feel, for a moment, that the continent has noticed you and decided, temporarily, to hold you gently. That is the feeling you came for.

Then you will step out and the wind will take your heat again, and you will understand that this place makes perfect sense without you.

**Leaving:**

Decontaminate. Brush and vacuum all felt dust from your suit *outside* the felt, before you transition back. The spores are desiccated and will rehydrate aggressively in Earth air - they can irritate lungs and will try to colonize any damp, peaty soil if you track them home. Shower, irrigate your sinuses, use eye drops, moisturize heavily. Expect to be hoarse and slightly headachy for a day. Drink water slowly.

And when you are home, in humid air, you will miss the singing. You will miss how the ground listened to you.
