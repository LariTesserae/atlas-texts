For an ordinary human, this is not a dangerous place. It is a *contagious* place. It won't try to kill you. It will try to include you. Preparation is not about surviving its hostility, it's about surviving its hospitality - and leaving clean.

Think of it less as a valley or a cave and more as a **wound that hasn't decided if it wants to be a mouth or an eye.** The stone is still embryonic. The light is still learning how to fall. The space is still learning how big it is. You are a fully-formed, hard-edged, noisy adult walking into a nursery. Everything you do will imprint.

Here is how you go in and come back out still human-sized.

### 1. BODY PREPARATION

**You are going from wind-scoured high desert to a refrigerator that sweats.**

*   **Acclimate up top first.** That basalt plain is high, thin air, cold wind, high UV. You will be slightly hypoxic before you even find the Drop. Sit for 15-20 minutes on the plain. Breathe. Let your heart rate settle. If you go in panting, the cool air at the bottom will hit you like a shock and you will start shivering immediately.
*   **Dress for two weathers at once.** Wear a windproof shell for the plain, but underneath wear a wicking base layer and bring a dry mid-layer you *only put on at the lip*. The air inside is 10-15 degrees cooler and it *removes* heat by contact, not wind. It's the cool of a cellar, a stone that has never been sun-warmed. You will not feel cold until you are already damp and chilled through. Cotton will kill you here. Wool or synthetic, even if you think you don't need it.
*   **Your eyes will be assaulted.** Bring good sunglasses for the plain, but take them OFF before you descend. The light inside is not bright like sun-bright. It's bright like *milk* bright. It pools, it clots. Sunglasses will make you misjudge depth and you will misjudge depth enough already. Bring a brimmed hat you can pull low instead. And bring eye drops - the weeping mist hangs in the air and leaves a film.
*   **Do not be hungry, do not be dehydrated, do not need to piss.** The floor has no privacy and the whole pocket watches. Eat a simple meal an hour before. Hydrate on the plain, but do not drink the water inside. That condensation is not groundwater. It has no mineral taste, no source. It's the stone exhaling. It will hydrate you but it will also make you feel... included in the local water cycle. You don't want to be part of that loop on your first visit. Carry your own water and keep it capped.

### 2. MIND PREPARATION

This place runs on five rules and your brain will try to correct them. Don't let it.

> Light has weight. Distance is polite. Water arrives without reason. Stone is unfinished. Solitude is abundant.

*   **You must be comfortable being politely ignored and completely seen at the same time.** Every creature in there has a territory of solitude assigned to it. They will not swarm you. They will not attack. A glassy isopod will keep working its trickle three feet from your boot and never acknowledge you. A pale amphibian will stare at you without turning its head. That stone-bird will look *through* you. It is not loneliness, it is a deeper, older kind of alone-ness where every living thing is equidistant. If you are a person who needs a reaction from your environment to feel oriented, you will panic. Practice sitting in a completely quiet room for 30 minutes without checking your phone before you go.
*   **Mute yourself.** Sound dies a foot from your mouth. If you shout, your voice will fall flat like a dropped towel. Whispering feels normal, talking feels rude. Decide *before* you descend that you will not call out, will not clap to test echo, will not narrate. Bring a small notebook if you need to think out loud. The ecosystem runs at low volume for a reason. Match it.
*   **Bring an anchor, not a plan.** Distance stretches and compresses when you aren't measuring it. If you try to count steps or estimate depth, the place will contradict you to be helpful. The most dangerous thing is to think "the bottom is only 12 meters down, I can jump." You can't. The most disorienting thing is to look up and have the sky suddenly seem close enough to touch, then look down and have your boots seem miles away.
    
    Your anchor needs to be non-numerical. A length of rope is useless - the rope will be longer going out than it was going in. Bring something with *personal* mass: a smooth stone from home you keep in your pocket, a specific song you hum internally, the familiar weight of a wedding ring. When you feel the size of the space negotiate, hold that object. It reminds your body what *your* scale is.

### 3. GEAR - WHAT TO BRING AND WHAT TO LEAVE

**Bring:**

*   **Gloves. Thin, tactile, work gloves.** The stone looks soft, folded like wax, and it *is* faintly warm and sweaty. Your instinct will be to touch every vesicle. Don't touch with bare skin for long. The stone remembers pressure. That handprint with too-long fingers is not a warning - it's a record. Bare hands leave oils and heat and the new stone learns you quickly. If you must touch, gloved, brief, back of the hand.
*   **Two independent light sources, and a promise not to use either.** Carry a headlamp and a small hand flashlight, but keep them OFF unless you are in genuine trouble. The pooled light on the floor is enough to see by, even in shadow. If you add your own light, you pollute the pool and the creature that drinks light will come to drink yours. It’s not dangerous, but you’ll feel its attention like a cold draft.
*   **A mechanical timepiece, not a phone.** Your phone will lose the sky the second you step over the lip, and the clock will mean nothing anyway. The light doesn’t move like sun. Bring a cheap wind-up watch. Know this: one weeping cycle is about 60-70 minutes. One mist hang-time is about 20 minutes. Plan to be in and *out* within a single dry cycle. If the walls start to exhale mist while you are at the bottom, you are on borrowed time. The mist is not toxic, but it’s when the place is most awake and most likely to keep a souvenir.
*   **A walking stick that is longer than you think you need.** Not to use inside - use it to probe the lip. From above you will walk over it without knowing. The edge is soft, not sharp. Use the stick to find the void before your boot does. When you climb out, you’ll need it because the lip will feel 6-12 inches higher on exit. No one knows why.

**Leave Behind:**

*   **Anything you want to keep clean.** Chalk, pitons, hammers, sample bags. Do not take rock. Do not take water. Do not take one of those translucent stems. The place is 100% new. A sample taken out will keep trying to grow or weep in your pack, in your house. And the hole you leave will weep a little faster to compensate.
*   **Loud gear.** Trekking poles that click, crinkly jackets, radios. Leave the Bluetooth speaker at camp with extreme prejudice.
*   **Expectations of a second visit.** Don’t plan to come back with better gear. First impressions are physical here.

### 4. THE APPROACH & THE VISIT

**Finding it:** You won’t find it looking for it. Walk the plain slowly, eyes soft, not scanning. Feel for a change in wind temperature - a cool exhale from nowhere, a place where lichen suddenly stops. When your stick finds nothing, you’ve found it. Mark your entry point with something impermanent and natural - a small cairn *away* from the lip, not on it.

**Entering:** Do not jump. Sit on the soft edge, swing your legs over, find purchase on the blistered ripples. The basalt looks smooth but it’s pocked with fist-sized holds. Descend facing the wall, like a ladder. Keep your mouth closed against the first wash of cool air so you don’t gasp and hyperventilate.

**While you are inside, obey etiquette:**

1.  **Do not step inside the knee-high stone ring** by the paper-bark tree. The silt inside is always damp. That’s not a puddle, that’s a cistern, a focus. The pale sprouts point at it. To step inside is to stand in the drinking bowl. You will be very politely asked to leave by the light pooling brighter around your boots.
2.  **Do not look straight into the woven dome** on the eastern bulge. Glance, admire the clever weave, note the perfect round door facing the light. Do not kneel to peer inside. That interior darkness is not shadow, it’s the *absence* of the rule that light has weight. It’s heavier than it should be. Staring invites vertigo and a headache that lasts for days.
3.  **Do not fit your hand to the handprint.** Don’t even hover over it. See it, acknowledge it, move on. It’s holding a bead of water that never falls because the stone never finished deciding to let go. Your hand is the wrong shape and the stone is still impressionable.
4.  **Do not feed, touch, or block the fauna.** Give each thing its lane. If a wingless dragonfly is hovering over a vesicle, wait. It will be gone in seconds. If a pale amphibian is crossing your path, freeze until it has passed. They are not afraid of you, they are *making room* for you. Respect that spaciousness. You’ll notice after about 30 minutes they have all, separately, oriented toward you without moving. That’s not threat. That’s census. You have been counted as fauna. Be still, be boring, and the count will move on.
5.  **Do not stay for the second mist.** When the walls start to bead faster and the air goes silver, start climbing. The mist is beautiful and it will make you want to breathe deep and stay. It’s the stone sighing. You will feel calm and sleepy. That calm is not yours. Climb before it gets thick.

### 5. THE EXIT

Climbing out is harder. The distance has stretched. Your arms will get more tired than they should. Do not rush, do not look down to check the floor - the floor will look farther away than when you descended and you will feel you are making no progress. Look at the wall in front of your face. Hand over hand. The stone is warm and will help you a little - it sweats under your gloves and gives just slightly.

When you pull yourself over the lip onto the flat basalt plain, roll away from the edge before standing. Don’t immediately turn and look back. Stand, breathe the thin wind air for ten count, let your ears pop back to normal scale. Then look.

It will be gone. Or rather, flat and empty. Until you look at it from the corner of your eye and there’s that soft, cool exhale again.

Walk away without checking your watch. Check it after you’ve walked a hundred steps on the plain.

**Afterwards:**

You will be cold for longer than you should be, even in sun. Your boots will have light dust on them that doesn’t brush off until you wash them properly. For a night or two you will dream of the tree that points at the wall instead of the sun, and you will wake up feeling like your bedroom is the wrong size.

Don’t go back the next day. The place needs to forget your shape a little.

If you did it right, you will have been an ordinary human who visited a place where the world was still wet, and you left no handprint of your own.
