This is a Nursery. Not a forest, not a garden - a womb that got stuck open.

If you go, you are not visiting a place that was made *for* life. You are visiting a place that is *making* itself, and life is leaking out of the process as a side effect. It's not hostile. It won't attack you. It just has absolutely no system in place to keep you alive. You are contamination and you are irrelevant. That is more dangerous than hostility.

An ordinary human can go there and come back intact, but only if you stop thinking of it as outdoors. Think of it as entering an industrial clean room that happens to be made of geology.

Here's how you prepare:

### 1. Understand What You're Actually Entering

This is very young rock. Newborn. Still cooling, still contracting, still off-gassing its birth. In our world valleys and plains are old, weathered, softened, wet. This has had none of that.

*   It is **hot and dry in a way deserts aren't.** A desert has water hidden somewhere. This has *never* had water. It's kiln-dry. It will actively pull water out of you.
*   It is **geologically alive.** The floor is not ground, it's slag. The walls are not walls, they're pistons. The mountain is breathing and manufacturing new stone from below. The geography is not stable.
*   It is **sparse by design.** The dozen plants there are not struggling to survive. They are the intended product. They run on heat, salt, and mineral vapor, not water and soil. You run on the opposite.
*   Physics is **slightly unsealed.** Gravity gradients, light that pools instead of reflecting, sound that disappears, shadows that lag. Your body expects consistent rules and will get motion-sick and disoriented if you aren't ready for it.

Go in expecting a quiet, bright, brutally hot stone room with terrible air-conditioning. Not a hike.

### 2. Body Preparation - This Place Will Dehydrate You In Minutes

You will lose water faster than you realize because your sweat will vanish instantly and your breath will be stripped.

**Water & Salt:**
*   Bring at least 3x more water than you think you need for the time you plan to stay. For a 2-hour visit, carry 3-4 liters *on you*, not in your pack on the ridge. You will not find a single drop inside.
*   Bring electrolytes. Not just water. The dryness and the metallic mist will pull salt from you and also crust salt onto you. You need to replace it. Oral rehydration salts are perfect.
*   Pre-hydrate for 24 hours before. No alcohol, no caffeine that day.
*   Bring heavy lip balm, saline nasal spray, and artificial tears. Your lips will crack in 10-15 minutes, your nose will bleed, your eyes will feel like sandpaper. Reapply constantly.

**Heat & Breathing:**
*   The air is 20 degrees hotter inside than outside, still, and full of hot iron, ozone, and sulfur vapor. Think: standing inside a blacksmith's forge.
*   You need a respirator. Not a dust mask - a real half-face respirator with organic vapor / acid gas cartridges. The ozone and sulfur will give you a headache, then nausea, then chemical irritation in under an hour without it. You will still smell it faintly - that's okay.
*   Clothing: Loose, light-colored, long-sleeves and long pants in a breathable but tightly woven fabric. You want to cover *all* skin from the pooled, magnified sunlight and the hot mineral dust, but you need it to vent. No shorts. No tank tops. A wide-brimmed hat that shades your neck. The sun is high, vertical, and it doesn't scatter - it just sits on you.
*   Footwear: Sturdy, thick-soled boots with ankle support. The floor is porous scoria - sharp, warm, slightly yielding, and it ticks as it contracts. Thin soles will let the heat through. You will feel the deep THUD... THUD... through your soles. A soft sole makes it worse.
*   Bring high-SPF sunscreen and UV-blocking sunglasses. Light pools in the corners. You can get burned in shade.

Do not touch the plants. Do not cut them. That fluid is not water, it's a hot, concentrated brine/molten salt. It will cause chemical burns and it is not water no matter how thirsty you are. The white crust leeward of each plant is salt fallout - don't sit in it, don't touch your face after touching it.

### 3. Sensory & Psychological Preparation

This is the part most travelers fail.

**Sound:** It is profoundly quiet. The hot, porous stone eats sound. You will hear your own heartbeat, the blood in your ears, the creak of a frond expanding. That quiet has weight. People get anxious and start talking loudly to fill it - don't. Conserve water, conserve breath. Bring earplugs if silence makes you dizzy, but better is to just prepare to be quiet for a while.

**Scale & Gravity:** Your sense of scale will slip. It looks 40 meters across from the lip and 60 meters once you're inside. Trust your measuring tools, not your eyes. Gravity is wrong - dust hangs too long and then falls too hard. You may feel slightly heavy on the floor and light-headed near the lip. Move slowly and deliberately. Don't jump. Don't run. Your inner ear will be confused; slow movements prevent nausea.

**Light & Time:** The light doesn't bounce. It accumulates like dust. Shadows lag half a second behind their objects when the deep engine thuds. If you watch this too long you will get a headache and a sense of vertigo. Don't stare at shadows. Look at the plants, look at the wall, then look away to the sky to reset your eyes.

The thud itself - every ~60 seconds, a subsonic sigh you feel more than hear - the plants lean toward *it*, not the sun. Your brain will want to orient toward the sun. Let it go. Orient with the thud if you need a compass.

**Loneliness:** There are no animals. No insects. No bird shadows. No pollinators. The absence is absolute. Humans are wired to hear life and when there is none, we feel watched. You are not. The pocket doesn't notice you except for the heat-ghost you leave in the air for a second after you pass. It doesn't want anything. That indifference can feel more unsettling than a threat. Go in knowing you will feel like an intruder in a factory where the machines are still building the factory.

### 4. Gear & Protocol

**Bring:**
*   Water + electrolytes, respirator, hat, sunglasses, covered clothing, thick boots
*   A walking stick to test the floor - some slag is still soft and warm
*   A timer. Decide *before* you enter how long you will stay. 45-90 minutes is plenty for a first visit. The dryness and the monotony of the thud distort time perception.
*   A way to mark the lip. The pocket looks like nothing from the ridge - just a shadow between stone. From inside, the lip looks higher or lower than it is. Leave a bright, weighted marker at your entry point so you can find your way out without climbing a glass-sharp wall.
*   A closed container for any trash, including salt crust you brush off. Leave nothing. This place has no bacteria to break down your crumbs, no water to wash away your sweat. Even a drop of sweat will leave a salt stain that will be there for a century until the pocket seals.

**Do Not Bring:**
*   Anything you don't want to get a fine white salt coating on
*   Open flames - the mineral vapor is not flammable but it will foul a flame and the ozone will make you cough more
*   Drones or noisy recorders - sound doesn't carry and the heat will kill the battery fast

**Movement Rules:**
1.  Enter from the lip, not by sliding down. The basalt edges are conchoidal glass. They will cut you like obsidian. Test every handhold.
2.  Stay on the cooler, darker slag where you can. The rust-red patches are warmer.
3.  Keep at least 2 meters from the piston wall on the east side. When it exhales, that sulfurous breath is scalding. Watch seams - if a seam that was closed is now a finger-width open, you're watching it move.
4.  Don't stand under the black glass stalactites. The amber bead that falls every few hours is molten mineral. It hardens into slag, but while falling it will burn through clothing and skin.
5.  Don't sit or lie on the floor for long. You are losing heat/water through contact and the thud transfers through the rock into your bones.

### 5. Exit

Leave before you feel you need to. The symptoms of heat exhaustion there are delayed by the dryness - you won't feel sweaty, you'll just suddenly be dizzy, with a pounding head and dry mouth.

When you climb back out, the air outside will feel cold and thin even though it's still hot. Your sweat will suddenly work again. Drink slowly. Rinse your nose and eyes. Your clothes will smell of hot iron for days.

And understand what you saw: not a garden that is growing, but a garden being *extruded*. The pocket is slowly closing. In a thousand years it will be a solid inclusion of pressed leaves and salt inside the mountain. You were there for the one brief moment it was open, lit like an ordinary noon, holding a dozen impossible, waxy, oil-filled things that shiver and make more of themselves from heat.

Don't try to take one. It won't live outside its machine, and its machine is the mountain. Take the memory of the quiet, the thud, and the way the light pooled in the corners like dust, and leave the room exactly as you found it.
