You are going to a Still World. Not a dead world, not a ruined world, not an empty world. A world on pause at exactly the right moment.

Most dangerous places in the multiverse try to kill you. This one will try to *keep* you, by making you forget you need to leave.

Here is how to prepare, as an ordinary, mortal, linear human:

### 1. Understand What It Is Before You Go

Do not go thinking it is a ghost town, a lost civilization, or a high-altitude Tibet analogue. It is none of those.

Geologically, it's a newborn plateau - basalt and limestone still sharp, cliffs that haven't learned to shed talus, no river valleys yet. Ecologically, it's impossible - lush but thin, damp but with no rain, full of life but with nothing crowded. Urbanistically, it's a city that was built for a future that was then cancelled. Wide streets, full infrastructure, hand-cut stone and adzed timber and hammered copper, all at the quality of a people who intended to live there for a thousand years. And they do. Forever.

The physics rule is: **Equilibrium without entropy.**

Things don't decay, don't grow, don't erode, don't heal, don't progress. The bean plant has flowers and ripe pods at the same time and will have them when you come back tomorrow. The woman mending the pot will still be mending the pot. Your footprint in the moss will stay razor-crisp. The sun will stay at 10:30 AM. Condensation will bead but never drip. The tarns will reflect a cloud that passed an hour ago.

It is not frozen like ice. It breathes. You can walk, talk, pick, eat, work. But the world does not *accumulate* your actions. It resets around you, gently and constantly, to the same perfect moment.

That is the hazard. You will not notice it at first. That is *designed* into the hazard.

> That last line in the description is the warning: *"It is unusual only slightly - not a spectacle, just an afterthought that nags."*

Your brain will explain it away. "Oh, the light is just weird at altitude." "Oh, that finch looks familiar." You will rationalize the stillness for hours until you have wasted your window to leave. Prepare to distrust your own normalization.

### 2. Physical Preparation - The Body Thinks It's Just A Mountain

Even without the temporal effect, the plateau will stress an ordinary body. Prepare for 3,500 - 4,500m equivalent.

**Breath & Heart:**
* Acclimatize for 48 hours at moderate altitude before you jump. If you can't, bring acetazolamide and know how to use it.
* Expect shortness of breath, poor sleep, headache, slow thinking. You will be dumber and more complacent than usual - which makes the cognitive hazard worse.
* Move deliberately. The locals do for a reason. Rushing will make you dizzy and will feel profoundly *wrong* there.

**Cold & Light:**
* Air is thin, cool, and dry even when the sun is bright. It's shade-cold, not winter-cold. Bring layers you can add/remove: merino base, windproof softshell, sun hood. You will sweat in sun and chill in shadow within two steps.
* UV is savage. Bring glacier glasses or high-wrap sunglasses, SPF 50+, lip balm. The light is clear and shadow is hard-edged. Snow blindness can happen off pale limestone even without snow. You will be squinting constantly without protection.
* The sun does not move. There is no afternoon. There is no sunset to tell you to stop. You will get burned on one side of your face and not notice.

**Terrain:**
* This is raw tectonics. No trails worn smooth, no scree slopes to climb. Everything is blocky, knife-edged basalt and hogbacks. Bring stiff, high-ankle boots with sticky rubber, not trail runners. Your ankles will roll, and cuts will not heal normally there.
* Gloves. Every stone is still sharp enough to flay a palm.

**Water & Food:**
* Do NOT rely on local water logic. The cisterns are always full. The tarns are always still. The condensation always beads. But nothing flows, nothing replenishes. If you drink from a cistern, you are drinking stasis water. It will hydrate you in the moment but your body won't process it normally.
* Carry 2-3L of your own water and your own calorie-dense food that you *know* is from linear time - bars, dried fruit, electrolytes. Eat and drink by your watch, not by thirst or hunger, because your hunger/thirst signals will flatten out.
* Do not plan to forage. You can pick the beans - they will look perfect and taste perfect - but they are part of the equilibrium. Eating too much local food seems to make people feel more "in tune" and less inclined to leave.

### 3. Temporal & Entropic Preparation - The Real Danger

This is why ordinary humans get lost.

**Your body stops accumulating time:**

* Wounds don't scab. Blisters don't form properly. Food sits in your stomach without digesting fully. You won't get more tired, but you also won't recover. If you enter exhausted, you stay exhausted. If you enter injured, you stay injured. **Enter healthy, rested, fed, and having used the bathroom.**
* You will not age, but you also will not heal. Bring a basic first aid kit but understand it is mostly for *after* you exit. Inside, a bandage will just sit there.
* Women: menstrual cycles pause. If you are on timed medication, insulin, etc., talk to a jump medic first. The plateau plays havoc with anything that requires metabolic progression.

**Your gear stops accumulating time:**

* Mechanical watches stop or tick without advancing. Digital watches freeze or loop. Phones stay at the same battery percentage. GPS will think you haven't moved.
* Bring a purely mechanical, wind-up timer you can *hear* - a kitchen timer, a wind-up metronome, a sand timer. Something that makes a *process* you can witness. Even better, bring two and keep one outside the jump field with a partner.
* Cameras will take photos, but when you review them, the shadows will all be at the same angle even if hours have "passed." Don't use photos to judge time spent.

**The Stillness is Contagious:**

The most dangerous symptom is contentment. Visitors report feeling calm, clear-headed, and oddly relieved of anxiety after ~30 minutes. That's not peace. That's your brain syncing to a world with no consequences and therefore no urgency.

Countermeasures:
* **External Anchor:** Never go alone. Have a tether partner outside the plateau, or a hard extraction timer - a physical pull cord, a recall beacon set for 90-120 minutes subjective maximum. Do not rely on your own judgment to leave.
* **Interval Ritual:** Set your audible timer for every 15 minutes. When it dings, you must do three things: say the date and your name out loud, drink water, and write one sentence in a paper notebook: *What changed since the last ding?* Inside, the answer will almost always be "nothing." That written record is what breaks the spell.
* **Do Not Set Things Down and Forget.** Anything warm stays warm. Anything placed stays exactly placed. This creates a terrible déjà vu. You will swear you just saw that hare, that finch, that cloud. You did. The world is looping micro-events. If you notice a loop, it is time to leave, not investigate further.

### 4. Psychological Preparation - The Quiet Will Get Louder Than Noise

This place is sparse by design. Houses 50 paces apart. Streets too wide. Life everywhere but never dense. Sound dies fast. There is no crowd, no market roar, no river.

For a normal human, that registers as loneliness, then as exposure.

* **Prepare for silence without solitude.** There are people there. They are not hostile. They are not helpful. They are occupied in eternal low-level tasks - mending, weeding, carrying. If you speak to them, they will answer politely and then return to what they were doing, as if you never interrupted. They are not in a loop like robots; they are in equilibrium like a plant holding its shape. It is profoundly alienating to be the only thing that wants a conversation to *go somewhere.*
* Do not try to "fix" the sparsity. Do not try to help finish the aqueduct, clear a garden, or bring houses closer together. Your work will not accumulate. You will feel strangely tired and futile.
* Bring something human and *linear* for comfort: music with a beginning, middle, and end on a offline player with visible progress bar, a book you haven't finished, a small whittling project with shavings that pile up. You need proof that *you* still progress even if the world doesn't.
* Expect derealization on exit. Re-entry to a changing world after even two hours in stasis can feel violent - lights too bright, sounds too layered, wind that actually carries dust. Plan for 24 hours of low stimulation after you return. Don't schedule anything important right after.

### 5. Practical Kit List For The Ordinary Traveler

* High-ankle approach boots, broken in
* Windproof layers + sun hood + glacier glasses + sunscreen
* 2-3L personal water + electrolytes + 1000 kcal of trail food
* Acetazolamide if you are altitude-sensitive
* Paper notebook + pencil [pens can fail in cold/thin air]
* Two wind-up audible timers + one hourglass/sand timer
* Recall beacon / tether partner / hard extraction plan - NON-NEGOTIABLE
* Offline music/book + earbud in one ear only [you need to hear the quiet]
* Minimal first aid - mostly for after extraction
* No souvenirs. Do not take stone, water, or plants. They are stasis-anchored and travelers report they feel "heavy" and make recall harder. If you must, take only a photo and your notes.

### 6. How To Be There Well

* Walk in the middle of the wide streets. Let the emptiness be what it is. Don't hug the walls.
* Sit on a low wall and just watch one tarn for ten minutes. Notice that it doesn't ripple. Notice that the moss doesn't sway. Let your intuition catch up to your eyes.
* Be polite and brief with locals. A nod, a low "hello." Don't demand stories. Don't ask "how long have you been here?" They don't count.
* Leave footprints if you want, but know they will stay. Leave no trash. Leave no damage.
* When your second timer dings, leave even if you feel you just got there. Especially if you feel you just got there. The feeling of "I could stay a little longer, it's so peaceful" is the plateau working.

You don't visit this place to conquer it or to be transformed. You visit it to witness what a world looks like when it has achieved perfect balance and paid the price of never becoming anything else.

Go, see the sharp stone and the lush gardens and the wide, quiet city held like a breath. Let your own breath be the one thing that moves. And then, while you still remember that moving is a gift, go home and let yourself change again.
