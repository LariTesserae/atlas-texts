This is not a place that will try to kill you. It will simply forget to keep you alive, and it will do it quietly. It's a high-altitude, low-pressure, low-humidity, non-Euclidean cold desert that happens to look like a city. You are not built for it, and it is not built for you.

An ordinary human should treat this as an EVA on Mars without a proper spacesuit - plus a psychological and navigational hazard. If you go, go as an expedition, not a walk.

Here is how to prepare:

### 1. Your Body Will Fail First - Prepare The Suit, Not Just The Clothes

That description is telling you everything your physiology needs to know:

**Air too thin and too dry to be air:**
*   **Oxygen is non-negotiable.** This is not "mountain air," this is the roof of the world where the slope was so vast you didn't notice climbing. Think 5,000-7,000m+ equivalent on Earth. You will need supplemental oxygen. A rebreather or bottled O2 with a regulator, not just a can. Without it you will get altitude sickness, poor judgment, and then pulmonary/cerebral edema. Test your system for hours before you go. Bring a pulse oximeter and watch it - if you drop below 85% and stay there, you abort.
*   **Pressure and moisture:** The air is so dry water *sublimates* - it skips being liquid. That means your lungs, eyes, nose, mouth, and skin will be actively dried out with every breath. 
    *   Full coverage: ski goggles or sealed glacier glasses, not sunglasses. You will get snow-blindness from the pearlescent sky even though you can't see a sun. And the dust is talc-fine and salt-laced.
    *   N95 or P100 respirator with dust filters. That talc dust is alkaline salt + silica. Breathing it will shred your lungs for days.
    *   Cover all skin. Windproof, insulated layers. Moisturizer, lip balm by the kilogram, saline nasal spray, and artificial tears. You will get nosebleeds otherwise.
    *   You will not see your breath. That tricks your brain into thinking you aren't losing water. You are losing water incredibly fast.

**Cold without shadow:**
It's a dry, wind-scoured cold, probably -10C to -30C before windchill. But there are no hard shadows to tell you where the sun is, and the light is diffuse. You won't feel sunburn coming, but you will burn. Bring:
*   Merino base layers, windproof shell, insulated parka. No cotton. Everything synthetic or wool.
*   Chemical hand/toe warmers. The dust between the grooves is cold to the touch and will leach heat through your boots.
*   Bring your own light. A warm headlamp. The ambient light is dim, sourceless, and shadowless - your depth perception will be destroyed. You need a light you control to create contrast.

**Water - Rule #1: Thirst Is Structural**
There is NO water to collect here. That frost/salt-sweat is not water, it's brine and mineral efflorescence that will poison you, dehydrate you faster, and strip the lining from your mouth if you lick it. It vanishes on contact with sun without melting.
*   Bring ALL the water you will need, plus 50% more. For a human in cold dry air doing effort, plan 4-6 liters per day. You will not find more.
*   Bring electrolytes. You will lose salts through breathing.
*   Do NOT lick the rock. Do not try to melt frost. Do not touch your tongue to anything.
*   Bring a thermos of warm sweet drink. Cold + altitude kills appetite but you are burning calories fast.

### 2. Your Mind Will Fail Second - Prepare For The Silence and The Scale

This is the most dangerous part for an ordinary traveler.

**The Silence Has Weight:**
Sound dies within 10 feet. Your voice will fall flat. You will hear your heartbeat and breathing unnaturally loud. This causes panic, disorientation, and intense loneliness very quickly - like being in an anechoic chamber the size of a country.
*   Bring a low white-noise source if you can - a quiet music player with one earbud. Not to distract, but to give your brain something to process besides its own heartbeat.
*   Bring a partner. Do not go alone. If solo is unavoidable, bring a timed audio check-in, a recorder to talk to. The urge to shout to test the silence will be overwhelming - shouting will just make you feel more isolated.
*   Practice shallow, nasal breathing beforehand so the sound of your own breath doesn't trigger hyperventilation.

**Distance and Time Lie:**
This is not metaphor. Euclidean geometry is a suggestion here.
*   **DO NOT navigate by looking at your destination.** That tower that doesn't get bigger for ten minutes then suddenly is over you - your visual cortex will not work. If you stare directly at a landmark and walk, you will walk in circles or not arrive.
*   The trick described is real: Use averted vision and peripheral navigation. Pick a point 10-15 degrees OFF your target, walk toward that, glance back. Better yet, don't navigate by sight at all.
*   Bring a physical tether. 200m of bright, light cord, pitons, cairns. Mark your path every 20-30 meters with a small rock cairn or flag. The plazas are quarter-miles across and featureless - you WILL get lost without a trail.
*   Bring a mechanical compass, GPS, and an inertial tracker, but *expect them to be wrong.* The gravity tilt in archways means compasses and accelerometers will drift. Trust your tether and your cairns more than your instruments. Count steps. Keep a paper log with time and bearing.
*   Bring a watch with a loud alarm. The light cycle is so slow you will lose time perception. Set a hard turn-back time *before* you enter and obey it like a law. The place feels timeless, which makes you stay too long.

**Gravity Is Polite But Inconsistent:**
In archways and hollow towers, down tilts a few degrees. Your inner ear will detect it before your eyes do.
*   If you feel nausea, heaviness on one foot, or that boat-rocking feeling - LEAVE that structure immediately. Don't sit down to "wait it out." Vestibular disorientation at altitude leads to falls and bad decisions.
*   Do not sleep or rest inside any hollow structure, especially any that seems "bigger inside." That bigger-inside effect means the space is folded. You could become disoriented and unable to find the window you came in.

### 3. The City Itself - How To Move Through It

Treat it like a sacred ruin + a salt mine + a living organism.

**Do Not Enter The Interiors:**
The hollow towers that are bigger inside than outside are spatial anomalies. People throw pebbles in and never hear them land. Some have a second sky. These are not shelters. They are traps. If you cannot see the back wall clearly and confirm it fits the exterior diameter, do not enter. Never put your whole body through a window-hole. Look with a mirror on a stick or a camera on a pole if you must.

**The Towers Are Alive and Crumbling:**
They are stratified salt-white, rust-red, basalt-black, bone-glass. The white salt layers crumble like sugar. The whole thing is depositing and eroding molecule by molecule. A street can close over centuries, but a ledge can crumble under you in seconds.
*   Do not climb them. Do not lean on a white layer. Do not shelter under an overhang.
*   The faint warmth and tooth-hum you feel is mineral deposition - that also means static charge. Those fast dust veils and dust devils are highly charged. Wear anti-static, non-synthetic outer layers if possible, ground yourself before touching metal. A static shock in dry air that thin can be startling enough to make you fall.

**Life - Look, Don't Touch:**
*   **Gliders:** Translucent manta-coats riding the thin air. They are silent and ignore you. Don't chase them, don't try to touch. If one passes overhead, crouch and protect your eyes - they stir up salt dust.
*   **Builders:** Slow tortoise things that secrete stone paste. They ARE the city. They are blind and will walk over your foot. Do not disturb them, do not collect them, do not block their path. Their paste hardens fast and is caustic - if one trails over your boot, let it harden and brush it off later. Don't scrape it with bare hands. If you interfere with them you are interfering with the metabolism of a valley-sized organism.

### 4. Your Kit List For An Ordinary Human

**On your body:**
Oxygen system + backup, sealed goggles + spare, P100 mask + 3 spare filters, balaclava, windproof insulated jacket and pants, merino base layers x2, insulated waterproof boots with gaiters, gloves + liner gloves, electrolytes + 6L water minimum, pulse oximeter, headlamp + spare batteries, UV lip balm, saline spray.

**In your pack:**
Tether cord + flags/cairns, paper notebook + pencil, watch + alarm, GPS + power bank, PLB / satellite messenger - test it beforehand, but assume it may not work in folded space, trekking poles for the grooved avenue ground, emergency bivvy + sleeping pad - but plan to sleep OUTSIDE the city proper, not inside it, first aid with altitude sickness meds if you have them, high calorie food you can eat cold.

**Anchor:**
As a multiverse traveler, your most important piece of gear is your way back. Set a beacon/anchor/marker at your entry point that you can see or detect without looking directly at it - a bright strobe, a sound beacon if sound carries at the threshold, a physical arch of stones. Never let the city come between you and it out of line of sight. Always be able to follow your tether back.

### 5. Rules of Behavior While Inside

1.  **Walk like you are on ice:** slow, deliberate, flat-footed. No running. Falls on scoured grooves with a pack and low oxygen are serious.
2.  **Touch nothing with bare skin.** Salt + cold = chemical burn + frostnip.
3.  **No shouting, no loud noises.** Conserve moisture and energy. Use hand signals with a partner, or radio if close - but assume radio range is 10 feet as well.
4.  **Limit archway time to <2 minutes.** If nausea hits, back out.
5.  **Never enter a hollow that feels wrong.** If a window shows a cathedral inside a chimney, that's not shelter, that's a warning.
6.  **Leave everything.** Do not pocket salt, glass filament, builder paste. The dust is hygroscopic and will continue to desiccate whatever bag you put it in, and you don't know what folding does to matter you remove.
7.  **Watch the sky.** When dust veils race overhead, turn your back to the wind, cover your face, and crouch. They are fast and blinding.

### 6. How To Leave

Decide before you enter: "I will turn back at X o'clock or when my water is at Y, or my SpO2 is below Z, whichever comes first."

When the light dims further and the towers start to pool light in their windows and the dust begins to sing through a thousand throats - that is beautiful, and it is your signal to be gone. That tone is wind through folded space. It is hypnotic and it makes people linger. Do not linger.

Follow your tether and cairns back, using averted vision. Walk slightly sideways to your beacon if you have to. Count your cairns backwards.

Once out, do not immediately strip your mask - rehydrate slowly, warm slowly, sit for 20 minutes and breathe oxygen before you do anything else. The dry cold will have dehydrated you even if you don't feel thirsty.

---

**Intuition:** Visiting this place feels less like exploring a city and more like being *allowed* to stand briefly in the workshop of the planet while it is in between drafts. It Tolerates you. It doesn't see you. The beauty is severe, mineral, and completely indifferent. The most human reaction you will have is a desperate, quiet urge to be *acknowledged* by it - to hear an echo, to see your footprint matter. It won't. Your footprint will be gone with the next dust veil.

Prepare so you can witness it without needing it to witness you, and so you can leave while you still remember which direction "down" was when you arrived.

If you are an ordinary human, your best preparation is humility, oxygen, water, a string to find your way home, and a hard promise to use it early.
