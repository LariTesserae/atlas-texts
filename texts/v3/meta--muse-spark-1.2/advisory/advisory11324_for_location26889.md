This is not a destination you travel *to*. It's a destination you drop *out* into. You have to understand that before you pack anything.

Most places in the multiverse are variations on a world - more air, less gravity, different light. This is not a variation. This is a pocket. A flaw. A thing that was made *inside* the world and then sealed off from it. You don't hike to it, you notice that the world has thinned and you let yourself fall through.

If you go, you need to prepare for three things at once: a desert, a drum, and a womb. And you need to prepare as an ordinary, soft, wet, noisy human for a place that is dry, loud, still, and totally complete without you.

Here is how you do it:

### 1. Understand What It Will Do To You

Your body will not like this place at first, and your mind will like it even less if you are not ready.

**It is a sensory contradiction.** It is blindingly bright but cool. You expect bright = hot. Here the light is cold. It comes from everywhere at once through translucent fibers, like being inside a paper lantern. There are no shadows to rest your eyes on. After 2 minutes you will squint, water, and get a headache if your eyes are unprotected. Snow-blindness can happen fast on the salt pan outside, and again inside.

**It is desiccating.** The air is cellar-cool and absolutely dry. Not desert-dry - *lab desiccator* dry. Your lips will chap in minutes, your nose will dry and may bleed, your throat will feel like you inhaled chalk. The pocket is in equilibrium. It has spent centuries drying out until the last trace of brine is just a white crust on the fibers. It will try to dry *you* out to match it.

**It is loud and unchanging.** This is the big one. Outside you hear a thin hiss of wind over salt. Inside, the mouth acts like the neck of a bottle and the whole woven dome acts like a harp body. You will hear/feel a single, complex chord - a low thrum in your chest and teeth with a high papery buzz on top - at about the volume of a loud shower or a busy road. You will have to shout to hear yourself.

And it never, ever changes. No gusts, no lulls, no attack or decay. Your brain is built to track change. When sound and light and temperature do not change at all, your sense of time breaks. A minute feels like an hour. An hour feels like a minute. People panic not because it's scary, but because it's *too* still. It feels like being paused.

**It is intimate and total.** This is not a cave you stand in. It is 4 meters across and you cannot stand. You sit, or lie on your side with knees bent. Every surface - floor, wall, ceiling - is the same continuous, drum-tight weave. No seam, no corner, no patch of raw earth to remind you of the outside. You are held. For some people this is profoundly calming, like being in a nest. For anyone with claustrophobia, it is immediate, primal dread. The mouth is exactly shoulder-width. You have to commit your whole body to get in and out. There is no casual peeking.

**It is finished.** This is the most important law of the place: *Nothing changes here.* Nothing grows, nothing rots, nothing moves. The lichen and the wiry air-plants on the walls are not growing or dying, just paused. There are no insects, no drafts that eddy, no dust motes. If you bring dust, moisture, heat, noise - you are the only thing that is out of equilibrium. The place will not adapt to you. You will feel like an intruder, because you are.

It is not hostile. It does not want to hurt you. It just has no use for you at all.

### 2. How To Prepare Your Body and Gear

You are a wet, salty, shedding, metallic human. Pack to minimize how much of that you impose on the pocket, and how much the pocket takes from you.

**For the Plain outside:**
The salt pan is featureless, white to every horizon, reflective, and windy. You will get lost without a tracker.

*   **Navigation and Safety:** Never go alone. You need one person who stays *outside* at the mouth the entire time. Bring a GPS beacon, a physical compass, sun-shade, and mark your approach line with low poles or flags. The saucer is only a meter deep and 20 meters wide - you will walk right past it if the light is flat. Tell someone far away your coordinates and return time.
*   **Sun and Skin:** Full coverage. Light long-sleeve natural fiber shirt, light pants, wide hat, UV sunglasses for outside - you will want to take them off inside - and strong sunscreen. The white pan reflects like snow.
*   **Water:** Bring far more than you think. At least 3-4 liters for a half-day, even though you will be cool. You lose water fast through your airway here without sweating. Add electrolytes and salty snacks. Bring lip balm, saline nasal spray, and eye drops. Use them before you get dry.

**For the Threshold:**
*   **Knees and Hands:** You have to kneel on hard, blistered salt crust and then put your hands on the braided lip to look in. Bring firm knee pads and thin, close-fitting gloves - cotton or undyed canvas. Not for warmth, for abrasion. The braid is hard and can be sharp on bare skin, and the salt will sting any micro-cut. Thin gloves still let you feel the coolness and the faint vibration.
*   **Upper Body Strength:** The mouth is shoulder-width and vertical for the first half-meter. You will lower yourself in by your arms and pull yourself out the same way. There are no ladder rungs, just weave to grip. If you cannot do a slow, controlled pull-up from a tight hole, do not go in. Bring a short, soft natural-fiber rope - hemp or cotton - for your outside partner to offer as a handhold, not to anchor inside. Do NOT hammer pitons or tie off to the braid. It is not rock. It is tensioned fiber.
*   **Clothing Inside:** Wear soft, matte, natural fibers with no loose shedding - no fleece, no down, no glitter. Empty your pockets completely before you enter. No crumbs, no hair ties, no loose dust. The pocket has no dust; don't add any. Leave metal, plastic, and glass outside if you can. Not because it is forbidden, but because metal buzzes and plastic squeaks against the dry weave and will feel violently out of place, and you will feel it vibrating.

**For Inside:**
*   **Ears:** This is non-negotiable. Bring vented earplugs or low-profile ear defenders. Not to block sound completely, but to take the edge off so you don't get acoustic fatigue and a ringing headache in 10 minutes. You still want to hear the chord, but you want to be able to think through it. If you have tinnitus, hyperacusis, or sound sensitivity, this place will be overwhelming.
*   **Eyes:** Bring tinted glasses for inside - a light amber or grey, not dark. The diffuse white light is fatiguing. You need to be able to see the beautiful edge-glow of the fibers without squinting.
*   **Breath:** Bring a light buff or bandana to breathe through if your throat is sensitive. The air is so dry it will catch. Breathe slowly through your nose. Do not bring a water bottle inside to sip. One spill would be an ecological event in there. Hydrate well *before* you go in and *after* you come out.
*   **Time:** Bring a watch that vibrates. You will lose time. Decide before you enter: "I will be inside for 15 minutes" and set a silent vibration alarm. When it goes off, leave, even if you feel like you just arrived. 15-20 minutes is plenty for a first visit. 40 minutes is a long time for most people. Do not try to nap or meditate for an hour the first time. The stillness can become dissociative.
*   **Light:** Do NOT bring a flashlight. You don't need it. Adding a point source of light ruins the one beautiful thing about the place - that the whole room is the light.

### 3. How To Prepare Your Mind

This is as important as water.

*   **If you are claustrophobic, do not force it.** Sit at the lip first. Put your head and shoulders in. Listen. Feel the vibration in your palms. You have still visited it. You do not have to fully enter to understand it.
*   **Expect the Held Feeling.** Four strides across, ceiling just high enough to sit upright in the center, walls curving in to meet you. It is an organ, not a room. People describe it as womb-like or like being inside a throat. You may feel comforted, or suddenly infantile, or suddenly panicked to get out. All of those are normal. Name it beforehand: "I am going to feel held."
*   **Practice Stillness Beforehand.** The pocket does not change. Your job is to not demand that it change. For a few days before, spend 5 minutes sitting in a bright, quiet room doing nothing - no phone, no fidgeting. Just noticing. It will help you not fight the invariance when you are inside.
*   **Have an Exit Script.** Agree with your partner on simple hand signals or rope tugs because you will not hear shouting well. One tug = "I'm okay." Two = "I'm coming out." Three = "Pull me now." Rehearse getting out. The mouth does not close, but your mind can make it feel narrower on the way out if you panic. Move slowly, exhale, make yourself thin, and pull hand over hand.

### 4. Protocol For Visiting

1.  **Arrive, Kneel, Listen.** Don't go straight in. Kneel at the saucer's edge for a few minutes. Let the wind fall in. Look at the braid, the polish, the salty crust, the tiny lichen rosettes. Put your palms on the lip and feel the thrumming.
2.  **Enter Dry and Empty.** Empty pockets, sip water, put in earplugs, put on glasses, breathe out.
3.  **Go In Head-First, Slowly.** Feet last, so you can push back out. Keep your body low, distribute your weight - the woven floor is strong but you are not standing on earth. Don't drag your feet or scuff.
4.  **Sit in the Center.** Put one palm flat on the floor. Feel the coolness and the tremor. Let your eyes adjust. Don't touch or pick at the fibers, the lichen, or the air-plants. They are not growing, but they are not dead. Look closely without touching. Notice how there is no seam anywhere. Notice how the light has no source.
5.  **Stay Till Your Timer.** Breathe slowly. If anxiety rises, focus on the vibration in your palm or count the weave intersections near your knee. Remember: the sound was here before you and will be here after you. You are just listening for a little while.
6.  **Leave Everything.** When you exit, brush no salt into the mouth, leave no water, no hair, no offering. Check your gloves for fibers - do not pull. On the way out you will see the mouth does not close. It does not need to. Just back out the way you came, slow and thin.
7.  **Aftercare Outside.** Sit with your back to the wind away from the saucer for at least 10 minutes in silence. Drink water slowly, re-wet your eyes and nose, let your ears ring down. The plain will seem absurdly big and empty after being held. Don't drive or navigate immediately. Talk through what you felt. Write it down that evening - you will forget how time felt otherwise.

You don't need to be special to go there. That's the point. It's a small, perfect, finished thing that any ordinary human can visit if they come dry, quiet, covered, soft, and ready to be still.

Don't try to bring it back with you. Let it stay loud, bright, cool, and paused without you, the way it was before you found the flaw in the plain.

And make sure someone is holding the rope when you drop out of the world.
