This place is not a lake. It's not a ruin. It's a **container.**

Treat it like you are being invited to climb inside someone else's terrarium, or bioreactor, or lung. It's beautiful, it's warm, it's quiet, and it will try to grow *on you* if you let it. An ordinary human body is not built for it. You can visit, but you need to visit wrong on purpose - as a visitor, not as substrate.

Here is how you do it and come back.

### 1. Understand What You're Actually Walking Into

Forget "mountain lake" in your head. Reframe it:

* **This is an indoor place that is outside.** The air at that altitude should be thin, cold, and dry. It isn't. There is a bubble here - warm, humid, oxygen-rich, pressurized. The meniscus at the rim isn't just surface tension, it's a field holding a sky-ocean in a bowl. That field is what keeps the weather out and the warmth in. If it fails, everything spills. You do not want to be inside when it fails.

* **This is a young, active industrial farm.** Not abandoned. The concrete is still curing, the steel still has sharp edges, the machines are all on and running slow. You are not an explorer of a ruin. You are a mouse walking through a factory floor while the machines are running. Act like it.

* **This is a place with no food chain.** That is the biggest warning. Lush growth + zero animals = something is chemically or physically hostile to animal life, or the system is designed to *be* the only life. The pollen, the spores, the biofilm, the water itself - it hasn't had to contend with being eaten. It will treat any new animal protein as fertilizer or scaffolding. That includes you.

* **Space and gravity are soft here.** The basin looks 60 meters deep but feels kilometers deep. Cold and warm water braids instead of mixing. Vines grow down. A sheet of water stands vertical between plates. Bring your mountain instincts, but do not trust your eyes or your inner ear. Vertigo and nausea are not "in your head" here - they are readings.

The feeling of the place is womb-warm, silent, saturated, patient. It does not want to scare you. It wants you to relax, to float, to stay, to let the moss brush your wrist and not pull away. That is the hazard.

### 2. Preparation BEFORE You Leave

You are an ordinary human. You don't have gills, you don't have root-hairs, you get trench foot.

**Body Prep:**
* Do not go with any open cuts, scrapes, fresh tattoos, sunburn, eczema flare-ups, or sinus/ear infections. Warm, stagnant, photosynthetic water at blood temperature is a perfect incubator for bacteria, fungi, and whatever engineered biofilm is in there. It will colonize a wound in hours.
* No cotton. No loose, dangling clothing. No jewelry or loose straps. Everything you wear will be a trellis.
* Acclimatize to altitude *before* you approach the rim. Hike up slowly over a day if you can. The inside of the bowl is sea-level pressure and humid; the outside is alpine. Going quickly from thin-cold to thick-warm and back will hammer your sinuses and lungs.

**Anchor Outside:**
As a multiverse traveler, do your normal displacement prep *outside* the rim, not inside. Set your recall point / anchor / beacon / tether on the basalt trail, 10-20 meters *below* the concrete lip on the outside of the tower. Do not set it on the rim wall or on the stair. The field at the meniscus plays hell with compasses, altimeters, GPS, and anything that measures "where" you are. If you need to snap back, you want to snap to clean mountain air, not to mid-water.

Tell someone where you're going. Leave a timed note. This place is silent - no one will hear you shout.

### 3. Kit List - For a Short Visit [20-40 minutes max]

You do not need dive gear. In fact, you should not bring dive gear unless you are trained and willing to accept you may get tangled and drown. The safest visit keeps your head above water the whole time.

**Essentials:**

1.  **Feet & Hands:** Grippy canyoneering shoes or neoprene boots with hard soles. The steel is slick with biofilm and moss. Thin, tight, long-cuffed work gloves - the kind with rubber palms. You will need to grip rust-free, sharp-edged steel that still has burrs, and you do not want to touch moss/ferns with bare skin. They are soft for a reason - they want to be touched.

2.  **Skin Coverage:** A full, tight swim skin, rash guard, or thin 1mm neoprene suit with long arms and legs. Not for warmth - for keeping the water *off* your skin. Every inch you leave exposed will be in warm water full of pollen, spores, and micro-fibers. It will itch for days. It will try to root.

3.  **Face:** Low-volume snorkeling mask or swimming goggles + nose clip. The pollen that hangs underwater like smoke is not just pretty. You don't want to inhale it or get it in your eyes. Do not open your mouth underwater. Do not drink.

4.  **Breathing:** The air above the meniscus is breathable but heavy and green-smelling. If you have asthma or are sensitive to mold, bring a simple N95 or half-face respirator for the air portion. The ozone/iron mineral tang means there is ionization happening.

5.  **Tether:** 15-20m of floating paracord or caving rope. Clip one end to your waist, one end to the *outside* railing of the service stair. Not to the interior. The interior is covered in plants that will swallow a carabiner and make it part of the lattice. You want a line that tells you which way is OUT when the light and gravity start to lie to you.

6.  **Cutting Tool:** A small, blunt-tipped bandage shear or river knife on your chest, not your ankle. Vines as thick as your wrist can drift around a leg. You need to be able to cut yourself free one-handed. Do not use it to cut plants for souvenirs.

7.  **Time & Light:** A cheap waterproof watch with a timer. A small waterproof headlamp, even for daytime. The light refracts strangely - pockets under the walkways go black-green and you lose depth perception instantly.

8.  **Dry Bag on the Rim:** Outside your tether point, leave a sealed dry bag with: a large thermos of *clean* drinking water and electrolytes, a dry towel, a thermal blanket or dry hoodie, antiseptic wash, antihistamine, and a second pair of socks. You will be chilled when you get out into real mountain air while still wet, even though the water was warm.

**Do NOT Bring:**
* Fishing or net gear, drones, or anything you plan to submerge and retrieve. It will snag.
* Glass bottles, loose sunscreen, bug spray. The water is a closed system. Don't contaminate it and don't give the biofilm new chemicals to integrate.
* Any animal. Obviously.

### 4. How To Enter and Move

**Approach:** Stop 5 meters before the rim on the outside. Look. Listen. Note the wind, the temperature, the sound. You are about to step into still, silent, humid air. That contrast is jarring - prepare for your ears to pop.

**Entry:** Use the galvanized service stair only. Test every rung. It is new steel, it will be sharp and cold above the waterline, warm beneath. Do not jump. Do not slide on the rail - it's furred with moss.

Go down slowly until the water is at your knees, then stop. Stay there for 2-3 minutes. Let your body adjust to the bath-warmth and the humidity. Look sideways across the water at the opposite wall. That sideways view is correct. Looking *down* through the grating will give you vertigo. If you feel dizzy or your ears feel pressure from the slow turbines, that's your cue that you're sensitive to the field. It's okay to stay knee-deep.

**Rules While Inside:**

1.  **Keep your head above the meniscus.** The most dangerous decision is to dunk your head or try to swim. You can see everything from chest-deep on the stair. If you must go deeper, never go deeper than your shoulders and never let go of the stair with both hands.

2.  **Touch as little as possible, and never pull.** Brush is okay, grab is not. The vegetation has no soil, so *you are soil.* A fern leaning into current will put a root hair on your glove and hold it. Lace your fingers through a railing and a vine will lace through you. If something touches you, move through it slowly and let it slip, don't yank.

3.  **Do not interfere with the machines.** Don't touch the big slow screws, don't block a grate, don't try to turn a light on or off. They are circulating the water so slowly you can barely feel it. Blocking that circulation even for a minute creates a dead zone right around you - warm water goes stagnant, O2 drops, spores concentrate.

4.  **Watch the invisible water.** If you see a distinct ribbon of colder water, or shimmer like heat haze underwater, move away. Those are shear layers that don't mix properly. They will make you cold and disoriented very fast. And stay clear of the vertical rain-wall between the charged plates. It looks amazing. It will also pull the moisture out of your lungs or give you a nasty static burn. Observe from 2 meters.

5.  **No staying still for long.** The system rewards stillness - pollen settles on you, moss brushes you, silt gathers. Count slowly to 60, then shift your weight, move a rung, swish the water with your hand. Remind the place you are an animal that moves.

6.  **Timebox it.** Set your watch timer for 20 minutes *in the water* max for a first visit. You will not feel cold or tired while you're in there - it's too comfortable. That's the trap. Your skin will be macerating, your core temp will be creeping up, and you won't notice until you're out. When the timer beeps, you leave, even if you feel fine. Especially if you feel like you want to stay and float.

### 5. How To Leave

This is as important as going in.

* Come up the stair slowly. Pause with your torso out of the water but your legs still in. Let water sheet off. The meniscus will cling to you like a skin - you will feel it break. Don't splash it violently.
* Outside the rim, rinse immediately if you can. Wipe down with your clean water, especially face, neck, ears, and any skin that was exposed. Dry and get warm. The real mountain air will feel freezing after the greenhouse.
* Do not take anything with you. No leaf, no flower, no water sample in a bottle unless you have a proper quarantine protocol for an extradimensional invasive hydroponic. That white pollen that looks like smoke? It is designed to grow without a pollinator, on steel. It will grow on the inside of your bag.
* Wash all gear in hot water and dilute bleach when you get home. Wash your suit separately. Shower with antiseptic soap. Watch for 2-3 days for rash, itchy eyes, sinus congestion, or a persistent greenish cough. If you get any, tell a doctor you were in warm, stagnant, high-bio-load industrial water.

Decontaminate psychologically, too. This place is quiet in a way that stays with you. The absence of insects, fish, birds - your brain will keep trying to fill it in. You may dream about it. You may feel a weird longing to go back and just lie back on those table-sized lily pads. That is normal. It is also the place doing what it was built to do: be a perfect, complete, contained world that feels better than the windy, thin, imperfect world outside. Remember: it is 60 meters across. You can hold the whole thing in your head. That's why it feels so intimate and so wrong.

If at any point while inside you feel:

* A sudden intense calm / euphoria, like you should just close your eyes and float
* That the bottom is calling you, that you *must* see how deep it really goes
* That a vine or root is gently tightening, not just brushing

Treat those as evacuation signals. Clip your tether, go up one rung at a time, hand over hand, eyes on the outside sky, not the green below.

You don't need to be fearless to visit. You need to be shallow, brief, covered, tethered, and a little bit rude - a guest who doesn't get comfortable, who doesn't take off their shoes, who leaves before dinner.

Go, look *across* the water at the light coming sideways through a sky-ocean, feel the slow push of a turbine on your chest, see the factory-garden breathing slowly without you - and then climb back out over the rim, back into thin cold honest air where things are eaten and things die and nothing holds the water up but a bowl.

That's how you keep it a visit, and not an incorporation.
