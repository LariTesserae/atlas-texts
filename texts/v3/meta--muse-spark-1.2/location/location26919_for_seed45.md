It is night that never ends, but it is not cold.

You are very high.

Not on a mountain - *in* a mountain that is still deciding to be a mountain. A newborn range, raw and unweathered, thrust up so fast the earth hasn't had time to learn how to fall apart yet. The elevation is punishing and absolute. The air is thin enough that each breath feels deliberate, but it is warm. A dry, deep, kiln-warmth that radiates not from a sun - there is no sun here - but from the rock itself.

This is a night desert at the top of the world.

### The Ground Under Your Feet

There has been no water here for a very long time, if ever. Not a trace of riverbed, not a salt crust where a sea evaporated. Not even frost. The mineral is anhydrous and thirsty. The ground is a field of warm, dark basalt and fresh scoria, still sharp enough to cut. It hasn't been rounded by wind or rain because erosion hasn't started yet. This is pre-erosion geology. Embryonic.

Everything is angular, as if chiseled yesterday and left where it fell.

Plates of black glass the size of houses lean against each other at impossible angles, their edges still razor-clean. Needle-spires of cooling lava stand like organ pipes, hissing faintly as they contract. Between them, the scree is not pebbles but splinters - obsidian flakes, pumice cinders, olivine crystals that catch what little light there is and throw it back green. You walk and the ground crunches with a sound like breaking pottery. No soil. No dust even, really - dust requires time and grinding, and there hasn't been enough time.

In the valleys - and there are valleys, broad and open enough that you can see three or four ridges receding into the dark, each one higher than the last - the rock is not yet rock. It is warm and semi-plastic. You can see, in the gloom, slow upwellings where the plateau bulges upward a hand's breadth while you watch, then cracks with a sigh, exposing a dull cherry-red glow from within before crusting over again. Creation is happening at walking speed.

### The Light and the Air

The light is low. Not pitch black, but the kind of darkness your eyes never quite adjust to. There is no moon, no visible stars through the haze. The sky is a deep, warm indigo-black, opaque and close, like the inside of a sealed dome. The illumination comes from below.

Veins of cooling magma thread the ridges and pulse, dim and slow. Some outcrops of mineral - perhaps thallium salts, perhaps something with no earthly name - hold a faint, cold phosphorescence, a bruise-purple afterglow that marks the edges of cliffs so you don't walk off them. Your own hands look strange in it, the skin unfamiliar.

The air is still and warm as blood, around 30 or 35 degrees Celsius, and utterly dry. It doesn't move like wind. When it does move, it's because the mountain moved first - a displacement, a breath exhaled by a newly opened fissure. It smells of hot iron and ozone and clean stone, a smell like a forge just after the fire has been put out. It has no humidity. Your lips crack within minutes. Your sweat evaporates before it can form.

### The Silence and The Turbulence

For a place so violent, it is mostly quiet.

This is the paradox of it. The scale is regional - you can see for kilometers in the clear, dry dark, across a broken archipelago of rising mesas and sinking blocks - but the sound doesn't carry. The sparse air swallows it.

What you hear is intermittent and close:

The *tick... tick... TICK* of a basalt column cooling and splitting somewhere to your left.

A low, subsonic groan as an entire flank of the ridge, a kilometer long, lifts a few meters on a cushion of pressure, grinding.

Then, all at once, a collapse on the far escarpment - a cliff face that was there a minute ago simply gives way, not with a crash but with a long, whispering avalanche of glass shards that flows like water in the low gravity and then stops. The dust it should raise never rises; it falls straight down.

And then silence again. A profound, high-altitude silence that makes your ears ring. The turbulence is not in the sound, it's in the *motion*. The landscape cannot sit still. While you stand on one slab trying to get your bearings, another slab behind you is being born. The horizon jitters.

### What Lives, What Was Built

Nothing lives here in any way you would recognize.

No plants. No lichen, no fungus, not even the bacterial slick that colors desert rocks. The mineral is too new, too hot, too dry. No animals. No insects. No birds. There is no food chain to support.

And yet, it is not empty.

Scattered across the open dark, at intervals that feel intentional because they are so far apart, are structures.

They are not technology. There are no machines, no lights, no antennas, no straight lines that survive close inspection. At a distance, you think you see a watchtower on a ridge, a low wall, a cairn marking a pass. Up close, you realize they are not built.

They are accretions. The turbulent earth makes shapes that mimic building. Basalt cools into perfect hexagonal colonnades that look like ruined temples. Windless chemical vapor deposits sulfur and crystal into hollow, vaulted domes the size of chapels, thin as eggshell, chiming when a cooling tick runs through them. On one plateau, a field of slender, top-heavy spires lean together, their tips fused - from the valley below they look exactly like the skeletal remains of a town.

Perhaps something did try to live here once and left only these mineral ghosts. Perhaps the planet itself is trying to learn the shape of a building before it learns the shape of a river.

There is something faintly unusual about the physics here, too - a wrongness you feel in your inner ear more than see. Things fall slightly too slowly for how thin the air is. Shadows fall slightly the wrong way from the underground glow. If you throw a shard of obsidian, its arc hangs a fraction too long at the top, as if the mountain is still deciding how strongly to pull.

You stand on the newest rock on this world, warm through the soles of your boots, in a darkness that is warm on your face, surrounded by kilometers of sharp, rising, falling, cracking emptiness that is too quiet for how violently it is changing. The air is dry enough to mummify you if you stood still long enough.

And while you watch, a new ridge, a few valleys over, begins to shoulder its way out of the earth, glowing at the seams, silent and inexorable.
