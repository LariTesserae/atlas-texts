High, wet, and hot - that is the first contradiction you feel in your lungs.

The air should be thin up here. It is thin, but not cool. It sits heavy and close, sweet with rot and green steam. This is a mountain that does not know it is a mountain, a high plateau lifted into the normal light of a white sun, and the heat does not fall away with altitude. It clings. Condensation beads instantly on your skin, on every surface, and does not evaporate.

There is no ground.

From the horizon to the ridge you stand on, the terrain is constructed. Not built the way hands build, but grown into architecture so complete that geology has been replaced. What looks from a distance like a range of sharp, young hills, a fresh upthrust still unworn, still knife-edged and startlingly vertical, resolves as you get closer into structure. Everything is plant.

Not forest. Forest has soil underneath, rock underneath. Here the substrate *is* the plant.

The valley - and it is a valley, wide enough to hold weather systems, open enough that you can see three or four repetitions of the same forms fading into haze - is held up by columns and braids and arches of living tissue the size of buildings. Think of banyans crossed with cathedral vaults, crossed with coral. Huge trunks the width of houses plunge down hundreds of feet, but they are not trunks. They are bundles of aerial roots, vines, and lignified leaves fused into cables as thick as your body, woven tight, then weaving again. They spread at the top into broad, flat canopies like lily pads magnified to the scale of plazas, and at the bottom they splay into root-mats that form the floors, the walls, the cliffs.

It is sparse. That is the strangest thing about an all-living world. You would expect choking jungle, but the giants stand apart from one another. Each colossus holds a territory, a clear span of humid, empty air between it and the next, so the landscape reads as architecture, not thicket. A scattered city of green towers. Between them is nothing but deep, wet air and the falling water and the woven causeways that sag and swing.

The light is ordinary, unfiltered high sun, but it is broken. It comes down in pillars through the steam, hits the waxy upper surfaces and is thrown back up. The undersides of everything glow. The whole valley is luminous with reflected green and yellow.

Water is everywhere without a river. There is no basin for a river because there is no basin. The water is in the air, on the air, becoming the air. It beads on every leaf edge and falls without rain. It seeps out of the vertical root-cliffs in slow weeps that become curtains. It sweats from the underside of every platform, condensing out of the hot breath of the mountain itself and dripping back onto the level below in a constant, heavy percussion. The sound of it never stops. Drip and pour and sudden gush when a leaf the size of a roof finally tips its load.

And the other sounds. It is loud here, but not with animal noise. Nothing moves on its own. No birds, no insects, no hum. The loudness is entirely plant and water.

It is the sound of tension and growth.

You hear creaks like a ship's hull as the living cables tighten and swell in the heat. You hear the soft, awful pop of new shoots forcing through old fiber, a sound like knuckles cracking, but scaled up and happening by the hundreds at once. You hear the sigh of vast leaves exhaling water vapor, and the low rush when the wind moves through the towers. And intermittently, without warning, a roar as something gives way.

Because this place is young and turbulent. It is not stable.

Erosion here does not mean wearing down. It means fresh-making. The whole valley is in a state of frantic, hyperactive becoming. The architecture does not endure. You can watch it. On the far ridge, a new tower has erupted in the last hour, a pale green spear of tightly rolled leaves punching upward fifty feet, unfurling as you watch, steaming. At the same time, at the valley wall to your left, a platform you thought was solid is collapsing in slow, fibrous surrender, its supporting vines browning and snapping one by one under the weight of collected water, folding in on itself and dropping into the void in a tangle that will be caught and rewoven by the structures below before it ever hits anything you could call earth.

The rules:

1.  Everything that exists is grown. If you tear a hole in the floor, you see not soil or stone but more weave, more root, layer after layer down into darkness, warm and damp and faintly pulsing.
2.  Water moves upward as much as down. It is lifted by the plants themselves in impossible volumes, drawn up through the columns and transpired out at the top in clouds that never leave the valley, that condense and fall again only to be lifted again. The mountain drinks itself.
3.  Growth is not seasonal. It is continuous and competitive and loud. Space is not filled because the builders are too busy building *taller* and *apart*. Each tower grows away from the others, sharp and unweathered, its edges still crisp with new leaf, because to crowd is to be strangled.
4.  Nothing rots the way rot should in this heat and wet. There is no discarded matter. What dies is immediately knitted back in. A fallen limb is already being threaded through with white exploratory rootlets before it has stopped bouncing.
5.  Strange geometry holds. Leaves grow in perfect right angles. Roots braid into lattices that are too regular, like wickerwork, then dissolve that regularity in the next span with a wild, fleshy knot the size of a house that serves no purpose except weight. The plants seem to be remembering buildings that never existed, forming cloisters, colonnades, and then forgetting and becoming something else.

Standing on the woven mat that is the cliff-edge, feeling it give slightly and warm under your feet, you look out across that open, scattered, dripping, creaking expanse. The air shimmers with heat and spray. A tower half a mile away shudders and extends another six feet with a groan you feel in your chest. Water pours off its new crown in a sheet. The sun is straight overhead, ordinary and indifferent, making everything sweat, making everything grow, making the whole impossible, made, living highland steam.
