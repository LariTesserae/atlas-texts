It is an ocean that should not be where it is.

High. So high that the world below is only a rumor of blue shadow and cloud. You are on the roof of a continent, a plateau so vast it makes its own horizon - and the plateau is drowned.

Not flooded. *Submerged.* The water did not come and go. It simply is. A lens of ocean, kilometers deep in places, shallow in others, held in place on top of the world as if gravity forgot to pull it down. The surface of this sky-ocean is not a surface at all, but an interface with the air - a cold, restless skin of wind-chop and low mist where the water meets the thin, cool atmosphere and shivers, but never breaks, never evaporates, never drains. It rains upwards and downwards at once and neither matters. The light is normal, midday light, sun-white and clean, but by the time it has filtered through a thousand feet of cold water it comes down in soft, shifting columns, like light through cathedral glass.

And everything in the water is plant.

Not plants *in* the water. The water is *in* the plants. The substrate itself is living.

From the basalt floor of the high plateau - ancient, dark, weathered smooth by eons of current that flows but never erodes any further, settled into its final shape - rise the trunks. If you can call them trunks. Each one is wide as a canyon wall, farther apart than you think a forest should allow. This is a sparse vastness; you can swim for a mile between two of them and see nothing but open, cold water and the snow of particulate and plankton drifting in the light shafts. Then another trunk looms, and it is a world.

They are kelp and tree and reef and factory at once. Their bark is not bark but a dense weave of kelp-fronds the size of sails, of mangrove-like prop roots that have become buttresses as thick as hills, of liverwort and moss that has scaled up until a single leaf is a meadow. They have grown until they forgot they were individuals. Their canopies do not break the surface - they *are* the surface, a continent-wide mat of interwoven lily pads each a mile across, leaves so thick you could walk on them if there were anywhere to walk from, their undersides hanging forests of root and vine down into the abyss. Between the great trunks, the water is open, empty, loud.

Because this forest is industrial.

There was never anything built here. No stone cut, no metal smelted, no city, no ruin, no road. No hand has touched this place. And yet the entire ocean thrums like a factory floor at full operation.

Listen.

Each trunk is a vertical engine. You can hear it before you see it - a low, deep hydraulic groaning that vibrates your ribs. The trunks breathe. They draw cold water in through intake mouths at their base, vast sphincter-valves opening and closing with the slow inevitability of pistons, filtering, pressing, refining. Inside them, something pulses. Not a heart. A boiler. A mill. The water is taken in clear and expelled in great, warm, nutrient-rich plumes from vents high up their sides, jetting horizontally in steady, never-varying streams that drive the permanent currents of the sky-ocean. The currents have been the same forever. They will be the same forever. This place is in equilibrium, a closed loop that found perfection long ago and stopped.

The trunks sweat oil and sap. They extrude. From seams in their bark, they press out long ribbons of kelp-steel, buoyant fibers that harden in the cold water and then slough free, drifting away like endlessly produced kelp-rebar. From their leaves they exhale bubbles in industrial quantities - not a few champagne streams, but a constant, roaring exhalation of oxygen that makes the water fizz and roar, that turns whole volumes of the ocean into a rising white column of effervescence that never pops at the surface but recirculates, dissolves, is inhaled again. The sound is immense. The creak of the trunks flexing but never breaking, the hiss of the vents, the thunderous sigh of the bubble-forges, the snap of fibers under tension - it is loud like a mill is loud, a contained, purposeful deafening.

Nothing changes. That is the strangest thing.

The tide does not go out. The trunks do not grow. The plumes do not sputter. The light does not dim. Erosion has finished its work here; the basalt between the roots is scoured to a dark glass, pitted with the holdfasts of ages, but it erodes no more. The water is cool - cool enough to ache your hands, cool enough that everything moves slowly, deliberately - but it never gets colder, never freezes. The leaves that fall do not rot; they are caught and re-woven. The fish - and there are so many fish, clouds of them, silver, blind, translucent, grazing on the endless production of the trunks - do not overpopulate or die off. They school in the same torus around the same vent, year after year after eon, a living belt of abundance in the sparse cathedral spaces.

And there is weirdness in the physics of it.

Buoyancy misbehaves. You can be neutral in the water column and suddenly feel the pull sideways, toward a trunk, as if the forest has its own gravity. The water between trunks is clearer than water should be, viscous as air, until you pass through a plume and it becomes thick as soup. Some of the vines hang down from the canopy and stop, perfectly horizontal, holding position in the current without support, as if they have grown into the shape of a flow-chart. Some of the roots pump water uphill. The sound, though loud, has no echo. It is absorbed by the living walls and produced again exactly the same.

If you float in the open water, far from any trunk, you will see in every direction the pillars receding into blue haze, the canopy above like a green, mottled sky, the bright water-surface beyond it like a second, more distant sky. The scale is too large to hold in your head. Weather happens *inside* the ocean here - slow-turning gyres of warm and cold water the size of countries, with their own clouds of plankton storms, their own lightning made of bioluminescence that flashes steadily, at the same interval, forever.

It is a wilderness that is also a machine. A machine that was never made. A factory with no workers, no product, no purpose except to keep being itself, perfectly, loudly, coldly, forever.
