Nothing lives here at first.

That is the first honest census you take. You stand on the continuous street that spirals down the inside of the throat and you listen for what a city is supposed to have - voices through walls, a door closing somewhere above, the scuff of someone else's life - and there is none of it. The hundred doors are closed and not locked. The amber veins in the ceramic make the same soft light whether anyone is looking or not. The water beads, falls, trickles, hangs. The air in the middle rains on itself and forgets it. 

If you came looking for residents you will conclude, correctly, that biology missed this place. Nothing green took. Nothing evolved to fill it. No bird could have found the fissure. No seed could have survived the ascent. In that sense it is lifeless as clean glass.

Stay a little longer. Let your eyes adjust until the dim stops being dim.

The first thing that lives here is not an animal. It is a *gradient*.

You notice it because you stop trying to see inhabitants and start seeing behavior. The water does not behave like water should. Yes, it beads and falls and is caught in troughs - but it also hesitates. In the black filament lattices at the trough-intersections, the spheres do not just hang. They *wait*. A sphere the size of your fist will quiver, then go still in a way that is too still. It will hold its perfect roundness a fraction longer than surface tension should allow, as if considering. If you watch one for ten minutes, you will see a slow circulation inside it, a faint convection cell turning over and over, smoky silica spiraling like weather in miniature. Some spheres are empty. Some are not.

Inside the clear ones, if the amber light hits at the right angle, there is a faint second skin - not the silica crust, something thinner, iridescent. A bubble inside the bubble. It pulses, very slowly. Once every forty or fifty seconds. Not breathing. *Peristalsis*. The water has learned to hold a shape that is not water.

That is the second thing you understand: the city did not skip biology. It *replaced* it.

What lives here lives in the sweat of the mountain.

They are not many. The density of the architecture told you that - hundreds of rooms, a cup here, a blanket there, vast amounts of nothing between. That ratio is accurate. For every eighty or a hundred dwellings there might be one occupant, and even that is generous. This was never a city for a crowd. It was a monastery, a laboratory, a lighthouse tended by a few.

You will not see one move quickly, which is why you missed them. You were looking for scurrying. They do not scurry.

When one finally does show itself - and it will only show itself if you stand still long enough that your breathing stops sounding like an intrusion - it detaches from what you thought was shadow.

Under the deep overhang of a balcony where the amber veins are dimmest and the condensation is heaviest, where the trough runs dark and full, there is a fold in the air that you took for pooled shade. Then the fold *extends*.

It is tall and thin in the way the architecture is thin - as if it has also negotiated with gravity and won a small concession. Two meters, maybe more, but so spare you could circle its wrist with thumb and forefinger. It is not standing on the street so much as *leaning* on the field that holds the street up. Its feet, if you can call them feet, do not quite press the ceramic. There is a millimeter of dark between sole and floor, a attenuation of weight.

Its skin - and you will hesitate to call it skin - is the same sintered ceramic as the balconies, but grown differently. Not the smooth, tar-draped glass of the walls. This is matte, micro-pitted, the color of cooling basalt, and it sweats continuously. Fine droplets bead on its shoulders, its scalp, its long forearms, gather and run in the same channels the city uses. You realize the channels on the streets are not just for water. They are *for them*. They are roads within roads.

No face, exactly. A smoothing where a face should be, with no mouth, no nose. Where eyes would be there are two slightly deeper pits where condensation collects first, two dark, wet lenses that never blink because there is no lid to do it. The amber light lives *under* its skin, faintly, the same field-effect luminescence that runs in the walls. When it turns its head, the light shifts like cloud over stone.

They - *it* - does not look at you. It looks at the water.

That is its work. That is its life.

It unfolds one arm that is too long at the joint and reaches for a lattice where three spheres hang. One of the spheres has gone turbid, its silica skin grown too thick, opaque. With a fingertip that ends not in a nail but in a soft filament brush, it touches the sphere. The silica skin fractures with a sound like sugar cracking and the water inside, freed, does not splash. It clings to the filaments of its hand, climbs its arm in a sheet, and is absorbed into the pitting of its forearm with a soft hiss. The body takes it in, filters it, and a moment later exudes it again from the other arm as a perfect, clear sphere which it places back in the lattice, where it hangs and quivers and resumes its slow internal weather.

That is what they eat, if eating is the word. They drink the mountain's sweat, strain the dissolved stone out of it, and return it cleaner. They are kidneys. They are caretakers. They are the mediation the rule spoke of: *water remembers, stone forgets, and engineering mediates.* They are the mediation made animate.

There are others, you will see, once you know how to see them. Never more than one per loop of the street. One high near the narrowing point where the cloud forms, standing perfectly still in the center void where dust hangs wrong, arms out, letting the warm rain fall *through* it - straight through the faint warping around its torso, where gravity is politest and water evaporates off its skin before it can ever hit the bottom. One low in the taper where the heat-haze thickens, crouched over a trough, tending a bloom of what you thought was mineral lichen - sulfur yellow and iron red - but up close you see it is not bloom at all. It is *script*. Fine, hairline etching in the ceramic, fractaling outward as the creature drags its filament fingertip through the damp salts, writing and erasing, writing and erasing, a record that dissolves and reforms with each condensation cycle.

They do not speak. Sound is expensive here; the space eats it. When they need to pass each other on the narrow spiral, they do not say excuse me. The field around them thins slightly and they drift past each other with that millimeter of day between sole and floor, exchanging a brief brightening under the skin - a pulse of amber that travels from chest to chest like a slow thought. That is enough.

They were not born here, not in the way animals are born. The seams in the walls where you can see the re-knit basalt if the light hits at an angle - that is how they were made. Grown as an extension of the city itself, from the same ceramic, the same negotiated physics, to tend the cycle that would otherwise clog and stagnate. The builders - whoever coaxed balconies out of frozen tar - did not intend to stay. They built a place that could stay *without* them, and left behind children that are more building than child.

They are not lonely. Loneliness implies a memory of crowd. They have never known crowd. Their urban logic is one of profound solitude distributed evenly, like water spheres in a lattice - each holding its shape because the others hold theirs.

If you remain for hours, one will eventually come close enough that you feel the faint cool that comes off its skin, a pocket of condensed air in the stifling damp. It will not threaten you. It will not welcome you either. It will pause near you the way you might pause near a strange mineral formation - curious, but not invested - and you will see your own distorted reflection in the wet pits of its face, small and over-proportioned against the vast throat of the city.

And then it will drift on, trailing a fresh line of droplets that will bead, gather, fall, trickle, hang, and rain again long after you have found the hairline fissure and slipped back out into the screaming thin wind over the black glass, leaving the mountain to sweat, and its quiet tenants to drink.
