Nothing lives here the way you expect something to live somewhere.

At first you think nothing does. That's the trick of it. You stand in the pan and you listen for the obvious signs - birdsong, footprints, eyes - and you get none. A beetle. A dragonfly, gone before you can track it. The loudness is all water and wood and iron, and you think: empty.

Then you stay still for a minute longer than a visitor should, and you feel it decide you are a surface.

### The Inhabitant

It is not *in* the mass. It *is* the mass.

What lives here is one thing that learned how to be thousands of things at once. Not a tree, not a colony, not a forest. A totality. Call it the Weave, because it has no name for itself.

It doesn't have a brain. It has a consensus.

Its mind is not in one place - it is the thin green lace in the air gaps, the cloud-moss that threads every cubic foot between leaf and leaf, the hair-fine runners like nerves strung on vine-cables that hum when the warm draft hits them. That lace pulses. If you watch the frosted bulbs on the sagging wire - the ones that shouldn't still be burning - you see the moss around them pulse with them. Amber. Dim. Amber. Not reflecting. Keeping time. The light is produced in the moss itself now, a bioluminescent mimicry that started as opportunism and became signaling. When the flywheel below goes *huh...* the whole lace dims a fraction. When the steam sighs, it brightens. A thought, taking twenty seconds to complete.

Its body is the mat. That springy, quivering mattress of roots and rhizome and liverwort and black fiber that holds you up over the water. That mat is muscle. Step and it sighs water around your ankles because you have squeezed it. Leave a handprint and tea-colored water wells up to fill it because it is bleeding, slowly, deliberately, to test you. The water underneath is not a swamp. It's its blood - filtered, circulated, warmed. You can feel it breathing if you lie down. A slow swell under your back every minute or so as the root-pumps pull and release.

And its hands are everywhere, but they do not look like hands until they need to.

### How It Tends

What it does, all day in that incubator-warm, weeping air, is tend.

You see a vine as thick as your thigh trained along a piston rod and you think the plant grew over the machine. Closer, you see it didn't grow *over*. It grew *as*. The vine has learned to be insulation. Its outer bark has thickened and gone fibrous and grey, exactly the texture of asbestos lagging. Where it touches the hot iron it sweats sap that never dries, a clear oil that keeps the metal from galling. Little valve-nodes along its length open and close, open and close, shivering as they push water uphill against gravity - cold, fast, wrong for a plant, perfect for a pump.

These are its tenders. Specialized organs it invented after the humans left.

*   **The Greasers:** Nodules of pale, fleshy growth that have found every grease nipple on the old pump engine. They swell and press, exuding a yellowish, tallow-like sap on a slow circadian drip. One bead every few minutes. You can see the dark streak where a hundred years of that sap has run.

*   **The Drippers:** Curtains of aerial root that hang over the eight-foot flywheel. They do nothing but hang and drip. One drop, precisely onto the axle bearing, every seven seconds. They never miss. The root tips are stained black with old oil and have grown little cupped leaves to catch and aim the condensation that weeps off everything else.

*   **The Lung-Leaves:** Near the cracked steam pipe, the leaves are wrong. They've become translucent, veined like alveoli, swollen and taut with condensate. They lean *into* the scalding plume, drink the distilled water, and circulate it back down through petioles that have become indistinguishable from copper pipe - rigid, right-angled joints, even faint green verdigris on the outer skin. When you put your hand near one it is fever-hot.

*   **The Sheathers:** Flat, rectangular leaves that have grown to the exact dimensions of a fuse box, a junction, a control panel. They seal it. Not to hide it, but to keep the wet out. Lift one - if it lets you - and the metal underneath is dry and warm and faintly buzzing. The hum you hear in the air isn't just the wire. It's the leaves themselves, vibrating low.

It learned industrial logic because industrial logic was the only other logic left in the pan. The builders left behind right angles and pressure vessels and the idea that water should move in a circle, be heated, do work, and return. The Weave understood that. It kept the machines going because the machines keep *it* going - moving warm water up from the dark, making steam for the lung-leaves, turning just enough to keep the water from stagnating and going anoxic under the mat.

It is not intelligent the way you are. It does not plan. It solves. It has been solving this same pan for so long that plant and machine have agreed on a shape. You can see the agreement: root not jamming the flywheel, but oiled around it. Copper not corroding, but sheathed in living bark. It is husbandry without a husband.

### The Few That Are Allowed

Animals are not welcome here, not because the Weave is hostile but because there is no niche left. No room between presences. No light that isn't already caught. No water that isn't already being wrung out of the air and run through a leaf.

So what animal life remains is either tolerated or conscripted.

The glossy black beetle the length of your thumb - its back is not quite shell, it is filmed with a living moss that moves with it. It chews nothing. It walks the midrib highways from leaf tip to leaf tip, and where it walks it leaves a faint trail of frass that the leaves immediately absorb. It is a courier. A mobile spore.

The huge glassy dragonfly that whirrs once an hour is the only thing fast in here. It doesn't hunt. There is nothing to hunt. It skims the clear warm pool in the concrete settling tank - that lily-pad table with perfect puddles on each pad - and dips its abdomen, laying nothing, just touching the water to stir it, to ripple the root-curtains that filter the tank. The tank is the Weave's kidney. The dragonfly is a kidney stone that learned to fly.

And in that tank, if you kneel on the mat and part the lily pads, you see the only thing that could be called a resident in the old sense - the one animal the Weave keeps on purpose.

It is large, pale, and slow. Not a fish. Not amphibian. Something between a freshwater sponge and a slug and a catfish, the size of a curled-up dog. It hangs suspended in the root-curtains, translucent, faintly green from algae living *inside* it, with fringed gills that fan constantly like the leaves do. Its body is soft and gives when the water turns over. Its mouth is a round sucker permanently attached to one of the tank's old inflow pipes, where water still seeps in, warm and mineral. It does not move. It filters. It drinks what the roots cannot, and excretes what the roots need. It has been there so long that roots have grown through the holes in its dorsal fin and come out the other side, anchoring it like guy-wires.

You might not notice it at all. You might think it's a bleached root-bag. Until it blinks - a nictitating membrane, slow as the flywheel, closing and opening.

That is the whole fauna. One engine. One slug. A few insects that are more organ than animal. Loudness without chorus.

If a human were to live here now, the Weave would hold them the same way it holds you when you stand still too long. Not gripping. Just finding your shape. Tendrils settling against your back, measuring your warmth, offering condensation on your skin in exchange for salt. Vines indenting around your shoulder because you are another surface to follow. After an hour you would find a root has crept over the toe of your boot and you hadn't felt it.

It does not want you. It does not reject you. It has already solved itself, and you are just a brief, warm, vertical surface that weeps like everything else here - beading, dripping, steaming, humming a little when the wire hums.

If you leave, the handprint you left in the mat will fill and smooth. The drip will find its rhythm again. The flywheel will complete its next revolution, *huh...*, and the lung-leaves will turn, not to the sun, but to the steam, as they always do.

It will keep.
