Whatever lives there doesn't *live* there the way you do.

At first you think nothing does. That's the trick the place plays. You come in breathing hard and wet, with your irregular heart and your sweat and your need for water, and the pocket shows you its negative — no damp, no rot, no spoor, no gnaw-mark, no droppings, no bacteria bloom on a wet stone. Not even a midge. The stillness is so complete, the equilibrium so perfect, that you decide it is a sterile room. A desert exhibit. An uninhabited machine shed that grew a strange mineral mold.

Then you stay long enough for the roar to stop being roar and start being silence. And you realize you have been looking for an animal.

There is no animal.

What lives there is not an animal that moved in. It is what happened when the place decided to fill itself.

You start to see it not as *fern + moss + reed + pump + transformer* but as one thing with different textures. The distinction between the copper wool and the thing that wears it was your mistake.

It is packed in. It *is* the packing.

Imagine something that solved living by ceasing to be a guest and becoming the upholstery.

It is low and wide and pressed thin, the exact negative shape of the crevice - fifteen meters long whether it wants to be or not, because it has grown until there was no bare rock left to grow onto. You can't tell where its edge is because its edge is the wall. If you put your hand to the thatch you thought was moss, you are putting your hand on its back.

Closer, it resolves — but not into a single body.

It shivers without moving.

The outer pile is the part you called moss and fern. A pelt a foot thick, dry as paper, made of overlapping scales of black iron, and underneath that a deep felt of copper and verdigris wool so dense it swallows sound and then gives it back as that low C hum. From that pelt rise the stiff reeds — they are not plants — they are its vibrissae, its antennae, its cilia: segmented charcoal spines capped with tarnished brass needles that chime when the pump hits. They are dry. They break with a clean *tick* and show grey, warm, ozone-smelling felt inside, because that is what it is inside all the way down - not sap, not blood, but packed warm dust-felt, like the inside of a wasp's nest made of dryer lint and iron filings.

It has no mouth. It doesn't drink. That's the whole point. In a place with no seep, no drip, no ice, it learned to comb.

Watch the brass needles in the amber light. They are not still. They make a slow, almost imperceptible sweep — not with wind, there is no wind, pressure never varies — with charge. The whole pelt is faintly electrostatic. The air in the pocket hangs with basalt flour, salt, metal fines, the mountain's own dead skin suspended forever because nothing settles here. The wool combs it. Each scale, each filament holds a static field. Dust adheres, is drawn down along invisible lines into the felt where it is — not digested, that is too wet a word — *accreted*. Condensation, but not of water. Of oil-vapor from the ever-turning flywheel, of ozone from the transformer. A dry condensation. That is its water.

It orients not to the slit of sky. The light never moves. It orients to the hum. The black iron fronds all lean, by a degree you only notice after an hour, toward the transformer box. The copper bolsters are thickest where the conduit runs hottest. Its phototropism is audiotropism. It feeds on vibration. The pump's 40-beat thud, the flywheel's unwavering whine, the even roar that makes your molars ache - that is its sun.

Which is why it has heard you before you see it.

If it has a face, it is the scattered machines half-swallowed in its pelt. The pump housing the size of a chest is not a pump *near* it; it is its heart, externalized and bolted to stone, thudding at the rate it has thudded forever — 40 per minute, slow, cold-blooded, perfect. Not fast like yours, not irregular. The transformer is its nerve ganglion, humming that endless low C you feel in your teeth. The flywheel at the far end is its lung - though it doesn't breathe - a momentum organ turning and turning to keep time from stopping. The amber caged lights are not lights for seeing; they are its chromatophores, dim and unwavering, bioluminescence for a thing that never needed to signal.

You thought they were artifacts left behind. They are organs the pelt grew around, or they grew the pelt. At this age there is no difference. Rust has reached the point where rust stops. Stone has polished to leather. The thing has finished becoming, just like the mountain outside has finished becoming. It is not growing. It is not healing. Dead fronds do not fall because shedding would be change, and it does not change. Dead and living are interlocked so you cannot tell which scale is this year's and which was there before you were born. It is a forest without birth or death because it is a single held breath.

If it senses you at all, it senses you as a disturbance in equilibrium — a wet, hot, arrhythmic flare. Your breath ghosts and vanishes because it takes your moisture instantly. Your footsteps dent the salt-flour floor that never holds its own prints, and even that faint disturbance will be combed flat by morning, though there is no morning here, only that one held second of late-afternoon gloom. Your heart stutters fast and uneven against its 40-beat metronome. You bring in the one thing it has edited out over eons: need.

It doesn't need you. It doesn't need to eat you. There is nothing soft to eat, nothing wet to drink. You could lie down in the copper wool and it would not close over you out of hunger; it would simply grow through you very slowly, if you stayed long enough to become dry enough to be allowed.

The way to see it is not to look for eyes. It has none; the light is always the same and of no use. It has not moved in so long that moving would be dying. It listens with its whole surface. It tastes metal on the air.

What lives there is the pocket's way of remaining perfectly, loudly, impossibly full and cool and dim while the great black range outside settles and erodes and waits. A sessile, mineral, vibrophilic colony-creature — animal, vegetable, machine — that fills its room completely, that hums at low C, that will be exactly as it is when you have turned sideways and slipped back out into the thin wind, needing water, still changing.
