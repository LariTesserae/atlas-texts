Who lives there doesn't live there the way you live somewhere.

No one lives there singular. No one *holds* it.

It is occupied the way a body is occupied — not by a resident, but by a workforce. By a population so dense it stops being a population and becomes a tissue.

If you stand still long enough for the tick-tick of condensation to start beading on your skin, they let you see them by not moving.

First - the cutters and the carriers.

They are the ones you hear before you see, because that low thrumming that you mistook for a generator is them. Leafcutter ants in columns that never break. Not a line, a conveyor. Each with a sail of fresh green larger than its own body, held up like a parasol, moving inches per hour over ground that is already felted with their sisters' passage. They do not hurry. Hurry would be wasteful. The column goes from the canopy where the leaves are cut to the earth where the leaves disappear, and it does not end in either direction. You could watch for an hour and not see the head or the tail. The leaves do not feed the ants. The ants do not eat leaves. They carry them down into the dark, chew them to paste, and feed them to something else.

Which is the second resident - the farmers and the digesters.

Beneath the leaf litter, in the dark compost that you thought was soil, the fungus lives. Not as mushrooms, though it makes mushrooms when it needs to breathe. As a mat. As a white, filamentous industry spread for acres under every step, threading through rot, through root, through the paste the ants make. The fungus is the stomach of the valley. Everything that falls - leaf, fruit, feather, chitin, shit, skin - is taken by it within hours. There is a crew already waiting within millimeters of wherever you drop anything. Velvet sheets of saprophyte over deadfall, green-glowing gills underneath a log, yeasts and bacteria in such numbers they make the air warm. You feel the waste heat coming off the litter the way you feel heat coming off the brick wall of a power plant at night. That wet heat that never leaves, even after dark, is not weather. It is work. It is metabolism scaled up until climate and respiration become the same thing.

On that mat, living *on* the living, is the third resident - the cladding.

Nothing is left uncovered. That is the rule that has not been broken in a very long time. If there is a surface, something is growing on it. Bark is not bark - it is bark plus lichen plus moss plus beetles the exact color and furrow of bark who do not move until you breathe on them. Leaves are not leaves - they are leaves plus moths who have grown leaves on their backs plus beetles who have grown moss on theirs. Vines as thick as your thigh hang, root when they touch, and are themselves rooted upon by philodendrons with leaves the size of serving trays, and those philodendrons are cupped with bromeliads, and each bromeliad holds a teaspoon of brown water that is itself a complete, sealed world with its own tadpoles, its own mosquito larvae, its own diving beetles. Ferns grow from the forks of trees. Orchids grow on ferns. Fungi grow on orchids. Mites graze on fungi. You cannot put your hand down without putting it on five things that are eating or being eaten, and the flattest truest soil is just another leaf that has finished being a leaf.

And then, in the air just above all that, the electricians.

Because even industry needs signal lights. In the deepest dark under the second earth of the canopy, where no starlight has ever reached, the valley wires itself with light that needs no wire. Click beetles on a single fallen trunk throw their lamps on and off together - on - off - on - off - a whole bank of them, a factory floor safety system with no factory. Fireflies stitch constellations together under leaves and lose them. Fungus gnats hang in cool nebulae. The fungi themselves make a steady, sour green at the gills. It is not display. Nothing here can afford display. It is coordination. Work lights for a shift that never ends, because it is night now, and night is the meaningful time here. The work is done in darkness on purpose.

The larger animals - and they are there, by the thousands - have learned to be small and patient inside this.

Tree frogs the size of your thumbnail packed shoulder to shoulder into the buttresses of roots, throats puffing with no sound you can hear, because sound would be an interruption. Crickets occupying every stem like rivets. Millipedes moving like slow belts over the paste. A puddle the size of a bathtub holding five hundred tadpoles that churn the water to broth, and above it dragonflies with the wingspan of your hand that do not hunt so much as dip and pick, because chasing is inefficient when food is already on the same leaf as you. Even the predators have become browsers. A snake holds a branch for a day and waits for the frog that was always going to come to that branch.

None of them own anything. None of them are visiting. None of them are lost.

The form of life that succeeds here is not the solitary, not the fast, not the loud. It is the contiguous, the steady, the colonial. The superorganism. Ants that are not ants but one ant distributed across miles. Fungi that are not individuals but one digestion spread across the basin. Bacteria in numbers that turn condensation to broth and broth to soil and soil to leaf again. 

If you stand there - one more warm, damp, breathing column packed into the density, beading over with water the way every leaf beads over - you will be catalogued very quickly not as a visitor but as a surface. As an available ground. Within minutes, something will test you. A scout ant will palp your boot. A mite will turn toward the salt on your ankle. A leech in the litter will lift its head to the carbon dioxide of your breath. Not with hostility. With the same neutral, endless assessment that the mat makes of a fallen mango: *is this usable? where does this go in the loop?*

You are not excluded. You are simply not needed. The valley has already solved for occupancy. It achieved, a very long time ago, the shape it wants to be - saturated, sealed, thrumming, warm, dripping tick tick tick - and now it only elaborates on itself, slowly, through the bodies of whoever can live without light, without hurry, without waste, and without ever needing to be alone.
