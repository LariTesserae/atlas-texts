Nothing lives here.

That's the first thing it teaches you, and the first lie it tells.

You stand in that loud, dim, wind-combed bowl and you feel the emptiness as a pressure in your ears and you think: nothing could live here. No food, no shelter, no thaw, no night. And because you think it, you don't see them at first. Because they have already solved the problem you are still trying to solve with your shivering and your breathing and your blinking.

They live here by not living the way you do.

At first you think it's the stone.

You walk past a boulder in its perfect socket and out of the corner of your eye you think you saw it adjust. Just a fraction. Settle a little deeper. You stop and look directly and it's only stone - colder than other stone, maybe, beaded with that same sweat that never drips. You tell yourself it was the wind's drone playing tricks on your balance.

Then you pass another. And another. All those boulders sitting so precisely in their cups. All those yardangs aligned so perfectly. The wind hasn't moved a grain in a thousand years, but all the stone is fitted. And you start to understand that fitted is not the same as placed.

Closer, you see which stones are not stone.

They are the same color as cold ash and old bone. The exact same color, because they have been here long enough to borrow it. Their skin, if you could call it skin, is not skin at all but a rind - basalt-smooth in some places, rough with black lichen in others, patched with that hard cushion moss that only greens when you crouch very close. It is not growing *on* them. It *is* them. A living crust that drinks the air the way the tarns do, beading and vanishing and beading without ever dripping. If you stood next to one and held your breath, you would see it do the same thing your breath does: exhale a faint, white pour that sinks and pools around its feet before it dissipates. Only much slower. One breath every twenty minutes. Maybe every hour.

They are tall when they stand. Taller than you by a head, by half a body - it's hard to tell because they never stand straight. They are folded. Compressed by the same pressure that holds the sky down. Long limbs tucked, long backs bowed, heads ducked forward as if into the permanent wind. You find them where the plateau has provided a seat for them: in the lee of a ridge, in the socket of a boulder that is slightly too large, and most often in those three-walled shelters.

That is what the shelters are for.

You thought they were empty because there was no ash, no tool mark. You were looking for signs that someone *had* been there. You should have been looking for signs that someone *is* there and has learned to leave no sign. The floor inside each shelter is worn smooth - not by many bodies, but by one body sitting for a very long time. Sitting so still that the wind smoothed the stone around it.

If you come upon one unawares - and you only ever come upon them unawares, because when you look for them directly the distance stretches and you never arrive - you will think at first it is another cairn. A stack of flat stones that leans as one stone, fused by rime. Then you will see the seam.

The seam that is not a crack. The vertical seam down what should be a chest, the horizontal seam where a head bows to meet knees. The faint, deliberate fissure that holds a collar of needle ice that never grows and never thins, just like the tarns. And inside that fissure, a wetness. A black, perfectly still wetness that reflects a sky that isn't there.

They don't have faces. Not anymore. The wind that shaped the yardangs into organ pipes has shaped them too. Their heads are smooth, keeled ridges, fluted on the windward side. No eyes - there is no need for eyes when the light comes from everywhere and nowhere. No mouth that you can see, until the condensation beads and you realize the whole front of the head is a shallow, porous concavity that collects the air's damp and channels it down into the fissure. They drink without moving. They always have enough.

What they have instead of a face is an ear. Or rather, they are an ear.

The drone that lives in your chest more than your ears - that low, chorded moan layered with the overtone that seems to come from just behind you - you thought that was the stone being played by the wind. It is, but you had the instrument backwards. The stone is not hollow. *They* are hollow. Their long, folded bodies are resonators, their chests are soundboxes, their fluted heads are stops. The wind blows from the northwest at the same speed it has always blown and passes over and through them, and they hold the note. That's how they stay in equilibrium. They don't fight the wind. They *are* the way the plateau holds its chord indefinitely. When you hear that clear, bell-like tone that holds without wavering for hours, you are hearing one of them breathing out.

And they are listening.

That is their whole metabolism. The plateau roars, and they listen so perfectly that the roar never needs to change. They listen to the water bead and vanish, to the drop that falls without a ripple, to your boot *tok* five hundred meters away that arrives before your boot touches stone. They heard your heart beating and your breath pouring and your thoughts raising their voice to be heard over the drone a very long time before you arrived, because in a place where echoes arrive before the sound, a listener can hear the future.

That is why you will not see them move.

Movement here is not continuous. Remember how a cairn that looked twenty steps away took a hundred to reach, and you passed another just like it without seeing it? How the horizon sometimes folds and duplicates a shelter? They move the way distance moves here. They don't walk. They *reposition*. You blink - and the shelter that was empty now has its tenant, still folded, still beaded with sweat, but now its fluted head is turned a fraction toward you. You turn around because you felt the overtone come from just behind you, and the boulder that was behind you is now a little taller, a little more tucked.

If you try to watch one directly, you will get sick. Your weight will shift as if the ground is forgetting to pull. Your own echo will return too early and you will feel the nausea of causality coming unstitched. They are perfectly in phase with the plateau's wrongness. You are not.

There are not many. Or there is only one stretched very thin. Counting does not work here - you can see for kilometers and count maybe five things that are not rock, but if you walk, you find a sixth where the fifth should have been. The white hare that covers too much ground without marking the dust is one of them when it decides to be seen from far away. The lammergeier that circles without ever banking is one of them when it decides to be seen from above. The glassy, leggy specks under the lip of ice that tremble without moving and exist only in peripheral vision - those are not their young. Those are their *thoughts*, or the dust they have exhaled, waiting to settle.

They were not built for this place. Nothing is built for this place. They became it. Maybe they were the people who stacked the cairns and built the low wall that encloses nothing and then forgot what it was for because remembering is a form of change and change is forbidden. Maybe the plateau grew them the way it grows needle ice - because a place that has opted out of time still needs something to keep its shape from forgetting. They sit. They drink air. They hold the note. They keep the cold at exactly minus ten, minus fifteen, not a degree more or less, by being exactly as cold themselves.

And they have noticed that you are warm.

You are the only thing in the bowl that still knows how to change. You metabolize too fast, you drip, you shiver, you hesitate, you make noise that is not the drone. You are an error in the equilibrium. The plateau has been at this exact temperature for a very long time and does not want to be warmed.

If you stay long enough - and you will stay a little too long, because the distance will make you walk until the dim light fails and you will not feel like you have moved at all - you will find a shelter that is empty and perfectly sized for you. The wind will have smoothed a place for your back. The floor will be waiting. Your breath will fall and pool around your boots and not dissipate quite as fast as before.

And if you sit, just to rest your knees which have already been told everything about elevation, you will hear the drone resolve. Not into wind. Into a held breath. A collective, patient, chorded breath being held by all the sitters at once, listening to see if you will finally learn how to hold yours indefinitely too.

Above you, that single, steady, too-bright point that was not there before will be directly overhead. It will not twinkle. It will watch to see if you get the note right.

Most visitors don't.
