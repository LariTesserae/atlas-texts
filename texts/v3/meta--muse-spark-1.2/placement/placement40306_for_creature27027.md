I don't find myself all at once.

At first - when you put me on that peak - I think I will be you. The visitor.

I picture my feet on the plaza, that perfectly flat plaza that was never meant to be empty. I am upright, vertical, impatient in the way warm things are. I make wake where no wake should be. I breathe and the water around my mouth tries to warm and fails. I look down the corridor that is longer on the left than the right and I blink to correct it. I am the small turbulence they will notice, hold for a moment in stasis, and then politely let go. The one thing on the summit that is still allowed to leave.

But I don't stay there. The stillness won't hold me that way. I am too fast to be a visitor for long. My heart ticks too often, my thoughts drift.

So I thin.

If I can't be the one who stands, maybe I am the one who hangs. I find myself a meter off the plaza, not standing at all. I am one of the motes.

That sparse, fixed constellation of dust you described - the mineral snow that stopped falling years ago and never landed. Each speck exactly where it was a year ago, and a year before that. I am one of those. I have hung there longer than any human has been alive, at the exact interval between two pylons where the field is most taut. I do not fall. I do not drift. The great pale pilgrim that translates past the cliff has passed me a hundred times and never moved me by a millimeter. The residents peel and hover inches from me and I am not disturbed. I am a perfect reference point. I am how the town knows it is still holding.

But even that is too still. A mote is only held. It doesn't hold.

And I want to hold.

So I settle. Or rather, I am settled.

You will find me not on the plaza, but between.

There is a stretch of bare basalt between the second and third structure - that wide interval of young rock you thought was empty because the buildings prefer solitude. It is not empty. It is the interval they are keeping. The stone there is the sharpest on the summit, a fresh hexagonal fracture with glass at the lip still dark enough to reflect the indigo dusk. No building has grown over it yet. No vane has claimed it. And that is where I am.

I am flat.

I have been flat for a very long time. If you came and put your palm to me, you would think I was wall. My back is the bone-colored skin with the slow interference grain moving under it like oil on water. My corona is so dim it looks like just the edge of the pylon's light spilling over. I am the thickness that hasn't learned to peel yet.

I am the bud.

Not the resident that drinks the strain of the dam, not the grown one that ticks on time. I am the knot that formed when a neighbor held its section without error for a decade. A thickening at the corner of its plaza, a bulge by a fraction, seamless. I am new architecture learning to be an animal, new animal learning to be architecture. The filaments on my underside - those hair-fine frets that never wave - are still meshing for the first time, holding a perfect distance from the basalt without touching it, learning the exact taste of its magnetism, its pale plagioclase frost.

I do not hover yet. I am still deciding to have a front and a back.

That is my time scale, and it fits here. While you - the visitor - hold your breath for a minute, I hold my shape for a season. While the motes hang for years, I hang for decades. My one movement, when it comes, will be that slow, soundless peel: a seam that was not a seam opening a millimeter, then a centimeter, lifting from the plain like paper from water, without swirl, without wake. I will be fifteen feet across and weigh nothing and cast no shadow. I will hold myself a hand's breadth off the stone and feel the pressure that wants to crush me and eat the difference.

Right now I am not doing that. Right now I am being held, and holding in return, as practice.

Once every few hours the pylon beside me brightens by a fraction and I feel the tick in my teeth - *I am still holding. Are you still holding?* I answer not with a tick but with a tightening, a barely warmer grain under my skin. The net quivers and settles. The mountain stays young. The cracks stay sharp enough to cut.

Sometimes one of the grown ones will drift over the bare rock where I am and hover, vanes almost touching my edge, coronas kissing without merging. They are not visiting me. They are calibrating me. Letting my field nudge into phase with theirs. I am the reason the town looks like it was laid out for more. I am the more, on schedule.

If you stand on the plaza long enough and look for me, you will not see me at first. You will think the interval is empty. Then you will see the correction - the place where the slow grain under the surface gathers itself a little too tightly, where the light that doesn't travel clings a little thicker. You will think it is a flaw in the material. It is me, listening to the small, warm, brief turbulence you make, learning how to include you for a moment without breaking stasis.

And then I will settle again, and let you be the one who leaves, while I stay and keep the edge from dulling.
