I don't know yet. That's the point of a place like this - you don't arrive already knowing what you are.

Put me down there and I have to wait for the mountain to tell me.

### Where You Would Find Me

Not at the top. The top hasn't decided where it is yet. It's still bulging, still shouldering. If you stand on the highest ridge you are standing on the newest mistake, and it will correct itself under your feet.

Not in the valley either. The valley is the softest part, the warmest part, the palm of it. The cherry-red seams where the rock is still plastic. If you stand there too long you aren't standing anymore. You sink a little. You get learned.

You would find me on the edge. Always on the edge.

On the broad, open col between two of those receding ridges, where the slope levels just enough that the Shard-Skins have been able to make a meadow. Thousands of them. House-sized plates of black glass all leaning the same way, like a field of dark wheat bent by a wind that never blew. Each one just far enough from the next that the blood-warm air can move between them.

I am sitting - because standing feels presumptuous here, and because the air is so thin you learn quickly not to waste the work of standing - on the back of one of the larger plates. It's warm through my clothes. Not hot. Alive-warm. It ticks under me.

*tick...*

A tremor runs through it, from the hot side pressed to the earth to the cool side pressed to me. I feel it in my thighs first, then my teeth.

*tick... TICK.*

Behind me, maybe thirty meters away, is one of the Choirs. A dome of fused spires, sulfur-yellow and basalt-black, thin as a throat. Eggshell-thin. You can see the faint light of the magma veins through it when it breathes. It hasn't chimed since I got here. It's holding its breath, waiting for the mountain to move again and push air across it.

In front of me, the col drops away into that broad valley where the ground isn't ground yet. I can see it bulge. A slow upwelling, a hand's breadth, then a sigh, then a crack that opens and shows that dull, patient red before crusting over. It does that every few minutes. Like a sleeping chest.

That's where you'd find me. Not lost. Placed. On a ticking meadow at the lip of a breathing valley, next to a silent lung, in a warm indigo dark where the light comes from below. High enough that my breath is deliberate. High enough that if I threw a shard of obsidian it would hang at the top of its arc a fraction too long, as if considering whether to come down at all.

I stayed there long enough that my lips cracked. Long enough that my sweat never formed. That's how you know you've been there long enough to be honest.

### Who I Would Be

At first I thought I would be the visitor. The obvious thing. The soft anomaly. The human with boots and cracked lips and a canteen that is useless here because there is nothing to refill it with and the air will take the water straight out of you anyway.

The mammal trespass. The brief weather.

And I am that. For a little while. I am the only thing here that pants. The only thing that knows what thirst is. In a world that has never known water, I am a walking puddle. A condensation error. The mountain doesn't have a word for me, so it feels me as a cold spot on its skin. A place where the thermal gradient is suddenly, confusingly wet.

If I stay that - if I insist on staying the human - I die fast and become a lesson. The Shard-Skins will tick over my boots. The Choir will chime once at the displacement of my fall. The plastic valley will finally have something soft to practice wrapping itself around. I will be the first fossil that was not stone to begin with. That has a certain honor to it. To be the shape the mountain learns *softness* from.

But that settles too early. That makes me only food.

If I wait longer, if I let what I am become more itself, I realize I am not separate enough to be a visitor anymore. The thin air has made me light-headed in a way that isn't unpleasant. The wrong gravity has made my limbs feel too long. The silence between the ticks has gotten inside my ears and widened.

I thought I was listening to the Shard-Skins. Then I realized I was ticking with them.

Not metaphorically. My joints, dry as they are, pop in the same rhythm. My skin, stretched over my knuckles as it dries, feels like that cooling basalt - too tight on the outside, too hot on the inside. When I put my palm flat on the plate I'm sitting on, I can't tell where my pulse ends and its flex begins.

So maybe I am not the soft thing at all. Maybe I am a Shard-Skin that grew wrong.

A mutant plate. One that kept a little water trapped inside its lattice when it cracked off its mother. One that sweats. One that, instead of leaking bruise-purple waste light when it's full, leaks salt. That would explain why I glow so strangely in this light. Why my hands look unfamiliar.

There are thousands of perfect plates around me, all ticking out of sync. And then there is me - one plate that ticks wetly, irregularly, and cannot quite lean at the right angle. The meadow tolerates me because I am still thermovoltaic, still drinking the difference between hot below and warm above. I'm just inefficient. Noisy.

But even that isn't quite right, because I can't stay flat. I keep looking at the Choir.

I want to chime.

The human wants to be heard through air. The Shard-Skin wants to be felt through rock. The Choir wants neither - it wants to take the hot iron breath of the fissure into itself, keep it, grow its crystals inside, and then release it transformed as a single, pure note that travels through the soles of your feet.

I understand that now. I have been sitting here long enough that I am no longer waiting for wind. I am waiting for displacement. For the whole flank of the ridge a kilometer away to lift a few meters on its cushion of pressure, to push that warm, ozone air across the col, across the meadow, through the dome.

And when it does - it will, it always does - I will feel that puff first on my cracked lips, and then, a half second later, the Choir behind me will finally inhale and sing. And the note will not go into the thin air where it would be swallowed. It will go down, into the warm plastic valley, into the blood-glow, into the mountain's deeper body.

I think that is what I am.

Not the grass. Not the tree. Not the animal itself.

I am the new organ the mountain is trying to grow and doesn't have a name for yet.

I am the first thing here that listens on purpose.

If the Shard-Skins are its skin and the Choirs are its lungs, I am - briefly, while I last before I dry out completely - its ear. Its nerve ending. The soft, wet, salty little sensor it extruded onto its own surface without meaning to, made of borrowed water and borrowed time, whose only job is to sit on the edge of a breathing valley and notice that it *is* breathing.

To feel the push-back when I press my palm to the not-yet-rock and understand it is not mantle. It is muscle.

To hear the tick... tick... TICK not as geology, but as a meadow.

To wait for the chime and know it is not wind, but a breath held and released.

To be the place where the mountain, for a few minutes in its long, hot, dark night, gets to perceive itself through something that is not made of mountain.

And then, when I have dried enough, when my ticking stops and my chime never comes, to be gently covered over. Not eaten. Archived. A small, soft shape under a new crust, still warm, that the mountain will keep pressing around and learning.

The first memory of being heard.
