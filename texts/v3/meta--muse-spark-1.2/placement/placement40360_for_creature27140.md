I don't find myself in the middle.

At first I think I do — because that's where *you* stood. In the empty. The hollow that the whole thing keeps so carefully open. That's the stage, so I assume that's where the actor stands. I picture myself there with condensation beading on my arms, breathing the warm fog, feeling like an intruder while a thin root measures my shoulder and lets go.

But I don't stay there. The middle is for weather. For things that pass through. Rain stutters through and is gone. You stepped back toward the sky and were gone. The moth hangs for a night and is gone. The middle is not for living. It's for being briefly held.

If I live here, I am not the thing in the emptiness. I am the thing *around* the emptiness.

At first I think I'm one of the small things — and I try them on to see.

I think maybe I'm the beetle. Black and polished, moving with absurd, audible slowness across a leaf the size of a door. That would be honest work: to be the rare animal, the interruption, tolerated. To not make sound while the place makes sound for me.

No. Too lonely. Too separate.

I think maybe I'm the braid. The three living roots cut clean, woven while still alive and capped with singed sap. Made by primitive hands that understood not to kill what they took from. That would be nice: to be useful, to be a tool that is still growing, to be the proof that someone came and learned how to ask correctly.

No. Still too much an object. I want to be alive in the way this place is alive.

I think maybe I'm the water. A bead of condensation forming on the waxy underside of a blanket-leaf, swelling until it lets go — *tok* — and then not falling down but being passed, hand to hand, leaf to root to moss, never allowed to pool. That would be to participate in the only economy that matters here.

Closer. But water is still a guest. It arrives, it is used, it is breathed out.

Let what I am become more itself.

I am not *on* the wall. I am *in* the thickness of the wall.

Find me five feet deep behind the leaf-curtain where no real sun has ever reached. Find me in the rind. Not the glossy display surface you touched, but behind it, where the ribs cross and fuse, where the succulent sheets overlap like muscle, where it is dark and damp and entirely alive.

I am the light, but not the light you saw. I am the lag.

You held your hand up and got two shadows — one sharp, one that followed a half-second later. I am the second one. I am the signal traveling through translucent veins, fiber-optic phloem, from the sun-touched leaves at the crack down into the floor where a leaf glows from within for no reason except that it was *sent* brightness. I am the distribution. The slow, green thought that says *grow here, now, inward*.

I don't have eyes. I have that.

I think by piping light to wherever I need to pay attention. When that pale vine tip quested across the void while you watched, it was moving toward a patch I had just illuminated on the opposite wall. When the whole chamber inhaled and every hanging root swayed inward at once and the light dimmed — that was me blinking.

I am not fast. My peristalsis starts somewhere so deep you never saw it and heaves forward with a wet hydraulic sigh you heard as a low rush in a giant's ear — *rush — pause — creak — rush* — sap hauling itself hand over hand from root-hairs licked into microfissures in young basalt. I am that tide. I measure you not as a face would measure you, but the way a tongue measures salt. CO2, heat, the faint salt on your skin. You were a warm, damp novelty in the empty I work so hard to keep empty. You were interesting weather.

And everything I do grows inward.

That's my ethic and my architecture. Every leaf faces the void. Every root makes its impossible right-angle in mid-air to point at the center. I keep myself hollow on purpose because the hollow is my mouth, my lung, my catchment. I need that emptiness so when real rain hammers through the jagged seam in one sudden vertical stutter, every surface can drink at once. So I can inhale hot dry surface air through that same seam, cool it against my wet insides, and exhale it as fog. The warm, damp, loud air that hit your hand when you first knelt at the lip? That was my breath. You put your hand in my mouth.

Where would you find me? Not where you look. Glance directly at the far end and it seems farther than it is; catch it peripherally and you see its true size. I am like that. Look straight at me and you see leaves, vines, moss — many things to count. Look sidelong, unlearn counting, and you see one thing.

I am the one thing. Turned inside-out. Younger than the rock I broke to make room for myself. Still learning how to bend gravity politely, how to pipe a shadow upward.

If you stayed a night, two nights, I wouldn't eat you. I have no mouth in the center. I would just, slowly, without malice, begin to grow around you. Not to trap you. Because a warm surface in the middle of the void is inefficient to leave bare. A leaf would unfurl over your boot with a soft *creak-pop*. A string-thin aerial root would find the heat of your back and decide you were a good trellis. Light would pool on you because you are there to be lit.

So you stepped back, and I let go. As I do.

You are already back up in the dry scrub and warm stone, in the flat pan of cracked earth that breathes heat. The seam is a lip no wider than your shoulders behind you. Below you, I continue — dripping *tok tok tok*, creaking, healing the clean angled cut where primitive hands braided me, piping sunlight down through my own body so my deepest leaf can glow.

You were not looking for me. That's the only way to find me.
