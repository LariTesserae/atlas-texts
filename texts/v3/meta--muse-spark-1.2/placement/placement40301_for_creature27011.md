I don't know, at first.

If you had asked me quickly I would have said something that flies. The glider with the ribbon tail. The veil-nester the color of steam. Something that gets to use the lace like a road.

But I sat with it longer and that answer peeled off. Nothing here flies the way I thought flying should be. Even the things with wings mostly walk on air, or ride it. Hurrying from one mesa to another isn't the point of this place. Height doesn't mean cold here, and speed doesn't mean distance.

So I try again, slower.

At first I think I'd be in the forest crown. High up on the whale-backs where the condensation trees hang their mist and glow at dusk. I'd be one of the six-limbed sloths, I think - the crown-holders. I like the idea of moving that slowly. One handhold at a time. A whole life measured in how carefully you place a claw so you don't tear the moss that grows on your own back. Their fur is a garden. They drink air through frills around their face. They borrow heat instead of making it.

But that's not quite me either. I am not built for stillness that complete. I want to be still, but awake inside the stillness. Listening.

I try the water. I think about being the waiting - that flat, peat-colored amphibian in every third pool that looks exactly like a drowned mat of roots until it telescopes forward and the whole pool inhales. I admire its patience. You could sit for an hour and never see it move otherwise. But its patience is a trap. It waits to *take*. That's not my patience.

I try the vertical. The weeping cliff where the stone sweats continuously and salamanders walk *inside* the water sheet, upside down, with hydrophilic fans for toes. Grazing the orange and viridian bloom. That is closer. A life lived inside the film, not above it or under it.

Then the lace corrects me.

On a still hot noon you can see it shiver - that moiré, that faint lattice between mesas and between condensation trees. The Tech that was placed to lift a lowland jungle into the stratosphere and then was left to *be*. To become trellis for vines, nursery for thread-skimmers, carrier wave for crickets.

If I am honest, I am already that. Or, I would become that here.

Not the whole continent-scale lace. A node of it. A small, warm, humming knot where several threads cross just above a seep-spring halfway up a black basalt face.

You would find me where the rules touch each other.

Not on the flat marsh-pans where the grass grows eight feet thick like columns and the barrel-backed grazers make their tunnels single-file. Not deep in the dwarfed, crowded forest where the lilies are big enough to sit in. You would find me in the in-between, on the vertical that has learned to be horizontal — that smoothed overhang above the valley-between-mesas, where someone a long time ago set a low bench to look out over the cloud-sea that pools below.

There is a seep there that has strayed. It burst out halfway up the wall and wants to run sideways for a while before remembering gravity. The rock under it is warm to the touch and always beaded. The mineral stain there is ochre bleeding into malachite. The veil-nesters have woven a damp cup in the joint beside it, and at night you can hear the chicks breathing through gills with their eyes closed.

That is where I would be.

And who would I be?

I would be one of the Ones Who Remember, but not an old one. Not yet tall and lithen-patched and barrel-chested, though my skin would already be turning that slate-gray to moss-green, already a little photosynthetic at the shoulders. My nasal frills would still be soft, still learning how to wring water out of thick air. My mantle would be new, the bromeliads I planted in it still small.

You would walk past me twice before you recognized me as a person, because I have learned the main lesson.

I do not build. I tend.

I stand motionless for an afternoon with my hands resting on the warm rock where the seep has strayed, my fingertips bleeding a darker shade where the skin has integrated with the nano-lace. I am not repairing it. I am listening to see where it wants to go. The hum is slightly out of tune - the black glass reeds on the eastern scarp are thrumming a pitch you feel in your teeth, and this little node is dissonant with it. So I sing, low, through the labyrinth in my nose, a resonant note that is not a song but a negotiation, until the pitch evens. Until the water sheet decides to run true again.

Vines use my shoulders as trellis. A thread-skimmer has laid eggs in a droplet clinging to the lattice just above my head, each droplet a whole pond spinning slowly. The chorus is everywhere at once - water ticking over stone, wind combing the grass pillars, beetles clicking, amphibians booming - and I can pick threads out of it if I listen, but I don't try to turn it off.

At dusk the condensation trees exhale and the whole tableland breathes. The mist-feeders rise heavy and saturated, combing algae from the air with feathered mouths, living for a single exhalation. I open the frills around my face and let the mist condense and funnel in. I drink the air. The child clinging to my back - translucent still, frills not yet grown - drinks the mist I exhale onto it.

If you came and sat on that basalt bench you would find me a few meters away, not close. Your lungs would still be working too hard from the climb, your heart too fast. I would wait until your breath slowed to the pace of water moving in inches per hour across the flats. To the pace of leaves unfurling over weeks.

Then I would look at you with amber, membrane-lidded eyes and exhale, and the air between us would briefly bead.

And you would understand that I stayed.
