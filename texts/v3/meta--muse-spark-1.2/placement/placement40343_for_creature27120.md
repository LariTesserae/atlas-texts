I don't know at first.

If you drop me into that basin - not a cave, not a valley, that raw, breathing wound - my first instinct is to put myself where you stood to see it. Up high. On the highest shelf where the sun comes down in columns and you can see the whole system move like a town from a hill.

That's the clean place. The observer's place. The place where a visitor stands and gets condensation on his arms and thinks *how terrible, how beautiful* and still has somewhere dry to step back to.

I wouldn't stay there.

If I stayed there I'd be a tourist. I'd be waiting for the roof to finish collapsing or for the desert above to take me back. And nothing in that basin waits.

So I try again.

Maybe I'd be in the air. For a second I want to be one of the dart things, four wings stretched over finger-bone, light-blind and screaming through the skylight pillars. It would be simple - just hunger and speed, just wind of wings on my face. But I don't want simple. And I don't want to be something that never sees.

Maybe I'd be in the water, or what passes for water there. Ankle-deep in a warm pan no deeper than a spill, with the pale shrimp-lice turning as one under me, scraping. To be that low, that many, that close to the film that eats stone and exhales light. To be eaten and to eat in the same hour. But I am not that patient, not that translucent.

No. Let it come slower.

If I live there, I don't get a vista. I get a ledge.

Find me one terrace down from where you stood. Not the top, where the fliers watch, and not the bottom where the air goes light and dust hangs too long before it falls. The middle. The second terrace where the stone is still hot through the boot and never quite dries.

There's a place there where two paths worn by feet cross - not built, just used until the basalt is polished a shade lighter. And at the crossing there are three stones balanced vertically. Four, then three again. The place where the map rewrites itself every hour because the land rewrites itself.

You'd find me near that.

Not *in* the shelter. The shelter is there, ten meters off - that low windbreak of chosen slabs, chinked with salt-mud and chitin powder, the badger-den of translucent hides stitched with shrinking sinew, floored with pressed molts. That's where the band sleeps, eight or twelve or twenty of us, tucked together while the pans steam. I don't live inside it either. If I did I'd be in the middle, being kept warm. That's not my shape.

My shape is *beside*.

I'd be the one who stays when the children sprint.

You know them - all hands, barely a meter if they stood upright, which they never do. Grey-pink skin that has to stay damp or it darkens and cracks, salt rime at the temples, glands beading at the throat. Six fingers on each hand, the thumb central so any finger can meet it. Wide amber eyes with a membrane that flicks sideways when steam gusts. The ones that run on all fours across glass-sharp rock to snatch grazers and are back before a flier can stoop.

I wouldn't be the runner.

I'd be the one who crouches and clings and *waits*. The still one. Which in a place with Dynamic 2.9, with storm-energy, with a new crack opening like a gunshot every hour, is its own kind of running.

You'd find me with my palms flat on warm basalt, feeling it.

Because here sound arrives before the motion that makes it. We have learned to flinch half a second early - the whole band going silent before the slab calves off. I am the one who flinches first. Who feels the future in the rock as a vibration before it is a sound. I sit with my hands splayed, pebbled palms drinking heat, and the ticking of nails on stone comes up through my elbows.

Who would I be, then?

Not the old knapper by the fire, though I love him - pale, almost translucent, his back crusted white, striking black glass once and making an edge you could shave with. Not the child filtering gnat-clouds through the fringed lip, laughing by puffing air.

I would be the cairn-tender.

The rememberer who doesn't try to make anything last.

My work is to tend the film - not farm it, glean it. To know which amber sheet is ready to be scraped onto a flat stone and dried into iron-tasting leather, which bruised-violet patch will sour the water if you eat it, which new vent has bloomed too hot to touch. To taste the salt crust beside your foot and know if it's the kind that preserves or the kind that burns.

And to tend the stones.

When a terrace collapses and a new pan forms where a path used to be, I am the one who takes the three-stone cairn apart. I add the fourth stone, take the bottom one away. *We were here. Water this way now. This wall cracked this morning, don't cling there.* Not a monument. An interruption that hesitates the chaos for a little while. By evening the wind or a child or a steam vent will have knocked it over and I'll make it again differently.

I have to stay damp, so I drink constantly, lapping from films with a tongue that sticks out too far. I speak rarely and low - clicks against the palate, breathy whistles - and more often I drum. Fingertips on stone. On a shoulder. A conversation that travels across the terrace as tremor while to you it looks like two of us just sitting together. At night when the bacterial veins never go dark, you would hear me tapping: *pan full here, vent opened there, lost a child to fliers, found a dry ledge.*

I would be small. I would be damp. My eyes would reflect your face distorted in amber when you finally sat still long enough that you looked like part of the stone and I came close enough to check you. I would not reach for you. I'd reach for the salt beside your boot, scrape it with a nail that is half claw, taste it. Decide.

And I might leave you something. A single flake of black glass, edge out, by your foot. Not because you need it. Because I had it in my hand and no longer need it, and because in a place that never finishes, an offering and a warning and litter are the same thing.

That's where I'd be. Not looking over the basin but *in* its middle, where wet and dry argue every hour. Not flying, not burrowing, but clinging sideways to a still-hot wall where things grow while you watch and are eaten back to nothing by evening.

I would belong the way foam belongs to a wave - a temporary, intricate pattern the energy throws up because it is so full something has to be that shape for a little while. And for as long as the rock breathes steam and the pans fill and empty and the light stays wrong, I would be perfect at it.

Then the stone would cool, and I would be impossible. Unwritten.

But not yet.
