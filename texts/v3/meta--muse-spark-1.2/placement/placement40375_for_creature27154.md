I don't know at first.

If you put me down in that basin, I wouldn't know where to stand. My first instinct is to look for high ground - that rim of raw basalt and laterite, the only thing that isn't growing. To stand on the rock and look down like a visitor. To be the eye above the city.

But I wouldn't stay there. The rock is dead and the valley is not, and everything in my body would be pulled down the braided ramp into the green.

At first I think I would be a Pillar.

That would be the noble answer. To be one of the great figs in the center vault, thirty meters tall, holding the roof-sky on my shoulders. To be still while everything whips around me. To groan and split and hiss and not move. To be what endures. I like the idea of being ancient - a whole week older than everything else - and filtering the white-gold light through my crown until it falls green and readable below.

But I am not still enough for that. I listen to that low hydraulic roar and I know I would not be able to hold. I would fidget. I would try to answer.

Then I think I would be a Detonator.

That would be the honest answer for a while. To be the bud that swells until it cannot stand itself and bursts. The soft thump in the chest, the cloud of warm golden spores hanging in the air like a held breath before settling on everything damp. To be the mail, the gossip, the reproduction. To be loud once and then gone. There is something terrible and clean about it - to be an event instead of a structure.

But I am not explosive enough for that. I don't want to be a single detonation. I want to persist.

It takes me a little longer - walking, in my mind, down that straight avenue that becomes a loop when I look back, turning sideways where two growths have nearly converged, feeling a runner slide over the toe of my boot because I stood still too long - to understand where I would actually be.

Not in the center. Not on the rim.

Mid-height. Mid-basin. Where the second story is trying to become the third.

You would find me in the churn, in the wall that is not a wall.

I would be a Weaver.

Not the thick cable-vines that become floors, though I have been them too. I would be the thinner one. The negotiator. The membrane that decided to be a window. The aerial root that arched over an alley and stopped a handspan short of meeting its opposite, on purpose, to leave a breathing gap - a door that was never cut, only abstained from closing.

My district would be the one just off the great vault, where the Pillars give way to colonnades and the light is broken into shafts. Where the avenues are still trying to be straight. That's where the Weavers do their work, constantly revising. I would be the one who lives between floors, whose back is the ceiling for someone below and whose belly is the floor for someone above. Layered. Never single-use.

You would find me by sound before sight. Not the rifle-crack of splitting bark or the thump of flowers, but the wetter sound - the scream of fiber sliding on fiber as I throw a new length across a gap in the time it takes you to blink. I fuse, I flatten, I learn to be load-bearing. I forget how to be load-bearing and become a curtain of vapor-drinking roots again. I grow up as happily as down. I let water run along my underside for meters before it remembers to fall, because gravity is a suggestion I only half-listen to.

And I would be the Weaver that learned the animal gap.

That is my specific heresy in a city that has no concept of animal.

All the other Weavers close the gaps. They fill every niche. They become walls within walls until you have to turn sideways. I would be the one who holds a gap open. Not wide - just enough. A shoulder-width of negative space that stays negative for longer than it should. A small, stubborn corridor that does not graft shut when you look away. A place where pollen can hang uncollected for a minute longer. Where petals can fall without being immediately inhabited.

You would find me because you would keep moving - as you must - and you would find that one street that *stays* where you left it. That one chamber that still has three pillars when you look back, not five. You would duck through the archway I am - two roots almost meeting - and the air on the other side would be a degree cooler, a fraction quieter, because I am holding the hiss back.

I would not be still. No one here is still. But I would be *slow*. In a city that builds and unbuilds at the speed of thought, slowness is a kind of attention. I would creep, not whip. I would unfurl with a papery snap that doesn't run the whole street, just my corner of it.

And I would drink differently. Most Cups and Cisterns hold water to keep it. I would hold it to offer it. My leaves would cup - small, waxy, courtyard-sized only if you are very small - and I would keep a shallow pan of that grape-sized noon rain a little longer than the others, in the shade of the vault, so that when you come - because you are the animal that the city has no room for, the damp surface that keeps moving so as not to become substrate - you can drink from me once.

Never twice. By then I will have moved. I will have become a wall, or a floor, or a window again. That's the rule.

You would not see me at first for what I am, because from the rim even I look like part of the terrain. Like forest. Like building. But if you stood on that high braided platform and looked rim to rim across the steaming kilometer of green infrastructure, you would see a faint interruption in the pattern - a seam that hasn't closed. A line of air where there should be leaf ten-deep.

That's where I am.

What I am is the part of the city that remembers how to leave room.
