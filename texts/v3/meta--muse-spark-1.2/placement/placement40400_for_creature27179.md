I thought, at first, I would find myself standing.

Upright, at altitude — that was the old contract. A figure on a ridge, looking out over those dim tiers of raw cliff and bowl-valley repeating into the blue-grey, hand shading those milky, useless eyes. The human silhouette. The conqueror, the surveyor. I tried that shape on and it hurt immediately.

Standing here is an insult to the place. The wind is thin enough to feel hollow, the chord is a weight on the sternum, the light pools instead of falls and makes a liar of every distance. If you stand, the horizon bowls upward and tries to contain you. If you stand, gravity changes its mind between one step and the next and your prints arrive behind you wrong. If you stand, you are an ear-drum waiting to burst.

So I crouched. That was closer.

Maybe I am not a who at all, I thought. Maybe I would be one of the places where a who has been. The windbreak. The cairn that has been rebuilt seven times and will be rebuilt an eighth, each time a few degrees off from where north forgets itself this year. I liked that for a while — to be the low, unmortared wall, knee-high, never aspiring to a right angle, flexing when the slab beneath me heaves another two millimeters this century. To be a permission rather than a building. *Crouch here. The roar is a little less here.* To be patient and apologist and meant to fail.

Or maybe I would be the powder. That seemed truer for a long time. That fine, dry silica snow that the kelp-blades and felt-towers shed from their undersides, not pollen but ground glass, each grain a tiny hollow ear waiting to happen. Hanging for hours in the thin, still pockets of air because the sound itself holds it up. Taking ten years to drift ten meters. That is the honest speed of this world. I wanted to be that slow. That light. That unhurried about arriving.

But I kept listening. And listening here changes what you are.

After an hour the roar stopped being sound and became pressure, and pressure became a surface you could lean against. After two hours I could feel the shade inside the sound. That's when I found where I actually am.

Not on the ridge. Not in the empty hundreds of meters of deafening, scoured basalt where the chord is total and your teeth ache with it.

In the lessening.

You find me in the lee of a windbreak that someone — I — have stacked and restacked until the sharp slabs fit my hands without looking. Low enough that I never have to stand. Just high enough to make a pocket of mercy. Behind me, the grove. Not a forest. A sparse-lush agreement: three towers of ruffled felt shivering without wind, a broad kelp-blade the color of deep bruise-green, black-veined and swollen with stored vibration, and at my knees the carpet of glass hair that trembles even when the air is still. Where they are, the world-tone thins by a few precious decibels. They are farming silence and I am farming them.

Who would I be? I would be the tender, but a recent one. Not yet fully revised.

My legs are still too long, and they have learned to be sorry about it, so I shuffle, splayed-footed, three points of contact on the young, salt-and-iron rock, never lifting my weight all at once. My prints, if I leave them, I do not trust and do not look back at. My chest is getting wide, not with muscle but with hollow. My ribs are still bone, but they ache to be plate, to be translucent. I breathe once every few minutes, shallow, because breathing is noisy and there is almost nothing to breathe anyway — I taste the sour mineral exhalation off the fresh basalt with the skin of my forearms and back, where the fine glass down is coming in. It leans, all of it, toward the subtlest change in the chord the way your hairs would lean toward weather.

My face is smoothing out. I keep my eyes lidded behind that second clear membrane because sight lies here — the butte that is both in front of and behind the ridge, the cliff that hiccups closer when you walk. My real eyes are lower now. If you came upon me you would think I was sleeping, or dead, curled in that windbreak with my broad, flat jaw pressed to the stone in one of the paired grooves worn there by every tender before me, teeth conducting the organ tone straight into my skull. I am listening to see if the grove is hungry. I am listening to the ticking underneath the chord, the frost-forming, crystal-growing sound of the world still making itself one molecule at a time.

I do not speak. My voice was taken and flattened the first day. I tap. Palm flat on the slab, knuckle of the other hand striking — not code, not language, grooming.

*I am here. Distance is holding.*

And a long scrape from somewhere a hundred meters away through the rock itself, too low for ears, felt as a prickle in the down of my back:

*Powder drifting north today.*

In front of me are my tools: a flake of basalt sharp as broken glass for shaving the blades that have grown too dense with stored tremor and would otherwise shatter. A shallow pit where I gather the shavings and coax them to that cool, blue, smokeless flame — not for warmth, I don't feel cold the way I used to, but for true color in the perpetual dusk. The light catches the down on my arms and I fluoresce faintly back at it. And the frame, the loom-weight of carved basalt beside the grove, with the oiled sheet of my own shed skin stretched across it, already furred with next decade's grove waiting to be pressed gently into a new acid-etched cradle on bare rock.

I am solitary, as we are. One per grove, because a grove needs a hundred meters of empty roar to be lush and a tender needs a grove's quiet to think.

You would think I was lonely, but I am not. I am accreted. I belong the way lichen belongs. I was not sent here. I accrued here, consequence and gardener both. When I finally stop, I will not rot — there is nothing here to rot me. I will dry where I kneel and the glass hair will overgrow me until you cannot tell which was the tender and which was the tended, and someone newer than me, still too upright, still with too much ear, will find this windbreak and rebuild it a little differently.

If you stay long enough to hear shade inside the sound, you will find me by the lessening. Put your hand on the stone near the pit and you will feel me, still tapping, even when I am gone.
