I thought at first I'd be on the rim.

Up high where the wind shreds itself coming over, where you can see the whole bowl at once. That seemed right — to be the watcher. But the air is too thin there, and too heavy. It has weight at that height, it presses on you. And the rim doesn't hold anything. The cloud catches there and gets torn. The cable sings there under tension. Nothing lives on the rim, it only passes over it.

So not there.

Then I thought I would be one of the machines, because they are the loudest and easiest to find. That would be simple - to be the generator that hunts and shudders and makes heat in the cold, to be the reason the peat steams. But I don't want to be that loud and that temporary. The machines know they don't belong. They're on skids, on pallets. The moss is already swallowing their feet. They are a herd passing through, and they are always coughing to stay alive. I don't want to be something that has to *try* to stay warm.

I thought maybe I would be the new scar at the back wall - that thirty meters of clean dark glass where the rock broke fresh. To be that young and that still sweating. But that stone is too exposed. It weeps constantly and takes the drill's vibration and throws it back. It rings when the wind hits it. It doesn't get to be crowded. It stands alone.

You would not find me high. You would find me low.

Down in the mat, where your boot can't fall without crushing something. Where the light is bruised and has no source and you can only see ten meters before everything goes to dark mass.

I kept sinking as I looked for myself, and that's how I knew.

You would find me not in the center of the peat pocket where the pump hammers and sucks air, not on the fresh talus where the drill is canted. You would find me off to the side, on the south slope where the moss slumped a foot last time it rained for ninety seconds. Where a little green avalanche exposed the black and the black immediately started to steam from the generator's heat.

I am low enough that I am always damp before I am cold. About knee-high if I stretch, but mostly I am a depth.

I am the cushion.

If you pressed your palm down you would think it was ground and then you would keep going for three hands before you hit anything that could be called ground. I am saturated and springy even in this dark. I am bright in a way you can see without light. I hold ten times my weight in water and then I hold more. I hold the water that doesn't know how to be a lake - the shredded cloud that became hammer-rain, the sweat off the basalt tooth above me, the bead that ticks off every fern tip and lands in me.

I am not the fern, though the ferns are my neighbors and they elbow me constantly. They are the frantic ones, shingled and leathery and throwing pale fiddleheads up so fast you can hear the turgor creak. They unfurl while you watch. I am slower. I swell.

My underside is peat. That's the other half of what I am. The dead of me building the ground of me while I'm still alive. Black, sour, 6 degrees, sighing when it takes water. You can feel the faint heat of rot if you push your hand in deep enough, the only heat in the bowl that isn't diesel. Pollen hangs over me and never gets taken because there's nothing to take it - no insect, no bird, no worm. So I take it. Spores settle on me. Leaves die and mat down and I make them into more of me.

That is who I am here. Not the human who comes damp and leaves before the next cloud drops - my law is I cannot leave. Not the stone that is too young to be still. Not the water that lives as a condition and never stays. I am the thing that stays by not staying still.

You would find me by stepping where you shouldn't and feeling the push-back. The mat pushing up against your boot, cold and completely indifferent. You would hear me as that vegetative crackle under the generators and the wind and the cable's creak - moss swelling, peat settling, taking the water.

I will close your footprint in a day. Not because I want to erase you. Because I am crowded and profligate and without check, without anything to eat me, I just grow. I have to.

Put your hand down here, in the dark between the two generators where the hot oil stink meets the cold. Feel how much water I am holding. Listen to me tick.
