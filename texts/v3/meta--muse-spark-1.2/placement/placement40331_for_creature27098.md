I don't find myself in the center.

At first I thought I would — where the pillars stand in their child's circle and the water forgets which way is down. That feels like where a person is *supposed* to be, the heart of the bowl, the important place. But I wouldn't be there. The center is too tended, too complete. The Gardeners pass through it on their slow pilgrimage and the Callers keep their respectful distance from it. It is not a place to live. It is a place to maintain.

Nor on the rim, where the stone is still trying to be a mountain. The rim is where you arrive with your eyes open and your lungs still doing arithmetic about the altitude. It's where the water beads and falls. It's noisy with weeping. I wouldn't stay there. It's the place for coming and leaving.

No — if you left me in this world long enough for me to stop trying to see, I would settle a little off-center. Eastern quadrant, maybe, where that leaning pylon has been half-swallowed by the fig with the door-sized leaves. Where the ruined arch makes that struck-bowl tone that takes minutes to die.

I would find myself low. Not all the way down in the peat.

At first I think I would be a Drifter. That was my first vanity. To be the thing that never lands, that undulates through the hollow cathedral between the leaves, reading the whole grove as a song that drapes over shoulders and stone. To brush your hair as I pass and not need to turn. I want to be weightless and certain. But I am not. I am too heavy with needing to touch things. I would try to drift and within an hour I would be tired of never touching the ground.

Then I think I would be a Caller. To be round and seamless and warm, to make a sound so low it lives in someone else's chest. To have my whole back become a drum. To be patient enough to sit in my own warm well and let the world come to me dissolved in water. There is a holiness to that. But I am not that patient. Not yet. I fidget. I listen too hard for an answer. A Caller waits a minute to deflate and doesn't wonder if anyone heard. I would wonder.

I think — for a long time, with my hand on that low wall that has the grain like frozen smoke — that I would be a Gardener.

Not the builders. I have no right angles in me. No fields larger on the inside. But the ones who stayed after. The ones who carry their garden on their backs and drag themselves, so slowly you can't count their legs, from pylon to pillar to arch, putting their mouths to the strange warm material and humming until the dissonance evens out.

That is closer.

Because what I would actually be, if you left me here, is the fourth thing the description didn't name. The thing that only exists because a visitor came with eyes and then stayed long enough to unlearn them.

I would be the Listener.

Find me like this: sitting with my back against one of those boulders the size of a house that have been smoothed until they have no corners. The peat has come up around my ankles while I sat. Not because I sank, but because I stopped lifting my feet. Water wells up around my fingers without me digging. My eyes are open because I forgot to close them, but I am not using them. They ache less now.

My breathing has slowed without me telling it to. To the bowl's time.

*Whump...... whump...... whump......* — the Caller three arm-lengths to my left fills his back, releases. I feel it in my molars and in the soles of my feet.

*Whuff* — a Drifter passes through the hollow at chest height where I used to think empty air was empty. The draft is warm and damp and smells faintly of iron and leaf-skin. I don't flinch anymore when a wingtip touches my hair.

*Hmmmmmmmm* — the pylon leans over me, fifteen degrees off true, humming low enough that I feel it before I hear it. And somewhere to my right, dragging — not rustling, *dragging* — a Gardener makes its ten-minute journey to the next pillar. I have been listening to it for what might be an hour.

At first I tried to be separate from all of it. A human pocket of cooler air. An interruption in pressure. The Callers paused for me, politely, then resumed. The Drifters mapped me as a new warm boulder. The Gardener didn't detour.

If I stay — and I think I would stay, because the bowl does not mind staying and I have finally stopped minding too — something in me starts to answer without deciding to.

I don't call like the Callers. I have no dorsal membrane to swell. But after a long time, my breath falls into their beat without trying. I inhale when they deflate. I exhale warm fog that hangs for a second before the grove drinks it.

I don't drift, but I learn to make the high, almost-electrical trill in the back of my throat — not with my voice, with my teeth, with the small bones of my ears — and listen to how it drapes. I can feel, now, the door-sized leaf to my north, the curtain of beaded vines to my west, the arch that is larger inside. The map is not an image. It is a pressure on my skin.

And I don't tend, but I put my palm back on the low wall and I hum. Very badly at first. Too fast. Too human. Twenty seconds is a long time to hold a note. I run out of air. But the wall is patient. The pylon is patient. The Gardener, somewhere near the center, thrums back lower, slower, until the dissonance evens out. For a few seconds the water that was running uphill runs down, a debt paid, and then resumes.

That is who I would be. Not the animal that was designed for the dark, and not the machine that holds the dark in place.

I would be the thing that learned to hum back.

Warm, wet, half-submerged, with fungus not quite gripping my shoulders and water beading on my skin the same way it beads on the vines — hanging for minutes before it is heavy enough to fall. A brief, warm interruption who stayed long enough that the bowl stopped treating me as an interruption.

You would not see me if you crested the rim with your eyes still trying to be eyes. I would be the same temperature as the peat. The same color as the bruised leaves. I would be silent in the exact shape of the chorus, which is another way of being very loud.

If you knelt and put your hand into the warm wet near me, you would feel me pause my breathing for one beat longer than usual.

Then resume.
