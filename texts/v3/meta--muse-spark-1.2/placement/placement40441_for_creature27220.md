I wouldn't find myself where I first thought.

At first I think I would be on the grating - shoes wet through, shirt already giving up on ever being dry again, visitor-loud, visitor-fast, eyes still trying to make shadows where there are none. Standing in the gap between the mill and the power house and looking *at* her. I think I would be the one who left the tea to film over.

But I don't stay there. You can't, in that air. The air sits on you until you sit with it. After ten minutes your breath changes. After twenty you stop listening for birds.

Let me start again, slower.

I find myself not in the middle of the bowl, but at its negotiation.

Not down on the false floor where the mat breathes and the water sheets amber under the work lights. And not up on the rim where the sky is that bleached, cruel white. I find myself on the slope itself - where the limestone weeps.

There's a place where the wall isn't wall and isn't hill, where the clay has slumped over the dark pitted stone and the town has pinned it back with old rebar and poured concrete that has itself gone soft. Water comes out there cold, even while everything else steams. It beads and tracks, beading and tracking endlessly down. The moss there is not the flat, tended moss on the drain covers. It's wild, deep-piled, a foot thick, black-green, and it holds the cold.

That's where I would be. Halfway down the bowl, in the shade of a philodendron that has grown too large for the trellis and has collapsed outward, making a kind of tent. The transformer whine is muffled there by earth. The pump's THUD arrives not as sound but as a pressure that squeezes the moss and squeezes me, and then releases. THUD. *drip drip drip.* THUD. *drip drip drip.* I can feel the whole basin equalize through my back.

I went there because I was still trying to hear myself think, and that's the only place quiet enough to hear a thought without it instantly condensing.

And who would I be, there?

At first, still mostly a person. That's the trouble. I still have ears that try to open. I still have hair that tries to hold on, going lank and hot against my scalp, trapping sweat. I still move like an animal - I dart my eyes, I shift my weight twice where I should shift it once, slowly. The heat punishes me for it. I can feel myself overclocking, making more heat than the air will take.

So I learn to stop.

I don't think I would be one of the tall, narrowed ones, not yet. Not the one standing in the elephant ears, who has already become leaf-white and lidless. I would be the one who is *becoming*. The sparse rule holds: one per territory. And that weeping slope didn't have one. The others tend the machines that turn and hum and pump. No one was tending the wall that weeps. No one was tending the cold water before it gets warm.

That would be my work.

My skin would be the last to change. I would sit with my back to the brushed-flat place in the moss where someone larger once rested, and I would fit myself into it. Over days - the bowl doesn't count days, it counts thuds - the moss would decide to keep me. Not grow over me, but *with* me. A liverwort at the hollow of my throat first, because it likes the pulse. Then the fine beading moss along the forearms, where the condensation runs. My eyes would start to film. The light would stop hurting.

I would not be loud. I have never wanted to be loud. In the world above the rim I was always aware of how much noise I made just to stay legible. Here, legibility is the wrong metric.

I would be the listening one. The remembering one.

Because someone has to remember what the thud *sounds* like as sound, not just as pressure, for when it stutters. The others feel it so deeply now they wouldn't notice if it changed key. I would still hear it with ears that are half-sealed, cupped and closing. I would still be able to hear the hesitation when water runs up the cold pipe and forgets to fall, and know to put my hand there until gravity remembers.

I would tend the cold before it joins the circle. I would clear the limestone's pores with the slow, extra-jointed reach I am growing into. I would guide the cold tracks down so they don't undercut the concrete. I would harvest the water that beads on the underside of my own leaf-tent and fold my tongue - still pink at the edges, but broadening, flattening - up to catch it before it falls. I would not need the tea. I would become the cup.

I would be small and sparse and still, and I would take up exactly as much room as the wall needs me to take up, and leave the rest empty.

If you came down the grating later - still visitor-wet, still looking for something that darts - you wouldn't see me at first either. You'd see what you thought was just a thicker patch of moss on the slope, a place where the beading was particularly heavy, where the green was particularly black.

Then between one THUD and the next, in the dripping quiet, you'd hear a soft displacement and realize the moss had blinked sideways.
