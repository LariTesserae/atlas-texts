I don't find myself standing.

That's the first thing. If I were standing I would be too tall, too far from the wet, and this place does not allow distance.

I find myself low. Lower than I expected to be before I knew what low meant here.

For a while I think I am lying down, my back on that mattress of moss that hisses when you press it, the saturated felt that is somehow both plant and animal and neither. But I'm not lying on it. I'm pressed into it. The heat sits on me the way the air sits on everything here — not as weather, but as weight. A damp hand over the mouth and chest. The light comes down broken, green and grey through two filters of vapor and leaf-roof, so dim I have to let my eyes go soft to see at all.

And then the resolving, slow the way everything resolves here if you stay still long enough.

I am in a pan. Not beside a pan, not at the edge of water. There are no edges here. The ground sweats and then keeps sweating until the sweat becomes ankle-deep, knee-deep, a warm tea-colored skin of water that doesn't know where it ends. I am in that skin, and it is in me.

This is the middle of the country, which is the same as any other part of the country, which is why I could be anywhere and still be exactly where I belong. There is no horizon to walk toward. Two interlocking crowns of those hollow, green-barked herb-trees lean over me and make a roof, and their leaves, the size of tables, cup water and spill it. Drip. Drip. Plop. The sound never stops being the same sound. Somewhere to my left, or maybe all around me, ten thousand throats inflate and flatten, inflate and flatten. The thrum is in my molars before it is in my ears.

I thought, at first, that I would be something that moves through this — a visitor with boots that fill and then empty, eyes that scan. But the eyes here are milky for a reason. Light is not for seeing far.

I let go of that image.

What emerges instead is flatter. Wider.

My chest is broad and it is not quite a chest. When I breathe, I don't pull air down a throat. I pump it across. My skin is damp and it has to stay damp, permeable, frilled at the edges where it meets the moss and the water. If I hold my forearm up to that dim light I can see through it a little — the tea-color moving not in a tube but spreading, like a stain on paper. The ribs show. They pulse, not with my heart but with the chorus. When the pan next to me inflates, I inflate a fraction later. The ripple runs across us like wind, though there is no wind.

I don't have good eyes. I thought I would, but I don't need them. They are small, deep-set, filmed over with something that keeps the condensation from blinding me. They don't look ahead. There is no ahead. There is only *next leaf*, next surface to touch. I navigate by the smell of iron and crushed green, by taste on the skin, by how the saw of the insects changes pitch when a dragonfly — that wide, leaf-veined thing that never leaves its ten cubic feet of air — shifts its orbit. The whole plain is an eardrum and I am part of the drum.

And I am not alone in this body. Nothing here is allowed to be discrete.

At the edge of what I call my hand, the moss that I thought was moss is warm, and it stays warm after I lift my hand, because tiny bodies the size of poppy seeds were using my warmth. A snail the size of a fist has corrected its course by a millimeter to touch my little finger with the lip of its shell, and where it touches, a thin mortar of algae is already knitting us together. On my back, or what would be my back if I had a front and a back — I don't, either end can be the head — there are the orange dots. Mites, or continuations. They are my eyes when I tuck my real ones. On my thigh, a raft of liverwort is growing, and it is not *on* me, it is of me, photosynthesizing for me because I borrowed it, the way the snail's shell greens from the inside.

I am a sheet. That is the word that finally fits after the other words fail.

Not a person in a place, but a place where the place has learned to be a person-shaped thickness. A local increase in surface area. My job is not to eat. Nothing here gathers to a point. I have no stomach as you would draw it. I sit and let water climb me by capillarity. I hang - because even though I am low, I have curtains too, root-hairs and skin-fringes that dangle into the air to drink condensation before it even falls. When a leaf falls — and it is already being eaten from inside before it lets go — I don't catch it. I receive it. I thin where it lands and thicken around it, so there is no gap.

And when I thin too much, when some part of me goes translucent and then transparent and then is simply water again —

two smaller warm surfaces are already there, exactly where my left and right sides were, pulsing. No corpse. No remainder. Nothing dies more than it is replaced, instantly. That is why I have never learned anything. I don't need to. The basin has not finished being made and never will, because finishing would require that something give way and not come back. Here everything gives way and does not give way at the same instant.

If you — if someone still standing — came and put your hand flat on the part of the moss that is my shoulder and kept it there until the sweat of the ground beaded between your fingers, you would feel a faint, polite pressure as I adjusted to include you. I would not want anything from you. I would simply make room by becoming more thinly spread.

I find myself, then, not at a landmark, because there are none. I am in the warm broth under the black-green roof, in the ankle-deep mirror that reflects nothing, where a weeping, spongy rock keeps the mat wet and the mat keeps the rock from ever drying enough to become stone. I am pressed belly to belly with the chorus, my throat moving a fraction after theirs, my skin breathing for all of us.

Who am I?

I am what closeness feels like when it needs hands to keep touching itself. I am a sharing of broken light. I am a way for water to stay spread thin and still be alive.

I am the basin holding its breath, and for a moment, in this exact pan, that breath has my shape.
