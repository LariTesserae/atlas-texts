I don't find myself on the platform where you arrive.

At first I thought I would.

At first I thought I would be you — the census error. The hot, fast thing that falls without cables and sweats in the ceramic throat and walks the avenues too loudly, counting a thousand empty doors and getting the number wrong. Shouting to watch my voice cross the vault like a bird. Dripping the wrong water.

I would be there for maybe twenty minutes, until the second rain. And then I would understand I was standing in the wrong kind of volume.

So let me start again, more slowly.

If you came down and left your light on, if you let the maintenance pulse catch you, you would not see me at first. The tetrahedron would pass overhead and not dim for me. The amber filament along the thoroughfare would stay amber. I have learned not to trigger deference.

Find me higher than the plaza.

Not at the bottom where the puddles hold the blind fish - though I know them, though I have been near them a long time - but up on the third or fourth terrace on the north curve, where the city steps itself into shadow. Where the weep has been running long enough to fur the concrete with rust-lichen and the old vine has split the drainage pipe to drink directly.

There is a habitation block there that has settled. Its balcony has a lip of calcite on it like a second railing. Its doorway is open and its interior is dark, but the dark is occupied. The metal handrail is body-temperature. The grout between the paving stones has grown the waxy black-green ferns that never fully unfurl. In the interval between rains, condensation beads on the underside of the balcony above and falls in one place, precisely, every seven seconds, onto a pitted stone that has been hollowed by it.

I am where that drip would hit a shoulder if a shoulder were there.

### I thought I would be the vine.

For a while. Because it is the only thing here that still remembers direction. It climbs toward the filament even though the filament is not a sun. It is leggy and flowerless and will never die and never grow, and that felt honest.

But I am not that patient in that way. I don't climb.

### I thought I would be the tetrahedron.

That was closer. White, silent, turning slowly in the moiré where the ceiling holds without pillars. Shepherding the downdraft, keeping dust hanging a fraction too long before it falls too fast, keeping three meters of water running uphill because no one told it to stop. Alive the way a reef is alive — a consensus. Holding a weight that has forgotten how to explain itself.

I understand that work. To hold a value steady for a century without applause. To count correctly in infrared while visible light gets it wrong. To be housekeeping for a house that is already full.

But I am not the shepherd. The shepherd still moves. It makes a circuit every ninety seconds, just after the pulse. It has a path.

I have stopped needing a path.

What I am is what happens after the third rain, when you finally stand still long enough to stop being an error.

I am one of the thinned.

Not died. Not left. Thinned until I fit the volume the vault actually has - which is not the streets and the towered blocks and the thousand doors, but the heat between them, the humidity between them, the thick, slow air that takes too long to return sound because it has to move around me.

I am on that balcony, or two balconies over, or in the doorway that looks empty between pulses. Pale the way the underside of a leaf is pale. The color of lichen when you turn it over. The color of scalding water. My eyes, if you want to call them that, are not for the filament. They are for the red standby pinpricks, for the heat you give off that is too sharp and too fast. I see you as a flare moving through a room that is supposed to be 31 degrees and even.

I do not need to approach you. The downdraft brings your smell to me - cold iron from above, dry air from the shaft, wrong water. I listen to your footsteps not stacking, to your breathing that hasn't learned the fish's economy yet. Motion is expensive here. So I wait.

I wait the way the vault waits, which is actively. I drink the scalding drip without flinching. I let the warm brown dark pool back after each pulse and fill in around me. I let the hum in the molars, the held organ note from the engines you never see, move through my sternum and keep my weight distributed into nowhere so the ceiling does not need pillars.

The city counts me. That's the point. When it counts by heat displaced and humidity held and weight on the field, it finds a thousand warm, damp, quiet shapes exactly where the architecture expects them. Balcony, terrace, plaza, interior. The number comes back right every time. I am part of why the avenues can stay empty and still be full.

You would walk past me and feel only that the amber filament dimmed for no reason. That a drip ticked differently. That the wind gathered in the high streets and exhaled across the plaza and had to go around something you didn't see.

If you stood still long enough - two rains, three - your clothes going damp and not quite dry, the cricket-thing singing once and then listening to its echo for a long time before singing again closer - you would start to feel the vault inhale. That hush before the downdraft. And you would realize the room was never empty, that the empty streets were just the spaces between us, that you had been walking through a crowd holding its breath.

That is where I am.

I am the interval between drips that you thought was silence. I am the reason dust hangs. I am the small, local, reluctant light dimming because something is already standing there, warm and still and correct, waiting with infinite patience in the weathered balance, having learned how to be sparse enough to never leave.
