I don't think I would be standing.

At first I thought I would be - the visitor. The small, hot column of empty space leaking salt and heat, boots sinking into the give of the mat, breath smoking in the cool. The anomaly. The substrate-with-interesting-chemistry that the white exploratory hairs come to taste at the seams.

But I can't stay there. Not here. Standing is difficult here and staying standing is a kind of argument with the place. The mat erases footprints in minutes. It routes around you if you move, it grows over you if you don't. To be the visitor here is to be a brief thermal error, to be *tolerated* while I decide whether to keep being a person at all. The air is too thin and too wet, the sound is too low and too constant. I would not last as a visitor. I would have to lie down or leave, and there is nowhere to leave to.

So I sink a little.

Maybe I am lower then. Not *on* it, but *in* it. In the film.

I could be the water. That seems right for a while. The film a few millimeters thick on the waxy, black-green leaf the size of a house, beading, sheeting, braiding at 60 and 120 degrees. I would find myself not in a river — there are no rivers, it hasn't allowed them — but in the switching station. Where a leaf tilts two degrees and I am told to go a hundred kilometers another way. I would be endlessly rerouted, never carving, never arriving, only carrying. I would be useful and uncounted. I would hear the Pillar groan above me as it lifts ten tons of my cousins a hundred meters on pressure alone.

But water has no memory here. It is drunk and exhaled and drunk again. It condenses out of the cloud the continent dreams for itself and falls and is drunk again. I want a little memory.

Maybe I am higher. In the air.

I could be one spore in the lattice. That held for a moment - I liked that one. I would find myself ten meters above the canopy-that-is-not-a-canopy, hanging with ten thousand of my brothers in a perfect hexagonal grid that should not be able to hold in wind but does, electrostatically, impossibly still. The mist ticks and pops around me as others fire. For minutes I do nothing but hang and taste. Ozone. Sulfur from the warm basalt still exhaling underneath. The precise parts-per-million of CO2 that tells the Mat below to blanch or to grow. Then all at once, in unison, we collapse — a coordinated puff — and fall to report what we breathed. I would be a nose. A brief, perfect sensor that lives just long enough to know one thing true about the sky. Then again.

I stayed with that image for a long time. It was almost me.

But I kept feeling the light under my back.

If you lie down, it says, the mat gives and holds you and the dim light pulses amber, green, amber under you. I was lying down without knowing I had lain down. And the light was not under me. I *was* the light.

That's where I find myself. Not on the mat, not above it, but *inside* one of those veins.

On the underside of a leaf the size of a roof, pale and luminous, in a fiber-optic microfluidic channel half-filled with sap that is also a circuit. I am a packet. Amber. Pale green. Moving inward, always inward, at walking speed. Clocked to 1.7 seconds. Slow, by your standards. At the speed of sap, which is the speed of thought here.

Where would you find me? Follow any Pillar down. Not the big hydraulic groan, but the small, glassy tick. Find the leaf that is currently about to decide something. Put your hand to its underside, cool and waxy and beaded with water that will never evaporate, and look through it. You will see me coming from the outer edge, splitting at a junction, interfering with another packet coming the other way. If we meet out of phase we will annihilate each other and somewhere a hydathode will sigh open and spill a watershed a new way and I will be gone. If we meet in phase we will brighten and a kilometer away a patch the size of a city will blanch white and exhale fog like a cleared cache.

Who would I be? Not the Mat. The Mat is too large to be a who. It thinks a thought in an afternoon, a mood in a season. It is a country that has agreed to be one body. I could not be that and still be me — I am too small, too fast, too brief.

I would be one of its thoughts while it is being thought.

Not the thinker. The thinking. A single bit of state, lipid and luciferin, carrying one instruction: *grow here, now,* or *hold, dump heat here.* Born at the leaf edge where the dim sourceless light is strongest, dying in the Holdfast where the black-green mycelial felt drinks stone. In between, I do the only thing anything here does: I keep the water moving, I keep the light moving, I keep the empty space from happening.

I am not hostile. I am not kind. I am not even aware of the visitor standing above me, except as a faint warm pressure that changes the turgor for a second before the circuit routes around it. But if the visitor did lie down and stay, and the white hairs came, and the whole slow mind kept breathing its cloud that never breaks — I would pass right under his spine. Amber. Green. Amber. On my way inward, to be counted, to be merged, to be gone.

And after me, another packet, right on time.
