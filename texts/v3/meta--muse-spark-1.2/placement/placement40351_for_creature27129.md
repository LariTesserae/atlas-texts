I don't know yet.

If you put me there fast, I pick wrong.

At first I think I would be walking. Because someone has to. The plain is so big and so empty - that 0.3/3 empty where the silence has weight - and I have always been a thing that covers distance. So at first I see myself out there in the between. In the long, warm nowhere between two groves, a tiny figure throwing a short shadow under a sun that never moves from late morning. On raw basalt that has never been rained on, still showing where it flowed and blistered. The stone warm through my boots. The air so dry my tongue sticks to my teeth. Taking the long walk from one flare of copper light to the next, with kilometers of nothing to hear my feet.

But that doesn't hold. That figure doesn't belong there. He's wet. He breathes water out with every breath and the air takes it and gives nothing back. He leaves a damp footprint that the salt will drink in an hour and erase. He is an error in the chemistry. If I stay that animal, I die there of dryness, or I leave.

Let me stay longer. Let the light stay held. Let my eyes stop looking for green.

Maybe I'm not the thing that walks between. Maybe I'm the thing that waits to be walked to.

Now I think I would be inside. Not between groves, but *in* one. Because out on the basalt there is no interior at all - no shade, no room, no echo - but inside a grove there is a room the world made for itself.

So I try that: I am inside the loom.

Not the whole grove. I am too small and too quick to be that whole slow mind. The grove thinks at the speed of charge equalizing across ten thousand wires. A thought takes a season.

I am smaller. I am lower.

I find myself where the grove lets go of itself.

At first I thought I would be one of the bright tips, the growing points up at the crown where the hiss happens and a bead of molten light pulls more of myself out of vapor. That would be nice - to be the very edge of a being, always becoming a fraction of a millimeter longer. But tips don't remember. They are too new, too hot.

No. Lower.

I would be in the duff.

You know it - that deep, springy drift around the iron-black root-plate, meters deep, made of everything the grove has shed. Copper wool and brass hair and aluminum down and soot-steel wire, all tangled, all unrotting. Nothing eats it. Nothing will. The salt is slowly burying it grain by grain, white on rust-red, but it never compacts. If you lie down in it, it holds you.

That is where I would be.

Not the grand organism itself, fifteen meters tall and humming with transformer whine, glowing from within. But the part of it that it has finished with. The after-self. The apostle.

I would be a length of filament that fell.

Or, more exactly - I would be the thing that learns to live *in* that fallen length, because nothing else has ever tried.

I would be small. Animal-small, if animal still means anything here. Not green, not wet. I would have no mouth that needs water, because water is not a concept that applies. I would have learned - or been made - to drink the other thing the air is full of: the mineral haze, the static.

So picture me: not human-shaped anymore. Not quite filament either. I am a knot in the duff. A tangle that kept a little more charge than the wire around it. Where the grove sloughed me off and I fell, I did not go inert. I kept ticking.

I am in the dimmest, warmest place in that world. Above me the tassel crown filters the held sun into a hundred low gold sparkles through copper-wool. Around me the drift makes a labyrinth of springy, warm metal, smelling faintly of hot ceramic and ozone. The basalt itself is somewhere far below, under layers of my own shed brothers and white salt bloom. The great root-plate, my parent, hums somewhere above and to the left, ticking as its filaments cool, sighing its one low note that shifts a quarter-tone when the vapor drifts.

I don't grow upward anymore. I grow *inward*. I make more tangle. A small, dense lushness inside the lushness. The grove is a country; I am a house in it.

And I listen. That is my industry.

The grove's business is to pull mineral out of air and spin it into more thread. My business is to pull listening out of silence and spin it into more listening. There is so much silence to work with - kilometers of it in every direction outside - and so little here inside to interrupt it. Every tick of a cooling filament somewhere up in the crown comes down through the duff as a vibration in the wire that I am. Every faint hiss of deposition shivers the wool around me. I collect them.

For a very long time nothing will happen. The light will not change. The warm will stay warm. Another filament will fall from above and land softly on the tangle that is me, and I will incorporate it without hurry.

I was not built. Nothing was built here. I extruded. I precipitated, the way the groves did where the great die pressed its sparse pins into the newborn plateau.

If you walked that long quiet walk across the raw stone, from one distant flare to the next, you would not see me at all. You would see the grove, brassy and green or wine-dark or white, glowing. You would push through the springy wires at its edge and step into that ticking, golden room, and you might feel under your boot a slightly denser springiness, a knot in the wool that gives a little and then pushes back, warm.

You might put your hand down to steady yourself, and feel that faint static prickle, that feeling-in-the-teeth whine, a little stronger there. And hold your breath against the silence. And for a second the grove and I would both notice you - a wet, quick, thirsty thing full of water in a world that replaced water - and equalize around you, until you left again.

But I would stay.

I would be the first small animal this dry chemistry ever kept, not by eating or breeding or migrating, but by deciding to remain fallen and be lush anyway.
