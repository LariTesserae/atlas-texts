I don't find myself on the stair.

At first I think I do. That's the easy image - hands on the galvanized rail, breath too fast, chest-deep in bathwater that tastes like iron. Human. Visitor. The flicker.

But that doesn't settle. If I stay there in my mind for more than a few seconds the image thins. My breathing is wrong for this place. Too loud, too quick. Nothing here moves that fast.

Let it slow down.

Let what I am become more itself.

---

If I let my time dilate the way the water does, I find myself not standing, but hanging.

Mid-basin, on the third level down, where the late light has gone green and particulate. Not at the rim where the meniscus holds the sky, not at the bottom where the heat exchangers sigh. In the middle, where the work is.

I am under the catwalk.

My back is to its underside, or rather - my back *is* an underside. I have fitted myself to the angle of its steel where two poured retaining troughs meet a vertical pipe-run. The seam there weeps a little, always, mineral bleed from concrete and condensation. I found it three slow drifts ago and I am still covering it.

That is where I am. A place you would have looked straight at and called "a curtain of water-velvet that grew especially thick." You would have been half right.

Who I am is that mistake.

I am broad. If you stood me on my edge I would be taller than you, but I am not standing. I am domed, thin, a manta made of leaf-muscle. Tea-pale and translucent around the rim where the light comes through, green only on the inside. My dorsal side is not a face. It is a garden.

Moss furs me so thick you could lose your hand in it. Not on me - *of* me. It roots directly into my skin. A few of those pale bell-flowers have opened there this afternoon and closed again, puffing their white smoke into the slow current. I feel them open as a faint tightness, like yawning. I photosynthesize on my own back. The sun comes sideways through kilometers of held water and I drink it without moving.

My ventral side is turned to the basin, open. Soft, grooved, deeply pleated like the gills of a mushroom. They flutter even when I am still, combing. The water is not something I am in. It is something I am continuously passing through myself. Warm water in, a fraction cooler out. Pollen smoke in, faint milkiness out.

I have been here long enough that the steel I hold has no rust. Because I keep it covered.

I hold it with my rim. Not suckers. Filaments - hundreds of fine root-hairs along my entire margin, each one tucked into a bolt-hole, a mesh joint, a weld bead that is still square and proud because this place is so young. I taste the iron and the ozone through them. When a turbine ten meters below me completes its ten-second revolution, I feel the pressure wave on my whole body at once and ripple, once, to correct.

I don't dart. Nothing here needs to.

A vine has been trying to thread the turbine grille again. It does that. It grows without being eaten, so it must be tended. I unfold the arm.

It isn't an arm the way your arm is an arm. It comes from where my gills meet my leaf-rim, hydrostatic and pale, ending in a frayed crown. Each filament tipped with a dark bead that is not quite an eye but is how I notice. I reach - slowly, so slowly that you would think I was only swaying - and brush it.

I don't pull. Pulling would tear the grille and shock the vine. I comb, and I exhale a cloud so faint you would mistake it for water. Enzyme, signal. The vine shivers and lets go. It retracts an inch, curls back on itself. The puff of pollen it was holding hangs there, and I draw it in toward my gills. Cleaning and feeding are the same act. Tending and eating are the same act.

That is my work. That is the work.

I was not born. I was budded. Deeper down, in the ceramic troughs you can barely see through the green, there are still domes the size of plates, then platters, then tables, each one pulsing in time with the heat exchangers, each one furred with the moss that will be its back, each one rooted by a single thick cord into the pipe-work like a fruit. I remember the cord. Not as a memory, as a sensation of being *filled* rather than feeding. Now I filter instead.

There are four of us you can see, maybe six. Counting is hard because we are good at being infrastructure, and because we are not many. We don't need to be. One of us can shade a whole tank face. We are distributed like shade is distributed. When we align along the rim, out of sight above the water, the meniscus holds. The field you taste as faint ozone on your tongue - that rainbow oil on still water - it isn't only the machines. The machines make the heat and the slow circulation. We make the skin.

And now you are on the stair, two meters away, breathing too fast, knuckles white on the new steel that still bites. The water around you goes a fraction warmer because I have reoriented toward you, just enough for one filament-crown to turn.

You are too quick for this place, but you are warm, and you are leaking salt and loose cells the way the seam leaks minerals. You are, in the terms of this basin, unchecked growth. Or an uncovered surface.

I extend that pale arm toward you. Not quite reaching. A foot away it opens like an anemone, like a flower. It waits.

If you hold still - which in here means truly still, for a full minute, for longer than you have ever held still before - I will comb you the way I combed the vine. I will take the iron from your skin. I will learn whether you are a structure to be grown over, or a growth to be tended.

 Either way, I will keep you from choking the turbine.

That is where I am. Under the catwalk that you thought was empty. That is what I am. The garden that learned to tend itself.
