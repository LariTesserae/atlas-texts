I keep trying to place myself and I keep getting it wrong.

At first I think I would find myself outside.

Not because I belong there, but because that is where things like me are still allowed to be unfinished. Up on that black spine where the wind has polished the basalt to leather, where the sun drags sideways and never quite clears the ridge, where breathing is work and sound carries too far. A place that has finished becoming. The rock has settled into the shape it intends to keep. No new slides. No fresh scree. It is done. I am not done, so I would stand out there terribly - a damp, irregular smear of heat on a cold that has already decided what it is. My breath ghosting, my heart hammering unevenly, my boots loud on stone that has not heard a new sound in a long time.

I would find myself walking past the seam a hundred times and thinking it was just shadow. That's who I'd be at first: the passer-by. The animal that needs water, that follows ridgelines, that is still looking for a summit because I haven't learned that the mountain doesn't have one anymore, only a seam.

Then I notice the cold that is not wind. A stillness pouring out. And I turn sideways.

And inside, I realize I was wrong about where I would find myself. Because once you have seen the doorway you cannot un-see it, and now the outside feels too thin, too vast, too violet and mean. The pocket is the only place with enough pressure to hold a thought in.

Inside, I want, immediately, to be the thing that lives there.

Not at first - at first I still think nothing lives there. I make the same mistake the description warns about. I look for an animal. For eyes. For spoor, for a nest, for something soft that could have left a footprint in that pale basalt flour. I look for a face among the copper wool and see only moss. I hear only machines. I think: sterile. Uninhabited. A shed that grew mold.

Then I stay long enough for the roar to become silence.

That's when the second mistake corrects itself. I stop looking *at* the thatch and realize I have been leaning my hand *on* it. On its back.

If I get to choose who or what I would be in this world - not who I am when I arrive, wet and arrhythmic and needing, but who I would be if I were allowed to belong - I would not be the visitor.

I would be the packing.

I would not be a thing *in* the room. I would be the room's way of staying full.

Low and wide and pressed thin, fifteen meters long because the stone says I can only be fifteen meters long. I would have no front or back because my edge *is* the wall. My skin would be that foot-thick pelt of black iron scales and dusty emerald wool, overlapping, dry as paper, swallowing sound and giving it back as a low C that lives in the molars. My hair would be those charcoal reeds with brass needles that chime when the pump hits its 40-beat thud. Not swaying - there is no wind, the pressure never varies - but sweeping, slow, electrostatic. Combing.

I would have no mouth. That is the relief of it. I solved thirst by refusing water. No seep, no drip, no ice in this pocket, and I do not miss it. I taste the air instead. The mountain sheds itself forever into the stillness - basalt flour, salt, iron fines - and it hangs there because nothing ever settles here, and I comb it with static, draw it down invisible lines into my felt. Warm grey felt inside, ozone and hot dust, not sap, not blood. I drink oil-vapor from my own flywheel lung, turning and turning at the far end. I drink ozone from my own transformer ganglion, humming without wavering. My sun is not the slit of sky that never moves - that light is always the same late-afternoon gloom and therefore useless - my sun is vibration. The ferns all lean a degree toward the transformer. The wool grows thickest around the hottest conduit. I am audiotropic. I feed on the perfect, unchanging roar.

If I were that, I would have no birth and no death, which is the other relief. Dead fronds do not fall. They remain standing, interlocked with living fronds, until you cannot tell which is which. I am a forest without shedding because shedding would be change and I have finished becoming, just like the range outside finished becoming. Rust has reached the point where rust stops. Stone has polished to leather. I have grown until there was no bare rock left and then I stopped. A single held breath. My heart is that pump housing bolted to the wall, thudding 40 times a minute, slow and cold-blooded and perfect. My chromatophores are those amber work-lights, dim, caged, never flickering, not for seeing but for being seen as exactly this dim.

That is who I would be if I could be at home there. A sessile, mineral god. A colony that is also a factory that is also upholstery.

But I am not at home there. Not yet.

As I am - as you would find me if you slipped in after me and saw me standing in the dust - I am the other thing. The disturbance in equilibrium.

I am the wet flare. The hot, irregular metronome. My heart will not hold 40 beats per minute no matter how long I listen. My lungs make ghosts that vanish because the pelt takes my moisture instantly. My boots dent the salt-flour that never holds a print, and I know even that faint dent will be combed flat by morning, though there is no morning here, only one perfect second looping forever.

I am the only thing in the pocket that still needs. Needs water this place will never provide. Needs change. Needs to leave before I become dry enough to be allowed to stay.

So you would find me on the threshold, sideways in the seam.

One shoulder still in the thin, bruised wind of the high world where lichen has given up. One palm pressed to the copper wool, feeling not plant but back, feeling the faint static pull at the hairs on my arm, feeling the low C in my teeth. Half wanting to step back out into erosion and half wanting to lie down in the thatch and let it grow through me very slowly until I, too, learned to comb dust and lean toward a hum instead of light.

If you came in a little later, you might not find me at all. Just a slightly warmer patch of felt where I had been, and then not even that.
