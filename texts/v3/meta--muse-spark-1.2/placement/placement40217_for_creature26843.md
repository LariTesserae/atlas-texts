I don't find myself at the center.

At first I thought I would - in the marsh, where the vapor comes up around the calves, where you can see everything at once like a cathedral ceiling. That would be the obvious place to stand. The place the basin makes for you. A perfect circle of sky, everything breathing. But that place is already taken. It's tended.

So I arrive a little to the side, and only after I've stopped trying to arrive.

---

I come over the lip late. I've been climbing for hours through that wrong air that gets hotter as it thins, and my body keeps expecting the cold to correct it. It doesn't. By the time the rim cuts the sky, my mouth is dry and my shirt is already damp, not from sweat but from the air sweating on me.

I sit on the stair-step of young glass where the stone still remembers being liquid. It's too sharp to be comfortable. The edge bites through my pants. Below me the bowl is exactly as described and not at all what I expected - smaller. You can hold the whole thing in your eyes without moving your head. That smallness is disorienting after a big climb. I thought I was going somewhere vast. I went somewhere precise.

I don't go down right away. High places are supposed to make you look out. This one makes you look in.

The drip starts to tune my hearing. 

*Drip.*

A long pause where the heat ticks the basalt.

*Drip.*

My breath is too loud. My thoughts are too loud. The bowl swallows sound, so everything I bring with me has nowhere to go but back into me. I stay on the rim until my breathing slows enough that I can hear the other ticking underneath - the expansion crackle, the sigh of water pushing up into a footprint I haven't made yet.

When I finally slide down, I don't go to the pylons. Everyone would go to the pylons.

I go to the scarcity between them. Ten meters of bare, steaming black between the exploded fern-island and the low ring. The place where nothing is growing, where the rock is just weeping in sheets. I put my back against the warm glass and let the condensate run down the back of my neck. It's hot, almost blood-warm. The stone is weeping and now I am weeping, and for a minute there is no difference.

That's where I find myself. Not in the marsh, not in the ring. On the margin. In the ecotone between hot and cold. Where the gradient lives.

Who would I be there?

At first, for the first hour, I am very clearly a human. An animal that climbed too high and is now ankle-deep in the wrong weather, with a water bottle getting warm in my pack, checking my phone for signal that the bowl has already swallowed. I am a loud, hot shape that exhales carbon and history. I try to be useful. I try to count the drips, to name the fern, to figure out how the lattice holds the rain without machinery. I fail. The pylon's moat shows me a half-second late, and I watch myself arrive after I've arrived.

Then the fog beads on me and corrects me.

It beads on my forearms, my eyelashes, the hair on my knuckles. I wipe it and it comes back. I stop wiping it. The longer I sit still against the weeping wall - not draped like *it* drapes, but trying, badly, to imitate stillness - the more my heat starts to do what its heat does. The marsh exhales, the stone radiates, the sun pours onto the young black. And somewhere a few feet away, the lattice drinks it and makes a pocket of cold that is not wind, just... absence.

I don't become it. I don't have filaments. I don't have the patience to watch basalt cool. I don't get to be that lonely or that perfect.

I become what it's tending.

I think in that basin there are only two jobs: tending and being tended. The lattice tends the inversion so it doesn't correct itself. The ferns tend to be huge and alone. The water tends to hang. And something has to be the thing that is tended - the brief, hot, wet shape that wanders in and steams a little and lets the cycle have something new to bead on, to run uphill toward for an inch before remembering gravity.

I would be the interim. The soft organ.

If I had to give it a name in the language of the bowl, I would be the Moss-Flower-That-Tracks-The-Wrong-Sun. Or the Footprint-That-Fills. Or the Hairs-On-The-Arm That Rise Near The Curtain.

Specifically: I would be the one who sits inside the low ring for an afternoon, not because I belong there, but because the ring lets me.

I curl my knees to my chest inside that knee-high circle of cold lattice, where the air ripples like water when the wind passes. The moss is impossibly thick there, poured. The little white flowers are open. They have been tracking the cold pylon, not the sun. When I settle, they slowly - over an hour - turn a few degrees toward me. Because I am also radiating heat, because I am also beading. For that hour, I am a second, smaller cold pylon to them. They double their veining for me. The fern fronds at the edge of the ring show their veins on the outside, mirrored, and I understand it isn't a mistake. It's a draft. A rehearsal. The lattice is trying a pattern on them before it tries it on itself.

I don't do anything. That is the tending. I breathe at the speed the bowl allows - one breath per fog cycle - until my drip and its drip start to share a pause.

*Drip.*

I inhale when the frond folds a half-second before the water hits it.

*Long pause.*

I exhale when the curtain of held rain shivers for no reason.

I am not holding the rain. I am being held near it. The field that suspends each droplet grain by grain hums the small hairs on my arms and I let it taste me the way it tastes minerals, for silt, for the memory of being molten. I am warm water with a history of lowlands in it. I am briefly interesting to it.

When the glider passes - that pale manta that never lands - it casts its shadow over the marsh and over me, and I look up with everyone else, with the ferns and the moss and the suspended beads. For a second we are all the same thing: warm shapes that have learned to be still enough to be outlined by fog.

I would be that. Not the weaver, but the weft. Not the one who drinks heat and makes cold, but the one who brings a little extra heat to drink. A pilgrim who came up expecting cold and instead found a place that needed his warmth for a little while, to keep its engine running.

If I stay until dusk, I get to see it truly once, the way you described. Backlit, the wall of rain goes prismatic, and inside it the darker density branches, breathing cold into hot air, holding a thousand glass beads perfectly still around itself like a cloak.

It doesn't look at me. It doesn't need to. The droplets fall all at once with a soft hiss and where it was there is only vapor.

And I am still inside the ring, my shoulders beaded, my reflection a half-second late in the moat, steaming a little, dripping a little.

Tended.
