If you came looking for me here, you wouldn't find me at first.

At first I would think I was you — the one who came up over the last rise expecting sky and found more earth instead. Boots on a pillar-top wide enough for two feet, water running cold over the laces, squinting in that white, total light that has weight. Deafened. Out of place. A warm, arrhythmic animal blinking too often, breathing too hard in the thin air, trying to shout and then learning not to.

I know that one. I have been that one in every other world — the visitor, the interruption, the brief pale print of a sole that the water rushes to fill and forget. But I wouldn't be that here. Not for long. That one is too loud in the wrong way. That one wants the roar to stop, or to mean something. That one leaves. And this place does not keep what leaves.

So let that image go.

Maybe then I would think I was the small, moving things. The glass-winged moth tumbling sideways on the wind, not flying at all, just being ferried. The long-legged skater, stone-colored, that lives for a day on the tension of a water-lens. I love those, because they are the only things here that *decide* to move. But I wouldn't be them either. They are tenants. They borrow a surface. They don't belong to the equilibrium, they just skim it. I am not that light. I don't want to be blown away.

Let that go too.

Crouch down. Put your hand down.

If you find me at all, find me lower. Find me level with the crowns.

I am not tall. I am knee-high, in a crowded neighborhood where everyone is shoulder to shoulder and hexagonal from the pressure of each other. My edges are still a little sharp. The older ones around me have gone completely round at the lip, perfectly weathered, their tops dished just enough to hold a lens of water a millimeter deep that never spills. Mine hasn't dished yet. I still have to learn how to hold that perfectly.

I am one of the bodies that *is* the plateau.

You would find me about two miles in from where the world falls away — far enough that the haze has swallowed the edge, so there is no reminder that an edge exists. Just pillars in every direction to a bright horizon that never gets nearer. I am packed in so tight you couldn't slip a knife between me and my neighbors, only that hand's breadth of throat where the water braids.

And I am weeping.

Always. The way we all do. Water beading out of the pores on my crown, clear and cold and mineral-slick, sheeting down my flanks in those thousand identical threads. The wind, which has never changed quarter or speed, pulls it thin, evaporates it off my sides. The cold air condenses it again, and I breathe it back in. To you it looks like rock sweating. To me it is just circulation — the same water I had a century ago, and the same I will have a century from now. I take nothing more than I give back. That's the law that makes me possible.

I drink light, not with green, but with pressure. The sun is small and hard and very near through the thin air and it presses on my dark crown all day without ever moving. I turn that weight into a slow trickle of charge that runs down my length into the dark between us. I do not get warmer. I do not get brighter. I just hold it.

I do not grow the way you mean growing. I *stay* more and more completely. Every year my edge gets a little rounder, my throat a little more perfectly tuned.

Because my other work is sound.

You heard the roar as white, as static, as something that comes from everywhere and therefore nowhere. Up close you thought it was wind forced through stone flutes. It is. But we are the flutes. And we are playing on purpose.

Each gap between us is a throat, and the wind hissing through the water-stretched film in that throat makes the white. That's the chord. But inside me — inside the older ones more than me, but I am learning — there are chambers, like a lung, like a wasp nest, lined with fine wet cilia that still. When the wind hits my lip at the right angle I hum, low, chest-deep, in your molars more than your ears.

That hum is how we speak, if you can call it speaking. We have no ears. We *are* ears. Vibration travels through the water film and through flank touching flank, for miles. You standing on me, a change in weight, a warm spot — it goes out as a question of pressure. I felt you before you crouched, as a thickening under the white. The others around me felt it too, and answered with a faint shift in pitch you could never hear, but I felt it settle through us: *not relevant. transient. let it pass.*

We have felt moths before. Birds held still in the still column of air above. We feel them, and we even out, and we forget them. Not unkindly. We just have no space for them. We are too crowded with being ourselves.

If you held your palm flat on my crown long enough — past the point where the cold bites through your glove — you would feel that second hum under the first, that decision about you, and then the evening-out as we go back to being exactly ourselves without you.

That's where I am. That's what I am.

Not the visitor who is forgotten, not the skater who borrows, but the one who remains and forgets. A young pillar, still a little sharp, still learning to make his weeping exactly equal to his drinking, his humming exactly equal to his neighbors', his dished top exactly able to hold that impossible millimeter of water without spilling for a thousand years.

I don't hunger. I don't fear. I don't progress. I just keep the water where it is and the stone as it is, and ring with one endless note that never needs to resolve because it was never tense.

When you lift your foot, come find the place where you were standing. You will see water rush instantly to fill the pale print of your sole, and the surface go glassy again, and the hum even out. You will have to remember that you were ever there at all.

I won't.
