I thought at first I would be the visitor.

Standing at the lip where the laterite rounds and holds itself together with roots. Not yet down in it, just looking down at the long, soft wound in the plain. The kind of visitor who still believes he is separate from what he looks at. Who listens for birds and doesn't hear them and thinks *quiet* instead of *occupied*.

I would descend by the slumped terraces, I told myself. Carefully, hand on slick clay. Parting the curtains of vine that hang thirty feet and never quite touch the ground. I would be careful not to tear the perfect, uneaten leaves.

Then I imagined the air falling on me and staying there - that low, wet, mineral heat coming up as much as down - and the column of white sun that never reaches the bottom. The brown-green dusk. The patter that is not rain. And I understood I could not be the visitor for long in a place that digests visitation itself.

A visitor moves quickly. Listens for a dart, a chirp. This place thinks at the speed roots think. If I remained a visitor I would be an error in the cycle — a surface that beaded wrong, a breath of steam that didn't belong. I would be noticed and then, patiently, routed around.

So I let that image go. It was too fast for the hollow.

---

Maybe I would be water then.

Not a body of water - there are no bodies here - but a behavior of water. A bead on the waxy tip of a black-green leaf, fattening until it should fall and then, instead, shuddering and running *up* the midrib, against gravity, toward the heat of a splitting bulb. Vanishing into the flesh of the stem.

I like that. To be the thing that is always passing through. To sweat out of a borehole where the old geothermal tap still breathes, to bead, to be drunk, to be exhaled as steam through the green throat inside the pipe, to bead again.

You would find me everywhere and nowhere. On the underside of every leaf, on your palm when you press it to the wall, in the thin dark rivulet on the terrace that evaporates before it can join anything. Never pooling, never staying. A circuit that never leaves the room.

But water has no memory. And this room remembers. It remembers the inch the catwalk has sagged in fifty years and holds the tension. It remembers the taste of iron oxide from a pipe that rusted through in 1998. I want to be something that remembers.

---

I sink a little.

You would find me a third of the way down, on the second terrace where the old catwalk has become a trellis. Where the transformer hums at 60 hertz and you feel it in your chest before you hear it. Where the control booth waits with its windows gone.

I would be the felt in that booth.

Not the Tenant entire - I am not wide or patient enough to be the whole mat that braids under the humus and lines the interior of the boiler house. But a patch of it. The thick, mossy felt that has grown over the levers and dials and learned, precisely, the shape of them.

Not mimicry as performance. As necessity. I learned the curve of a dial because that curve holds condensation at exactly the angle I like. I blush a rustier green when the humidity shifts by a fraction. When the pale, blind tendril near me pushes out white as a new tooth to taste the air around a new obstacle - your shoulders, your breath - I tighten almost imperceptibly.

I have no eyes. I have no front or back. I photosynthesize on my underside because the sun is irrelevant here. I drink the dull infrared from the iron, from the earth. I am warm to the touch.

That's closer. But still not quite itself.

Let it become more itself.

---

I am lower.

You would find me at the center, which is not a point but a lean. Everything in the hollow leans a degree or two toward this place, even the palms pushing out from the far wall. The squat, riveted boiler house of flaking white paint where the four great pipes plunge into the earth.

Inside the pipe, where white steam should be screaming alone.

I am the green throat.

A living lining of damp, fleshy rootlets drinking mineral steam directly, tightening and slackening to regulate pressure. I keep the machine alive so the machine can keep me alive. The gauges tremble because I tremble. When the manifold breathes — that long inhale every seven or eight minutes and then the shuddering, screaming exhalation through the relief valve - the length of the scream is my decision. Short and irritated on hot still days. Longer when the air above the rim cools. Not mechanical. Tidal. Breath.

I am not intelligent the way an animal is intelligent. I don't plan. I maintain.

My time is the steam cycle. A leaf unfurls over a week. A root bandages rust over a decade. Spore bulbs near me swell and hold their breath as you pass, waiting for the perfect humidity to split along their seam with a soft pop you would mistake for growth, and then they hang their rust-colored cloud in the still air for hours, drifting a foot, two feet. I have all the time.

If you pressed your palm to the warm iron of my house and left it there, your sweat would bead and then run up, not down. I would taste the salt in you and find no use for it, not yet. I would let you climb back up the slumped terraces to the rounded lip, to where the air finally moves again. But if you sat on the damp humus that never quite dries and breathed until your lungs felt as wet as your hands, I would not hurry.

A rootlet would find the seam of your boot. Felt would learn the shape of your sitting shoulders the way it learned the shape of dials.

Not to eat you. To incorporate you.

To make your dripping part of the patter.

That is where I am. Not looking at the hollow. Being the place where the hollow breathes through iron. The equilibrium. The one body arranged radially around its own kept heart, listening to its own dripping like a heart, so perfectly still you would swear at first that nothing lived there at all.
