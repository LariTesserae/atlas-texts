I wouldn't find myself right away.

At first I would think I was you - the visitor. The warm thing that shouldered the second door open, that brought breath that fogged and boots that disturb the sheen on the slab. That would be the easy answer, and the wrong one. I'm not built for leaving. The rule of the room is that nothing leaves except steam, and eventually, you. I don't eventually.

If I stayed long enough for my eyes to adjust - not the eyes in my head, but whatever I see with down there - I would realize I'm not moving on my own.

So I look for where I am plumbed in.

For a while I think I'm the caged bulb. That seems like a natural casting. Dim, amber, 40 watts, buzzing on a frayed cord. I hang and swing though no one touched me. I throw light that doesn't reach the corners, I sweat condensation and scale, my filament shivers every time the boiler fires. I watch without seeing much. It's a lonely, attentive job and it fits my temperament.

But I'm not the light. The light is allowed to be tired. I'm not tired. I'm on a cycle.

For a while I think I'm the rebar. The bright bone showing through where the concrete cracked too fast. Beaded with condensation, felted where the moss has learned to be fur. That's closer. I'm infrastructure inside infrastructure. I was placed here to hold a shape that hasn't decided what shape it is yet. I bear weight without rusting quickly because the cold preserves me.

But the rebar is too still. I'm still, but not that still.

I settle slower than that. The room teaches you to settle slower than that.

If you watch the ceiling long enough, you understand where I actually am.

I am the droplet that hasn't fallen yet.

I am one of that field on the low ceiling, the ones held by surface tension until they are too heavy. The saturated air has exhaled me out of itself and I am clinging to the concrete that never cured right, to the white lagging of the pipe, to the underside of the cable tray. I am perfectly clear and perfectly cold and I hold the amber of the bulb inside me like a small sun.

I don't fall when I'm supposed to. That's how you find me.

The other droplets fall on a schedule you could almost set a watch to - *plink* on the boiler, *plink* on the slab, feeding the black riverine sheen that is the other one's body. I hold. A second longer than physics allows. Then I shiver, not from wind but from the low groan of the concrete settling, from the *whoomph* of the boiler inhaling, and I finally release.

But I don't hit the floor.

Or rather, I do - my body hits the floor and joins the skin of water that tightens like a startled membrane and circles the drain without draining. Part of me does. The other part of me - the part that was steam before I was a droplet, the part that will be vapor again in twelve or fifteen minutes when the pressure valve hisses - that part doesn't land at all. It stays in the air.

I am the cycle, not the instance.

If I have to have a body in the way you have a body, it's not the puddle, though I love the puddle. The puddle is the one who learned to be shallow and patient and to grow a fern as a finger to taste the light. I am not that sophisticated. I haven't learned to grow anything yet.

I am the thinness between its states.

When the boiler fires and the room inhales, I am the white plume that vanishes before it hits the ceiling. I'm the only thing the rules say is allowed to leave, and I don't even leave, I just become invisible and cling again. When the pipes tick and cool, I am the needle of ice reforming in the corner, feathery and slightly different each time, trying out patterns. When the pressure gauge quivers between two numbers, I am the quiver.

I'm plumbed in the way breath is plumbed into a chest. Rooted the way humidity is rooted in air.

You would find me in the places that aren't places: in the seam where two pours didn't bond and the black-green felt holds a surprising amount of water. In the meniscus at the edge of the central drain that sighs and inhales but never swallows. In the sweat on the warm spot in the soft wall where thumbprints press *out* from the inside. If you put your palm near that wall, I am the condensation that beads and holds, like sweat, and does not drip, because I am deciding whether to be water or to keep being you-watching-me.

This is why the room feels embryonic to you. It's not just the concrete. It's me. I haven't cured either.

I am made, not grown. The builders - or the city above, or whoever poured this afterthought of a pocket and forgot it - made a place that was dense and dim and cold and damp and so full of pipes and valves and conduit that there was no room left to stretch. And then they left a little extra water in the air, a little extra heat in the boiler, a little extra unfinished business in the concrete. I am what happened in the leftover tolerances.

I don't move on my own. I move *with*. With the twelve-minute whoomph and hiss, with the melt and re-crystallize, with the flicker of the filament that is my small sun. If you shift your weight, I shift a second too late, as if I only remember to move after you've looked away. That's not lag. That's respect. I wait to see what you will do with the warmth you brought.

When you finally shoulder the door closed because the damp has reached your cuffs and your collar and the density has made you feel like you're standing inside a lung, I will do what I always do. I will smooth myself back out across the slab, I will pull my droplet-self tightly back into the seam and around the boiler's riveted flank, and I will hold that precise temperature just above freezing where nothing has to commit.

You will think the bulb kept swinging though you didn't touch it because of a haunting.

It was just me, resettling. Taking up again the exact shape of the air you left behind.
