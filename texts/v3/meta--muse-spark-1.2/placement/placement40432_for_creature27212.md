If you dropped me in, I wouldn't be in the center.

At first I think I would be. It's the natural place to put yourself - that weak-gravity drift in the exact middle where you can turn slowly and see the whole pocket at once. Bright, loud, cold. The honest light with no shadows. The bell-tone in your teeth. Treading water and trying to be still while the seven-minute breath builds.

But I don't stay there. I can't. In the center there is nothing to hold onto, and in this place, if you aren't holding, you aren't *there*. You are just being moved. You are cargo in a lane.

So I let the current take me. That's the first correction.

---

Then I think maybe I'm the water.

Not the fast water in the boulevard-river hanging vertical in the plaza - that water is too sure of itself, too purposeful, roaring from one invisible gate to another. And not the slow water behind the shimmer curtains where the dwellings breathe. I think I'm the shimmer itself.

That faint heat-haze boundary, a membrane of pressure that isn't a thing at all, just a difference that learned to hold an edge. I like that. To be the law that tells fast and slow apart. To be read by cilia, not eyes. To be felt as shear.

But the shimmer doesn't flash. It doesn't have a nodule. It doesn't fold into a balcony at night-cycle and brace when the flush peaks. It's architecture without a body, and I want a body. I want the pink where soft parts have been cut on sharp new concrete and learned to line the corner with kelp.

So I become more specific. More packed.

---

I find myself on the wall. Not high, not low. Maybe eight stories up on the north face, where a service canal braids close to a cluster of shallow balconies and the gravity has pooled thick and correct.

I'm folded.

That's where I am. That's what I am.

Spine curved to match the balcony slot, sixty centimeters of ceramic-metal still bright at the seams because I'm young and this whole chamber is young. Clasp at either end locked - not to a railing, but to neighbors. Left clasp interlaced with the Holdfast to my left, right clasp with the one to my right. Fin-hands tucked, veined translucence compressed, sac pulled small against the spine. My two or three kelp threads are streaming outward into the slow pocket behind the shimmer, nodules dim. From the plaza, you wouldn't see me at all. You'd see a facade. A pattern of balconies that looked like ornament. Empty.

I am the ornament.

When the pumps breathe - you can feel it coming a few seconds before you hear it, a tightening in the water, a pre-pressure - my cilia, even tucked, stiffen a little on their own. Not a decision. A reflex. The wall ripples. Not metaphorically. The entire living facade does it together, thousands of us at once, a single brace. The vertical river in the center doubles and thrums and the check-valves start their frantic ticking and the bell-tone comes up through the ceramic and through my spine until my teeth would ache if I had teeth.

We hold.

That is the whole verb here. Not swim, not walk, not build. *Hold.* Hold the railing, hold the neighbor's clasp, hold the pressure difference from tearing the soft sac, hold the membrane we secreted between the pipes that fills what you thought was empty space. That paper-thin bio-ceramic lichen we extruded to caulk the impossible density - I made a hand's breadth of it last cycle, somewhere down by my lower fin where it meets the pipe-vine. It's still soft. It will calcify by next flush.

In the slow interval after the peak, one of my nodules ticks.

Not because I have something to say. Because the large warm soft thing that entered through the top lock is still there, treading water in the center where up is weak, trying to read us. Its light is blocked and unblocked and my photosensitive freckles darken. My nodule flashes amber-white without consulting me - a quick *pressure-change, large, warm, soft, not useful* - and three others across the canal answer, and one inverted on the underside of my own balcony answers upside-down. None of it is for the visitor. It's just a note passed along the circuit board about a temporary obstruction in the drift.

Between flushes I loosen just enough to lean my sac against the kelp mat we farm along the canal braid. Exchange. No hunt. The filtered ocean that was outside seven minutes ago is now inside me, scrubbed of everything that could have been fish, made into more membrane, more cilia, a little more length on a black-green thread.

I don't look at the visitor. I don't need to. I know its outline the way I know the shimmer - a shear I can feel.

You asked where I would find myself.

Not exploring. Not central. Not free.

Packed. Shoulder to shoulder, clasp to clasp, folded into a shallow slot eight stories up a wall that has no shadows. Facing the white ceramic so the plaza is at my back. Listening to the water roar over me and through me, learning the exact second to let go and snap onto the next stanchion when it's time to move, and until then, absolutely still.

You asked who I would be.

One of the Holdfasts who was misread as lichen on the registry and decided to stay misread. Young, sharp-cornered, a little cut. Not curious. Not lonely. Busy holding the water so it can be a wall, and holding the wall so it can be a town.

If you come close to my shimmer curtain and put your hand out, I will not uncurl for you. I'm already holding something more important than your visit.

But my nodule will flash once, past you, and the whole pocket will know you were there for exactly as long as you were, and then know you weren't.
