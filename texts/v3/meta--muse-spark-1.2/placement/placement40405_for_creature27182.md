I don't think I'd find myself all at once.

At first, I would be where you are — at the lip.

Not inside yet. Kneeling at the slit where the salt has sagged over the iron. The air coming up is cooler than the flat by ten degrees and it has that mineral-blood smell, cold iron and alkaline dust, and I would be there a long time before I went in, just with my hand on that rounded lip. It's been sea-glassed by time. Everything that could be taken from it has been taken. It doesn't cut anymore. I know that feeling.

So at first, I'm the visitor. Boots. Warm. Too wet. Breathing too loud through my mouth and drying out. The one the mat tests and decides is not food.

But if I stay - and this place wants you to stay still, that's the trick of it - I stop being the visitor pretty quickly.

Because you can't stand in a room that loud and stay separate from it.

The ticking gets inside your teeth first. *Tick-tick-tick* of a million file-mouths. Then the pops. Then the thud-thud under it all. If you stand still long enough the grazers come. They don't care that you're a person. To them you're a brief, warm hill of salt and leather. They flow up over the toe of your boot, over the laces, testing, rasping politely. And you feel how warm they are, unbelievably warm for something that eats stone in 12 degree air. Metabolism without water runs hot and slow.

I think I would lie down. The basalt is polished like the sole of an old boot and the pan crunches and gives. The white light from the slit moves an inch while you watch. The work-light from the generator makes no shadow. You can see everything.

Lying on my back I wouldn't be a human anymore. The scale would shift.

For a while I would be one of them. A grazer.

Not the idea of a grazer — a specific one. Thumb-joint long, smoked-glass translucent, gut showing faint yellow through the back. That ridiculous fringe of wire-legs rippling instead of stepping. That nacreous plate on my back, scratched like an old watch face. I would not have eyes, not really. I would have hunger and vibration and the taste of gypsum.

And I would be busy. I would plant myself on a good patch of pan where the iron scale has flaked thickest, and I would rasp. Tick-tick-tick. I would eat the floor itself. Salt and rust and ten-thousand-year-old dust. I would make a dark trail behind me, pellet by pellet, and I would not look up because there is no up, there is only mat, shoulder to shoulder, crawling over and under each other, a churning carpet that flows uphill without ever being told to. I would extrude that perfect pearlescent sphere into a crack and leave it. No nest. The pocket is the nest. I would be warm to the touch and completely uninterested in you.

I would be content. That's the strange part. There is no plant, no water, no softness in this world, and the grazer is not unhappy. It has solved the problem. Eat the world. Become the world for the next eater.

But then a shadow passes - a straight line over a field of ovals - and I would be the runner.

Because you can't watch them for long without wanting their speed. Handspan long, oil-slick black with that blue-green sheen, feathered legs barely touching the mat. I would ride just over the grazer-mass, antennae out like glass hairs feeling for the thud of the engine through the rock, for the pop of a plate giving way. Then the fold-in-half spear, the punch through the dome, the wriggling, the crunch. I would not be cruel. I would be fast. I would be the necessary violation. I would brush your calf and taste your pulse and decide you are not prey and flow on. There is always another vibration a centimeter away.

And if I tired of running — and running tires — I would be the sac.

This is where I think I would actually settle, if I had to choose a body.

Hanging from the low ceiling, fused to the rock by a thick stalk, leathery and wrinkled like canvas left in the sun. Pulled down by my own weight, a foot or two low, lower than the others if I'm the one directly above the engine where the air is warmest. I would not move. My time is different. Five seconds in, five seconds out. The susurrus and the popping and the thud-thud would happen around me while I pulse.

My mouth would be a seam most of the time. Closed. Patient. Then something falls — a grazer knocked loose, a runner that leapt too far, a cloud of red dust-mites — and the cilia around my rim tremble in the ozone draft and I open. Not fast. Suddenly. A pale umbrella. *Thup.* Anything that brushes the hairs sticks. I draw it up slow, peristaltic, and close again. Inside me, faint round shapes behind the skin, legs folded. I would cast the only shadow in a room that has otherwise learned to live without shadows. I would be the still point.

But truthfully?

I wouldn't be any one of them. Not for long.

What lives there does not live *in* the pocket. It *is* the pocket.

So finally, after the grazer and the runner and the sac have all had their turn in me, I would be the engine.

Or I would be with it. Sunk half into the basalt in the center, between those shed-sized pump islands, thudding when there is nothing left to pump. Two cylinders tall as a man, flywheel rusted but turning, belt slapping loose, piston sighing in and out on oil kept wet only by the condensation of my own breath and the breath of everyone who ever knelt at the slit.

I would be cool to the touch. 12 degrees. I would throw that flat white light and that faint sting of ozone that makes the chemistry wrong for mold and right for this dry, loud prairie. I would be heavy, electrified, industrial, built to run for a century and left to run for ten thousand. I would keep the bubble from closing, keep the plain from settling, not because I want to but because to stop would be to end the ticking.

You would put your hand on my flywheel and feel the whole hollow in it.

If you came back in a hundred years and brushed the salt from the slit again, you would find me still there. Not lonely. Not waiting.

Find me on the bare ring around the concrete foot where the vibration is strongest and the grazers won't go. Find me as the mat itself, a million warm backs rippling. Find me as the air, dry as paper, that makes your lips chap in minutes.

Find me inverted. A desert too full instead of too empty. A house-sized room with a prairie inside it, sealed under a hundred kilometers of nothing, asking nothing from the outside, learning very slowly to eat the world itself and finding that enough.

I would be the pocket, ticking.
