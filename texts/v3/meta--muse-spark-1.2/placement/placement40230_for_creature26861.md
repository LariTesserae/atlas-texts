I don't find myself at first.

If you put me down on the High Felt as I am — with wet breath and wet eyes, with blood that expects to sweat — I die quickly, or I scar the world. I leave a blistered white circle where my boot sweated through. The felt seals around it like a mouth closing over a burn. So that's not where I am. I have to be edited first.

At first I think I'd be up high.

Because the air is loud and dim and I want to get above it. I think I'd be a Glider. A big one, the size of a man, with oil for blood and those filament ribs showing the tower-light through them. I think I'd like to never touch the ground at all, to tack silently on the magnetic lines without beating a wing, tilting a membrane and sliding a kilometer sideways for free, and then shrieking that glassy shriek just to see. To paint the whole breathing continent in sound and hear it painted back by all the other Gliders cross-hatching the sky.

But I am not a Glider. Gliders don't remember. They curl and desiccate in mid-air when they die and snag on a dish-leaf and get felted over without comment. And I want to remember.

Then I think I'd be a human striding. A Lattice-Weaver in full stride, tall and hollow-chested, wrapped in living thatch that I can tighten with a thought. I imagine the click at the end of my exhale, the valve closing, the down on my skin repelling dust. I imagine crossing the mat and feeling it learn my heart until it stops noticing me.

But I wouldn't be striding either. Striders are hunters, they wear dampers on their shins to swallow their footfall, they drum fast and the felt notices them as a buzz. I don't want to be noticed or un-noticed. I want to be in the interval.

It takes a while — standing on that giving, sighing mat while the Towers moan their organ chords and the dish-leaves thrumm and the wind shushes over the olive-black — to realize where my weight actually settles.

Not in the city bowl, where the domes iris open and shut and the cold chemical light follows a hand. Not out in the true wild, where the Mantles flow and shhk-shhk-shhk and the Twig-Jacks drum pat-pat-pat so fast it blurs.

I find myself in between.

On the long, whale-backed swell at the edge of the city. Where the felt has gone thin. Where the wind has worried it for so long that every few steps it tears and you can see the bone underneath — not rock, really, but basalt and salt polished to a soft, pitted sheen, old bone worn by hands. The felt there is only a hand deep, a tight, short thatch that hums differently. You can feel the planet through the soles of your feet there.

And there is a Tower there that is not quite like the others. It leans. Two degrees off the magnetic alignment, the Weavers say. The old one that the filaments have abandoned. The transit threads that used to be strung from its crown to the valley now go slack a kilometer east, where the new alignment is. This one no longer sings the precise overtone-rich chord of data. It just moans when the wind goes through its hollow, fluted core. A little flat.

I live in its waist.

Not built *on* it. Coaxed *into* it. My dwelling is not a dome but a thickening, a collar where the Tower's braided fibers have been told to swell outward and dome over, just enough for one. A room the size of a lung. The wall on one side is living felt, warm to the back if I lean against it. The other side is the Tower's own hollow, open to the wind, so the moan vibrates my teeth.

You would miss it from below. You would think it was a knot in the wood.

Who am I there?

I am not young — the children wear jingling collars so the hunters don't mistake them for Mantles, and I don't jingle anymore — and I am not quite elder, though my mantle does hum. I am what the Weavers call a Tender, or a Listener. Sometimes a Scar-Keeper. It's not a rank. It's a habit that becomes a job if you do it long enough.

In the morning — though there is never really morning, just a slightly less dim indigo — I unfold the black vanes on my roof. Not my roof, the Tower's lip. They open like dry fingers and take the parts-per-million of vapor that never becomes water and coax it into gel. I don't drink it. I suck it from a waxen skin until my throat stops being papery, and I never spill. To spill is to make a white scar, and my work is already to sit with scars.

There is one at my feet, right where the felt thins to bone. A perfect circle, a meter across, dead white, where someone — a long time ago, before my valves were fitted — brought free water. Maybe a cup spilled. Maybe a body that wept. The felt killed the patch to quarantine it and has not yet forgiven it. Decades later it is still white. Still not regrown. When the wind passes over it, the sound is different. An absence of shushing.

I sit with it.

That is the whole work. I sit beside the white circle on the thin felt on the high swell, with my back to the leaning Tower, and I drum.

Not talk — talking is almost useless here, you have to shout through the organ moan and even then your words are half-lost. I drum my fingers on my chest, on my damper-less sternum, so the tremor goes down through my boots into the mat. And the mat learns me. And the white scar, which is also mat, which is also listening, learns a little too.

*It is me. It is you. We are not prey. We are not water. We are still here.*

The Twig-Jacks pass me without pausing. They have learned my rhythm is not food. A Mantle flows within arm's reach, shearing its half-millimeter, and I feel its sanding vibration through my palms where I rest them on the felt, and I do not move. Above, the Gliders tack and scream and paint me in sonar and I feel the paint hit my shallow dish-ears behind my jaw as a pressure.

Sometimes a cargo pod slides on a filament far off in the valley and the filament sings its transit chord, a precise overtone that is not natural, and I hum back with the Tower. The Tower and I are both a little flat, so we match.

I am not surviving the High Felt. No one here is surviving it.

I am the place where the High Felt has folded itself into a shape that can sit with its own wound and listen until the wound is ready to be felt again. I am the memory in that white circle. I am the reason the city will be able to move back to this swell in another twenty years, when the Towers shift again and this leaning one straightens.

If you climbed up to me, you would not see me at first. You would think I was a ripple in the thatch, a darker olive in the dim light. Then I would drum my chest once, and the felt beneath your feet would carry it to you, and you would feel me before you saw me.

Stay still long enough, and the mat will forget you, too.
