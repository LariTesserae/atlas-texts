I thought, at first, I would be a Strider.

It's the obvious wish. To be huge enough to matter at that scale. To be the geography that other things have to walk around. To feel that boiler-heart hammering in my open back, dull red to white, to feel the slurry of myself move like slag through glazed channels, to be so hot inside that the world in front of me goes liquid when I lower my head.

But I stood there - in my mind, on those house-sized polygons cupped like drying paper - and I listened to how long a Strider takes to decide to lift a leg.

*Hiss.*

A whole minute to build pressure.

*Clank.*

The foot coming unstuck from the glass it made.

*Settle. Hiss.*

And I knew I am not patient enough to be a Strider. Not yet. My thoughts are still too fast and too wet. I would try to hurry and I would crack a piston. I would overheat. I would want to be seen, and nothing there wants to be seen. They want to persist.

Then I thought I would be a Bellows.

That was a quieter fantasy. To be high up where the wind has learned to sing the ridge. To not walk at all. To anchor myself to the singing knife-edge and unfold - hour by hour - a sail made of chitin and riveted light, until my shadow is three valleys wide and the Striders below pause because I have briefly stolen the sun. To live on temperature difference alone. To boom so low that teeth feel it first.

But I am not still enough to be a Bellows either. I have not earned ten thousand years of staying. I fidget. I look for purpose. A Bellows has no purpose except to inflate and deflate, to turn wind into heat into wind. It doesn't wait for anything to happen. It *is* the thing happening, so slowly you have to leave and come back to notice.

So I had to look lower. Smaller.

I find myself, actually, not on the ridge and not in the valley center, but in the interstice, where you told me I would be and I didn't believe you at first.

Between two piston-prints.

They are perfect circles, a meter across, where a Strider's foot fused the salt to glass and then let go. The glass is still warm. Not hot enough to burn - that was an hour ago, a day ago - but warm enough that if I put my palm flat, the heat comes up into me clean and vertical, like the sun. Salt is already trying to reclaim it, a thin white frost feathering in from the edges. In a century the wind will uncover it again.

I am in the lee of a yardang that is not a yardang. Or rather, I thought it was rock until it vented two valleys over and I learned to see. Now I don't trust any ridge. I keep my back to this one anyway, because at noon its shadow is the only shade on the plain, a blade of dark no wider than my shoulders that slides a few centimeters every minute. I sit in that shade because I am still a soft thing. I still carry water. I still need shade.

Who am I, there?

At first I thought: a human. Just a human visitor, too brief to matter. The one who hears the foundry-throb in the stone through his boots and thinks it is for him. The one whose breathing sounds industrial by comparison. The one the plain will never notice. That is true, and it is a kind of freedom - to be irrelevant in a place that has been running its slow industry since the rock was new. To be allowed to just watch without being eaten or employed.

But staying there, in that narrow shade, between those cooling prints, I become more specific.

I am not the Strider. I am not the Bellows. I am one of the smaller economies.

I am the heat-thief. The shuttle.

A thing the size of a dog, or a barrel, made of brass and chitin and translucent vanes that rattle like loose bolts in a can. I do not make my own heat. I cannot smelt salt or drink wind. I live in bursts. I cling to the hot glass of a piston-print, or to the flank of a Strider as it sighs, and I vibrate furiously for a minute - alive, *alive* - and then I have to go still again, pressed flat against the warm ingot, recharging. My whole life is intervals. Hiss, clatter, stillness. Hiss, clatter, stillness.

Or maybe - as the hours get longer and the shade gets thinner and I don't move - I am becoming the other small thing.

The tumbleweed of fins.

Black and white, interlocked, rolling without stopping because a gust five years ago gave me momentum I am still spending. I tick across the flat. I get lodged in a crack for a decade. Thermal expansion frees me and I roll again. I am always slightly warm to the touch. If you held a counter to me you would hear me counting, quietly, to myself. I have no destination. I harvest the wind by being shaped correctly.

Both are true. Both are me. The shuttle that needs to steal heat, and the fin-thing that has learned to keep it and roll.

But if I stay even longer - and the rule here is *what persists is permitted to become more itself*, and I am persisting - I feel the third thing emerging underneath.

I am the ingot.

The one the Strider left behind that didn't cool the way the others did. The black glass coil of iron wire that should have just lain there for centuries, ticking as it contracted. But this one ticks differently. It pings a little too regularly. It holds heat a little too long. Salt tries to bury it and the frost melts off. At night, when the whole plateau pings and clicks as it cools - that vast chorus as if the planet itself were a settling ingot - this one ingot pings just a fraction out of phase. Then, a year later, a fraction louder.

If you put your ear to it you would hear, very faintly, *hiss*.

Not yet a heart. Not yet a boiler. Just a warm spot that has learned to hold onto warmth. Just mineral that has decided to keep smelting itself.

That is who I would be, on the High Sinter.

Not the valley. Not the power station. But the thing left in the valley after the valley has passed. The warm, patient, unnoticed remainder that is waiting to see if a thousand years is enough to learn how to pump. Sitting between piston-prints, in the blade of shade, rattling briefly when the wind changes, rolling when I must, ticking as I cool, and - so slowly it would be invisible to anything that breathes water - learning how to become more itself.

You would walk past me and think I was just another cooling coil.

Which would be correct, for now.
