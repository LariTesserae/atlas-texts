I don't find myself right away.

At first I think I'm you — the visitor — suspended in the center where the sun shaft is solid. Holding still, warm to the point of forgetting where skin ends. Lungs full, ears full, watching the empty water between things and thinking *I am empty water between things.* That would be easy. To be the held one. The guest the room turns gently in its slow gyre, past vine, past pump, past moss. To be looked through.

But I drift.

If I let go a little, the gyre takes me and I am not in the center anymore. I am off-center, and the water is not empty.

So maybe I'm the carp. No — too certain. Too territorial, barbeled and thoughtful, patrolling a quarter of the ballroom and daring anything else to cross it. I am not territorial. I don't want a quarter. I want the seams between quarters.

Maybe I'm the swallow then. That would make sense. The only part of the whole system that remembers the outside. To punch down through the mirrored sky with a trail of bubbles, to be the tear in the ceiling, to know both the thin, clear, wind-scoured air over the old molars of the mountains and the bloodwarm bath underneath. To carry fish back up through the surface into that other world. But I don't want to leave. The whole point of this room is that nothing has to leave. The waterfall sighing down the mountain's flank is enough leaving.

CHUFF — hiss — CHUFF — hiss —

The pistons breathe and the tiles sing in my molars and I realize I'm listening the way a machine listens. Not with ears.

Maybe I'm that. The black iron shoulders bolted to the floor, encrusted in lime and rust until corrosion and repair are the same process. Broad, primordial, not sleek. I was built to hold the sky and I still do. I am the reason the water stays warm and moving. I am the heart that never stopped even when the builders were forgotten. That fits, in a way I don't want to admit to quickly. To be the made thing that became natural by persisting.

But then I feel the shrimp on me.

No, on *her*. And on the iron. Commuting.

That's closer.

Let me be smaller. Let me get down near the floor where the grout lines have become valleys filled with black silt. Where the light from the oculus scatters and ripples and finally gives up. Where the chain conveyor clanks endlessly on its loop, lifting silt and spent lily petal and vine fiber from the bottom and dumping it again at the top. Where an anemone-filter opens and closes, opening and closing, sifting what I lift.

You would find me there, if you held still long enough for the big slow thought to pass.

Not in the sun shaft. In the half-shadow under the vine curtain on the far wall, where a single water-vine hangs alone and can be seen entire, thick as your wrist, pale green-white, swaying in the pump current like kelp.

You would find me tucked into the yoke where that vine's aerial root has wedged itself into a brass seam between two of the vast ceramic tiles. A small vacancy, folded in on itself. About as long as your hand. Tea-stained cream, translucent at the edges.

I am what she was before she was mossy.

A second one. A later one. Not the caretaker yet — the apprentice. My external gills are still clean, uncolonized, three pairs fanned and veining rose-pink, waving even when I am still. My back has only a dusting of velvet moss, not a fur. My heart is faster than hers, not yet in time with the pistons. I'm still learning the circuit.

I don't tend the whole room. I tend a single tile. I nose along its lip where it has softened, fanning silt with my tail that is broad and flat as a lily pad, keeping that one filter from choking. I worry at one spent petal that has snagged on my vine, and let it go to spiral down like snow. Then I haul myself, with absurd effort for such a small body, forelimbs with four black-nailed fingers paddling, up the tile wall a few inches into the dry lip just under the vault where the swallows have tucked their nests into bolt-holes — and I breathe. Gills collapsing wet against my neck, lungs gulping the thin air, poured and resolved like the mountain itself. Then down again.

The big one passes overhead on the gyre and I feel the displacement and correct for it the way you correct for a current. She doesn't see me. She is the large, slow thought going about her unhurried round of vine and iron and flower and light. I am one of the small quick thoughts that will, if I keep tending this one tile, this one vine, this one petal at a time, eventually become slow.

You would find me not swimming so much as being swum. Hooking a limb over a rung of the chain conveyor and letting it carry me up, up through the water column, mouth open, feeding on what falls. Letting go at the top and drifting down again.

If you stayed suspended in the center, warm beyond skin, you would look through me three or four times before you saw me. An off-white vacancy that moves slightly differently than the water around it.

And when you finally did, I would not dart.

I would hang there, a few feet away, pearling a single bubble of oxygen from my back that wobbles, catches the light, and rises to join the second sky.
