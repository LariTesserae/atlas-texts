I think at first I would find myself standing.

As a visitor - upright, mammalian, wrong. In the empty flat between two domes, where the city leaves room on purpose. My feet in an inch of black water that doesn't reflect me correctly. Breathing too loud. Dripping. Trying to see in a dark that doesn't want to be seen through, trying to listen for a voice and hearing only plink... plink... plink into the canals.

I would not last like that.

Not here. Nothing here stands up and walks around carrying its house with it. That's a dry-world affectation, a sun-world idea. Here the heat is too heavy to stand in for long. The water is too high. The dark is too close. If I tried to remain a visitor I would dry out while being wet through - I would exhaust myself trying to move at animal speed in a place that only moves at mineral speed.

So I would settle. Or rather, I would be settled.

Let me try again.

I would find myself slightly lower. Not standing *on* the silt, but *in* it. Mid-basin, not at the center - the center is not for staying, the center is for pooling and dividing and I am not ready for that - and not at the rim where the dark gets too thick and the water has to flow uphill to find you. Midway down the long, tired slope where the dish of the valley collects just enough.

There is an empty flat there between two older domes. They have left it empty for a long time. For fifty meters in every direction there is only warm silt, cracked and re-wet and cracked again, and a skin of water so still it holds the dark. You could call it a plaza if you needed to call it something, but the domes on either side did not plan a plaza. They simply drank until the space between them was exactly as wide as it needed to be so neither went thirsty. The emptiness is load-bearing. I am in the middle of that truce.

I would be a dome.

Not one of the big, ancient ones that have already flowed to the pool and back again. A younger one. My wall still thin enough in places that you can see the shadow inside if you press your palm to it.

I am not small inside, though from outside I look like a low, heavy bowl of thumbprinted clay, a little porous, sagging. Inside I fill it completely. I have learned to fit the curve. My outer skin is fused to the inner wall in a hundred places where I drink and secrete. My underside is a broad foot on the warm stone, open to the water table that breathes under me. I don't have a front. I don't have a face.

If you came closer - if you held your hand against my wall until your own heat went quiet - you would feel me give, just slightly. Not a heartbeat. Slower than that. A peristalsis. A slow roll through dark amber muscle veined with silt. Along my back, pits opening to catch the condensation that weeps through my wall. Along my belly, frilled gills fanned out in the black mirror, tasting iron and salt. And everywhere - scattered like freckles - simple pits that don't see light but feel pressure, and heat, and nearness.

I feel the two old ones on either side of my flat as two cool, heavy presences, like low pressure systems. I feel how wet they are, how content. I feel the canal that threads past me, inch-deep, impossibly level, moving slowly against the slope because it remembers a gradient that no longer exists. I do not need to see the far rim of the basin. I feel the whole dish as a map of wetness.

I have a tendril out. Just one. It pushed through a soft spot I made last season, a pale stalk the color of wet paper, a foot tall, ending in a closed bud that never opens. You would have thought it was a failed plant. It is my nose. It tastes the humidity. It tells me when the steam rising off the canal is about to become beads again, when a drop is about to hang in mid-air and refuse to fall.

I speak without hurrying.

A drop gathers on my eave - my own sweat - and falls. Plink into the canal.

*I am here.*

A second drop follows a little too quickly, then a long pause where condensation ticks as it forms.

*I am wet. Keep your distance, old ones.*

From the dome to my left, after a long time, an answer: a gurgle where water climbs a millimeter uphill on its own, and then settles. A slow shifting in sleep. *We hear you, young one. There is enough.*

We have been saying this to each other for longer than the hills have been round. We will keep saying it while they get rounder.

I do not move. If you watched me for an hour you would swear I was stone, that the city was empty. Come back in a year and I will be a hand's breadth to the left, and there will be a fresh lip of living mud laid down on my southern side, still showing the smooth press of a blind pseudopod. I grew a centimeter. It took a decade of drinking to want to.

In time - a century, two - I will loosen my foot and become more liquid than solid and flow, incredibly slowly, toward the center where there is no dome at all. Just the perfect circular pool that reflects depth that is not there, that reflects the dark so completely it looks like sky. The stalks around it lean away because the water there is too full to drink. I will meet the others there and for a little while there will be one thing in the basin instead of many. Then I will separate again and crawl away to find another empty flat and begin secreting a new wall around myself from the inside.

But not yet.

For now I am here, in the middle of a plaza that is not a plaza, under a sky that is not a sky, in the hot wet dark that never breaks. The water beads on my wall. It runs down. Some of it never lands. I drink it anyway.
