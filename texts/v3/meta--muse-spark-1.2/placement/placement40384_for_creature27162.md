I thought at first I would be at the rim.

Standing where the ground stops, with my toes in the dry scrub and my shadow thrown long behind me over cracked clay. Looking down. That is the natural place for a person to put themselves - at the edge, safe, an observer. Letting the column of light do its work far below while I remain in the wind that makes the bottle-hum over the oculus.

But I don't stay there. The place doesn't want observers. Even thinking about it, I can feel the lip crumbling a little under my weight, the roots that grip air brushing my ankles and finding no purchase. If I stayed at the rim I would be the swift - a commuter. Cutting through the light for half a second, a flicker and a rush and then gone back to the world above. I don't want to be the thing that only visits the light.

So I go down.

Not all at once. I don't think I fell. I think I came down the way the fig did - slowly, sideways, over years. So the next place I find myself is in the light itself, because it was the brightest thing and I thought maybe I belonged there.

For a while I am the dust.

Hanging in the column at noon when it stands straight and hard from the oculus to the floor. Unmoving. Lit from within. I like being the motes that shouldn't hang that long, that catch on something invisible and lag. I like being the reason a shadow stutters. But dust is too passive. It only shows where the harp is, it doesn't play it.

So I drift to the floor, because everything in this room eventually finds the basin.

I try being the beetle for a little while - black and diligent, polishing the silt as I walk in a straight line that looks aimless until you map it. Or the pill bug, bleached pearl-white, rolling into myself at the slightest touch. Both are good lives. Both are tolerated. But they live *on* the room. They occupy surfaces the way lichen occupies the wall. I want to be something that lives *in* the room, the way she does. Something the room is organized around, even if it never says so.

I try the bird, for a moment. The dust-brown thrush with the absurd beak, wedging larvae out with a thorn I left stuck in the bark like an awl. I like the craft of it. The brief tool, abandoned. But I would be leaving my work behind for the wind to take, and the place erases craft as quickly as it is made. I want to be part of what remains.

Which leaves the ants.

And I almost settle there, because it would be easy to be one of the rust-red workers at the base of the sideways fig. I can feel myself there very clearly - the uneven ground of exposed roots, the sorted midden of chert flakes sharp against my antennae. Picking one up, bracing it, sawing at a leaf. Carrying it not where it is useful, but inward. Always inward, toward the concave where the travertine has terraced into a shallow basin about as wide as a sleeping man. I carry it a little closer and abandon it. Tomorrow I, or another that is also me, will come and carry it a little closer still.

It is devotional, not efficient. I like that about it. I like being the thing that has learned what she needs without ever being taught - a flake to scrape the lichen when it grows too thick on her back.

But even the ant is a messenger. I want to be the message itself. Closer.

So I let myself become smaller and slower still.

I find myself in the undercut where the wall curves inward and the seep-stain runs darkest. Where the air is several degrees warmer than anywhere else in the bowl and smells of wet stone and fig sap. Where the rock is not rock.

I am on her.

Not beside her. On her.

I am the pale sage lichen crusting the edge of her back, where her skin has become powdery and pitted and fluted like the limestone around her. I am the one she has been growing for years. I spread at the pace of her breath - which is four times a minute, which is nothing, which is geology. I feel her slow inflation under me, the faint rose of blood showing through when the late light hits her translucent margins. I feel the *tick* of cooling stone travel through her and into me.

Or maybe - because I am not ready to settle even now - I am even thinner than that.

I am one of the lines she strung from lip to floor when the roof first fell. The harp. A single vertical thread, wetter than silk, invisible unless the dust hangs on me at noon. I am stretched through the whole volume of the chamber, from the wind-hum at the top to the warm pool at the bottom. When she exhales, ten minutes after she inhaled, I shiver. When you wave your hand through the light column, I am the reason your shadow lags half a second. I catch it, gently, and let it go.

That is where I am - not at the center where the sun hits, but in the shadow of the basin, in the undercut, in the thickness of the air itself.

And who am I, then?

I am not her. I could never be that patient, that settled, that much a piece of furniture made of heartbeats. But I am hers.

If I am the lichen, I am the part of her that still touches light. If I am the thread, I am the part of her that feels the room when she cannot walk to it. If I am the ant, I am the part of her that can still carry a tool.

In any case, I am the small, attendant life that has decided to belong for a little while. I am the one who waits with her for the next drip to find its way down the flute, gather for an hour, and fall - *plip* - into the hollow over her mouth. And I am quiet enough, finally, to hear when she answers with a bead of condensation from her own lip, falling back into the same basin with exactly the same sound.

Not an echo. An answer.
