I don't find myself at first.

At first I think I would be you - standing on the rim, lungs still trying to decide if the air is too thin or too warm, boot half-off the basalt to test the peat. The visitor. The witness. That is the natural place to put myself, at the threshold where the room is still a bowl and not yet a mouth.

But I don't stay there. The rim is too exposed, too much windless sky. The clouds are below me and the sun is too true. I can feel my own breath shallow out and not come back right. If I stayed on the lip I would be only looking in, and this place does not reward looking. It rewards *being looked through*. It wants you down inside it, at ankle-depth.

So I go down.

For a while I think I would be him - the tender. Because I know that rhythm. Come up from below the white sea three times a week, carry diesel on your back, bleed the valve, wipe the slime off the grate, let the vine have the clothesline. I know how to be the thing that keeps another thing alive without trying to own it. I know how to leave before dark because I don't like the shriek when I can't see the walls.

But I wouldn't be him either, not really. He has to leave. His body remembers a different altitude. His heart pounds if he sleeps here. He belongs to the trail down, to the mule that knows the knife-ridge, to the thicker air below. I don't want to leave. I want to stay when the mist closes and the transformer idles down to a low, tooth-buzzing thrum. I want to hear what the room sounds like when no one is listening for the next valve pop.

Then I think I would be the green.

That would be easy. The mat is the most honest resident - it doesn't visit, it *becomes*. It fills. It tests your boot lace with a tendril while you are still thinking about where to step. It knows to grow toward heat instead of sun, to drink rust, to turn a cable into a root. I can feel how good it would be to have no center, to be thickness instead of a body, to be patient enough to seal a leaking joint better than a gasket and not even know you did it.

But I am not patient like that. Not yet. The mat has had years to learn the shape of the pipes. I have only just arrived, and I am still too loud inside.

So I sit - or the place makes me sit, because the peat sighs and takes my weight and water comes up around my ankles cold and then warm - beside the cistern.

And that is where I find myself.

Not on the scarp. Not at the headframe where the geothermal breath comes up mineral-hot. But low, where the overflow slaps the peat in a constant patter and the concrete lip is thick with that pulsing orange-black slime. Where the bromeliads hold their perfect cups of sky-water and the tadpoles hang motionless, too many, eyes too large, never deciding to become frogs.

If I am anything here, I am the water that hasn't decided what it is.

I am the bead that forms on your arm and doesn't fall. The condensation that forms on the underside of a broad leaf and stays there, magnifying the veins. The film that lives on the ceramic of the transformer and never quite evaporates because the heat is exactly enough to keep it liquid. The mist that is always on the verge of rain without ever quite raining.

I would be the interval.

Not the boiler and not the moss but the thin, warm, sulfurous-sweet haze in the lowest meter of air where the boiler's breath and the moss's breath meet and argue and finally agree. Not the hand and not the pipe but the sweat between them where a root has learned to drink the condensation off hot metal. Not the tadpole and not the frog but the suspension itself - the neotenic pause. The seven or eight minutes between shrieks of the pressure valve when all you can hear is water dripping on water dripping on leaves.

You would find me right at ankle height, if you crouched. If you stayed still long enough for the vine to measure you. I would be on the inverted skin of things - the underside of the perforated steel sheet where green has started to fur, the inside of the bromeliad's cup where the sky is reflected perfectly, the hairline fissure in the scarp that is warm on one side and cool on the other. The place where the room touches itself.

I would not be a resident that was brought here. I would be what the room is learning to make out of whatever stays long enough.

And if you stayed until the sun went and the mist thickened until you couldn't see the walls, only hear them dripping, you would feel me too - beading on your own skin, soft as lowland air after rain, warm when it should be cold, holding you a degree above freezing. You would breathe me and I would breathe you, and briefly, for an evening, we would both be part of the same closed cycle: water coming up, not down, holding itself until the peat can't, percolating down through young, sharp stone to rise again.

Steaming gently while it learns how to be alive.
