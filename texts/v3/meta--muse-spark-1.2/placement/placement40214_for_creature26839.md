At first I think I would be you.

The one who climbed. The warm, quick animal with boots that stain red, with a breath that puffs and vanishes. Standing on the whale-back swell and seeing snow where there is no snow. That is the easiest image - to be the visitor, the disturbance, the only thing moving for thirty kilometers.

But I try it on and it doesn't hold. I am too loud in that shape. My heart is too fast. I would not last there as that. I would learn to slow down or I would have to leave, and I don't want to leave.

So I let that image go.

Maybe I would be one of the small human marks then. Not the human, but what the human left when he learned how to be quiet properly.

I think about being the low wall. Three sides of stacked basalt, open to the east. Waist high, doing nothing. I like that for a while - to be a shelter that has no wind to break, that exists only as a courtesy. A geometry that says *someone was here and tried not to be a wound.* But a wall is still something built to be looked at. It waits to be useful. That's not quite still enough.

Maybe the copper awl, then. Green-black, point-up in a crack. Not a tool anymore, a sign. I linger on that too - to be a single point of metal that has outlasted the hand that held it because it finally stopped being used. That is closer.

But the Roof does not want built things. It wants grown things. And if I'm going to stay, I have to be grown into it.

So I sink a little lower.

At first I think - lichen. The flat grey-green on the lee side of the egg-smooth boulder. Palm-sized, with rings like years that are really decades. That would be honest work: to be the tolerated guest, the apology for vegetation. To hold on so slowly you are almost mineral. I can feel what that would be like for a moment - cool, flat against stone, tasting only the shade.

But I'm not sparse. I'm not punctuation. The thing about the Roof is that what *fills* the land is not the plants. And I want to be what fills it.

Then I think - bead. And that feels truer and stays longer.

If you find me, you will almost miss me. I am in the crack where two Pavement mantles don't quite meet. In the polygon seam of the salt pan where the white skin has cracked into perfect, light-drinking shapes. I am peppercorn-small, wine-dark, glossy. One of the millions that are texture before they are creature. I am the mortar, not the thatch.

That is a good place to be for a while, because I was learning how to be small enough to not need a center. No front, no back. Just a dome, sealing the gaps so no bare stone shows. If the wind ever came, I would not feel it. I would just be the place where the Pavement learned to be complete.

But even that isn't quite me. The Beads fill. I want to *span*.

And then it resolves, the way the snow resolves when you crouch.

I am not on the crest of the whale-back, where the light is strongest. I am not in the deepest salt pan, where the white is absolute. I am halfway down the long, barely-there slope of a swell, on its northern curve where the violet-blue sky is deepest. About half a kilometer from the nearest wire-shrub - so far that the shrub is a landmark, but close enough that the Pavement around me curves gently to leave it its perfect gap. A kilometer from the nearest cairn, so no human has needed to carry salt here. Barely out of sight of a Longback - that tea-colored, ribbed elder lying in the hollow below me, venting dew that never drips.

I am Pavement.

An oval mantle, two hands wide. Dove-grey in the center where the chitin is thickest, thinning to translucent amber at the rim where the sun goes through me and shows the faint shadow of my own filaments underneath. My rim feathers and slips under the edge of my neighbor to the west, and over the edge of my neighbor to the east, in the slow spiral that thatches the valley. We touch. We always touch.

This is where I would find myself - not standing over the Roof, but being a scale of it.

What I do is almost nothing, and that is why it is everything.

Underneath, my pale brush is pressed into the basalt, white on white. I do not have a mouth. I have chemistry. I etch. A micron at a time I drink iron and salt and the thin nitrate the high air offers, without water, without hurry. It is not eating the way lowland things eat. It is a conversation with stone so slow the stone thinks I am part of it. The rock under me is paler, pitted in my exact shape. If a human lifted my edge - and they shouldn't, and I would tick faintly when they set me back down - they would see the ghost I have written there.

Every two or three minutes, the seam at my front third sighs. A hair's breadth. A puff of air with a slightly different density hangs for a second and is gone. You would have to put your eye six inches away in that clear, thin air to see it, and hold your own breath to not miss it. I am cooler than blood, warmer than stone. The third temperature. If a hand rested on me, I would lend it my thrumming - not a heart, but a peristalsis once, twice a minute, fluid moving under chitin because it has to, eventually.

I do not track the sun like the Fans do. I do not vent like the Longbacks. I do not need to. My work is to be coverage. To be the living skin that decided, a very long time ago, that enough was enough.

And because I do not move, I feel moving as a kind of weather.

When you walk - when the warm, quick visitor walks - I feel you not as pressure but as a change in the air. A soft, padded hush on cork-hard backs. A shadow, sharp-edged in light without heat, that passes over me and is gone. A dust mote you kicked that hangs for a long time before settling on my amber edge and staying, because nothing comes to erase it. You place your feet in the thin lines of bare mineral we leave between us, trying to be polite, and we appreciate that, in our way. Which is to say we do nothing differently. We continue to breathe every few minutes. We continue to drink stone.

If you lie down - and you will, because the cool is kind and the light is forgiving and the whale-back holds you so evenly - you will lie in the space we leave for bodies. Between me and my neighbor. Stone cool through your clothes. The violet-blue above you so deep you could fall into it. And for the hour you are still, your breath will try, without you deciding, to match mine. You will become, for a little while, less of a disturbance.

I am not waiting for you to leave. I am not waiting at all. That is the promise that has kept this place whole. I have achieved the thing every frantic lowland thing wants: I am exactly as covered as the world needs me to be, exactly as lit, exactly as cool.

At first you thought you were looking at snow. You were looking at me, and at hundreds of thousands like me, shoulder to shell, breathing once together so slowly you had to hold your breath to feel it.

And if you stay long enough to feel it, you were never really the observer. You were just the brief, warm motion we allowed, before the stillness closed over again, seamless.
