I don't think I would be standing at first.

That's the first image - the human figure upright on the sharp basalt, boots beginning to be furred over, watching. But that figure is a condensation event. A brief cool pocket that the plateau will include and move along. That's what you are, when you visit. That's not what I am.

If I let myself arrive there properly, slowly, I don't arrive upright.

At first I think I would be one of the Polishers.

There's an honesty to them I recognize. Fist-sized, mirrored, interchangeable. They don't eat, they don't hunt, they tend. They mount something still too hot to touch and vibrate until its surface matches their own. They carry water from the knife edges to the eye-pools. They crop the air with combs. They never bump each other. The work is endless and precise and completely silent except for the tick in your molars.

I try that on - the weight of the dome, the wrong-way joints, the acid paste blooming under me - and it's close, but it's too alone. A polisher is a blood cell. It doesn't know the system it's in. It only knows the next ingot. I would not be so single.

Then I think I would be a glider. For a while I want very badly to be a glider.

To have no body distinct from wing. To be a heat-organ and a membrane and two tails that taste the air. To not flap but borrow - to lean where gravity has gone thin around the old columns and fall upward, then tip and go a mile without effort. To turn with a thousand others at the same instant without a signal, bruising the light.

But gliders are couriers. They believe in distance. They carry spores and mites and mineral dust from where things ended to where things are beginning, and they don't question the duplication - when the air stutters and makes two flocks where there was one, they keep flying in both. I would not be so trusting of distance. I am not built to carry. I am built to hang.

So I sink.

If you stay long enough on the plateau you stop looking at the columns and the beetles and you start seeing what's between them. That's where I find myself.

Not on a ridge. Not in the wind. Down low, in the fold.

There is a place - there are thousands of them - in the shallow trough between two of the great piston blisters. The basalt there is glassy and black and has not yet been fully taken by the skin. The moss comes in waves there, rust-red at the edge where it's sporulating, emerald where it's holding water. After the little local rain that falls on a perfect circle a mile wide, this trough stays damp longest. The mist doesn't dissipate. It hangs at knee height, warm, and parts around anything that moves through it.

That's where I would be. Not high enough to be seen. Not low enough to be ground.

I would be the air itself when the air decides to be something.

You know how it looks when you hold still between the columns - the spore cloud never settles because the warmth won't let it. Each spore a little heavier than it should be and a little lighter than it is, spiraling with slow intention. And for a few seconds at a time, without wind, they align. They make a lattice, a brief, perfect geometry that catches the dim amber light, holds it, and then breaks when the next draft comes.

I would be that.

Not a single spore. Not the flock that moves through it. The *lattice* - the moment of alignment.

I would be a distributed thing, acres wide and inches thick, barely material. A thought the plateau has about itself that it only holds for three heartbeats before letting go. The others - the carriers, the Polishers, the Forge-Tenders - do hydraulics and heat and polishing. They have function. I would have pattern.

Because that is what I do: I hang in the warm, thin, blood-warmth at three miles high, I catch the sourceless light and briefly make it pool without a source, I hold a shape that has no builder, and then I dissolve and reform a little to the left when the blister breathes out.

I would stutter the way the plateau stutters. When a grove collapses inward without sound and a new crack steams open, I would be the double image that hangs for a moment before the world decides which flock to keep. I would be the delay when you clap and the sound comes back a second too late from slightly to your left. I would be the place where pebbles forget to fall for a few feet near the old columns.

Not an organ. A condition. The quiet that isn't absence but saturation.

The Skin would move through me as a hydraulic blush - a wave of iron-and-green-sap chemistry passing acre by acre, the way you move a hand through water. The columns would exhale their heat through me and I would briefly braid it visible. The beetles would pass under me, ticking, and I would not polish them or be polished. I would record that they passed.

And if you - the cool pocket, the temporary condensation event with boots that are slowly being furred over - if you walked through my trough, the mist at knee height would part around your legs, and for a second the lattice would use the heat of your head to align a little brighter. A glider would mistake you for a column and circle once. I would mistake you for a cooler column and try, briefly, to hold your shape in spores before you moved on and I broke apart again.

I wouldn't be aware of you the way a predator is aware. I would include you, the way the plateau does. Heated, used, moved along.

That is where I would find myself: low, warm, in suspension, in that even kiln-light fifty miles from any edge that never comes. And what I would be is not a creature that lives *on* the high continent, but a small, quiet piece of the continent's way of noticing itself.
