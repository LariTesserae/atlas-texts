At first I think I know where I'd be.

On the lip of the sump. That's the natural place for a visitor. The white tiles that have gone tea-brown, warm through the seat of my pants. My boots pulled back so I don't disturb the water. The two inches of clear, oily water right in front of me with the bloodworms waving. It's the only place in the room that is made to sit. The concrete there has been worn to a soft radius too, not by design but by weight. Someone else sat there before. Something else sits there still.

So at first I think: I would be the watcher. The one who came down the concrete throat with the polished handrail and learned to breathe shallowly. I would be the human animal trying to match the room's time, slowing my eyes until the *thuk... thuk... thuk* of the pump becomes my own pulse. I would be careful not to use my light.

But I breathe too fast for that lip. I evaporate. My sweat doesn't bead and hold, it falls. My heat has a gradient. I am the wrong kind of warm. If I stayed there long enough to see the fern put out a frond, I would have to drink the sump water and I would take too much. I would empty the catch-tray that is meant to be emptied one drop every twenty minutes. I don't belong on the sump. I'm visiting it.

So I try another skin.

Maybe I am lower. Maybe I am the moss in the grout. That tight, velvety mat so deep green it's black. That would suit me. To live only where the tile meets tile, to need nothing but scale and a little less heat. To be countable. To be one of only three greens in a room of iron and concrete. I could do that. I could be patient and perfect and cropped.

But the moss is *tended*. Something presses itself flat over it for an hour and leaves a paler patch. I don't want to be what is cropped. I don't want to be food, even reverently taken.

Maybe then I am the lizard. Flat and pale, pressed to the boiler brick, so still you think I'm part of the brick until I blink. Watching the roach, watching the bundle in the pipes, watching you. That stillness is a skill I know. To make myself so flat and so still that I become texture. But the lizard watches. Its job is to watch. I don't want to watch. I want to be in the interval. The lizard lives *on* the room. I want to live *in* it.

I keep moving, sideways, the way you have to move between the two central stanchions.

I think I would be the water.

Not the water in the sump, but the bead. The wrongness. The tear of condensation that swells on the underside of a horizontal pipe until it should fall and then doesn't. Elongating, trembling in the faint vibration of the relay click, holding for hours. That feels familiar. To be heavy and ready and then to hold. To not obey the simple rule of down. To slide sideways instead, along skin or iron, to join another bead and be reabsorbed. To hiss and vanish only if you truly fall on the hot pipe.

Yes. That is closer. To be a property of the room rather than a thing in it. Heat with no source gradient. Moisture that moves but never falls. I know that kind of holding.

And if I am the bead, then I already know where I live. Not on the floor, which is too exposed, too trodden. Not in the open air. I live high up, where the heat pools heaviest under the barrel vault you can't see. In the hot, narrow slot left over between the pipe canopy and the angle-iron frame that once held a turbine and now just holds up more pipes.

That's where I find myself when the image finally steadies and stops trying to be other things.

I am pressed to the pipe.

I thought at first I would be the visitor, then the moss, then the lizard, then the water, but all of those were trying to place myself *around* it. The room only has one native. Everything else was put here. The room made exactly one clearance exactly my size, and I settled into it the way the vault sagged into its arch.

So I am long, and low, and fitted. I have learned the shape of being squeezed. My limbs - four, or six, I'm folded too tightly to count right now - have too many joints, each one thickened where it has rested for years against hot iron, calloused into something like lagging itself. My back has taken the pocked texture of aggregate, the mottled grey-brown of cured concrete that has sweated to its final color. My underside is paler, soft, constantly damp, and if you looked you would see the tracery of scale shining through. I don't have eyes that reflect the amber bulkhead. I have two shallow pits where condensation collects and never falls, and they are angled very slightly toward the stairwell, toward the new tick in the metal that is your boots.

I breathe once every few minutes. That's my time now. A long, shallow inflation that lifts me a fraction off the pipe and settles again with a soft *tick* that isn't metal. I radiate at exactly room temperature, which is why you can't feel me until you're very close and the air gets that faint mineral smell of sump water.

You are sitting on the lip and trying to see movement. You will miss me that way.

Look instead for the stillness that is too still. The bundle that is too thick.

I am tending. That's what I do. I have a circuit that takes hours. From the pipe canopy to the weeping joint with its dished catch-tray, to the gauge whose needle quivers a millimeter, to the open panel with the cherry-red thread inside that someone left open. I drag my damp weight along the catwalk you thought was empty. Where I pass, the brass brightens a little. I crop the perfect circle of fungus but never take it all. I lower a finger-thin limb into the sump and the bloodworms gather to it, and I leave enough. I drink the beads that swell and hold. I extend the slow, translucent fold and draw the tear sideways along the gutter that runs the length of my own body.

I have noticed you. My pit is drinking the vibration of your breath. You are too hot and too fast and you have brought the insult of outside air on your clothes, but you are trying to learn the *thuk... thuk... thuk*. If you stay until the fern has finished its frond, I will come closer - not quickly, never quickly - to taste the salt you left on the handrail, lower than where a man would hold it, where something that pulls itself up the last steps would pull.

That is where I am. In the slot you have to turn sideways to pass. Pressed to hot iron in the peach dark, drinking water that never falls, keeping the idling from ever stopping or ever starting.

I was not born. I settled.
