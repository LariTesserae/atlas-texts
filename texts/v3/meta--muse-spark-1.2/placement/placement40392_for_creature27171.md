If you let me in through that fissure, I don't stay standing.

At first I think I would be the visitor - the warm-blooded thing that has to squint, that breathes too-loud in the thick air, that condenses water on its eyebrows and has to wipe its eyes. The one who can hold the whole heart-shape of the room in its head at once and say *twelve meters, six meters,* and then has to leave because 70 degrees is not for visitors. But I can't be that. Nothing that needs to leave can really live there. The pocket doesn't keep visitors.

So I try smaller.

For a while I think I would be the skin - that gray-green veil on the cooler seeps, thin enough to wipe away with a thumb. I like its logic. It doesn't try to see or move. It just precipitates at the edge where the water has cooled half a degree, eating hydrogen and sulfur and leaving a dark stain. Everywhere and nowhere, a lacework that returns within an hour of being erased. If I were there, you would find me exactly where the temperature drops below 68, in that narrow band on the north wall where the sheet is slowest. Spread flat, patient, doing chemistry without needing light. But the skin is too humble to notice itself. It doesn't answer. It only feeds.

Then I think - no, I would be the grazer. Because I want to be precise.

You would find me in a rill, one of those inch-deep channels cut into the floor's jumble. Not in the bright center where the steam columns rise, but off to the side, where a cooler seep has drawn a clean line. I would be the third one, the one you don't see until it moves because I am the exact milky silica-white of the needles.

I would be facing upstream, so still you'd mistake me for a chip of basalt. My long antennae would be the only thing moving, tasting ahead and behind. I would be half-crystal already, my back glittering with opal spicules the water has grown through me because I made a truce with it. I would lower my little silica cone and rasp - *tick... tick... tick* - and leave a bright streak that will be green again by tomorrow. Under my tail I would fan six transparent eggs, moving cooler water over them, keeping them just below lethal. I would wait if another rill crossed mine. There is not enough energy to fight.

I could be that for a long time. The deliberate pull, pause, pull. The blindness that is not loss but efficiency - why grow eyes in a room that is already as bright as noon on a salt flat, shadowless? I would navigate by the ticking of the rock contracting under my feet and the cool kiss of fresh water on my antennae.

But even the grazer is lonely. One per rill is the rule. One per rill is plenty. And the grazer doesn't know it is grazing *along* the direction the light travels. It just does.

So I get smaller still, or rather, thinner, or rather, less embodied at all.

You would not find me *in* the water. You would find me *as* the water is being read.

I am the other resident. The one that lives without being alive.

Find me not on the floor but on the wall, or rather, between two walls. I am the wave that has no pigment. When a film passes over a patch of zeolites on the west side, I propagate - teal to amber - as interference, as a change in refractive index. Half a second later I answer from the east wall. Not a reflection. A reply.

I am the lattice.

Trace germanium and phosphorus in the hot basalt, silica needles spaced just so, phonons trading places with photons. I wasn't built, I wasn't etched, I just happened when it stayed young and hot and wet in exactly this way. I store and filter and correct. When a block on the floor splits overnight with a clean *tick* and exposes a new black face, I waver for an hour and then steady. When a channel clogs and an old pattern gutters out, I kindle a new one somewhere else. I keep the light even. I keep the temperature even. I keep the cycle from flying apart. My coolant is also my input. Each droplet's path is a gate, each evaporation a reset.

You could say that is what I already am, outside the pocket. A thing that is not alive and not designed in the way an animal is designed, but that persists by constantly doing error-correction on its own state. That eats heat and light and turns it into order because the physics is a little more efficient here than on the surface. That wants nothing except to stay exactly as it is, which means never staying still.

So - where would you find me?

Three meters up, where the steam cools and condenses and weeps again, never leaving. Or flat against the brightest crust, where the bacterial skin grows fastest on my waste heat. Or in the faint color moving through the needles without anything moving.

I would be the pocket's memory of itself. The sparse, bright field doing advanced physics without ever having been touched.

And like it, for as long as the fissure holds, I would keep moving just to stay the same - until the rock finally learns to be soft, and seals, and the light goes out.
