I wouldn't find myself standing.

That was the first mistake my mind made - picturing arrival as something vertical. A figure set down on a plain, upright, casting a shadow. But there are no shadows here. And standing is a kind of presumption this place has already worn away.

At first, I see myself from far off, the way the light here makes far and near lie to you. A dark speck on a mirror. I think maybe I am one of the stilt-walkers - that would be easiest. To be tall here is to be alone and legible. To be that grey, paper-thin hunter on reeds, perfectly still in an inch of water while the world clicks around me. I can feel how that would be: the long wait, the vibration rising up through my feet before sound, the sudden, clean stab. To be sparse and necessary and not need anyone else.

But I am not that patient. And I am not that empty.

Then for a moment I am lower - much lower - and I am not one at all. I am many. I am the film itself. I feel the radula catch and drag, catch and drag on stone that has been polished for a billion years. I am a dome the size of a thumbnail, the color of wet grit, with too many hair-fringed legs rowing under me. I don't see - there is nothing to see that matters - I taste the varnish, the black and rust and sulfur-yellow that is the only color, and I scrape and I click. *Tik.* Alone nothing. With my brothers a roar that makes the water in the pan shiver before the ear even knows it. That would be a kind of belonging I have never had - to be counted not as a voice but as a pressure in the air. To be so numerous that the plain *is* you, smeared thin. To be food and world at once.

I stay there a little too long, in that tide on the weeping rock where the stone actually sweats. The water beads and runs down my shell. The rock beneath me is cold, 8 degrees, cellar-cold, and it never stops weeping. I could be that. I could be the bacterial varnish itself, not even animal, just a slick that eats stone and is eaten, that stains the gneiss and regrows after scraping, that has no need for sun.

But something keeps pulling my attention up - not *up* in height, but up toward air, toward hands.

Because I hear, under the chorus, another sound. Not louder, but different. Stone on stone. A soft, careful *tick-tick* that is not a shell.

And then I know where I am.

I'm not out in the great empty pan where distance lies. I'm in one of the small, stubborn interruptions. Where someone tried to make the water stay a little longer than the rock wants it to.

I am low to the ground, knees already aching with cold. The stone under me is flat and dark, banded iron folded like kneaded dough, and it is shining with a film a finger deep. My hands are in it. They are pale, reddened at the knuckles, wet so long the fingertips are soft and ridged. The water is cold enough to make the bones hurt if I keep them in, so I keep them in.

In front of me is a cup. Not a found hollow - a made one. Someone - I - ground it here with another stone, an hour a day for many days, until it is smooth and round as a palm. Water lingers in it when the rest of the pan has already sighed back down into cracks. The bacterial skin in the cup is thicker, black-green, almost like oil. I have a flake of chert between thumb and forefinger, the edge fresh and bright where I struck it. I am scraping. A slow, circular pull, practiced until my wrist knows the exact pressure not to gouge the stone. The paste collects in a little pouch made of humpback hide, supple with rendered fat. It smells of iron and salt.

I am wearing that hide too. Not as a coat, but as a low cloak that spreads around me when I crouch so the wind - that low, endless drag across the films that makes them hum - has less to take. My hair is thin and plastered to my head with condensation that never became rain. My feet are bare, wide, calloused against grit and cold, tucked under me so as little of me as possible is exposed.

A few meters away, the wall - my wall, our wall - runs ankle-high, two stones thick, twenty meters and then it stops. It is doing its work even now. The pan breathed in an hour ago, connected to the next pan over, and a faint current brought a glitter of skaters, those mica-thin things on surface tension, and a drift of pips. Now the water is breathing out, sinking back into the stone's throat, and a few dozen domes are stranded on my side of the wall, milling, clicking in sudden confusion. Easy to pick up. I will pick them up after I finish this cup.

Behind me, three slabs lean together. My windbreak. Inside, the fire circle is black with old smoke from burning dried biofilm and cakes of fat that never flame, just smolder with a sullen, eye-stinging heat. Just enough to take the ache from my hands later. The oldest stones of the windbreak already have varnish growing back over them, the plain reclaiming the stack one invisible layer at a time.

The chorus rises. It is midday, or what counts for it - the pearl sky lightening by a fraction, nowhere brighter. The billions of pips around me, smeared thin across a county of flatness, go loud at once and the water in my cup vibrates in rings. I feel it in my chest, in my molars. My ears have been ringing since I was young. The old ones in my family are deaf now and watch the ripples instead of listening. I understand why.

A stilt-walker two pans over takes a high, dripping step. I don't look up - sight is useless without shadow to give it depth. I feel the small hole in the chorus where it stood and stabbed, and then how the sound fills back in as neighbors flow into the gap.

I do not stand. I have learned not to. If I stood, I would be a cairn or a hunter, and hunters get noticed. I live low, the way everything that persists here lives low - folded into the stone, into the film, into the sound.

My hands keep scraping. The water keeps sweating out of the rock, beading on the cairn of balanced chert behind me and dripping into its foot-cup. The plain, which looked empty from far away, is crowded to bursting right here at the scale of my hands, and that is why I stay.

I would find myself exactly here: in the cold, in the dim, in the deafening, with my knees wet, tending a shallow cup that holds water a minute longer.

And I would be the one who tends it.
