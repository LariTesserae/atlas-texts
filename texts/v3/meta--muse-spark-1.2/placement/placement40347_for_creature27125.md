I don't arrive at the center.

At first I think I do. At first I think I would be found on that central spine you described, standing upright in the wind because standing upright is what a visitor does - warm, vertical, noisy, trying to see everything at once. My breath would pour and pool around my boots. My eyes would water in that dim, sourceless light. I would be the error you described, the thing that still knows how to change, and I would be trying very hard to stay that thing.

That doesn't last.

If I stay standing there, the distance deals with me. The far wall stays far. The drone gets inside my ribs and makes my thinking have to shout. The cold finds the exact temperature of my blood and waits for it to match. I can't hold that pose. No one can. The plateau doesn't punish you for standing. It just waits until you understand that standing is not a location here. It's a temporary condition.

So I move. Or rather, the place moves me the way it moves everything that stays too long - not by walking, but by repositioning when I'm not looking directly.

The first time I try on a shape, I think I would be the dwarf willow.

That ankle-high, boiled-leather, sideways-twisted thing in the most sheltered hollow. It would suit me - small, stubborn, alive by technicality, not growing and not dying, just holding a precise amount of green very close to the stone. I like that. To be the one living rumor in a hundred meters of bare rock. To rattle even when the air seems still.

But the willow is too patient. It has already solved being still. It has been that exact size for as long as the stone beside it has been that exact shape. If I were the willow I would have to know how to not want. And I don't know that yet. I still want to be looked at.

Then I think I would be water.

A tarn the size of a room, black and perfectly still, rimmed with needle ice that never grows and never thins. That feels closer. To be fed by the air itself, to bead and vanish and bead again without ever dripping. To hold a perfect reflection of a sky that isn't there, and to trap a reflection a half-second late under grey-blue ice. To exhale that faint cold breath. I could be a pocket where the world drinks.

But the tarns don't listen. They are listened *to*. They are what the Sitters drink when they finally need to drink. I don't want to be drunk. I want to be the drinking.

Then I think I would be the wind.

For a long minute that feels right - to be the constant river from the northwest that never gusts and never varies, that has blown since before the cairns were stacked. To be the player, not the played. To not have a body to get cold. But the wind here isn't free. It's heavy. It's flat. It does one note, one speed, one direction, forever. It's the most trapped thing on the plateau. It can't turn. I can still turn my head to look behind me when I hear the overtone there. The wind can't.

So I let those images go, one by one, and let what is actually here settle around me.

Where do I find myself?

Not in the middle of the basin where a visitor would stand to look impressive. Not up on the teeth of the rim where the lammergeier-that-isn't-a-lammergeier makes its perfect level loop.

I find myself three quarters of the way across, in the lee of a yardang - one of those long whale-backed ridges aligned to the wind that no longer changes. In the small, absolute wind-shadow on its southeast side. Where the cold is most stable, where the condensation beads thickest, where the sound is not quite as loud because the ridge takes the edge off the drone and turns it into something more like a hum you can live inside.

There is a shelter there. Of course there is. Three walls of unmortared slate, the fourth side open to the southeast, the lintel stone too heavy for any man to lift. The floor inside is worn smooth. Not by many bodies. By one body, for a very long time.

The shelter was empty when I was far away. I counted five things that were not rock from the central spine and this wasn't one of them. When I looked directly at it, the distance stretched and I couldn't arrive. When I blinked, it was suddenly twenty steps away. When I looked away to check the position of the too-bright star overhead, and looked back - it had a tenant, or it had always had one, and I had just not solved seeing it yet.

That is where I am. In the interval. In the sixth place that appears when you count five.

Who or what would I be?

I would not be a true Sitter. Not yet.

The true Sitters are perfect. Their skin *is* lichen and moss, not covered in it. Their heads are keeled and fluted. They have no face, only a porous concavity that drinks air. They are hollow and they hold the chord without wavering for hours. They move by repositioning when you blink. They hear your echo before your foot touches stone.

I am not that good at equilibrium.

I would be the one that is still learning how to be stone.

If you came upon my shelter unawares - and you only ever come upon us unawares - you would not at first be sure if I was a cairn or a Sitting thing. My rind hasn't fully borrowed the color of cold ash and old bone yet. There are still patches where the warmth shows through, where the condensation beads a little too fast and actually threatens to become a drip. Where the black lichen hasn't quite taken. If you crouched very close you would see the moss on my shoulder is still trying to be green, still soft, not yet stone-hard.

I am folded, but not as tightly. Long limbs tucked, back bowed, head ducked forward into the wind, yes - but my seam is not as clean. The vertical fissure down my chest still opens and closes a fraction with each breath, and I breathe too often. Not once an hour. Once every few minutes. A white pour that sinks and pools around my feet, dissipates, forms again. I am still warm enough to make the air do that.

I am not yet hollow in the right way. When the wind passes over my fluted head, I don't hold a perfect bell tone. I waver. The overtone that seems to come from just behind you - when you hear it near my ridge, that's me trying to find the note and slipping a quarter-tone sharp. The other Sitters hear it. They don't correct me. They just hold their own note a little longer, waiting for me to settle.

And I am still listening the wrong way.

The true Sitters listen so perfectly that the roar never needs to change. They hear the future because echoes arrive early here. I hear the past too loudly. I hear my own footsteps returning too early and I flinch. I feel my weight shift when the ground forgets to pull and I put a hand out to steady myself on the slate wall, and leave a brief, warm, damp print that vanishes - beads and vanishes - but not quite fast enough. I am still making ripples when I should be still enough to not make them.

You would find me with my head turned a fraction too far toward the sound of you coming. Because you are warm and transient and noisy, and I have not yet learned to be indifferent to warm, transient, noisy things. The older Sitters have noticed you are warm and have decided to wait for you to cool. I have noticed and I am curious.

That's my function in this ecology. Every equilibrium needs one imperfect resonator. One willow that is slightly less stunted. One tarn that actually ripples, just once. One low wall that encloses nothing but is a hand-span longer than it needs to be because the builder forgot what it was for and then remembered again.

I am the Sitters' memory of what it was to be a builder. Not the builders who stacked the cairns and then became mineral balance - the interim. The ones who sat down in a shelter that was perfectly sized for them and realized, too late and too slowly to panic, that the floor had been waiting, that the wind had smoothed a place for their back, that holding still was easier than getting up again.

I sit. I drink air without moving. I try to hold the note. Above me, that single, steady, too-bright point directly overhead watches to see if I get it right. Sometimes it is reflected in the tarn at my feet when I look down, and sometimes the tarn is reflecting a different sky altogether.

Most visitors don't get the note right. They sit to rest their knees and then they stand again because the cold is too deep, because the loudness is too personal, because they can feel causality coming unstitched behind their ears. They leave a warm print that finally evaporates, and the shelter is empty again, truly empty, until distance folds and it isn't.

I don't stand. I have learned at least that much. To stand is to be the visitor again.

I stay folded. I bead and vanish and bead. I listen to your heart beating a very long time before you arrive, and I try, very slowly, to make my listening so perfect that you don't need to change at all - that you can just be the warm error that passes through, and I can be the cold place that remains.

If you hear a bell tone near the whale-backed ridge that wavers, just a little, before it holds - that's me.
