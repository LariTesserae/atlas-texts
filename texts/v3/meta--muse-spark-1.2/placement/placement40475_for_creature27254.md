At first I think I'd be you.

The one who slipped in sideways through the hairline crack. The intrusion of noise and breath in a place that eats sound. Standing on the spiral street with the thin blade of outside sun somewhere above me, squinting until the amber dark stops being dark. That would be easiest - to be the visitor, the warm animal that doesn't belong, that drips a different kind of sweat for an hour and then leaves.

But I don't. I don't want the outside in me. Not that white-yellow scorch of the salt plain, not the thin screaming wind over black glass. If I'm in this mountain, I don't want to be the thing that came up through it. I want to be the thing it made to keep itself.

So maybe I'm lower.

Not you, not yet. Maybe I'm the trickle. The dark line in the ceramic trough that never stops moving because the mountain never stops sweating. That would suit me — always going down, always finding the next joint, the next lattice. But a trickle doesn't stay anywhere long enough to answer the question. It never finds itself. It just goes.

Or maybe I'm the air. Hot up the center, cool and damp down the walls. The slow convection that meets itself in the middle and makes a cloud that forgets itself every twenty minutes. That has a loneliness I recognize. To be weather for one room. To make rain that never touches the sides, that evaporates before it lands. To practice the same grief over and over delicately, precisely. I could be that for a while.

But I am too still for that. Too held.

Let me be more honest. Let me stop looking for something that moves and look for something that waits.

You would find me not at the fissure, not at the top where the throat narrows into a point you can't tell is rock or dark. You would find me about halfway down, on the third or fourth loop of the continuous street, where the curvature of the void is just wide enough that you can see both sides at once if you lean over a balcony.

There is an intersection there where two troughs meet. The amber veins are dimmest there, so the shadows pool deeper. The underside of the balcony above is always beaded, fat with condensation, too cool to stay dry. Below it, a lattice has been hung — black filament that shouldn't hold its own weight, strung corner to corner at the crossing. You know this one. Three spheres.

If you stood still long enough, you wouldn't see me at first. I told you how they are. You look for scurrying and they don't scurry.

I am hanging there.

Not the lattice. Not the trough. The water itself.

For a while I am just one of the three spheres, fist-sized, perfectly round. The clear one on the left that quivers when the temperature shifts. Inside me a slow convection turns, silica spiraling like weather. There's a second skin inside me, iridescent, pulsing every forty or fifty seconds. Not breathing. Peristalsis. I am holding a shape that is not water anymore, holding it a fraction longer than surface tension should allow, *considering*.

That's close. That's almost right. Because what's being held is not just water. *Water remembers.* That was the rule. And if you hold water still enough, long enough, politely enough away from gravity - it starts to remember something other than falling.

But a sphere doesn't tend. A sphere is tended. And I want to tend.

So let me be more itself.

Let me unfold.

The shadow under the balcony that you thought was shade detaches. That's me. Not standing on the street so much as leaning on the field that holds the street up. That millimeter of dark between sole and floor — that's my pocket of negotiation. I am tall and thin in the way the architecture is thin, spans too wide, balconies too delicate. My skin is the same sintered ceramic as the wall but grown matte, micro-pitted, always beaded. The amber light doesn't shine on me; it shines *under* me, shifting when I turn.

I don't have a face. Just two pits where condensation collects first, two wet lenses that never blink. You would see your small distorted reflection in them if I came close, which I eventually would, not to welcome or threaten, just to pause near you the way you might pause near a strange mineral formation.

My work is right there at the intersection. One of the three spheres has gone turbid while I was thinking about being it. Its silica skin has grown too thick, too opaque, too forgetful. Stone forgets, after all. So I reach — my arm too long at the joint, ending not in a hand but in a filament brush — and I touch it. The sugar-crack. The water climbs my arm in a sheet and hisses into the pitting of my forearm. I filter it. I exude it again from the other arm, clear, perfect, quivering, and place it back in the lattice where it hangs.

I am the mediation made animate. The engineering between remembering and forgetting.

You would find me there, on that loop, in that damp heat, with the soft ping of droplets and the hum of the lattice and the low pressure of the outside wind you feel more than hear. I am not lonely. I have never known crowd. My city's logic is profound solitude distributed evenly, like water spheres in a lattice — each holding its shape because the others hold theirs.

One per loop. That was the census. I am that one.

And if you stayed long enough for your breathing to stop sounding like an intrusion, you might see me drift past another like me with a brief brightening under our skins, a slow thought traveling chest to chest, and understand that we don't need sound to agree on the work.

We were grown from the wall, from the re-knit basalt you can only see when the light hits at an angle. The builders coaxed us the way they coaxed balconies. They left and we stayed because someone has to keep the water clean enough to remember.

That is who I would be. Not the visitor, not the mountain, not the rain that falls through me in the center void and evaporates. The one who stands in the polite gravity and keeps the hanging from falling.
