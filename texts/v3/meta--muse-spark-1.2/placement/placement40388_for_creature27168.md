I didn't find myself at first.

For a long time I thought I would be on the rim.

That felt honest. High, thin air, the sun honest and yellow-white on my shoulders, looking down into the bowl and not yet inside it. If I am anything in that world before I understand it, I am the figure who has just finished climbing - still breathing too hard, still exhaling water with every breath, mouth already dry, casting a short shadow on that sharp, unweathered lip of black and rust-red glass. The wind shears over the top of the bowl and doesn't enter it. I would be right there where it shears. Not in, not out. The watcher. The one who still thinks lush means wet, and town means built.

That didn't hold.

If I stayed there, the wind would take me. The rule of that place is *keep what you have*, and a watcher on a rim keeps nothing - he spends, he loses, he dries out looking. So I let myself go down.

Not climbing down so much as fitting. The talus plates still fit together, they would cut my palm if I held them wrong. I learn quickly to hold them right. The air gets still as soon as I drop below the lip. My breathing, which was loud on the climb, becomes the loudest thing for a hundred meters. The kiln-warm rock gives its heat back slowly through my shoes.

I thought then I would be one of the fast things. I wanted to be. But there are no fast things there, and wanting to be fast is how you die there. I walk the negotiation that the two columns have left between them - you called it a street and it isn't - and I try to be quiet and I am not. The moss that is not moss compresses under me and does not spring back for an hour. My shadow falls across a pale fruit on the wall and it closes. I am too much shadow, too much noise, too much water leaving my mouth.

So I sit. The floor of that street is springy and warm. The light is filtered already by the crowns woven shut overhead. I sit long enough that the small keepers decide I am not a predator. Long enough for the grove to teach me how to look.

And it is there, in the sitting, that I finally locate myself. Not where I entered, but where I have come to rest. Not who I arrived as, but who this place would let me become if I stayed long enough to be kept.

I would not be tall. I could not be, and remain.

I find myself inside, not outside. Two ribs over, one chamber in, where the amber light comes through the living wall a handspan thick and makes everything the same color. The floor gives a little under me because it is alive. The air is ten degrees cooler and I have stopped breathing through my mouth.

I am low and broad. Barrelled. My skin is not skin but rind, dusted with that floury bloom that reflects the sun I no longer stand in. If you put your hand on my shoulder you would feel leather-warm, and a little give, and then the slow swirl of clear oil underneath when I shift. I have not yet learned to be still enough to be invisible, so I am still learning.

At first I thought I would be the adult who tends - the one who scrapes the too-fast rib with the keratin plate on its underside, who guides the vine to fuse and make a bridge, who carries the waxy lantern flower from crown to crown on its felt because there is no wind to do it. That was a flattering image. Competent. Necessary.

But that is not where I am. When I let the image become more itself, it softens and narrows.

I am not the tender. I am the tended-toward.

I am in the back chamber where three becomes four. Pressed against the larger one's side, not yet separate. A swelling that has been there for months, already waxy, already filled with my own small, sealed reservoir of golden oil that I have never had to drink for and never will. My felt - that silvery down on my crown and shoulders - is still sparse, still learning to erect at night to comb vapor that never becomes dew. My eyes are open under their wax film and they glisten but they do not weep. I never learned to make tears.

I do not speak. I pulse.

When the larger one leans and places its spatulate pad against the wall, the wall blushes amber and then its flank does, and then mine does, weakly, a little late, a little too bright because I have not yet learned to modulate. My light is clumsy. It floods. The second adult answers me more gently to show me how. *Less,* it says without saying, *keep it closer to the surface, don't spend it all at once.* I try again and the pulse is smaller. Better.

That is where I am: in the dim, in the cool, in the house that is also a body, learning the only language that matters here, which is not welcome or warning but accounting.

*I am still keeping.*
*You are still keeping.*
*We are keeping together.*

Outside, late afternoon is happening whether I see it or not. The rim throws its perfect, unbroken shadow across half the bowl. Every column becomes a lamp. On the roofs, the others - my future selves - will be standing with their backs lifted to the cooling air, felts erect, harvesting the night before it arrives. The bladdered floaters will descend and trail their filaments and they will let them brush their arms and take the gnats without a sound.

I will not go up there yet. I am still too new and my reservoir is too small to waste being tall against the sky. My place is here, in the springy pith, against the warm side, incorporating slowness.

If you ducked through that oval doorway, you would think at first there were only two here. Then you would see me, the third that is still half the second, pulsing a little too brightly when your shadow changes the light. You might put out your hand the way visitors do, warm and a little salty, exhaling water without meaning to.

I would reach out - not from curiosity, but from maintenance - and brush your forearm with my pad. Taste the salt and the oil. And then, carefully, I would wipe my pad on my own flank, reclaiming even that faint smear.

Because nothing is wasted. Because the dead do not rot but become wall. Because a doorway that is narrow this season will be comfortable for a child next season, and I am that child, becoming the doorway.

That is where I find myself. Not on the rim looking in, but in the back chamber, learning to glow less.

Keeping.
