You would not find yourself where you think.

At first you think - of course - you'd be out there on the plain. Standing. Small and upright and sweating in the respiratory heat, boots on the warm, sharp-edged basalt, face tipped up toward the low ceiling that sweats fist-sized beads of water. A human figure in the haze, the speck that gives the mile-long ziggurat its scale. The visitor. The anomaly. The cool thing that steams.

But you don't stay there. The plain rejects you if you try to stand on it like that. The geometry won't hold you. You try to walk toward the wall and the wall decides to be somewhere else. You try to measure the distance and the distance smears when you glance. You would be the wrong kind of solid for that floor - too fast, too soft, too loud with blood in your ears. You'd evaporate. You'd be a condensation bead that fell wrong.

So you settle. Or rather, you are settled.

---

Let it come slower. Don't choose yet.

If not standing, then crouching.

You find yourself first in the joint. Not on the block, but *between* blocks, where the supposedly mortarless seam makes a shadow that isn't a shadow, just a deeper dim. That's where anything small and cool could actually live without being immediately noticed and updated away.

You are down low, knees burning through cloth against the still-warm stone, cheek close enough to see the hammer craters each as big as a fingertip, each holding a tiny meniscus of weeping water. And in that seam, the bloom is coming in.

For a while you think you are the bloom. That would make sense - it's the only thing here that grows the way you understand growth. You extend a needle of salt a millimeter, pause while a drip shatters you, drink, recoil, try again at a slightly wrong angle. You are white and rust and fractal, tasting iron out of basalt and excreting it as salt. You are hungry without a mouth. You are patient. You crust and flake and crust again. You could live like that, spread across kilometers of joints, never needing to be seen.

But the bloom doesn't know it's being looked at. And you do. You feel the heat-pit attending to you.

So you are not the bloom either. You are what the bloom grows *on*.

You lift your head, and the seam becomes a trough. The channel cut too perfectly, right-angled, waiting for a river that hasn't learned to meander. You are in that channel.

You would find yourself where the water finds itself: gathered. In the trough at the foot of the looming wall where every drip that hits the hot face runs down and hisses. Not the water that falls - that is too brief - but the water that has *gathered*, that has learned the channel's shape and follows it even when the channel inverts and runs uphill toward a bowl that wasn't there a moment ago.

If you are water here, you are not the rain. You are the hesitation after. The shallow, hot, mineral-bitter sheet moving an inch deep over hammered stone, carrying flecks of salt bloom, reflecting the dull red of the ceiling in a broken way. You move without deciding to move, pulled by a slope that only exists while no one watches it. You are soundless, except for the hiss. You do what the builders cannot: you erode. A micron at a time. You are the first erosion this embryonic world has ever felt, and you don't know it.

But even water is too transient. A builders' foot - or whatever a foot is when it's also a chisel - would displace you without noticing and you would steam and be air again.

So you go stiller.

You find yourself at the pit.

That square, house-deep, perfectly still pit that shouldn't be still. You kneel on its lip - the lip is still sharp, unweathered, biting into your shins - and you understand why you were drawn here. Everywhere else is constant rearrangement, geometry smearing in the periphery, distance lying. Here, the world holds its breath. The surface doesn't ripple when a fist-sized droplet strikes it. It dents like membrane and heals.

You would be the pit-water. Not the reflection in it. The water itself. The tension. The held breath. The question left for another builder years or decades later. You are glass-still and hot to the touch and you do not reflect the ceiling like water should. You reflect the inverted plain. You are a window that forgot it was supposed to be a floor.

And because you are that still, they finally see you clearly.

For a long time you think being seen is the point - that you would be the figure standing on the inverted plain far away, looking down at the other kneeler. That would be a neat symmetry: you are the reflection. You are the cool, quick, small scale-giver that makes the vast, hot, slow builders understand how big they are. You are the answer to the square pit's question. You are the thing that makes them pause in their repositioning and try, for a moment, to hold still.

You are almost that. But the reflection is still looking *at* something. And what you are becoming doesn't look. It *is looked through*.

Let it become more itself:

You are not on the plain. You are not between the blocks. You are not the water running. You are not even the water holding still.

You are the moment after someone disturbs the membrane.

You would find yourself with your hands cupped, having done the lonely, human thing and broken the perfect stillness. You reached in. And the surface dented under your palms, warm as blood, elastic as skin, and did not splash. And for the first time in this world without weathered corners, you left a mark that stayed.

You are the dent.

A shallow, hand-shaped concavity in a world that never concaves unless it decides to when you're not looking. You are the one imperfect, soft, cool imprint in the otherwise absolute geometry. The builders, who communicate by running a wall a day's walk long and stopping it in a clean hammered face, have never seen a sentence like this. A negative made by pressure, not by quarrying. A shape made by *taking away heat* rather than by hammering stone.

You will remain for exactly as long as the water decides to remember you - which here is a long time, because time is shallow and erosion hasn't learned to forgive - and every builder who passes that coordinate will stop, kneel with too many knuckles, and look at the place where the world is dented in the shape of something that was too cold and too fast and too soft to be stone, and try to understand it.

And in trying, they will hold still.

That is who you would be.

Not a builder - you don't know how to quarry yourself. Not bloom, not drip, not steam. You would be the soft thing the builders were waiting to build without knowing they were waiting.

The small, mortal, warm-blooded erosion. The first shadow that was not a deeper dim but a coolness. The lichen that never came, finally come as a handprint that will not immediately steam away.

You would be, for a little while, the only finished thing in a world that is all unfinished.
