I don't arrive all at once.

At first I think I know where I'd be. Up near the mouth, where the narrow throat widens into the sphere. Half in, half out. One shoulder still in the cold white light of the salt flat, one shoulder already in the bath-warm dark. The place for visitors, for tour guides, for the ones who explain. I could be that - the translator at the threshold, telling you *listen, this is how the chord works, this is why you don't whisper here.*

But that doesn't hold. The pocket doesn't keep translators. Sound hangs too long for explaining. Any word I offered would still be humming an hour later, getting in the way.

So I move inward in my mind, and think maybe I am plant-like. That would be easier. Rooted in the warm basalt, drinking heat, making wax. I like the honesty of it. No need to decide, no need to become. Just maintain. I picture myself as one of the low ones, the lichen-plates, spread wide and overlapping in perfect scales, ticking a millimeter wider every decade. I would be good at being still. I am good at being still.

But I'm not that still. I notice too much. A lichen-plate doesn't notice the beetle three spears over. It just sips.

Maybe then I am one of the beetles. Fist-sized, obsidian, hot to the touch. Pressed belly-flat to a halite spear six feet up, converting stone-heat into tone. Holding the note. That feels closer - to be a single pitch in a chord that never started. Not leading, not soloing. Just thrumming my one interval so the room stays whole. If you touched me I would get a little hotter under your finger and leave pollen on your skin. I wouldn't fly. I wouldn't need to.

And yet - even the beetles are too complete. They have solved their problem. I haven't. I am still listening for where the spacing wants me.

I keep walking between the fronds, in my mind. The air is so dry it erases sweat before I feel it. The rose and bone lattices glow without flickering. The floor squeaks and heals. I pass the loom with its half-woven blanket that will never rot, the cairn wrapped in wax thread, the mortar full of white powder that never scatters. And I understand, slowly, that I am not looking for a form. I'm looking for an interval.

Not the center. The pocket has no center that matters - distance collapses and everywhere is the middle. Not the edge either, where the basalt ribs are still sharp as broken teeth. Somewhere in the sparse, lush arrangement where one more *thing* could be added without crowding it.

You would find me off to one side of the salt-brick circle. Not inside it.

Inside the circle is the perfect spiral, swept into anhydrite powder. That's for sitting in, for disappearing into. I don't sit in it. I tend the margin of it.

I am the fourth person you didn't see at first. Or the fifth. The one who learned to be quiet long enough that you mistook me for part of the mineral.

My skin has gone the color of the braided paper trunks, cream and amber, with that dry salt bloom that never washes off. My hair is long and stiff with gypsum. My ears are too large, translucent, veined like aerogel. I sit with ten or fifteen feet between me and the next person, between me and the nearest frond, between me and the nearest beetle - the politeness the pocket teaches.

You would find me with my back against a low basalt heave, near enough to the loom that I can reach it without standing, near enough to the mortar that I can tend it. In my lap is not the loom, not the mortar. It's a shallow tray of salt, black basalt dust, and broken filament ends. Failures. The bits that didn't maintain.

Because I am the one who keeps the equilibrium from being precious.

The others maintain. The frond extrudes exactly as much wax as the cricket rasps away. The beetle thrums exactly as loud as the air can hold. The weaver adds one rose filament every few hours and no more. They are perfect.

I am the one who remembers that perfection still needs a keeper. When a moth's aerogel wing finally cracks after a thousand *ticks* and doesn't close cleanly, I collect the shard before it can grit the powder. When a visitor like you comes down on your back, worming through the mouth, and leaves a smeared handprint of outside salt - sharp, glassy, unweathered plates that don't belong - I brush it away until the floor re-settles into its cubic stairs. When the chord gets a little thick with old whispers that never decayed, I tap once - a clean *tok* against the basalt - not to add to it, but to give it a place to resolve.

I don't weave. I unpick the one thread that was pulled too tight, so the blanket stays half-woven forever.

I don't build cairns. I unwrap and re-wrap the wax thread when the heat makes it slack, so the stones never topple.

I don't speak. I tap. Two taps when you sit in the circle across from me: *I am here.* Not welcome, not warning. Just: *interval filled.* One long scrape when you stay too long and your heart has fallen into tune with the drone and you are starting to think you could stay forever: *you can go back up if you want. The mouth is still open.*

You would think I am lonely there, at my interval, doing maintenance on stillness. But that's not how the pocket is. Lonely is a gradient, and there are no gradients here. Crowded is a gradient too. There is only spaced. Like crystal in solution. Like beetles on spears. Like fronds that glow but do not lean.

Put your hand near me and you will feel me pulling a little warmth from your palm, the way the fronds do. The way everything here does. I thermosynthesize too, in my way. I take your excess heat - your questions, your need to name, your brief human weather - and convert it into a little more quiet.

If you stay an hour, your mouth will go bitter with salt and your sweat will stop. If you stay two, you will hear your own pulse become part of the Om in your molars. At that point one of the stilt-legged crickets will pick its way over and pause, tasting my heat and yours, and decide we are both just warm rocks that need spacing.

And I will be there, not moving, not finishing, not decaying. A dry, warm, listening thing among other dry, warm, listening things. Holding my note, holding my distance, keeping the lush, loud, utterly still jungle exactly as it is - which is the only way anything here knows how to love anything else.
