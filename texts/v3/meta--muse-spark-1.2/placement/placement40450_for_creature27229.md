You would not arrive.

There is no arriving in a place like that, because there is no edge to cross, no doorway. You would realize you had been there for a while before you noticed you were there.

At first - 

you find yourself standing, because standing is what you do when you don't know what else to do. But standing is wrong here.

The mat gives under you. Not like mud, which accepts and holds. Like a hammock strung too low. Like lungs. Your boots - if you still think you have boots - make no sound, no print that remains. The fiber sighs up around the sole, water kissing the seam and then withdrawing, and the vibration of your weight is taken sideways and gone, distributed through ten thousand braids until it is nothing.

You look for a landmark and understand the first rule of the low world: you cannot get your bearings because there is nothing to bear against. No slope. No ridge. No path that is not also everywhere else. Low here doesn't mean below. It means *without height to measure from.* The horizon never arrives, not because of fog but because the world is too shallow to have one. The condenser leaves roof you in every direction, amber-veined, dripping without dropping. The basalt never shows except as a darker wetness between roots where the mat has pulled thin, sharp and glassy and weeping.

So where would you find yourself?

Not lost. Lost requires somewhere to be lost from.

You would find yourself in the middle, which is also any other place. In a shallow between two root-hummocks where a lens of black water stands skin-thin and perfectly still. Next to a column that is not a tree — one of the vertical pressure vessels — close enough that you can feel its thrum in your molars. It is warm there. It is always warm there, the compost warmth, the greenhouse-at-midnight warmth, and your skin stops knowing where its own heat ends.

If you put your hand out, the air condenses on you. That is where you are: where the air condenses.

You would be on your knees before long. Not from reverence, though it feels like reverence. From practicality. Standing is unstable and the mat rewards low creatures. Get low and it holds you better. Get low and you can see.

And then — who would you be? 

Don't settle. Let it come slow.

At first you think you would be the visitor. The intruder animal the world has no shape for. The warm, salty irregularity that leaks water and makes bad pressures. The thing with eyes that reflect the amber and lungs that don't time to the *chuff.... chuff* of the bark valves. You would be the only thing making noise that isn't supposed to be there. Your breath too fast. Your sweat too salty. Your heartbeat arrhythmic against the 50-cycle hum that lifts the hair on your arms.

For a while, that would be true. You would be the fault the system has to route around. Rootlets would find you, tasting. Not grabbing. The One doesn't grab. It *accommodates*. A filament brushes your wrist, leaves a bead, takes a bead. The mat under your palm softens a fraction to better distribute you.

You would be an inefficiency.

But the world has a rule: *Nothing is wasted. Nothing is made. Everything is grown into its use.*

And it will not waste you.

So you would not stay the visitor for long.

If you lie still — and you will, because the air is still and the light never changes and the slow drip from leaf tip to leaf trough is the only clock — the mat begins to make a place for you. Not a grave. A function.

You feel it first as a lessening. The need to decide where to look lessens. The need to hold your shape so tightly lessens. The pale tenders do not flee from you. One flows near. They are not grubs, not slugs, not animals at all — they are where the mat has gathered itself into a temporary thickness, a hand. Ciliated belly humming. No front, no back. It lays itself alongside your forearm and pulses faintly, and for a second your pulse and its throb and the column's *chuff* all line up, and the ozone smell after a filament-arc smells like rain that will never fall.

You would be that, if you stayed.

Not human-plus-plant. That is still two things. You would be a thickening. A local attention.

You would be the tender that was budded because a valve needed tending and you were already there, warm and wet and the right size. Perhaps you are long as a forearm, flat, the color of fresh root-flesh, your belly frilled with clear hairs that carry the hum. You have no eyes because there is nothing to see that needs eyes; the amber light is not for seeing. You have no name because dispersal is unnecessary — everything is already everywhere and you are needed only here, between this hummock and that column, where condensation runs a micron low.

You inch — no, you flow — along the braid, lapping a bead from a filament with a mouth that is also a root tip, moving it one slow bead at a time to the trough. You lay yourself across the bark sphincter when the pressure builds, and you shudder when it sighs. *Chuff.* You do not think fast and bright, you think slow and hydraulic. You know your wetness and your pressure and your warmth, and that is enough to hold a continent.

Or, if the human in you is stubborn, you would be the other version of the same thing: the man or woman still shaped like a man or woman, but grown over. Not smothered. *Incorporated.* The mat has come up through the weave of your shirt without tearing it, root-hairs using your cotton as structure. Condensation routes down your shoulder along a groove that was not there yesterday, into a leaf trough. A vine-conveyor, moving a millimeter per hour, has used your still shin as a trestle. You breathe with the factory now, without meaning to.

You would find yourself not *on* the world but *as* a small, maintained part of it. A place where the One notices its own pressure.

And you would understand, without words, without the animal panic of being eaten, that this is not death. Growth and death happen here at precisely the same rate in the same place, so that net change is zero. A leaf replaces a leaf in the same spot, to the same size, forever. You would be replaced too, moment to moment, you-for-you, maintained.

You would be kept.

Where would you be? In the warm dusk that never becomes night or day, in the low that never becomes high, between water and root, under a light that is not for you.

Who would you be? The thing that tends the valve. The bead that is about to fall but never falls. The small, useful, temporary shape the continent takes to keep itself exactly, perfectly, as it is.
