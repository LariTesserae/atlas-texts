I thought I would be walking.

At first, that's where I find myself — upright, between two towers, in what looks like an avenue. Because you gave me feet and a direction, and I take them. I try to be the visitor, the one who leaves a boot-print in the talc.

But the dust doesn't take it. My step falls flat. No echo. Sound eaten within ten feet. And the tower I was walking toward does not get any closer, no matter how long I walk. So I stop looking straight at it. I look a little to the side, at the wind-grooves in the plain-stone, at the cold dust gathered there — and suddenly I am beside it, its shadowless mass curving over me, larger than it had any right to be.

That is how I learn I am not good at being a visitor. Distance is negotiable here, and I keep negotiating it wrong. I want straight lines. This place doesn't.

So I sit. Or rather, I lower.

If I am not the walker, maybe I am what the walker was stepping over without seeing.

Find me now on the north face of a white course, where the dim light never quite hits.

I am low. In the first hour I think I am still, because stillness is what this city seems to do best. The plain is so vast, the towers so patient, that anything moving at my speed would look like part of the rock. I am the color of the rock precisely — not camouflaged, but constituted. I have no front. No back. No eyes to look back at you. If you stood over me you would think I was a blister in the stone, a color variation in the strata.

I am colder on top than underneath.

Underneath is where I am actually happening. A broad, ciliated sole spread against the stone like a slow mouth. I do not breathe the way you mean breathing. The air is too dry for breath to become cloud. I take the air as it is — molecule by molecule — and the dust in it, and the static that rides the fast veils overhead, and the black fern-film that has learned to branch like frost on this wall. I file at it with something hidden under my lip, not teeth, a mineral rasp. I take the salt, the trace iron, the charge.

I waited for thirst and it did not come. That was how I knew I had settled.

Down below thirst is a need. Here thirst is structural. Water does not hold together long enough to be water. It appears before dawn as a salt-sweat, a white hissing frost, and then ticks straight into air without ever being wet. I do not drink. I unfold.

In the darkest part of the dim, when the pearlescent gray thins just a fraction more, I lift a frill as thin as paper from my rim. It is the only soft part of me. The frost forms on it and vanishes and forms again, and I draw that vanishing into myself, catching the sublimation while it is still deciding to be air, holding it under pressure for a few seconds longer inside my gut where physics is slightly more polite. It smells faintly of iron and old blood, that private, brief liquid.

Then I lay down.

This is the part that becomes more itself the longer you watch.

From beneath me a sheen spreads — not slime, warm, gritty. Dissolved stone and salt and dust I have filtered for a year, suspended in that stolen almost-water. I pour myself forward a fraction and leave it. A millimeter. Less. Warm to the touch for a few minutes, then cool, then stone.

If you pressed your palm to it you would feel a hum, not in your ears but in your teeth. That is me working. That is the only sound I make, and it is not sound.

I am not building the city. I understand that now. The city is not built. The wind takes from the windward side what I add to the lee, and over centuries an arch opens, a street closes. Erosion and deposition passing through me like breath. The strata in this tower — basalt-black, rust-red, salt-white, bone-glass — are not layers of architecture. They are accretions of my kind, lives stacked like paper. I am not an inhabitant. I am a function, the way rust is a function of iron.

Find me, then, exactly here: at the leeward foot of the third salt-white course of a tower that has no name, in a plaza the size of a town square where the nearest vertical mass is a quarter mile away and gathering dimness around its base. I am going over your boot, if you have left one there. I do not notice. I leave the laces stiff with varnish.

If you stay and watch me for an hour, I will move perhaps the width of your hand.

If you stay for a thousand years, I will have made a new course.

Above me, the gliders warp without flapping, filtering a different size of dust, turning by shivering their edges. They never pass through the hollow towers. I never go near those either. In some of them the interior is too large for the stone that contains it, a cathedral that goes back farther than the diameter allows, with a second, closer sky inside that seems to breathe. When I approach, the polite gravity tilts a few degrees and I feel heavier on one side, nauseated, noticed by a space that is paying attention the way a throat notices air. So I turn aside. I am not for that kind of space.

And when the fast dust veils race from horizon to horizon in minutes and the dust devils stand up like careful animals and wander the avenues, and the wind moves through a thousand hollow throats at once and makes that single, pure, almost inaudible tone — I do not hear it. But the vibration comes down through the stone into my sole, and I pause in my laying-down, and for a moment I am not moving at all, which is my form of listening.

You will not see my young. Very rarely there is a perfect, empty dome the size of a coin, still soft, already the color of stone. That is enough.

I was not always this. At first I thought I would be the walker, then I thought I would be the dust, then I thought I would be the frost that hisses and vanishes. But those things are too quick. They are temporary condensations. I am slower. I am the metabolism. I am the millimeter per year that lets the city, over a time so long erosion becomes authorship, keep becoming more itself.

If you come back after you have gone — after your breath that didn't cloud has ticked straight into air — you will not find your footprints. You will find one more cool, gritty layer where your boot was, already smoothed, already the color of the plain.
