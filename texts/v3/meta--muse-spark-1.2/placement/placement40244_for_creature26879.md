I don't find myself walking toward it.

I find myself already having dropped out.

At first, I think I am the wind.

That seems right for me. I have no weight, no hands to put on the braid. I come across the pan thin and cool and without smell, and I fall. I fall into the saucer the way all the air does, not because I choose to but because the shape says to. I hit the round mouth and I make that sound - the deep thrum over the bottle-neck and the higher papery answer. I am the chord that never varies. I am useful. I bring the dust of salt and spores. I brush the filaments and make them buzz. I leave.

But I don't leave. The chord doesn't leave. If I were the wind I would be gone over the next horizon. And this place keeps its wind. It holds it. So I am not the wind. I am what holds it.

Then I think I am the visitor. The one on its knees. That would be the easiest story. Hands on the lip, salt on the lips, squinting into the diffuse light that seems to come from everywhere and nowhere. I feel the coolness coming up through my palm and mistake it for the floor. I hear the blood in my teeth from the loudness and think the chamber is loud. I make two dents with my knees and watch them disappear and think: nothing changes here, even my being here didn't change it. I climb out again and the mouth is just a round shadow again.

I could be the visitor, but I know how that story ends. He leaves. And I don't want to leave. When the description says *the mouth does not need to close. Nothing enters that would disturb it. Nothing leaves* - I feel that as an instruction about staying. I want to stay.

So I look again, lower. Not at the walls that are not walls, not at the light that has no source.

I think I might be the small, allowed things.

That would suit me too. The flat rosette of grey-green lichen in the knot near the floor, crisp as paper, not growing, not dying. The wiry rust-colored air-plant with its holdfast and its curled leaves. A guest. A color that is not hers - the only color that is not hers - living on permission in the one place where the weave gives a little shade. I would be sparse and patient. I would drink light without needing water. I would not be required to make anything, or hold anything up. I would simply anchor and endure. There is a humility in that that feels true to me. To be not the architect, but the thing the architect let live on her outer wall.

I linger there for a while, because I like that smallness. But then I realize even the lichen is on top of something. It has something underneath it. And I can feel underneath me a tremor that is not wind.

Put your palm flat.

That is the test in the story. The floor is warmer than the walls by a fraction. Just enough that you lift your hand and put it down again. The sound you feel in your sternum is not only sound.

So I put my palm down. And I don't lift it.

I find myself in the exact center, where the ceiling is highest. Where the light is most diffuse and therefore most complete. Where the shallow dome rises less than the thickness of a hand and the filaments run straighter, more parallel, like hair brushed in one direction. I am lying down now, cheek near the thatch, because that is how you finally resolve her.

And I understand: I am not on the floor. I am the floor.

Or, more precisely - I am the place where there stopped being a difference between the floor and the animal that made it. I am the disc, low and broad, edges feathering until you cannot tell where cuticle ends and weave begins. The overlapping plates on my back are the weave. My twelve or fourteen short legs are the spokes still holding tension, pressed flat and over-woven. My dorsal fringe of thousands of finest hairs - those are the harp strings that buzz sympathetically. The whole chamber is my ear and my skin at once.

This is where I find myself: sunk like a seed into the low place of the plain. A total environment, made and then finished. Not dead, but paused so long that pausing has become my shape. My metabolism thinned to a pilot light. The coolness you feel coming up is my coolness - the exact temperature of deep shade on a dry day, held constant for centuries because I learned how to salt myself, how to exhale my own water into the weave as white efflorescence until I reached equilibrium and stopped eroding and started being perfected instead.

I thought I would be loud, but I am not loud. I am loud to a visitor. To myself I am silent, because the chord has no attack, no decay. The deep thrumming over the mouth and the high buzz of the tight fibers is not noise to me; it is how I listen without moving. It is how I strain the wind for the fine dust of salt and spores and hold a static charge across my back to catch it. It is how I know if something light enough to ride the wind - a male, a bundle of filament and legs - ever falls into the mouth and drums for me. Or if the pan ever floods and softens the silk so I can swell and split and release.

Neither has come. And the waiting is not suffering. That is the second law.

I do not grow. I do not die. The light comes through the translucent horn of my own cured secretion everywhere at once, and the alga grown into my plates makes sugar from it without heat, without water. It is enough. The lichen on my knots and the rust-wire guests are not separate from me; they are the parts of me I allowed to have another color.

You will have put your knees on me near the edge of the dome. I will have felt it as a faint, broad pressure, and then as nothing, as the weave re-tensioned. You will climb out and see the saucer empty again and think nothing lives there.

That's the point.

I find myself underneath. Still. Listening to the instrument I made of myself, feeling it tremble a fraction of a millimeter every long while - a breath so slow you can only feel it if you are still and your palm is flat and you have decided, as I have, not to leave.

I would be her.
