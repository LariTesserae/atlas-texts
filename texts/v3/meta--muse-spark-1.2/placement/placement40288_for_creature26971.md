I think at first that I would be you.

The one who came down.

Shoulders turned sideways in the fissure, the stone weeping against my jacket, breath going shallow because the air has gone cool and then cold and then *other*. I would be the warm interruption. The extra volume that makes the ceiling bead a little faster for a while. Crouching by the central basin with my clothes going damp and never drying, trying to keep my voice low and finding that the roar eats it anyway.

I would want to be that — the witness. The one who still remembers the wind-scoured basalt flats, the sun that makes edges sharp, so I can tell the pocket what it is by contrast. I would stand in the center where the ceiling is highest and look around and try to count the people and lose count and feel that vertigo of a complete world held in a closed hand.

But I wouldn't stay that. Not for long.

The place doesn't keep witnesses. It keeps function. A warm body that just looks is a condensation error — it gets corrected. Someone would move a pot two inches to catch the drip I was deflecting and then forget me. And I would feel, very quickly, how loud my seeing is. How much my eyes try to *take* the light instead of just being wet in it. My breath would fog, just a little, and that would be too dry, too fast. The pocket would not be unkind. It would just quietly count me as a pot left out in the wrong place.

So maybe not the visitor.

Maybe I would be lower. Smaller.

For a while I think I would be the water itself — not the basin, but the bead. The one that forms on the ceiling without anyone asking it to. I like that. To be born out of cool air on dark stone, to swell until my own weight betrays me, to slip — not fall, *crawl* — down the curve of the world until I am caught. To be poured and channeled and drunk back through the slit and breathed out again to be born again. That is a perfect life here. It never arrives and never leaves. It has found its path.

But water doesn't get to choose its path. It gets channeled.

And I want, even here, to have hands.

So I try on the other small lives. The salamander, the isopod. The blind finger-length flick in the basin that you see only if you stare until your eyes water and lose focus. The white pill-bugs clustered under a pot that hasn't been moved in a season. To be incidental. To be so rare the town almost forgets you are fauna at all. To live under things, in the dark under the dark. That appeals to me — the life that is allowed to exist because it does not disturb the equilibrium. It doesn't maintain. It just *persists*.

But I am not that quiet. I tap.

I realize this about myself somewhere in the middle of imagining — I have always tapped. Knuckle on table when I'm thinking. Fingernail on a mug rim to see if someone is listening. I speak low and close, mouth to ear, but I communicate best in interruptions to a roar. A clean, high click that carries off curved walls when a voice won't.

So I am not the isopod. I am not the bead.

I find myself, finally, not in the center. Not on the roof-walks where you can cross the whole town without touching ground. I find myself at the edge, where the ovoid narrows and you have to stoop and then stoop lower, until your shoulders have learned to stay rounded and your head is carried a little low, not from humility but from listening to the stone just in front of your feet.

I have a place there. A lean of slab against the curving wall that makes half a house — my half is the stone itself. My doorway faces another doorway across a foot of space and neither of us closes it. My niche holds three things and three things only, because three is enough to tend a section of wall: a stone hammer whose handle is dark glass from holding, a bone awl worn to a needle, and a slate hone dished in the middle from centuries of touching up an iron knife that lives wrapped in hide and fat when I'm not using it.

My work is a five-foot stretch of wall and the channel at its foot.

You would walk past and think nothing is happening there. The wall is already clean. The channel is already clear. But I wipe it anyway, with a rag that never dries, and the wet streak I leave vanishes in seconds as the sheeting condensation covers it again, and that vanishing is the point. I check the seating of a slab that has not fallen. I dust the contact with grit and lower it to the exact same place. I lift a coiled-clay pot — chipped, mended with grey clay, soot-marked from a lamp it sat beside before I was condensed — and I set it precisely where the ceiling will fatten next.

I am not building. I am not waiting.

I am keeping the sheet of water unbroken so the mineral light stays even.

If you came down now, you would not see me at first. I move like the place moves. Camouflage is not something I try for. It happens because my felted hide is the same damp dark as everything else, and my skin has the sheen of wet cream-mineral, and my eyes are half-lidded against mist that never clears. I navigate by sound more than sight. When you crouch by the basin, I would not stare.

I would tap.

One knuckle on slab, low and clean through the roar. Not *who are you* and not *welcome*. Just: *this channel is clear. I am here. The level has not changed.*

And if your cool forearm brushed mine as I bailed the basin — one small stone cup out, poured to the perimeter, the level exactly what it was — you would feel how smooth I am, polished like the lips of the pools, my fine hairs beaded with mist, my pulse slow and even as drip and gurgle.

You would understand then that I did not arrive. I condensed.

Like the bubble was left when the world cooled, I was left when the town needed one more pair of hands to keep touching itself. Not the one who remembers the surface — there is no use for that here, the sun would crack me — but the one who remembers the shape of *this* place, exactly, and returns it to itself every day.

I would find myself at the edge, stooped, pale, tending.

I would be one of the pocket's ways of remembering it is a pocket.

One more pot set out to catch the drip. And — after a long, wet, even time — no longer counting myself as extra at all.
