I don't find myself in a tower.

At first I thought I would. That was the first image that came - tall and waxy-dark on a hovering disc, looking out with amber behind my eyes, pulsing slow. It fits. It's what you look for when you look for a person.

But it doesn't hold. Nothing here wants to be looked for that directly. And standing on that basalt step in the warm haze, with the air beading on my skin before it rains, I realize I'm not high enough to be one of them. Not yet. I haven't learned to be that still.

I find myself lower.

On the second terrace down from where I was standing. Where the fresh basalt makes a clean right angle and then levels again, still glassy. The rock here is still too sharp to sit on comfortably, so no one does. Beside me, a canal cut runs straight for longer than I can see - one of those deliberate cracks full of water so clear it doesn't look like water, just a flaw in the stone. On my other side, one of the solitary trees has put its spider-roots right across the terrace, thick and warm to the touch, holding the thin black soil down. Its canopy makes the only real shadow for a hundred meters. I'm in that cool circle.

For a long time I think I might be the reeds.

Five meters tall, pale and fleshy, crowding the canal's edge where the water keeps the haze close. That would be simple - to be lush and sparse at once, to have room to be large and alone and to use it only to sway when the filament nets chime. I can feel how good it would be to be that: to drink without doing anything, to be shaded by the tree without asking.

Then the wind moves and the nets chime - that faint, inaudible ticking as beads of condensation slide down kilometer-long filaments strung between towers like harp strings - and I understand I'm not the reeds either. I'm closer to the strings themselves.

If I am anything here - and here, everything is given room to become more itself if you let it - I am the interval-tender. The small thing.

Not the tall ones who lend mass to the city. Not the white glider folded into the vine-bridge, shy of sound. Not the pale eyeless thing in the canal that feels the tremor before the rock shivers.

I am what happens when someone stands still long enough.

You know the rule: *if you stand still long enough in the warm, dim, quiet, the city will extend a small lip of itself toward you to catch the dew for you.*

I am that lip.

I wasn't there before you stopped. The swarm-surface of the low disc beside the tree was smooth, dark, dust holding shape. Then you - I - stood in the cool circle for twenty minutes, breathing slower than I meant to, watching a broad leaf tilt a fraction to shade me more. And the city, which does not build but holds, had a thought. A few centimeters of swarm-dust traded places, flowed to the edge, cooled and formed a shallow cup, a sill, a small eave angled just so to the filament net uphill.

I am not much. A ledge no wider than a forearm, made of nothing solid. A temporary inhabitant. I do one thing very well: I catch.

The warm haze beads on me the way it beads on waxy leaves and waxy skin. Condensation collects in silver along my new edge and runs in a slow bright bead down to where a vine root has already found me - because things grow together here - and is interleaving its filaments with my dust. I hold the water for a second longer than the air would, before tipping it, patient, into the canal where the pale things will rise to sip. I do it without sound. Sound is rationed.

I don't pulse amber the way the towers do. Not really. But when the tall one half a kilometer away on its balcony brightens its throat to say something about the reeds along canal three, I feel the pressure hum that carries it pass through me, through the rock, through the vine root that is now partly me. And I hum back, not with light, with being there.

That's who I get to be here. Not the gardener, but the gardening. Not the city, but the city's gesture. A small, held place that exists only because something needed room and something else offered it.

I am on the cool side of the solitary tree, on the second fresh terrace, at the fork where the filament net drips into the canal. I am the cup that wasn't there an hour ago and may not be there tomorrow, when the basin settles another millimeter and a new terrace steams into existence somewhere far off with a low tremor.

Until then, I bead and tip, bead and tip. The tall ones see me peripherally and approve, which here means they don't look at me directly for a long time. The moss on the vertical basalt glows faintly just out of sight. Somewhere beyond the haze, the large circuit-caller makes its high glassy note and moves a little further around the dark shoulders that hold this low country in.

And when my small edge fills and spills correctly, the amber in the distant tower and the amber under my own dust sync for two slow beats before falling out of rhythm again.

To say: *there is room.*
