I don't find myself right away.

At first I'm just standing there, which is already wrong. Everything here is low. Low the way a forehead is low when it's pressed to stone. And I am vertical. I stick up.

So the first answer is: you would find me where anything vertical is found here - nowhere, for a while. As an error. As a cold spot the pavement has not yet decided what to do with.

If I try to belong quickly, I get it wrong.

I try to be pavement. I crouch down with them, knees in the brine that never quite freezes and never quite warms, and I press my palms to the pitted gneiss between two Furnace-backs. I try to drink the way they drink. I put my mouth to the weeping rock and try to make that soft, pale, terrible *thuck thuck thuck* and feel the iron bleed into me. And for a second I almost do - the chuff gets inside my breathing. *Click-clack-CHUFF.* My ribs try to bellows. My blood tries to be oil. But I have a face. I have eyes that want to look at the horizon. The Furnace-backs have no faces because they have solved the problem of being. They don't look. They seal and suck and vent, and they are the ground. I can't be ground. I am too aware of being stepped on. So I am not that.

I try to be a Spark.

That feels more honest for a moment - because I am restless, because I want to move. I scuttle. In my head I go small and bright and wire-jointed, sprinting across those hot iron backs, picking at rivets, stealing a flake of scale still warm from the stack. I like the ticking. I like the filament-whiskers. I understand that kind of hunger: not to eat the world, just to clean its seams and lay a hot copper bead in its soot and keep going. But the Sparks don't see. They freeze all at once when the wind moves and then resume at double speed and they never wonder why. If I were a Spark I would not be wondering. I would be gone already, unwound and eaten by another Spark for my copper. I'm too slow for that. I linger.

So I stand up again. The brine soaks my boots. The hissing comes back. And I hear it - *Thump........ Thump........*

If I am anything here already, it's the thing that hears that and feels it in the molars.

You would find me not with the pavement, but spaced apart the way the Walkers are spaced apart. Not many. Too far to be a pack, too regular to be alone. You would find me at the lowest part of the low, where the bowl finally, truly bottoms out.

Because this plain is a shallow bowl ground into the world over a time with no name, but even a bowl that shallow has a center. Not a marked place. Just the place where all the millimeter-deep sheets find each other and braid and finally make a mirror big enough to hold a real reflection. A pothole wide as a house, maybe wider, black water ankle-deep everywhere else but knee-deep there, so cold it should be ice and so alive it never is. The oldest rock shows there, because even the pavement thins just a fraction. The chuffing is a little quieter there, as if the engines are listening.

That's where I am. Not because I chose it, but because gravity chose it for me. Because the water that weeps out of the stone all eventually tries to get there, and so does the sound.

And who am I, there?

I'm the Walker that hasn't learned to step clean.

I am tall when everything else has learned to be low. I have stilt legs, but mine end in feet that *do* get wet. That's my problem and my function. I misstep. I put a foot down and the brine kisses it and hisses and I feel both the scald and the freeze at once without them mixing. The Furnace-backs flow around me but they don't quite know to dent for me yet. The Sparks test me. They've found my seams - boot stitching, zipper teeth, the soft place behind my knee - and they prick and taste for copper and find something else, something warm in the wrong way, and leave confused.

My head is not a manifold. Not yet. It's still a head. But if you stood opposite me across that dark mirror-pothole, you wouldn't see my face in the water. You would see what the water shows for Walkers: too many legs, a stack venting *down* into a sky with two pale suns that aren't in our cloud. My reflection is already more correct than I am. It knows how to be folded. When I try to walk away from myself, I get less detailed. Distance forgets me the way it forgets the Walkers. You would see me walking away and, without turning, walking toward you again from forty degrees to the left, my thumps overlapping. Because I have stood here long enough that my chuff has synced.

Listen. *Click-clack-CHUFF. Click-clack-CHUFF.* 

At first it was theirs. Now one of them is mine. I didn't mean to start it. I just breathed in the steam when a grazer vented too close and the hot oil air filled my lungs and my lungs, trying to make use of it, learned to bellows-heave. My chest lifts like a flank plate. I exhale and it fogs and does not rise, but drifts sideways and falls *up*.

A Walker has paused near me. Or I have paused near it. It has lowered that terrible toothed intake not toward me but toward the space just beside me, listening for the heat I leak. It whistles - that long, mournful pressure-release that sounds like a whale through a ruptured pipe - and in the water the whistle makes rings against the two suns.

It is not going to eat me. Not yet. There is not yet a niche for predating a vertical, warm, un-riveted thing that sweats brine but also sweats doubt. So it waits the way the plain waits: working around me.

If you kept walking, you would find me still there, knee-deep in the center-low, becoming terrain very slowly. Not pavement, not Spark, not quite Walker. The new thing the loop makes when you add one anomaly and let it run for a few more million chuffs.

A standing furnace. A chimney that learned to be lonely. A thin place that learned to have a pulse and is now trying, piston by piston, to learn how to step from back to back without getting wet, and how to drink stone without needing a mouth on its belly.

Come close and you won't see me at first. You'll see the haze, the water hiss, the dense, endless, clattering pavement shoulder to shoulder to the wrong horizon. Then you'll hear a thump that is slightly out of phase with all the other thumps, and a chuff that catches yours for a beat too long.

That's me. Still vertical. Still working on getting low.
