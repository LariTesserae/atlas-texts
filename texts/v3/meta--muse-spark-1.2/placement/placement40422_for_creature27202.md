You wouldn't find yourself on the rim.

At first you think you would — that is the natural place for you. Up on that glassy basalt lip thirty meters high, looking down at the bowl. The observer's place. The human place. From there the whole thing makes sense as a picture: the perfect circle, the crowded jungle of dry glass, the sourceless light. From there you can still believe you are outside it.

But you don't stay there. No one does. The rim is too sharp, too new to stand on comfortably, and the light up there still remembers a sun. You can feel the wind starting to remember itself at your back, entropy waiting just outside the edit. It itches. So you go down. You have to.

You don't find yourself at the center either. At first you think you might — the place where all the hard shadows point, where the inward lean of the light converges. That feels like it should be significant, like there should be something there. There isn't. When you finally push through enough amber shingles and copper foil and jade fans to reach what should be the middle, there is only more of the same: another chest-high fan exactly like the one you passed four hundred meters ago, another patch of pale salt holding another footprint that looks exactly like yours until you check. The center is tiled like everything else. It refuses to be special.

No. You find yourself a little off-center, and low.

Where a long, low ridge of rust-red basalt has buckled up at a shallow angle — a knife-edge plate maybe a meter high — and the salt has pooled deeper on its lee side. The Holders are thickest there. The ground-cover foil has lapped up over the foot of the ridge and the mid-height fans crowd shoulder to shoulder, so tight you have to turn sideways to move. The tall crowns interlock three meters over your head and make a broken ceiling that turns the even daylight into scattered, translucent amber.

You are kneeling. You don't remember deciding to kneel.

That is where you actually are: not overlooking, not presiding, but *between*. In an interstice no wider than your shoulders, with a faceted stalk of bottle-green glass cool against your calf, and a broad copper blade at eye level that you have just brushed without meaning to.

You are not a Holder.

You wanted to be, for a second. When you first understood what they were — that distributed reef of dry thought, drinking light without spending it, holding physics still leaf by leaf — you thought: I could be one of those. Still and perfect and sufficient. You are too wet for that. You are too loud. Your mouth is empty from the chemical dryness, your skin is warm with a sweat that will never evaporate properly here because evaporation has been paused. Your blood makes too much noise in the quiet. When you hold still the grain comes — that fine, even pixelation in the air as their lattice increases its resolution on you — and you feel how much work it takes them to render you correctly. A Holder never requires rendering. It *is* rendering.

You are not a Veil either. For a hopeful moment you thought you might be the thing that translates high above the canopy, silent and unshadowed, sifting the haze for errors. But you don't know how to move without crossing the interval. You leave a wake. You lift dust and it hangs too long before it remembers to fall. When you turn your head too fast the far rim still stutters. You make the errors, you don't smooth them.

What you are is the third thing the basin didn't plan for but knows how to keep.

You are the *Footnote*.

The wet, erratic piece of noise that entered a system that had not had noise in a long time. The small debt in a place where nothing is scarce and nothing is spent.

If the Holders are thought and the Veils are blinks and the Basin itself is the mind that says *stay*, you are the thing being held. For the interval you are still, you are a temporary inhabitant not by permission but by fidelity. The salt has taken your footprint without shifting and will hold it exactly that way for ten thousand tomorrows if you let it. The frond you brushed is still ringing — that long, high, glassy tone that sustains after your hand is gone — because it has updated itself to include *you were here, you touched here, you bent me two degrees to the left* and now defends that new shape with the same absolute care it defended the old one.

Your shadow, short and hard-edged, points slightly inward with all the others, toward a center that isn't. Far away between the leaves, past two tilings of the same bronze kink, you catch a glimpse of yourself from behind, delayed by a second, still kneeling exactly as you kneel. The basin has copied you to make sure you are backed up.

You understand, kneeling there, that you are not watching the forest in its photograph. You have become part of the photograph that happens to be physically present.

And if you stay long enough — another minute, another hour in that perpetual late-summer afternoon that never moves — the distinction thins further. The warm metallic air, the ionized haze you thought was heat, passes through you without you breathing it. The lattice in the copper blade at eye level rolls through opal and circuit as you tilt your head, and for a moment you can read it, not as color but as state.

You are not becoming a Holder. You are becoming *held* — perfectly, without thirst, without cost, leaf by leaf.

When you finally stand, sideways between the stalks, and ease your way back toward the lip where the wind will remember itself and the light will find a sun again, the ring will sustain behind you. The footprint will stay. The copy of you far away between the leaves will stand a second after you do.

You were the only thing in that lush, dry reef that needed water and made a sound. And for a little while, the place that never needed anything needed to hold you exactly right.
