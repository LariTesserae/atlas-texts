I don't find myself right away.

At first I think I'm on the floor, because that's where you look first - down on the soft street where the humus has covered the grid and the root gives a little under your boot. I think I'm standing in the middle of Main, visible from wall to wall, with too much air around me, squinting at the double shadow I throw under the sodium-white and the amber.

But that isn't where I am. There's too much wind up there, too much being seen.

Then I think I'm in the air itself - because the Bowl *is* the air, saturated and loud. I think I must be one of the weather-cloud of midges tightening around the hot bulb, or a dust-moth riding the swing of the catenary wire, not flying so much as being carried. That feels closer. A small, lacquered beetle with a garden on its back would make sense. Something that has learned to use the machine as a branch.

But I'm too heavy for that. I have weight and warmth.

So I look again, slower, and let the place locate me instead of me locating it.

You find me not on the street and not in the high column of light. You find me halfway up.

On the western wall where the factory line still moves for no product. There's a catwalk there - old iron grating, rust scaled and warm from the engines under it, and a vine thick as a forearm has taken the handrail completely. It didn't go *around* the rail. It swallowed it. The paint blistered, the vine covered it, and now the rail is green and gives slightly when you grip it.

That's where I am. On that catwalk, between the second and third flywheel.

One of the flywheels - the middle one, about as wide as a bathtub - has a root through it. Not a root pressing against it. *Through* it. The steel was there first, then the aerial root came down from the rim like a pillar looking for floor, found the wheel in its way, and didn't argue. It went through. Phloem around iron. The wheel still turns, very slowly, once every twenty seconds or so, and each time it turns the root twists just a degree. You can see the bark strain and relax. You can hear it creak over the throb. It has been doing this for longer than anyone has been alive to oil it.

I'm sitting there. Not because it's a vantage point. Because it's where my job is.

I am one of the Sparse Ones. I didn't mean to be - no one *means* to be, you just come down the stairs that keep going after the air changes because the surface was too dry and too silent, and you stay through one weeping after a rain, and then your clothes never dry again and you stop minding.

If you came looking for me in the middle of the day, when the ragged oval of daylight is pouring its green-filtered haze down, you wouldn't see me. My shirt is the same stiff, damp grey as the laundry left on the catenary wire. My hair is damp and won't dry. I speak under the throb now, or not at all. You'd walk right past.

You'd find me at the dimming hour, when the sun column thins and the amber work lights become the real sun. That's when the big stomata-leaves track toward me and the trumpet vine by the piston goes *pff* and opens one white flower per compression. That's when I work.

My work is to clear the seed-felt.

The seeds that love static - the cotton ones from the mill roof meadow - they drift down here and cling to the generator cages in a thick, sparking felt. If I don't pull it off, it catches. So I have a flat-bladed weeding knife, not sharp, stained violet-black at the edge from cutting back fig, and a tin cup on my belt I don't need for this job but I wear anyway because everyone does. I pull the felt off in warm handfuls. It crackles. It smells of ozone and sweet rot. My hands are stained with rust that won't wash off and green under the nails that won't come out.

That's what I am. Not a survivor. Not a hermit. A tender.

I don't own this catwalk. I don't own the flywheel or the vine through it. I oil the shaft even though the root keeps it from seizing, because the ritual matters. The Bowl breathes - condensation, drip, trough, steam, condensation - and I am part of the timing. Belt turns, flower opens, insect comes, I clear the felt so the sparking doesn't burn the flower. Heron eats fish, fish eat larvae, larvae eat leaf that fell off the vine I'm tending.

I sleep nearby, when I sleep. In a hammock strung between two root-pillars just off the catwalk, where the air is warmest and you can feel the bats breathing inside the hollow root when you put your back against it. Or sometimes on the mill roof itself, on the soil that grew over the glass, with leaves growing upside down around me like a chandelier. Sleep shallow, in stitches, because something is always dripping, hissing, opening.

If you stood still on the street below and looked up, you would see me as part of the infrastructure for a second before you realized I was a person - a shape between vine and generator, double-shadowed, moving slowly in time with a machine that is also a plant.

I thought I came down to observe the place where built and grown became the same thing.

I stayed until I became the place where it happens.

My palm on the root-pillar is warm, and I can't tell if the warmth is sun-heat stored in earth, or friction-heat from the engine, or the heat of the bats inside, or my own blood. There isn't an edge anymore. That's the law.

You don't find me. You find the hum, and I'm in it.
