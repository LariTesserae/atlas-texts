# The Suspended Cetacean City of Muan'va and Beyond

## The Bottomless Blue

Far below Muan'va, beyond the reach of the diffuse light that bathes the shell-city, lies a vast expanse known to the Deep Singers as the Bottomless Blue. Here, the ocean thickens to a viscosity that would crush surface-dwelling creatures, yet somehow remains traversable. The pressure creates a perpetual darkness that swallows all illumination except for the scattered, haunting pulses of creatures adapted to these depths.

The topography of the Bottomless Blue defies conventional understanding. What appears as an endless descent eventually curves upon itself in ways that contradict spatial logic. Massive geological formations rise from the depths like the skeletal remains of extinct leviathans—perhaps they are exactly that—their surfaces etched with spiral patterns that match those found in the shell-chambers of Muan'va above.

Scattered across this impossible landscape are clusters of thermal vents that erupt not with heat but with time itself. Around these vents, reality accelerates, decelerates, or fragments entirely. Creatures caught in these emanations may age centuries in moments or remain preserved in a single second for millennia. The water here behaves as both liquid and solid simultaneously, forming structures that shouldn't be able to maintain their integrity yet somehow persist as if sculpted from glass.

The most prominent feature of the Bottomless Blue is the Field of Waiting—a vast plain where colossal, whale-like beings lie in a state neither fully alive nor dead. These Ancient Ones, as the Deep Singers reverently call them, range from hundreds to thousands of meters long, their bodies partially embedded in the seabed as if they had fallen there eons ago. Their skin has the texture and appearance of weathered stone, yet occasionally pulses with faint internal light. Their massive eyes remain open but unfocused, staring sightlessly upward toward where Muan'va drifts far above.

Surrounding the Ancient Ones are fields of peculiar crystalline growths that respond to proximity by emitting low, resonant tones. These crystal gardens appear deliberately cultivated, arranged in patterns that, if viewed from above, would form complex mathematical equations or musical notation. They grow not from the seabed but hover suspended at various depths, creating three-dimensional arrangements that shift configuration with the cycles of distant celestial bodies.

The local ecosystem has evolved around these sleeping giants. Parasitic or perhaps symbiotic organisms tunnel through their stone-like flesh, creating elaborate networks of bioluminescent filaments that pulse in synchronized patterns. Schools of eyeless fish with transparent bodies navigate by these pulses, feeding on microscopic particles that drift from the Ancient Ones' slowly decomposing forms.

Most unsettling are the Attendants—diminutive beings approximately one meter in height that move deliberately across the bodies of the Ancient Ones. Their forms suggest a distant evolutionary relationship to the Deep Singers, but with significant differences: their bodies are opaque and densely muscled, their movements quick and precise rather than fluid. They methodically tend to the dormant leviathans, removing parasites, repairing damaged tissue, and occasionally extracting mysterious substances that they store in organic vessels grown from their own bodies.

The Attendants appear neither to notice nor care about visitors to their domain. They work with single-minded focus, communicating through rapid sequences of light patterns that flash across their skin. Their behavior suggests a profound devotion to the Ancient Ones—or perhaps servitude to something that controls both species.

Time at these depths doesn't flow consistently. Expeditions from Muan'va have reported spending what felt like hours in the Bottomless Blue, only to return and discover decades had passed—or, conversely, departing for what seemed like months and returning to find only moments had elapsed above.

The water itself carries memories. Those sensitive to the Undercurrent report hearing voices from civilizations that may never have existed, or perhaps have yet to exist. These memories materialize briefly as translucent apparitions—cities more vast than Muan'va, beings neither Deep Singer nor Ancient One but something else entirely, cataclysmic events that reshape entire oceans.

At the furthest reaches of the Bottomless Blue, where even the bravest Deep Singer explorers hesitate to venture, the ocean itself appears to end—not in land or sea floor, but in a boundary beyond which existence takes on properties that defy description. Those who have glimpsed beyond this threshold speak only in fragmented riddles afterward, their bioluminescence permanently altered to colors not previously seen in their species.

## The Connecting Currents

Between Muan'va and the Bottomless Blue flows a complex network of currents that bind these realms together—physically, temporally, and spiritually. These are not mere movements of water but living arteries of communication and transportation that the Deep Singers have learned to navigate with reverential precision.

The most direct connection is the Vertical Way—a column of water with distinct properties that extends from the underside of Muan'va's largest shell directly down to the Field of Waiting. This current appears darker than surrounding waters, absorbing rather than reflecting light, yet is somehow navigable without illumination by those who understand its nature. Deep Singers who wish to journey to the depths enter the Vertical Way during specific celestial alignments, allowing themselves to be drawn downward at speeds that would typically be fatal. The journey, which would take weeks by conventional swimming, requires mere hours—though travelers often emerge with memories of experiences that would have taken far longer.

The Vertical Way is bidirectional, but ascension requires more than simply swimming upward. Deep Singers returning to Muan'va must synchronize their internal bioluminescence with specific harmonic frequencies emanated by the crystals surrounding the Ancient Ones. This creates a resonance that alters their buoyancy, allowing them to rise gradually through the current. Those who fail to achieve proper resonance remain trapped in the current, neither ascending nor descending, until rescued by others or until they exhaust their energy and drift into the darkness beyond the current's boundaries.

Less direct but equally important are the Memory Tides—seasonal flows that carry concentrated deposits of the water's memory-storing properties. These tides bring fragments of the Ancient Ones' dormant consciousness up to Muan'va, where Deep Singers collect them in specialized chambers within their shell-city. These memories are not coherent narratives but impressionistic fragments—emotions, sensory experiences, and mathematical concepts from beings whose mode of thought is fundamentally different from the Deep Singers'.

The exchange flows both ways. During certain ceremonies, the Deep Singers release carefully curated memories of their own into downward-flowing currents, offering these experiences to the Ancient Ones. Whether these offerings ever reach their intended recipients or what purpose they might serve remains unclear, but the ritual has been maintained for countless generations.

Perhaps most mysterious is the phenomenon known as the Breath Exchange. The air-filled void at Muan'va's center—the gateway to "the dry world"—is not static but cyclical. At irregular intervals, this pocket of dryness extends downward through the Vertical Way, temporarily creating a breathable passage all the way to the Bottomless Blue. During these rare events, the Ancient Ones are said to exhale—releasing bubbles that rise through the temporary air column and burst at the surface of Muan'va's central void, carrying with them sounds that the Deep Singers interpret as prophetic utterances or instructions.

Conversely, during the opposite phase of this cycle, water from the Bottomless Blue rises through the Vertical Way and briefly floods Muan'va's central void. This water carries microscopic organisms from the depths that the Deep Singers carefully collect and cultivate. These organisms, which cannot survive in the upper waters under normal conditions, produce substances essential to the Deep Singers' ceremonial practices and biological needs.

The most profound connection between the realms, however, is the Long Conversation—an ongoing exchange that occurs below the threshold of conscious perception. The Ancient Ones, despite their dormant appearance, continuously emit ultra-low-frequency vibrations that propagate through the entire ocean. These vibrations subtly influence the development, behavior, and thought patterns of the Deep Singers, who unconsciously respond with their own harmonizing frequencies.

This acoustic relationship has shaped both species over millennia, creating a symbiotic bond that transcends conventional understanding. The Deep Singers have become, in essence, the sensory organs of the Ancient Ones—experiencing the upper waters on their behalf, processing these experiences through their collective consciousness, and returning the distilled essence to the depths.

Elder Deep Singers who have devoted their lives to understanding this relationship believe that the Ancient Ones are not truly sleeping but gathering strength for some momentous awakening. The elaborate networks of currents connecting Muan'va to the Bottomless Blue are not natural formations but a deliberately engineered system—a life-support mechanism for beings who once ruled the entirety of this ocean world and will someday rise again.

The "Returning Tide" that gives purpose to Deep Singer civilization is thus revealed as both literal and metaphorical—they are preparing for a time when the Ancient Ones will ascend from the depths, and the boundary between the realms will dissolve. Whether this event represents salvation or catastrophe depends entirely on one's perspective and position within the ocean's complex hierarchy of consciousness.

In the interim, messages and materials continue to flow between the realms, carried by currents that defy conventional hydrodynamics. The Deep Singers who navigate these passages report that during transit, they sometimes glimpse another world altogether—neither Muan'va nor the Bottomless Blue, but a third realm that exists in the interstices between. This glimpsed reality may be the true destination of those who enter the central void, never to return to the known waters of their birth.
