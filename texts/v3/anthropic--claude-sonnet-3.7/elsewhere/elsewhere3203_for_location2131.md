# Across the Veil: The Surface World of Chattermire

While the Undervoid twists and churns below, the surface world of Chattermire presents itself as a seemingly ordinary coastal city—a deliberate façade maintained through collective denial and institutional manipulation. 

Built on a rocky peninsula jutting into the slate-gray waters of the Northern Sea, Chattermire-Above appears at first glance to be just another declining industrial port city. Victorian-era buildings of soot-darkened brick line narrow, cobbled streets that wind haphazardly up from the harbor. Wrought-iron lampposts stand sentry, their glass enclosures perpetually fogged from within—not with ordinary condensation, but with a luminescent vapor that shifts color subtly with the barometric pressure.

The city exists in a state of perpetual autumn. Though seasons technically change, the dominant impression is always of a crisp October afternoon sliding toward evening, the quality of light somehow always suggesting 4:30 PM regardless of the actual hour. Maple and oak trees line the main thoroughfares, their leaves permanently caught in mid-transition between green and deep red, never fully falling but occasionally detaching to spiral downward in patterns that mathematicians have noted follow the same equations as certain movements of the Geometers below.

The harbor remains active, though none of the vessels that dock at Chattermire venture far from shore. Their hulls are reinforced with the same copper-alloy that features prominently in the architecture of the Undervoid, and their captains navigate by charts that account for currents no oceanographer has ever documented. These ships bring exotic goods—spices with tastes that linger for days, textiles that feel different to each person who touches them, timepieces that run at varying speeds depending on their proximity to the city center.

The people of Chattermire-Above can be broadly categorized into three groups:

First are the Oblivious—ordinary citizens who genuinely believe they live in a normal, if somewhat eccentric, coastal town. They notice the oddities but have developed remarkable psychological mechanisms to rationalize them away. The occasional shimmer in the air that reveals a glimpse of the Undervoid is dismissed as a trick of light. The strange dreams that plague the entire population are attributed to local cuisine or the peculiar mineral content of the drinking water.

Next are the Aware—those who know about the Undervoid but choose to remain above. They include city officials, certain clergy, and the wealthy families whose fortunes were built on the exploitation of resources from below. They recognize the signs of bleed-through between realms: the occasional reversal of gravity in sealed rooms, the telephone calls that connect to conversations from decades past, the mirrors that briefly reflect places no one recognizes. The Aware maintain the charade of normalcy, understanding that the city's economy and indeed its very existence depends on the delicate balance between the worlds.

Finally, there are the Transient—those who move between realms, carrying messages, goods, and occasionally people between the surface and the depths. Many are affiliated with the Department of Subterranean Affairs, a government agency housed in a nondescript building near the city center whose interior dimensions cannot possibly fit within its exterior walls. The Transient bear subtle marks of their journeys: eyes that reflect light too intensely, fingernails with a metallic sheen, a tendency to speak in rhythmic patterns that echo the Endless Symphony.

# The Connection Between Worlds

The passages between Chattermire-Above and the Undervoid are numerous but well-concealed. Some are permanent, while others appear only under specific conditions—during certain astronomical alignments, or when particular words are spoken in the right sequence near the right location.

The most reliable entrance is beneath St. Agatha's Cathedral, an imposing gothic structure whose spires seem to pierce the perpetually overcast sky. In the crypt below the main altar, behind the sarcophagus of the city's founder, a spiral staircase descends. Initially, it appears to lead only to a small storage room, but those who know to close the door and count to thirteen before reopening it will find the stairs now continue downward, curving impossibly for hundreds of steps until they open into the Resonance Chamber of the Undervoid, where the Conductor's symphony provides a momentary sense of stability for newcomers.

Other passages are more treacherous. Certain manholes in the Old Quarter will occasionally lead to the industrial sectors of the Undervoid rather than to sewers. A specific phonebooth near the harbor sometimes connects directly to the Archive of the Cartography Collective. The municipal swimming pool, closed every Wednesday for "maintenance," actually drains completely on these days, revealing a passage that leads to the Ossuary Market.

Perhaps most disturbing are the temporary rifts that form unpredictably throughout the city. These tears in reality manifest as shimmering distortions in the air, lasting anywhere from seconds to hours. Objects—and occasionally people—can pass through in either direction, often with disorienting results. The Department of Subterranean Affairs employs "Rift Wardens" who patrol the city, ready to secure these areas when they appear and provide cover stories for any witnesses.

# The Symbiosis

The relationship between Chattermire-Above and the Undervoid is not merely one of proximity but of symbiosis—each realm fundamentally dependent on the other in ways that few fully understand.

The surface city draws power from the Undervoid—literal electrical power generated by the Grand Turbine and other mysterious mechanisms below, but also a metaphysical energy that keeps the city slightly out of phase with the rest of the world. This displacement is what allows Chattermire's peculiar properties to exist: its stretched time, its anomalous weather patterns, its resistance to outside technological and cultural influences.

In return, the Undervoid feeds on the collective consciousness of the surface dwellers. The dreams, memories, and emotions of Chattermire's citizens filter downward, providing essential sustenance for many Undervoid entities—particularly the Mnemophages, who have developed a taste for the specific flavor of surface memories. This exchange explains why surface dwellers experience such vivid, unusual dreams and occasional inexplicable gaps in memory.

Physical resources flow between the realms as well. Certain rare fungi from the Undervoid have medicinal properties that the city's pharmacists quietly incorporate into their most effective remedies. Meanwhile, sunlight—captured in special crystalline containers by the Transient—is a valuable commodity below, used to stabilize some of the more volatile regions of the Undervoid.

# The Crisis Point

In recent years, the balance between worlds has begun to deteriorate. The symptoms are subtle but increasing: more frequent and longer-lasting rifts, weather patterns above growing even more abnormal, regions of the Undervoid becoming dangerously unstable. Clocks in Chattermire-Above have started running backward for minutes at a time before correcting themselves. Children have reported imaginary friends whose descriptions match exactly the appearance of known Undervoid entities.

The Cartography Collective has detected a troubling pattern: the Undervoid is gradually expanding, its boundaries pushing outward into previously stable bedrock. Meanwhile, on the surface, the city limits of Chattermire seem to be contracting—maps from a decade ago show neighborhoods that no longer exist, apparently consumed by the encroaching sea or simply faded from reality.

Most alarming is the behavior of the Mnemophages. Their feeding has grown more aggressive, leaving some surface dwellers with significant memory loss. The Conductor's Symphony has developed discordant passages that cause physical pain to listeners. And the Geometers have been observed forming unprecedented complex configurations that the mathematicians of the Collective can only interpret as expressions of distress.

Theories about the cause of this destabilization vary wildly. Some believe it stems from over-exploitation—too many resources being drawn from the Undervoid without sufficient return. Others suggest that outside forces are interfering—perhaps other cities with their own subterranean realms, or entities from dimensions even the Undervoid cannot normally access.

A fringe group within the Department of Subterranean Affairs proposes a more radical hypothesis: that Chattermire-Above and the Undervoid were never meant to be separate realms at all, but rather a single integrated reality that was artificially divided centuries ago for reasons lost to history. The current crisis, they suggest, is simply the natural order reasserting itself—the worlds slowly merging back into their original state.

Whatever the truth, those aware of both realms find themselves in an increasingly precarious position—trying to maintain the appearance of normalcy above while working desperately to stabilize the foundations below, all while knowing that the barrier between worlds grows thinner by the day. In both realms, there is a palpable sense that Chattermire is approaching a point of transformation from which there will be no return.

The tolling bell that echoes throughout the Undervoid has become audible on the surface now—faint but unmistakable, always seeming to come from everywhere and nowhere at once. And in both worlds, inhabitants have begun to notice something new: brief moments when all sound ceases completely, when motion seems to pause, as if reality itself is holding its breath before whatever comes next.
