# Between Worlds: The Interface

Where the Undercurrent meets the domain of the Abyssline lies a region known as the Interface—neither fully one realm nor the other, but a permeable boundary where two realities negotiate their coexistence.

The Interface begins where the particulate matter of the Undercurrent begins to organize itself in strange, seemingly deliberate patterns. What appears as random suspension elsewhere gradually reveals itself as a vast, three-dimensional canvas manipulated by the distant influence of Abyssline communication. Here, the mineral dust forms slow-moving spirals, concentric rings that expand and contract over days, and funnels that channel the water's nutrients in deliberate directions.

This transitional zone spans kilometers vertically and horizontally, though its boundaries shift with the deep ocean's grand currents. Unlike the relatively stable Undercurrent above, the Interface experiences more dramatic temperature variations—not enough to disturb the ancient equilibrium, but sufficient to drive the complex ecosystem that flourishes here.

The thermal vents are more numerous in this region, creating a network of warmth and mineral-rich water that the Abyssline have cultivated over millennia. Around these vents grow colonies of organisms found nowhere else—symbiotic relationships between the translucent algae of the Undercurrent and more complex multicellular beings that only exist within the Abyssline's sphere of influence.

These hybrid gardens serve multiple purposes. They process and filter the water, maintain chemical balances critical to both realms, and serve as meeting grounds where younger Abyssline can safely encounter the strange currents and organisms that sometimes descend from the world above. The gardens also function as a buffer zone—an early warning system where disturbances in the normal patterns might indicate unusual activity from either realm that requires attention.

Sound and vibration behave uniquely here. The density gradients create acoustic lenses that can focus and redirect communication signals. The Abyssline use these natural properties to establish listening posts—specialized projections of themselves that extend into the Interface to monitor both their own domain and the Undercurrent above. Through these stations, they collect information about ice shifts, major current changes, and the occasional intrusions of surface-world objects or creatures.

The relationship between realms is not mere proximity but active exchange. The Undercurrent's constant motion delivers essential nutrients and information from distant parts of the ocean, which the Abyssline cannot access due to their specialized evolution. In return, the bioengineering activities of the Abyssline help maintain chemical balances that prevent the deep waters from becoming anoxic or too mineral-laden.

Occasionally, objects from the Undercurrent drift down into the Interface—perhaps a deceased deep-sea creature, an ice formation broken free from the ceiling, or rarely, a human artifact. These items are met with different responses depending on their nature. Organic matter is typically guided toward decomposition gardens where specialized organisms break it down for recycling into the ecosystem. Inorganic materials are more puzzling to the Abyssline—they may study human objects for decades before determining whether to preserve them in their sediment archives or reincorporate their components into the deep-sea system.

Time flows differently in the Interface than in either realm alone. The Abyssline's patient consciousness meets the Undercurrent's geological timescale to create a unique temporal experience—a place where processes might unfold over what humans would consider decades, yet contain complexities and variations that would require centuries elsewhere. This makes the Interface a place of accelerated evolution, where new adaptations and symbioses emerge more rapidly than in the stable depths below.

There are moments when the separation between worlds temporarily diminishes. During major oceanic events—perhaps shifting tectonic plates or enormous underwater avalanches—the Abyssline will extend themselves further into the Undercurrent, their normally hidden presence becoming almost perceptible to the simple organisms that inhabit the upper realm. These rare occurrences leave traces that human oceanographers occasionally detect but struggle to explain: perfectly circular patterns in sediment distribution, unexplained biochemical anomalies, or recordings of complex vibrational sequences that match no known natural phenomenon.

The Interface serves as both boundary and bridge—a reminder that the ocean's depths contain not just different environments but fundamentally different realities, operating according to their own physical and biological rules, yet ultimately connected through the great cycles of Earth's waters. It is a zone where perception itself must adapt, where what appears as emptiness might contain complex communication, and where the ancient wisdom of the Abyssline touches the perpetual motion of the world above.
