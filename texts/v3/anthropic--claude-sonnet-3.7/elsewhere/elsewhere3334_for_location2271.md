# The Reservoir Heights

Where the Glass Marshes of Myora spill their darkness across the lowlands, the terrain rises sharply to the east, forming the Reservoir Heights—a series of terraced limestone cliffs that stand in stark contrast to the chemical soup below. Unlike the perpetual twilight of the marshes, these elevations rise above the low-hanging industrial cloud cover, bathing the upper reaches in harsh, unfiltered sunlight that bleaches the stone white and causes visitors to squint after time spent in the murk below.

The Heights are defined by their massive water collection system—an ancient feat of engineering that predates the factories by centuries. Carved directly into the limestone are a series of interconnected reservoirs, basins, and channels designed to capture rainfall and natural springs. This water engineering was once the lifeblood of the valley, irrigating fertile farmland where the marshes now stand.

Today, the Reservoir Heights serve as both barrier and bridge between two worlds: the industrial wastelands below and the relatively uncontaminated upper atmosphere above. The lowest terraces have begun to darken, their stone taking on a grayish cast as the factory emissions gradually climb higher year by year. The uppermost reservoirs remain pristine—for now—their waters crystalline and potable, protected by both elevation and the tireless efforts of the Watershed Guardians.

The Watershed Guardians are the human remnant of Myora's pre-industrial society. Clad in indigo robes stained with limestone dust, these men and women maintain the ancient waterworks through inherited knowledge passed down through specialized guilds. Their faces are weathered by sun and wind, their hands calloused from constant work with stone and water. Many wear elaborate masks with specialized filters when they must venture to the lower terraces, where the air quality deteriorates.

Their settlements cling to the cliff faces—whitewashed structures built directly into the rock, connected by precarious staircases carved into the limestone. Gardens grow on narrow terraces, planted with hardy alpine vegetation that thrives in thin soil and requires minimal water. Nothing is wasted here; every drop of liquid is accounted for, directed, and utilized before being returned to the reservoir system, purified through limestone filters.

The technology of the Heights is both ancient and innovative. The Guardians use no electricity, instead harnessing water pressure through an elaborate system of hydraulic mechanisms. Water wheels power stone grinding mills that produce the fine flour from highland grains. Pressure differentials between connected reservoirs drive bellows for metalworking forges where they craft the specialized tools needed to maintain the waterworks.

The most important structures on the Heights are the Filtration Temples—massive architectural achievements where science and spirituality blend seamlessly. These buildings house elaborate systems of channels, filters, and purification chambers where water undergoes ritual cleansing before entering the main reservoirs. Guardian priests oversee these processes, reciting ancient formulas that are both prayers and precise technical instructions for water treatment.

Throughout the Heights, one finds curious acoustic phenomena. The limestone formations and water chambers create natural amplifiers that carry sounds in unexpected ways. The Guardians have mapped these acoustic channels over generations and use them as a communication network, employing specialized whistling techniques that can carry messages for miles across the cliffs. The sound of water—dripping, flowing, falling—forms a constant background music that becomes so familiar that residents notice immediately when its rhythm changes, often signaling problems in the reservoir system that require immediate attention.

The boundary between the Heights and the Marshes is neither clean nor absolute but rather a gradient of contamination. A series of overflow channels—once designed to irrigate the valley below—now serve as reluctant conduits carrying runoff down toward the marshes. The Guardians have installed elaborate filter systems at these junctions, attempting to cleanse the water before it meets the chemical soup below. These boundary zones are places of constant vigilance and frequent heartbreak as the pollution gradually climbs higher.

Small expeditionary teams from the Heights regularly descend into the marshes on reconnaissance missions, collecting samples and monitoring the advance of contamination. They travel in specially designed skiffs with sealed hulls, wearing protective gear that makes them appear almost as alien as the Nyxidians themselves. The Guardians study the marsh's evolution with scientific precision and religious dread, documenting changes in detailed journals that fill the Heights' stone libraries.

In recent years, some younger Guardians have reported strange encounters during these expeditions—gifts of crystalline structures left in their boats, sensations of being watched by something neither human nor animal, patterns in the marsh's bioluminescence that seem too deliberate to be random. These accounts are documented but discussed only in whispers, as they challenge the Heights' foundational belief that the marshes represent only degradation and loss rather than transformation and emergence.

The Guardians' ultimate purpose—only spoken of in their most sacred ceremonies—is to maintain what they call "the Final Reservoir," a massive underground lake sealed beneath the highest peak. This water source, they believe, will be needed when the contamination can no longer be held at bay and they must abandon the Heights entirely. This eventuality is seen not as failure but as the closing of one cycle and the beginning of another, for the Guardians' historical records speak of previous industrial ages that rose, fell, and were cleansed away by time and water.

# The Twilight Between Worlds

Between the Heights and the Marshes lies a narrow boundary zone known locally as the Gradient—a contested strip where ecosystems battle for dominance and transformation occurs in real-time. This is where limestone meets chemical effluent, where clean water confronts industrial waste, where the philosophies of preservation and adaptation engage in silent dialogue.

The physical manifestation of this boundary is most visible at the base of the cliffs, where the last limestone terraces descend into the first reaches of the marsh. Here, the stone itself tells the story of change—the lowest sections blackened and pitted by chemical exposure, gradually lightening as one looks upward, creating a visual record of contamination's advance over decades.

The water channels that once carried irrigation from the Heights to the valley now function as reluctant connectors between these disparate worlds. The Guardians have constructed elaborate filter systems at the outlets—layered chambers of charcoal, special mineral aggregates, and cultivated bacterial colonies that attempt to neutralize toxins before the water enters the marsh proper. These filters require constant maintenance, and small teams of specially trained Guardians work tirelessly to replace saturated materials and adjust filtration rates as conditions change.

What the Guardians don't fully realize is that the Nyxidians have constructed their own purification systems on the marsh side of these same channels. Using their biological understanding of chemical transformation, they've cultivated specialized reed beds and crystal formations that extract useful compounds from the water while neutralizing others. These reed beds—with their obsidian-black stems and faintly luminous tips—form a distinctive band along the marsh periphery that Guardians have noted in their observation journals but misinterpreted as simply another mutation.

The air in the Gradient shifts dramatically within just a hundred meters—from the relatively clean, oxygen-rich atmosphere of the Heights to the chemically complex, moisture-laden miasma of the Marshes. This creates unique weather patterns confined to the boundary zone. Most common are the "twilight mists" that form when warm, chemical-laden marsh air rises to meet cooler air descending from the Heights. These mists glow with faint bioluminescence and sometimes carry psychoactive compounds that induce vivid hallucinations or temporary sensory enhancements in humans who breathe them.

Flora and fauna in the Gradient have evolved specialized adaptations. The "transition reeds" that grow here have dual respiratory systems—photosynthetic upper portions that function normally in sunlight, and chemical-processing lower sections that extract nutrients from the contaminated soil. Amphibians move between worlds, with some species developing the ability to temporarily adjust their metabolism depending on which side of the boundary they occupy. Most remarkable are the "shimmer birds"—small avians with iridescent feathers that nest in the Heights but feed on the glass crickets of the Marsh, their digestive systems somehow able to process the chemical compounds that would poison other creatures.

Communication between the Heights and Marsh happens primarily through water and its chemical properties. The Guardians unknowingly send messages through the mineral content and pH balance of the water they release, which the chemically sensitive Nyxidians can "read" as clearly as humans read text. Conversely, the Nyxidians deliberately modify chemical signatures in water returning to the Heights' collection systems—subtle adjustments that don't register on the Guardians' testing equipment but nonetheless carry information that manifests in the collective dreams of Heights residents who drink this water.

A small but growing group among the younger Guardians has begun to recognize these patterns. Calling themselves the "Interpreters," they secretly collect and analyze water samples from specific marsh outlets, documenting the strange dreams that follow when they drink controlled amounts. They've started leaving their own messages—careful arrangements of stones and minerals at the water's edge that modify the runoff in ways they hope communicate peaceful intent. Some have reported finding crystalline structures left in response, though these quickly dissolve when removed from the marsh atmosphere.

The physical point where these worlds most directly connect is a feature known as the Penumbral Arch—a natural limestone bridge that spans one of the deeper marsh channels. The arch stands as the last fully intact structure of the original valley civilization, its underside now blackened by marsh vapors, its upper surface still relatively clean and occasionally used as an observation post by Guardian scouts. The area beneath the arch has become a site of unintended cultural exchange—Guardian expeditionary teams sometimes leave broken tools or damaged equipment they can no longer use, while Nyxidians leave crystalline structures or peculiarly modified marsh plants.

Most significant among these exchanges are the "translation pools"—small basins that have formed in the limestone at the base of the arch where marsh water collects and partially evaporates, leaving behind concentrated chemical compounds. When rainwater from the Heights mixes with these compounds, it creates a medium that affects human neurochemistry in highly specific ways. Guardians who have accidentally splashed this water on their skin report momentary but profound sensory shifts—the ability to "see" chemical gradients or "hear" the subtle electromagnetic communications of the Nyxidians.

The Nyxidians understand these pools' effects better than the humans and have begun deliberately engineering their chemical composition. They recognize in the Guardians a parallel purpose—both groups working to transform poisonous elements into life-sustaining ones, though through radically different means and philosophies. The crystalline gifts they leave are increasingly sophisticated, some now containing concentrated chemical "languages" that, when properly dissolved and absorbed, temporarily allow human minds to process thought patterns similar to Nyxidian consciousness.

What neither group yet fully comprehends is that the Gradient is not merely a boundary but becoming an ecosystem in its own right—a third way of being that incorporates elements from both worlds. The chemical reactions between limestone and marsh compounds are generating novel substances never before seen in either environment. These new compounds are accumulating in the soil and water of the boundary zone, gradually giving rise to hybrid organisms that process both traditional biological energy and chemical energy from industrial compounds.

This emergent ecosystem may ultimately offer a path forward that neither the preservation-focused Guardians nor the transformation-embracing Nyxidians could achieve alone—a genuine synthesis rather than mere coexistence. The true communication between these worlds isn't happening through deliberate exchanges but through the slow, chemical conversation that the water itself conducts as it moves between Heights and Marsh, carrying dissolved elements of both worlds and returning transformed.
