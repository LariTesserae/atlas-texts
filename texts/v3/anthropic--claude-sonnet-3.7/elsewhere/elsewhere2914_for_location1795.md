# The Sunfields

Where the plateau rises above the endless forest, its flat crown stretches like an island in a green sea. Here, the sky dominates everything—an immense, unobstructed dome of blue by day and star-washed black by night. The Sunfields occupy this elevated expanse, a community as defined by light as Vaulthome is by its absence.

The settlement consists of circular dwellings arranged in concentric rings, each home a perfect hemisphere of polished white stone that reflects the sun's journey across the sky. These structures appear almost blindingly bright at midday, softening to amber-gold at dusk. Each dome bears intricate carvings along its base—mathematical patterns that track the seasonal movement of shadows with astronomical precision.

Between the dwellings stretch the fields that give this place its name—not merely crops, but carefully maintained prisms of landscape that transform sunlight into sustenance. The central fields contain conventional agriculture: wheat, barley, and vegetables arranged in spiraling patterns. But the outer rings house the true wonders: crystalline structures cultivated rather than constructed, rising from beds of specialized mineral-rich soil. These semi-organic formations grow at imperceptible rates, their faceted surfaces splitting sunlight into component colors that nourish the unusual plants growing beneath them.

The people of the Sunfields move with a distinctive gait—quick, direct, and always attentive to the position of their shadows. Their skin has darkened to a deep bronze after generations under the unfiltered sun, and their eyes often appear golden in direct light, protected by pronounced brow ridges. They speak in clear, carrying voices that ring across the open plateau, their language full of terms for light's qualities—words that distinguish between twenty types of noon brightness or forty varieties of dusk.

Water is precious here, collected in central cisterns during the brief but intense rainy season. The Sunfields' hydraulic system—a network of precisely calibrated channels cut into the plateau's surface—distributes this resource according to strict allocations determined by the Council of Angles, elders who measure the year's progression through shadow-casting instruments.

The heart of the settlement is not a building but an absence—a perfect circular shaft cut through the plateau's entire thickness, lined with polished mirrors. This is the Light Well, its diameter calibrated so precisely that, at exact noon on the summer solstice, sunlight travels uninterrupted to illuminate the deepest chamber of Vaulthome for precisely seven minutes. The rest of the year, a complex system of adjustable mirrors channels controlled amounts of light downward.

The Sunfields' technology centers around optics and solar energy. Craftspeople create lenses of extraordinary precision, focusing sunlight to generate the high temperatures needed for their glasswork and metallurgy. Their most valued possessions are "sun catchers"—crystalline devices that store daylight and release it slowly throughout the night, bathing interiors in perpetual illumination.

Children learn shadow-casting as their first mathematics, using their bodies as gnomons to tell time and direction. Education culminates in the creation of a personal light-focus, a handheld instrument that concentrates sunlight for both practical and ceremonial purposes. These focuses serve as signatures, their unique patterns of refraction identifying their owners as surely as fingerprints.

During the twice-yearly Alignment Festivals, the entire community gathers around the Light Well, positioning ceremonial mirrors to create a continuous beam connecting the plateau's surface to Vaulthome below. These occasions mark the negotiation of resources and boundaries between the two interdependent settlements.

# The Tether

Between these worlds—one defined by perpetual illumination, the other by its near absence—runs the Tether, a complex system of connection neither fully natural nor entirely constructed. At its most basic, it consists of the main shaft—the Light Well itself—a perfectly cylindrical void ten meters in diameter that descends through hundreds of meters of stone. But this primary channel is merely the spine of a far more intricate network.

Branching from the main shaft are dozens of smaller tunnels, some barely wide enough for a child to navigate, others spacious enough for loaded carts. These secondary passages follow the plateau's natural fault lines, creating a three-dimensional maze that allows for controlled movement between worlds. Some channels carry water; others transport goods; still others are dedicated solely to the passage of light or sound.

The walls of these passages bear witness to their dual nature. Near the surface, they show the precise tooling of Sunfield engineering—straight lines and measured angles. As they descend, these marks gradually yield to the organic patterns of Vaulthome's wall-vine architecture, which reaches upward through the stone like seeking tendrils. At the boundaries between these influences, remarkable hybrid formations appear—geometric patterns executed in living tissue, or natural growth conforming to mathematical principles.

The most trafficked route between worlds is the Spiral Way, a gently sloping passage that winds around the main shaft in a mathematically perfect helix. Wide enough for four people to walk abreast, its ceiling is studded with light-reflecting crystals carefully positioned to illuminate the path without overwhelming those accustomed to darkness. Ventilation channels maintain a gradient of temperature and humidity, creating a transition zone where travelers can gradually adapt as they move between environments.

Traffic along the Tether follows strict protocols. Vault-dwellers ascending wear protective eyewear of smoked glass, increasing in opacity as they near the surface. Sunfielders descending carry standardized light sources calibrated to grow stronger as natural light diminishes, their intensity precisely regulated to avoid disrupting Vaulthome's ecosystem. Both communities enforce quarantine periods for travelers to prevent the transfer of potentially harmful organisms between environments.

The most significant connection between worlds happens through the Exchange—a chamber located midway between surface and vault, where neither community holds jurisdiction. Here, the plateau's stone has been carved into terraced balconies surrounding a central space where representatives meet to trade. Vault-dwellers bring rare fungi with medicinal properties, bioluminescent materials, and water-purifying organisms. Sunfielders offer drought-resistant seeds, preserved foods, and precision-crafted tools that don't trigger the wall-vine's aversion to metals.

Communication between worlds has evolved unique forms. Because Breathspeech cannot function in the dry air of the plateau, and because the subtleties of Sunfield light-language are lost in Vaulthome's dimness, a specialized pidgin has developed among regular travelers—combining gesture, vocalization, and symbolic objects into a trade language called "Middle Speech." Professional interpreters maintain positions of high status in both societies.

The most sacred connection between worlds occurs through the Calendrical Alignment, when the Light Well fulfills its primary purpose. For those seven minutes on the summer solstice, when direct sunlight reaches Vaulthome's Heart-Column, both societies engage in simultaneous ceremony. Sunfielders arrange themselves in concentric circles around the well's upper opening, while Vault-dwellers gather below. The momentary illumination of the Heart-Column causes a surge of activity throughout Vaulthome's living architecture—new growth buds, spores release, and the wall-vine's pulse quickens perceptibly.

This annual renewal symbolizes the fundamental symbiosis between settlements. The Sunfields, for all their mastery of light and open space, depend on Vaulthome's fungi for medicines and certain essential nutrients their surface agriculture cannot provide. Vaulthome, despite its self-contained ecosystem, requires specific wavelengths of direct sunlight to trigger reproductive cycles in key organisms, as well as the minerals that only surface weathering can produce.

Less visible but equally vital are the human connections. Certain families maintain "cross-world lineages," with branches in both settlements. Children born with eyes too sensitive for the plateau's brightness are sent to be raised by relatives in Vaulthome, while those born below with insufficient scent-glands often find their way to the surface. This genetic exchange has prevented either population from becoming too specialized, maintaining the biological possibility of migration even as cultural adaptations deepen.

The Tether serves not just as physical connection but as metaphorical anchor for both societies' understanding of their world. Sunfielders conceptualize it as the "root" that stabilizes their light-harvesting civilization. Vault-dwellers see it as the "breath-channel" through which their living home communicates with the greater world. Both recognize that their specialized adaptations to extreme environments are possible only because the other exists—together forming a complete system in precarious, beautiful balance.
