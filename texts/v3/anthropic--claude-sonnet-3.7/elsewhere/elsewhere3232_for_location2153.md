# The Surface Remnant

Above the mountain that houses the Underswell, a vast alpine meadow stretches across a high plateau. The air here is thin, crisp, and carries the scent of wild thyme and glacier lily. Most travelers pass through this meadow without notice, experiencing only a beautiful landscape. Some, however, feel a subtle vibration through the soles of their boots—a barely perceptible resonance rising from deep below.

The meadow appears untouched by civilization, but this is an illusion carefully maintained. Stone cairns dot the periphery at precise intervals, weathered so thoroughly they seem like natural formations. Each contains a small channel that descends into darkness, carrying sound from above to below and occasionally, in moments of perfect atmospheric stillness, allowing the faintest echoes of the Underswell's song to reach the surface.

At the meadow's center stands what locals call the Vestige—a circular arrangement of seven pillars, each twice the height of a person. They're made of the same stone as the Underswell's chamber, but exposed to centuries of wind, rain, and frost that have carved deep channels into their surfaces. These channels are not random; they form complex spiraling patterns that, when the wind blows just right, produce harmonic overtones that resonate with the subterranean symphony below.

The Vestige was once the grand entrance to the water temple, a place where initiates would prepare before descending to commune with the waters. Now it appears to most eyes as merely an unusual rock formation, its doorways sealed by an ancient cataclysm, its purpose forgotten.

But the meadow has its own inhabitant—one who remembers.

The Keeper appears, at first glance, to be an elderly woman, small and bent with age, wrapped in layers of handwoven cloth dyed with subtle blues and ambers that seem to shift in hue as she moves. She tends a small stone hut near the Vestige, surrounded by a garden of rare alpine herbs that shouldn't thrive at this altitude yet somehow flourish under her care.

Locals from the nearest village—a half-day's journey down the mountain—know her simply as the Meadow Woman. They bring her supplies each season and take in exchange her healing tinctures and weather predictions, which are unfailingly accurate. Some say she's been there for three generations, never aging, but such talk is discouraged as superstitious.

Closer inspection reveals oddities about the Keeper. Her skin has a translucent quality in certain lights. Her movements are too fluid for her apparent age, as if she's partially suspended in an invisible medium. When she speaks, her voice contains subtle harmonics that linger in the air like the aftermath of struck crystal.

The Keeper is neither fully human nor fully of the Chorus. She exists in both states simultaneously—her body primarily flesh but partially composed of the same water-matter as the Underswell's inhabitants. She was once a young musician who discovered the Underswell centuries ago and chose a third option beyond leaving or becoming a Remembrancer: to serve as intermediary between the worlds above and below.

The Keeper's hut appears simple from outside, but within, it descends into a spiraling chamber whose walls are lined with thousands of small crystal vials—each containing a water sample from the Underswell, each preserving a distinct variation of its song from a specific moment in time. These are her life's work: a physical archive of the Chorus's evolving composition.

The garden she tends is not merely medicinal but musical. Each plant has been carefully bred over centuries to respond to vibrations rising from below. Their growth patterns—the angles of stems, the arrangement of leaves—encode changes in the Underswell's condition. To those who know how to read them, these plants are living sheet music, a botanical transcription of the submarine symphony.

# The Passage Between

The connection between the Surface Remnant and the Underswell is not a simple matter of physical proximity. The cataclysm that sealed the original entrance created a complex network of interstitial spaces—not quite caves, not quite crevasses—that wind through the mountain's heart like the chambers of a nautilus shell.

These passages, known to the Keeper as the Resonant Ways, cannot be navigated by conventional means. They shift and reconfigure based on acoustic patterns. A wrong note sung at the wrong moment might cause a tunnel to seal itself; a correctly timed harmony might open a path that wasn't there moments before.

The Keeper makes the journey downward once per year, on the winter solstice when the surface world is at its quietest. She brings offerings gathered from the meadow—particularly resonant stones, herbs with unique acoustic properties, and occasionally messages or small gifts from those surface dwellers who have earned her trust.

The journey takes three days, during which she neither eats nor drinks, sustaining herself instead on the increasing density of sound as she descends. The passages grow progressively more liquid as she travels deeper—first humid air, then mist, then shallow pools, and finally fully submerged corridors where her partially aqueous nature allows her to breathe the water as it grows thicker and more syrupy.

Along these passages grow the Semi-Diaphanous Fungi—organisms that exist partially in physical space and partially in harmonic space. They appear as delicate, translucent mushrooms whose caps refract light into specific wavelengths that correspond to musical tones. The fungi serve as navigational markers, their specific arrangements indicating whether the Keeper is following the correct path. They are tended by tiny creatures called Pitch Mites—distant evolutionary cousins of the Measures—who adjust the fungi's positions in response to shifts in the Underswell's song.

The most remarkable feature of the Resonant Ways is the Echo Archive—a large, roughly spherical chamber located approximately halfway between surface and Underswell. Here, every sound ever made in either realm is preserved in perpetuity, trapped in endlessly circulating air currents that never quite dissipate. The chamber appears empty until a visitor produces a sound, at which point all historically related sounds emerge from the swirling air—echoes of echoes, conversations with the past.

It was in this chamber, centuries ago, that the Keeper made her fateful decision. After discovering the Underswell as a young musician seeking inspiration, she realized that its song was too complex, too perfect to be captured and brought to the surface world intact. Yet she could not bear the thought of its beauty remaining completely hidden. In the Echo Archive, she proposed a third path to the Chorus—to become a living bridge between realms, neither fully human nor fully of the water, capable of distilling aspects of the Underswell's harmony into forms the surface world could comprehend.

The transformation was gradual, taking decades as her body slowly incorporated the water-matter of the Underswell while maintaining enough of her humanity to survive in the thin mountain air. During this time, she learned the ancient art of water-singing from the Chorus and the forgotten science of harmonic architecture from the Remembrancers.

Today, the Keeper serves multiple vital functions. For the surface world, she translates aspects of the Underswell's knowledge into healing remedies and weather predictions. For the Underswell, she provides a tenuous connection to the changing world above, bringing news of human developments that might eventually affect their secluded sanctuary.

Most importantly, she identifies rare individuals with the potential to understand the Underswell's significance—those whose souls resonate at compatible frequencies. These selected few she guides down through the Resonant Ways to experience the chamber firsthand and make their own choice: to leave enriched, to remain as a Remembrancer, or perhaps, once in many generations, to train as her eventual replacement.

For while the Chorus is functionally immortal, the Keeper's hybrid existence has limits. Every few centuries, she must find a successor willing to undertake the slow transformation and assume responsibility for maintaining the harmonic balance between worlds—ensuring that even as civilizations rise and fall above, the ancient song below continues uninterrupted, its perfect acoustics preserving the possibility of communion between the realms of air and water, surface and depth, ephemerality and permanence.
