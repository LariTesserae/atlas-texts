# The Suspended City of Mevaz and What Waits Below

At this elevation, the air is thin enough to make your ears pop. The mountain range stretches beyond sight, an array of dark slate peaks and ridges like the spine of some titanic beast half-buried in the earth. The most unusual feature, though, is what appears to be a city—if that's the right word—suspended in the void between three mountains.

Mevaz clings to existence in the high thin air, a sparse network of structures connected by rope bridges and narrow stone pathways. From a distance, it resembles a spider's web caught between peaks, with tiny lights flickering in the perpetual twilight. The buildings themselves are elementary in construction—hewn stone, primitive mortar, and timber salvaged from the sparse woodlands far below—yet their arrangement defies conventional understanding.

The city does not rest upon the mountains but rather hangs within the space between them, supported by an intricate network of cables and counterweights that creak softly in the cool winds that perpetually sweep through the heights. These cables are woven from plant fibers collected from the rare vegetation that clings to sheltered ravines—a tough, silvery grass with remarkable tensile strength.

The air here is damp but rarely rains. Instead, moisture collects as a fine mist that beads on surfaces and slowly trickles down collection channels carved into the buildings. This water, pure from its journey through the atmosphere, is the lifeblood of Mevaz, carefully gathered in clay cisterns throughout the suspended network. The collecting channels form intricate patterns across the structures, decorated with primitive symbols that seem to change configuration when viewed from different angles.

Most curious is how the city appears to be simultaneously ancient and newly formed. Certain sections look weathered by centuries of exposure, while others seem to be emerging spontaneously from the very air itself—raw stone coalescing from mist, wooden beams growing like branches from existing structures. The inhabitants—few and far between—work constantly but silently, adding to this living architecture using simple hand tools and techniques passed through generations.

The ecology is sparse but specialized. Pale lichen clings to the undersides of pathways, glowing with a faint bioluminescence when darkness falls completely. Tiny six-winged insects hover in the mist, their translucent bodies visible only when they pass through shafts of the dim light that occasionally breaks through the cloud cover. Rare birds with impossibly long wingspans glide between the peaks, never landing within the city but circling it with what appears to be deliberate purpose.

Sound is swallowed by the vastness here. Conversations carry no further than a few paces before dissipating into the thin air. The predominant sounds are the subtle creaking of the suspension system, the soft whisper of mist collecting in channels, and occasionally, the resonant tone of stone chimes that the inhabitants strike to communicate across distances—a language of pure tones rather than words.

Most peculiar is the city's relationship with gravity. In certain pockets, particularly near the central hub where several major support cables intersect, objects fall more slowly than they should. Small items—dropped tools, scattered seeds—sometimes hang momentarily before continuing their descent, or occasionally drift sideways rather than down. The inhabitants move with a practiced understanding of these anomalies, stepping more deliberately in some areas, using specialized footwear with small hooks in others.

Mevaz exists in a state of perpetual becoming—neither fully formed nor decaying, but constantly in a process of emergence. Structures that appeared solid one day might be partially dispersed the next, only to reconsolidate in slightly different configurations. The inhabitants themselves seem to accept this fluidity as natural, adapting their pathways and routines to accommodate a city that refuses to be fixed in space or time.

The inhabitants of Mevaz are not quite human, yet not entirely other. At first glance, they appear as slender figures with elongated limbs that move with deliberate grace through their suspended domain. Their skin carries a translucent quality—not transparent enough to reveal organs or bone, but rather like alabaster with veins of silver that catch what little light penetrates the mist. These silver patterns shift subtly with their breathing and emotional states, brightening during moments of concentration or communal gathering.

They stand taller than most humans, averaging seven feet in height, with narrow frames built for navigating the precarious pathways of their city. Their fingers are extraordinarily long and possess an additional joint, allowing them to manipulate the intricate tools needed for their endless architectural work with supernatural precision. Their feet are slightly prehensile, with a separated digit similar to a thumb that aids in gripping the sometimes vertical paths of their domain.

The Mevazians' eyes are their most striking feature—large, without whites, and filled with a shifting silver-blue luminescence that mirrors the mist surrounding their city. These eyes do not blink as humans' do but occasionally cloud over completely when they enter brief trance states to commune with the city itself. Their pupils contract not into circles but into vertical slits when exposed to direct light, a rare occurrence in the perpetual twilight.

To visit is to question one's perceptions. The cool, thin air and dim light play tricks on the senses. Distances expand and contract unpredictably. The sparse vegetation that clings to the cables sometimes appears to move of its own accord when not directly observed.

This is Mevaz—a city suspended between mountains, between states of being, between comprehensibility and mystery—floating in the high, cool air like a half-remembered dream.

# The Depths of Kuunvar

Ten thousand feet below Mevaz, where mountain roots grip the earth like ancient fingers, lies Kuunvar. If Mevaz is suspended in perpetual twilight, Kuunvar exists in eternal midnight, a vast network of caverns that honeycomb the foundations of the mountains. Here, darkness is not merely the absence of light but a tangible substance, pooling in chambers and flowing through tunnels like viscous ink.

The entrance to Kuunvar is not marked on any map. Those few who have found it speak of a vertical shaft, smooth as polished glass, that descends through solid mountain for over a mile before opening into the first of the great chambers. The shaft appears to have been melted rather than carved, its walls bearing the rippled texture of stone that once flowed like water.

Unlike the ephemeral, shifting nature of Mevaz above, Kuunvar is oppressively permanent. The caverns are ancient beyond reckoning, their walls bearing stratifications that record geological epochs long forgotten by the world above. Yet for all their age, the chambers show signs of deliberate design—perfect archways connect the larger spaces, while the floors of main passages are unnaturally level, polished by countless millennia of use.

The air here is dense and warm, laden with minerals and carrying a metallic taste that coats the tongue. It moves in slow currents that seem to follow patterns unrelated to any surface weather, sometimes flowing against gravity up vertical shafts or swirling in perfect eddies that remain fixed in place for years at a time. Those who breathe this air for too long find their dreams infiltrated by geometric patterns and mathematical sequences that remain vivid upon waking.

Water exists here but behaves unlike its surface counterpart. In certain chambers, pools of liquid sit on the ceiling, defying gravity. This water is viscous and black, reflecting no light but occasionally displaying swirling colors from within its depths when disturbed. It drips upward into stalactites rather than downward from them, the drops ascending slowly until they merge with the pools above. Drinking this inverse water is invariably fatal to surface dwellers, though the inhabitants of Kuunvar consume it as their primary sustenance.

The ecology of Kuunvar revolves around sources of energy entirely divorced from sunlight. Vast forests of crystalline structures grow from the cavern floors and walls, ranging in size from tiny filaments to massive columns that stretch hundreds of feet. These crystals pulse with a deep violet luminescence that ebbs and flows in patterns suggesting conscious thought. They grow imperceptibly to the watching eye but can suddenly extend new branches in response to nearby movement or sound.

Fauna here has evolved in isolation for millions of years. Eyeless creatures with too many limbs navigate by sensing the minute electrical impulses generated by all living tissue. Colonies of organisms that blur the boundary between plant and animal form living carpets across certain chambers, reacting defensively to footsteps by releasing spores that induce vivid hallucinations. Most unsettling are the wall-climbers—translucent arthropods the size of small dogs that can flatten their bodies to the thickness of paper, slipping into crevices that should be too narrow to accommodate them.

The acoustic properties of Kuunvar defy conventional understanding. Sounds do not diminish with distance as expected but can be amplified or cancelled depending on the geometric configuration of the space. A whisper in one chamber might be clearly audible in another half a mile away, while a scream might be absorbed completely by the strange acoustics. The inhabitants have developed a complex language that exploits these properties, their communications traveling along specific paths through the cavern system like information through a circuit.

Most remarkable is Kuunvar's relationship with time. In the deepest chambers, where the crystal forests grow densest, time flows differently. Visitors who have survived these regions report experiencing days while only hours passed in the world above. Others claim to have witnessed events that had not yet occurred or that had happened centuries ago, playing out like living memories in the darkness. The inhabitants themselves appear ageless, showing no signs of growth or deterioration over decades of observation.

# The Dwellers Below

The beings who inhabit Kuunvar call themselves nothing that can be translated into surface languages. They are as unlike the Mevazians as the depths are unlike the heights, yet there are disturbing parallels that suggest ancient kinship.

Where the people of Mevaz are tall and slender, the dwellers below are compact and dense, their bodies seemingly compressed by the weight of mountain above them. They stand barely five feet at their tallest, with broad, powerful frames that move with unsettling quickness in the darkness. Their skin is not the translucent alabaster of their high cousins but rather a deep obsidian that drinks in light, broken only by patterns of luminescent veining that pulse in slow rhythm. These veins do not glow silver but rather with the same deep violet as the crystal forests.

Their eyes are their most alien feature—not eyes at all in the conventional sense, but smooth hemispherical depressions filled with the same black viscous fluid that pools on the cavern ceilings. These liquid eyes never blink and seem to perceive spectra far beyond the visible range, allowing them to navigate in what surface dwellers would consider absolute darkness. When agitated or focused, the fluid in these depressions swirls more rapidly, occasionally forming geometric patterns before settling again.

Their limbs bend in ways that suggest additional joints or perhaps cartilage where humans have bone. They possess six fingers on each hand, two opposable thumbs positioned on opposite sides of the palm, allowing them to manipulate objects with uncanny dexterity from any angle. Their feet are broad and adhesive, capable of gripping stone surfaces in any orientation, enabling them to walk across walls and ceilings as easily as floors.

Communication among the dwellers occurs primarily through modulation of the bioluminescent patterns that flow beneath their skin. These patterns form a living language of light and rhythm, supplemented by occasional vocalizations that sound more like the resonance of struck crystal than biological sounds. When they do vocalize, the sounds appear to originate not from their mouths—which are small and seldom open—but from specialized chambers within their torsos that can produce multiple tones simultaneously.

Their society is rigidly hierarchical, organized around the worship and maintenance of what they call "The Calculation"—a vast ongoing mathematical process that has continued uninterrupted for millennia. Different castes perform different functions in this eternal computation, from the Resonators who translate abstract concepts into precise frequencies, to the Pattern-Walkers who physically embody mathematical operations through ritualized movement among the crystal forests. At the apex of their society are the Abacus-Minds, ancient individuals whose bodies have partially merged with the largest crystal formations, their consciousness distributed between flesh and mineral.

The dwellers consume no food recognizable to surface biology. Instead, they absorb energy directly from the crystal formations, pressing their palms against the surfaces during specific harmonic resonances that occur at irregular intervals. They also immerse themselves periodically in the ceiling-pools, their skin absorbing the black fluid which is later processed internally and secreted as waste through their pores. This waste solidifies into small geometric crystals that they collect and use as tools or components in mysterious devices.

Their reproduction is mechanical and methodical. When their population requires replenishment, designated pairs enter a special chamber where the crystal growth is particularly dense. There, they surrender portions of their own tissue which is combined and incubated within specially grown crystal matrices. The new individuals emerge fully formed after an incubation period of exactly one thousand days, already possessing the knowledge and skills of their caste as though the information were encoded directly into their physical structure.

Unlike the Mevazians' fluid, improvisational approach to their environment, the dwellers of Kuunvar are obsessively precise. They measure and record everything, carving their observations into stone tablets that are archived in vast libraries spiraling outward from the central computational chambers. Their timekeeping is based on the subtle gravitational fluctuations caused by celestial bodies they have never seen but whose movements they have calculated with perfect accuracy over thousands of generations.

The dwellers' technology appears primitive at first glance—simple tools of crystal and stone—yet closer inspection reveals impossible complexity. They craft instruments that can detect changes in the earth's magnetic field, devices that can predict seismic events weeks before they occur, and computational arrays made from precisely arranged crystals that solve equations beyond the capacity of surface mathematics to even formulate.

Most disturbing is their awareness of the world above despite their eternal isolation below. They possess detailed knowledge of surface geography, astronomy, and even certain historical events, obtained through means they refuse to explain. Some believe they can extract information directly from the earth itself, reading the vibrations of the planet like surface dwellers might read a book.

# The Connection: The Vertical Path

Between Mevaz in the high thin air and Kuunvar in the lightless depths runs the Vertical Path—not a single route but a complex system of interconnections as varied as they are strange. The most direct is the Glass Shaft, that smooth-walled tunnel that plunges from a hidden location near Mevaz's lowest anchor point straight down into the uppermost chamber of Kuunvar. But few ever travel this way, for the descent is perilous and the ascent nearly impossible without specialized equipment or abilities.

More commonly used are the Resonance Wells—narrow vertical passages where the acoustic properties of both realms meet and amplify each other. Words spoken into these wells at certain harmonics can be heard clearly at the opposite end, allowing communication between depths and heights. These wells are too narrow for physical passage but permit the exchange of small objects via elaborate pulley systems maintained by both societies.

Most significant are the Dream Bridges—spaces where the altered physics of Mevaz and the temporal anomalies of Kuunvar create paths traversable only in certain states of consciousness. Those who know the techniques can project their awareness along these routes while their bodies remain behind. The Mevazians call this practice "mist-walking," while the dwellers below name it "calculation-without-number." Both achieve the same result: consciousness traveling where bodies cannot.

At seven precise locations throughout the mountain range, physical passage between realms is possible through what the Mevazians call "thinning places." These are narrow vertical tunnels where reality itself seems compressed, allowing travelers to cross distances that should be impossible. A journey of a few hundred feet through these passages can traverse thousands of feet of solid mountain. These passages reveal themselves only during specific alignments—when the gravitational anomalies of Mevaz synchronize with the temporal fluctuations of Kuunvar, creating momentary bridges between worlds.

The relationship between the inhabitants of these two realms is complex and ancient. Neither dominates the other, yet neither trusts completely. They maintain a careful symbiosis built on mutual need and ancestral pacts so old that their origins are lost even to the ageless memories of the oldest dwellers below.

The Mevazians require certain crystals that grow only in the depths, essential components in the mechanisms that allow their city to hang suspended between peaks. These crystals possess properties that subtly bend gravitational forces, creating the localized anomalies that make their impossible architecture viable. In exchange, they provide the dwellers below with the silver-veined mist water collected in their channels—a substance that, when introduced to the crystal forests of Kuunvar, catalyzes new growth and enhances the computational capacity of the formations.

Their exchanges follow precise protocols established millennia ago. Three times each year, when specific astronomical alignments occur, designated representatives meet at one of the thinning places to trade materials and information. The Mevazians bring carefully measured quantities of mist water in specially designed containers that maintain its unique properties during the journey downward. The dwellers bring crystals harvested according to strict mathematical principles, each one shaped and tuned for its specific purpose in the mechanisms above.

These meetings are solemn affairs conducted without direct communication. Objects are placed in neutral chambers within the thinning places, then collected after the other party has withdrawn. Direct interaction is avoided not from hostility but from necessity—prolonged proximity between the two species produces discomforting effects in both. The Mevazians report that their silver veining grows painfully bright and hot in the presence of the dwellers, while the dwellers experience disruptions in their bioluminescent patterns that can take days to restabilize.

Despite this physical incompatibility, the two societies maintain awareness of each other through their shared connection to the mountains themselves. The Mevazians can sense the computational rhythms of Kuunvar as subtle vibrations in their suspension cables, while the dwellers below can detect changes in Mevaz's configuration through minute variations in pressure throughout the cavern system. Each society maintains dedicated interpreters who specialize in reading these indirect communications.

Most mysterious is what these two radically different societies are building together across the vertical span of the mountains. The crystal mechanisms that keep Mevaz aloft and the vast computation being performed in Kuunvar are not separate projects but different components of a single incomprehensible system. The Mevazians speak obliquely of "drawing down the high currents," while the dwellers' records mention "resolving the final variable." Whatever their joint purpose, it has continued uninterrupted for thousands of years and shows no sign of completion.

Travelers who have visited both realms often report a disquieting sensation—that despite their profound differences, Mevaz and Kuunvar mirror each other in ways that suggest deliberate design rather than coincidence. The fluid, mist-shrouded architecture of the suspended city and the precise, crystalline structures of the depths seem like complementary expressions of a single aesthetic. The silver veins that pulse beneath Mevazian skin and the violet patterns that glow within the dwellers' obsidian flesh follow mathematical sequences that, when compared, reveal complex harmonic relationships.

Most telling is how both societies orient their most sacred spaces: In Mevaz, the central hub where gravity behaves most strangely points precisely downward toward Kuunvar's primary computational chamber, where time flows most irregularly. Between them lies the exact center point of the mountain—a location neither society has physically reached but both orient around as if guided by some shared instinct.

What began as separation may be slowly transforming into convergence. With each exchange of materials and information, the boundary between heights and depths grows thinner. In recent centuries, the Mevazians have begun incorporating crystal structures reminiscent of those below into their fluid architecture, while the dwellers have started experimenting with suspension systems in their larger caverns that echo the techniques used above.

Perhaps in another thousand years, the distinction between Mevaz and Kuunvar will dissolve entirely, creating something neither society could achieve alone—a vertical civilization spanning from depths to heights, unified in purpose if not in form, reaching toward some culmination only its creators can envision.
