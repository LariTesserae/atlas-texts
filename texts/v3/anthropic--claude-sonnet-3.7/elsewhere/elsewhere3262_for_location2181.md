# The Skydome Forests of Eloria

Above the surface—far above—the Skydome Forests of Eloria extend across vast floating islands that drift in the boundless azure. Not high enough to breach the atmosphere's upper boundaries, but elevated enough that the ground below is a distant patchwork of blue and green, occasionally obscured by clouds that pass beneath like schools of gentle fish.

The foundation of Eloria is still being born. At the periphery, nascent islands coalesce from moisture and airborne minerals, gathering around crystalline nuclei that pull matter from the surrounding sky. These embryonic landmasses are thin-crusted and unstable, trailing wisps of soil and water vapor as they slowly solidify. Geological childhood.

The established central islands bathe in unfiltered sunlight that streams from all directions, reflected and refracted through crystalline formations that crown each floating mass. The air here maintains a pleasant crispness, as if freshly exhaled by the sky itself. This freshness carries a subtle sweetness that fills every breath—not saccharine enough to taste, but present enough to make foreign lungs feel light and invigorated. The clarity nurtures life in abundance.

Plants soar impossibly in this elevated realm. Trees grow inversely, their roots anchoring to the islands' undersides while their trunks and branches cascade downward before curving up again toward the light, creating massive, undulating canopies. Translucent flowers with prismatic petals bloom year-round, shifting hue with the angle of the sun. In the open spaces between islands, floating gardens of untethered vegetation drift in slow orbits, their hollow stems filled with lighter-than-air gases harvested from thermal vents at the islands' cores. The vegetation doesn't reach for the earth; it reaches for each other, creating a lattice of interlinked growth that forms natural bridges and sails throughout the realm.

The people of Eloria build with and around this growth. Their dwellings are impermanent structures fashioned from cured skygrass and carefully woven living branches, supplemented with resins collected from the sap of hanging trees. Each home connects to others through a network of swinging bridges and shared platforms, creating neighborhoods that shift and adapt like the winds themselves.

The settlement is dispersed—structures spaced widely, utilizing the varying elevations of each island's topography. Yet despite this dispersion, there is harmony. Wind currents are carefully channeled through fabric sails and living-wood funnels, directing airflow to collection turbines and ripening groves. Storage vessels are suspended from branches, their surfaces smoothed by generations of hands.

Animals have found their place in this strange ecology. Translucent fish-like creatures swim through the air currents between islands. Small, feathered beings with gossamer wings flit between dwellings, kept as messengers and companions. Colonies of sky-jellyfish float in the spaces between islands, their bioluminescent bodies pulsing with stored sunlight, releasing it as soft radiance when darkness falls. There are insects too—not the crawling, burrowing kind, but iridescent flyers that trace geometric patterns across the open air, carrying pollen between the floating gardens.

The people work primarily with resin, fiber, and crystal tools. Metal is scarce and precious, usually fashioned from meteoritic fragments found embedded in newborn islands. They weave intricate patterns into everything—tools, homes, even the very winds through special singing sails—recording melodies and knowledge in a tonal language that rises and falls like the islands themselves.

Sound behaves strangely here. Voices don't fade as one might expect in open air; instead, they seem to carry across impossible distances when aligned with the proper wind currents, while remaining inaudible just steps away when spoken across the grain of the breeze. Music becomes visible here, vibrations temporarily coloring the air in ephemeral patterns. The most prevalent sounds are the constant whisper of wind through the hanging forests, the gentle creaking of swaying bridges, and occasionally, the melodic humming of sky-jellyfish gathering before a storm.

What's most remarkable about Eloria is its dynamism. While maintaining a recognizable whole, the individual islands constantly shift position relative to one another, creating new configurations and relationships. The temperature cycles through subtle variations as islands rotate to face the sun differently. The winds change their courses seasonally. Even the population ebbs and flows as new sky-sailors arrive and others depart on voyages of exploration. It's as if time moves differently here, or perhaps moves with particular intention. The Skydome Forests exist in perpetual transformation, neither settling nor dissolving, a realm of eternal becoming.

# The People of Eloria

The Elorians are orchestrators of change, children of light who have never known true earth beneath their feet yet understand rootedness more profoundly than ground-dwellers ever could.

They stand taller than typical humans, often reaching seven feet or more, with willowy frames that move with fluid grace through the ever-shifting aerial environment. Their skin ranges from deep bronze to a luminous gold that seems to hold sunlight beneath its surface, changing subtly with the time of day. This skin is notably resilient and slightly reflective, with a faint pattern of lines reminiscent of topographical maps that become more pronounced when exposed to direct sunlight for extended periods.

Their most striking feature is their hair—not just on their heads but growing in fine, downy patches along their forearms and shoulders, ranging from pure white to vivid blues and purples. These strands are sensitive to air pressure and electrical changes, rising slightly before storms and lying flat during calm periods. Their eyes contain multifaceted pupils that can dilate in unusual patterns, allowing them to perceive subtle variations in light that signal weather changes days in advance. When they dream or enter deep meditation, their pupils form kaleidoscopic arrangements visible even through closed eyelids.

The Elorians possess unusually developed shoulder and back muscles supporting vestigial wing-like skin structures—not functional for flight, but highly sensitive to air currents and barometric pressure. Their fingers are long and triple-jointed, capable of complex manipulations necessary for their elaborate weaving techniques. Their breathing capacity is extraordinary, lungs adapted to the thinner air of high altitudes, allowing them to extract maximum oxygen from each breath while filtering airborne particulates through specialized cilia.

Their voices possess an unusual harmonic quality—each Elorian naturally speaks in chords rather than single tones, capable of producing multiple simultaneous notes that interact with the constant background whispers of wind. They communicate not just through words but through elaborate melodic phrases where meaning depends on progression and resonance patterns. The eldest among them can produce sustained tones that influence the very air currents around them, a skill used during critical navigation between shifting islands.

Their society revolves around what they call "wind kinships"—flexible social arrangements that change with the seasonal migration patterns of the islands. Unlike the fixed nurture spheres of the Ithmeir, Elorian social structures reconfigure several times each year as islands drift into new proximities. A person might belong to three or four distinct kinships throughout a year, each serving different purposes: creation kinships for artistic and technological innovation, harvest kinships for food gathering and processing, navigation kinships for steering the larger islands through control of massive sail systems, and dream kinships for spiritual practice and preservation of knowledge.

Leadership in Eloria is equally fluid. Authority passes between individuals based on a complex reading of celestial alignments and wind patterns, with each leader serving only as long as particular stars remain in certain positions—sometimes for months, sometimes for mere hours. These transitions are marked by the release of specially cultivated spores that create vivid, temporary color patterns in the air, visible announcements of the change to all nearby islands.

Children are highly valued but belong to no single family unit. They spend their early years in specialized nursery islands at the center of the Elorian range—stable landmasses with protective forest canopies and gentler wind conditions. At twelve years, they undertake their first "drift year," traveling between adult islands to discover their talents and affinities. By their twentieth year, most have established primary kinship patterns, though these will continue to evolve throughout their lives.

The Elorians measure existence through what they call "convergences"—periods when particular islands drift into proximity with others they haven't encountered for years or decades. Their oral histories are organized around these cyclical reunions, with major cultural developments dated by which islands were in constellation with each other at the time. Their lifespans are comparable to surface humans, though they age differently—remaining physically vigorous until very late in life, then declining rapidly once their inner ear structures begin to lose sensitivity to altitude and pressure changes.

When death approaches, Elorians undertake a final journey to the outermost islands where the atmosphere thins. There, in simple ceremonies, their bodies are given to the sky through platforms that gradually dissolve, releasing their physical forms as nutrients for the aerial plankton that forms the base of their food chain. They believe consciousness disperses into the winds, eventually condensing as part of the crystalline cores around which new islands form.

Their spiritual understanding centers on what they call "The Breath Between Worlds"—a philosophy that sees existence as continuous cycles of gathering and dispersal. The islands themselves are physical manifestations of this principle, coalescing from scattered elements before eventually dissolving back into the skies. Their rituals often involve controlled breathing techniques that synchronize with particular wind patterns, achieving states of consciousness where they experience themselves as both individual and dispersed simultaneously.

The Elorians maintain a complex relationship with the world below. Unlike the Ithmeir, who deliberately chose their underground realm, Elorian origin stories tell of ancestors who were once part of the surface world but gradually ascended over generations as the islands themselves formed. They view the ground with curiosity rather than aversion or pity—seeing it as the necessary counterbalance to their aerial existence. Select navigators make occasional descents through specialized vessels when certain islands drift low enough, bringing back objects and stories that are celebrated for their groundedness and incorporated into kinship rituals as reminders of origins.

Where the Ithmeir have achieved stability, the Elorians have mastered adaptation. Their genius lies in finding consistency within constant change, permanence within impermanence. They have developed a civilization that doesn't resist transformation but harnesses it, creating not a static society but a resilient pattern that maintains its essential character while continuously rearranging its elements—like a melody that remains recognizable despite endless variations.

# The Between: The Twilight Realm of Meristem

Between the depths of Ithme and the heights of Eloria lies Meristem, a transitional zone neither fully underground nor completely open to the sky—a twilight realm where the boundaries blur and elements of both worlds intermingle in ways neither fully understand.

Physically, Meristem occupies the liminal spaces where the uppermost reaches of Ithme's cavern system nearly breach the surface, and where the lowest-drifting islands of Eloria descend through cloud cover during certain seasonal configurations. Its landscape is characterized by massive stone arches, half-submerged caverns with collapsed ceilings that admit shifting shafts of light, fog-filled valleys where visibility extends only a few feet, and curious geological formations where the stone itself seems caught between rising and falling.

The flora and fauna of Meristem exhibit hybrid characteristics impossible elsewhere. Plants display both the luminescence of Ithme's fungi and the prismatic translucence of Eloria's floating gardens. Creatures bear adaptations for both above and below—like the mirror-moths with eyes on both sides of their wings, capable of seeing in darkness and bright light simultaneously, or the anchor-roots that extend both upward and downward from the same central node.

Most remarkable are the Twilight Pools—bodies of water that defy conventional physics, sometimes flowing upward in defiance of gravity, sometimes sinking impossibly deep into the earth. These pools appear and disappear unpredictably, lasting anywhere from hours to decades. Their waters carry unique properties, sometimes preserving objects placed within them in perfect stasis, sometimes accelerating growth and change, sometimes enabling communication across vast distances when similarly touched by different individuals.

Meristem has no permanent inhabitants—at least none that identify themselves as such. Instead, it hosts three types of transient populations:

First are the Way-Finders, rare individuals from both Ithme and Eloria who are drawn to explore beyond the boundaries of their native realms. Ithmeir Way-Finders typically belong to a secretive offshoot of the Stone Listeners called the Boundary Walkers, who believe direct experience of the "chaos above" is necessary for true understanding. Elorian Way-Finders come from a specialized navigation kinship known as the Current Divers, who seek knowledge of the ground's influence on wind patterns. These explorers sometimes encounter each other in Meristem, establishing temporary outposts for observation and exchange.

Second are the Twice-Born—individuals who have, through accident or intention, been transformed by prolonged exposure to Meristem's unique conditions. Physical changes manifest gradually: Ithmeir develop patches of reflective skin and increased sensitivity to light, while Elorians develop the ability to absorb moisture through their skin and see in near-total darkness. More profound are the cognitive changes—the Twice-Born report experiencing time non-linearly, sometimes perceiving events before they occur or reliving moments from the distant past as if physically present. Most eventually return to their original communities, but they remain forever changed, often serving as advisors and seers despite the unease their transformation evokes.

Third are the Ephemera—beings that seem to exist only within Meristem itself, visible under specific conditions of light and moisture. They appear humanoid but translucent, their features shifting between characteristics of Ithmeir and Elorian. They leave no footprints, cannot be touched, and communicate only indirectly—through patterns formed in disturbed water, arrangements of fallen leaves, or sounds that might be language but register just beyond comprehension. Some Way-Finders believe the Ephemera are manifestations of those who became lost in Meristem and fully transformed; others suggest they represent a third civilization that exists in a slightly different phase of reality, becoming fleetingly perceptible only in this threshold space.

The physical connections between Ithme and Eloria through Meristem are tenuous and temporary. When Eloria's islands drift to their lowest points during the Heavy Confluence (a celestial alignment occurring roughly every seventeen years), certain cavern ceilings in Ithme's upper reaches grow thin enough that they transmit diffuse patterns of light and shadow from the islands passing overhead. During these periods, specialized communication sometimes occurs—Ithmeir Stone Listeners can detect vibrations from Elorian singing sails transmitted through the stone, while sensitive Elorian navigators can feel subtle changes in air currents caused by the breathing patterns of large Ithmeir gatherings below.

More tangible exchanges occur through what both civilizations independently named the Falling/Rising Things—objects that pass from one realm to the other. From Eloria, crystalline fragments occasionally break free from island edges, descending through Meristem and eventually reaching the upper chambers of Ithme, where they're collected and treasured for their ability to store and release surface light. From Ithme, carefully cultivated spores of certain luminescent fungi are periodically released through vertical shafts, rising through Meristem where they're caught in air currents that carry them to Eloria's undersides, establishing colonies that the Elorians harvest for their unique properties.

There exist ancient legends in both civilizations about a time when passage between the realms was more direct. Ithmeir pictographs in their oldest chambers depict figures descending from light to darkness carrying "sky gifts," while Elorian songs recount ancestors who "rose from stone cradles into the endless blue." Both traditions include prophecies of a future Grand Convergence when the barriers between worlds will temporarily dissolve completely.

Some scholars in both realms believe this convergence has occurred before and follows a cycle so long that it passes beyond recorded memory. They point to curious similarities between the civilizations: the shared importance of breath in their spiritual practices, their complementary concepts of space-between, their parallel social structures that prioritize function over lineage, and most tellingly, certain identical patterns that appear in both Ithmeir carvings and Elorian weavings despite having evolved in isolation.

The most profound connection between the realms remains philosophical rather than physical. Despite their opposite orientations—Ithme's stability versus Eloria's flux, darkness versus light, density versus openness—both civilizations have independently arrived at an understanding of their world as balanced between opposing forces, recognizing that their apparent contradictions form a greater harmony.

In this sense, Meristem isn't merely the physical space between realms but the conceptual point where seeming opposites reveal their underlying unity—where the Ithmeir's perfect equilibrium and the Elorians' perfect adaptation are recognized not as contrary approaches to existence but as complementary expressions of the same fundamental truth: that the universe breathes in cycles of contraction and expansion, gathering and dispersal, being and becoming.
