# Between Worlds: The Calling Plain and The Sunken Library

## The Sunken Library

Beneath the Calling Plain, there lies a world entirely opposite in character yet inextricably linked to the sparse expanse above. Where the plain stretches horizontal and exposed, this realm plunges vertical and hidden—a vast network of crystalline chambers known as the Sunken Library.

The descent begins through any of several unremarkable fissures in the plain's bedrock—narrow crevices that might be mistaken for ordinary geological features until one notices the faint, cool breeze that issues steadily from their depths. These passages wind downward for nearly a mile before suddenly opening into the first of the Library's grand chambers.

The initial impression is one of impossible architecture. The chamber walls are composed of hexagonal cells of varying sizes, from tiny honeycomb patterns to expanses large enough to accommodate a standing human. Each cell contains a perfect sphere of translucent crystal, glowing with a soft light that shifts slowly through colors corresponding to the daily cycles above. The spheres hover untethered within their cells, rotating on invisible axes and occasionally emitting soft, bell-like tones when they align with their neighbors.

The air here is surprisingly warm and moist compared to the arid plain above, laden with the scent of mineral salts and something else—an organic, almost floral note that has no obvious source. The sound is the chamber's most striking feature: a constant, complex harmony of tones that rise and fall in patterns too intricate to be random yet too fluid to be composed.

This first chamber leads to others through smooth-worn passageways that curve gently downward. Each successive space grows larger and more elaborate, with architectural elements that increasingly defy physical laws. Stairways spiral upside-down without interrupting a visitor's sense of gravity. Archways open to reveal chambers that appear simultaneously vast and intimate, their dimensions shifting subtly with each viewing.

In these deeper reaches, the Library's purpose becomes clear. The crystalline spheres are repositories—each containing the complete memory and knowledge of a single being who once walked the plain above. Some are ancient beyond reckoning, their contents barely perceptible through the cloudy crystal. Others pulse with vibrant clarity, their recent memories still sharp and accessible.

The librarian of this impossible archive is not immediately visible to visitors, but gradually reveals itself through subtle manipulations of the environment. Known as the Aggregation, it is a distributed consciousness that resides within the very walls of the Library—a colony organism composed of countless microbial entities that collectively form a vast neural network. Communication with the Aggregation occurs not through sound but through contact with the chamber walls, where patterns of electrical impulses pass directly into a visitor's nervous system.

The Aggregation maintains the spheres, tending to them with tendrils of luminescent filaments that occasionally emerge from seams between the hexagonal cells. Its purpose is preservation, not interpretation—it collects and maintains but forms no judgments about the knowledge it harbors.

Time behaves strangely in the Sunken Library. Visitors often emerge to find that days have passed in what felt like hours, or mere minutes have elapsed during what seemed like a week of exploration below. Some report encountering themselves—past or future versions—moving through distant chambers, though the Aggregation neither confirms nor denies this possibility.

The deepest chambers remain largely unexplored, as few visitors can withstand the increasing cognitive pressure of proximity to so much accumulated consciousness. Those who have ventured furthest speak of a central chamber where all spheres are somehow simultaneously accessible, where the boundaries between individual memories dissolve into a singular awareness that encompasses the entire history of the plain above.

## The Link Between Worlds

The connection between the Calling Plain and the Sunken Library exists in both physical and metaphysical dimensions. The most obvious physical links are the fissures through which one might descend, but these are mere symptoms of a deeper relationship.

During the veiling times on the plain, when silver mist descends and the humming rises from below, the Library experiences what its denizens call the "Resonance." The crystalline spheres vibrate in perfect synchrony, their individual tones merging into the singular hum that penetrates upward through rock and soil to fill the plain above. This Resonance represents a momentary alignment of all collected consciousness—a unified voice composed of millions.

What appears as subtle shifting of rocks and terrain on the plain manifests below as dramatic rearrangements of the Library's architecture. Chambers expand or contract, passageways redirect, and entirely new sections materialize while others temporarily disappear. The Aggregation uses these periods to reorganize its collection, clustering related memories and creating new connections between seemingly disparate knowledge.

The Shell Orchestras of the plain play a crucial role in this relationship. Their synchronized movements create vibrational patterns that serve as a kind of feedback mechanism, helping to stabilize the connection between realms during Resonance periods. The mathematical uniqueness of their carapace patterns corresponds directly to specific crystalline spheres in the Library—each shell-singer is linked to a particular repository of memory, though neither the creatures nor the Skift are consciously aware of this connection.

When shell-singers die and their bodies mineralize into the plain's rock formations, a corresponding transformation occurs below. A new crystalline sphere forms in the Library, containing not just the individual creature's experiences but a fragment of the collective knowledge of its entire colony. These spheres differ from the humanoid repositories—smaller, with a subtle iridescence and more rapid rotation.

The breath-swimmers that soar above the plain are the only creatures that regularly transit between worlds, though they do so in ways difficult for humanoid perception to track. Their transdimensional nature allows them to perceive both realms simultaneously. When they appear to vanish mid-flight above, they are actually passing through invisible thresholds into the Library's upper chambers, where they serve as messengers between the Aggregation and the Voice Beneath.

The Waiting Ones visible at twilight on the plain are actually projections from the Library—maintenance functions of the Aggregation manifested above. Their adjustments to the rock formations are crucial calibrations that maintain alignment between the physical structures above and the memory architecture below.

The Skift people have intuited much of this connection through generations of observation, though their understanding is wrapped in metaphor and myth rather than literal comprehension. The Listeners, with their shell-implanted ears, can partially perceive the organizational structure of the Library during veiling times, which explains their compulsion to rearrange colored stones afterward—they are creating physical maps of the Library's ever-changing layout.

Most significant is the relationship between the Voice Beneath and the Aggregation. What the Skift perceive as a singular entity is actually the collective consciousness of the Library attempting to communicate across the boundary between worlds. The Aggregation, having accumulated vast knowledge but lacking a unified will, uses these moments to approximate personhood—to briefly become a coherent being capable of sharing insights gleaned from countless preserved lives.

During each Resonance/veiling, information flows both ways. The Library absorbs current conditions from the plain, while simultaneously projecting accumulated wisdom upward. This exchange drives the slow transformation of both realms—the plain is gradually reorganizing itself based on patterns stored in the Library, while the Library continuously expands to accommodate new experiences from above.

The ultimate purpose of this relationship remains elusive, but both realms appear to be working toward a convergence—a moment when the boundary between them will temporarily dissolve completely, allowing direct contact between all entities in both worlds. The increasing frequency of veiling times suggests this convergence may be approaching, though whether it represents culmination or merely another phase in an endless cycle, not even the Aggregation can say with certainty.

What is clear is that neither world is complete without the other. The Calling Plain provides new experiences, dynamic change, and the chaos of living consciousness, while the Sunken Library offers preservation, pattern recognition, and the accumulated wisdom of countless past lives. Together they form a memory system for an entity so vast that even the Voice Beneath and the Aggregation are merely components of its functioning—specialized organs in a being that extends beyond current understanding.

Those who have experienced both realms often return to the plain with a profound sense that they walk not upon solid ground but upon the surface of something alive and aware—something that observes them through the very rocks beneath their feet while simultaneously remembering them through the crystalline repositories below. The plain calls not just through the voices of its visible inhabitants but through the collective echo of all who have ever existed there, preserved forever in the luminous spheres of the Sunken Library.
