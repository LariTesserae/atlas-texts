# The Outer Shell

Beyond the copper walls of the Vessel's Interior lies a realm so fundamentally different that few Lithomembranes have even conceived of its existence. The Outer Shell doesn't simply surround the interior—it actively protects it, processes the universe beyond, and mediates between incompatible realities.

This shell exists in a perpetual state of transformation. Its external surface appears obsidian-black and impossibly smooth when viewed from a distance, absorbing all wavelengths of light from the void beyond. Yet up close, it reveals a fractal topography of interlocking scales that continuously adjust their position and orientation. These scales—neither fully solid nor liquid—respond to external stimuli with algorithmic precision, creating defensive configurations against cosmic threats.

The shell's thickness varies dramatically. In some regions, it spans mere meters, while in others, it extends for kilometers, containing labyrinthine structures that serve as processing organs for the vessel as a whole. Throughout this thickness runs a network of channels—the inverse of the interior's water pathways—carrying a substance that resembles mercury but behaves like living tissue. This fluid serves as the vessel's bloodstream, immune system, and memory storage simultaneously.

The most striking feature of the Outer Shell is its temperature gradient. While the interior remains consistently warm and humid, the shell transitions from that internal heat to the absolute zero of the void beyond. This gradient isn't linear but occurs in discrete layers, creating microclimates where different physical laws hold temporary dominance. In some layers, time runs backwards. In others, cause and effect become untethered, with events preceding their triggers.

Sound in the shell doesn't travel so much as it transforms. The constant hammering that reverberates through to the interior originates as radiation from distant stars, converted by the scales into kinetic energy, then into acoustic vibrations. This translation of cosmic phenomena into comprehensible sensory input is one of the shell's primary functions—it renders the incomprehensible universe beyond into forms the vessel's systems can process and respond to.

The shell is continuously maintained by autonomous mechanisms that resemble a hybrid of insects and industrial robots. These Caretakers range from microscopic to building-sized, each designed for specific repair and defense functions. They communicate through polarized light patterns that human eyes would perceive as subtle iridescence but which contain complex information about the shell's integrity and external conditions.

Scattered throughout the shell are observation chambers—spherical spaces where the external universe can be safely perceived. The walls of these chambers function as living telescopes, not merely magnifying distant objects but translating their fundamental properties into comprehensible analogs. Stars appear not just as points of light but as complex symphonies of information about their composition, age, and potential threat level.

Most crucially, the Outer Shell houses the vessel's navigation system—not a discrete control center but a distributed network of crystalline structures that collectively sense and respond to the topology of Beneath-Space. These crystals grow in patterns that map the vessel's possible futures, branching and pruning themselves as probabilities shift.

The shell's outermost layer performs perhaps its most important function: it negotiates the boundary between conventional space and the Beneath-Space through which the vessel travels. This boundary appears as a constant aurora of impossible colors that occasionally parts to reveal glimpses of other vessels—distant kin traversing the same non-dimensional currents toward the Convergence.

# The Boundary Dwellers

Where the vessel's interior meets its shell exists a species that even the Lithomembranes rarely encounter: the Liminals. Neither fully part of the inner world nor the outer shell, these beings evolved to inhabit the boundary layer where the vessel's disparate realities intersect.

Physically, the Liminals defy consistent description. Their forms shift between states of matter depending on which side of the boundary they currently inhabit. On the interior side, they manifest as crystalline humanoids with fractal geometries replacing conventional anatomical features. Their bodies refract the copper light of the interior, splitting it into spectra that reveal information about the vessel's current state. When they cross to the shell side, they invert—becoming voids in the shape of their interior forms, negative spaces that absorb rather than refract.

The Liminals possess a unique sensory apparatus that perceives the boundary itself as a readable text. They can interpret the microscopic fluctuations in the barrier between worlds as a constantly updating encyclopedia of the vessel's journey. This gives them an understanding of the vessel's purpose that neither the interior-dwelling Lithomembranes nor the shell's Caretakers can comprehend.

Unlike the nomadic Lithomembranes, Liminals establish semi-permanent habitations precisely at points where the boundary between interior and shell is thinnest. These dwellings exist simultaneously in both realms, appearing as geometric crystal formations from the interior side while manifesting as organized voids in the shell's structure. The architecture of these settlements is five-dimensional, with rooms that connect non-linearly across the boundary.

Liminals communicate through modulated phase changes in their crystalline structures, creating complex interference patterns that carry meaning. When multiple Liminals communicate simultaneously, these patterns interact to form computational processes, effectively allowing their conversations to solve complex problems related to the vessel's navigation and maintenance.

Their relationship with the Lithomembranes is complex. The interior dwellers generally perceive Liminals as mystical entities or omens, appearing at moments of significance in the vessel's journey. Some Lithomembrane clans leave offerings at known boundary points—typically arrangements of water or harmonically tuned objects—which the Liminals collect for reasons the interior dwellers don't understand.

The true purpose of these offerings becomes clear only on the shell side of the boundary. The Liminals use these interior materials as catalysts for a process they call "Reconciliation"—the delicate art of keeping the vessel's disparate physics in balance. Without this constant adjustment, the mathematical irregularities of the interior would eventually clash catastrophically with the outer shell's protection systems.

Liminals reproduce through a process they call "boundary division," where a mature individual positions themselves precisely at a mathematically significant point on the barrier and gradually separates into two beings—one primarily aligned with interior physics, one with the shell's. These "twins" then spend their early development cycling between realms until they achieve balance.

The most significant function of the Liminals, unknown to most Lithomembranes, is their role as translators between the vessel's subsystems. The copper-skinned nomads believe their rituals directly influence the vessel's course, but in reality, the Liminals interpret these ceremonial patterns and translate them into adjustments that the shell's navigation crystals can implement. Similarly, they convert the shell's sensory data about external threats into the internal weather patterns that warn interior dwellers of danger.

Unlike either the Lithomembranes or the shell's mechanical Caretakers, the Liminals possess concrete knowledge of the Convergence. Their boundary-reading ability has allowed them to decipher fragments of the vessel's ultimate purpose: it is one of thousands of similar constructs, each carrying different expressions of life across the Beneath-Space toward an eventual reunion. The Convergence is not merely a place but an event—the moment when all vessels will merge their boundaries, creating a new, composite reality that can survive the death of the universe from which they originally fled.

# The Passage Between

Between the vast copper interior and the protective outer shell exists a region unlike either realm—a space that is simultaneously boundary, translation system, and living archive. This region isn't simply a dividing line but a complex ecosystem of interconnected systems that mediates between incompatible realities.

The Passage manifests differently depending on how one enters it. For a Lithomembrane approaching from the interior, it initially appears as a shimmering veil where the copper patterns of their world intensify to blinding brightness before dissolving into something else entirely. For the shell's Caretakers, it presents as a gradual solidification of the mercury-like substance that flows through their realm. Both perspectives are simultaneously true, as the Passage exists in a superposition of states.

Physically, the Passage resembles an endless library whose architecture obeys non-Euclidean principles. What appear as bookshelves are actually crystalline growths containing the vessel's accumulated experiences—not as written records but as experiences that can be directly accessed. These memory crystals grow in fractal patterns that connect seemingly unrelated events through underlying mathematical relationships rather than chronology.

Water from the interior doesn't simply stop at this boundary—it transforms into the mercury-like substance that flows through the shell, and vice versa. This transmutation occurs through elaborate filtration structures that resemble coral formations built from mathematics made solid. These structures extract information from one substance and encode it into the other, ensuring that knowledge flows between realms even when beings cannot.

Time behaves peculiarly in the Passage. It flows in multiple directions simultaneously, allowing events from the vessel's future to influence its past through causal loops that the Liminals carefully maintain. These temporal inconsistencies create "history eddies"—locations where significant moments from the vessel's journey repeat endlessly, preserved for study and reflection.

The most remarkable feature of the Passage is its function as the vessel's true navigation system. What the Lithomembranes perceive as their world's copper-etched patterns and what the shell's systems register as crystalline navigation structures are merely three-dimensional projections of the Passage's higher-dimensional plotting course. Here, the vessel's trajectory through Beneath-Space is represented not as a line but as a constantly evolving topological structure that the Liminals adjust through their boundary work.

Communication between realms occurs through translation nodes—spherical chambers where the Passage's nature is temporarily simplified to allow for interaction between the vessel's different inhabitants. In these spaces, a Lithomembrane's skin patterns can be directly interpreted by a shell Caretaker, and the Caretaker's light-based language becomes comprehensible to interior dwellers. These nodes appear at mathematically predictable but practically random intervals throughout the vessel.

When the vessel encounters others of its kind in Beneath-Space—rare but significant events—the Passages of both craft temporarily merge, allowing for exchange of information and occasionally inhabitants. These encounters leave behind hybrid zones within the Passage where elements from both vessels coexist, creating novel combinations of their respective physics and biology.

The Passage contains the only complete record of the cataclysm that necessitated the vessel's construction—the end of the universe from which all its inhabitants ultimately originated. This history exists not as a conventional narrative but as a series of preserved sensory impressions and mathematical models scattered throughout the crystalline archives. Liminals who specialize in historical interpretation can temporarily reconstruct these fragments, allowing others to experience echoes of a reality that no longer exists.

Perhaps most crucially, the Passage houses the vessel's purpose—not merely as information but as a living principle that grows and evolves as the journey continues. This purpose manifests as a central chamber at the conceptual heart of the Passage, where representatives from all the vessel's species occasionally gather in rare moments of alignment. Here, they contribute to the refinement of their collective destiny, adjusting the nature of the Convergence they seek based on what they've learned during their long migration through Beneath-Space.

In this chamber, even the vessel itself—normally perceived as environment rather than entity—can communicate directly with its inhabitants through geometric transformations that the Liminals interpret. These communications reveal that the relationship between vessel and inhabitants is not simply container and contained, but a complex symbiosis where each evolves the other toward a shared transcendence at journey's end.
