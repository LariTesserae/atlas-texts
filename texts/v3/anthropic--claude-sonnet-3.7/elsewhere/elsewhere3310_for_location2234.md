# The Nascent Hollows and The Divided Sky

Below the surface, an underground realm is taking form—the Nascent Hollows, a place of geological birth attended by the mysterious Underfold who shape and tend this emerging underworld. Yet above, worlds away though physically close, stretches its counterpart: The Divided Sky.

The Divided Sky is precisely what its name suggests—a region where the atmosphere has spontaneously stratified into distinct horizontal bands, each with its own color, density, and properties. These atmospheric layers aren't gradual transitions but sharply delineated bands with visible boundaries that can be observed from the ground. The effect begins approximately two hundred feet above the earth and extends upward for nearly a mile.

Unlike the thunderous cacophony below, the Divided Sky is marked by an unnatural acoustic precision. Each layer carries sound differently—whispers might amplify in one band while shouts dampen in another. Wind creates eerie harmonics as it crosses boundaries, producing sustained tones that shift with weather patterns. During storms, lightning doesn't flash but crawls horizontally along the dividing lines, creating luminous seams in the fabric of the sky.

The lowest band glows amber during daylight and deepest indigo at night. Above it, a pale green layer refracts sunlight into dancing patterns. Higher still, a band of silvery-white opacity often obscures what lies beyond. The highest visible layer shifts between vivid crimson and electric blue depending on factors no one fully understands. The boundaries between them sometimes waver but never dissolve.

Birds and insects navigate these layers with evident awareness of their properties. Certain species exclusively inhabit specific bands, developing specialized adaptations. Ravens circle the amber layer, their feathers taking on a coppery sheen. Translucent moths with extraordinary wingspans drift through the green band, becoming nearly invisible within it. Stranger creatures—things that might once have been bats or flying squirrels but have transformed—dart between layers with practiced precision.

Most remarkable are the Aeroliths: floating islands of earth and stone suspended at the boundaries between atmospheric layers. These range from boulder-sized chunks to massive platforms capable of supporting vegetation and small pools of collected rainwater. They move with glacial slowness, orbiting invisible anchors or occasionally drifting to new positions when strong winds overcome their mysterious inertia.

The Divided Sky has its own cultivators: The Windweavers.

Where the Underfold are beings of fluid form and stone-sense, the Windweavers are creatures of opposing nature—sharply defined silhouettes of negative space, moving voids that reveal distant layers of sky through their forms. They appear as roughly humanoid shapes but with elongated necks and limbs that taper into ribbon-like appendages. Their "bodies" are defined by borders rather than substance—moving windows cut from the fabric of reality.

They communicate through modulations in air pressure that human ears interpret as distant musical phrases or half-heard conversations. Their movements create visible disturbances in the atmosphere—ripples of density that spread outward like stones dropped in water, but vertical rather than horizontal. When they gather in numbers, these ripples form interference patterns that alter weather across vast distances.

The Windweavers tend the boundaries between atmospheric layers, repairing breaches and occasionally creating temporary passages. They cultivate the Aeroliths, encouraging plant growth and guiding their slow migrations. Most critically, they maintain the divide itself—the unnatural stratification that would otherwise gradually break down into normal atmospheric gradients.

Their "young" begin as tiny vacuities—pinprick absences that gradually expand as they absorb ambient energy. Mature Windweavers protect these developing voids within specially maintained atmospheric pockets where conditions remain perfectly stable. Unlike the patient decades of Underfold formation, new Windweavers develop over a single season, their growth accelerating dramatically during electrical storms.

Humans have built observation towers at the periphery of the Divided Sky's influence—stone structures with platforms positioned to penetrate the lowest layer. Those who spend time in these towers often develop a peculiar awareness of atmospheric pressure and can forecast weather with uncanny accuracy. A few rare individuals learn to detect the Windweavers' presence, describing them as "moving holes in thought" rather than physical entities.

## The Veil Between

Between these worlds—the forming caverns below and the stratified sky above—lies what locals call the Thinning. This is not merely the ground separating above from below but a permeable boundary where the influences of both realms bleed into ordinary reality.

The landscape of the Thinning appears normal enough at first glance: rolling hills covered in tall grasses, scattered groves of twisted oak and ash, small streams cutting through limestone outcroppings. But certain anomalies become apparent upon closer inspection:

The water in every stream or pool reflects not the sky above but one specific layer of the Divided Sky—usually the green or silver band, regardless of what actually hangs overhead. Plants grow in spiral patterns around certain unmarked spots, their stems and leaves subtly altered—grass with crystalline edges, trees with bark that ripples like water when touched.

Most telling are the breathing stones—flat rocks of various sizes that rise and fall almost imperceptibly, as if the earth itself is drawing breath. These stones mark hidden entrances to the Nascent Hollows, though not all can be moved or manipulated by human hands. Those that can reveal narrow passages that wind downward, eventually connecting to the developing cave system below.

Weather behaves strangely in the Thinning. Rain might fall in perfect geometric patterns. Fog forms in vertical columns rather than horizontal blankets. Wind blows simultaneously in multiple directions without turbulence—different air streams flowing through each other like currents in water. During certain lunar phases, gravity itself seems to fluctuate subtly, making objects feel momentarily heavier or lighter.

The human settlements in this region are small, impermanent, and adapted to these conditions. Buildings are constructed with flexible joints to accommodate ground shifts caused by the forming caverns below. Roofs are designed with complex gutters and channels that collect rainwater according to which atmospheric layer it fell from, each type believed to have different properties for agriculture or medicine.

The people themselves have developed customs at the intersection of both influences. They wear clothing with intricate knot-work that mimics the patterns of the Underfold's vessel arrangements. They practice a form of tonal singing inspired by both the resonance of the caves and the harmonics of wind between sky layers. Their calendar tracks not just seasons but the migrations of Aeroliths and the formation of major chambers beneath their feet.

Most significant is the Exchange—ritual points throughout the year when carefully selected items are carried down into the deepest accessible parts of the Nascent Hollows and left as offerings, while samples of water from different cave pools are brought up and released into the air using specialized clay vessels that aerosolize the liquid toward specific layers of the Divided Sky.

## The Connection

Though seemingly separate, the Nascent Hollows and the Divided Sky are engaged in constant dialogue through subtle mechanisms that most humans never perceive.

The primary connection is hydrochemical. Water that seeps through the porous limestone into the forming caves carries dissolved minerals that the Underfold process through their vessel arrays. This processing doesn't just extract what they need—it alters the molecular structure of the remaining water in specific ways. When this water eventually returns to the surface through springs and seeps, it evaporates with altered properties.

This modified water vapor rises to the boundary layers of the Divided Sky, where the Windweavers harvest it to maintain the integrity of their atmospheric divisions. The precise mineral compositions determine the colors and properties of each layer. In return, condensation from specific sky layers carries unique energetic patterns when it falls as rain, influencing how the caves form when this water returns underground.

The breathing stones are nexus points in this cycle—places where the pressure differentials between deep caves and divided sky create microscopic exchanges of air and moisture. They are, in effect, the system's lungs.

More profound is the resonant connection. The groans and cracks of forming caverns create subsonic vibrations that propagate upward, influencing the boundaries between atmospheric layers. Conversely, the harmonic tones produced by winds crossing between sky layers travel downward through rock, subtly directing patterns of stress and fracture in the developing cave system. The two realms are continuously singing to each other in voices too low or too high for human hearing.

During rare alignments—when certain Aeroliths position themselves above major forming chambers—the connection intensifies. Underfold rise nearly to the surface while Windweavers descend to the lowest atmospheric band. At these times, they engage in communication or exchange whose nature remains entirely unknown to human observers. All that can be detected are unusual phenomena: water that flows upward, sounds that leave visible traces in the air, stones that become temporarily transparent.

The people of the Thinning believe they live in a world being continuously created through the dialogue between depths and heights—a place where solid matter is learning from air how to flow, and air is learning from stone how to hold form. Their traditions suggest that when the Nascent Hollows finally complete their formation and the Divided Sky achieves perfect stability, some final transformation will occur.

Whether this transformation will unite the realms or further separate them remains the subject of their oldest and most persistent prophecies—stories told in settlements that themselves may not survive the next major collapse below or atmospheric realignment above. For now, they persist in their role as witnesses and intermediate translators, moving carefully through a world suspended between birth and completion, solidity and ethereality, darkness and divided light.
