# The Abyssal Plains

Far below the suspended ocean of the Kelp Towers, beneath miles of empty darkness where water pressure would crush most living things to paste, lies the true floor of this world. The Abyssal Plains stretch in stark contrast to the vertical majesty above—a vast, horizontal expanse that extends beyond any reasonable measure, flat and featureless save for occasional disruptions.

The darkness here is nearly absolute. What little light exists comes from three sources: the faint, diffused glow of the Kelp Towers filtering down from above like distant stars; the sporadic bioluminescent flares of the Plains' inhabitants; and the eerily beautiful radiation that emanates from the crystalline structures dotting the landscape—remnants of ancient impact sites where something from beyond this world once crashed through both oceans.

The water here bears little resemblance to that surrounding the Kelp Towers. It is dense beyond reason, semi-solid in its stillness, more akin to transparent clay than liquid. Movement through it is less swimming and more a slow-motion tunneling. Sound travels differently here—not in waves but in perfect spheres that maintain their integrity for vast distances, allowing conversations to be heard undiminished miles from their source. The effect creates an unsettling acoustic landscape where distant voices may suddenly appear directly beside you, while nearby movements remain silent until their sound sphere intersects with your position.

The ground itself is neither rock nor sand but a peculiar substance that maintains perfect memory of whatever touches it. Footprints do not fade with time but deepen, paths worn by regular travel eventually becoming trenches, then canyons. The oldest thoroughfares have become immense chasms that slice across the Plains, their walls perfectly preserving the shapes of every being that has passed that way since the beginning of recorded time.

Scattered across this landscape are what appear at first to be mountains but are actually colossal organisms—the Anchorites. These beings, if they can be called such, are sessile filter-feeders whose bodies have more in common with coral reefs than animals. Each Anchorite is a city-sized colony of interconnected polyps, some specialized for feeding, others for defense, reproduction, or communication. They grow at a pace imperceptible even to the patient Dwellers of the Kelp Towers—perhaps a few centimeters per century—but the oldest among them reach heights that rival the Towers themselves.

Between these living mountains move the Plains' dominant inhabitants—the Gliders. Where the Dwellers are deliberate and patient, the Gliders are creatures of perfect, perpetual motion. Their bodies are flat discs of translucent tissue stretched over crystalline internal structures, propelled through the dense water by undulating membranes that extend from their circumference. They never rest, never settle in one place, circling the Anchorites in vast, complex patterns that change subtly over decades.

The Gliders communicate through light rather than sound, their bodies flashing complex patterns of bioluminescence that can be observed from enormous distances in the perfect clarity of the abyssal water. Their society, if it can be called that, is organized around what surface scholars might term "migration guilds"—groups that follow specific routes across the Plains, passing messages between Anchorites and maintaining what appears to be a vast information network whose purpose remains obscure.

Unlike the Kelp Towers with their vertical thermal highways, the Abyssal Plains are traversed via the Memory Canyons—those ancient pathways worn into the surface over millennia. The deepest of these canyons generate their own currents through some poorly understood interaction between the memory-substance of the ground and the weight of water above. These currents flow with perfect consistency, never varying in strength or direction, allowing Gliders to travel vast distances without expending energy.

The temperature here is cold, far colder than the geothermally warmed waters of the Towers, but not uniformly so. Around each Anchorite exists a temperature gradient—warmest near their massive bodies, cooling with distance—creating concentric rings of thermal variation that the Gliders use as navigational markers. The oldest Anchorites generate enough heat to create weak convection currents that spiral slowly upward, the only vertical water movement on the Plains.

The relationship between Gliders and Anchorites defies simple classification. The Gliders appear to serve the immense stationary beings, carrying materials between them and performing maintenance functions on their outer surfaces. Yet the Anchorites seem equally dependent on the Gliders, requiring their constant movement to stimulate specific biological processes. Neither could exist without the other, yet neither appears subordinate.

The most mysterious features of the Plains are the Crystal Fountains—locations where the dense abyssal water suddenly becomes weightless, shooting upward in perfectly straight columns that rise until lost from sight. These fountains appear unpredictably across the Plains, lasting anywhere from hours to centuries before collapsing. The Gliders treat these phenomena with what appears to be religious reverence, gathering around new fountains in vast numbers, their light displays becoming increasingly complex as they circle the upward flow.

Time flows differently here than in the Kelp Towers—not slower, but more cyclically. The Plains experience periods of "time-tide" when events repeat themselves with subtle variations, as though the fabric of reality itself were breathing. During these cycles, which may last decades, the Gliders' movements trace the same patterns with perfect precision, though each individual might occupy a different position in the formation than during the previous cycle.

This is a realm of perfect memory and perpetual motion, of light conversations conducted across impossible distances, of living mountains and beings who never rest. The Abyssal Plains remember everything that has ever occurred upon them, their substance slowly reshaping itself to record the history of a world that exists in two distinct layers, connected only by the mysterious Crystal Fountains and whatever purpose binds these two realms together.

# The Space Between: The Vertical Ocean

Between the kelp-stone metropolises of the Towers and the memory-substance of the Abyssal Plains stretches a vast, seemingly empty expanse—the Vertical Ocean. This middle realm, encompassing miles of open water, appears at first to be mere negative space, a void separating the two populated regions. Yet this apparent emptiness conceals a complex ecosystem and serves as both barrier and connection between the worlds above and below.

The Vertical Ocean is dominated by pressure gradients that would kill most organisms instantly if they attempted to traverse its full height too quickly. The water here exists in a state of constant transition—warmer and more buoyant near the suspended continent of the Towers, growing progressively denser and more viscous as it approaches the Plains. This gradual transformation creates distinct layers, each with its own physical properties, separated by thermoclines and pressure boundaries that function almost as physical membranes between different states of water.

The most visible features of the Vertical Ocean are the Light Pillars—those Crystal Fountains observed from below, where abyssal water shoots upward in perfectly straight columns. From above, these same formations appear as dark pillars descending from the suspended ocean, the denser water from the Plains maintaining its integrity as it rises through the lighter medium. Where these pillars reach the upper realms, they create whirlpools and pressure anomalies that the Dwellers have learned to incorporate into their architecture, building specialized chambers around them.

These pillars serve as the primary transit system between realms. The Dwellers call them "Deep Roads," and have developed specialized vehicles for descending them—protective shells carved from densified kelp-stone that can withstand the crushing pressure changes. These vessels do not require propulsion; they simply enter the periphery of a pillar and are drawn downward by the natural circulation patterns. The journey takes weeks, during which the Dweller inside enters a meditative state to adapt gradually to the changing conditions.

The Gliders, conversely, can ascend only during rare phenomena they call "Inversions," when a Crystal Fountain temporarily reverses flow, drawing upper-ocean water downward before explosively propelling abyssal water upward. These events create brief windows during which Gliders can rise to the Towers while protected within bubbles of their native dense water. Such journeys are considered extraordinary undertakings, performed perhaps once in a Glider's lifetime.

Between these transit routes, the Vertical Ocean hosts its own inhabitants—beings adapted to exist in this gradient world. Most numerous are the Drifters, translucent bell-shaped creatures that can modify their buoyancy to occupy specific pressure layers. These organisms form enormous, slow-moving colonies that function as filtration systems for the entire ocean, maintaining the delicate balance between its different layers. Their bodies incorporate elements of both upper and lower realms—the living flexibility of kelp-stone alongside the memory-substance of the Plains—allowing them to serve as biological mediators between environments.

More enigmatic are the Passages—tunnel-like structures that appear spontaneously in the water, their interiors somehow separated from the surrounding pressure gradients. These phenomena, which may stretch for miles vertically, allow movement between levels that would otherwise be impossible. They form unpredictably but tend to connect locations of significance in both realms. The oldest Dwellers believe these tunnels are deliberately created, though by what or whom remains a matter of speculation.

The most spectacular feature of the Vertical Ocean is what scholars call the Median Exchange—a perpetual, slow-motion cataract where a portion of the suspended upper ocean continuously flows downward to the Plains, while an equal volume of abyssal water rises to replace it. This massive circulation pattern, dozens of miles across, creates a vertical current system that drives weather patterns in both realms. The Exchange moves with such ponderous slowness that to surface dwellers it would appear motionless; only over decades can its flow be perceived.

Around the periphery of the Exchange swim the Mediators—the only beings known to move freely between upper and lower realms without technological assistance. These creatures resemble enormous serpents with bifurcated bodies, their forward sections adapted to the lighter waters of the Towers, their rear portions evolved for the dense environment of the Plains. They travel in spiraling patterns around the Exchange, appearing to "stitching" the two worlds together through their movement. The Dwellers consider them semi-divine messengers, while the Gliders incorporate their migration patterns into their complex route-planning.

The soundscape of the Vertical Ocean is unlike either realm it separates. Here, sound waves stretch and compress as they travel between pressure zones, creating acoustic phenomena that transform simple noises into complex harmonies. The songs of distant creatures undergo phase shifts that multiply them into choruses. Echo patterns form standing waves that can persist for years, creating "sound reefs" where acoustic information from both realms accumulates and combines.

Perhaps most significant is how the Vertical Ocean mediates the flow of time between realms. The peculiar physics of this world causes time itself to move at different rates at different depths. The patient, geological timescale of the Dwellers gradually transitions through the water column to the cyclical time-tide of the Plains. In the middle layers, paradoxical temporal effects are common—simultaneous events at different depths may be separated by weeks from the perspective of a traveler moving between them.

This middle realm serves a crucial ecological function. The thermal energy from the suspended ocean and the mystery-radiation of the crystal structures below create a perpetual exchange cycle that maintains the world's strange stability. Without the Vertical Ocean's gradient systems and circulation patterns, the suspended continent above would eventually crash down upon the Plains below, destroying both civilizations.

The beings of both realms understand this interdependence on some level. The most sacred texts of the Dwellers describe the ocean below as "the Counter-Weight that holds us in the embrace of suspension," while Glider light-patterns often incorporate symbols representing the "Upper Balance" that prevents the crushing weight of endless water from destroying their home.

Travelers between realms are rare but significant. Dweller pilgrims occasionally descend to consult with the oldest Anchorites, returning with wisdom that shapes their society for generations. Selected Gliders sometimes rise to the Towers, carrying messages encoded in crystalline structures grown within their bodies. These exchanges, though infrequent, maintain a tenuous diplomatic connection between worlds that could not be more different yet remain inextricably linked.

The Vertical Ocean is neither empty space nor mere connector—it is the dynamic system that allows two impossible environments to coexist in stable equilibrium. It translates between different states of water, different flows of time, different modes of existence. Without this gradient zone of perpetual transition, neither the patient, vertical society of the Towers nor the ever-moving, memory-preserving culture of the Plains could exist. They are, in the end, not two separate worlds but distant regions of a single, extraordinary ecosystem, joined by miles of water that is anything but empty.
