# The Gardens of Eshalar and The Obsidian Foundry of Morvash

Beyond the eastern ridges of the Shoulder Mountains, where Eshalar's terraced waterways dwindle into the thin mountain air, the landscape transforms. The gentle slopes give way to sheer cliffs that plunge into a vast caldera—a wound in the earth three miles across. This is Morvash, the Obsidian Foundry.

Where Eshalar is defined by water, Morvash is ruled by fire. The caldera floor glows with perpetual heat, illuminating its depths even during the darkest nights. Vast sections remain molten, creating pools of magma that shift and circulate in slow, hypnotic patterns. The air shimmers with heat distortion and carries the sharp, metallic scent of superheated stone and mineral vapors.

Unlike the orderly, horizontal expansion of Eshalar, Morvash extends vertically—a city carved directly into the caldera walls. The dwellings and workshops are hollowed from the living rock, connected by narrow staircases and bridges of hammered metal that cling precariously to the sheer surfaces. From a distance, the inhabited sections appear as a honeycomb pattern descending in tiers toward the molten floor.

The structures themselves are austere and functional, their entrances reinforced with frames of heat-resistant alloys. No wood exists here; everything is stone or metal. Windows are narrow slits designed to minimize heat loss in winter and gain in summer. Doorways are low, forcing entrants to bow slightly—an architectural feature that began as practical heat conservation but evolved into a cultural gesture of respect.

Light in Morvash comes predominantly from below rather than above. The magma pools cast everything in a flickering orange-red glow that creates sharp, dancing shadows. Even during daylight, the caldera's depth means direct sunlight reaches the lower levels for only a few hours. This inverted illumination gives Morvash a perpetual twilight quality, with shadows that rise rather than fall.

The technological achievements of Morvash center around metallurgy and geothermal engineering. An elaborate system of ceramic pipes harnesses the volcanic heat, directing it to communal forges, bathhouses, and heating systems. These pipes, crafted from specially formulated clay that withstands extreme temperatures, form a circulatory system throughout the caldera city, painted with heat-indicating pigments that shift from deep purple when cool to bright crimson when dangerously hot.

Most impressive are the great foundries carved into the lowest inhabitable tier. Here, naturally occurring vents of superheated steam are channeled through precisely angled nozzles to create forges capable of temperatures unachievable elsewhere in the known world. The metals produced in these workshops have unique properties—lighter than expected for their strength, holding edges that never seem to dull, and sometimes exhibiting faint luminescence in darkness.

What Eshalar achieves with plant cultivation, Morvash accomplishes with mineral refinement. Their artisans have identified and cataloged hundreds of distinct ore types in the surrounding volcanic system. They can control the cooling rate of metals with such precision that they create alloys with properties that seem almost magical to outsiders—metals that remember their original shapes when heated, or that conduct sound but not heat.

The signature creation of Morvash is obsidian, but not the natural volcanic glass found elsewhere. Through processes kept strictly secret, the craftspeople have developed methods to infuse the molten stone with trace elements from the unique mineral deposits of the caldera. The resulting material—sometimes called "true obsidian"—can be drawn into threads fine as silk, formed into sheets thin enough to be translucent, or cast into tools with edges measured in molecules rather than millimeters.

Sound in Morvash is distinctive—a constant underlying rumble punctuated by the rhythmic hammering of the forges and the occasional hissing burst when water meets molten rock. Conversation is conducted in raised voices that develop a particular resonance among long-term residents. Music, when performed, incorporates these ambient sounds rather than competing with them—percussive instruments crafted from hollow metal chambers that amplify and transform the caldera's natural acoustics.

Where Eshalar's terraces catch the golden light of sunset, Morvash's aspects face inward toward the eternal glow at its center. At night, when the surface world darkens, Morvash seems most alive—the contrast between the inhabited sections and the molten center becomes most dramatic, creating a living mandala of orange light and black stone that slowly pulses with the breathing of the earth below.

# The Shapers of Morvash

The people of Morvash bear the marks of their environment in their very bodies. They stand shorter than the Eshalari but with powerfully developed upper bodies—broad shoulders, muscular arms, and hands that appear oversized with fingers thickened from generations of forge work. Their skin carries a permanent undertone of ash gray, darkest on exposed areas like the face and hands. Fine particles of metal and stone dust become embedded in their skin over time, creating subtle patterns that catch light with an almost metallic sheen.

Most distinctive are their eyes—slightly larger than typical human proportions, with irises that display unusual coloration ranging from copper-orange to a deep volcanic black. These eyes have adapted to the perpetual dim light and heat distortion of the caldera, allowing them to perceive subtle variations in temperature as visible auras around hot objects. Many Shapers can judge the temperature of metal within a few degrees simply by observing its heat signature without tools.

Their hair grows thick and coarse, resistant to the constant heat. Most Shapers wear it cropped short or tightly braided against the skull to prevent it catching fire during forge work, though elders often grow a single long lock that is ritually singed at the tips during important ceremonies. Their beards, when grown, are typically styled with metal beads or wire, often incorporating small carved obsidian pieces that record significant achievements in metalcraft.

The Shapers speak in deep, resonant tones that carry over the ambient noise of the caldera. Their language is rhythmic and percussive, with words that mimic the sounds of their forges. They have developed an elaborate technical vocabulary for describing states of heat, metal composition, and material transformation that has no equivalent elsewhere. Like the Eshalari's silent garden language, the Shapers communicate across the noisy caldera using a system of hammer taps on metal surfaces—complex messages transmitted through pattern and resonance.

Society in Morvash follows what they call the "Stratification Order"—a complex system that is neither purely hereditary nor entirely meritocratic. Families typically specialize in particular metals or techniques, passing down closely guarded secrets through generations. However, children showing particular aptitude may be apprenticed outside their family line. Leadership centers around the Council of Master Shapers, with each of the five major disciplines—Obsidian Craft, Alloy Formulation, Structural Engineering, Heat Channeling, and Pattern Welding—represented by its most accomplished practitioner.

Children in Morvash begin their education with heat tolerance training, gradually spending increasing periods near the forges from the age of four onward. By seven, they can typically handle hot tools that would blister ordinary hands, and by adolescence, they can directly manipulate materials that outsiders would find painfully hot. This is not merely conditioning but a physiological adaptation passed down through generations and enhanced through specialized mineral-rich diets and medicinal preparations.

Their clothing is minimal and functional—sleeveless tunics of heat-resistant fiber woven from rare caldera plants that grow in the upper rim, treated with mineral solutions that make them virtually fireproof. Many wear aprons of treated leather overlaid with small metal plates that record their lineage and specialization. Most distinctive are their gloves—custom-fitted second skins of layered material that protect while preserving tactile sensitivity, often incorporated with metals that conduct heat away from the fingertips during delicate work.

What outsiders find most unsettling about the Shapers is their relationship with heat and fire. Where others instinctively recoil, the Shapers approach. They speak of different heats having distinct personalities and purposes. Their highest art involves what they call "heat whispering"—entering a meditative state where they claim to communicate directly with the molten heart of the caldera, negotiating rather than commanding the transformation of materials.

The Shapers believe metals possess a kind of memory—that they retain impressions of their creation and subsequent forging. The most prized creations in Morvash are "awakened metals" that have been worked while the craftsperson enters a trance state, supposedly transferring a portion of consciousness into the material itself. These objects are said to develop symbiotic relationships with their users, subtly changing in response to how they're employed over time.

The most secretive sect within Morvash is the Order of Obsidian Blood, master crafters who undergo a ritual transformation in their final initiation. Through a closely guarded process, they infuse their bloodstream with microscopic particles of specially prepared obsidian, which permanently alters their physiology. Their skin develops a subtle black-glass sheen, and their blood, when rarely seen, has a darker, almost oil-like quality. These masters can shape obsidian with touch alone, their blood apparently creating a connection that allows direct manipulation of the material's molecular structure.

Unlike Eshalar's subtle relationship with time, Morvash embraces transformation and change. Their calendar tracks the caldera's cycles—periods of greater and lesser activity that correspond to deep geological processes. They celebrate phases of particularly intense volcanic activity as "Renewal Times," seeing destruction as necessary for new creation. Their mythology centers around the concept of "The Great Forge"—the belief that the world itself is constantly being melted down and reforged into new forms, with humans serving as both the crafters and the crafted.

The Shapers practice what outsiders might recognize as a form of ancestor worship, but with a distinctive twist. They believe the consciousness of their greatest crafters becomes part of the materials they worked most intensely during life. Complex forge rituals often involve invoking specific ancestors associated with particular techniques or metals. The oldest and most revered tools are believed to contain accumulated wisdom of generations, and apprentices often sleep with small pieces of metal crafted by their predecessors beneath their pillows to receive guidance through dreams.

Visitors to Morvash are rare and viewed with suspicious curiosity. The descent into the caldera requires specialized knowledge of paths that shift with the volcanic activity. Those few outsiders permitted entry are constantly monitored, offered restricted hospitality, and typically blindfolded during certain passages between areas. The Shapers freely trade their finished goods but jealously guard their methods, believing that their techniques are not merely practical knowledge but sacred truths revealed by the living heart of the mountain.

# The Trading Path: Between Gardens and Fire

Between Eshalar and Morvash lies a passage known as the Veil Walk—a natural corridor through the highest reaches of the Shoulder Mountains that connects these two radically different worlds. This narrow trail spans approximately twelve miles as it winds through passes and along precarious ledges, but the journey typically takes three days due to the challenging terrain and necessary acclimation stops.

The path begins at Eshalar's uppermost terrace, where the last gardens thin into hardy alpine meadows. Here stands the Departure Arch, a simple structure of fitted stone without mortar, entwined with a species of blue-flowering vine found nowhere else. The arch marks both physical and symbolic transition—beyond it, the Eshalari's careful water management no longer extends, and travelers enter what they consider "untended land."

The first section of the Veil Walk climbs steadily through increasingly sparse vegetation. The carefully channeled waters of Eshalar give way to natural springs that emerge unpredictably from the mountainside. These springs are marked with small cairns topped with distinctive blue stones, indicating water safe for drinking. As elevation increases, the temperature drops noticeably, and the humid microclimate of Eshalar dissipates into the thinner mountain air.

Approximately four miles from Eshalar stands the first waypoint—the Bridge of Mists. This natural formation occurs where a hot spring emerges near the path, creating a perpetual cloud of steam that obscures a narrow stone bridge spanning a deep ravine. The bridge itself is ancient, its origin uncertain, constructed of the same precisely fitted stones as Eshalar's oldest structures but weathered by centuries of exposure. Crossing this bridge marks the true departure from Eshalari influence.

Beyond the bridge, the character of the path changes dramatically. The trail narrows and follows the mountain's natural contours rather than engineered terraces. Hardy mountain conifers replace the cultivated plants of Eshalar, their needles adapted to the harsh conditions and thin air. The path traverses several steep sections where iron handholds—the first sign of Morvash craftsmanship—have been driven into the rock face.

The midpoint of the journey centers around the Equilibrium Cave, a large natural chamber where travelers typically spend the first night. The cave maintains a constant temperature year-round due to geothermal activity deep below. Here, the influences of both civilizations mingle visibly—the floor has been carefully leveled and sections terraced in Eshalari style, while the walls bear metal sconces of Morvash design. Most notably, the rear of the cave features a perpetual spring that fills a series of small pools before draining into a crevice that glows faintly with heat from below—water meeting fire in delicate balance.

The second day's journey continues along the mountain ridge, where travelers encounter the weather phenomenon that gives the Veil Walk its name. At this elevation, rapid temperature differences between the humid Eshalar valley and the heat-radiating caldera of Morvash create unpredictable weather patterns. Tendrils of mist form and dissipate with startling speed, occasionally becoming thick enough to completely obscure the path. These veils of mist are said to disorient travelers not just physically but temporally—local guides speak of moments when the mist parts to reveal glimpses of the path as it existed in other times.

The third section of the journey begins at the Whispering Col—a high pass between twin peaks where the wind creates a constant, hollow tone as it rushes through naturally formed tubes in the rock face. From this vantage point, travelers get their first glimpse of Morvash's caldera in the distance, often visible as a faint orange glow through the thinning air. The descent toward Morvash becomes steeper, with sections of the path now incorporating metal stairs and reinforced platforms crafted by the Shapers.

The final approach reveals increasing evidence of Morvash influence. The sparse mountain vegetation gives way to peculiar growths—metallic-looking lichen that clings to rocks near heat vents, and crystalline formations that catch and reflect the distant glow of the caldera. The air grows noticeably warmer and carries the faint scent of sulfur and hot metal. Natural rock formations give way to clearly worked surfaces, with directional markers made from polished obsidian that gleam even in minimal light.

The Veil Walk concludes at the Anvil Gate—a massive portal of black metal embedded directly into the mountain face, marking the upper entrance to Morvash. Unlike the simple, vine-covered arch at the Eshalar end, this entrance is an imposing demonstration of the Shapers' craft. The doors themselves, standing fifteen feet high, are covered in relief sculptures depicting the relationship between fire and earth, with inlaid veins of copper that seem to pulse with the reflected glow from the caldera below.

# The Traders: Those Who Walk Between

Neither fully Eshalari nor Shaper, the traders who regularly traverse the Veil Walk have evolved into a distinct social group with their own customs and adaptations. They call themselves the Veilwalkers, though others sometimes refer to them as "the Between People."

Physically, Veilwalkers exhibit characteristics of both parent cultures, modified by their unique lifestyle. They possess the height and long limbs of the Eshalari, but with the muscular upper-body development of the Shapers. Their skin shows adaptations to both environments—the blue-green undertones of Eshalar around their joints, combined with the metallic ash-gray patterns of Morvash across their faces and hands. Their eyes are particularly distinctive, with the larger proportions of Shapers but the unusual coloration of Eshalari, often displaying heterochromia—one eye amber or violet, the other copper-orange or obsidian black.

The Veilwalkers' most notable feature is their extraordinary adaptation to rapid environmental changes. They can transition between the humid gardens of Eshalar and the scorching heat of Morvash without the acclimation period others require. Their lungs process the thin mountain air efficiently, and their metabolism adjusts quickly to temperature fluctuations. These adaptations allow them to complete the Veil Walk in half the time others require, sometimes making the journey in a single, continuous trek without sleep.

Their clothing combines elements from both cultures—layered, adjustable garments like those of Eshalar but incorporating the heat-resistant fibers and metal elements of Morvash. Most distinctive are their boots, masterworks of cross-cultural craft, with soles of flexible obsidian mesh set into carefully cultivated living wood that continues to grow even after crafting, self-repairing minor damage over time.

Veilwalkers speak a pidgin language that merges the flowing, water-like speech patterns of Eshalar with the percussive rhythms of Morvash. They are typically fluent in both parent languages but prefer their hybrid tongue among themselves. They have developed specialized terminology for the phenomena unique to the mountain passage—over fifty words for different mist conditions and their effects on perception, and complex descriptions for the psycho-temporal disorientation sometimes experienced on the path.

Unlike either parent culture, Veilwalkers maintain no permanent settlement. They live perpetually in transit, maintaining temporary camps at established points along the Veil Walk. The largest of these, situated near the Equilibrium Cave, serves as their closest approximation to a home base. Here they store communal supplies, conduct trade among themselves, and perform the ceremonies marking major life transitions. Children typically remain at this camp until age seven, cared for communally, before beginning their training in the trader's path.

Socially, Veilwalkers organize themselves by "Caravan Kinships"—flexible groups typically consisting of seven to twelve individuals who may or may not be biologically related. Each kinship specializes in particular trade goods and follows its own schedule of passages between the two cities. Leadership within kinships rotates based on which environment they currently inhabit—approaching Eshalar, those with stronger gardener lineage lead; near Morvash, those with stronger Shaper blood take precedence.

Their relationship with the two parent cultures is complex. Both Eshalari and Shapers view the Veilwalkers with a mixture of necessity and suspicion. They rely on these traders to transport goods between their worlds but treat them as not quite equal to true citizens of either realm. Veilwalkers can never become master gardeners or metal crafters but are granted limited access to both societies' inner workings. They typically maintain small residences at the periphery of each city, never fully integrating into either.

The Veilwalkers' religious beliefs center around the concept of "The Third State"—neither liquid nor solid, neither plant nor mineral, but the transitional phase between opposites. They revere the mists of the mountain path as physical manifestations of this principle, believing that reality itself becomes more malleable within these boundary zones. Their most sacred ritual, the "Mist Meditation," involves sitting motionless within dense mountain fog while ingesting a preparation made from both Eshalari plants and mineral compounds from Morvash, seeking visions of what they call "the unified state before separation."

What the Veilwalkers trade goes beyond physical goods. They serve as information brokers, cultural translators, and diplomats between the two societies. Their neutral position allows them to mediate the occasional disputes that arise over resource allocation or territorial boundaries. They maintain detailed oral histories of relations between Eshalar and Morvash, recounting centuries of interactions through memorized stories passed down through generations.

The most valuable trade goods moving from Eshalar to Morvash include specialized plant fibers for forge filtering systems, medicinal compounds that treat metal-dust exposure, and preserved foods that provide nutrients scarce in the caldera environment. From Morvash to Eshalar flow precision metal tools for delicate garden work, heating elements for the colder seasons, and specially treated obsidian cutting implements that maintain molecular-level sharpness for plant surgery.

Beyond commercial exchange, the Veilwalkers facilitate a controlled transfer of knowledge between the civilizations. Innovations in water management from Eshalar might be selectively shared with Morvash's engineers, while advancements in material science from the Shapers occasionally make their way to the gardeners. This knowledge exchange is carefully regulated by traditional agreements, with certain fundamental techniques remaining strictly protected by each society.

Perhaps most importantly, the Veilwalkers serve as living proof that the seemingly opposite philosophies of the two cultures can coexist within single individuals. They embody the paradox of their world—that two civilizations with fundamentally different approaches to existence can remain separate yet interdependent, distinct yet ultimately connected through the thin path that winds between them, carrying people and goods and ideas through the veils of mist where time itself seems to blur at the boundaries.
