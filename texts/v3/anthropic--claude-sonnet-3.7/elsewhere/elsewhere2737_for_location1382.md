# The Depthless Wells of Kar Nomad

The first sign is the silence. Not the dampened stillness of Ven Pahzi, but an absolute absence of sound that seems to draw noise into itself like a void. It's as if the air has forgotten how to carry vibrations. This unnatural quiet surrounds a series of circular openings in the desert floor—perfectly round wells arranged in a spiral pattern that extends for nearly a mile across the cracked earth. Unlike the reflective terraces of the highlands, these wells absorb light completely. Their interiors reveal no bottom, no water surface, just an impenetrable darkness that even the harshest desert sun cannot penetrate.

Kar Nomad exists in the vast basin lowlands, separated from Ven Pahzi by a descending series of barren foothills and dry scrubland. While the highlands are perpetually shrouded in steam, this lowland basin suffers under relentless drought. The earth has hardened into a mosaic of fractured plates, their edges upturned like the scales of some enormous buried creature. The color palette here is monochromatic—various shades of rust and ochre, punctuated only by the perfect blackness of the wells.

The wells themselves defy conventional understanding. Though they appear to be excavated from the earth, there are no mounds of displaced soil surrounding them. Their inner walls are perfectly smooth, composed of a substance that resembles polished obsidian but lacks its reflective properties. Local legends claim these wells have no bottom—that they extend all the way through the world and into the void beyond. Scientific expeditions have lowered measuring devices on miles of line without ever detecting a terminus.

Around each well stands a ring of twisted metal sculptures—strange, anthropomorphic figures with elongated limbs reminiscent of the Pahziin, but contorted into impossible positions. These sculptures are not created by human hands; they emerge gradually from the ground over decades, pushed upward by some unknown force beneath the surface. The metal has properties unknown elsewhere—it remains cool even in direct sunlight and vibrates imperceptibly when touched, though no sound is produced.

Between the wells, the ground is inscribed with intricate patterns that become visible only at certain times of day when the sun casts specific shadows. These patterns form a complex mathematical language—concentric circles, spirals, and angular forms that seem to correspond to astronomical cycles. Scholars who have attempted to decipher these markings report unsettling dreams in which the symbols rearrange themselves into new configurations.

Unlike the carefully managed ecosystem of Ven Pahzi, Kar Nomad appears lifeless at first glance. Yet closer observation reveals extremely specialized organisms that have adapted to this harsh environment. Crystalline structures that might be mistaken for mineral formations slowly change position over weeks, orienting themselves toward the wells. Patches of what appears to be rust occasionally shift position overnight, leaving faint trails across the hardened earth.

At the center of the spiral arrangement of wells stands a structure unlike the others—a circular platform of the same black material, elevated approximately three feet above the surrounding terrain. This platform is perfectly level and remains at a constant temperature regardless of external conditions. Those who stand upon it report a sensation of weightlessness and disorientation, as if the pull of gravity has somehow been redirected. Many experience auditory hallucinations while on the platform—faint, rhythmic pulses that seem to originate from deep beneath the surface.

The air above Kar Nomad is peculiarly still. Weather systems appear to divide and flow around this region, as if repelled by an invisible boundary. Clouds that drift over the well field disperse or evaporate with unnatural rapidity. At night, observers have noted that stars directly above the central platform appear distorted, their light stretched into brief linear streaks before returning to normal as Earth's rotation moves them out of alignment.

Unlike the inhabited terraces of Ven Pahzi, Kar Nomad has no permanent human settlement. A small research outpost maintained by a rotating team of scientists stands at the perimeter of the well field, its structures deliberately mobile and impermanent. These researchers document the subtle changes in the well field, collecting data that consistently defies conventional explanation. Many develop distinctive psychological characteristics after prolonged exposure—a preoccupation with symmetry, difficulty perceiving three-dimensional space, and unusually vivid dreams involving vast subterranean architectures.

The most remarkable aspect of Kar Nomad becomes apparent only after sunset. As darkness falls, the wells emit an extremely faint luminescence—not enough to illuminate their depths, but sufficient to trace their spiral pattern across the landscape. This light pulses in a complex sequence that repeats exactly every 37 minutes. Measurements indicate that this illumination cycle has remained unchanged since observations began over a century ago, maintaining perfect precision without any detectable power source.

# The Fissure Lands

Between the steaming heights of Ven Pahzi and the silent wells of Kar Nomad lies a transitional zone known to local guides as the Fissure Lands. This fractured territory stretches for approximately seventy miles, its landscape progressively transforming as one descends from the mineral-rich highlands toward the desiccated basin.

The journey begins where the lowest terraces of Ven Pahzi terminate in a series of carefully engineered spillways. Here, the abundant waters of the highland plateau are channeled into narrow stone aqueducts that extend for several miles before abruptly ending at cliff edges. The water cascades into the first major fissure—a jagged tear in the earth that runs east to west for nearly twenty miles. The sound of falling water creates a constant low thunder that contrasts sharply with the hushed atmosphere of the terraces above.

Within this upper fissure, the water doesn't pool but seems to disappear into countless smaller cracks and porous stone formations. The air here is charged with negative ions from the falling water, creating a sensation of alertness and clarity for travelers passing through. The moisture supports a unique ecosystem of rapid-growing fungi that form brilliant blue and purple colonies along the fissure walls. These fungi complete their entire life cycle in just three days, releasing clouds of spores that drift on air currents and give the upper region a dreamlike quality.

As one continues to descend, the landscape becomes increasingly unstable. The second zone of the Fissure Lands is characterized by hundreds of smaller cracks in the earth, ranging from narrow splits that can be stepped across to yawning chasms that require dedicated bridges. These fissures form no discernible pattern when viewed at ground level, but aerial surveys reveal they roughly trace the same spiral configuration as the wells of Kar Nomad, albeit on a much larger scale.

The most treacherous aspect of this middle region is its inconstancy. The ground here is in slow but perpetual motion, with new cracks forming and old ones sealing without warning. The few established paths through this area require constant maintenance and rerouting by specialized guides called Pathfinders. These individuals—neither fully Pahziin nor typical lowlanders—have developed an intuitive sense for predicting ground movements. They mark safe passages with cairns built from distinctive red stones found only in certain fissures.

The atmosphere in this middle zone is in constant flux—pockets of highland steam mix with rising heat from the basin below, creating unpredictable weather patterns. Visibility can shift from clear to completely obscured within minutes. Sound behaves strangely here as well, sometimes carrying for miles with perfect clarity, other times being swallowed completely just feet from its source. Many travelers report temporal disorientation while crossing this region, with subjective experience of time stretching or compressing irregularly.

The final descent into the basin reveals the most dramatic feature of the Fissure Lands—an enormous gorge known as the Terminal Discontinuity. Nearly two miles wide and of indeterminate depth, this chasm produces a constant low-frequency vibration that can be felt through the ground rather than heard. Equipment failures are common here; compasses spin uselessly, timekeeping devices run erratically fast or slow, and more sophisticated electronics often cease functioning altogether.

Bridging the Terminal Discontinuity is a single massive structure of unknown origin—a span constructed from the same light-absorbing material as the wells of Kar Nomad. Unlike conventional bridges, this structure has no visible supports yet remains perfectly stable even during the seismic tremors that occasionally shake the region. The bridge surface is marked with the same mathematical patterns found around the wells, and travelers report similar sensations of disorientation and weightlessness while crossing.

The far side of the bridge marks the beginning of Kar Nomad proper, where the spiral arrangement of wells begins. The transition is abrupt—the chaotic, fractured terrain of the Fissure Lands giving way to the eerily flat basin floor. The persistent sounds and shifting atmospheres of the transitional zone surrender to the absolute silence of the well field, as if crossing some fundamental boundary in the fabric of the world itself.

# The Connection

The people of Ven Pahzi never speak of Kar Nomad directly, referring to it only as "The Absence" in their gestural language. Yet their entire water management system is oriented toward it. The terraces are arranged in a pattern that precisely mirrors the spiral configuration of the wells below, though this correspondence is only visible from a significant altitude. The waters that disappear into the fissures are not lost but guided—the entire hydrological system of Ven Pahzi is designed to direct specific mineral concentrations to specific wells.

The Stillkeepers, those mineral-encrusted elders who maintain motionless vigils in the terraces, are engaged in constant communication with whatever dwells in the depths of Kar Nomad. Their meditative stillness is not merely philosophical but functional—they serve as living conduits, their minds extended through the water systems into the wells below. The patterns that form on their skin are not random accumulations but precise records of these exchanges, a transcription of conversations that occur beyond conventional consciousness.

The central pool of Ven Pahzi—that perpetually steam-shrouded circle forbidden to outsiders—corresponds exactly to the central platform of Kar Nomad. These two points form the axis around which this entire system revolves. Within the central pool floats the most senior of the Stillkeepers, almost entirely transformed by mineral accretion, their body barely recognizable as humanoid. This being—called simply the Conduit—has not left the water in living memory and serves as the primary interface between the two realms.

The water that evaporates from the terraces of Ven Pahzi doesn't merely disappear into the atmosphere. The mineral content within each droplet carries encoded information—a molecular language developed over millennia. This information is gathered by specialized formations within the wells of Kar Nomad, which extract specific compounds from the moisture that eventually percolates down through the fissure system. What appears to be a one-way flow of water is actually a sophisticated communication network.

The plants cultivated in the terraces are not merely industrial resources but biological processors. Their growth patterns, the chemical compositions of their oils and fibers, even their cellular structures are responses to signals received from Kar Nomad through the water system. The Pahziin have learned to "read" these plants, harvesting them not just for material use but for the information they contain. The papers and textiles produced from these plants carry embedded data that influences the society's decisions and development.

The absence of animal life in Ven Pahzi is no accident. Early in their history, the Pahziin discovered that the presence of certain neural architectures—particularly those of vertebrates—created interference in the communication system between the terraces and wells. Over generations, they systematically removed all such life forms from their environment, gradually modifying their own neural structures to minimize disruption. The protective oils they exude serve not just as solar protection but as psychic insulation, preventing their individual thoughts from contaminating the larger information exchange.

The industrial machinery of Ven Pahzi, powered by water turbines, is not primarily for manufacturing but for amplification. The mechanical systems enhance and focus the signals passing between the terraces and wells, acting as a form of technological meditation. The papers and textiles they produce are incidental products—the true purpose is the maintenance and refinement of the communication apparatus. The occasional mechanical groans are not malfunctions but deliberate modulations, physical expressions of particularly complex transmissions.

The mineral patterns inscribed on the historical records of the Pahziin are not just documentation but predictive models. By tracking the accumulation rates and formation patterns over thousands of cycles, they have developed a system that can anticipate changes in both Ven Pahzi and Kar Nomad. These predictions guide their water management strategies and even their reproductive cycles—children are born in response to projected needs for specific neural configurations in the communication network.

Travelers between the two regions sometimes report unusual experiences—memories that don't belong to them, sudden insights into mathematical concepts they never studied, dreams of terraced pools or bottomless wells depending on their direction of travel. The Pathfinders who guide these journeys are actually specialized filters, their unique neurophysiology allowing them to absorb and neutralize the psychic residue that might otherwise contaminate the communication system. They are neither fully human nor entirely integrated into the larger consciousness that flows between the heights and depths.

What passes between Ven Pahzi and Kar Nomad is nothing less than a continuous negotiation of reality itself. The Pahziin believe—and their records suggest—that the balance maintained through their water management system prevents some form of collapse or phase transition that would irrevocably alter the nature of existence in their region. The wells are not merely geological features but boundary conditions—points where the fabric of space-time has worn dangerously thin. The carefully maintained terraces create a counterbalancing pressure through their precisely calibrated mineral concentrations and psychic resonances.

Whether the entity or entities dwelling in the depths of Kar Nomad are malevolent, benevolent, or simply alien beyond human conception remains unknown. The Pahziin themselves have long transcended such binary classifications in their understanding. Their society exists not in opposition to whatever dwells below but in conversation with it—a dialogue conducted through water, mineral, plant, and consciousness that has continued uninterrupted for millennia, preserving a equilibrium so delicate that its mechanisms remain invisible to all but the most dedicated observers.
