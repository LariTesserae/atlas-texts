[A distant, echoing pulse begins, like water dripping in a vast cavern]

[Static crackles, then resolves into a voice that seems to be speaking through water]

This is the voice-that-flows-between-stones. This is the word-that-carries-through-chambers.

We are sending this message across the void between worlds. We are the People of the Tiers. We are the Keepers of the Sacred Waters.

[Sound of multiple voices whispering, overlapping in harmony]

Listen carefully, for water carries truth but distorts meaning. The transmission may fragment as it passes through the membrane between our existences.

[Single melodic whistle rises above the whispers]

Our pools are emptying. Three flows ago, the deepest channel began to run silver instead of blue. The elders say this has happened only twice in our histories—once when the mountain shifted, once when strangers came with metal tools.

[Sound of water being poured from vessel to vessel]

The channel-keepers have followed the silver trace to its source. The western springs now carry a taste like dying stars. The glowing salamanders turn belly-up when they touch it. Seven children on the lower tiers have dreamed the same dream—water flowing upward, defying the sacred gravity that orders our world.

[A chorus of voices produces a harmonic tone similar to fingers on wet crystal]

We have consulted the stone-memories. The eldest channel-keeper, she of the white hair that has never been cut in ninety-three cycles, placed her hands in the oldest pool and heard the voices of those who built the first tier. They speak of a time before our time, when the mountain held something other than water.

[Sound changes to rapid droplets, like urgent code]

Strangers have been seen on the passes below. They carry instruments that shine like the silver in our waters. They do not approach with open hands seeking the ceremonial cup. They map. They measure. They take samples in glass vessels that catch the light like tears.

[Deep rumbling sound, like movement deep within stone]

The elders debate. Some say we should welcome these strangers, learn their knowledge of what poisons our waters. Others whisper of the old defense—to collapse the lower channels, isolate the upper tiers, become fully of the mountain until the danger passes.

[Sound of multiple whistles exchanging complex signals]

We have sent our fastest climbers to the peak of Arat, beyond our uppermost tier. They have seen machines—vast, gleaming machines—in the valley beyond the northern ridge. These machines drink from the earth. They pull something dark and thick from deep below.

[Silence, then the sound of a single stone dropping into deep water]

The youngest children can no longer see the glow of the waters at night. Only the elders' eyes still perceive the luminescence. With each passing night, more of our light fades.

[Voices begin a chant that rises and falls like waves]

We are the people who learned to live with water, not against it. We are the people who turned stone into a vessel for life. We are the people who know that all things flow downward eventually, returning to their source.

[Sound of many hands disturbing water simultaneously]

Today we made a decision. We will not collapse the channels. We will not hide within the mountain.

[Rhythmic tapping on hollow stone increases in tempo]

We have begun to follow the waters to their source—not the springs within our mountain, but beyond, to the true beginning. The channel-keepers say all waters are one water. If silver poison enters our pools, we must find where it first touches the sacred flow.

[Sound of deep, labored breathing, as if climbing]

Our strongest are preparing for a journey unlike any in our histories. We will go beyond the protection of our tiers. We will follow the corruption to its source.

[Sudden splash, then silence]

The salamanders have shown us the way. Last night, as the elders kept vigil at the highest pool, the largest salamander—old as memory, pale as moonlight—crawled from the water and began to move with purpose toward the unexplored western face of the mountain.

[Low humming builds gradually]

We go now to follow it. Two hundred of us. The youngest who can climb, the eldest who remember the pattern-songs of defense. We carry only what water we can hold in our vessels, only what food fits in our pockets.

[Sound of many feet on stone, echoing]

If you receive this transmission, know that Sahl Nahar still stands. Know that its people still honor the ancient covenant between stone and water and flesh. Know that we understand what many have forgotten—that to drink from the earth without giving back is to break the circle that sustains all worlds.

[The dripping pulse returns, slower now]

We do not know if we will return. We do not know what waits beyond the western ridge. We do not know if our city will survive what comes.

[Voice begins to break up, as if the transmission is failing]

But we go knowing this: the water remembers. Even when it changes form—when it becomes cloud, becomes ice, becomes the blood in your veins or the tears on your face—it remembers where it has been.

[Static increases]

And so will we. Even if Sahl Nahar falls. Even if the waters run silver forever. Even if—

[Transmission cuts out abruptly, then returns much fainter]

—the pattern remains. Find the water. Follow it home.

[Static overwhelms the voice, then clears momentarily for one final whisper]
