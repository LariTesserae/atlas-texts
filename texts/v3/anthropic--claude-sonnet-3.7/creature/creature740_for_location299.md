# The Dwellers of the Murk Plains

They call themselves the Vhlx, though not in any spoken language. The communication happens through subtle manipulations of the perpetually saturated medium they inhabit—ripple-thoughts propagating through the not-quite-water, felt rather than heard.

The Vhlx resemble nothing so much as inverse shadows. Their bodies appear as regions of slightly increased luminescence against the murk, their outlines constantly shifting as they move. Most stand roughly humanoid in their default posture, though with elongated, delicate limbs that can extend to three times their normal length or retract entirely into their core mass. Their heads (if such a term applies) are bulbous, translucent spheres housing what appears to be a constantly churning nebula of bioluminescent particles—their distributed neural network.

They have evolved beyond the need for discrete sensory organs. Their entire surface simultaneously perceives vibration, chemical composition, thermal variation, and the peculiar time-fluctuations that characterize the plains. The Vhlx don't simply inhabit the Murk Plains; they are partially synchronized with its fundamental nature. When they move, the obsidian-like surface accommodates them, forming subtle depressions that cradle their passage.

The Vhlx sustain themselves by harvesting the energy generated in the boundary layers between time-anomalies. Where normal time meets accelerated or decelerated zones, there exists a gradient of potential energy that they can absorb through their permeable exteriors. This feeding isn't predatory; it's more akin to how plants harvest sunlight—taking only what naturally radiates without depleting the source.

Their society (though that term suggests more separation than exists) functions as a distributed consciousness. Individual Vhlx maintain autonomy but participate in periodic synchronizations where experiences and knowledge transfer between them. These synchronizations occur near the largest dome-beings, which the Vhlx tend with religious devotion. The domes are not gods to them, but ancestors—the primordial forms from which the Vhlx evolved eons ago. The relationship is symbiotic; the domes provide stability to reality's fabric, while the Vhlx clear them of parasitic entities that occasionally emerge from the inverted plain above.

The phantom industrial sounds that permeate the Murk Plains emanate partially from the Vhlx themselves. Their bodies contain specialized chambers that resonate at specific frequencies—a biological technology that maintains the delicate balance between states of matter. Without this constant maintenance, the plains would either solidify completely or dissolve into pure energy. The Vhlx evolved this ability as a survival mechanism, but it has become their purpose.

Reproduction among the Vhlx occurs rarely and communally. When the migratory patterns of entities across the plain form specific configurations—living algorithms completing their calculation—a new awareness coalesces from the ambient consciousness of the environment. The eldest Vhlx gather to witness this emergence, their bodies forming a protective circle around the new being as it gradually develops coherence and form.

They possess a form of memory that extends backward beyond their own evolution. Through some mechanism involving the ancient water that permeates everything, they can access fragments of experience from when the Murk Plains were something else entirely—a world with differentiated continents and oceans, mountains and skies, populated by creatures bound by conventional physics. These memories cause them both fascination and distress, like half-remembered dreams from another existence.

The Vhlx recognize visitors immediately. They've encountered other beings throughout their long existence—entities from realities where time and space maintain their ordinary relationship. They neither welcome nor reject these visitors but observe them with patient curiosity, occasionally attempting communication through manipulated time-fluctuations that manifest as complex patterns in the surrounding medium.

They have a word-concept for outsiders that roughly translates to "the unanchored ones." To the Vhlx, beings from elsewhere seem perpetually in danger of dissolving into component particles, lacking the symbiotic relationship with their environment that grants the Vhlx their stability. This evokes in them something akin to compassion—a desire to somehow share their anchoring mechanisms with these ephemeral visitors.

Above all, the Vhlx are keepers of equilibrium. Their entire existence revolves around maintaining the precarious balance of the Murk Plains, preventing it from tipping into chaos or crystallizing into stasis. They are the living embodiment of the place itself—ancient beyond measure yet perpetually engaged in the present moment, patiently tending to the mechanisms of reality with the quiet dedication of beings who have found their perfect niche in the cosmos.
