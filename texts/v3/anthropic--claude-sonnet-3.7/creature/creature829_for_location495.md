# The People of the Verdant Mills

The Tenders are the primary inhabitants of the Verdant Mills, a people whose lineage is as intertwined with this place as the roots beneath their feet. At first glance, they appear simply as slender humans with an earthy complexion that ranges from deep umber to moss-tinged olive. Look closer, however, and subtle distinctions emerge: their irises contain intricate patterns resembling growth rings, their hair grows in thick, fibrous strands that many weave with living vines, and fine lines trace their skin like the veining of leaves.

These are not mere aesthetic quirks but adaptations from generations of symbiotic living. The Tenders' blood runs with trace amounts of the same photosynthetic compounds that power the Mills' infrastructure, allowing them to draw subtle nourishment from sunlight. Their lungs have evolved to filter the constant humidity and spore-rich air, and their ears are uniquely attuned to discern minute variations in the Mills' ambient symphony—often detecting mechanical irregularities long before visible signs appear.

Society among the Tenders is organized into familial guilds, each specialized in tending to different aspects of the Mills. The Rootwardens manage the foundation systems and water channels, their fingers possessing an uncanny sensitivity that can detect blockages beneath yards of soil. Canopyshapers spend their lives aloft, guiding the growth of the upper reaches and maintaining the delicate balance of light filtration. Geargrowers oversee the integration of mechanical elements, using secreted resins from their palms to seal and strengthen critical junctures.

Most revered are the Patternkeepers, elders whose memories store centuries of growth cycles, maintenance records, and botanical knowledge. Their skin, wrinkled and textured like bark, bears ritual scarification that encodes crucial information about the Mills' systems. When a Patternkeeper dies, their body is ceremonially interred within a specially prepared hollow in one of the great trees, where it contributes directly to the biological intelligence of the Mills.

Children in this society begin their education through play that subtly instructs them in the rhythms of their world. By age seven, they undergo the Grafting—a coming-of-age ritual where a small cutting from a parent tree is implanted beneath their skin, typically at the back of the neck. This creates a lifelong connection to the greater organism and is believed to anchor their consciousness to the Mills.

Not all who dwell here are Tenders by birth. A small population of Immigrants exists—outsiders who have chosen to bind their lives to the Mills. They lack the biological adaptations of native Tenders but compensate through specialized tools and training. After twenty years of service, they undergo the Absorption ceremony, drinking a carefully prepared infusion that permanently tints their skin and initiates subtle physiological changes. Their descendants will eventually develop the innate traits of born Tenders.

Operating at the fringes of this society are the Drifters—transient workers who navigate the waterways between the Mills and downstream communities. Recognizable by their weathered skin and pupils that contract vertically rather than circularly (an adaptation to the shifting light conditions of their travels), they serve as merchants, messengers, and cultural intermediaries. Though respected for their role, they are viewed with a mixture of fascination and mild suspicion by the more rooted Tenders.

Rarely seen but crucial to the Mills' function are the Mycelians—reclusive specialists who tend the underground networks of fungi that process waste and transmit nutrients throughout the system. Their prolonged exposure to the fungal networks has left them pale and sensitive to direct sunlight, with fingertips that branch into delicate filaments capable of manipulating the microscopic structures of decomposition. They speak infrequently but communicate through a complex language of spore releases that convey emotional states and technical information simultaneously.

The governance of the Mills operates through the Confluence Council, where representatives from each guild gather monthly when the water levels in the central basin reach their peak. Decisions are rarely made quickly, with proposals often "planted" for several cycles before action is taken. Leadership passes not through heredity or election but through a complex assessment of an individual's resonance with the Mills itself—measured by how the waters flow in their presence and how readily the plants respond to their touch.

What outsiders often fail to understand about the people of the Verdant Mills is that they do not see themselves as separate from their home. The distinction between person and place, between flesh and fiber, is considered a philosophical misunderstanding. In their language, the word for "self" and "garden" share the same root, differing only in intonation. They are not caretakers of the Mills—they are extensions of it, as vital to its function as any gear or growing thing.
