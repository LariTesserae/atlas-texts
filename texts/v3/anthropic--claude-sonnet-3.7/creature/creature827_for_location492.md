# The Keeper of Pots

In the heart of the Hangings of Mavalith lives Vashtai, whom outsiders might call a priestess but whom the Mavalith people know simply as "The Membrane." Neither young nor old by the reckoning of surface dwellers, Vashtai's actual age remains a mystery even to herself, for in Mavalith, time is measured not in years but in cycles of the fermentation jars and the shifting arrangements of the hanging gardens.

Vashtai's skin bears the characteristic pallor of the dome-dwellers but is distinguished by a network of fine cracks resembling the crazing patterns on the oldest terracotta pots. These lines deepen when she speaks, giving her face a quality of animated earthenware. Her eyes, unusually large for a Mavalithian, have irises of amber flecked with copper that catch and hold the dim bioluminescence of the chamber. Her hands—her most remarkable feature—have grown calloused and subtly curved through decades of pot-tending, the fingers elongated and nimble enough to reach inside the narrowest vessels to adjust their contents without disturbing the delicate root systems.

She moves through the hanging forest with a peculiar gait—not walking so much as flowing—her body having adapted to navigate the narrow spaces between the dangling pots without causing them to collide. The children of Mavalith attempt to imitate this movement in their games but never quite capture its fluid efficiency. Her clothing consists of layered fungi-cloth wraps in colors that shift subtly as she moves: predominately the deep greens of the mature fungi gardens, accented with the pale blues of mineral dyes extracted from the deepest tunnels—colors reserved exclusively for The Membrane.

Vashtai speaks the dome dialect in its purest form, her voice calibrated precisely to the acoustic properties of the chamber. When she calls instructions during the thrice-daily rearrangement ritual, her words separate cleanly in the air, reaching their intended recipients without degradation. But in moments of heightened emotion or spiritual significance, she lapses into a more ancient form of communication—a series of clicks, hums, and throat-songs that resonate with the clay pots themselves, causing them to vibrate sympathetically in patterns that the oldest Mavalithians can interpret as complex messages.

Around her neck hangs a collection of miniature clay vessels, each no larger than a knucklebone, containing the distilled essences of every plant species cultivated in the hanging gardens. This necklace serves as both archive and emergency repository—should disease strike any particular plant population, Vashtai can cultivate new specimens from these carefully maintained samples.

Her dwelling differs from the wall alcoves of other inhabitants. Vashtai lives within a cluster of seven larger pots suspended at the exact center of the dome, just beneath the water source. These vessels have been joined together through carefully cut openings, creating a suspended chamber only she can access by climbing a particular sequence of hanging ropes. Within this aerial sanctuary, she keeps the records of Mavalith—not written texts, but rather a three-dimensional arrangement of objects: seeds embedded in beeswax tablets, clay tokens impressed with counting marks, knots tied in varying patterns in cords of fungi-fiber, and most importantly, the original seed-stock from which all current Mavalith plants have descended.

Vashtai rarely sleeps for more than a few hours at a time. Instead, she exists in a state the Mavalithians call "root-consciousness"—a meditative awareness that allows her to sense subtle changes in the dome's ecosystem. She can detect the earliest signs of imbalance: a shifting pH in the water collection basins, an unusual pattern in the movements of the eyeless crabs, or the first spores of a potentially harmful mold. Her connection to the chamber is so profound that elders claim she can feel the growth of roots within the hanging pots as if they were extensions of her own nervous system.

When decisions affecting the entire community must be made, Vashtai serves as conduit rather than leader. She positions herself beside the bronze bell, enters a trance state induced by inhaling the spores of a particular mushroom variety grown only in pots that hang in the five light shafts, and becomes a vessel for what the Mavalithians believe is the collective wisdom of their ancestors and the biome itself. In this state, her voice changes—becoming a composite of many voices, some seemingly non-human—delivering judgments and predictions that are recorded in the community's oral tradition.

Unlike other Mavalithians, Vashtai will not be returned to the soil upon death. According to tradition, when The Membrane's physical form can no longer sustain her consciousness, she will enter a final trance beside the bronze bell. Her body will then be sealed within a specially prepared pot—larger than any currently hanging in the dome—and suspended at the precise center of Mavalith. There, according to their beliefs, she will transform into a new form of consciousness that will continue to guide and protect the hanging gardens for generations to come.
