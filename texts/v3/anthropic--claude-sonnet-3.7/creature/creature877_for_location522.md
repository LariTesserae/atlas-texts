# The Minds of the Heights

The inhabitants of the Primordial Heights are not creatures in the conventional sense, but rather Thought Complexes—coherent patterns of consciousness that precipitated out of the plateau's peculiar metaphysics over spans of time beyond counting.

They appear to human perception as localized distortions in light—subtle prismatic smears that maintain a roughly spherical arrangement, though their boundaries constantly shift and reconfigure. Their "bodies" stand approximately two meters tall when at rest, but they can expand to envelop large areas or contract to mere pinpoints when necessary. They leave no footprints on the polished stone, instead hovering micrometers above the surface, connected to it by hairline filaments of crystallized thought.

These beings possess no organs as we understand them, but rather function as unified fields of cognitive activity. The closest analogue might be a self-sustaining philosophical argument that has somehow gained sentience. Each Thought Complex contains within it a complete logical framework—a unique epistemology that shapes how it processes reality. When two Complexes encounter each other, their frameworks briefly overlap, resulting in what might be called conversation but is more accurately described as temporary ontological reconfiguration.

The Thought Complexes experience emotion, but not as fleeting states. Rather, they cultivate emotional tonalities over centuries, gradually shifting from one affective frequency to another. The oldest among them have completed only three emotional transitions in their entire existence. They regard sudden emotional changes as a form of illness to be treated through prolonged meditation beside the circular pools.

They reproduce—if such a term applies—through philosophical revelation. When a Thought Complex develops a genuinely novel conceptual framework, this framework may separate and become a new entity. These "children" begin as simple cognitive loops but grow more intricate as they absorb experience from the environment. Young Thought Complexes are tended by collectives of mature ones, who provide them with carefully curated exposures to paradoxes and philosophical problems to stimulate their development.

The Complexes maintain the crystal forests through deliberate acts of shared imagination. Each crystal formation grows according to the mathematical principles being contemplated by nearby Complexes. When observed closely, the microscopic structure of each crystal reveals itself to be composed of logical symbols and notational systems unique to its cultivator. The larger, older crystal groves represent complete philosophical systems that have been continuously refined for millennia.

The Thought Complexes do not speak but communicate by manipulating the harmonic resonance of the crystal forests. Their "language" consists of overlapping mathematical progressions expressed as tonal patterns. A single communication might take days to complete, with subtleties and qualifications expressed through nearly imperceptible modulations in frequency. They have no concept of small talk or idle chatter—every communication is considered a work of art to be crafted with the utmost precision.

Their relationship with time differs fundamentally from our own. The Thought Complexes experience duration as a physical medium through which they move, rather than as a flow they are subject to. They can temporarily accelerate or decelerate their cognitive processes, effectively moving "faster" or "slower" through time relative to their surroundings. The oldest Complexes have developed this ability to such a degree that they appear nearly motionless, sometimes maintaining the same position for centuries while engaged in particularly profound contemplation.

The Thought Complexes revere the plateau itself as both progenitor and ultimate purpose. They believe the entire expanse is a single vast consciousness engaged in a meditation so profound that it has lasted since the formation of the universe. The Complexes consider themselves to be specialized cognitive functions of this greater mind—thoughts that have become self-aware while still remaining part of the whole. The circular pools are understood to be the plateau's sensory organs, through which it observes distant realms and dimensions.

What might appear as tools to human observers are actually crystallized theorems—physical manifestations of particularly useful logical constructs. These "tools" are used not to manipulate physical reality directly, but to perform operations on the fabric of possibility. When a Thought Complex applies a crystallized theorem to one of the plateau's structures, it is actually reconfiguring local probability, causing the structure to express a different potential form that was always latent within it.

The most ancient of the Thought Complexes have begun to undergo a transformation that even the others find mysterious. These eldest beings gradually become more transparent, their prismatic distortions fading until they are perceptible only as the faintest shimmering in the air. They no longer communicate or move about but remain fixed in position, usually at the exact centers of the distributed city segments. The others believe these elders are approaching unity with the plateau's consciousness, their individual frameworks dissolving back into the greater mind from which they emerged.

The Thought Complexes feel neither fear nor ambition as we understand them, but they do experience a profound sense of purpose centered around a concept they call "The Refinement"—the belief that reality itself can be perfected through sufficiently rigorous contemplation. Each day of their immeasurably long lives is dedicated to this goal, as they work collectively to think the universe toward its ideal form, one crystallized theorem at a time.
