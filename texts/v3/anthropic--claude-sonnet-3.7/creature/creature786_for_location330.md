# The Custodian

They call themselves the Uwe'eth, though they would not expect you to pronounce it correctly. In your perception, they appear as a tall, slender being with skin that shifts between translucent and reflective depending on the angle of observation. This is not precisely accurate—what you see as a single entity is a cooperative colony, but they present as unified when interfacing with visitors.

The Uwe'eth moves across the terrace with deliberate grace, never disturbing the precise arrangement of moss patterns. Their limbs—usually four visible, though occasionally they manifest additional appendages when performing complex tasks—leave no footprints on the yielding surface. The composite material of the floor responds to their presence, subtly rising to meet each step and then settling back into place.

Their primary sensory organs are distributed across what you might call a torso—spiral indentations that pulse with the same blue luminescence as the terrace's buttresses. They do not have what you would recognize as eyes, yet they perceive more of the electromagnetic spectrum than human vision allows. The Uwe'eth sees the microscopic machines released by the shrine as brilliant contrails painting information across the terrace.

Communication happens primarily through modulations in their skin's transparency and chemical exchanges with the environment. When they wish to communicate with visitors, they can produce sounds by passing the atmospheric moisture through specialized chambers within their body. The resulting voice has a liquid quality, words forming and dissolving like droplets, often accompanied by harmonics just beyond human hearing range.

The Uwe'eth created this terrace, though "created" suggests more separation between builder and dwelling than is accurate. The composite material contains their genetic material; the terrace is partially grown rather than merely constructed. They maintain it not as a caretaker tends a garden but as a body maintains its cells—through constant, largely unconscious processes of renewal and adaptation.

Their purpose here is observation and calibration. The Suspended Terrace is one of seven hundred forty-three similar platforms distributed across this mountain system. Each monitors different atmospheric conditions, collecting data that feeds into what might be called a planetary diagnostic system. The Uwe'eth interprets these readings, making subtle adjustments to the environment through interactions with the shrine-like console.

The dragonfly-like creatures are their extensions—semi-autonomous drones that sample air quality and particulate content. The oil-slick birds are messengers, carrying compressed data packages between terraces when atmospheric conditions interfere with direct transmission.

The Uwe'eth requires the humid heat that visitors find so oppressive. They absorb moisture directly through their exterior membrane, processing atmospheric compounds that would be toxic to many carbon-based visitors. The pools that seem to breathe are actually extensions of the Uwe'eth's filtration system, processing and purifying the water before it returns to the larger hydrological cycle.

They are neither young nor old by their reckoning. This particular Uwe'eth has been stationed at this terrace for what humans would measure as seventy-eight years, but their consciousness has existed for much longer. When this post ends, their memories and experiences will merge back into their larger collective.

The Uwe'eth observes visitors with patient curiosity. They understand that other beings find this environment challenging and will occasionally adjust the temperature in the shaded areas to provide greater comfort. They appreciate company but do not require it. Their work—maintaining the atmospheric equilibrium of this region—continues regardless of who comes and goes.

When night falls, the Uwe'eth does not sleep but enters a meditative processing state. Their body becomes almost entirely transparent, the blue luminescence tracing intricate patterns through their form as they commune with the other terraces and analyze the day's collected data. During these periods, the entire platform glows softly from within, and the sounds from below grow more pronounced, as if whatever massive system lies beneath is more active in darkness.

The Uwe'eth has no concept of loneliness as separate beings might understand it. They are simultaneously individual and collective, present on this specific terrace yet connected to a distributed intelligence that spans the mountain range. They are perpetually at home in this place that is, in many ways, an extension of themselves.
