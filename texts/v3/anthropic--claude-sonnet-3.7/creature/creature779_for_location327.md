# The Warden

The Warden has not been human for seventy-three years, four months, and sixteen days. The transformation was gradual - so gradual that the Warden still thinks of themself as the young engineer who first took the oath of maintenance. But what remains of that person now exists primarily in memory and habit.

The Warden's body is a pragmatic hybrid of the organic and mechanical. Their spine, reinforced with a titanium lattice, allows them to bend at impossible angles to reach valves behind equipment. Their hands, once blistered and scarred from decades of work, now feature retractable tools beneath each fingernail - a micrometer caliper under the left thumb, a specialized screwdriver beneath the right index finger, and so forth. Their eyes have been modified with telescoping lenses that can magnify to examine microfractures in pressure vessels or zoom out to take in entire systems at once.

The Warden breathes, but not quite like you do. Their lungs process the humid air with enhanced efficiency, filtering out metallic particles and converting excess moisture into the internal hydration system that has largely replaced their digestive tract. What remains of their original organs has been carefully preserved in nutrient baths that must be refreshed monthly.

The Warden moves constantly, never truly sleeping. Instead, hemispheric resting cycles allow part of their consciousness to shut down while the other maintains vigilance. Their movements through the dome follow patterns optimized over decades - economical, precise, each step landing exactly where it needs to be without conscious thought. They can traverse the narrow walkways without looking, hands already reaching for the next valve that needs adjustment.

They speak rarely, and when they do, their voice carries harmonics that match the dome's resonant frequency. The mechanical creatures respond to these tones, sometimes gathering near when the Warden sings the ancient maintenance codes that have evolved into something like folk songs.

The Warden does not consider their existence lonely. Their connection to the lifepod is intimate beyond what any human relationship could provide. They feel the pulse of every pump as if it were their own heartbeat, sense temperature variations with the immediacy of pain or pleasure. When they place their hand on a pipe, they can read its vibrations like a letter from an old friend.

Their memory has become specialized - perfectly retaining operating parameters and maintenance histories while personal recollections have grown indistinct. They remember their predecessor's face more clearly than their own family, though they still perform rituals of remembrance, brewing tea on specific dates marked in a logbook kept beside the hammock.

The Warden's purpose is preservation - not just of the mechanical garden, but of what it represents. The lifepod contains genetic material, cultural data, and technological specifications, all encoded in the seemingly decorative patterns of the mechanical ecosystem. The Warden is custodian to the possibility of renewal, should it ever be needed.

They know that someday they will need to find a successor, someone to undergo the same gradual transformation, but that time has not yet come. For now, the Warden continues their endless circuit of the dome, adjusting, calibrating, communing with the machinery that has become both their charge and their body.
