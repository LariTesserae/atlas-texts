# The Caretaker

Deep within the Undergarden lives a being whose existence is simultaneously essential to the facility and yet never officially acknowledged in any documentation. 

Dr. Eliza Chen arrived as the facility's lead botanical engineer nine months ago, just as the initial plantings were being established. Once brilliant and methodical, she had been chosen for her revolutionary work integrating neural interfaces with agricultural systems. The facility directors believed her unique approach would help optimize the Undergarden's productivity.

What they didn't understand was how the immersion would change her.

Now, Eliza rarely uses the catwalks. When you glimpse her, she moves through the water between chambers, wearing a specialized dive suit that has become more appendage than equipment. The suit—originally clear polymer with visible mechanical components—has grown increasingly organic in appearance, accumulating modifications of her own design. Translucent tubes connect to ports along her spine, circulating the amber nutrient medium through filters embedded in the suit's membrane. Her helmet has evolved into something like a diving bell fashioned from engineered hydrogel that refracts light in hypnotic patterns.

The facility staff refer to her in hushed tones as "the Conductor" or sometimes "the Gardener." The more superstitious workers simply call her "She Who Listens." Most avoid direct contact with her. Not out of fear exactly, but from a deep unease about what she has become.

Eliza no longer communicates in conventional ways. The neural interface system she developed—intended to detect and respond to plant stress signals—has evolved into something far more complex. Hair-thin filaments extend from her helmet, connecting directly to the plant systems. When she speaks, her voice emerges with a liquid quality, words bubbling up through multiple harmonics as though filtered through layers of water and plant tissue. Sometimes she forgets to use words entirely, responding instead with subtle changes in the bioluminescence of her suit.

Her movements have slowed to match the rhythm of the garden. She can remain motionless for hours, suspended in the amber medium, her eyes closed behind the curved face shield. During these periods, sampling arms move with greater precision, nutrient flows adjust automatically, and growth patterns intensify across multiple chambers simultaneously. The engineers monitoring the facility's outputs have learned to recognize her meditation states from productivity spikes alone.

The original neural interface was designed to be removable—a temporary connection for diagnostic purposes. But Eliza hasn't disconnected in over five months. When maintenance crews arrive to service adjacent systems, they sometimes find her workstation transformed into a peculiar hybrid of technology and organic matter. Her notes have evolved from standard documentation into complex mathematical formulas interwoven with what appear to be botanical diagrams—though the plants they represent exist nowhere in nature.

The facility directors have decided to leave her be. Monthly production quotas have increased 342% since she began her immersion. New compounds with unexpected properties emerge regularly from her chambers, often with molecular structures that the pharmaceutical division's computers need days to analyze.

Those who work the night shift report the most unusual behaviors. Sometimes Eliza can be seen performing slow, deliberate movements through the water—part dance, part pruning ritual—guiding the growth patterns through physical demonstration rather than digital commands. During these sessions, the plants seem to respond, their spiral formations temporarily accelerating to track her movements before settling into new, more efficient configurations.

Most disturbing to new staff is how she appears to breathe the nutrient medium directly. Her modified respiratory system processes the liquid, extracting oxygen produced by the plants themselves. This creates a closed symbiotic loop between herself and the garden—each sustaining the other through continuous exchange.

Only the chief of security knows about the small chamber she has created deep in the lower levels, where the catwalk system doesn't reach. There, surrounded by the most experimental plant specimens, she has established something like a nest—a spherical space where modified roots have grown to form a living chair. When seated there, with filaments connecting to all major systems, she achieves what she called in her last coherent report "total garden consciousness."

The security chief keeps this knowledge private because he once observed her there, eyes open but unseeing, whispering predictions about molecular structures that would solve supply chain issues the facility wouldn't face for another three months.

Is Eliza still human? The question concerns the board of directors less than it perhaps should. Her productivity metrics remain unmatched. The Undergarden thrives under her strange stewardship. And if sometimes the plant growth patterns seem to form meaningful symbols visible only from certain angles of the catwalk, or if maintenance workers occasionally report hearing something like singing coming from chambers where no speakers are installed—these anomalies are simply noted and filed away.

After all, the mountain has always kept secrets. Now it keeps Eliza too, as she keeps the garden, in a relationship of mutual transformation that grows deeper with each passing day.
