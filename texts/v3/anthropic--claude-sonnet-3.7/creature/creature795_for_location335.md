# Inhabitants of The Draining

The Tidewalkers are the most common inhabitants you'll encounter in The Draining. Their skin has taken on a translucent quality after generations below, with visible blue-gray veins tracing patterns beneath. Their eyes have enlarged slightly, pupils permanently dilated to capture what little light exists, giving them an unsettling, watchful appearance. They move with deliberate economy, each step calculated to conserve energy in this harsh environment.

Tidewalkers organize into nomadic family units called "circuits," ranging from five to fifteen individuals who follow the rhythmic flooding and draining cycles through established territories. They've developed an intricate language of whistles and clicks that carries through the ambient mechanical noise, allowing them to communicate across the vast chambers. Their language includes over thirty distinct terms for different water sounds and pressure changes—essential knowledge that warns of environmental shifts.

Most wear layered garments of treated fungal fiber and salvaged materials, designed to wick away the omnipresent moisture while trapping body heat. These clothes are adorned with collections of found objects—gears, valve handles, circuit boards—that serve as both family identifiers and trade goods. The most prized possessions are pre-Draining artifacts, particularly tools or data storage devices.

A complex social hierarchy exists among the circuits, determined primarily by knowledge rather than strength. Those who can accurately predict flooding cycles or locate valuable salvage rise to leadership positions. The eldest member typically carries the circuit's "flowmap"—a physical record etched into sheets of scavenged metal that documents safe passages and dangerous zones within their territory.

The Stillpool Collective represents the closest thing to permanent settlement in The Draining. Situated around a massive pump station that maintains unusual stability, this community of approximately two hundred individuals has established a trading hub where circuits exchange goods and information. The Collective maintains the most complete archives of pump station schedules, having dedicated generations to observing and recording the patterns of The Draining's mechanical heart.

More secretive are the Deepdivers—individuals who have adapted to spending extended periods underwater, exploring the fully submerged lower chambers. Their bodies show more extreme adaptations: webbing between fingers, enhanced lung capacity, and a layer of insulating fat that protects against the cold. Deepdivers rarely interact with other inhabitants, emerging occasionally to trade unusual salvage for essential supplies before disappearing again into the flooded depths.

The Pipewrights form a quasi-religious order dedicated to maintaining critical sections of The Draining's infrastructure. They believe that the machine complex is a living entity they call "The Great Circuit" and that their maintenance work represents a sacred duty. Pipewrights can be identified by their distinctive tool-harnesses and the detailed mechanical diagrams tattooed across their bodies, which serve as both technical manuals and religious iconography.

Less numerous but greatly feared are the Rust Barons—opportunistic groups who control territories containing critical resources or passage points. They extract "flow tolls" from travelers and sometimes raid vulnerable circuits. Rust Barons modify their appearances with mechanical augmentations crafted from salvage, replacing damaged limbs or enhancing their natural capabilities with crude hydraulic systems powered by the pressure differentials within The Draining.

Most inhabitants share a complex relationship with the pale creatures of The Draining. The translucent fish that inhabit periodically flooded chambers are a vital food source, while the fungal colonies provide both medicine and materials. Some circuits have domesticated the pale, eyeless rodents called "clicks," using their sensitive whiskers to detect pressure changes that precede flooding.

Perhaps most mysterious are the Whispers—rarely seen individuals who claim to communicate with The Draining itself. They speak of voices heard in the rhythmic pumping and of messages hidden in the patterns of condensation. Most dismiss them as delusional, their minds affected by isolation or toxic exposure, yet their predictions about system changes often prove unnervingly accurate.

All inhabitants share certain physiological adaptations: enhanced night vision, heightened hearing attuned to specific mechanical frequencies, and immune systems remarkably resistant to the exotic fungi and bacteria that thrive in this environment. Children born in The Draining develop these traits more prominently with each generation, suggesting a gradual evolutionary divergence from surface humanity.

What unites these disparate groups is their intimate knowledge of The Draining's rhythms—a sense that has become almost instinctual after centuries of adaptation. They move through this mechanical underworld not as intruders but as another functional component of its vast, breathing system.
