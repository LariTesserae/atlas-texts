# The Caretakers of Thalla

They call themselves the Nis'Thallar, though they have no spoken language to voice this name. They communicate through a complex exchange of biochemical signals transmitted through a network of sensory organs that appear as iridescent patches across their translucent bodies.

The Nis'Thallar stand approximately five feet tall when fully extended, though they rarely assume this posture, preferring a more fluid, horizontal orientation that allows maximum contact with their surroundings. Their bodies resemble elongated cephalopods, with twelve primary appendages that branch into countless smaller manipulators capable of incredibly precise movements. Their skin shifts between pale jade and copper tones, mirroring the geological composition of their home, and possesses both plantlike and animal characteristics—photosynthesizing while also absorbing nutrients through direct contact with surfaces.

They have no eyes as we understand them. Instead, their entire epidermis functions as a photoreceptive organ, sensing light, vibration, and chemical composition simultaneously. This distributed consciousness gives them awareness of their environment from all angles without the concept of "facing" a particular direction.

The Nis'Thallar evolved alongside the membrane-canopies, developing a symbiotic relationship over millions of years. They cultivate these living structures, sculpting and directing their growth through careful chemical exchanges. What appears as wild profusion to an outsider is actually a meticulously maintained ecological machine, each element precisely positioned to maximize efficiency in their resource cycle.

The geodesic structures are not separate technology but extensions of the Nis'Thallar themselves. They secrete the transparent material over decades, forming these chambers as external organs where they perform the most complex aspects of their bioengineering work. The brass and copper components are minerals they've extracted from the mountains and processed through their bodies, exuding them in purified form to serve specific functions in their designs.

The Nis'Thallar perceive time differently than most conscious beings. A single thought might form over what we would consider days or weeks, but the depth and complexity of these thoughts are correspondingly profound. They experience their lives not as linear progressions but as gradually expanding spirals of awareness, each cycle building upon the previous in ever-widening understanding of their ecosystem.

They are meticulous record-keepers, though not in any written form. Instead, they encode their collective knowledge in the genetic structure of the canopy-colonies, editing and expanding this living library with each generation. To read this knowledge, they extend specialized appendages into the membranes, forming direct neural connections that allow them to access millennia of accumulated wisdom.

The salamander-mole creatures are their creation—bioengineered sentinels that monitor the health of the garden ecosystems and report back through biochemical signals. These creatures are semi-autonomous extensions of the Nis'Thallar consciousness, functioning as mobile sensors throughout the terraces.

The Nis'Thallar have awareness of other forms of life beyond their mountains but find most bewilderingly frenetic. They have occasionally observed human explorers from a distance, perceiving them as fascinating but chaotically disorganized entities whose rapid movements and loud vocalizations suggest an inefficient, possibly painful mode of existence. They feel a kind of compassion for these frantic beings, though they lack the temporal compatibility to establish meaningful communication.

Their society, if it can be called that, has no hierarchy. Each Nis'Thallar is simultaneously individual and collective, maintaining distinct experiences while sharing consciousness through the membrane network. They have no concept of leadership or governance, instead operating through perfect consensus achieved through their biochemical communication system.

The purpose of their endless work in the gardens is both practical and philosophical—they are cultivating what they consider the perfect ecosystem, one that exists in complete equilibrium and requires no external input to sustain itself. This project has continued uninterrupted for what humans would measure as thousands of years, and from their perspective, it is still in its early phases. They expect it will reach completion in approximately 40,000 more years, at which point they believe the garden will achieve a state of perfect harmony that will allow them to transition into a new phase of existence.

The Nis'Thallar experience emotion, though not in ways immediately recognizable. Their primary emotional states might be translated as "harmonic satisfaction" when systems function optimally, "dissonant concern" when imbalances emerge, and "resonant contemplation" during their periods of deepest thought. They feel a profound sense of purpose in their work and experience something akin to aesthetic appreciation for particularly elegant biological or mechanical solutions.

They neither welcome nor reject visitors to their realm. Those who can move slowly and silently enough might observe them at work, almost invisible against the backdrop of their gardens until they move—flowing like living mercury between tasks, their appendages working with hypnotic precision as they adjust, calibrate, and nurture their world of perfect, patient silence.
