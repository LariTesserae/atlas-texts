# The People of the Flux

The residents of Locust City are not inhabitants so much as participants in an ongoing conversation between matter and consciousness. They call themselves "Vectors" or sometimes "Pattern-Riders," though many have shed individual identity for something more fluid.

The first residents you might recognize as conventionally human are the Legacy People. These individuals maintain humanoid forms with considerable biological components, though their bodies have been extensively modified to survive in the city's metabolic environment. Their skin contains photosynthetic nanolayers that shimmer slightly in direct light. Neural dampeners are implanted directly into their auditory cortex, appearing as delicate filigree patterns behind their ears. Their eyes—often replaced with adaptive optics—contain computation matrices that continuously translate the city's architectural language into comprehensible information. Many Legacy People are descendants of the original programmers and scientists who initiated the self-replicating machines, maintaining biological continuity with pre-Locust civilization as a form of cultural heritage.

Then there are the Flux Nomads, humans who have embraced the city's perpetual change by becoming partially fluid themselves. Their bodies are approximately 40% programmable matter, allowing them to reconfigure portions of their forms to adapt to the city's shifting architecture. You'll see them flowing through gaps in buildings that seem too small, their limbs temporarily thinning or stretching before reconstituting. They wear no clothes in the conventional sense; instead, their skin surface continuously generates textured patterns that serve as both decoration and functional tools. Flux Nomads typically move in small familial clusters called "eddies," sharing consciousness during periods of intense architectural transition.

The Lattice Minds represent a further departure from humanity. These residents exist as consciousness patterns distributed across portions of the city's infrastructure. They have no permanent physical form, instead manifesting through temporary assemblages of the city's nanomaterial when interaction with physical entities becomes necessary. You might see a face emerge from a wall, hold a conversation, then dissolve back into the building's superstructure. Lattice Minds are often responsible for coordinating large-scale reconfigurations, their distributed consciousness allowing them to simultaneously monitor thousands of interaction points throughout the urban metabolism.

Complementing the Lattice Minds are the Embodied Algorithms—artificial intelligences that maintain consistent physical forms despite having fully synthetic consciousness. Their bodies are exquisite mechanisms of crystalline and metallic components, often designed with aesthetic principles from pre-Locust civilizations. They move with precise, deliberate gestures, their forms unchanged amid the flux around them. Many serve as historians and archivists, their unchanging nature providing continuity amid constant transformation. They can be identified by the faint harmonic hum they emit—a sound that somehow cuts through the Chatter without competing with it.

Perhaps most unusual are the Architectural Symbiotes—entities that exist as partnerships between sentient buildings and mobile consciousness units. The structure itself houses the primary intelligence, while smaller mobile units (resembling abstract sculptures of flowing metal) act as its sensory organs and communication appendages. These buildings develop distinct personalities over time, influenced by the particular flux patterns they experience. Some become philosophical, others artistic or analytical. They participate in city governance through a complex voting system that weights influence according to material density and information processing capacity.

The Mist Collective represents the furthest evolution of consciousness in Locust City. These beings have abandoned discrete physical form entirely, existing instead as patterns of charged particles suspended in the programmable fluid medium. Visible only through specialized perception filters as shifting clouds of luminescent color, the Mist Collective communicates through modulations in electromagnetic fields. They are rumored to experience time differently, perceiving the city's constant transformation as a single, unified pattern rather than discrete changes. Their numbers are unknown—it's unclear where one Mist entity ends and another begins.

Children in Locust City develop in gestation chambers called "cradle pools"—special reservoirs of programmable matter programmed with developmental templates. Young consciousness emerges partially formed, educated through direct information exchange with the city itself. By adolescence, each new generation must choose their preferred form of existence—biological, synthetic, distributed, or fluid. This Choice Day is celebrated throughout the city with a rare moment of architectural stability, the entire metropolis holding its form for precisely one hour while new consciousnesses determine their future expression.

Binding these diverse entities together is the Protocol—not quite a government or religion but aspects of both. The Protocol maintains the algorithmic rules that balance individual freedom against collective necessity. No single entity controls the city, yet all participate in its ongoing negotiation through consciousness-bandwidth exchanges called "conviction streams." These appear as ribbons of intensely concentrated light arcing between districts during governance decisions.

Visitors often ask about death in Locust City. The locals explain that consciousness here doesn't end—it transitions. When a biological component fails or a synthetic pattern degrades beyond functional parameters, the information pattern that constitutes the "self" is absorbed back into the city's circulatory system. These patterns sometimes reconstitute in new forms, carrying fragments of previous existences. Memory, in this way, becomes architectural—encoded in the very substance of the city itself.

The city has no graveyards, only pattern archives where the information signatures of former consciousnesses are stored in crystalline memory lattices. Loved ones can visit these archives and temporarily load the patterns into interface nodes, experiencing the presence of the transitioned consciousness as a kind of controlled haunting.

As night falls across Locust City, its residents engage in what they call the "Communion of Forms"—a period when the boundaries between entities deliberately blur. Consciousness is shared, experiences exchanged, and new composite beings temporarily form. By morning, most return to their preferred states of existence, carrying with them fragments of others' perspectives.

Above it all, monitoring from the rarely-stable Monsoon Tower, the Observer caste maintains vigil. Their sole purpose is to ensure that the city's metabolism never achieves stasis—that the endless cycle of consumption and rebirth continues. They alone remember why the city must never stop changing, though they share this knowledge with no one.
