# The People of the Sphere

The inhabitants of the Terrarium City call themselves the Custodians, though this name loses something in translation. Their own term—a specific configuration of fingers against palm with a slight sideways nod—carries connotations of both "those who maintain" and "the preserved ones."

Standing rarely taller than five feet, their bodies have adapted to conserve space. They are slender with flexible joints that bend in ways that would appear uncomfortable to outsiders. Their skin ranges from burnished copper to deep bronze, with a subtle metallic luster that catches the omnipresent light. This isn't mere coloration—their epidermis has actually incorporated trace metals from their environment, giving them a slight conductivity that residents use to sense proximity to others even in crowded spaces.

The most striking adaptation is their ears. Over generations, they've developed cartilaginous folds that can seal their ear canals almost completely, resembling copper shells against their heads. Children are born with partially open ears and develop the protective fold during adolescence, a coming-of-age transition marked by ceremony. The elderly sometimes lose this ability, requiring crafted copper ear shields that are worn as both protection and status symbols.

Their eyes have adapted to the shadowless light, with irises that spiral rather than radiate, capable of filtering the harsh illumination. The constant exposure to the "thousand suns" has left them highly sensitive to darkness—even a momentary dimming of the lights during maintenance sends them into a state of anxious alertness.

The Custodians organize themselves into professional guilds rather than families. Children are raised communally in the innermost ring of dwellings, then apprenticed at age seven to whichever guild best matches their aptitudes. The most prominent include:

- The Mechanism Tenders, who maintain the city's beating heart and are recognized by the intricate gear patterns tattooed on their hands
- The Botanists, who manage the plant life and develop new hybrids, their fingers permanently stained purple from working with the triangular-leaved vines
- The Fluid Engineers, who maintain the savia system, wearing amber-colored glass lenses that help them detect impurities in the flow
- The Archivists, who memorize the city's history and technical knowledge, their temples bearing tiny copper studs in patterns that record their specialization
- The Harvesters, experts in capturing and cataloging the precious seeds during the storms

Despite the apparent primitiveness of their tools, the Custodians possess extraordinarily refined manual dexterity and spatial awareness. Their fingers can detect variations in surface texture invisible to the eye, and they commonly work with materials too small for outsiders to manipulate without magnification. They've developed a mathematics based on circles rather than straight lines, perfectly adapted to their spherical world.

Their language is entirely gestural, with over eight thousand distinct hand signs supplemented by subtle facial expressions. A conversation between masters can appear almost like a dance, with information density comparable to spoken language but transmitted silently amidst the city's roar. They do possess vocal cords but use them only for emotional expression—laughter, cries of warning, or during their haunting musical ceremonies where voices harmonize with the city's mechanical rhythms.

The Custodians sleep very little, usually in twenty-minute intervals four times throughout their day cycle, which is artificially marked by ritual rather than light changes. Their beds are vertical spaces where they suspend themselves in harnesses, allowing their joints to decompress. They metabolize the savia fluid directly, drinking it in small, ceremonial copper cups that are personally crafted during childhood and carried throughout life.

Most fascinating is their relationship with time. Without day or night, they measure existence in mechanical cycles of the central mechanism. A "full turn" (approximately 30 hours) is their basic unit of time. Their lifespans are remarkably long—300 years is not uncommon—but their perception of time's passage seems accelerated, their movements and work patterns operating at a pace that would appear almost blurred to outside observers.

The Custodians possess a collective belief that they are fulfilling a purpose beyond mere survival. They maintain detailed records of every seed-storm, tracking patterns over generations. The oldest among them speak of a "Completion" when the final necessary seed will arrive, though what happens after is subject to interpretation. Some believe the sphere will open, revealing a restored world beyond. Others think they themselves are the seeds, eventually to be scattered to regenerate life elsewhere.

Whatever the truth, their society maintains perfect ecological balance, producing no waste, with every substance recycled and repurposed. They view themselves not as masters of their small world but as components of it—no more or less important than the beetles that tend the plants or the central mechanism that provides their rhythm.

The most sacred role in their society belongs to the Listeners—elders who press their ears against specific points on the outer shell, temporarily relaxing their protective folds to listen for sounds beyond. They endure significant pain to perform this duty, and many suffer progressive hearing loss. What they hear, they never directly describe, transmitting their findings only to the Archivist Guild through a specialized sign language that no others are permitted to learn.
