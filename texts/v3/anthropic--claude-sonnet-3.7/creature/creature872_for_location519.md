# The Gardeners and Others in the Black Cloud Gardens

The Gardeners are the most visible inhabitants, though "visible" is relative in this place of perpetual dusk. They're tall, almost spindly people with exceptional balance—a necessity for those who spend their lives navigating narrow catwalks and viaducts suspended above the void. Their skin has a subtle luminescence, not quite bioluminescent but somehow capturing and amplifying the faint light from the engineered flora. This adaptation developed over generations of minimal sunlight exposure, their melanin evolved to collect rather than block radiation.

Gardener families pass down specialized knowledge through oral tradition, each clan responsible for different sections of the vast suspended ecosystem. They don't fully understand the original designs—those were lost centuries ago when the Engineering Caste disappeared—but they've developed an intuitive relationship with the gardens, feeling the subtle rhythms of the machinery below like a second pulse in their bodies.

Their clothing is practical but distinctive: layered garments of moisture-wicking fabrics treated with fungal compounds that prevent rot in the constant humidity. Every Gardener wears a chest harness equipped with specialized tools, collection pouches, and at least one "whisperlight"—a glass vial containing symbiotic bacteria that emit a soft glow when agitated by the wearer's breath. Their most prized possessions are their gloves, made from the membrane wings of the larger cloud-bats and treated with oils from pressure-sensitive plants, allowing them to detect minute changes in the atmospheric systems through touch alone.

Children of the Gardeners learn to walk on narrow beams before they learn to run on solid ground. By adolescence, they can navigate the entirety of their family's territory in complete darkness, relying on the vibrations underfoot and the distinctive sounds of different machinery sectors. Their dialect is unique, incorporating descriptive terms for various states of darkness and moisture that outsiders find incomprehensible.

But the Gardeners are not alone.

The Depth Tenders live below the garden levels, maintaining the ancient machinery that powers everything. They're stockier than the Gardeners, with hyper-developed hearing that can distinguish between hundreds of different mechanical sounds—detecting failures before they occur. Their eyes have adapted differently, with enlarged pupils that can detect the infrared signatures of the machines they tend. They rarely come to the upper levels, uncomfortable with the relatively bright bioluminescent light and the sensation of open space.

Tenders communicate through a combination of tactile sign language and subsonic vocalizations that resonate with the machine vibrations. Their skin is pale to the point of translucence, with a network of visible veins that have an unusual blue-black coloration—a genetic adaptation resulting from generations of exposure to the electromagnetic fields of the generators. They mark their bodies with conductive ink tattoos that they claim help them "feel" the machine-spirits more clearly.

The Commerce Clan occupies the junction points where viaducts meet and where the rare outsiders might visit. They're the most "normal" appearing inhabitants, though still distinctly adapted to Cloud Garden life. They maintain the ancient trading relationships that keep the Gardens supplied with what they cannot produce—primarily metals and complex electronics to replace what can't be repaired. In exchange, they export the unique medicinal plants and engineered organisms that thrive nowhere else.

Commerce Clan members are natural diplomats, switching easily between the technical jargon of Depth Tenders, the environmental terminology of Gardeners, and the common trade language of the outside world. They're identifiable by their layered robes with numerous hidden pockets, and the calculation beads they constantly manipulate while negotiating—clicking through complex exchanges without needing to write anything down.

The Drifters have no permanent homes, moving constantly through the Gardens. Originally maintenance workers, they've evolved into a nomadic class that travels where needed, following the patterns of rainfall and electric storms that require human intervention in the otherwise automated systems. Their bodies are lean and wiry, with a distinctive gait adapted to constant movement on the narrow pathways. They carry everything they own in compact backpacks made from the fibrous outer husks of the lightning plants.

Drifters are the Gardens' historians and messengers, carrying news and genetic material (plant cuttings, seeds, spores) between otherwise isolated sectors. They're recognized by the weather-sensing organs they wear—modified plant bulbs attached to their temples with thin filaments that change color to warn of approaching electrical storms or pressure changes.

Less human are the Climbers—descendants of genetically modified primates introduced centuries ago to maintain the highest and most inaccessible parts of the canopy. They've evolved into a distinct species with four equal-length limbs, each ending in hand-like appendages with retractable climbing hooks. Their bodies are covered in fine water-repellent fur with integrated symbiotic fungi that break down toxins in the environment.

Though not technically sapient by outside standards, the Climbers have developed a complex social structure and rudimentary tool use. The Gardeners consider them sacred, leaving offerings at certain junction points and never interfering with their activities. The Climbers, in turn, seem to understand which plants need protection and which invasive species must be removed to maintain the Gardens' delicate balance.

Most mysterious are the Mist Walkers—rarely seen figures that move through the densest fog banks, seemingly insubstantial themselves. Some believe they're descendants of the original Engineers who built the Gardens, others think they're just a myth created to explain the automated systems that occasionally activate without apparent cause. Gardeners who claim to have encountered them describe humanoid forms that seem partly merged with the machinery itself—bodies with components that shift between organic and mechanical states.

What's certain is that sometimes when systems fail catastrophically, they repair themselves overnight without any known intervention. And children who get lost in the mists sometimes return with strange new knowledge and an inexplicable affinity for the most ancient machinery.

All these inhabitants share one belief: the Black Cloud Gardens are not just a place but a single vast organism that they are part of—like cells in a larger body—each performing their function to maintain the impossible balance of this world that breathes in darkness and exhales life.
