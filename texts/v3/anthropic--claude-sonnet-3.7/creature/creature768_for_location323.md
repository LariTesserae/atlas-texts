# The Keepers of Dael Verre

The inhabitants of Dael Verre are known as the Varesh, though they never speak this name aloud. Standing slightly shorter than average humans, their bodies have adapted to life on the cliffside through generations of careful evolution and deliberate selection. Their feet are narrow but with unusually spread toes that grip stone surfaces with natural surety, and their hands feature elongated fingers with thickened skin at the fingertips, perfect for the intricate gestures of their silent language.

The Varesh possess eyes of remarkable adaptability—large irises that contract to pinpoints in bright sunlight or dilate almost completely in dim conditions, allowing them to navigate the cliff paths by starlight alone. Their coloration varies within a narrow range: skin tones from alabaster to warm sandstone, hair in shades from platinum to deep amber that they typically wear in elaborate braids wrapped close to the skull. Most striking are the small, scale-like patches that appear along their spines and shoulders—not true scales but hardened skin with a faint iridescence that catches the light when they move.

Every Varesh is born with perfect pitch and extraordinary hearing, able to detect sounds well beyond normal human range. This gift explains their culture of silence; what seems quiet to visitors is, to them, a symphony of subtle sounds—the breathing of their six-legged reptilian companions, the distant calls of cliff birds, even the slow seepage of water through limestone. Speaking aloud feels to them like shouting in a library.

The Varesh organize themselves not by family but by vocation, with seven distinct guilds maintaining the city's perfect equilibrium:

The Stone Shapers tend the living rock, not merely carving it but communicating with it through vibrations created by specialized tools. They don't merely build; they negotiate with the mountain, requesting space rather than demanding it. Their hands are always dusted with limestone powder, and they alone know the secret language of geological time.

The Water Keepers manage the intricate hydraulic systems, reading weather patterns with uncanny accuracy. They wear garments dyed with a particular blue pigment derived from a rare mineral in the cliff face, and they move with fluid grace even by Varesh standards. Their most sacred duty is maintaining the great deep-cistern that predates even their earliest records—a perfectly cylindrical shaft that descends into darkness beyond any measurement they've attempted.

The Green Tenders cultivate the city's flora with methodical precision. Each plant in Dael Verre grows according to ancient templates, pruned and guided from the moment it sprouts. These guild members can often be recognized by the tiny glass vials they carry, containing pollens and seeds categorized by systems outsiders cannot comprehend. They speak to plants through touch, their fingers transmitting intentions that somehow translate into perfectly controlled growth.

The Beast Whisperers oversee the relationship between Varesh and fauna. They don't train the animals in any conventional sense but rather maintain a multi-generational covenant with each species. The spotted burden-birds (called "vrith" in their hand language) are not domesticated but rather willing partners who have evolved alongside the Varesh. The Beast Whisperers can be identified by subtle markings painted around their eyes, mirroring the patterns of whatever creature they most closely work with.

The Path Walkers serve as both merchants and diplomats on the rare occasions the city must interact with outsiders. Unlike other Varesh, they train extensively in spoken languages, though using their voices causes them physical discomfort they carefully hide. They are the only guild permitted to leave the city for extended periods, traveling the mountain passes and valleys to trade for the few resources Dael Verre cannot produce. They return changed from these journeys, carrying the noise of the outside world like a contamination they must purge through weeks of absolute silence.

The Pattern Weavers preserve knowledge and maintain the social order. Their complicated hand language has thousands of subtle variations impossible for outsiders to detect. They record nothing on paper or parchment—all history, science, and civic structure exists solely in memorized patterns passed from master to apprentice. They can be recognized by the intricate knot-work tattoos that begin at their wrists and disappear beneath their sleeves, each knot representing a specific body of knowledge they've mastered.

The final guild, the Balance Keepers, is rarely seen but exists at the heart of Varesh society. Numbering exactly nine at all times, they live in the highest chambers of the cliff city, emerging only at specific astronomical alignments. Their purpose remains their most closely guarded secret, but observers note their unusual appearance—eyes of solid amber color with no visible pupil, and skin bearing symmetrical patterns that seem to shift subtly when not directly observed.

The perfect stasis of Dael Verre is no accident but the result of rigorous effort by its inhabitants. Each Varesh undergoes a ceremony upon reaching adulthood (precisely on their eighteenth year) where they consume a tea brewed from limestone-filtered water and seven specific herbs. This concoction permanently alters their physiology, extending their lifespan to roughly two hundred years while rendering them capable of only extremely selective reproduction.

The Varesh choose to conceive children only when another resident approaches death, maintaining their population at exactly 343 individuals—seven cubed, a number of mystical significance in their belief system. This balance extends to everything in their society; each plant, animal, and stone has its purpose and place, contributing to an ecosystem of perfect stability.

What outsiders perceive as stagnation, the Varesh understand as harmony—the conscious rejection of chaos and growth for its own sake. They believe they are not merely inhabitants of the mountain but its voice and hands, extensions of an ancient consciousness that moves at geological speed. Their quiet city exists as a counterpoint to the frantic change of the outside world, a reminder that some things can remain beautiful through stillness rather than constant reinvention.
