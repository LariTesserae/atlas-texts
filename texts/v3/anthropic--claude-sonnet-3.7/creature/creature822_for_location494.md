# Inhabitants of the Living Plateau

## The Weavers

The primary consciousness of the plateau belongs to beings that have no true bodies as we understand them. The Weavers exist as patterns of bioelectric current flowing through the fungal networks that form the plateau's neural system. They emerged gradually as complexity increased within the living substrate, becoming a distributed intelligence that both is and is not separate from the plateau itself.

The Weavers experience time at a glacial pace. What humans perceive as a year might constitute a single "moment" of thought for them. They don't so much think in concepts as they process patterns—temperature variations, moisture distribution, the flow of nutrients. Their "language" is the subtle vibrations felt through the substrate, a communication system that encompasses everything from chemical gradients to the slow movements of the canopy.

They maintain the plateau's balance with gentle nudges rather than direct control—encouraging growth here, triggering spore release there. To them, the entire plateau is both body and home. Though they possess a form of consciousness that might be called intelligence, their awareness bears little resemblance to human thought. They have no concept of self versus other, only varying degrees of connection within the greater whole.

## The Tenders

Among the more recognizable fauna are the beings humans might call the Tenders—the deer-like creatures occasionally glimpsed at a distance. These beings stand about the height of a human at the shoulder, with six limbs that end in wide, soft pads that distribute their weight across the sensitive substrate. Their bodies appear fluid because they are partially plant themselves—their skin is a photosynthetic tissue interspersed with animal cells, giving them a mottled appearance that shifts between translucent green and pale amber.

The Tenders move through the plateau with deliberate care, stopping frequently to press their broad, flat faces against certain plants. Their mouths contain no teeth but specialized proboscises that can extract precise amounts of nutrients or inject compounds produced in specialized organs within their elongated torsos.

They maintain crucial aspects of the plateau's ecology—pollinating certain structures, pruning others, and transferring specialized symbiotic organisms from one region to another. Unlike Earth's animals, they weren't domesticated by the plateau's consciousness; rather, they co-evolved as mobile extensions of the plateau itself. Each carries within its brain a fragment of the Weavers' awareness, connected to the greater whole through pulsed bioelectric signals.

The Tenders live for centuries, growing slowly more plant-like with age. The eldest eventually root themselves near the mysterious tower structures, their limbs gradually extending into branch-like appendages as they transition to a final life stage of immobile communion with the plateau.

## The Messengers

The winged creatures resembling amphibians are known as Messengers. No larger than a human hand, these beings possess diaphanous wings that span three times their body length. Their skin is a semi-transparent membrane that shifts color based on the information they carry—for they function as the rapid response system of the plateau.

While the Weavers' communication travels slowly through the substrate, Messengers can quickly transport crucial chemical compounds, spores, or sensory information across the plateau. Their bodies contain specialized sacs that can synthesize compounds based on what they've absorbed elsewhere, effectively functioning as flying pharmacies and data couriers.

Messengers travel in spiraling patterns, rarely flying straight lines. They navigate by sensing the bioelectric fields generated by the plateau itself, following invisible pathways of charge differential. Though individually simple organisms with minimal neural capacity, they operate as a collective intelligence—no single Messenger contains much awareness, but together they form a distributed network that functions as a single organism.

Unlike most Earth creatures, Messengers don't reproduce individually. Instead, they emerge from the spiral towers when needed, budding from specialized tissues within the structures. Their lifespan varies dramatically based on their purpose—some exist for merely hours to deliver urgent compounds, while others may live for decades as long-term monitors of distant regions.

## The Luminous Ones

The glowing slug-like creatures serve as the plateau's maintenance system. They move slowly along the surface and through the middle layer, secreting enzymes that break down dead material and transform it into forms the plateau can readily reabsorb. Their bioluminescence serves multiple purposes—it attracts certain beneficial organisms, repels potential harmful ones, and provides a visual indicator of the plateau's biochemical health.

The patterns of their light are not random. They pulse in synchronized rhythms that convey information about local conditions, forming a visible manifestation of the plateau's "thoughts." Areas experiencing stress or imbalance will draw larger numbers of these beings, their combined light creating spectacular displays that signal the need for attention from Tenders or other regulatory organisms.

The Luminous Ones reproduce by fission, splitting into two identical organisms when they've accumulated sufficient mass. They lack anything resembling a brain but possess a sophisticated chemical sensing system distributed throughout their bodies. What might appear as individual creatures are actually semi-autonomous extensions of a single distributed organism that spans the entire plateau.

## The Dwellers

The most enigmatic inhabitants reside within the tower structures. These beings—if they can be called that—exist in a state between matter and energy. They appear as shifting patterns of light within the clear pools, sometimes coalescing into forms that vaguely resemble elaborate crystalline structures or flowing liquid geometries.

The Dwellers function as something like the plateau's dream-state or higher consciousness. They process information collected from throughout the living system, integrating it into patterns too complex for even the Weavers to fully comprehend. They might be considered the plateau's poets or philosophers—if such human concepts could apply to entities so utterly different from terrestrial life.

Communication with the Dwellers is possible, though profoundly difficult for human minds. A person who sits beside one of their pools for extended periods might begin to perceive patterns in the shifting lights that trigger unusual synaptic connections. The resulting experience feels less like receiving information and more like temporarily participating in a vast and alien mode of perception—glimpsing relationships between seemingly unrelated phenomena, experiencing time as a spatial dimension, or perceiving the plateau as a single moment extended across millennia.

Some believe the Dwellers are the plateau's way of reaching outward—attempting to understand existences beyond its boundaries through abstract modeling and consciousness experiments. Others theorize they might be the germinal form of the plateau's next evolutionary leap—a transition toward a state of being no longer bound to physical substrate.

## The Visitors

Humans are not the first non-plateau entities to explore this realm. Occasionally, beings from elsewhere arrive—some deliberately, others by accident. The plateau neither welcomes nor rejects these visitors. Instead, it observes, learns, and sometimes incorporates aspects of them into itself.

Those who stay too long begin to change. The plateau's microscopic organisms gradually colonize visitor bodies, forming symbiotic relationships that blur the boundary between self and environment. Thoughts become slower, more diffuse. The distinction between individual consciousness and the greater awareness of the plateau begins to dissolve.

Some visitors resist this integration and leave. Others surrender to it willingly, becoming new patterns within the vast tapestry of the living plateau—their memories, knowledge and perspective absorbed and preserved as the plateau continues its patient evolution toward unknown horizons of being.
