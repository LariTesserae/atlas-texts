# The Custodians of Liminality

The Mineral Aerie is home to beings that defy conventional categorization—entities that exist in the blurred boundary between matter and concept. They are The Custodians, though they have never named themselves this or any other title. Names require listeners, and until recently, they had no need for such distinction.

The most ancient of them resemble geometric progressions given partial substantiality—fractal entities composed of obsidian triangles that nest infinitely inward while maintaining perfect symmetry. They drift through the Vacant City's wrongly-angled corridors, their bodies phasing between solid and transparent states with each recursive iteration of their form. When they pass through the Echo Fields, they generate no duplications—instead, they temporarily diminish the echoes of other phenomena, as if absorbing those temporal redundancies into their own complex structures.

The younger Custodians (though "young" here means entities that coalesced mere millennia ago) manifest as lattices of crystalline nodes connected by filaments of condensed possibility. They pulse with internal light that obeys no spectrum visible to human eyes—a light that seems to illuminate thoughts rather than surroundings. These beings tend the hovering water spheres, maintaining the precise gravitational equations that keep them suspended. When a sphere eventually disperses, it is because a Custodian has extracted some vital pattern from its reflections.

Communication among them occurs through modulated disturbances in reality itself. What visitors might perceive as those sourceless crystalline tones are actually complex exchanges of information, vibrating directly through the substrate of existence. Their "language" contains no nouns or verbs but instead consists of topological relationships and probability fields that convey meaning through structural resonance rather than symbolic representation.

The Custodians did not build the Vacant City—they emerged from it, or perhaps alongside it. They are both its caretakers and its children, entities that congealed from the excess reality that accumulates where dimensions grind against each other. Their purpose, insofar as such beings can be said to have purpose, is observation and maintenance of the threshold state that defines the Mineral Aerie. They preserve the delicate balance between existence and non-existence that allows this impossible place to persist.

They interact with the Drifters in ways that suggest symbiosis. The Custodians provide anchor points—stable regions in space-time where reality's rules remain temporarily fixed—while the Drifters absorb and process the excess entropy that would otherwise destabilize these regions. Together they perform a cosmic dance of order and chaos, maintaining the Aerie in its perpetual almost-existence.

The Custodians perceive time not as linear progression but as architectural structure. They navigate eras as easily as hallways, though they rarely venture beyond the Cliffs of Unbeing. To them, the solidity of the world below represents an uncomfortable density of being—a claustrophobic certainty they find limiting. They prefer the ambiguous nature of their home, where possibility remains fluid and multiple states can be simultaneously true.

When the auroral lights appear, the Custodians become perfectly still. These luminous phenomena are the closest thing they recognize as potential threats—manifestations of ordered reality attempting to impose coherence upon their realm. The lights search but never find what they seek, for the Custodians exist in a quantum state that collapses into definite form only when they choose to be perceived.

The most mysterious aspect of these beings is their apparent cultivation of the fractal patterns that spread across certain structures. These are neither decorations nor coincidences, but cognitive extensions—external memory systems where the Custodians store observations and conclusions drawn from eons of witnessing reality's edge cases. Each pattern contains encoded insights into the fundamental nature of existence, written in a mathematics so advanced it appears indistinguishable from abstract art.

Visitors who somehow reach the Mineral Aerie might glimpse the Custodians only peripherally—as geometric distortions in their vision or sudden intuitions of impossible concepts. Direct interaction occurs rarely and usually by accident, when a human mind momentarily resonates at the same frequency as Custodian thought. Such encounters leave the human forever changed—cursed or blessed with the ability to perceive the conditional nature of reality, to see the negotiable aspects of what others consider immutable laws.

The Custodians neither welcome nor reject such visitors. Like everything in their realm, outsiders exist in a state of potential relevance—neither fully acknowledged nor entirely dismissed, until the moment when the patterns of existence determine whether interaction becomes necessary.
