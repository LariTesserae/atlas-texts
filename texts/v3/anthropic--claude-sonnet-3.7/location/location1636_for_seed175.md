# The Cathedral Plateaus

The sound arrives before anything else. It forces itself upon you—a deep, ceaseless roar that seems to come from the fabric of reality itself. It's not painful, but it fills every available space, makes your chest cavity vibrate with its insistence. The sound is everywhere, inescapable, eternal.

As your eyes adjust to the dim light, you begin to discern the source: massive industrial turbines—thousands of them—stretching across an endless high plateau. They are ancient things, weathered by countless seasons, their once-gleaming metal now patinated with verdigris and lichen. They reach up into the low-hanging clouds, drawing moisture from the perpetually damp air. The condensation runs down their vast frames in rivulets, creating a secondary symphony of drips and splashes beneath the overwhelming drone.

The plateau extends beyond sight in all directions, a continental expanse that carries the same features to horizons unreachable in a lifetime of walking. The elevation brings you close to the cloud ceiling, which hangs perhaps only a hundred meters above the highest turbines. The air is cool against your skin, carrying the taste of minerals and wet stone.

Between the turbines—which stand like ancient sentinels, spaced at mathematically precise intervals—stretches an expanse of low, lush vegetation. Mosses in a hundred shades of green, blue, and purple carpet the rocky ground. Ferns with translucent fronds unfurl in the spaces where more water gathers. There are no flowers, no trees, no bushes—just the primordial simplicity of cryptogams thriving in the perpetual cool dampness.

The ground itself is a marvel of geological time. Flat expanses of stone are interrupted by perfectly circular depressions filled with water—thousands upon thousands of them, ranging from hand-span to lake-sized. Each pool is rimmed with precisely eroded stone, as though the plateau has been subjected to the same rainfall pattern for millennia, creating these vessels through infinite patience. The water in each is perfectly still, despite the roaring air around them, as though existing in a different reality.

The turbines themselves are a paradox. No evidence remains of who built them or why. They bear no markings, no signs of maintenance or control. Yet they turn, eternally harvesting some energy that remains invisible. Their motion never varies, never accelerates or slows. The mechanical precision feels both industrial and sacred, like the heartbeat of a mechanical deity.

Looking closer, you notice the turbines don't connect to anything. No power lines, no transformers, no grid. Each stands in perfect isolation, performing its endless task for no discernible purpose. Perhaps they maintain the climate itself—the perfect moisture, the enduring coolness, the unchanging conditions that have allowed this plateau ecosystem to reach perfect stability.

Nothing moves across this landscape except the turbines and the low clouds. No birds fly overhead, no insects buzz between the ferns, no creatures disturb the perfect pools. The only change comes from the endless process of water condensing, falling, collecting, and evaporating again—a cycle as established as the rotation of the planet itself.

As twilight deepens, you realize the dim light never truly fades to darkness. A soft, diffuse illumination persists from the turbines themselves—not electric light, but a bioluminescent glow from the mosses and lichens that have colonized their structures. The sound remains undiminished, a wall of noise that has become almost meditative in its constancy.

This is a place of industrial sublimity—a manufactured landscape that has transcended its origins to become something timeless. The turbines will turn tomorrow exactly as they turn today, as they have turned for centuries before and will for centuries after. The water will condense, fall, and collect in precisely the same pattern. The vegetation will neither expand nor retreat. The sound will never cease.

The Cathedral Plateaus breathe their mechanical breath, maintaining a perfect equilibrium that requires no witnesses, no caretakers, no purpose beyond its own magnificent existence.
