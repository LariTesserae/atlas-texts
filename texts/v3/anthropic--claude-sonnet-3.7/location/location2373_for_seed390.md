# The Suspended Terraces of Nimburest

The city of Nimburest grows in tiers along the nearly vertical faces of what locals call the Reaching Mountains. From a distance, the terraced structures appear as horizontal striations against the sheer granite walls, like layers of sediment in some accelerated geological process. The mountains themselves are newly formed by planetary standards—sharp-edged and angular, their peaks still in the process of being sculpted by wind and occasional rains.

The air here is cool and thin. Breathing demands intention from visitors unaccustomed to the elevation. At dawn, residents emerge from their tiered homes to find the lower levels of the city shrouded in clouds that have crept up the valleys overnight. By midday, these clouds burn away, revealing the patchwork of cultivation terraces that extend like green shelves from the city's lower sections.

Water is precious in Nimburest, though not scarce. The mountains' heights capture moisture from passing clouds, which condenses on specially designed collection meshes—vast, fine-threaded nets stretched between adjacent peaks. The collected water trickles through an intricate network of ceramic pipes and channels, flowing downward through the city in a carefully engineered ballet of gravity and pressure. Every third day, the municipal sluice gates open to flood the agricultural terraces in sequence, creating a momentary waterfall effect down the mountainside before the moisture is trapped in the spongy, engineered soil.

The urban structure follows this water system. Narrow streets wind along contour lines, connected by staircases carved directly into the mountain stone. Buildings are stacked atop one another, each level's roof serving as the foundation for the level above. Construction never ceases; the sound of stone being cut is perpetual background noise, though muffled by distance and the absorptive quality of the prevalent greenery. New terraces are constantly being hewn into the rock face, extending the city's reach in a slow-motion conquest of the vertical.

Vegetation flourishes with unlikely vigor at this altitude. Genetically modified alpine plants with deep root systems stabilize the terraced soil. Vines with adhesive tendrils climb up the stone walls, their leaves shifting color through the day to maximize photosynthesis in the thin air. In the hanging gardens that connect neighboring structures, fruit-bearing shrubs grow alongside medicinal herbs, their scents mixing with the metallic tang of the omnipresent machinery.

The industrial heart of Nimburest lies within the mountains themselves. Tunnels extend deep into the stone, where turbines harness the power of underground thermal vents. Steam rises through shaft networks, powering the city's factories, which produce the specialized equipment needed for high-altitude living—from oxygen concentrators to the precision tools used in continuing the city's construction. Conveyor systems transport materials between levels, often visible as they span the gaps between distant sections of the settlement.

Animal life is sparse here. Occasional birds with unusually large lungs glide between the terraces. Specialized mountain goats have been domesticated for their wool and milk, kept in small numbers on the wider agricultural platforms. More common are the insect-like drones that flit between buildings, maintaining the water collection systems and pollinating crops where natural insects cannot survive.

At night, the city glows with a diffuse light that seems to emerge from within the stone itself. Phosphorescent algae, cultivated in transparent tubes, line the major pathways and public spaces. Workshops remain active through the darkness, their rhythmic mechanical sounds providing a counterpoint to the otherwise profound silence of the heights.

When seasonal rains come, they transform Nimburest temporarily. Water cascades down the mountainsides in controlled channels, but some always escapes, creating ephemeral waterfalls that dance between terraces. For a few days, the city gleams with moisture before returning to its typical cool dryness.

The newest sections of Nimburest, highest up the mountainsides, remain rough-hewn and unfinished—raw cuts in the living stone, with scaffolding extending into empty space. Here, the boundary between natural formation and human construction blurs, as if the city were less being built and more being revealed from within the mountain itself, an urban fossil being carefully extracted from the rock that has always contained it.
