# The Overgrowth

You first see it as a shimmer of green on the horizon, a verdant dome that rises gently from the low-lying marshlands surrounding it. The locals call it simply The Overgrowth, and they speak of it in hushed tones, as if raising their voices might draw its attention.

As you approach, the humidity thickens. The air becomes a tangible presence, heavy and hot against your skin, carrying the complex perfume of thousands of plant species in various stages of bloom, growth, and decay. This close, you realize what appeared to be a simple dome is actually a living skyline—a city entirely consumed and transformed by plant life.

The Overgrowth was once a conventional human settlement, built on slightly elevated ground among the marshes. Now it exists in perfect equilibrium with the vegetation that has claimed it. Buildings still stand, but their forms have become mere suggestions beneath blankets of vines, mosses, and flowering creepers. What were once streets are now tunnels through dense foliage, dappled with harsh sunlight that filters through gaps in the canopy above.

Water is everywhere but rarely seen directly. It seeps from walls, trickles along leaf channels, and collects in the cupped flowers that have adapted to serve as reservoirs. The ground squelches underfoot—a living sponge of moss and mycelium rather than soil. During the daily afternoon rains, the entire structure seems to sigh and expand, drinking deeply.

Despite the overwhelming plant presence, The Overgrowth teems with animal life. Birds of impossible colors nest in the higher reaches, their calls strangely muted by the absorptive quality of the surrounding vegetation. Insects form living clouds that pulse and shift like weather systems. Amphibians, evolved to particular niches within this enclosed ecosystem, display bioluminescent patterns across their translucent skins. Small, quick mammals dart between the levels of growth, some no larger than your thumb.

Most unsettling are the larger creatures that move slowly through the deepest parts of The Overgrowth. They resemble nothing so much as sections of the city that have uprooted themselves—amalgams of plant, building, and unknown flesh that patrol their territories with patient deliberation. The locals insist these beings are benign guardians, but few venture close enough to test this theory.

The structure of The Overgrowth defies conventional urban planning. What might have been a city square is now a cathedral-like space where immense flowers bloom and release clouds of spores that drift like golden snow. Former high-rises have become living towers, their internal spaces carved into warrens by roots and branches that somehow respect the basic structural integrity while completely reimagining the purpose of each room.

There is a rhythm to life here—slow, deliberate, but undeniably active. The plants themselves seem to migrate over weeks and months, opening passages and closing others. Certain sections bloom only during specific moon phases, creating temporary neighborhoods of color and light. Other areas remain perpetually in growth or decay, locked in their particular moment of the cycle.

The few humans who still dwell here have adapted completely. They build nothing, bring no technology. Instead, they have learned to read the growth patterns, to navigate by subtle signs in the vegetation. They cultivate relationships with specific plants, encouraging growth in patterns that provide shelter without disrupting the overall harmony. Their skin has taken on a faintly green cast, and some whisper that they no longer eat but photosynthesize like their botanical neighbors.

At sunset, when the harsh brightness finally relents, The Overgrowth transforms again. Bioluminescent fungi activate throughout the structure, creating constellations of soft blue and purple light. The temperature remains oppressive, but the humidity condenses into a fine mist that catches this gentle illumination and turns the air itself into a shimmering medium.

This is neither wilderness nor city—it is a third thing, a hybrid state where the boundary between built and grown has dissolved entirely. The Overgrowth exists in a perpetual present, simultaneously ancient and newly formed, constantly dying and being reborn, whispering its strange truths to anyone patient enough to listen.
