# The Suspended Cultivation

In the dim light that filters through the dome, clouds of nascent vapor curl around the growing platforms. These enormous discs of translucent metal hover impossibly in the air, their edges catching what little light penetrates the atmospheric control systems above. This is Stratum Eleven, one of the newer cultivation levels in the Hanging City of Keldai.

The air is cool and slightly damp—deliberately maintained by moisture regulators that release a fine mist every seventy minutes. Droplets collect on the undersides of the platforms, growing heavy before falling in artificial rain to feed the levels below. The sound of this water striking various surfaces—metal walkways, glass collection basins, leaves of climbing plants—creates a constant patter that residents claim helps them sleep at night.

From the observation platform where you stand, the vast open space of the stratum stretches in all directions. Floating cultivation discs at varying heights create a sparse three-dimensional constellation of green against the dark background. Some platforms grow dense with vegetation—experimental crops and modified forest patches—while others remain newly formed, their surfaces just beginning to accumulate the engineered soil that will eventually support life.

The city-builders are at work on the far side, where the dome's wall meets the void. Their massive machines—construction extrusions, they call them—slowly push new platforms out into existence. The process is geological in nature though entirely artificial: raw materials harvested from the mountain core are superheated, extruded, and then cooled rapidly in the controlled atmosphere, forming the characteristic cell-patterned platforms that defy gravity. The constant low hum of these machines underlies all other sounds in the stratum.

Connecting the platforms is a complex network of cable-cars and thin bridges, appearing almost like spider silk in the dimness. Workers in protective gear move between the platforms, tending to the plants, adjusting moisture levels, introducing new specimens. Their headlamps create moving points of light against the general gloom.

The fauna is surprisingly abundant for such a controlled environment. Engineered pollinators—some recognizable as bee or butterfly descendants, others entirely novel creations—move between flowering plants. Small maintenance drones that have taken on animal-like behaviors dart between platforms, carrying seeds or monitoring humidity levels. More concerning are the unplanned species: colonies of cliff-dwelling birds that have found their way inside and now nest in the structural supports, or the mysterious glowing creatures that sometimes appear on newly-formed platforms, examining the raw material before disappearing again.

What makes Stratum Eleven truly unsettling, though, is its unstable reality. The extreme elevation—this level sits nearly three kilometers above ground level, embedded into the side of a mountain that should not be able to support such a structure—has unexpected effects. Occasionally, sections of the stratum phase partially out of conventional space-time. Workers call these "slips," moments when a platform or section of walkway seems to occupy multiple positions simultaneously or vanishes altogether before reappearing. The city engineers insist these are merely optical illusions caused by temperature gradients and light refraction, but residents know better.

The slips are getting worse. Just yesterday, an entire cultivation platform with three workers disappeared for six hours before returning—with plants that had somehow grown three weeks during their absence. The workers refused to discuss what they saw.

Despite these anomalies—or perhaps because of them—the vegetation throughout the stratum grows with unusual vigor. Modified ferns curl around support struts, their fronds extending impossibly toward light sources. Fruit-bearing vines snake along walkways, producing heavy clusters of unfamiliar produce that the nutrition scientists insist are safe for consumption despite their occasional tendency to change color while being observed.

In the distance, a deep resonant tone signals a shift change. The slow-moving cable cars that have been heading outward now reverse direction, bringing the day crew back to the residential hub embedded in the mountain wall. Night workers emerge from the access tunnels, their specialized goggles already adjusted for the dimming light as the dome's opacity increases for the nocturnal cycle.

The cultivation continues its patient growth, plants extending toward light sources that sometimes aren't there. The construction extrusion machines press onward, creating space that wasn't previously possible. And somewhere in the depths of the stratum, another slip begins to form, reality bending around the impossible city hanging in the void.
