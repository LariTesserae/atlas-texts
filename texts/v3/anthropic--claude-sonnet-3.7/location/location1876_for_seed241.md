# The Treadwater

Vast plateaus stretch beyond comprehension, rising like titans from an unseen world below. Their flat tops, rounded by ages beyond counting, form a landscape that has reached a kind of immortality through relentless, patient transformation. The stone is dark and dense, worn smooth in some places to a polish that gleams dully in the ordinary daylight.

Water is the quiet architect here. It seeps from countless weeping walls where the plateau faces meet the sky, creating perpetual tears that track down the vertical surfaces. The tops of these mesas are not truly flat but subtly concave, holding shallow lakes that slowly feed the tears at the edges. The water doesn't pool deeply but spreads widely, creating mirror surfaces that reflect the uninterrupted sky. Where the plateaus connect via occasional land bridges, the water finds its wandering paths, tracing silver threads across the dark stone.

The air is warm, comfortable on the skin, carrying just enough moisture to feel alive but not oppressive. It moves in vast, lazy currents across these elevated plains. Sometimes the wind picks up enough to ripple the shallow lakes, but mostly there is a stillness that makes sound carry for impossible distances.

Vegetation is sparse but deliberate. Succulents with thick, waxy leaves grow in perfectly circular formations around the lakes' edges. Their pale green stands out against the dark stone. Occasional stands of slender trees rise no more than twenty feet high, their trunks smooth and silver-white, their canopies flat and spreading like hands reaching for the sun. Their roots somehow penetrate the ancient rock, finding invisible fractures to anchor themselves.

The fauna moves so slowly and infrequently that you might mistake them for oddly shaped rocks. Reptilian creatures with armored plates sun themselves on warm stones, sometimes not moving for days. Bird-like beings with impossibly wide wingspans ride the thermal currents, so high they appear as mere specks, descending rarely to drink from the lakes.

Scattered across this world-sized network of plateaus are the Pillars. These impossibly slender structures rise straight up from the stone, their material gleaming like polished metal but with an inner light that suggests something beyond mere reflection. Each Pillar is perfectly proportioned, exactly 100 meters tall, and spaced precisely 10 kilometers apart in a grid that extends beyond the horizon in all directions.

The Pillars are silent machines. They draw water from the atmosphere and from deep below the plateau surface, maintaining the perfect hydration of this world. They regulate temperature with invisible fields that can be felt as a gentle resistance when passing between them. Their technology is unobtrusive yet omnipresent, operating on principles that suggest manipulation of fundamental forces rather than mechanical processes.

When night falls, the water on the plateau surfaces begins to glow faintly with bioluminescent algae that have evolved in perfect symbiosis with this regulated world. The light is just enough to trace the outlines of the landscape without diminishing the majesty of the stars above.

Time passes differently here. The cycles of day and night march onward, but change comes at a pace almost beyond perception. A new fissure might form over centuries; a stand of the silver trees might spread a few meters in a millennium. Even the Pillars, for all their advanced technology, operate at this same unhurried pace, making minute adjustments to maintain the perfect equilibrium of this place.

This is the Treadwater, a world balanced on the edge of permanence, where even the technology that maintains it has become part of its ancient, ongoing story.
