# Neverseed

The high plateau of Neverseed sits like a vast altar to forgotten gods, a place where the boundary between raw material and conscious entity blurs. Here, in the perpetual amber twilight, the atmosphere appears dense but oddly non-reflective, absorbing light rather than scattering it. The air is warm, sometimes to the point of discomfort, but utterly dry—so dry that the concept of water seems theoretical, something spoken of in the whispered mythologies of the plateau's inhabitants.

The terrain extends for hundreds of miles, a seemingly endless expanse of sharp-edged, newly-formed geometric basalt and obsidian formations. These dark, glassy structures jut from the ground at impossible angles, their edges unworn by time. Patches of rusty dust collect in the angular valleys between formations, occasionally swept into brief, violent dust-devils that twist upward before collapsing back into stillness. The ground has a subtle give to it, as if the entire plateau is freshly cooled and not entirely settled.

What passes for vegetation here is sparse and puzzling—crystalline structures that might be mineral or might be alive, growing in slow, visible pulses. They capture something from the air that isn't quite moisture, exuding a faint amber luminescence. These growths cluster in the shadows of the obsidian formations, never in direct exposure to the dim, diffuse light that comes from everywhere and nowhere.

The plateau is home to The Nest, a sprawling settlement of interconnected structures built from the same obsidian that forms the natural landscape. The distinction between what was built and what emerged naturally is often impossible to determine. The structures stack vertically, taking advantage of the high altitude, as if reaching for something above. Narrow bridges span between tower-clusters, their undersides marked with primitive but mathematically precise etchings.

The inhabitants—and they are abundant—defy easy categorization. They move in swarms that coalesce and disperse with apparent randomness but underlying pattern. From a distance, they might be mistaken for large insects, but up close, each reveals a unique configuration of chitinous plates, sensitive appendages, and what might be tool-using limbs. They communicate through subtle vibrations transmitted through the obsidian, making the entire plateau a resonating chamber for conversations too complex and alien for outsiders to comprehend.

Their technology appears rudimentary—stone and obsidian tools, woven fiber slings for carrying objects, primitive kilns that glow with amber light—but the results of their labor suggest deeper knowledge. Their constructions sometimes shift and rearrange when unobserved, and the boundaries between individual "buildings" seem fluid and purpose-dependent.

The most disorienting aspect of Neverseed is its constant state of flux. Despite the absence of water or conventional erosion, the landscape undergoes continual transformation. Obsidian spires rise and collapse with audible creaking and occasional sharp reports that echo across the plateau. The inhabitants neither flee nor seem surprised by these changes, instead incorporating them into their movements and constructions with practiced precision.

At irregular intervals, the entire plateau experiences what the few human observers have termed "churns"—periods of accelerated change where structures rise and fall in minutes rather than days, and the swarms of inhabitants move in coordinated, complex patterns that resemble computational processes more than panic or celebration. During these churns, the amber light brightens momentarily, and new crystalline growths emerge fully-formed from the ground.

Sound here is minimal but laden with information—the subtle click of obsidian against obsidian, the whisper of the dust, and occasionally something that might be music or might be the plateau itself breathing. All sounds carry unusual distances in the dry air, but seem to stop abruptly at the plateau's edge, as if contained by an invisible boundary.

Those who have spent time on Neverseed speak of dreams filled with geometric patterns and the unshakable sense that the plateau itself is awake and watching—not malevolently, but with the cool curiosity of something that processes time and purpose in ways fundamentally different from human understanding.
