# The Weeping Meadow

High upon a lofty plateau, the Weeping Meadow sprawls beneath an unnaturally still sky. The air here is warm and heavy, clinging to the skin with a peculiar intimacy that feels both comforting and intrusive. From a distance, the landscape appears almost normal—a vast, open expanse ringed by jagged peaks that pierce the horizon like freshly-formed teeth, their edges still sharp and unworn by time.

But as one draws closer, the wrongness becomes apparent.

The ground undulates subtly, as if breathing in impossibly slow rhythm. The sparse vegetation consists of thin, crystalline growths that might be mistaken for grass from afar, but reveal themselves as delicate mineral formations—quartz-like filaments that conduct moisture upward from hidden aquifers. They chime softly when disturbed, producing notes that hover at the threshold of hearing, more felt than heard.

Water is the dominating presence. It doesn't flow in conventional streams but weeps continuously from the very substance of the plateau itself. Rock faces glisten with perpetual tears that emerge from nowhere, gathering into shallow pools that reflect the sky with perfect stillness. These pools never overflow, their waters instead disappearing at the same rate they form, creating a constant state of brimming fullness. In places, the ground itself seems saturated, not quite marshy but unnervingly moist, yielding slightly underfoot with each step.

The mineral growths cluster more densely around these pools, creating complex geometries that seem to follow mathematical patterns just beyond comprehension. Their arrangement suggests intentional design, yet no hand shaped them. They grow with glacial slowness, extending perhaps a few millimeters per decade.

Most disquieting is the absence of animal life. No insects buzz, no birds call, no creatures disturb the crystalline growths or drink from the weeping pools. The silence is profound, broken only by the nearly imperceptible chiming of the crystal grass and the soft patter of water emerging from solid stone.

The very substance of reality feels tenuous here. In certain spots, particularly where the light strikes the water pools at oblique angles, space itself seems to fold inward. Visual distortions ripple outward in slow, measured pulses—objects behind these ripples momentarily fragment, reassembling slightly altered, colors shifted toward spectra not normally visible to human eyes.

Time progresses strangely. The sun crosses the sky at its usual pace, but shadows sometimes lag behind their casting objects, catching up in sudden jerks. The warm air occasionally develops pockets of different temperatures with boundaries as distinct as glass walls.

At the meadow's center stands the only evidence of intelligence—not a built structure but a perfect arrangement of seven crystal formations, each taller than a human, positioned precisely to match the pattern of a constellation that doesn't exist in our sky. Each formation contains what appears to be a primitive tool suspended within its transparent mass: a hammer, a blade, a needle, a bowl, a flute, a stylus, and at the center, a perfect sphere that might be a counting stone. These artifacts seem made by hands, yet they float impossibly within solid crystal that shows no seam or break.

The Weeping Meadow exists as a place where reality's rules have partially unspooled. It changes, but with such slowness that transformation can only be perceived across generations. The water weeps, the crystals grow, the ripples in space-time pulse outward, all in a rhythm of cosmic patience that makes human timescales seem absurdly fleeting by comparison.
