# The Calling Plain

The land unfolds like a thin leather skin, stretched taut over invisible bones. Low, undulating terrain extends to distant horizons where the plain meets murky skies in an unsatisfying blur. The ground is mostly bare—a patchwork of exposed rock and compacted soil the color of weak tea, occasionally broken by stubbornly persistent tufts of stiff, razor-edged grass that grow in loose spiral formations.

Morning brings a skittering mist that clings to the ground, gathering in shallow depressions before burning away by midday. The air stays cool even as the weak sun climbs higher, a perpetual autumn chill that seeps through clothing and settles in the joints. This coolness preserves the occasional puddles left by the infrequent rains—shallow mirrors that reflect a sky eternally threatening weather that rarely manifests in full force.

The plain's emptiness is its defining feature, yet this emptiness thrums with life. The terrain appears newly shaped, as though some great force recently pressed its palm here, leaving impressions that erosion hasn't yet had time to soften. Sharp-edged rock formations punctuate the landscape at irregular intervals, their fracture lines fresh and glinting with embedded minerals that catch what little light filters through the constant thin cloud cover.

Listen—the emptiness is an illusion.

The air vibrates with sound. A persistent, rhythmic clicking rises and falls across the plain, emanating from countless chitinous creatures that have evolved to use the rock formations as amplification chambers. These hand-sized, many-legged beings—somewhere between crustacean and insect—scuttle across the terrain in pulsing waves, their movements synchronized in patterns that suggest intelligence without confirming it. Their shells bear natural markings like primitive hieroglyphs, different for each swarm.

Overhead, the source of many of the plain's loudest sounds: vast flocks of hollow-boned avians with translucent skin stretched between elongated digits. They ride the thermal currents, emitting mournful calls through specialized throat sacs that resonate across the emptiness. Their migration paths form invisible highways above, marked only by their haunting cries and the occasional molted feather spiraling down.

The plain's few structures stand in mute testimony to those who came before. Simple stone circles—some merely ankle-height, others reaching to the waist—dot the landscape at seemingly random intervals. Their arrangement follows no discernible pattern to the casual observer, but those who have studied them insist they map celestial bodies now absent from the sky. Within many circles lie primitive tools of knapped stone and occasionally copper, green with verdigris—cutting implements, hammers, objects whose purpose has been lost to time.

More recent inhabitants have left their mark as well. Crude shelters of stacked stone with roofs of woven dry grass huddle near the rare permanent springs, where water bubbles up through cracks in the bedrock, cool and mineral-rich. These dwellings show signs of regular abandonment and reoccupation, as their builders follow the patterns of the plain's abundant fauna. Nothing here is permanent except the land itself.

At irregular intervals—perhaps once every twenty days—the plain transforms. Dark clouds gather with unusual speed, bringing not rain but a dense, silver-tinged mist that reduces visibility to arm's length. During these "veiling times," the clicking creatures fall silent, the avians land and huddle in motionless groups, and from somewhere beyond perception comes a low, vibrating hum that seems to emanate from the ground itself. Those who have experienced this phenomenon describe a sensation of being watched by something vast and patient.

When the mist recedes, the plain appears subtly altered—rock formations shifted slightly, new depressions formed, old ones filled. The primitive inhabitants mark these changes with arrangements of colored stones, creating a crude calendar of the plain's mysterious breath.

Night brings its own transformations. Bioluminescent fungal colonies that remain invisible during daylight hours begin to glow with gentle blue-green light, revealing patterns across the plain that suggest deliberate cultivation. Small nocturnal mammals emerge from hidden burrows, their oversized ears swiveling constantly to track the movements of predators—six-legged hunters with paddle-like feet that allow them to move silently across even the most fragile surfaces.

The plain endures, newly formed yet somehow ancient, sparse yet teeming, empty yet full of voices calling across the cool, dim expanse.
