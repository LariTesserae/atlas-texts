# The Heart Chamber

This is a wholly built space, ancient beyond reckoning, and yet somehow intimate—a cathedral of function. You stand within what appears to be the inner chamber of an impossibly complex mechanical heart, constructed entirely from dark, polished wood that gleams with moisture.

The chamber sits high in whatever larger structure contains it, perhaps a mountain or a colossus—you cannot tell from within. The ceiling arches twenty meters above, ribbed with interlocking wooden beams that have been worn smooth by time. Every surface is carved with minuscule pictographs and symbols, so densely packed they appear as texture rather than language.

Water seeps constantly through invisible joints in the wooden construction, running in rivulets down curved walls and collecting in channels that flow through the floor in intricate patterns. The moisture feeds countless species of moss and lichen that carpet every surface not regularly disturbed by the machine's movement. Emerald cushions of bryophyte spill over carved edges, their tiny fronds moving in the cold air. Delicate shelf fungi, pale as bone, protrude in tiered formations from the corners where beams meet walls.

The chamber operates in a perpetual state of frigid dampness. Your breath fogs before you, joining the mist that hangs in the air, occasionally condensing into droplets that fall back to feed the vegetative carpet below. The constant moisture has not rotted the wood—whatever ancient technology treated these timbers has rendered them immune to decay, though not to the slow polish of time.

The noise is overwhelming, a thunderous cacophony of wooden gears, water channels, and counterweights all working in perfect concert. THOOM-ka-THOOM-ka-THOOM-ka—a rhythm like an enormous heart, so loud you feel it in your chest more than hear it. Enormous wooden pistons slide through greased channels. Wheels of interlocking teeth, some as tall as a person, others delicate as watchworks, turn with metronomic precision. The sound never varies in tempo or volume—it is the absolute unchanging rhythm that has beaten here since before history.

Near the center stands a raised platform surrounded by radiating walkways of polished wood. The few creatures that inhabit this space can occasionally be glimpsed—pale, eyeless salamanders that slip between moss patches, and tiny translucent crabs that scuttle through the water channels, feeding on whatever microscopic life thrives in this strange ecosystem.

The chamber's purpose remains enigmatic. The machine seems to pump water throughout its system, but to what end? Perhaps it regulates something in the greater structure, or perhaps it simply is—an eternal machine built by forgotten hands for purposes no living being remembers.

The strangest feature is how the entire chamber—every gear, channel, and beam—is carved from a single, continuous piece of wood. There are no joins, no separate components fitted together. The entire heart chamber appears to have been carved from one impossible tree, or perhaps grown into its current form through some incomprehensible process of guided growth.

Despite being built, the place feels organic. Despite being mechanical, it feels alive. And despite being deafening, there is a peculiar peace here in witnessing something that has functioned perfectly, without change or interruption, perhaps since the beginning of time itself.
