# The Suspended City of Aquaterra

A vast dome of crystalline-clear water hangs in the mountain air, defying gravity yet perfectly stable. This is Aquaterra—a cathedral-sized aquatic sphere suspended between three mountain peaks, held in place by ancient engineering that no one fully understands anymore.

The water doesn't fall. It simply exists as a perfect, self-contained sphere perhaps four hundred feet in diameter, its surface tension maintained by a network of massive, living root structures that cradle it from below and wrap around its equator. These primordial roots emerge from the mountainsides, reaching out to embrace the water, their surfaces worn smooth by centuries of contact.

Within this aqueous realm, an entire city thrives. The architecture is entirely botanical—buildings formed from trained and tended waterplants that grow in dense, deliberate patterns. Hollow kelp-like structures serve as dwelling places, their interiors dry despite being submerged. Massive coral-shaped growths form community gathering spaces. Phosphorescent algae provide gentle illumination that refracts through the water in constantly shifting patterns.

The human inhabitants—perhaps a few hundred souls—have adapted to this dual existence. They've evolved specialized breathing techniques that allow them to move between air and water with minimal transition time. Their simple tools are crafted from plant materials harvested within their watery home—bone-hard seed pods fashioned into knives, flexible stems woven into nets, fermented saps stored in bladder-pouches.

The temperature inside Aquaterra remains consistently cool, regulated by the thermal properties of the massive water body. It's refreshing rather than cold—perfect for the semi-aquatic lifestyle of its residents.

Fish of spectacular variety populate the water-city, swimming freely through neighborhoods and gardens. They're not merely wild creatures but semi-domesticated partners in this ecosystem. Some species help maintain the plantlife, others provide food, while the most intelligent varieties—with their multifaceted eyes and peculiar chirping communications—assist with various civic functions, carrying messages and small items throughout the city.

The sound within Aquaterra is a constant symphony—the melodic bubbling of water through plant structures, the resonant hums produced by vibrating leaves, the clicks and songs of the fish, and the distinctive ringing that occurs when residents strike special sound-producing coral formations to signal community events. This soundscape is amplified by the water medium, filling the entire sphere with rich, overlapping tones.

Outside the sphere, three small platforms allow visitors to observe the city from dry land. The Aquaterrans occasionally emerge to trade with mountain dwellers, exchanging rare water plants and unusual fish products for metals they cannot produce in their liquid domain.

The entire ecosystem moves slowly, with growth and change occurring over seasons rather than days. The plantlife is constantly renewing itself, old structures gradually giving way to new ones in a carefully managed cycle that maintains the city's form while allowing for necessary evolution.

At regular intervals, small portions of the outer water layer transform into mist, creating a protective fog that shrouds Aquaterra from distant viewers, keeping it hidden from all except those who already know of its existence. This mist eventually returns to the sphere, maintaining the delicate water balance that has sustained this unusual settlement for untold generations.
