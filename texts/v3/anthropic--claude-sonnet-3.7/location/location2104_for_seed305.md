# The Crowded Spine

The obsidian needles thrust upward like a forest of black glass, packed so tightly together that even a hand cannot fit between them. These newly-formed volcanic extrusions create a cathedral-sized chamber of impossible density – a bristling, confined architecture of stone born from the earth's recent convulsions.

Heat dominates everything. The ground radiates a constant, punishing warmth that makes the air shimmer and dance. Small pockets of condensation form on the cooler sides of some spires, only to sizzle away as the day progresses. The obsidian absorbs the sun's assault, becoming almost too hot to approach, yet alone touch.

Between the forest of needles, the ground is a chaotic jumble of sharp-edged scoria and pumice fragments, their edges still keen and unworn by time. The occasional twisted and stunted desert grass emerges from cracks where minuscule pockets of soil have formed, their yellow-brown stems leaning desperately toward gaps in the needle canopy where sunlight penetrates.

The sound is unexpected and relentless: a deep, resonant humming that ebbs and flows as hot air moves through the forest of stone. When the intermittent desert winds find their way into this labyrinth, the obsidian needles sing in different pitches – a discordant, alien chorus that echoes through the chamber. Occasionally, the entire structure seems to groan as the cooling process continues beneath the surface.

Water appears in brief, dramatic moments. Every few days, a sudden cloudburst sends raindrops hissing against the hot stone surfaces, creating momentary plumes of steam that rise and twist between the needles. This fleeting moisture collects in small, natural basins at the base of certain spires, forming temporary pools that attract the few creatures hardy enough to venture here.

These visitors are rare – primarily small, heat-adapted lizards with scales that mimic the obsidian's sheen. They dart between shadows, hunting the occasional insects that make their home in the sparse vegetation. More unusual are the colony of specialized bats that roost during the day in the highest reaches of the tallest needles, somehow immune to the heat, emerging at dusk to sweep through the chamber in tight, practiced formations.

At the chamber's center, a crude stone altar stands – the only evidence of human presence. Weathered but relatively recent, it bears no markings or decorations, just a roughly hewn surface stained with what might be berry juice or perhaps something darker. Scattered nearby are several broken clay vessels, their fragments partially buried in the loose volcanic soil.

The entire formation exists in a state of slow transformation. The newest needles occasionally shed outer layers with sharp, cracking reports. Small avalanches of obsidian shards cascade down, gradually altering the pathways between spires. The heat causes imperceptible expansions and contractions, a breathing of stone that over time will reshape this cramped, cluttered chamber.

This is a place of birth and becoming – a landscape still finding its form, hostile yet mesmerizing in its crowded, chaotic beauty.
