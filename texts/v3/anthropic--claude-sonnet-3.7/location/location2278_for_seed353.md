# The Hollow Under Arasheed

The passage is narrow. You notice your breath first: the warmth of it hanging in front of your face, visible in the darkness as a momentary ghost against the wet stone walls. The corridor descends at an angle that feels purposeful – not a natural formation, though what tool carved it remains unclear. The only light comes from behind you, a distant amber glow from the city above that barely illuminates the slick walls.

Water trickles persistently, flowing down the walls and collecting in a shallow channel at the center of the floor. The sound of your footsteps changes as you follow this channel deeper: hollow echoes that suggest something larger ahead. The corridor opens suddenly into what was once a maintenance chamber, now transformed.

You've found the Hollow.

It appears, at first glance, to be an abandoned industrial chamber, perhaps once housing water purification equipment for Arasheed's lower districts. Massive pipes, now mostly dormant, crisscross the ceiling thirty feet above. The chamber itself is oddly spherical, as though hollowed out from beneath the city like the inside of a fruit. The floor is a shallow pool, ankle-deep and perfectly still except for ripples from the channel you followed and droplets from the ceiling.

But the darkness plays tricks. When your eyes adjust, you see that the water isn't quite water. It's too viscous, too dark, and sometimes when you look directly at it, too absent – as if parts of the floor simply aren't there. The city's maintenance workers avoid this place, though the equipment here is theoretically still tied into Arasheed's infrastructure.

In the center of the chamber stands what appears to be a control station – a metal platform rising from the pool with a console of levers, gauges, and brass switches. Dim emergency lighting casts the station in a red glow that doesn't penetrate far into the chamber. The machinery hums almost imperceptibly, a vibration more felt than heard.

The walls themselves are the most disquieting feature. What first appeared to be rust or mineral deposits are revealed, upon closer inspection, to be tiny movements – thousands of minuscule crustaceans, pale and eyeless, crawling in precise formations. They form patterns that seem almost like circuit diagrams or city maps, constantly shifting yet maintaining some unknowable organization.

Above these formations, nests cling to pipes and wall fixtures – not built by birds, but by what locals call "hollow mice" – creatures that resemble rodents but with translucent skin revealing organs that glow with bioluminescent bacteria. They scurry across the ceiling infrastructure, seemingly unbothered by gravity, gathering the strange fungi that grow in patches where steam occasionally vents.

The air is warm and thick with moisture, carrying an electric scent like ozone mixed with copper. Occasionally, a distant industrial sound penetrates from the city above – the clank of machinery or hiss of steam – but these sounds arrive distorted, sometimes playing in reverse or looping unnaturally.

Most disturbing is how the chamber's dimensions seem inconsistent. The far wall appears sometimes closer, sometimes impossibly distant. Pipes that should connect logically to others instead vanish into darkness or loop back on themselves. If you watch long enough, you might notice that some of the dripping water falls upward.

Those who've spent time here report the same phenomenon: the longer you stay, the more the boundaries between the Hollow and the city above begin to blur. The control station's gauges show readings for systems that don't exist in Arasheed's infrastructure plans. The maintenance charts on the wall display a version of the city that's subtly wrong – streets that run at impossible angles, buildings numbered in a system no city clerk recognizes.

The few urban explorers who've mapped this place claim there are other chambers connected to this one, but their maps contradict each other. The only consistent feature is the control station at the center, which all agree should never be touched. One engineer who studied the gauges claimed they were measuring the "strangeness gradient" between realities, but she disappeared shortly after presenting her findings.

The Hollow remains – a pocket dimension of industrial purpose gone awry, hidden beneath a city that has forgotten its existence, populated by creatures that have adapted to its warped reality, neither fully abandoned nor properly maintained, quietly humming with its own inscrutable purpose.
