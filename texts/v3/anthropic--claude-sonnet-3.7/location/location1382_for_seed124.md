# The Terraces of Ven Pahzi

The rising steam obscures them at first—the perfect angles cutting through the billowing white curtains. As the hot winds shift, you see how this highland plateau has been transformed into a rising series of obsidian-black terraces. Each terrace holds a shallow pool of water, perfectly still despite the heat that raises a constant mist from their surfaces. The water reflects the hard white sky above, creating a disorienting effect—geometric planes of mirrored light surrounded by dark stone borders.

Ven Pahzi sits high among the younger mountains of this region. The natural plateau has been sculpted over generations into this vast network of terraced fields, each precisely leveled and connected by an intricate system of canals and spillways. From your vantage point, the terraces extend outward for at least a mile in all directions, creating a human-made basin that holds the abundant water that would otherwise cascade down the mountainside.

The dark stone that frames each pool is not natural to this place. It was quarried from volcanic deposits further inland and transported here, prized for its ability to absorb and retain the intense heat of the sun. This heat keeps the water from stagnating, maintaining a constant cycle of evaporation and condensation. The air is thick with moisture that never quite materializes as rain, instead hanging as a perpetual haze that softens all distant features.

Between the terraces grow carefully cultivated aquatic plants—some with broad leaves that float on the surface of the pools, others rising on thick stalks above the water. Their colors range from deep purples to vibrant greens, creating patches of lushness amid the geometric precision of the stonework. Some plants produce large bulbous pods that the locals harvest for their oils and fibers.

The settlement itself is built on a slightly elevated section of the plateau. Buildings of the same black stone rise in tightly packed clusters, rarely exceeding three stories. Their flat roofs are designed to collect the condensation that forms in the cooler hours before dawn, channeling it through interior cisterns. Narrow streets wind between these structures, many bridging over the canals that extend from the terrace system to provide water throughout the urban area.

The industrial heart of Ven Pahzi is visible as a collection of low, wide buildings at the southeastern edge of the settlement. There, the abundant water powers a series of turbines—their mechanisms largely hidden from view, but their presence betrayed by the occasional metal-on-metal groan that carries through the thick air. These engines drive looms and presses that process the harvested plant materials into textiles and papers prized throughout the region.

Despite the constant activity of water movement and industrial processes, Ven Pahzi maintains an unusual quietude. The omnipresent moisture dampens sound, and the people who tend the terraces and operate the machinery move with practiced efficiency, requiring minimal communication. The most persistent sound is the soft hiss of evaporation and the occasional hollow drip as water moves through the canal system.

The light here is relentless—the sun's intensity magnified by reflection off the countless water surfaces. Those who live and work here wear wide-brimmed hats woven from local fibers and treat their skin with protective oils. The harsh brightness bleaches color from everything it touches, rendering even the most vibrant plant matter pale after prolonged exposure.

Though human presence is evident in every carefully engineered feature of this landscape, animal life is notably absent. No birds disturb the mirror-like surfaces of the pools; no insects buzz around the flowering plants. The locals attribute this to mineral properties in the water, though some whisper about deliberate efforts in generations past to create a place of perfect stillness, where even the smallest ripple can be observed and interpreted as an omen.

The terraces of Ven Pahzi exist in a state of slow transformation. The mineral-rich waters gradually deposit new layers on the stone borders, thickening them incrementally over decades. The plant species are selectively cultivated to grow stronger with each generation, adapted to the intense heat and constant moisture. Even the settlement expands at a glacial pace, new structures appearing only after years of planning and preparation.

As day transitions to evening, the quality of light shifts but barely diminishes. The white sky takes on a yellow tinge, and the reflection across the terraced pools creates the illusion of molten gold flowing across the landscape. The heat persists well into the night, releasing slowly from the black stone that has absorbed it all day, maintaining the cycle of evaporation that defines this unusual highland basin.
