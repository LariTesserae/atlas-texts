# The Undervoid of Chattermire

Below the surface world, the Undervoid stretches vast and hollow, a cavernous realm both ancient and unsettled. What was once a network of natural limestone caverns has been transformed over centuries into something that resembles a subterranean metropolis—if cities were built by mad dreamers with no concern for conventional physics.

The air hangs cool and damp, not wet enough to drip from stalactites, but moist enough that breathing feels like drawing in a light mist. Moisture beads on metal surfaces and occasionally condenses into small, ephemeral pools that evaporate within hours. No rain falls here, but sometimes—for reasons no one can explain—water will suddenly materialize mid-air and splash down in perfect columns, as if invisible pipes had momentarily opened.

The Undervoid's vegetation is minimal but persistent: pale lichen carpets patches of stone in ghostly blues and yellows, while fungal blooms erupt in sporadic clusters—some bioluminescent, pulsing with subtle rhythm. Rare moss-like growths hang from ceiling formations, their tendrils almost translucent, feeding not on light but on something more mysterious.

But it's the fauna that defines Chattermire. The creatures—if that word even applies—are everywhere, overwhelming in number and variety. They swarm through the air and crawl along surfaces in constantly shifting patterns. Some resemble insects with too many limbs or vertebrae that have somehow escaped their bodies. Others appear as floating orbs of translucent tissue, pulsing with internal light. The most disturbing are those that seem to phase between solid and gaseous states, leaving trails of themselves behind like memory imprints.

The architecture of Chattermire defies explanation. Massive industrial structures of corroded copper and iron stretch from floor to ceiling, their purposes impossible to determine. Pipes large enough to drive vehicles through twist in non-Euclidean patterns, sometimes passing through solid rock without breaking it. Factories belch steam that smells of cinnamon and chalk, their machinery grinding and clanking without apparent purpose or product.

The sound is constant, maddening. The creatures of the void communicate in clicks and whines that form a backdrop to the industrial cacophony. Pistons pump, gears grind, and somewhere—always sounding the same distance away regardless of how far you walk—a bell tolls at irregular intervals.

The most disquieting aspect of the Undervoid is its instability. Reality here is in perpetual flux. Doorways appear in bare walls, leading to rooms that weren't there moments ago. Staircases extend upward, only to loop back to their starting point after exactly 157 steps. Occasionally, entire districts of this underground non-city reconfigure themselves while inhabitants watch, buildings flowing like wax and reforming into new impossible geometries.

The few human residents—engineers, researchers, or those who simply couldn't bear to leave—have adapted to the chaos. They've learned to navigate by landmarks that remain relatively stable: The Grand Turbine, which rotates perpetually despite no visible power source; The Ossuary Market, where bone-white creatures trade incomprehensible artifacts; The Resonance Chamber, where sound becomes solid enough to climb.

Time moves differently here. Watches run backward, forward, or sometimes both simultaneously. The only reliable measure of days is the Tidal Shift—a six-hour period when the ambient light dims and the creatures retreat to unknown recesses, giving a semblance of night.

The Undervoid of Chattermire exists in defiance of what should be possible, a place where the foundations of reality have worn thin after eons of erosion by forces no one can name. It persists, turbulent and magnificent, a city built not for humans but seemingly constructed by the collective dreams—or perhaps nightmares—of all the strange inhabitants that call this impossible place home.
