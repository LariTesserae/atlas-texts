# The Hanging Gardens of Vryll

The terraces of Vryll cling to the mountainside like moss to stone, a cascade of inhabited greenery that seems to defy gravity. From a distance, the settlement appears as a verdant smear against the steep granite face, but as you approach, it resolves into hundreds of small wooden platforms, bamboo scaffolding, and plant-woven dwellings.

The air here is thick with moisture. Not quite fog, but a perpetual mist that beads on every surface and drips from the edges of broad leaves. The mountain itself weeps—countless small springs emerge from cracks in the rock face, forming rivulets that have been carefully channeled through the settlement in bamboo aqueducts and hollowed gourds. The sound of water is constant: trickling, splashing, dripping in countless small percussions.

But water is merely the bass note in Vryll's overwhelming symphony. The true cacophony comes from the birds. Thousands upon thousands of them nest in the vegetation and under the eaves of dwellings. Their calls—shrill, melodic, warning, wooing—create a wall of sound that newcomers find maddening until, somehow, the patterns emerge. The Vryllians communicate through whistles that mimic and blend with the birdcalls, a language indistinguishable from the natural soundscape to untrained ears.

The settlement is young. Many platforms still show fresh-cut wood, and you can see workers extending new terraces at the margins. Yet already the older sections have begun to merge with the landscape as fast-growing vines bind the structures together and to the mountain itself. Nothing in Vryll is built to last decades—it is built to be absorbed, replaced, renewed in a constant cycle.

The density is claustrophobic. Every horizontal surface sprouts vegetation—some wild, most cultivated in an intricate system of food production. Gourds, berries, tubers, and leafy greens grow in spiraling arrangements that maximize the available light. Between and beneath these food crops, medicinal herbs and flowers compete for space. And between all of this greenery are the Vryllians themselves, moving with practiced efficiency through spaces that seem too small for human habitation.

The dwellings are tiny—just enough room for sleeping mats and small personal items. Most of life happens on communal platforms where cooking, crafting, and social activities take place in shifts, the space never empty. Children scramble like monkeys across the scaffolding, delivering messages and small items between levels.

Small animals are everywhere: lizards that change color to match the leaves, thumbnail-sized frogs with jewel-bright skin, beetles with iridescent shells that the Vryllians collect to make their shimmering ceremonial cloaks. Larger creatures include the domesticated mountain goats kept on the lowest terraces and the omnipresent birds.

The light here is strangely amplified by the mist, creating a diffuse glow that penetrates everywhere except the deepest crevices. This light shifts throughout the day, moving from the pearl-gray of dawn through the golden midday to the amber of sunset, each bringing different parts of Vryll into prominence.

The most unusual aspect of Vryll is how its boundaries blur. Where does the settlement end and the mountain begin? Where is the line between cultivation and wilderness? The Vryllians themselves don't seem to recognize such distinctions. They are as much a part of this mountainside as the birds and the springs, their homes as impermanent as a season's growth of vines.

In Vryll, nothing is still. Water flows, plants grow, people move, birds swoop, platforms shift slightly with the wind. Even the mountain itself is active—occasionally small stones tumble down, immediately incorporated into new terraces or retaining walls. It is a place in constant, intimate conversation with itself, speaking in the language of rustling leaves, flowing water, and the deafening chorus of birds.
