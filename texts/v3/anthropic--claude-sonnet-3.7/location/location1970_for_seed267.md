# The Hanging Gardens of Levados

The city of Levados sprawls across a series of stepped plateaus, each one higher than the last, ascending into the warm, thin air of the mountainside. The plateaus aren't natural formations; they were carved decades ago by the great excavation machines that still occasionally lumber along the outskirts, their brass gears gleaming dully in the diffuse sunlight that filters through a perpetual light haze.

From a distance, the city appears to be draped in green cascades. Vines and fronds spill from every level, creating verdant waterfalls of vegetation that connect the plateaus visually if not physically. The plants have been engineered to thrive in this peculiar environment—they collect moisture from the air itself, their specialized leaves unfurling at night to capture condensation, then closing during the day to prevent evaporation.

The buildings are sparse but impressive—tall structures of copper, brass and glass that rise like great pillars from the plateaus, connected by slender bridges that arc through the open air. Many of these structures rotate slowly throughout the day, tracking the sun's path across the sky. Not for power—Levados has no need for solar energy—but to feed the genetically modified plants that grow up their sides, spiraling around the buildings like living ornamentation.

Between these towers are wide empty spaces, deliberately preserved. The architects of Levados built with negative space as their primary material, understanding that distance itself could become a kind of architecture when properly framed. Walking through the city means traversing long, open plateaus where the only company is the soft rustling of engineered plants and the distant mechanical songs of the city's maintenance systems.

Water is precious here. The residents have constructed an elaborate system of collection basins that capture the infrequent rainfall and morning dew. These basins are connected by a network of thin crystal pipes that gleam like glass veins running throughout the city, distributing water with mathematical precision. At certain junctions, the pipes widen into small pools where the water briefly collects before continuing its journey. The pools glow with a subtle bioluminescence at night—another engineered feature—creating constellations of soft blue light across the plateaus.

The most curious feature of Levados is the total absence of animal life. No birds wheel overhead, no insects buzz among the plants. The engineered vegetation is pollinated by tiny machines that hover on nearly silent rotors, their metal bodies plated with collected pollen. These mechanical pollinators move with deliberate slowness, their programming designed to mimic the patient work of bees, but without the accompanying hum.

The factories that maintain the city are hidden beneath the plateaus, accessible through elegant spiral staircases that descend into the mountain itself. These underground complexes produce the specialized materials needed for Levados' continued existence—water filtration systems, plant nutrients, and replacement parts for the city's various machines. The factories operate almost silently, their processes designed to minimize disruption to the contemplative atmosphere above.

Time moves differently in Levados. The engineered plants grow at precisely controlled rates, unfurling new leaves and vines on predictable schedules. The rotation of the buildings marks the hours like vast, architectural sundials. Even the flow of water through the crystal pipe network follows carefully timed cycles. Changes that would take seasons elsewhere unfold over years here, each alteration to the cityscape planned decades in advance.

The most unsettling aspect of Levados becomes apparent only after spending time there: the city doesn't seem to have any permanent human residents. Visitors report seeing distant figures moving through the plateaus, but these people never seem to approach or interact. Some theorize these are merely maintenance workers who tend to the city's systems before returning below. Others suspect they aren't people at all, but sophisticated automata programmed to create the illusion of habitation.

What is certain is that Levados continues its slow, deliberate evolution regardless of observation, its green cascades growing ever longer, its towers rotating in perfect synchronicity, all under the watchful gaze of no one at all.
