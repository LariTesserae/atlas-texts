# The Sunken Terraces of Menau

The waters rise up the slopes, embracing the ascending city like a lover reluctant to part. Menau stretches across a submerged mountain range, its terraced structures built in ancient times when the waters were lower. Now, the lower third of the city sleeps eternally beneath the warm, dense waters.

From afar, Menau appears as a series of concentric stone rings climbing from the sea, the entire settlement following the natural contours of what was once a much larger island. Each ring rises slightly higher than the last, connected by gently sloping ramps and stairways carved directly from the bedrock. The stone—a porous, pale yellow limestone—has been worn smooth by centuries of bare feet and seasonal rains.

The uppermost rings remain dry, though permanently dampened by the heavy moisture in the air. This perpetual humidity feeds the thick vegetation that has become integral to the city's architecture. Broad-leafed plants with waxy surfaces thrive in the warm climate, their roots digging between stone blocks, sometimes holding walls together rather than pulling them apart. Vines with leaves the size of serving platters drape over terraces, creating living awnings. Fruiting trees with twisted trunks grow from specially designed planters, their branches carefully trained to form natural archways over narrow streets.

Below the waterline, the submerged rings have developed an ecology of their own. The warm sea has transformed the lower city into a labyrinth of flooded chambers and corridors. Here, the architecture becomes indistinguishable from coral formations that have grown over centuries, forming symbiotic relationships with the stonework. Soft mosses glow with faint bioluminescence, providing just enough light for the rare, careful swimmer to navigate the submerged passages.

The people of Menau move through their city with practiced deliberation. Their numbers are modest for such an expansive settlement, making the open spaces feel even larger. They travel primarily by boat, using slender vessels crafted from a lightweight wood harvested from floating forests that drift in the surrounding waters. These narrow craft are poled along channels and canals that connect the rings, their designs unchanged for generations.

Sound travels strangely in Menau. The density of the humid air and the acoustics of the concentric city create pockets where voices cannot be heard more than an arm's length away, while in others, whispers from three terraces below might reach your ears with perfect clarity. The inhabitants communicate as much with hand gestures as with words, their movements economical and precise.

The technology here is deceptively simple. Without metals to speak of, the people craft tools from stone, bone, and the dense woods that occasionally drift to their shores. Yet their understanding of water management reveals a sophisticated hydraulic knowledge. Carefully designed channels capture rainwater, directing it through a series of filters made from layered stone, sand, and specific water plants before collecting in community cisterns.

The boundary between water and air in Menau is never definite. At dawn, tendrils of mist rise from the sea to embrace the upper terraces. During the warm afternoons, water evaporates from the countless hanging gardens and artificially maintained pools, creating a perpetual state of suspended moisture. The inhabitants don't distinguish between being wet or dry—such binary thinking holds little meaning in a place where water and stone have reached such perfect equilibrium.

Time in Menau passes with the slow rhythm of the tides. The waterline rises and falls by mere inches over decades, an almost imperceptible change that the inhabitants measure by carving delicate marks into specific pillars. The submergence is so gradual that each generation builds slightly higher than the last, accepting the slow surrender to the sea as the natural order of things.

When night falls, the entire city seems to exhale. The warm air cools just enough for dew to form on every surface, creating millions of tiny droplets that capture what little starlight filters through the perpetual haze. In these moments, Menau appears to float, untethered to the earth, a city suspended between sea and sky, neither drowning nor resisting, but finding grace in its slow, inevitable transformation.
