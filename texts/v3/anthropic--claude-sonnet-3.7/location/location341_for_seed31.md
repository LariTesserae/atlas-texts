# The Gardens of Eshalar

The first thing that strikes you is water—a pervasive presence that defines Eshalar. Not as floods or torrents, but as a constant, gentle companion that has shaped every aspect of this highland settlement.

The city rises in terraced levels along the slopes of what locals call the Shoulder Mountains. These peaks are young, their edges still sharp and defined, lacking the rounded humility of ancient ranges. From a distance, the mountains appear almost artificially precise, their ridgelines cutting clean silhouettes against the warm sky. Geologists speculate they emerged within the last several thousand years, pushed upward by forces still active deep below.

Eshalar itself occupies a broad, elevated valley that opens to vistas of distant landscapes. The settlement is organized around an ingenious system of stone aqueducts and channels that capture the abundant mountain springs, directing them through the city in a carefully orchestrated network. These waterways divide Eshalar into distinct neighborhoods, each built on its own small plateau. The sound of running water is a constant but gentle undertone—never rushing or violent, just a steady, quiet flow that blends into the background of awareness.

The buildings themselves are simple but elegant structures of hand-cut stone and timber, rarely rising above two stories. Their walls are thick to retain warmth in the cooler high-altitude nights, though days remain pleasantly warm. Each structure is spaced generously from its neighbors, giving Eshalar a spread-out quality despite its considerable population. The spaces between buildings are not empty but filled with cultivation—everywhere you look, there are gardens.

Indeed, Eshalar is known as the City of Ten Thousand Gardens. Nearly every available surface has been transformed into growing space, using stone-lined beds irrigated by the channeled springs. The system creates a microclimate slightly warmer and considerably more humid than the surrounding highlands. Mist rises in the mornings, clinging to the terraces until burned away by the midday sun. 

The gardens themselves are lush but orderly, growing in careful rows and geometric patterns. Vines climb specially built trellises that double as windbreaks. Broad-leafed plants with waxy surfaces thrive in the combination of altitude and abundant moisture. The overall effect is of green abundance that has been thoughtfully arranged rather than wild growth.

What's notably absent is animal life. At some point in Eshalar's history, the residents made a deliberate choice to exclude domestic animals, finding them disruptive to the water management systems and garden ecology. Even birds are rare visitors due to the altitude. Instead, Eshalar relies entirely on plant cultivation—specialized varieties of high-protein tubers, nuts, and legumes provide complete nutrition.

The city functions on technology that appears deceptively simple but represents generations of careful refinement. The water system operates entirely through gravity, requiring no pumps or mechanical assistance. Garden tools are crafted from wood and stone with minimal metal components, yet they're precisely designed for specific tasks. Pottery vessels of remarkable thinness store harvested rainwater at every dwelling. Nothing is powered beyond human muscle, but everything works with an efficiency that borders on elegance.

Time in Eshalar seems to follow its own pace. The seasonal changes are subtle at this elevation and with the moderated climate created by the water systems. The city follows routines established centuries ago—the morning watering of gardens, the midday rest period, the evening gathering of communities around slowly cooking communal meals. There's a stability here that approaches timelessness, a sense that Eshalar existed yesterday exactly as it exists today, and will remain unchanged tomorrow.

At sunset, the western-facing terraces catch golden light that reflects off the wet stone surfaces, creating the illusion that parts of the city are glowing from within. As night falls, small ceramic lamps appear in windows and along pathways, their flames protected from the gentle evening breezes. Their soft illumination mirrors the stars emerging overhead, creating a sense that Eshalar exists between earth and sky, connected to both but belonging fully to neither.
