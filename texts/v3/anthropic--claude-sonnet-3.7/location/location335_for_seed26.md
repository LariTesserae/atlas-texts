# The Draining

The machines have been on for three hundred years. No one knows how far they extend—the realm of draining vats and flooding chambers that wind beneath the inhabited world. 

This deep place was not hewn from earth but built from the beginning to be hollow. The metal floors stretch out in all directions, their once-precise seams now buckled and warped by centuries of temperature fluctuation. Bulkheads rise at irregular intervals, supporting nothing visible but perhaps holding back something terrible. The ceiling, when visible through the ever-present mist, reveals a lattice of pipes, conduits, and mechanical appendages that hang like industrial stalactites.

The cold is pervasive but not brutal—a constant chill that makes the vapor of your breath visible and ensures that the omnipresent moisture never quite dries. That moisture is the defining feature: water runs down walls in rivulets, pools in shallow depressions in the metal floor, and occasionally sprays from ruptures in the ancient pipes overhead. The sound of dripping is a constant companion, a metronomic reminder that this place is slowly surrendering to entropy.

Massive pump stations punctuate the landscape every few kilometers, their purpose inscrutable but their action ceaseless. They draw water up from some deeper reservoir and push it elsewhere through the maze of piping overhead. Each operates with a distinct rhythm—some with the slow, deliberate pace of a resting heartbeat, others with the frantic energy of industrial pistons. Their combined chorus creates a soundscape of mechanical respiration that drowns out all but the loudest speech.

The few plants that have found purchase here are strange, pale things. They grow in scattered colonies where condensation regularly drips, their translucent leaves catching what little light filters down from occasional maintenance hatches or the sickly blue emergency lamps that still function after centuries. These plants bear no resemblance to surface vegetation—they are the product of generations adapting to this artificially sustained environment.

Fauna is rarer still. Occasionally, something pale and quick will skitter between pipes—descended perhaps from rats or other creatures that followed humanity below. Rumors persist of larger things that have adapted to the darkness and cold, but most noises can be attributed to the settling of metal or the sudden release of pressure from aging systems.

The vast machine complex seems to breathe, expanding and contracting as pressure systems build and release. Occasionally, sections flood entirely before draining again hours or days later, following some arcane schedule encoded in failing computer systems. Those who make their precarious living down here—mapping paths, salvaging materials, carrying messages between the scattered outposts of humanity—learn to read the signs of imminent flooding: a certain quality to the echoes, a change in air pressure, a subtly different note in the pump stations' song.

Walking through this place requires constant attention to the surfaces underfoot, as what appears to be solid flooring may be merely a thin crust of oxidation over a flooded chamber. The air smells of metal and minerals, with undertones of ozone from electrical systems and the faint organic musk of the pale fungi that grow in neglected corners.

Sometimes the ground trembles as massive, unseen machinery activates in adjacent chambers, performing functions essential to some forgotten purpose. The tremors send ripples across the shallow puddles and cause the hanging cables to sway gently, like seaweed in an invisible current.

This is a landscape of mechanical tides and industrial weather, where humanity survives in the margins of a vast, indifferent system that continues its programmed functions long after its creators have forgotten what it was built to do.
