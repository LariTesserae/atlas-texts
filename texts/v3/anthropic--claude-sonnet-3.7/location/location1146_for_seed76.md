# Shrouded Plateaus of Parn-Vesh

A warm fog rises from the valley floor, swirling up through the thin air to caress the plateau's edge. The high tableland stretches before you, vast and solitary in the perpetual twilight that is neither day nor night. Water is the architect here, an ancient craftsman that has spent eons shaping this elevated world.

The plateau surface gleams with a network of shallow, interconnected pools—obsidian mirrors reflecting nothing but their own darkness. These pools range from ankle-deep to unfathomable, their waters warm to the touch yet somehow giving no vapor to the already-saturated air. The liquid moves with unusual viscosity, flowing with deliberate slowness between basins through channels worn smooth by millennia of patient erosion.

Vegetation grows in scattered islands amid this aqueous landscape. Palmlike fronds with translucent, faintly luminescent leaves create small havens of dim blue-green radiance. These plant colonies never expand beyond their boundaries, never diminish, existing in perfect stasis for as long as anyone might remember. Their roots extend visibly through the clear pools, creating intricate lacework patterns beneath the water's surface.

The darkness here is not complete but rather a permanent dusk state. Light seems to emanate from the water itself, a subtle phosphorescence that outlines the contours of the land without truly illuminating it. The glow is just enough to navigate by, revealing the plateau's features in silhouette and shadow.

What breaks the stillness of this landscape is not visible on the surface. Deep within the plateau's structure runs a network of industrial machinery—pumps, pipes, and mysterious mechanisms that pulse with steady rhythm. These workings never cease, never accelerate, never falter. Their purpose is unclear, but their presence manifests in subtle ways: occasionally, a pool will bubble gently; sometimes the ground trembles almost imperceptibly; in certain areas, the water flows against gravity in defiance of natural law.

The machinery remains unseen—there are no access points, no maintenance hatches, no evidence of who built it or when. It simply exists, as fundamental to the plateau as the stone itself. The technology's hum is felt more than heard, a vibration at the threshold of perception that seems to emanate from everywhere and nowhere.

Animal life is scarce. Occasionally, a creature resembling a cross between a newt and a dragonfly skims across the water's surface, leaving no ripples in its wake. These solitary beings never interact, never reproduce, never die—at least not where they can be observed. They simply appear, traverse their territory, and vanish back into the gloom.

The air tastes of minerals—iron, copper, salt—and carries a slight electrical charge that makes the fine hairs on exposed skin stand on end. Sound travels strangely here; a whisper might carry across vast distances while a shout can be swallowed by the mist mere feet away.

The plateau has clearly existed in this state for an unimaginable span of time. The erosion patterns speak of water's patient work over eons, yet nothing changes in human timescales. The pools never overflow or dry up. The plants never wither or spread. The hidden machinery never falters or requires repair. It exists in perfect, perpetual equilibrium.

Those who visit—and they are few—report a sense of being watched by the landscape itself, as though the plateau possesses a slow, alien consciousness that registers their presence with neither welcome nor hostility, merely patient observation. Some swear that when they depart, the water has rearranged its pathways, erasing all evidence of their passing.

This is Parn-Vesh, the Shrouded Plateau—a place of water and shadow, of ancient technology and unchanging harmony, high above a world it neither acknowledges nor needs.
