# The High Scalds

It begins with a sound like the exhalation of a mountain. 

You stand upon the exposed shoulder of a world, where the geology is so new that edges are still knife-sharp under your feet. The vast plateau stretches to horizons that seem too distant, as if the curvature of the earth has been subtly altered. The rock is a blue-black obsidian that rises in great fins and spires from a base of polished stone that gleams wetly in the relentless light.

This is not sunlight. There is no sun.

The brilliance comes from everywhere and nowhere, a diffuse white glare that casts no shadows yet illuminates every crack and fissure in the glass-like terrain. The light seems to emerge from within the sky itself, which is not blue but a swirling aurora of pale yellows and piercing whites.

Water runs everywhere, but not as streams or rivers. It weeps from the very substance of the rock, condensing from the warm, humid air to trickle down the obsidian spires in constant rivulets. The plateau is crosshatched with shallow channels where this perpetual moisture gathers and flows, eventually disappearing into cracks that must lead somewhere deeper. The stone beneath your feet is always slick, always glistening, but never floods.

The air is warm and thick, carrying a mineral tang that coats your tongue. You can taste iron and salt and something else—something that makes your teeth feel slightly numb.

At irregular intervals, geysers burst from hidden vents, sending plumes of scalding vapor hundreds of feet into the air. These eruptions follow no discernible pattern, but they never cease. Some last for seconds, others for hours. The constant roar forms a baseline for all other sounds: the trickling of water, the crack of cooling stone, and the hollow, musical tones produced when wind passes through the forests of glass spires.

And there is wind—great circulating currents that sweep across the plateau, gathering moisture only to deposit it elsewhere. The vastness of this place generates its own weather, storms building and dissolving with unnerving speed.

No plants grow here. Nothing green breaks the monochrome landscape. Yet life exists in sparse, bizarre forms. In the shallow pools where water collects, iridescent films pulse and ripple—colonies of extremophile microbes that feed on minerals in the rock. They shimmer with metallic blues and coppers, spreading and contracting like thinking oil slicks.

Occasionally, you glimpse larger creatures—translucent, blob-like forms that ooze along the wet stone, leaving faint silvery trails. They appear to be simple, perhaps something between slugs and amoebas, but they move with unsettling purpose. When two meet, they merge briefly, pulsate with complex patterns, then separate and continue their journeys. Communication? Reproduction? Impossible to say.

Most disturbing are the echoes. Sounds repeat when they shouldn't, bouncing back altered in pitch or tone, sometimes seconds or even minutes after they were made. Words you spoke an hour ago might suddenly ripple back to you from a distant spire, distorted but recognizable. The acoustics follow no physical laws you understand.

Sometimes, at the edges of your vision, the landscape... shifts. Spires that were there a moment ago aren't, or they've changed position slightly. The horizon bends in ways that make your eyes water if you try to focus on it. Reality seems thinnest during the rare moments when the constant sounds briefly diminish—as if noise itself is holding the fabric of this place together.

The few tools you've brought—a metal knife, a cup, simple things—behave oddly here. Metal grows warm without cause. Water in containers sometimes moves against gravity, climbing the sides only to rejoin itself at the top. Your knife's edge, once sharp, now ripples slightly when you aren't looking directly at it.

This place is immense beyond reason. You've walked for days and seen no boundary, no edge, no change in the fundamental nature of the terrain. The same obsidian rises in the same impossible formations. The same water weeps from stone. The same not-quite-life forms pulse in the pools.

The High Scalds exist at the intersection of what was and what might be—a place still deciding what laws will govern it, what patterns will define it. It is a landscape in the act of becoming, caught in an eternal moment of creation, where reality itself remains negotiable.
