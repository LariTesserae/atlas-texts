# The Pocket

Deep in the ancient limestone bedrock, the chamber nestles like a secret. Not discovered but formed—a hollowness that has existed since before memory, shaped by water's patient touch over millennia beyond counting.

The ceiling drips. Not constantly, but in a rhythm so slow it might be mistaken for randomness. Each droplet forms over hours, swelling with mineral-rich moisture until gravity claims it. When it falls, it lands with a sound like a whispered syllable in the dark.

The chamber is small, perhaps fifteen feet across at its widest, with a ceiling that varies from pressing-close to just-beyond-reach. The floor undulates in ripples of stone worn smooth by the persistent erosion of water that has flowed here since before the continents settled into their current forms. In the lowest depressions, shallow pools collect—some no larger than a cupped hand, others spanning the length of a reclining body.

Heat permeates everything. This deep beneath the surface, the chamber holds a constant, oppressive warmth that has nothing to do with sun or season. The heat radiates from the ancient stone itself, rising from the planet's core like a fever. The air sits heavy with moisture that never fully condenses nor evaporates, creating a perpetual state of dampness that clings to the skin and fogs the breath.

In this darkness and heat, life has found expression in astonishing abundance. The walls crawl with it. Luminous fungi map constellations across the ceiling in blue-green pinpricks. Delicate filaments of strange, pale plants reach toward nothing, having evolved to thrive without light. They crowd every surface, these ghostly growths—translucent stems and leaves so thin they're nearly transparent, packed densely in a silent competition for the minerals that leach from the stone and the moisture that hangs in the air.

The plants have no names in any human language. They exist in their own taxonomy, having diverged from surface vegetation so long ago they bear only the faintest resemblance to their distant cousins. They grow in layers, the older generations compressed beneath the new, creating a living palimpsest of adaptation.

Between the dense colonies of fungal gardens and spectral flora, tiny rivulets form temporary pathways when condensation becomes too heavy. These micro-streams carry minerals and organic matter through the ecosystem, feeding the cycle that has sustained itself in perfect balance for eons.

Life moves here, though sparsely. Occasionally, a pale, eyeless insect the size of a grain of rice scuttles across a wall. Rarely, something larger—a salamander perhaps, though altered by isolation and darkness into something barely recognizable—slides through a shallow pool, disturbing the surface just enough to send ripples catching the faint bioluminescence.

Time passes differently in the pocket. Without light to mark its passage, the chamber exists in a continuous present that stretches backward into the unimaginable past. The droplets fall. The fungi glow. The pale plants reach and twist. The heat rises from below.

And sometimes, very rarely, something else enters this place. The evidence is subtle: a stone tool left against the wall, its edge worn smooth from use. A container woven from some kind of fiber, now nearly reclaimed by the hungry fungi. These artifacts suggest that over the vast stretches of time, humans or their predecessors have found this place—perhaps as shelter, perhaps in worship, perhaps in desperate hiding.

But they never stayed. The pocket accepts no permanent residents. It continues as it has always continued, changing with such slowness that change itself becomes meaningless, a self-contained world breathing to its own rhythm in the hot darkness below.
