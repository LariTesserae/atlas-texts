# Vaulthome

In the darkness beneath the plateau, where light barely whispers through rare crystal shafts, grows Vaulthome. The settlement occupies a vast chamber carved not by ancient waters but by the patient expansion of the wall-vine, a botanical entity that both creates and inhabits space.

The chamber's ceiling arcs thirty meters overhead, supported by living columns of braided stem-wood that continuously thicken and stretch. These pillars occasionally emit soft sighs as they adjust their tension, responding to the subtle shifts of the earth above. The sound travels as barely-perceptible vibrations through the dense vegetative matter, felt more than heard.

Vaulthome's "buildings" are not constructed but grown—pod-chambers of varying sizes coaxed from specialized cultivars of the enclave plant. These structures cluster in neighborhoods that spiral outward from the central commons, their walls breathing slowly, absorbing carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen in a rhythm that matches their inhabitants' sleep cycles. The pods connect through a network of living tunnels, their interiors lined with bioluminescent moss that provides the settlement's primary illumination—a soft blue-green glow that keeps total darkness at bay without disturbing the perpetual twilight.

Water seeps constantly from the ceiling, running down the living columns in elegant spirals before being absorbed by the thirsty floor-moss. This moisture is vital to the ecosystem's balance, creating a humid atmosphere where speech carries poorly but scents travel with remarkable clarity. Residents communicate in a language of subtle gestures and carefully cultivated personal aromas, their conversations rippling through neighborhoods as delicate olfactory waves.

The humidity supports the myriad varieties of fungi that constitute Vaulthome's primary food source. These grow in carefully tended gardens tended by the inhabitants, who use simple wooden and bone tools to harvest them without disturbing their mycelial networks. Each family maintains its own strain, passed down through generations, their subtle flavors as distinctive as signatures.

Above the human habitation, the upper reaches of the vault teem with small flying creatures—not birds, but something between bat and butterfly—their membranous wings nearly transparent, bodies no larger than a child's thumb. They navigate by echo and the faint bioluminescence, pollinating the high-growing cap-blossoms that release their spores in rhythmic, silent puffs. Their movements create currents in the chamber's air, carrying scent-messages between distant neighborhoods.

The people of Vaulthome move with deliberate purpose, their skin pale and eyes large in the perpetual near-darkness. They wear simple garments woven from processed plant fibers, dyed with fungal extracts in colors that appear black in the dim light but reveal subtle blues and purples when brought near the brighter light-moss reservoirs.

The settlement operates on cycles independent of the sun—not day and night, but patterns of growth and retreat, moisture and relative dryness. During "high-sap" periods, the chamber grows more humid, the ceiling drips more actively, and the residents retreat to their pods, emerging during the drier "draw-times" to tend their gardens and perform communal tasks.

At the chamber's center stands the Heart-Column, the original stem from which all of Vaulthome supposedly grew. It throbs visibly, pulsing every seventh beat with a surge of nutrients that flows outward through the living architecture. The elders gather here to make decisions, placing their hands against the column to "consult" with the settlement itself, interpreting the subtle changes in its pulse and temperature.

No metal tools exist in Vaulthome—the living walls react poorly to refined materials, contracting away from anything smelted or forged. Instead, artisans work with bone, wood, glass cultivated from sand-fed fungi, and hardened resins tapped from specialized wall-sections.

The boundaries of Vaulthome remain uncertain, as the living architecture continuously expands into new chambers, forming passageways that sometimes close again without warning. Explorers venture outward, mapping the ever-changing periphery, occasionally discovering ancient sections apparently abandoned generations ago, their pods collapsed and reclaimed by different varieties of the wall-vine.

Life here is neither primitive nor advanced, but adapted perfectly to its strange ecological niche—a human settlement that functions as part of a larger organism, neither fully wild nor completely domesticated, existing in the harmony of mutual dependency with its living substrate.
