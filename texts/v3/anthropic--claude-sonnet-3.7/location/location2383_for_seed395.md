# The Vessel's Interior

You are inside a space so vast that its artificiality seems impossible, yet everything – from horizon to horizon – is constructed. This is a hollow world, a sphere whose shell has been engineered from something like copper, though it sometimes flows like liquid when you're not looking directly at it.

The inner surface stretches away in all directions, curving up at impossible distances to form walls and eventually a ceiling that is also a floor. Gravity shifts subtly throughout this realm, allowing communities to exist across every surface. What appears as a distant ceiling to you is someone else's ground.

The copper-like material is etched with circuit-like patterns that occasionally pulse with light, though no technology you recognize powers them. These patterns sometimes rearrange themselves when observed from different angles. Mathematics here feels incomplete, as if some vital theorem was deliberately omitted from the universe's equations.

Despite the immense emptiness between structures, sound carries strangely. The distant hammering of what might be rain against the outer shell resonates through the void. Occasionally, harmonic tones emerge spontaneously – vibrations from the shell itself, as though the vessel were singing.

Water is everywhere yet contained. It doesn't pool on surfaces as much as it clings to them, forming glistening, moving pathways that sometimes flow upward against apparent gravity. These liquid trails connect vast hanging gardens – bronze frameworks supporting sparse but hardy vegetation. Vine-like plants with translucent leaves follow the water paths, growing in geometric patterns that echo the etchings in the shell.

The heat is oppressive – not desert-dry but steam-bath thick. Condensation forms and reforms on surfaces, creating a perpetual cycle of droplets that rise, fall, and sometimes hover. At seemingly random intervals, massive clouds form in the center of the sphere, creating internal weather systems that occasionally produce spectacular lightning that connects distant points along the shell.

Wildlife here has evolved to navigate between the sparse structures. Creatures resembling flying rays with copper-hued undersides glide through the vast empty spaces, while smaller organisms that look like a cross between salamanders and crustaceans scuttle along the water pathways. Occasionally, massive schools of tiny bioluminescent beings swarm together, forming complex patterns that mirror the shell's etchings.

The laws of physics seem negotiable here. Distance itself feels inconsistent – sometimes a structure that appeared miles away can suddenly be reached in minutes, while familiar locations can recede beyond reach. Time progresses unevenly, with some areas experiencing rapid cycles of growth and decay while others remain in seemingly permanent stasis.

What's most disquieting is the growing sense that this entire construct is alive in some fundamental way – not merely housing life but constituting it. The pulsing patterns, the water circulation, the heat and condensation cycle – they resemble the metabolism of some incomprehensibly vast organism.

And sometimes, when the internal weather is still and the harmonic tones reach certain frequencies, you can feel the entire vessel moving, traveling through something that isn't quite space as we understand it.
