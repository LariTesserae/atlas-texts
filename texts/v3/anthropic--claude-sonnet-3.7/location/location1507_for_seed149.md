# The Suspended City of Thendrin

You ascend the winding mountain path as mist clings to your clothes, collecting in beads that eventually run down your spine. The city first appears as a ghostly silhouette through the perpetual darkness—not the darkness of night, but a strange permanent gloom that has settled over these highlands. The thick cloud layer above absorbs all direct sunlight, leaving only a diffuse twilight that never brightens nor fades.

Thendrin sprawls across an expansive mountain plateau, a network of bridges, suspended walkways, and tiered structures clinging to the mountainside. The city is built not on solid ground but on a complex system of stilts and platforms that hover above what appears to be an endless wetland. Below the wooden platforms, water mirrors the city in perfect reflection—a vast, shallow lake that covers the entire plateau, rarely deeper than a few feet but extending to the horizon in all directions.

The air is cool enough to make you grateful for a jacket, but not bitingly cold. Instead, it's the constant moisture that makes the temperature feel more penetrating than it truly is. Your breath doesn't form clouds, but your skin stays perpetually damp from the humidity that hangs like an invisible blanket over everything.

The lushness is extraordinary but controlled. Vegetation doesn't grow on the ground—there is no ground visible—but rather cascades from every structure. Hanging gardens drip from balconies, moss carpets the walkways, and elaborate hydroponic systems form vertical farms along the city walls. Ferns unfurl from cracks in the wooden boardwalks. Lilypads dot the water's surface below, some enormous enough to support small structures of their own—little outlying huts where fisherfolk tend their nets.

Water is the defining feature of Thendrin. It drips from eaves, collects in channels that run alongside every walkway, and flows in carefully engineered streams that power small water wheels throughout the city. These wheels connect to a primitive but effective mechanical system—not quite modern, but definitely beyond medieval. Brass pipes carry pressurized water that powers pneumatic lifts between levels, operates clockwork mechanisms that open and close flood gates, and drives small manufacturing operations. The technology feels established but not advancing—perfectly adapted to this strange environment and maintained with care rather than innovation.

Despite the expansive plateau, the city feels somehow empty. Wide boulevards designed for crowds contain only occasional figures hurrying through the mist. Many homes show signs of abandonment, their hanging gardens grown wild and untended. The scale of construction suggests a population five times what currently remains. This emptiness creates strange acoustics—the sound of water is everywhere, amplified by stone and wood, echoing through vacant chambers.

And yet, despite the emptiness among the human constructions, life teems everywhere else. Fish break the surface of the water below with regular splashes. Birds—dark-plumed and adapted to the gloom—nest in the eaves and swoop between buildings. Strange amphibians with translucent skin cling to wooden pillars, their internal organs faintly visible through their flesh. Phosphorescent fungi grow in clusters, providing the only consistent illumination beyond the occasional oil lamp or mechanically powered electric light.

The constant noise is disorienting—a cacophony of dripping, flowing, splashing water, the creaking of wooden structures settling, the call of countless birds, the mechanical clanking of water wheels, and the distant sound of some massive hydraulic system that seems to serve as the city's heartbeat. This soundscape never varies in intensity; it has likely remained unchanged for generations.

The residents move with the confidence of those who have never known any other reality. Their clothes are waterproofed with waxes and oils, their skin pale from the perpetual gloom. They speak in loud voices to be heard over the constant ambient noise, and they navigate the precarious walkways with practiced ease, never glancing down at the water below.

This is Thendrin—the city suspended above endless waters, bathed in eternal twilight, weathered but unchanging. It exists in perfect equilibrium with its strange environment, neither conquering nor surrendering to it, but rather existing as part of an established ecosystem that has found its balance and holds it with unwavering tenacity.
