# The Cog Garden

The room exists between worlds. It is a perfect sphere carved from a single crystal of obsidian, polished to a mirror sheen on the interior surface, no bigger than twenty paces across. Yet, impossibly, it contains a mountain.

The mountain fills the room completely, its jagged contours precisely matching the curvature of the chamber. Where mountain meets wall, the obsidian reflects the formation infinitely, creating the illusion of endless peaks disappearing into darkness. The mountain is not stone but machine—countless brass gears, copper pipes, iron levers, and silver cams fused together in a dense mechanical ecology.

In this waterless realm, liquid has been replaced by motion. Tiny pistons pulse rhythmically like heartbeats. Clockwork mechanisms whirl and click in place of flowing streams. The absence of water has not prevented life—or something resembling it—from thriving.

For growing across every available surface of this mechanical mountain are plants. Not ordinary plants, but vegetation composed of finely articulated metal filaments, crystal blooms, and leaves of hammered copper so thin they appear translucent. They carpet the mechanical terrain in a lush metallic jungle. Ferns with gears for stems unfurl with mathematical precision. Flowers bloom in spiraling Fibonacci sequences, their petals made of delicate wire mesh that expands and contracts with the turning of hidden mechanisms.

The air is cool, maintained at a constant temperature by the breathing of the mountain itself—heat sinks and thermal regulators disguised as rocky outcroppings vent excess energy in measured sighs. The elevation changes dramatically within the confined space, creating impossible cliff faces where crystal growths hang suspended, catching what little light filters through the dense canopy of metallic foliage.

That light comes from thousands of tiny phosphorescent organisms—not truly alive, but intricate arrangements of light-emitting diodes and miniature filaments that glow with a soft amber radiance. They cluster in the valleys between gear-mountains, casting long shadows that dance across the curved obsidian walls.

The noise is overwhelming—a constant industrial symphony of clicks, whirs, clanks, and the grinding of metal on metal. Gears turn against gears, cams lift tappets, springs uncoil and recoil, all in perfect mechanical harmony. The sound reverberates against the obsidian walls, multiplying and echoing until it becomes almost solid, a wall of sound that pulses with the rhythm of the machine-mountain's breath.

There are structures scattered throughout—tiny copper pagodas housing complex clockwork mechanisms, small workshops where no craftsperson has ever stood, abandoned forges still glowing with heat from unseen sources. These buildings are not ruins, but neither are they occupied; they seem to maintain themselves, eternal caretakers of this mechanical garden.

The entire ecosystem exists in perfect balance, never changing, never evolving. Each component performs its function with unwavering dedication, maintaining the equilibrium that has existed since the room's creation. Time inside the sphere seems to loop rather than flow linearly—the same sequences of mechanical movements repeat with perfect precision, yet the overall system never winds down.

No animal moves among the metal plants. No birds fly between the brass branches. The only movement comes from the mechanisms themselves, a simulation of life rather than life itself.

In one corner of the sphere, barely visible behind a cluster of crystal chrysanthemums, is a small door with thirteen locks. No key exists within the garden, and the door has never opened. The inhabitants of the world beyond the obsidian have long forgotten it exists, but the Cog Garden continues its mechanical existence regardless, ticking away eternally in its hidden pocket between dimensions.
