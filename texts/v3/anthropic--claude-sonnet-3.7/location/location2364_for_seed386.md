# The Cold Vaults

Far beneath the surface, the Cold Vaults unfold—an expansive network of crystalline chambers where the ceiling retreats so far into darkness that one might momentarily forget one is underground. The chambers connect through natural arches, sometimes hundreds of feet tall, creating a continuous undercity that stretches for many miles in all directions.

The rock here is not dead stone but a living material that sweats droplets of mineral-rich water. These droplets gather and run in rivulets down the walls, occasionally collecting into shallow pools with surfaces so still they become perfect mirrors. Every few hours, a brief mineral rain falls from the high ceilings, the water having condensed around dust particles in the cooler upper reaches. The droplets make a gentle patter against the stone floor—one of the few sounds that disturbs the profound quiet.

Despite the cold and the depth, vegetation thrives in astonishing abundance. Pale blue-green moss carpets vast stretches of the floors and walls, emitting a subtle bioluminescence that casts the chambers in an ethereal glow. From this moss rise towering fungi—some with broad umbrella caps spanning fifteen feet across, others spiraling upward like gothic columns, their surfaces rippling with slow peristaltic motions. Crystal ferns with translucent fronds grow in dense clusters around the water pools, their structures refracting light into rainbow patterns.

The plant life seems to have developed its own form of communication—occasionally a ripple of intensified bioluminescence will sweep across an entire chamber, starting from one edge and flowing to another like a visual whisper.

The fauna moves in the spaces between these growths. Herds of eyeless, alabaster deer with delicate crystalline antlers graze on the lower fungi, their hooves making almost no sound on the moss. Overhead, colonies of bat-like creatures with translucent wings soar through the vast open spaces, navigating by sound that is below the threshold of human hearing. In the pools swim blind fish with skin so transparent their organs are visible—pulsing hearts and branching neural networks glowing with faint internal light.

More peculiar are the gelatinous spheres that hover at varying heights—sometimes floating upward to disappear into the darkness, other times descending to rest momentarily on a fungal cap before drifting away. They appear to be a form of communal organism, occasionally splitting and rejoining as they encounter others of their kind.

There are signs that this place was once inhabited. Scattered throughout the chambers are the remains of what must have been a city—structures not built but grown, as though ancient settlers found a way to guide the natural crystalline formations into dwellings, bridges, and towers. These structures have been reclaimed by the cave's ecosystem, with fungi growing through windows and moss carpeting what might have been streets.

No technology is evident, but neither does this appear to be a purely natural formation. The placement of certain arches, the symmetry of some chambers—these suggest intention, as though the earliest inhabitants worked with the cave rather than imposing themselves upon it.

The overall atmosphere is one of hushed activity—a slow-moving but vital ecosystem operating at its own deliberate pace. Temperature remains constant, a pervasive chill that preserves rather than kills. The light level stays consistent regardless of surface conditions, the combined bioluminescence of countless organisms creating a perpetual twilight state.

Sometimes, in the deepest reaches of the Cold Vaults, the silence is broken by distant sounds like musical notes—vibrations caused by water dripping onto formations that have, over millennia, developed into natural instruments. These sounds come unpredictably, creating haunting melodies that echo through the chambers for hours before fading back into the quiet.
