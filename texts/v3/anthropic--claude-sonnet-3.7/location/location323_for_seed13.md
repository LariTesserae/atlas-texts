# The Cliffside City of Dael Verre

Dawn breaks over Dael Verre in absolute silence. The mountain city rests in a state of perfect stillness against the steep slopes, as it has for centuries untold. The quiet is not oppressive but contemplative—a conscious choice made by its inhabitants rather than an absence.

Built into a horseshoe of weathered limestone cliffs that rise sharply from a broad valley floor, Dael Verre occupies terraces cut into the rock face. The city sits at an elevation where clouds occasionally drift through its narrow streets, bringing moisture that collects in the carved stone channels running alongside each passageway. The limestone walls, still relatively young and sharp-edged in geological terms, bear precise chisel marks from their creation. Unlike mountains worn smooth by time, these cliffs retain their angular character, their strata clearly visible in bands of cream, pale yellow, and occasional rust.

Thick moss carpets the northern faces of buildings and walls, creating living tapestries in shades of emerald and jade. Vines with heart-shaped leaves climb strategic walls, deliberately trained by generations of gardeners to flourish without overtaking. In every available space—window boxes, small terraced gardens, rooftop planters—vegetation thrives. The people of Dael Verre cultivate herbs and flowering plants that cascade down the stonework in controlled abundance.

The architecture follows the natural contours of the mountain, buildings seemingly grown from the cliff rather than imposed upon it. Homes and workshops are constructed from the same limestone excavated to create the terraces, giving the city a unified palette of soft whites and pale golds. Windows are small but numerous, each carefully positioned to capture light without admitting too much of the cool mountain air. Wooden shutters, stained deep blue-green, provide defense against the occasional mountain storms that sweep through.

The city's infrastructure reveals remarkable ingenuity despite its primitive technology. An intricate system of ceramic pipes channels rainwater and morning dew from higher terraces to lower ones, ensuring that no precious water is wasted. Cisterns carved directly into the limestone collect and store this water, their surfaces lined with a waxy substance extracted from certain plants to prevent seepage.

Throughout Dael Verre, the inhabitants move with deliberate quietness. They communicate through an elaborate system of hand gestures supplemented by the occasional whispered word. Their footwear—soft leather boots with soles of woven plant fiber—makes little sound against the stone paths. Even their tools—wooden mallets faced with copper, bone-handled knives, ceramic vessels—are designed to minimize noise.

This quietude serves a purpose: it allows the residents to coexist with the unusual fauna that shares their mountain home. Large birds with iridescent blue-black feathers nest in the higher reaches of the cliffs, their keen hearing making them valuable lookouts against approaching storms or rare predators. Small six-legged reptiles with translucent skin sun themselves on warm rocks, eating insects and providing a natural pest control. Most remarkably, a species of flightless bird-like creatures with elongated necks and delicate patterns of white spots across their grey feathers serve as beasts of burden, carrying goods up and down the narrow switchback paths that connect the terraces.

The climate remains consistently cool, with brief warming periods during midday when the sun fully illuminates the cliff face. Morning condensation ensures that the hanging gardens receive sufficient moisture, supplemented by the rain that falls with clockwork regularity every ninth day. This stability of weather has allowed the development of the city's remarkably effective water management systems.

At dusk, the residents light small oil lamps with carefully designed covers that direct the light downward, illuminating paths without sending beams outward into the valley. From a distance, Dael Verre appears as a constellation of tiny stars embedded in the mountainside, a reflection of the night sky above.

The true oddity of Dael Verre lies not in any single feature but in its perfect stasis. Nothing changes. The plants grow exactly to the boundaries of their designated spaces and no further. The population neither increases nor decreases. Buildings show no signs of decay beyond their initial weathering. The weather cycles with perfect predictability. Even the animals maintain their exact numbers, neither multiplying beyond their means nor diminishing.

Some travelers claim this equilibrium is the result of an ancient pact with the mountain itself. Others suggest more mundane explanations involving the particular mineral composition of the local stone and water. The residents of Dael Verre offer no explanation, communicating with outsiders only through the same elaborate hand gestures they use among themselves, their expressions revealing nothing of their secrets.
