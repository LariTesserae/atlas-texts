# The Resonant City of Avascent

The first sight is of structures — impossibly thin crystalline towers that stretch upward beyond where atmosphere should end, delicate as mineral filaments yet tall enough to scrape against the vault of heaven. They neither gleam nor glow, but rather capture light and redirect it, bending brilliance through their forms like neural pathways in some vast, transparent brain.

This is Avascent, the Empty Metropolis.

Sprawling across a windswept high plateau, the city extends beyond all reasonable horizons. No one has walked its full breadth in living memory — if anyone still lived here to remember. The plateau itself sits among jagged mountains that rise like broken teeth against the sky, their forms worn smooth in places by countless centuries of rain and wind, yet still sharp enough to draw blood from clouds.

The air is thin and cool, carrying the metallic taste of altitude. Water exists here only in transient states — gathering as condensation on the crystal towers each morning, occasionally falling as brief, startling rains that sweep across districts like passing thoughts. These waters collect in channels and cisterns built into the very structure of the city, flowing through intricate aqueducts before disappearing into reservoirs deep below.

There are no plants here. No animals. Nothing grows or breathes or hunts. The city exists in pristine architectural solitude.

Yet Avascent is far from silent or still.

The primary sensation is *sound* — a vast, ceaseless symphony that emerges from the city itself. The crystal towers are tuned like impossibly complex instruments, each catching the high plateau winds and transforming them into tones. Some resonate at frequencies just within hearing range; others produce subsonic vibrations that can only be felt as pressure against the skin or bones. When rain falls, it strikes surfaces engineered to respond with precise tonal qualities, creating cascades of sound that ripple across neighborhoods.

The architecture follows no recognizable pattern of habitation. There are spaces that suggest plazas, corridors that imply movement, chambers that hint at gathering — but all scaled wrong, with proportions that seem to shift depending on where one stands. Doorways lead to vertical shafts. Stairways spiral into themselves. Bridges connect nothing to nothing across dizzying gaps.

The source of illumination is difficult to pinpoint. The sky above is bright — too bright — with a quality of light that seems to have mass, thickness. It casts shadows that are too sharp, too dark, creating stark contrasts between brilliance and absolute darkness. This light behaves almost as if sentient, focusing attention on certain structures while leaving others in shadow, then shifting its favor unpredictably.

Most disquieting is the city's movement. At first, it appears to be an illusion created by changing light. Then one notices that entire districts are slowly reconfiguring themselves. Crystal spires extend upward in real time. Archways bend to new angles. Plazas contract and expand like breathing lungs. The changes are gradual enough to doubt at first, but becoming undeniable after watching any section for several minutes. The city is in constant flux, rebuilding itself to some inscrutable pattern.

Who built Avascent? Its construction suggests intelligence but not humanity. There are no signs of inhabitants past or present. No furnishings, no debris, no artifacts of daily life. Just the endless crystal architecture, singing its strange music to itself, reconfiguring endlessly under the too-bright sky.

The only history here is written in erosion patterns — weathered edges on older structures, smoothed corners where wind has polished surfaces for millennia. The city is ancient yet perpetually renewed by its own movements.

In the distance, where the plateau edge meets sky, massive storm systems gather and dissipate with unnatural speed, as if the city's very presence disrupts atmospheric patterns. These storms occasionally sweep through, bringing brief torrential rains that activate the city's acoustic properties to their fullest, creating symphonies of such complexity that they seem to carry messages, meanings just beyond comprehension.

Then the rains pass, the sounds diminish to their baseline resonance, and Avascent continues its slow dance of architectural transformation, bright and empty and loud and utterly alone on its high plateau at the roof of the world.
