# The Mineral Aerie

Above the last wisps of atmosphere, where the planet's skin finally gives way to the void, the Cliffs of Unbeing claw at nothing. Their peaks reach such ludicrous heights that a person standing at the base would not perceive them as mountains but as walls of the world itself—vertical planes of obsidian and slate rising until perspective fails.

This is the realm where reality thins. The air is cool enough to make breath visible in tenuous clouds, though there should be no air at all. Silence predominates, but not absolute—for sound travels strangely here, disconnected from normal physics. Occasionally, a crystalline tone will ring out, sourceless, echoing for minutes before fading to nothing.

The urban sprawl of the Vacant City stretches across ledges and plateaus, buildings constructed from the same black volcanic glass as the mountains themselves. No chimneys smoke, no lights shine from windows. The structures appear carved rather than built, their geometry subtly wrong, with angles that seem to shift when not directly observed. Streets wind between them, laid out in patterns suggesting language or circuit boards when viewed from above. Yet no footfalls disturb the fine dust coating every surface.

What moisture exists here has been trapped for eons in perfectly spherical globules that hover motionless above certain intersections in the city. They reflect distorted images of surroundings that aren't quite there. Sometimes one will suddenly vaporize with a soft hiss, releasing a momentary fog that carries the scent of ozone and memory.

The only visible movement comes from the Drifters—translucent beings shaped like inverted teardrops that navigate the air currents between spires. They may be fauna, or they may be something else entirely—anomalies in space-time that have developed a form of sentience. They absorb light rather than reflect it, creating mobile shadows that slide across the glassy architecture.

At random intervals, portions of the landscape phase out of sync with the rest. A building might suddenly exist one meter to the left of its previous position. A courtyard might briefly invert, its floor becoming a ceiling from which dust falls upward. These events occur without sound or warning, as if reality here is poorly rendered and subject to rendering errors.

The erosion at this elevation is not from water or wind but from the friction between dimensions. Certain rock faces display perfect geometric patterns—hexagons, fractals, or Möbius topologies—worn into them by forces that have no name in conventional physics. These patterns sometimes animate in slow pulses when the light dims further during what passes for night.

Most unsettling are the Echo Fields—vast open plazas where anything that happens creates temporal duplications. A dislodged stone bouncing across the ground will replay its journey every few minutes, each iteration slightly different, until dozens of ghost-stones are tracing variant paths simultaneously.

No plants grow here, yet the surfaces of certain structures bear fractal patterns in dark gray and deep purple that slowly expand over centuries—perhaps mineral colonies, perhaps crystalline pseudo-life, or perhaps the visual manifestation of information propagating through the substrate of reality itself.

The air occasionally shimmers with curtains of dim light—auroras with no magnetosphere to explain them—casting brief shadows that don't align with the objects creating them. These lights move with purpose, as if searching.

This is a place where the laws of physics come to negotiate rather than rule. A realm suspended between being and unbeing, populated by echoes of things that may exist elsewhere or elsewhen, constructed by forces that may be deliberate or entirely accidental—the universe's subconscious expressing itself in cold stone and empty halls.
