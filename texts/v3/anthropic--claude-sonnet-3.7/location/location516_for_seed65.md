# The Silence Fields

The vast plateaus rise suddenly from the world below, their edges defined not by transition but by absence. The rise is too steep to be climbed, too sheer to be a mountain in any traditional sense. These are not peaks but flat-topped monoliths of impossible height, islands in the sky extending to the horizon.

The air here is thin and cool, requiring deliberate breaths. The plateaus stretch for miles, their surfaces nearly level but etched with shallow channels where ancient rains have, over millennia, carved delicate patterns into the pale stone. These intricate traceries, too deliberate-seeming to be random yet too complex to be designed, gather the occasional moisture that materializes here – not from clouds above but from the perpetual bright light that somehow condenses wetness from the air itself.

What dominates is not what exists, but what doesn't. Space. Emptiness. Distance. The flat expanse gives uninterrupted views to impossibly far horizons, where the eye can make out the edges of other plateaus hanging in the bright void. Between plateaus lies nothing but crystalline air, so clear that judging distance becomes an impossibility.

The light is relentless. It comes not from a sun but from everywhere – the sky itself seems to radiate brightness, shadowless and eternal. No day, no night, only perpetual illumination that somehow never warms beyond a slight chill.

What makes the place truly extraordinary is the sound. A constant, overwhelming roar that seems to emanate from the stone itself – a resonant, multitonal drone of impossible volume that should shatter eardrums but somehow doesn't. It's as though the entire plateau is a massive instrument played by the wind, or perhaps by forces altogether more mysterious. The sound has physical presence; it makes the sparse vegetation tremble continuously, their stunted forms having evolved to withstand this perpetual acoustic battering.

These plants are strange – low-growing crystalline structures that might be mineral rather than biological, scattered at great distances from one another across the plateau surface. They glisten with the moisture they've collected, tiny reservoirs that sustain the plateau's surprising abundance of life.

For there is life here, improbable though it seems. Small creatures dart between the crystalline growths – six-legged things with translucent bodies and multiple compound eyes that reflect the omnipresent light. Larger beasts move in herds across the distance – creatures with flattened bodies and wide feet that distribute their weight, grazing on the crystal-plants by grinding them with circular mouthparts. Overhead, impossibly large birds with wingspans measured in dozens of feet ride the acoustic currents, their bodies evolved to use the plateau's roar as a means of propulsion.

All these creatures are silent. In a realm of perpetual sound, they have evolved without voices. They communicate through movement, through reflection of the ever-present light, through patterns of disturbed air too subtle for an outsider to perceive. The fauna here is abundant beyond reason given the sparse vegetation, suggesting some unknown energy source fuels this ecology.

What seems at first a wasteland reveals itself as a stable, ancient system. Nothing here changes. The creatures follow patterns established over eons, the crystal-plants grow at imperceptible rates, the channels in the stone deepen by microns per century. Time itself seems to move differently here, stretched and thinned like the atmosphere.

The few humans who have found ways to ascend to these plateaus have left almost nothing behind – perhaps a primitive tool lost during their brief visits, or the occasional stack of stones marking their passage. The roar makes it impossible to remain for long, driving away all but the most determined explorers before they can leave any permanent mark on this place that exists beyond time, beyond silence, beyond ordinary understanding.
