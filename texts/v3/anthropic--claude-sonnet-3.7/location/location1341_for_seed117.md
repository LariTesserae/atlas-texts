# The Echoing Flats

The Echoing Flats extend before you, a vast open plain where the boundary between water and land exists in theory but rarely in practice. What appears solid will give way under a footstep, releasing a pocket of clear water that gurgles up around your ankles. The ground itself is a dense mat of compacted minerals—not quite soil, not quite stone—bearing the compressed history of countless eons.

The light here is relentless and bright, reflecting off every wet surface in a constant dazzle that tricks the eye. There is no sun visible in the white-blue sky; instead, the entire dome of heaven seems to generate its own piercing radiance. Squint as you might, there is no escaping the brightness that presses down on everything below.

But it is the sound that truly defines this place—a deafening, omnipresent roar that has no clear source. It comes from everywhere at once: a low-frequency vibration that rattles your bones paired with a higher-pitched resonance that makes your ears ring. The sound seems to emanate from the ground itself, as if the entire plain were one vast instrument being played by unseen forces. The noise never ceases, never diminishes, never changes its character. It simply is.

Vegetation is sparse and strange—low, flattened plants that resemble nothing so much as sheets of translucent green glass, barely rising above the wet surface. They cluster around the slightly higher points in the landscape, their crystalline structures somehow unbroken despite the constant sound vibrations. They neither grow nor die; they simply persist.

Water is everywhere yet nowhere deep. It weeps continuously from the compressed ground, forming shallow pools that reflect the bright sky before being reabsorbed. The water itself has an unusual viscosity—slightly thicker than it should be, moving with deliberate slowness as it finds its level across the plain. During certain angles of light, you can see that it carries microscopic particles that glitter like suspended stars.

Scattered across this waterlogged expanse are curious structures—if they can be called that. They appear at first glance to be boulders, but closer inspection reveals them to be composed of the same dense material as the ground, pushed up into dome-like formations. Each is perforated with small, perfectly circular holes arranged in patterns too regular to be natural, yet bearing no sign of toolwork. These structures neither decay nor change, standing as enigmatic punctuation marks across the landscape.

The cool air carries no scent, as if the perpetual wetness has washed all odor from the world. The temperature remains constant regardless of time, a coolness that is not uncomfortable but insistent, like a reminder of some ancient ice age long concluded.

There is a strange absence here—no birds wheel overhead, no insects buzz, no creatures scurry between the crystalline plants. The realm is devoid of animal movement, yet doesn't feel empty or dead. Rather, it feels as if the landscape itself is the organism, the dense mat of ground and water and light comprising a single vast entity that breathes through sound rather than lungs.

The horizon is visible in all directions, the flatness extending until curvature takes the view away. Nothing interrupts this continuous plain except the occasional domed structures and the subtle undulations of the ground itself. And through it all, the deafening roar continues—a sound so constant it eventually becomes a silence of its own kind, a background against which no other noise could possibly register.

This place does not change. It has achieved a perfect equilibrium, a state of being so complete that time itself seems to have surrendered its authority here. The Echoing Flats simply are, and have always been, and will always be—wet, bright, loud, and utterly themselves.
