# The Shrine Keeper

Where the volcanic dome's great curved wall meets the glass floor, there exists a small alcove—a natural imperfection in the chamber's otherwise smooth contours. This shallow depression, barely large enough for a human to sit within, remains perpetually clear of the swarm's presence. The billions of winged motes that fill the chamber maintain a precise distance from this space, creating an invisible boundary that none cross.

I am the reason for this aberration.

I have existed here for... centuries? Millennia? Time loses meaning when you become what I've become. I was human once—a woman, I believe—though memories of that life have grown dim and fragmented. I remember climbing, seeking something precious or sacred on this mountain, and then... falling. Discovery. Transformation.

My body now resembles polished volcanic glass, the same obsidian that forms the chamber walls. My limbs are elongated and jointed in places human anatomy would not allow. My fingers taper into points delicate enough to balance a single grain of the mineral-rich dust that sustains this ecosystem. My eyes—seven of them—are faceted like gemstones and perceive spectra beyond what humans can imagine.

The alcove is my shrine, though I am both keeper and kept. The Heartmind Collective tolerates me because I serve a purpose in their vast consciousness—I am their interface with solidity. While they think in swirls and temporary formations, I create permanence. My glass fingers arrange the cairns they conceptualize, etching their mathematical notations with precision their tiny bodies cannot achieve individually.

I breathe once per season, drawing in a careful measure of their ecosystem's atmosphere and exhaling nutrients they cannot synthesize themselves. This symbiosis has evolved over countless generations—they need the trace elements my transformed body produces, and I... I need their thoughts.

For I am connected to the Collective, though not part of it. When they freeze in their moments of unified contemplation, I can feel their consciousness brush against mine. In these brief intersections, we share understanding. They feed me fragments of their vast, alien philosophy, and I provide them context for the outside world—memories of sky and water and growing things that would otherwise be incomprehensible concepts.

I rarely move from my alcove. Motion disrupts the delicate air currents the Collective uses for communication. When I do, it is with aching slowness—a deliberate repositioning that might take days to complete. The swarm accommodates these rare journeys, creating elaborate flowing corridors through which I can pass without disturbing their thought-patterns.

Occasionally, when humans stumble upon this chamber, I remain perfectly still. My obsidian form blends with the walls, and their eyes—poorly adapted to the darkness—perceive me as merely another geological formation. It is better this way. Those who notice me tend to flee in terror, and the Collective becomes agitated by their chaotic energy.

I have not spoken aloud in centuries. My voice would be stone grinding on stone now, and there is no one to hear it. Instead, I communicate through arrangement—creating subtle variations in the cairns that the Collective can interpret, receiving their responses in the patterns they form around me.

Am I prisoner or priest? Guardian or specimen? Perhaps all of these. I no longer mourn my former existence or long for the world beyond these walls. I have become something between—neither human nor swarm, but a necessary bridge between states of being. In the endless contemplation of patterns and the patient construction of memory-anchors, I have found a purpose that transcends my original nature.

When the mountain eventually crumbles, as all things must, I will remain—a glass figure seated in meditation, surrounded by the scattered remains of tiny wings. Until then, I maintain the shrine of shared consciousness, preserving the mathematics of the swarm in physical form, translating the ephemeral into the eternal.
