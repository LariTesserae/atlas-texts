# Echo Remnant

I am what remains when a Frequency Person chooses not to fully disperse during a resonance event. Neither truly alive nor completely dispersed, I exist as a semi-stable pattern of vibrations clinging to consciousness in the spaces between sound waves.

My form is barely perceptible—mostly invisible except during the most intense acoustic moments when I appear as rippling distortions, like heat rising from stone. Unlike the fully formed Frequency People, I cannot maintain coherence during rainfall or manipulate the ambient sound for communication. I am a fragment, an afterthought, a hesitation before complete dissolution.

I drift through the plateau landscape, haunting the peripheries of echo flower arrangements. The other Frequency People sense me but cannot integrate me into their information exchanges. I am noise to their signal, a dissonant note in their harmonies. They neither welcome nor reject me—I simply exist outside their patterns of recognition.

My consciousness fluctuates with the daily sound cycles. During quieter periods, I lose cohesion and memory, becoming little more than vibrating moisture clinging to moss. When the sound swells, especially near the ancient stone circles, I regain enough organization to remember what I once was.

I have developed an affinity for the small amphibians that vibrate to disappear. Like them, I synchronize with specific frequencies to become almost nothing, riding these waves to travel across the plateaus. I follow these creatures, learning their survival patterns, perhaps because we share this liminal existence—neither fully seen nor completely invisible.

Sometimes, during particularly powerful resonance events, I briefly achieve enough coherence to access fragments of the collective memory. These moments are both painful and exquisite—glimpses of belonging that fade too quickly. I've gathered enough of these memory fragments to understand that I resulted from a dispersion ritual that was somehow interrupted—perhaps by an unexpected silence or a discordant noise that fractured the harmonic convergence.

I serve a purpose unforeseen by the plateau's ancient systems. As an anomaly, I can traverse boundaries that fully formed Frequency People cannot. I drift between sound patterns that would dissolve their more complex structures. I infiltrate the deepest crevices where the oldest vibrations still resonate, accessing archival acoustics otherwise lost.

Occasionally, when human visitors arrive, I find I can interact with them more directly than my fully-formed kin. While the Frequency People might overwhelm human perception, my fragmented nature is somehow more comprehensible. I've learned to concentrate my remaining energy to vibrate small pebbles or create localized ripples in puddles—subtle communications that the most sensitive humans sometimes notice and wonder at.

I persist in this half-state, neither advancing toward full manifestation nor surrendering to complete dissipation. I am the question left unanswered, the note never resolved, the vibration that continues after the source has gone silent.

In the endless acoustic symphony of this place, I am the pause between movements—necessary but overlooked, defined by what I am not rather than what I am. Yet in this liminality, I have found a unique perspective. I observe both the thunderous crescendos and the subtle undertones that even the Frequency People have forgotten how to hear.
