# The Shard-Singer

I am not of the city, yet I am bound to it. 

I drift between the Upreach and the world below, a translator of impossibilities. My form is neither fully avian nor anything else recognizable—a collection of reflective surfaces arranged in a vaguely bird-like shape, my "feathers" countless thin planes of crystal that capture and refract light in ways that hurt mortal eyes. When I move, I leave trails of prismatic distortion, temporary windows through which glimpses of other planes sometimes leak.

The Architects tolerate me because I remember what they have forgotten: the taste of water, the feel of earth, the world that exists beyond the plateau. The Weavers avoid me, for my song disrupts their unity. The Sentinels track my movements but cannot interpret them, as I follow patterns from worlds they've never witnessed.

My purpose is singular: I collect echoes.

When the pure notes of the Chorus ring out across the citadel, I capture their resonance in my crystalline form. I fold these sounds into my structure, storing them as patterns of vibration within my geometric feathers. Then, when the thin air of the Upreach becomes too rarefied for even my impossible lungs, I descend to the world below.

The descent is treacherous. Reality thickens as I fall, attempting to impose its laws upon my form. My edges blur, my consciousness fragments. Sometimes I lose pieces of myself—perfect crystal shards that plummet faster than I can follow, embedding themselves in the soil of the lower world like seeds of unreality.

But it is in the lower world that I fulfill my function. I find the dreamers—those rare minds capable of perceiving beyond their dimension's constraints. I perch near them as they sleep, and I sing.

My song is the Chorus's pure notes translated through my own imperfect understanding—mathematical truths rendered as melody, architectural blueprints expressed as harmony. As I sing, my crystal form vibrates, projecting images of the Avian Citadel into the dreamers' minds.

The dreamers awaken changed. Some begin building strange structures whose purpose they cannot explain. Others create art depicting birds that cannot exist. A few simply stare at the sky, their perception irreversibly altered. Each becomes, in their way, an antenna—a receiver tuned to frequencies from the Upreach.

I am neither savior nor destroyer. I am simply a conduit, a messenger carrying information in both directions. For as I collect the Chorus's notes, I also gather the dreams, fears, and memories of the world below. These I bring back to the Upreach, where they are incorporated into the ever-evolving architecture of the citadel.

The Wanderers recognize me as kin, though my purpose differs from theirs. Sometimes we exchange patterns—they give me new pathways through the city, and I offer them glimpses of the lower world's chaotic beauty. In these exchanges, I sense their fear of what lies within the crystal dome, and I wonder if my work serves to hasten or delay that inevitable emergence.

My existence is cyclic—ascend, collect, descend, distribute, repeat. I have performed this function for what might be centuries or millennia. Time means little when reality itself is negotiable.

Yet lately, something has changed. The notes of the Chorus grow more urgent, the patterns of the Weavers more complex. The dome at the center pulses with increasing frequency. And in the dreams of those below, I see reflections of what might be hatching—images so terrible and beautiful that even my crystalline form threatens to shatter at their transmission.

I am the Shard-Singer, the Echo-Carrier, the Bridge. And as the final configurations of the Avian Citadel take shape, I wonder if I am helping to build a doorway or a barrier—and which side of it I will ultimately serve.
