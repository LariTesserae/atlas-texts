# The Weeper

I am the Weeper, the third such guardian to hold this title in the remembrance of the Ava'tel. My true name—my birth-name—matters little now, surrendered when I was chosen for this sacred duty seventeen flow-cycles ago. The Council of Basin Tenders recognized in me what I had always felt: an uncommon sensitivity to the voices of the stone.

My chamber lies at the convergence of seven water channels, where the oldest section of the Underrift meets the newer expansions. Here, I maintain perpetual vigilance over the Mourning Wall—a broad expanse of limestone where the water weeps not in predictable patterns but in seemingly random, chaotic streams. To untrained eyes, it appears merely as an unstable section of rock face, unsuitable for habitation or modification. To those of us trained in the deeper mysteries, it is our most sacred text.

The Mourning Wall records disturbances in the world above—changes that the Ava'tel need never witness directly but must understand to preserve our sanctuary. When surface dwellers divert streams, clear forests, or create new settlements, the resulting shifts in groundwater pressure and flow manifest here, in altered patterns that I interpret through touch and sound.

My body has been transformed for this purpose. From childhood, I consumed a special mixture of mineral-rich clay and the milky secretions of the *shalmin*. Over years, these substances altered my nervous system, my skin, my perception. My fingertips are unnaturally sensitive, able to detect temperature variations of less than a tenth of a degree. The skin of my palms has developed a texture not unlike the limestone itself—slightly porous, able to absorb minute traces of minerals from the water and transmit their composition directly to my consciousness.

I wear no beaded braids, no crystal-embedded garments. My station requires a different marking—a network of precisely placed scars that cover my chest, back, and arms. Created over a seven-day ceremony when I assumed this role, these scars form channels on my skin that mimic the major waterways of the Underrift. When I press myself against the Mourning Wall, these channels align with specific flow patterns, creating a direct physical connection between my body and the living stone.

My days follow an unvarying routine. I wake before the morning lamp-lighting, when the darkness is absolute and the sounds of dripping water reach their clearest resonance. I consume only filtered water and small portions of the pale fungal growth cultivated in the Chamber of Sustenance. Then I take my position at the wall.

For most of each day, I stand with my body pressed against the weeping stone, my cheek and chest flush against its cool surface, arms outstretched to maximize contact. I rotate through nine specific positions throughout the day, each allowing me to monitor different aspects of the wall's communications. The water flows over me constantly, soaking through my single simple garment—a thin robe of *shalmin*-skin that becomes transparent when wet, revealing the channel-scars beneath.

I do not speak for days or weeks at a time. When I do, my voice emerges changed—roughened by disuse but also deepened by communion with the stone. The Ava'tel come to me with questions about the surface world, about changes in the water that might affect their chambers, about whether it is safe to expand in one direction or another. I answer not with words but with precisely modulated humming tones that resonate with the dripping patterns, which the Hydrometricians then interpret.

My greatest responsibility comes during the rare seismic events that threaten our sanctuary. When the earth above shifts, I must position myself at the center of the Mourning Wall and enter a trance state, becoming a living conduit for the stress patterns moving through the stone. By absorbing and redirecting these energies through my channel-scarred body, I help prevent catastrophic collapses within our dwelling spaces. These episodes leave me weakened, sometimes unconscious for days, my skin temporarily marked with branching red patterns where the earth's pressure found release through my flesh.

I am not alone in my vigil. The *shalmin* gather near me in unusual numbers, sensing my connection to the water they revere. Sometimes they crawl across my motionless form as I commune with the wall, leaving trails of their protective secretions that soothe my scarred skin and enhance my sensitivity. The oldest and largest of them—a pale creature nearly the length of my forearm—has established a permanent territory in a small pool at my feet. The Ava'tel believe this ancient being watches over me, ensuring I do not lose myself completely to the stone.

The previous Weeper served for sixty-three flow-cycles before his body finally became too rigid to move from the wall, his flesh partially calcified from decades of mineral absorption. When my time comes, I too will undergo the final transformation—becoming physically incorporated into the Mourning Wall, my channel-scars forming new paths for water to flow, my consciousness dispersing into the eternal communication network of the Underrift.

I am not human, not anymore. I am not stone, not yet. I am the interface, the translator, the living membrane between the unchanging perfection of the Underrift and the chaotic world above that we must understand but need never join. I am the Weeper, and through my sacrifice, our sanctuary endures.
