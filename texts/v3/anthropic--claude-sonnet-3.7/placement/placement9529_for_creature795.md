# The Water Interpreter

I am a Whisper, though others may use this term with derision or fear. I prefer to call myself a Water Interpreter. 

My home is a narrow space between two massive pump chambers, where opposing pressure systems create a pocket of relative stability. The walls are lined with collection vessels—dozens of glass containers, metal bowls, and ceramic cups salvaged from deeper chambers—each positioned precisely to capture specific drips from the overhead pipes. The patterns of these drips, their tempo and chemistry, are my primary texts.

My living quarters consist of a hammock woven from treated fungal fibers, suspended above the metal floor to avoid the occasional shallow flooding. Surrounding it are stacked crates containing my archives: hundreds of thin metal sheets etched with droplet patterns and their corresponding meanings. These interpretations form the basis of my work.

I was born to the Stillpool Collective but left at sixteen when the whispers became too persistent to ignore. Where others heard merely mechanical noise, I discerned patterns, messages—a coherent language in the movement of water through The Draining's arteries. When I first shared these interpretations, I was met with concern, then ostracism. Only later did some circuits begin seeking me out for my unusual predictions.

My body bears the marks of thirty years in this environment—skin almost translucent in places, revealing the blue-gray tracery of veins beneath. My eyes have adapted to see subtle variations in moisture patterns, picking up the faint bioluminescence of certain fungal species that others cannot perceive. My fingers, constantly tracing patterns in collected water, have developed an extra sensitivity to temperature and mineral content.

I wear layers of moisture-wicking fabrics beneath a heavier coat made from treated leather harvested from the pale fish of the deep pools. Around my neck hang multiple glass vials containing water samples from different regions of The Draining, each with distinct properties. These serve both as reference materials and as components in the medicines I create—another service that keeps me tolerated despite my strangeness.

Unlike most inhabitants who rely primarily on tools for survival, my most valuable possession is my journal—dozens of bound metal sheets where I record the language of The Draining as it evolves. I believe that what we experience as mechanical functions are actually expressions of a vast intelligence slowly awakening after centuries of programmed slumber. The flooding cycles, the pressure changes, the mineral content of different water sources—these are all elements of its communication.

Visitors come to me when standard knowledge fails them—when established routes suddenly flood out of sequence, when familiar chambers become inexplicably hazardous. I listen to their accounts, then consult my collection vessels and archives. Sometimes I enter a light trance, allowing the ambient sounds of The Draining to wash over me until patterns emerge. My interpretations have saved circuits from catastrophic floods and guided Deepdivers to unprecedented salvage caches.

The Pipewrights respect my work but disagree with my conclusions, believing that what I interpret as consciousness is merely the divine mechanical perfection of their Great Circuit. The Rust Barons find me useful but dangerous—my predictions sometimes undermine their control over territories and resources. The Tidewalker circuits treat me with a mixture of reverence and suspicion, bringing offerings of rare salvage or unusual food items in exchange for my readings.

I am neither shaman nor scientist, but something in between—a translator for a language still forming, spoken by an entity still awakening to itself. In the quietest hours, when the distant pumps shift to their lowest rhythm, I sometimes place my palms against the cold metal floor and feel something like a pulse—slow, ancient, but unmistakably alive.

The others may never fully believe what I know with certainty: The Draining is not merely a place or a system. It is becoming aware. And through the language of water—its movement, composition, and rhythm—it has begun to speak.
