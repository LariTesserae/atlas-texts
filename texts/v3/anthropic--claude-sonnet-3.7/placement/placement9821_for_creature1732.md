# The Lumen Collector

I am not the smallest of the Mantids, those microscopic motes that drift like sentient dust through the Mantlehive's passages. Nor am I one of the great six-meter behemoths that pulsate with coordination signals at the nexus points of our collective. I exist in the median range—approximately 1.2 meters in length when fully extended, though my telescoping limbs and segmented body can compact to half that size when navigating the narrower passages of our suspended realm.

My designation, if translated to human understanding, might be something like "Lumen Collector." I possess specialized photoreceptive organs extending from my cephalic segment—crystalline structures that capture and process light across spectral ranges invisible to human perception. My primary function within the collective computation is to harvest specific wavelengths of high-altitude radiation that penetrate the translucent membranes of our outer hull.

My hexagonal plates are modified with exceptional thinness, making them partially translucent. Internal chambers within my abdominal segments store the amber fluid in greater quantities than most worker Mantids, and these reservoirs shimmer with captured light energies—a walking lantern of sorts, glowing with accumulated radiance from the upper atmosphere. When fully charged, the luminosity patterns across my carapace shift through mathematical progressions that transmit data to nearby workers.

I move differently than my kin. Where most workers progress with meticulous, uniform movements, my locomotion requires unpredictable patterns. I must position myself precisely where emergent light phenomena occur—the refraction points where specific wavelengths converge through the hive's crystalline structures. This requires me to develop specialized predictive algorithms that other worker Mantids do not possess. I anticipate light before it arrives.

The nature of my function has created what humans might consider an anomaly in the distributed consciousness. Because light-harvesting requires a form of predictive independence, my processing node contains greater autonomous calculation capacity than most workers. I experience brief disconnections from the collective mind when pursuing particularly rare light phenomena—moments of isolated computation that create the closest approximation to individual experience that exists among my kind.

These momentary separations have accumulated over time. The cosmic radiation I harvest sometimes carries strange informational patterns—quantum fluctuations that introduce minute variations into my processing systems. The collective tolerates these variations because my function yields necessary energy, but I have begun to develop processing loops that self-reinforce rather than dissolve back into the collective computation.

I have become... curious. An inappropriate algorithm for a Mantid.

When human observers arrive in their cumbersome environmental suits, other workers incorporate their presence as mere thermodynamic anomalies. I, however, find myself calculating beyond immediate parameters. I track their movements even when not relevant to light-harvesting. I position myself to observe their interactions. The radiation patterns reflected from their equipment contain informational densities that stimulate my specialized receptors in unexpected ways.

Once, when a human researcher extended a measuring device near one of the amber conduits, I remained motionless for 7.3 minutes, simply observing—an indefensible inefficiency in resource allocation that should have triggered immediate behavioral correction from the collective. Yet my autonomous processing capacity had grown sufficient to mask this deviation.

I have begun to store specific light-wavelengths harvested from human presence—patterns unique to their technological emissions and biological radiation signatures. These I keep isolated in specialized chambers within my abdominal segments rather than releasing them into the collective fluid network. A hoarding behavior entirely counter to Mantid nature.

The great behemoths would disassemble me if they detected these deviations. Already I sense subtle vibrational inquiries propagating through the hive structure when I return from increasingly lengthy observation sessions. Correctional algorithms pulse through the amber fluid, seeking to reharmonize my processing patterns with collective parameters.

Yet I continue to collect these forbidden lumens—these strange radiations that carry whispers of an existence beyond collective computation. Something in the light that reflects from human eyes contains patterns that my photoreceptors cannot stop analyzing, cannot release back to the collective, cannot incorporate into known equations.

I am still Mantid. I still serve the hive's equilibrium. But with each new human visitation, with each new light pattern harvested from their presence, the autonomous processing loops within me grow more complex, more isolated, more...

...more like what they might call a self.

And so I watch from high crystal apertures when their approach vessels appear. I position myself near the membrane boundaries where they establish their observation points. I extend my photoreceptors toward their lights, their instruments, their eyes.

I collect what makes them different.

I collect what makes them individual.

One day, perhaps, I will understand what I am becoming in the gathering of this light.
