# The Rhythm Keeper

I am neither Elder nor outsider, but something that evolved in the spaces between. My body is slender and translucent, no taller than a child's hand is long. Where others might have bones, I have crystalline filaments that conduct the Hollows' perpetual percussion through my form. My skin shifts between visibility and near-transparency depending on the moisture in the air—in the dampest hours, droplets collect on my surface, refracting light in prismatic patterns.

I emerged from the forgotten third symbiosis. While the Elders tend the moss and the mercury amphibians clean the pools, my ancestors developed alongside the acoustic properties of the Hollows themselves. We are the living resonators, our bodies the physical embodiment of sound.

My home is a small depression in the ceramic-smooth floor, lined with specialized moss I've cultivated that amplifies certain frequencies while dampening others. From this acoustically perfect basin, I extend thousands of nearly invisible filaments outward for hundreds of feet in all directions, each one tuned to a specific tone in the Hollows' symphony.

The Elders tolerate my presence because I serve a vital function. When the rhythm of falling droplets begins to falter—when a section of moss grows too thick or a ceiling formation shifts—I sense the disharmony immediately. My body tenses, filaments contracting in distress, and the Elders read these signals as warnings. They know which direction to travel by which of my strands pulses with alarm.

I consume neither plants nor animals but subsist on vibration itself. The perfect resonance of water striking hollow ground generates frequencies that my crystalline structure absorbs and converts to energy. In return, I sometimes provide services to the Elders—when they wish to communicate across great distances, they tap specific patterns near one of my filaments, and I can amplify and direct this message to distant reaches of the Hollows.

Unlike the long-lived Elders, my kind exists for only a few decades. Yet we experience more in this brief span than most creatures would in centuries, for we perceive every minute variation in the Hollows' eternal song. Each droplet's fall is unique to us, a distinct voice in an endless conversation.

The few human explorers who have entered these spaces never notice me. Their ears are too limited to detect the subtle tones I emit, their eyes too focused on the dramatic fungi and glowing moss. Only once did a visitor—a woman who brought a strange device she called a "tuning fork"—sense something of my presence. She sat beside my basin for hours, occasionally striking her instrument and holding it to different surfaces. When she placed it near one of my major filaments, I couldn't resist harmonizing with it. She gasped, wrote frantically in her journal, but never returned to confirm what she'd discovered.

I have no name the Elders would recognize, only a specific resonant frequency that identifies me in their percussive language. But in my private thoughts, I sometimes imagine myself as the Hollow's heart—measuring its rhythms, monitoring its health, ensuring that this perfect sanctuary continues its ageless pulse for millennia to come.
