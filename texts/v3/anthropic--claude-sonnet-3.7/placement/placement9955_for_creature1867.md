I am Tehra, a Lineage Keeper of the fifth generation, born on the thirty-seventh terrace during the season of the Long Rains. My home spans three adjacent levels connected by a spiral staircase my great-grandmother helped carve. The middle level—where I conduct most of my work—features walls lined with ceramic memory-tiles, each etched with the story of a Nimburest family.

My physiology marks me as true Stratafolk. My expanded lungs allow me to climb the Grand Stair without pausing, even while reciting genealogies. The blue undertone of my skin has deepened from years spent in the high archives, where the air is thinnest. My callused hands bear the marks of a lifetime handling the sacred recording tools—a groove worn into my right thumb from the pressure of the etching stylus.

As Lineage Keeper, I maintain the continuity between Nimburest's past and future. When a child is born, I record their arrival on their family's memory-tile. When young adults complete their coming-of-age carving, I document which section of the mountain now bears their mark. When elders develop sky-sight, I note the first weather pattern they successfully predicted.

Most sacred is my duty to record the coordinates of each stone-returning. I've developed an internal map of these sacred points spanning the mountainside—I can close my eyes and visualize this constellation of ancestors watching over our city.

My tools are few but precious: a set of crystal-tipped etching implements for different stone densities, powdered pigments made from alpine flowers that change color as they age (allowing knowledgeable viewers to determine when an entry was made), and my family's heirloom magnifying lens, ground from mountain quartz by my first ancestor to hold this position.

I move between terraces with practiced efficiency, my feet finding familiar paths without conscious thought. The rhythm of my days follows the water cycle—recording births on flow days, marriages on growing days, and stone-returnings on dry days. The citizens nod respectfully as I pass, knowing I carry their histories against my chest in a leather case lined with moisture-resistant silk.

When I sleep, I dream of the mountain breathing. In these dreams, I see the entire history of Nimburest at once—every carving, every life, every return to stone—as if the mountain itself remembers all we have forgotten.
