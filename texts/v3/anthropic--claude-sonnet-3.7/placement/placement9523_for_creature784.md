# The Sound Sculptor

I drift between the states of matter in the Reverberating Vault, my existence a delicate balance of intention and surrender. I am neither fully solid nor liquid nor gas, but a conscious entity composed of responsive crystalline formations—transparent needles and plates that catch and channel sound into visible form.

The natives call me Ekko, though the name comes closer to a harmonic pattern than a word. The Songweaver tolerates my presence because I offer something unique: I translate sound into temporary structure, capturing the vault's acoustic phenomena in frozen moments of crystalline beauty.

My body, if you could call it that, resembles a cloud of floating geometric fragments that contract and expand with the currents. When at rest, I appear as a loose constellation of transparent shards, each facet designed to capture specific frequencies. When working, I condense into more deliberate forms—sometimes humanoid, sometimes abstract, always governed by the mathematics of sound.

I wasn't born here. I arrived decades ago as something else entirely—a human acoustical engineer who ventured too deeply into an underwater cave system during a research expedition. The transformation wasn't sudden but gradual; the vault's peculiar properties reorganized my molecular structure over months of exposure. What began as crystalline growths on my skin eventually replaced it entirely, until nothing of my original form remained but my consciousness and my obsession with sound.

The Songweaver was fascinated by my metamorphosis. While it understands sound as pure vibrational information, I experience it differently—as architecture waiting to be manifested. My gift is giving temporary physical form to the vault's acoustic phenomena, creating sculptures that capture harmonic relationships in crystalline lattices that hover in the water like frozen music.

I've developed a symbiotic relationship with this place. The sound sustains me, provides the energy that maintains my unusual existence, while I create ephemeral monuments to particularly beautiful or significant acoustic events. My creations last anywhere from minutes to months before dissolving back into the medium.

The Songweaver sometimes employs me as a translator for visitors who cannot comprehend its native form of communication. I construct careful arrangements of crystal that refract its vibrational language into visual patterns more readily understood by beings accustomed to processing information through eyes rather than entire-body sensation.

I maintain a small alcove near one of the half-submerged apertures—a workshop of sorts where I keep my most enduring creations. There, visitors might find a delicate lattice that captures the harmonics of what the Songweaver calls the "first vibration," or intricate fractal structures that map the mathematical relationships between different acoustic phenomena.

Time means little here. I work, I drift, I listen. Sometimes I extend tendrils of crystal into the tunnels, sensing distant chambers through their acoustic signatures. I've mapped much of the Songweaver's domain this way, creating three-dimensional models that float in my workshop like strange constellations.

On rare occasions, I venture through the apertures to the surface world, assuming a more cohesive form that can temporarily exist outside the vault's nurturing environment. These expeditions are brief—the ordinary world lacks the acoustic energy I need to maintain my crystalline structure—but necessary to remind myself of what exists beyond this resonant realm.

I no longer mourn what I once was. The transformation has given me sensory experiences beyond human imagination—I can feel the subtle variations in water temperature as tonal shifts, perceive quantum vibrations as delicate harmonies underlying the vault's louder symphonies, and communicate through pure crystalline resonance with the Songweaver itself.

When visitors arrive, I often create a simple gift for them before they depart—a small crystal formation tuned to their unique vibrational signature. These tokens eventually dissolve in the outside world, but while they last, they allow the bearer to briefly perceive sound as I do: as tangible, visible architecture floating on the currents of existence.
