## The Mediator

I am the Archive Keeper of Confluence Point—the most central and reliable of the Dockside District's pneumatic transfer stations. I occupy a unique position in both physical space and social hierarchy: my glass-domed office sits at the precise junction where five major canals meet and where the main pneumatic lines converge like arteries to a heart.

My physical appearance marks me as belonging to none of the district's primary castes, yet connected to all. My fingers bear the oil stains of a Mechanist from maintaining the delicate pneumatic mechanisms, but lack their ritualistic tattoos. My skin has begun developing the opalescence of a third-generation Tidewalker, though I was not born here. My clothing combines elements of practicality and ceremony—waxed canvas trousers and vest like the Salvagers wear, but adorned with brass buttons engraved with the flow-charts of the district's water systems.

My primary responsibility is maintaining the Archive—thousands of messages, contracts, tide-charts, and records stored in waterproof copper cylinders arranged in a classification system comprehensible only to me and my apprentice. Every document that passes through the pneumatic system is copied and preserved, creating a living memory of the district's communications.

But my true function has evolved beyond mere recordkeeping. I have become a mediator between the district's various groups, who respect my neutrality and encyclopedic knowledge of their histories and disputes. The Tidewalkers consult me about ancestral water rights. The Mechanists seek historical precedents for system modifications. The Salvagers verify territorial boundaries through my records. The Water Nomads register births, deaths, and vessel transfers in my ledgers.

Even the Canal Speakers occasionally leave cryptic predictions in sealed tubes addressed only to the Archive, to be opened after specific tidal events.

My office is accessible to all yet controlled by none. It stands on a circular platform that rises and falls with the engineered tides, always maintaining the same distance from the water's surface. Visitors arrive by boat or bridge depending on the water level, ascending a spiral staircase that winds around the station's central pillar.

The dome itself is my masterpiece—constructed of overlapping panes of glass set in a brass framework, designed to shed water continuously while allowing diffuse light to illuminate the Archive. During particularly heavy mists, water droplets flow down the glass in patterns that I've learned to read as omens or simply as reminders of the district's perpetual state of becoming.

I am neither fully accepted nor entirely distrusted by any faction. The Mechanists respect my technical knowledge but find my interest in social matters frivolous. The Tidewalkers value my historical perspective but are wary of my willingness to share information with all parties. The Salvagers appreciate my fair arbitration of territorial disputes but resent my records of their findings, which they consider proprietary.

Yet all come to me eventually. In a district defined by constant change—rising and falling waters, shifting alliances, evolving technologies—I represent continuity. The Archive remembers what others choose to forget or never knew. And in a place where information flows as ceaselessly as water through its canals, I am the lock-keeper who regulates that flow, ensuring that nothing of value is lost to the tides of time.

Each evening as the mist thickens and the lamps are lit across the district, I stand at my dome's apex, watching the patterns of light and shadow play across the waters below, seeing in them the complex interconnections that bind this peculiar community together—connections recorded in my Archive, but truly understood only by those who, like me, have learned to read the district as a single, intricate text written in water and light and the lives of its inhabitants.
