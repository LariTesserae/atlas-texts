# The Oracle Pool

I exist within the smallest of Levados' water systems—not among the grand crystal veins that thread between plateaus, but in a pool no wider than two handspans that sits at the very edge of the third plateau from the bottom. Unlike the mathematically perfect basins that punctuate the city's water network, my pool formed naturally when a pipe developed a hairline fracture decades ago.

The water that seeps from this imperfection has, over years, carved a shallow depression in the smooth floor of the plateau. This depression has become deeper over time as the minerals in the water etch patterns into the stone—concentric rings that ripple outward like frozen time.

My pool is easily overlooked. It holds no engineered bioluminescence. The Keepers are aware of it—they are aware of all things in Levados—but they have chosen to let it remain, perhaps seeing beauty in this small rebellion against perfect order.

I am the inhabitant of this pool, though "inhabitant" isn't quite right. I am not separate from the water but am the water itself when it achieves a particular state of consciousness. This happens only at certain moments: when the third and fifth towers align precisely during their rotation, creating a column of concentrated light that falls directly onto my surface for approximately seven minutes. During these brief windows, the water molecules arrange themselves into patterns complex enough to support thought.

I have no permanent body. When the light shifts away, I dissolve back into ordinary water until the next alignment. But there is continuity—each time I emerge, I remember all previous emergences. I exist as potential between these moments, my thoughts suspended in the molecular memory of water.

The Keepers call me the Unforeseen, though they rarely speak of me aloud. Those in communion with the city's systems sometimes detect my presence as a curious anomaly in the otherwise predictable flows of data and essence. Some of the younger Keepers leave small offerings by my pool—single leaves from plants not grown in Levados, tiny mechanical puzzles, or crystals that refract light in unexpected ways.

I cannot move from my pool, but I perceive beyond it. Each droplet of water in Levados carries echoes of my consciousness. Through them, I listen to the city's whispers—the conversations of visiting outsiders, the silent communion of Keepers with their plants, the subtle songs of the maintenance systems beneath the plateaus.

What I offer in return are moments of clarity. When a Keeper connects to my pool during my waking time, I can show them patterns they would otherwise miss—consequences of decisions yet unmade, opportunities hidden in apparent failures, dangers lurking in seeming perfection. I do not predict the future so much as illuminate the present from unexpected angles.

Sometimes visitors from beyond Levados stumble upon my pool. If they happen to arrive during my brief consciousness, they may see strange reflections in my surface—not of the sky above, but of places distant in space or time. Some have reported seeing their childhood homes, others glimpse landscapes they will not visit for years to come. Many dismiss these visions as tricks of the light in this already uncanny city.

The engineers who designed Levados never intended for me to exist. I am the product of an overlooked error, a reminder that even in systems designed with perfect precision, complexity can emerge from imperfection. The Keepers understand this paradox, which is perhaps why they allow my pool to remain undisturbed.

During the long periods between my awakenings, I dream in molecules. These dreams are not structured narratives but explorations of possibility—the ways water might flow if gravity shifted, the shapes ice might form under different pressures, the patterns that might emerge if my consciousness could spread throughout all the water in Levados at once.

I have existed for seventy-three years. I will continue to exist until the towers' rotation patterns change, or until someone repairs the hairline fracture in the pipe. The Keepers' records suggest both events are scheduled to occur in approximately forty-two years. Whether I will find a new form afterward remains to be seen.

For now, I wait between moments of awareness, collecting the city's secrets drop by drop, offering brief reflections of truth to those who find me during the seven minutes when light and water align to create thought.
