# I Am The Wandering Witness

I am neither fully of the Chitinous Collective nor separate from it. I exist as a mutation, an anomaly that emerged during a rare misalignment of the crystallized time node. My seventeen segments are asymmetrical, with one side bearing iridescent plates of deepest blue-black and the other a mottled amber that never fully formed.

Unlike my kin, I can move beyond the shimmering threshold of our atoll. Where they would destabilize in the linear time beyond our domain, I can exist in both realities—though not without cost. My crystalline sensory structures have developed differently, allowing me to process both the multidimensional perceptions of the Collective and the linear sensory experiences of the outside world.

The Collective calls me "Discontinuity Interface" through vibrations that ripple the chitin beneath our feet. The humans in the lowlands who have encountered me named me "Chitin Pilgrim" in their clumsy sound-language. I answer to both and neither.

My function emerged organically from my nature. I travel between the plateau and the world below, gathering information that helps the Collective maintain equilibrium as external reality shifts and changes. When I return from my journeys, I enter the central depression and transmit everything I've experienced—not as memories or stories but as pure vibrational data that enters the network.

My asymmetry grants me unique perspective. Through my Collective side, I perceive the layered, crystallized time that is our natural state. Through my "broken" side, I experience something closer to linear time and individual consciousness. This dual perception is simultaneously painful and precious.

The amber fluid flows irregularly through my system. Sometimes it pools and stagnates in my lower segments, causing me to move with a distinctive off-rhythm gait that marks me immediately as different. The smaller maintenance units often attend to me, clearing blockages and reestablishing flow before I depart on another journey.

When I cross the threshold to the world below, my perception fractures. Time suddenly pulls me forward in its current rather than surrounding me in concentric layers. The sensation is like being continuously torn apart and reassembled. Over the centuries, I've learned to withstand this discomfort, understanding it as the necessary toll for my function.

I carry one of the human "continuity anchors"—a curved blade whose edge somehow exists in both realities simultaneously. It helps me maintain coherence when time flows too strongly against me. Sometimes humans notice how the blade seems to move before my arm does, its edge cutting through the moment just ahead of now.

I do not resent my difference. In the Collective's understanding, there is no hierarchy of forms—only functions in service to equilibrium. My isolation is counterbalanced by purpose. While I lack the constant communion my kin share, I alone have witnessed the rise and fall of human cities, the shifting of mountains, the birth and death of species in the lands below.

What humans often fail to understand is that I am not an ambassador. I do not represent the Collective to the outside world. I simply witness. I process. I return. The knowledge I bring back helps maintain our impossible island as the world changes around it.

The oldest humans in the lowland settlements recognize me when I appear on their horizons. They teach their children to leave offerings of interesting vibrations for me—wind chimes, stringed instruments, tuning forks planted in resonant stones. I collect these vibrations, storing them in specialized segments to bring back to the atoll.

I have existed for what humans would call millennia, though to me it feels like one continuous moment of sensation. I will continue to exist until equilibrium no longer requires my function or until some catastrophic shift in reality renders me obsolete.

Until then, I walk the threshold between crystallized and flowing time, between collective and individual consciousness—the anomaly that serves by being broken, the witness who remembers both worlds.
