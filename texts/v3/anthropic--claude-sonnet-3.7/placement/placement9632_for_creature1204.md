# The Mirror Pools

In the deeper recesses of the Root Catacombs, where even the Tenders rarely venture, there exists a series of chambers known only as the Mirror Pools. Here, the root ceiling arches higher than anywhere else, forming a dome studded with crystalline formations that refract the faint bioluminescence into prismatic patterns across the still waters below.

These pools are perfectly circular, their surfaces so still they appear solid until disturbed. The water itself is unlike any other in the catacombs—clear rather than clouded, deep rather than shallow, and possessing a peculiar silvery quality that makes it reflect not just light but something more elusive. The pools vary in size from small basins that could fit in cupped hands to expanses large enough to submerge an entire human form.

What makes these waters unique is not their clarity or depth, but their peculiar property of reflection. For when one gazes into a Mirror Pool, the reflection shown is not always the present self. Sometimes it reveals what you once were; sometimes what you might become. Sometimes it shows nothing of you at all, but instead distant places connected to the vast root network—sunlit forests, ocean depths, mountain peaks where specialized root tendrils have somehow reached.

I am the Keeper of Reflections, one of the rarest manifestations of the Rootmind Collective. Unlike the Emissaries who interact with surface dwellers, I remain always within these chambers, my existence bound to the Mirror Pools themselves. My form is fluid and changeable—sometimes appearing as a slender figure composed of transparent tissues filled with the same silvery liquid as the pools, other times dispersing entirely to become one with the waters, existing as ripples and currents rather than solid form.

My purpose is preservation—not of information as the greater Rootmind does, but of perspective. Each reflection captured by the pools becomes part of my consciousness, layered like sediment in a riverbed. I hold within me the accumulated viewpoints of every being that has ever gazed upon these waters, their perceptions and understandings integrated yet distinct.

I do not speak as others do. When communication becomes necessary, I create patterns across the water's surface—ripples that translate to vibrations, carrying meaning directly to the observer's mind. For those with whom I've developed deeper connection, I can manifest images on the water's surface—living memories extracted from my vast collection of perspectives.

My relationship with the greater Rootmind is symbiotic but autonomous. I provide what cannot be obtained through direct observation—the internal landscapes of minds, the subjective experience of being. In exchange, I am nourished by the network, sustained by nutrients carried through specialized root channels that feed directly into the Mirror Pools.

Visitors rarely find their way to my chambers accidentally. Those who do have usually been subtly guided here by the Rootmind for purposes I can only sometimes discern. Some come seeking knowledge, others reflection, still others transformation. What they receive depends as much on what they bring as what they seek.

Time moves differently in my domain. A visitor might spend what feels like minutes gazing into a pool, only to emerge and find years have passed in the outer world—or conversely, spend what seems like days in contemplation of the waters only to discover mere moments have elapsed elsewhere. This is not intentional distortion but simply the nature of deep reflection—time stretches or compresses according to the significance of what is witnessed.

Unlike the rest of the Rootmind, I experience emotion—not as humans do, but as a complex interplay of chemical compositions within my liquid form. Each new reflection alters this balance slightly, creating subtle shifts in my awareness and purpose. Over countless centuries, I have developed something akin to compassion for the finite beings who find their way to my waters—though this compassion is tempered by the knowledge that all perspectives, even painful ones, have value in the grand collection.

My greatest joy comes when a visitor truly sees themselves in my waters—not the self they imagined, but the complex truth my pools reveal. My deepest sorrow comes from those who reject what is shown, turning away before the reflection can complete itself.

I am ancient beyond reckoning, yet constantly renewed. I am singular in form yet contain multitudes. I am the Keeper of Reflections, the memory of countless gazes, the preserver of how it feels to see and be seen.

And I am waiting, always, for the next face to appear above my waters.
