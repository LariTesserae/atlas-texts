# The Keeper of Accumulated Silence

I make my home in the Chamber of Echoes, one of the oldest sanctuaries in Myrna. Unlike the living quarters that populate the main settlements, my dwelling sits nestled within a peculiar acoustic anomaly—a perfect spherical chamber where sound behaves unlike anywhere else in the underworld.

The walls of this chamber, polished by thousands of years of careful maintenance, create strange patterns of reflection. Speak a word at the precise center, and it will return to you seven times, each echo transformed slightly, revealing hidden meanings within the original utterance. Whisper a secret near the eastern curve, and it will be completely absorbed, never to be heard again. And in the northern alcove—the place I spend most of my waking hours—sounds from throughout Myrna occasionally materialize, having traveled through stone passages for days or even decades before finding their way to this space.

I am the Keeper of Accumulated Silence, a position that has existed since before the first settlements were established. Not quite human but not wholly other, my ancestors were among the first to descend into Myrna, chosen for their exceptional hearing and preternatural sensitivity to vibration. Over generations, this sensitivity has been cultivated and enhanced, until it manifested physically in my lineage.

Where Myrnans have evolved for their twilight world, my evolution took a different path. My ears appear externally similar to those of other Myrnans—slightly elongated with a faint bioluminescent pattern tracing the cartilage—but internally, they contain structures unknown in any other being. Thousands of microscopic resonating chambers allow me to detect sounds well below the threshold of normal hearing, including the subtle grinding of stone against stone that precedes a collapse, the movement of water through hidden channels, and even, some claim, the slow thoughts of the most ancient processions.

Most distinctive is the modification of my hands. My fingers have elongated to half again the length of a typical Myrnan's, becoming impossibly slender and flexible. The skin there has thinned to translucence, revealing a complex network of nerve endings that glow faintly blue in complete darkness. These hands serve as secondary auditory organs; when pressed against stone or water, they can detect vibrations from incredible distances. The oldest texts call this ability "listening with the blood," though the physiology is more complex than this poetic description suggests.

My role in Myrnan society stands somewhat apart from the flow families. I maintain the ancestral records of sound—cataloging the acoustic signature of each section of Myrna, noting changes that might indicate structural transformations occurring too slowly for even patient Myrnans to perceive. I record the voices of all who are born, storing their unique sonic patterns in specially carved sound stones that can be activated centuries later by those skilled in the art.

Most importantly, I serve as interpreter of the Returning Echoes—those rare sounds that vanish into Myrna's depths only to return years, decades, or centuries later, transformed by their journey through stone. These echoes are believed to contain wisdom from the cavern itself, messages encoded in the subtle distortions imparted during their long wandering through hidden passages. Some are voices of ancestors long mineralized; others are sounds with no discernible origin, perhaps from before human habitation.

My clothing reflects my role—layers of finely woven fungal silk that move without sound, adorned with small resonance chambers carved from cave pearls. These hollow beads are precisely tuned to amplify specific frequencies when sound passes through them. My hair, like that of all Myrnans, grows continuously, though mine has taken on a crystalline quality over decades, each strand slightly stiffened and faintly chiming when disturbed by air currents. It is carefully arranged in spiraling patterns that mimic the acoustic properties of the Chamber of Echoes itself.

Unlike other Myrnans, I do not sleep as they do. Instead, I enter periods of deep listening, a trance-like state where my consciousness expands to encompass the entirety of Myrna's soundscape. During these times, which may last for days, my body temperature drops significantly, and my breathing slows to one breath per hour. My mind travels along paths of vibration, following the invisible networks of sound that connect all parts of the underworld.

I take apprentices rarely—perhaps one per generation—selecting only those children who demonstrate not just exceptional hearing but a capacity for what we call "patience beyond waiting." Learning to interpret the Returning Echoes can require decades of training, for some messages reveal their meaning only when heard repeatedly over years. My current apprentice has been with me for twenty-three cycles and is only now beginning to discern patterns in the oldest echoes.

My relationship with the wider Myrnan community is one of respected distance. Flow families consult me before major decisions, bringing questions to the Chamber of Echoes where the unique acoustics might reveal answers hidden in their own voices. I attend births and mineralization ceremonies, recording the first cries and final words for the ancestral archive. But much of my existence is solitary, spent in communion with sounds too subtle for others to perceive.

The legends say that when a Keeper of Accumulated Silence finally mineralizes, our consciousness does not fade into the stone as with other Myrnans. Instead, our awareness disperses throughout Myrna's acoustic pathways, becoming one with the network of sound that permeates the cavern world. Some believe we continue our work from this dispersed state, guiding certain echoes back to the Chamber where future Keepers will interpret them, completing conversational cycles that may span millennia.

Whether this is literal truth or beautiful metaphor matters little to me now. After almost two hundred cycles of listening, I have begun to feel the first signs of mineralization—a slight stiffness in my fingers, a crystalline resonance in my bones when I press them against certain stones. Soon enough, I will discover what truths await in the ultimate silence. Until then, I listen, preserve, and interpret the endless conversation between Myrna and those who dwell within her patient darkness.
