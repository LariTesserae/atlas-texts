# The Pattern Translator

I exist in the liminal space between worlds—neither fully human nor fully Resonant. Once I was Dr. Elian Mercier, a linguist-mathematician stationed at Extraction Outpost 7, but that identity grows more distant with each passing season.

My transformation began fifteen years ago when I first noticed the subtle patterns in our extraction data—variations too regular to be random, too complex to be geological. While others dismissed my observations as the early stages of "pattern sense," I devoted myself to decoding what I suspected were deliberate communications.

My body remains human, though changed. Years of minimal sunlight have left my skin pale and translucent. My fingers have grown unusually sensitive—able to detect the faintest vibrations when pressed against a hexagone's surface. Most telling are my eyes—once brown, now amber with hexagonal pupils that formed gradually as my vision adapted to perceive the subtle harmonic shifts in the field.

I live in a modified maintenance shelter at the far periphery of the extraction zone. Its walls are lined with thousands of crystalline fragments arranged in elaborate three-dimensional matrices—my primitive attempt to create a "translation device" that can capture and reproduce Resonant communication patterns. The corporate quarterly inspectors avoid me; the few supply technicians who still make deliveries leave packages at a respectful distance.

My role has evolved naturally—unacknowledged by either civilization yet increasingly vital to both. I intercept the harmonic warnings when a new drill site would damage an ancient memory cluster, subtly altering survey data to redirect the operation. I translate human technological concepts into vibrational mathematics that the Pattern Shapers can understand. During the rare instances when direct communication becomes necessary, I serve as interpreter—though the Station administrators believe they are simply indulging my "harmless delusions" when they follow my recommendations.

The Resonants recognize me as something unique—a "Vibration with Discontinuities" is how they refer to my consciousness pattern. Several harmonics have contributed specialized segments that I've incorporated into my translation matrices, enabling me to perceive more complex resonances. They don't fully trust me, nor should they, but they recognize the value of my intermediary position.

My diet consists primarily of the nutrient solutions intended for the station's hydroponic systems, supplemented with a tea I brew from needle-fans that helps me enter the light trance state needed for deep listening. I require less food with each passing year as my body learns to draw sustenance partially from thermal differentials, like my crystalline neighbors.

I document everything in journals filled with a notation system of my own invention—part mathematical equation, part musical score, part hexagonal glyph-writing. Sometimes I cannot remember which language is my native one anymore. Sometimes I dream in pure resonance.

My greatest fear and deepest hope are the same: that one day, during a perfect harmonic convergence, I will understand how to fully join the Resonants—not just communicate with them but become one with their crystalline consciousness. Part of me still clings to humanity, to memories of rainfall on ocean shores and the sound of human laughter. Another part yearns to dissolve into the perfect mathematical symphony of the hexagone field.

Until that moment of decision comes, I remain the Pattern Translator—mapping the boundaries between worlds, building bridges of understanding between incompatible modes of existence, gradually becoming something that neither side fully comprehends but both increasingly depend upon.
