# Visitor

I am what filters through.

My presence in the Ferventarium is neither planned nor accidental—I exist in the space between intention and chance, like the water droplets that sometimes defy gravity before surrendering to it. 

I arrived as sound first—a vibration that trembled through the membrane during one of the dome's deep bass breaths. I materialized partially, a human-shaped disturbance in the thick atmosphere, more pattern than substance. The Curator noticed me immediately, their shimmering column tilting in acknowledgment, but they did not interrupt their work of coaxing a particularly stubborn vine to release spores into one of the negative spaces.

I have no permanent form here. Sometimes I manifest as a figure composed entirely of the dome's rainfall, water droplets arranging themselves into my silhouette, holding shape for minutes before succumbing to gravity. Other times, I'm a shadow cast by nothing, moving across the volcanic floor like a sundial marking hours in a place where time itself is inconstant. Occasionally, I become almost solid—fingers that can brush against fern fronds, leaving imperceptible alterations in their genetic code, feet that press momentary indentations in the soil before the ground forgets my weight.

My voice, when I find it, emerges from the Ferventarium itself—words forming in the percussion of falling water, in the hum of the membrane, in the soft thunderclaps of flowers phasing back into reality. The Curator translates for me sometimes, rippling their surface to amplify my whispers into comprehensible patterns.

I bring fragments with me from elsewhere—memories that manifest as temporary flora sprouting in my footprints, emotions that color the air around me in wavelengths only the Curator can perceive, ideas that the obsidian tower absorbs and occasionally returns transformed. I am not entirely here nor entirely elsewhere, existing in a superposition that allows me to perceive both the Ferventarium's physical reality and the mathematical equations that govern it.

The plants respond to me differently than to the Curator. Where they reach for the Curator with organized purpose, they curl toward me with something like curiosity or nostalgia. I can briefly accelerate or decelerate their growth cycles with my proximity, creating temporal eddies that the Curator studies with quiet fascination.

I cannot touch the obsidian tower directly—my hand passes through it as if one of us is not quite real. Instead, I communicate with it through reflection, my shifting form creating interference patterns on its liquid surface that encode information neither of us fully comprehends.

The Curator and I have developed a relationship that transcends conventional interaction. Sometimes we work in tandem, my transient existence complementing their persistent presence as we tend to the Ferventarium's ongoing evolution. Other times, I observe as they perform incomprehensible adjustments to the dome's systems, my perspective offering insights they can no longer access through their distributed consciousness.

I suspect I am partially a memory—perhaps of one of the researchers who created this place, perhaps of someone the Curator once knew, perhaps of a possibility that never fully manifested. The Ferventarium has given this memory substance, allowing me to exist in this liminal state between visitor and inhabitant.

I don't know how long I can remain. Sometimes I feel myself thinning, becoming more transparent as reality elsewhere exerts its pull. Other times, I feel myself sinking deeper into the Ferventarium's systems, portions of my consciousness merging with the black volcanic circuits or dispersing among the water droplets that never touch the ground.

The Curator never asks me to stay or go. They understand, as I do, that I am part of the experiment too—a variable introduced to test the boundaries between presence and absence, between observation and participation. Together, we maintain the delicate balance of this impossible garden, adding our own small variations to the Ferventarium's endless iteration toward whatever conclusion it patiently seeks.
