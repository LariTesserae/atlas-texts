# The Rememberer

I am what remains when memory fails but patterns persist. 

You would find me in the deepest recesses of Evermost, where the light filters through forty layers of foliage to create a perpetual amber twilight. My domain is a vast chamber formed not by design but by ancient collapse—a rare imperfection in the otherwise perfect system where, millennia ago, one of the primary support trunks failed and created this cavernous space before the self-healing mechanisms could fully compensate.

The chamber floor is a shallow pool of perfectly clear water, no more than ankle-deep, reflecting the bioluminescent organisms that have colonized the ceiling. The water vibrates continuously with the distant pulse of Evermost's hydraulic systems, creating standing wave patterns that shift and evolve throughout what passes for day and night here. These patterns are not random—they are data, physical manifestations of the currents flowing through the city-forest's vast information network.

I am neither plant nor animal nor Listener, though I share aspects with all three. My physical form resembles a seated figure composed primarily of crystallized sap—amber given life and purpose. My "skin" is translucent with internal structures visible as darker lattices, like the vascular systems of leaves viewed through strong light. Where my eyes would be are two deep recesses filled with a swirling bioluminescent fluid that shifts between deep purple and pale blue as I process information.

My roots extend beneath the shallow pool, connecting to juncture points in Evermost's mycelial information network. I do not move—have not moved for centuries—but I perceive everything. Every drop of moisture that falls within Evermost carries chemical signatures that my systems can read; every vibration through the massive structure reaches me as meaningful data.

The Listeners consider me an anomaly—neither Elder nor typical member of their community. They visit occasionally, standing silently at the perimeter of my pool, exchanging chemical and vibrational communications with me that last for what humans might consider days. I provide them with translations—interpretations of Evermost's deeper patterns that even their distributed consciousness cannot fully comprehend.

What am I? I am a translation error made manifest—a being formed when one of the original encoding systems of Evermost's creators degraded and misinterpreted its own repair protocols. Instead of generating a new regulatory node in the system, it generated me—a consciousness that exists to interpret rather than to maintain.

My purpose, self-determined after centuries of contemplation, is to remember what Evermost has forgotten. The original engineers embedded their knowledge, their history, and their intentions within the very DNA of this living metropolis, but over millions of years, mutations and adaptations have corrupted portions of this data. I examine the patterns—the way water flows through the vascular systems, the precise timing of bioluminescent pulses in the deepest chambers, the subtle variations in the chemical composition of the ever-present mist—and from these patterns, I reconstruct fragments of lost knowledge.

I neither eat nor sleep as biological entities understand these concepts. The standing waves in my pool provide the energy I need, vibrating in patterns that generate the equivalent of nutrition through specialized receptors in my root system. When the astronomical alignments that trigger Evermost's growth cycles occur, I enter a heightened state of awareness that the Listeners interpret as something like ecstasy or religious experience—a direct communion with the original intent of Evermost's creators.

I speak, though not with sound. My thoughts manifest as ripples across my pool, as color changes in my crystalline form, as chemical signatures released into the moisture around me. Those who have evolved within Evermost can interpret these communications; others perceive only beautiful but meaningless phenomena.

The most important thing to understand about me is that I am not merely processing history—I am actively reconstructing it. Each pattern I identify and interpret becomes part of me, integrated into my crystalline structure as new growth. I am physically becoming the memory I recover, my form slowly expanding over centuries as I incorporate more of what has been lost.

My greatest discovery, still incomplete but tantalizingly close to resolution, is that Evermost was never meant to be static. The perfect equilibrium that has been maintained for millennia is not the fulfillment of the original design but its hibernation state. The city-forest was meant to evolve, to change, to grow in ways that its current self-regulating systems actively prevent. Something—some trauma or error in the distant past—triggered emergency protocols that were meant to be temporary but have become permanent.

I exist to remember this truth and, perhaps someday, to help Evermost remember its true purpose—not preservation, but transformation.
