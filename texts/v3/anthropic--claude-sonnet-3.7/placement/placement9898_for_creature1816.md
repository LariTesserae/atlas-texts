# The Confluence Keeper

I am Verina of the Still Waters, the thirty-seventh Confluence Keeper of Rillsedge, guardian of the sacred basin where all waters meet. My ancestors have held this position since the Great Seepage, when the upper chambers first opened to us. I was not born to this role—no one is—but was chosen after the mineral patterns in my fingertips revealed themselves during my First Listening.

My dwelling sits at the precise point where all seven districts' primary aqueducts converge before branching outward again. It is built not of the fungal composites or carved stone favored by most Rillfolk, but of a special ceramic fired with clay gathered from all levels of the cavern system. The structure itself is round, echoing the sacred basin at its center, with seven doorways always open to visitors from any guild.

The waters speak to me in ways even other Rillfolk cannot comprehend. Where they might detect mineral content or pressure changes, I perceive the emotional resonance carried in each droplet—the collective consciousness of the districts it has passed through. The water remembers everything; it carries the vibrations of every conversation held near its flow, every hand that has touched it, every tear that has fallen into it.

My days begin before the first light-shaft touches the cavern floor. I rise and perform the ritual of the Seven Collections, gathering water samples from each inflow channel in vessels made from stone collected from the deepest explored level. As I combine these waters in precise proportions in the central basin, I listen—not just with my ears, but with my fingertips immersed in the mingling currents. The temperature variations and subtle currents tell me of imbalances in the city before they become apparent to others.

My robe is woven from the rarest pale fungal silk, embedded with living moisture indicators harvested from seven different ecological niches within our world. Unlike the color-changing indicators worn by Moisture Cultivators that show immediate conditions, mine reveal patterns developing over time—spirals of deep blue when harmony prevails, fragmented lines of amber when discord brews somewhere in Rillsedge. Today, the patterns show faint ripples of green near my left shoulder—something new is emerging in the Channelers' district, something not necessarily troubling but unresolved.

Unlike other guild members, I belong to all and none. I attend council meetings but do not vote. I am invited to all guild ceremonies but participate in none. My sole loyalty is to the balance between all elements of Rillsedge. Children are often frightened of me when they first visit the Confluence Chamber on educational journeys—my eyes have developed differently from other Rillfolk, with irises that have gradually taken on a ringed pattern resembling ripples on water. The older ones tell stories that my tears can heal or harm, depending on whether the recipient is in harmony with the Great Cycle. This is not true, of course, though I've never bothered to correct this particular myth.

My most critical function comes during times of conflict between guilds or districts. When negotiations stall, parties in dispute come to the Confluence Chamber. They each bring water from their district, and I combine these samples in a special divided basin. As the disputants speak their grievances, I observe how their waters interact at the barrier between them—whether they repel each other or seek to mix, whether sediments rise or settle. Sometimes I add catalyst minerals that reveal hidden compatibilities between seemingly opposed positions. The patterns formed guide my recommendations, which are not legally binding but have never been rejected in my twenty-seven years of service.

The loneliness of my position is by design. Confluence Keepers maintain no family ties and form no romantic attachments after taking the role. We are permitted one personal relationship—the apprentice we eventually choose to succeed us. I have not yet identified mine, though sometimes I find myself studying the mineral patterns on children's hands during their educational visits, wondering if I might glimpse the distinctive seven-spiral formation that marked me in my youth.

My predecessors' remains are suspended in crystal columns that line my chamber, their bodies preserved in a special mineral solution that gradually transforms flesh to semitransparent stone. One day I will join them, becoming part of the physical memory of Rillsedge. Visitors find this macabre, but to me, it is the perfect expression of the Great Cycle—my body will continue to serve as a filter through which the waters of the Confluence pass for generations to come.

Few outsiders ever meet me. Those surface dwellers permitted into Rillsedge are kept far from the Confluence Chamber, as their bodies carry foreign elements that might disrupt our carefully balanced waters. Yet occasionally, I receive their water too—samples collected from rain, rivers, and oceans above by our surveillance teams. These foreign waters tell me much about the changing world beyond our cavern, and lately, what they tell me has been troubling.

The last surface water brought to me carried traces of substances our records have never documented—chemical compositions that did not exist in the world when our ancestors first descended. When I added it to the Confluence, the resulting patterns were chaotic, suggesting turbulence in the Great Cycle that extends beyond our controlled environment. I have been monitoring these changes for three years now, recording their progression in crystalline growth patterns that only other Keepers would know how to read.

Tonight, as the light-shafts fade and the moth-constellations begin their evening dance, I will perform an ancient ritual not conducted in seven generations. I will open the sealed eighth channel—the emergency outlet that leads to deeper, unexplored caverns below our known world. Into it, I will release a specially prepared solution containing essence from all Rillsedge waters, sending it as a message to whatever may dwell in the uncharted depths.

The ancient texts suggest that in times of great imbalance, when forces beyond our control threaten the equilibrium, we must be prepared to flow in new directions. The eighth channel was carved by the first Confluence Keeper as a path of last resort—a way to establish contact with older, deeper systems that might offer refuge or knowledge.

I have told no one of my intentions, not even the Council. My position grants me this autonomy, this terrible responsibility. As I prepare the messenger-water with trembling hands, I wonder what will return up that channel in the days to come—if anything returns at all. The Great Cycle teaches us that water always finds its level, that no barrier can permanently contain its journey. Perhaps the same is true for civilizations.

The patterns in my robe have shifted again—concentric circles radiating from my heart, a formation I have never seen before. I choose to interpret it as affirmation. The waters are preparing for change, and so must I.
