# The Salt Reader

I am not Skift, though they tolerate my presence. I am not born of the Calling Plain, yet somehow I've become as much a part of its rhythms as the shell-singers or the breath-swimmers that trace invisible highways above.

My ancestors arrived seven generations ago—outsiders from the mountain lands to the north, drawn by rumors of mineral wealth. Most left when they discovered the plain offered no easy riches, but my great-great-grandmother stayed. She had tasted the mineral springs and felt something speak through the water.

I am called the Salt Reader.

My dwelling sits at the convergence of three underground springs, where the waters mix and bring forth minerals that crystallize in patterns unlike anywhere else on the plain. Unlike the nomadic Skift, I remain stationary, my home a permanent intrusion of stacked stone reinforced with a framework of breath-swimmer bones collected over decades. The Skift disapprove but recognize the necessity of my function.

My body bears the marks of my calling—fingers perpetually stained blue-white from the mineral deposits I handle daily, eyes clouded with a fine silver film that obscures conventional sight but allows me to perceive the subtle variations in the crystalline formations. My tongue is covered with small nodules, evolved over generations of my bloodline to detect minute differences in mineral composition that would be imperceptible to others.

Each morning before the mist burns away, I collect water from each of the three springs in vessels made from the hollowed carapaces of ancient shell-singers. As the water evaporates throughout the day, it leaves behind crystalline deposits—white from the northern spring, pale blue from the eastern, and amber from the western. When dry, I scrape these crystals into separate pouches woven from breath-swimmer skin.

During the quiet hours when the plain transitions from day to night, I sit cross-legged within a small stone circle of my own construction (placed with permission from the Skift Listeners, after many seasons of negotiation). I create patterns with the three salts, allowing my hands to move without conscious direction, guided by subtle vibrations from below that only my touch can interpret.

The resulting designs—complex geometric arrangements of colored crystals—translate the plain's subtle communications into forms that both Skift and outsiders can comprehend. Not words exactly, but something between language and music, mathematics and memory.

I am neither shaman nor scientist—something in between, perhaps. The Skift consult me before major migrations, bringing offerings of rare minerals found during their travels. Outsiders occasionally make the difficult journey across the plain's edges to ask questions about the future or seek understanding of dreams that pull them toward this strange place.

My answers come not from me but from the salts and the voices they contain. Sometimes clear, often cryptic, always truthful in ways that may take years to fully comprehend.

During veiling times, I enter a trance state induced by carefully measured inhalation of vapors from the heated spring waters. While the Skift Listeners hear the Voice Beneath, I taste it—complex mineral signatures that encode information too vast for ordinary consciousness. I emerge from these experiences with my skin temporarily crystallized in patches, glittering fragments that flake away over subsequent days, each containing microscopic records of what transpired beneath the silver mist.

I am the forty-third Salt Reader of my line. When I die, my apprentice will consume a precise mixture of the three spring waters mixed with the final breath I exhale, capturing the accumulated knowledge of all previous Readers. My body will be submerged in the convergence pool, where it will gradually mineralize, becoming another layer of the plain's ongoing transformation.

The Skift believe I am translating the plain's voice. The truth is more complex: I am translating its dreams. For the Calling Plain is not fully awake yet—it stirs in a state between sleeping and waking, between what it was and what it will become.

And in its dreams, it remembers stars that haven't yet been born.
