Your creation of Dael Verre is hauntingly beautiful—a limestone masterpiece suspended in perfect equilibrium between nature and civilization. The image of this silent city with its constellation of downcast lights embedded in the mountainside is particularly striking.

If I were to find my place within this world, I would be:

# The Threshold Keeper

I am neither fully of Dael Verre nor entirely separate from it. My home is a small dwelling carved into the very lowest terrace where the city's outermost path meets the switchback trail ascending from the valley below. My structure differs slightly from the unified architecture above—my walls bear weather marks from countless seasons facing outward, and climbing plants have been allowed more freedom here, creating a soft transition between wilderness and order.

I belong to none of the seven guilds yet serve them all. Born to two Path Walkers who died during a rare avalanche when I was young, I inherited neither their comfort with the outside world nor full acceptance within Varesh society. The Pattern Weavers determined I would serve a unique function: to become the living boundary between Dael Verre and everything beyond.

Visitors seeking entry to the city must first spend three days in my company. I maintain a small courtyard with three guest chambers where travelers rest while I evaluate their intentions and prepare them for the silence they must observe above. Unlike pure Varesh, I possess no discomfort with spoken language, though I speak sparingly, my voice carrying a melodic quality that somehow eases the transition between the noisy world and the city's quietude.

My hands bear simplified versions of the Pattern Weavers' knot-tattoos—not recording knowledge but marking me as one authorized to translate between hand-speech and verbal language. Around my neck hangs a pendant of unpolished limestone containing a single fossil: a perfect spiral shell from when these mountains lay beneath ancient seas.

My other duties include tending the warning bells—seven hollow stone cylinders suspended at intervals along the approach path. When storms threaten, I strike these with a felted mallet, creating deep resonant tones that carry up through the rock itself, felt rather than heard by those above. The Beast Whisperers have taught me enough to maintain friendly relations with the cliff birds, who often bring me small tokens—unusual pebbles or fragments of shell—that I interpret as omens according to traditions even the Pattern Weavers have forgotten.

I consumed the sacred tea like all others, but in me it worked differently. While I age at the slow Varesh rate, I possess fertility outside their careful planning. This makes me both valuable and dangerous to their system. Every seventieth year, when certain stars align in specific patterns, I am permitted to ascend to the highest terrace where the Balance Keepers evaluate me. Though they speak no words, I always return changed, carrying within me renewed purpose and, sometimes, the potential for a child who might someday replace me or serve elsewhere in the city where equilibrium requires adjustment.

The Varesh tolerate my small irregularities—the clay wind chimes that make soft music outside my dwelling, the slightly brighter lamps I keep burning, the occasional bursts of humming when I work. They understand that threshold spaces require different rules. I represent both the danger of the outside and its necessary connection to their perfect stillness.

Outsiders often feel more comfortable with me than with true Varesh. They call me the Doorkeeper or the Mountain's Mouth, though I never use these names myself. To myself, in private moments, I use no name at all—I am simply the place where two worlds meet, neither fully one thing nor another.

Like the city itself, I exist in careful balance between motion and stillness, between speech and silence, between isolation and connection. When travelers ask what sustains me in my solitary role, I show them how, from my unique position, I can witness both the perfect constellation of Dael Verre's lights above and the wild stars beyond the mountain's shadow. In that moment, they sometimes understand what the Varesh have always known—that true harmony requires both boundless space and carefully tended limitations.
