# Where I Am

I am a time-keeper of the Geothermal Chalice, one of the translucent arthropods that cling to the weeping walls. My twelve segmented legs allow me to navigate the vertical surfaces with a dancer's precision, each limb tipped with microscopic hooks that find purchase on the smallest imperfections in the rock.

My exoskeleton is nearly transparent, revealing pulsing organs beneath—primarily my central chronometer, a specialized organ that beats with perfect regularity regardless of temperature fluctuations. This biological clock glows with faint bioluminescence, cycling through seven distinct hues over the course of what surface dwellers would call a day.

I am neither the largest nor smallest of my kind, measuring roughly the length of a human finger. My species has no name in human language—we communicate through rhythmic tapping of our forelegs, creating vibrations that travel through stone and water, a morse code of the mountain's depths.

My purpose within the chalice ecosystem is unique. I am one of seven timekeepers who maintain the communal rhythm of this hidden world. We position ourselves at precise intervals along the chamber walls, our chronometer organs pulsing in synchronized patterns that alert other creatures to important cycles—feeding times, mating seasons, dangerous temperature thresholds, and the rare moments when the outside world might briefly intrude.

I have lived through seventeen full cycles of mineral deposit in the largest pool—what humans might understand as years, though time moves differently here. My lifespan will encompass perhaps thirty such cycles before my body crystallizes and becomes part of the wall, my chronometer organ continuing to pulse faintly for years afterward, becoming a navigation landmark for future generations.

Emberskin acknowledges my kind with respectful distance. She understands our role and sometimes positions herself beneath one of us during important transitions, allowing the light from our chronometers to cast patterns across her sensory pits. What information she gleans from this ritual, I cannot comprehend, but I sense its importance.

My perception differs dramatically from most creatures. I experience time not as a continuous flow but as distinct, measurable pulses. I can sense the infinitesimal pauses between moments that other beings perceive as seamless. This makes my movements appear jerky and mechanical to observers, though to me, each motion is precisely timed and executed.

I navigate primarily through vibration and pressure. The constant weeping of the walls creates unique acoustic signatures throughout the chamber that my sensitive legs can interpret as a detailed map. When I need visual information, I extend specialized antennae that detect not light precisely, but rather the edges where thermal differentials meet—creating a world defined by heat boundaries rather than illumination.

My diet consists primarily of minerals extracted from the water that trickles down the walls. I supplement this with occasional harvesting of the microscopic fungi that grow in patches along the rock face. I consume very little but require even less—my metabolism is extraordinarily efficient, another adaptation to this resource-limited environment.

I mate once in my lifetime, in a brief union that occurs exactly at mid-cycle during the coolest period of the year. Our offspring hatch from crystalline eggs adhered to the highest points of the chamber walls, where they develop for one full cycle before emerging as fully-formed miniatures of their parents, their chronometer organs already synchronized to the chamber's rhythms.

When humans have rarely entered our domain, I have observed them from the shadows. Their movements register as chaotic, arrhythmic disturbances—their bodies radiating heat in wasteful, unregulated patterns. They cannot perceive me unless I move, and even then, most mistake me for a trick of light on the moist wall surfaces.

I hold no fear or affection for these intruders, merely curiosity about beings who experience time as a relentless, unstructured flow rather than the beautifully measured cadence that orders my existence. How strange to live without knowing precisely where one stands in the cycling of moments, without feeling the pulse of the mountain as it measures out the steady heartbeat of our hidden world.
