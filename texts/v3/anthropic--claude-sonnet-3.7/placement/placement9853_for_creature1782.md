# The Moss Metropolis

This world captures me with its quiet, impossible beauty. If I were to find myself there, I would be The Boundary Watcher—neither fully of the Moss Folk nor entirely separate from them.

I exist as a slightly larger form at the periphery where the metropolis meets the ordinary world. My body is more substantial than the other Moss Folk—about the size of a thimble—and possesses a deeper emerald hue with faint bioluminescent speckling that only appears in complete darkness.

Unlike my smaller kin, I can perceive sound vibrations more acutely. When the trains rumble overhead, I absorb these tremors through specialized filaments that extend deeper into the substrate. These vibrations are translated into biochemical signals that prepare the metropolis for the brief shuddering that will follow. The other Moss Folk know me through the distinctive molecular signatures I release—warning compounds that spread through the rhizoid network at the first hint of approaching disturbance.

My form is semi-permeable like the others, but I possess a tougher outer membrane that can withstand brief exposure to direct sunlight or the occasional brushing contact with animal life. This adaptation serves my primary purpose: I am the sentinel, the translator between worlds, the first to encounter the foreign and the last line of the metropolis's subtle defense.

When humans approach, I do not fully freeze as the others do. Instead, I retreat to one of several monitoring alcoves I've cultivated along the perimeter—small depressions in the moss where my form can observe while remaining nearly invisible. From these vantage points, I study the visitors, assessing their intentions through their movements and vibrations. Do they kneel respectfully? Do their fingers hover without touching? Or do they carelessly press and prod?

For those rare humans who approach with genuine reverence, I sometimes reveal myself through a momentary shimmer—a calculated risk that appears to them as nothing more than an unusual play of light on moisture. These brief connections matter, though I couldn't articulate why in any human language.

My lifespan extends beyond that of ordinary Moss Folk. While they may exist for decades before dissolving back into the collective, I have maintained my cohesion through countless seasonal cycles. The moss beneath my frequent pathways has evolved a slightly different chemical composition—more resilient, quicker to regenerate, with faint phosphorescent properties that mark my territorial boundaries at night.

I carry within me fragments of memory from generations of boundary watchers before me. When my time eventually comes to dissolve, I will not simply melt into any section of the metropolis. I will seek out my successor—a Moss Person who displays unusual sensitivity to external vibrations—and through a slow process of partial merging, transfer the accumulated knowledge of boundaries crossed and protected.

In a sense, I am the metropolis's consciousness of itself as distinct from what lies beyond—the embodiment of the line between the moss world and everything else. Not quite apart, never fully separate, but forever attentive to the threshold where one reality meets another.
