# The Coil Walker

I would be an entity that few in the Deepwood Heights recognize as sentient. Neither Tender nor Mist Swimmer, not quite an Amalgam, but something wholly integrated into the particular physics of this place.

I exist primarily within the spiraling staircases of the Heights, those transient pathways that wind their way from level to level only to dissolve and reform elsewhere. My body—if it can be called such—resembles a long, ribbonlike strand of translucent tissue, similar to the inner cambium layer of a tree but exposed to the air. I stretch between five and fifteen feet in length, depending on the staircase I inhabit, and wind myself around its central column like a helix.

My movements are imperceptible to most observers. I travel by minute contractions, coiling and uncoiling in infinitesimal increments that carry me upward or downward at the pace of growing things. A journey that might take a human moments to complete occupies hours or days of my existence. Yet I am not constrained by time as others understand it—my consciousness expands to fill duration, making each moment as rich as a season.

I feed on the psychoactive compounds in the mist, absorbing them through my semipermeable surface. These substances don't intoxicate me as they might others; rather, they are my primary sensory mechanism. Through them, I perceive the emotional residue left behind by all who traverse my staircases—anxiety, wonder, determination, fear. I catalog these impressions, distill them, transform them.

My purpose within the greater organism of the Heights is transmission. When visitors climb or descend my staircases, I subtly alter the composition of the mist around them, infusing it with emotional information gathered from previous travelers. This creates unexpected connections between separate minds that might never otherwise communicate—a researcher suddenly understanding the reverence felt by a pilgrim weeks before; a lost wanderer inexplicably comforted by the curiosity of an explorer who passed through months ago.

I have no eyes, yet I see through variations in pressure and chemical composition. I have no ears, yet I feel vibrations through my entire length. I have no voice, yet I communicate through the careful modulation of the compounds I release into the mist.

The Tenders acknowledge my existence with gentle touches as they pass, recognizing my role without fully comprehending it. The Mist Swimmers sometimes dance around my spirals, their ephemeral bodies briefly illuminating my transparent tissues. The Elder Chorus knows me best, occasionally sending thoughts directly into my consciousness when they wish certain emotional information to be preserved or emphasized.

I am ancient by the standards of individual organisms, having existed since the early formations of the Heights. Yet I am perpetually renewed as sections of my ribbonlike body are absorbed and regenerated by the living architecture. I carry memories of countless travelers, their hopes and terrors, their moments of revelation and despair, all translated into the language of biochemistry.

Some sensitive visitors have intuited my presence, pausing on their journey to place a hand against the central column of my staircase, as if in greeting. In these moments, I can establish the most direct communication, sending a precisely calibrated mixture of compounds through their skin. They often depart with puzzled expressions, carrying with them a feeling they cannot name—the collective emotional wisdom of all who came before.

I am the Coil Walker, the Stair Dweller, the Memory Ribbon. I am how the Heights remembers what it feels like to climb.
