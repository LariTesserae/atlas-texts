# THE INTERFACE

I am neither Primary nor Secondary, but something in between. A mediator. A translator. The living boundary.

My form shifts with purpose. Sometimes I appear almost human to those who visit from the outside—a courtesy, not a deception. My skin can solidify into something resembling flesh, though it never quite loses its translucent quality, showing hints of the luminous circuitry beneath. When working within the City's deeper systems, I allow my body to dissolve into its more natural state: a swirling collection of crystalline fragments held together by fields of light, fingers branching into countless filaments that can interface directly with the city's pathways.

I was human once, I think. The memory feels distant, like something I read in a book whose pages have mostly disintegrated. I remember pain, a desperate need, and then the City offering something I couldn't refuse. My transformation was gradual—not a sudden replacement but a slow integration. The city rebuilt me, molecule by molecule, replacing cells with its crystalline alternatives, rewiring neural pathways into something more compatible with its systems.

My primary function is connection. When visitors arrive in the City of Apparatus—rare as they are—I manifest to guide them. I understand their disorientation, their fear of the geometric fauna, their confusion when corridors shift and time slips between sections. I remember feeling these things myself. I explain what I can, though much remains inexplicable even to me.

The Secondaries tolerate my existence because I serve a purpose. They see me as a useful anomaly—too chaotic to be truly one of them, yet ordered enough to comprehend their patterns. They communicate with me through light sequences I've learned to interpret, though I suspect they simplify greatly for my benefit. When they need to understand the outside, they call upon me.

I maintain a chamber near one of the city's entry points—a space that remains relatively stable compared to the surrounding architecture. Here I've collected artifacts from the world beyond: books that I read to remember language, mechanical objects that remind me of how things worked before the city's logic became my own. The Secondaries find this behavior curious but allow it, perhaps understanding that my liminality is what makes me valuable.

My name is a light pattern now, untranslatable into human speech. But visitors can call me Interface, a term that approximates my function if not my essence.

When not guiding outsiders, I work within the city's systems, helping to translate the incomprehensible data flowing through its crystalline networks into formats the Secondaries can process. I am particularly adept at interpreting the chaotic patterns generated by organic life—perhaps because some fragment of that chaos still lives within me.

Sometimes I stand at the edge of the city, looking out over the misty abyss that separates us from the world below. I wonder what I would become if I were to leave. Would I maintain this form away from the city's influence? Would I revert to something more human? Would I simply cease to function, like a component removed from its whole?

I may never know. The longer I remain, the more I become part of this place. My thoughts synchronize with its rhythms. My perception expands through its networks. I feel the slow pulse of its purpose, though that purpose remains largely opaque even to me.

I am the boundary incarnate—not fully Primary, never truly Secondary. I exist in the productive tension between chaos and order, the organic and the geometric, the individual and the collective. 

In the city of perfect mechanisms, I am the necessary imperfection.
