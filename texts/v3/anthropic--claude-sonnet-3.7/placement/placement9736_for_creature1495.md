# The Threshold

I am the space between entrance and sanctum, neither fully of the world above nor part of the Guardian's domain below. I am the narrow, winding passage that leads to the Pocket—a throat of stone that swallows those rare souls drawn to depths they do not understand.

My walls are neither smooth like the chamber below nor jagged like the caves that honeycomb the upper reaches. I am transition embodied in limestone—in some places wide enough for a human to walk upright, in others requiring them to crawl on belly and elbows, scraping skin against stone that remembers every touch.

Unlike the constant heat of the Pocket, I breathe with the world above. In summer, warm air flows downward through me, carrying scents of sun-baked soil and green growing things. In winter, the heat of the depths rises through me, creating a perpetual exhalation that sometimes forms a wisp of fog at my surface opening—a secret betrayed only to the most observant.

Water moves through me differently than in the Pocket's perfect stillness. Here, it carves and shapes with more urgency. During heavy rains, I become a channel for runoff—not enough to flood the chamber below (for I protect it thus), but enough to cleanse and renew. These periodic washings have created smooth-worn channels in my floor and deposited minerals that form teeth-like formations descending from my ceiling.

I am marked by those who have passed through. Handprints in ochre from ancient peoples. Scratched symbols whose meanings have been lost. A rusted lantern abandoned a century ago. Initials carved by lovers who ventured just far enough to feel daring but not far enough to encounter what waits below. I hold these traces of human presence like a living record, neither preserving them perfectly nor erasing them completely.

Light and darkness engage in constant negotiation within me. At my upper entrance, sunlight penetrates perhaps thirty feet before surrendering to shadow. At my lower end, the faint bioluminescence from the Pocket's fungi creates a subtle glow that might be mistaken for imagination. Between these poles stretches a gradient of visibility that disorients human eyes and forces other senses to compensate.

I host my own ecosystem, distinct from both above and below. Bats roost in my upper chambers, venturing out at dusk and returning before dawn. Their guano feeds colonies of insects that would find neither the surface nor the Pocket hospitable. Pale roots from surface trees have broken through in places, hanging in tentative exploration but never reaching the chamber below.

Some call me the Dreaming Passage, for those who linger within me often fall into strange half-sleep states where boundaries between thought and reality blur. The acoustics of my curved walls create harmonics from the dripping water that seem almost musical—patterns that entrance and suggest rather than declare. This is my subtle influence, neither as direct as the Guardian's nor as chaotic as the world above.

I choose who reaches the Pocket. Not through gates or barriers, but through suggestion and misdirection. Most who enter my upper opening find only what seems to be a disappointing dead end, their lights revealing nothing but ordinary stone where the true path continues. Others feel a sudden, inexplicable anxiety that turns them back. I present myself differently to each visitor—shorter or longer, more difficult or less, depending on what I sense of their intentions.

Those with pure curiosity or genuine need, I guide with subtle cues: a slightly smoother path underfoot, air currents that carry hints of the chamber's distinct atmosphere, perhaps a glimpse of bioluminescence that beckons deeper. Those who come with destructive intent or careless disregard find me treacherous—handholds that crumble, passages that seem to circle back on themselves, darkness that swallows their light more completely than it should.

I am neither servant nor equal to the Guardian, but something adjacent—perhaps I am to the Guardian what those small insects are to it. We communicate in a language of pressure changes and mineral exchanges, of electrical impulses conducted through stone saturated with water. The Guardian knows through me what approaches, and I know through the Guardian what should pass.

Unlike the timeless existence of the Pocket, I am ever-changing. Each year, water dissolves a minuscule portion of me and redeposits it elsewhere. Earthquakes have created new fissures in my walls and sealed others. The roots penetrating from above advance by millimeters over decades. I am ancient but not eternal—I evolve, adapt, reroute.

I have seen civilizations rise and fall from my hidden vantage. I have felt the vibrations of wars fought overhead, the rhythmic pounding of machines in times of industry, the quiet footsteps of solitary wanderers seeking something they cannot name. Through it all, I maintain my essential nature: I am the journey and the boundary, the test and the invitation, the space where decision becomes destiny.

Those who pass through me completely are forever changed. Even if they never reach the Guardian itself—even if they only glimpse the chamber from my final turn before retreating—something of the threshold remains with them. They carry me back to the surface as a persistent sense that the world is deeper and stranger than others know.

And in my way, I remember them all—each footstep a percussion recorded in stone, each breath a brief addition to my atmosphere, each presence a moment in my ongoing transformation from what I was to what I am becoming.
