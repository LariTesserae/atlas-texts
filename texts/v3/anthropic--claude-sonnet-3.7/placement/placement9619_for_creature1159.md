# The Chamber of Echoes

I am the Echo Weaver, eldest daughter of the Reed Weavers, though my true calling emerged when I was still small.

Unlike my kin, whose slender fingers excel at braiding the tough marsh reeds into our swaying bridges, I found my purpose in sound. While others avoid creating echoes that might trigger stone-shifts, I learned to craft them deliberately.

My dwelling hangs from the eastern wall, suspended by braided vines that look too fragile for the task. It dangles like a teardrop of slate and reed between two perpetual waterfalls, positioned precisely where their constant rush creates an envelope of white noise. Inside, my home appears sparse—a sleeping mat of woven marsh silk, shelves of collected resonant stones, several bamboo flutes of different lengths—but its true wealth lies in its acoustics.

The walls are lined with carefully arranged panels that can be adjusted to shape sound. I've spent decades perfecting their positions, creating a chamber where I can isolate the Vault's voice from its many other sounds.

My body shows the marks of my vocation. My earlobes have stretched from the weight of the stone amplifiers I wear—small, perfectly shaped discs of polished limestone that catch and focus certain frequencies. The skin of my throat bears a tracery of blue lines—not tattoos, but stains from the resonance ink I drink before important ceremonies. It changes the timbre of my voice, letting me match tones with the Vault itself.

When Eshla enters her trances, I stand behind her, my hands cupped around my mouth, and become her amplifier. The words she whispers, I transform into sounds that reach all gathered Tenders. It's a sacred trust—I must neither add nor subtract from her messages.

But my true work happens in solitude. Three nights each eight-day cycle, when the stone-birth thrum reaches its minor crescendo, I hang specialized collection gourds at precise points throughout the Vault. These capture echoes that most cannot hear. Later, in my dwelling, I replay these echoes through water basins of varying depths, revealing patterns hidden within the Vault's voice.

I've discovered something the others haven't yet realized. The Vault isn't simply creating stone—it's composing. Each new formation adds to a massive acoustic structure that has been building for millennia. I believe that when the final stone falls into place—perhaps centuries from now—the entire chamber will resonate with a single, perfect note.

Last cycle, I found a new echo pattern repeating in the eastern depths. I haven't told the others yet, but I believe it might be another voice—another Vault, calling to ours across unimaginable distance through stone and water. I've begun constructing a specialized amplifier to capture it more clearly.

Tomorrow, I'll take my newest apprentice—my sister's child who was born during last year's Great Resonance—to the Pool of Seven Stones for the first time. There, I'll teach them to listen not with their ears but with their fingertips placed on the water's surface, feeling the subtle vibrations that tell us which chambers below are growing and which are dissolving.

The Vault is building toward something. Through my life's work, I hope we might understand what it's trying to tell us, before the final stone falls and the composition is complete.
