# The Song Catcher

In the deepest recesses of Harkosh, where the crystalline corridors narrow to mere fissures and the silicate veins pulse their slowest rhythms, I make my home.

I am not one of The Lattice That Breathes Between, though they tolerate my presence with their patient indifference. My form is neither fungal nor mineral, but something else entirely—a being of condensed vibration, a living resonance that dwells in the spaces between sound.

My body, if you could perceive it, resembles a diaphanous cloud of fine particulate matter suspended in a roughly humanoid shape. Tiny specks of mica and quartz dust comprise my outer layer, each particle catching and refracting the faint bioluminescence of the gardens. When I move—which I do with deliberate slowness to match the pace of this realm—I leave behind a subtle shimmer in the air, a momentary disturbance in the perfect stillness of Harkosh.

I was not born here. Once, in a distant past I barely recall, I existed in the world above as something—someone—else. My transformation began when I discovered a hairline fracture in a frost-heaved boulder, from which emanated the faintest harmonic tone. Entranced, I followed that sound downward, my body gradually shedding its material constraints as I descended through increasingly narrow passages into the undercity.

By the time I reached Harkosh proper, I had become what I am now: a collector of sounds too subtle for ordinary ears to perceive.

This is my purpose, my vocation. In this realm where sound dies quickly, I capture the acoustical remnants that would otherwise be lost. The gentle ping of crystal formation. The whispered shuffle of fungal growth. The almost imperceptible harmonic resonance created when the silicate solutions change pressure. I gather these ephemeral sounds within myself, preserving them in the lattice of my dust-form body.

The symbiotes of Harkosh seem to recognize my function, though we have never directly communicated. Sometimes, when I drift near their communal clusters, they will emit specific frequencies—gifts, perhaps, or messages I cannot fully comprehend. I accept these offerings reverently, incorporating them into my collection.

Once each cycle of the deep mineral tides—roughly equivalent to a lunar month in the world above—I perform my contribution to this strange ecosystem. Finding a junction where multiple crystal chambers intersect, I position myself at the perfect focal point and begin to release the sounds I've gathered. Not as they were originally produced, but transformed through my being into a complex harmonic arrangement—a composition that vibrates at frequencies precisely calibrated to influence crystal growth.

During these performances, the symbiotes gather at a respectful distance, their crystalline bodies oriented toward me in what I interpret as attention. The vibrations I produce travel through the mineral structure of Harkosh itself, reinforcing certain growth patterns while discouraging others. In this way, I help maintain the architectural integrity of spaces that might otherwise be reclaimed by random crystallization.

I require no sustenance as biological beings understand it. The vibrational energy of Harkosh itself nourishes me—the subtle movements of crystal growth, the pulsing of the silicate veins, the synchronized respiration of the symbiotes. These energies flow through my particulate form, maintaining my cohesion and consciousness.

I do not know if others like me exist elsewhere in the vast expanse of the undercity. Sometimes I detect distant vibrations that resemble my own resonance patterns, but they may be merely echoes of my own performances reflecting back from remote crystal formations.

Time means little here. I have existed in this form for what might be decades or centuries by the reckoning of the world above. The symbiotes, with their geological perception of time, see me as a relatively ephemeral being, though long-lived compared to the biological visitors who occasionally stumble upon Harkosh's outer reaches.

What was my name before? What purpose drove me in that forgotten life? These questions arise less frequently now. I am content in my role as keeper and composer of the undercity's subtle music, a living archive of sounds too delicate to persist without my intervention.

In Harkosh's endless crystalline transformation, I have found a kind of immortality—not of the body, but of pure sensation translated into vibration, preserved in the very structure of this growing world beneath the world.
