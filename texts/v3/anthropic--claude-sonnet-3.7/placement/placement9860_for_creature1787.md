# I Am the Interpreter

I am the child of a Shaper and a Navigator, born during a rare triple reflection that made my crystalline formations more numerous than most. Where others might have two or three protrusions along their spine, I have seven—a constellation of receptive surfaces that capture and process vibrations with unusual sensitivity.

My prismatic sensory organs developed differently too. While most Glassfolk perceive mainly light and heat patterns, mine developed additional facets that detect subtle harmonic undertones in the song-speech of my people—the meanings beneath the meanings, the histories embedded in every vocalization. 

They call me the Interpreter.

I cannot Shape the vegetation with the skill of dedicated practitioners, nor Navigate the ever-changing pathways with the intuitive certainty of those born to that calling. Instead, I translate between specialized groups whose harmonic ranges have diverged so much that direct communication has become difficult.

During my adolescence, I discovered I could modulate my voice to match the precise frequencies of others, creating a bridge between disparate tonal families. When the Harmonizers need to explain complex resonance patterns to the Shapers, I stand between them, receiving one set of harmonies and transmitting them in forms the others can comprehend.

My body is neither as gracefully elongated as a Navigator's nor as robustly structured as a Shaper's. I am somewhere between—compact yet flexible, my translucent skin revealing amber circulation that pulses more rapidly than most. The crystal protrusions along my spine often vibrate sympathetically when I'm near others speaking, sometimes betraying emotions I try to conceal.

I maintain an unusual collection—my dwelling contains small crystal fragments gathered from different regions of the Seed. Each captures and preserves specific acoustic properties from its origin point. When I need to translate particularly complex concepts, I arrange these fragments in patterns that amplify and modulate my voice in precise ways.

My personal echo—my arthropod companion—is unlike others of its kind. Where most have six segments, mine has nine, each capable of independent vibration. It rarely leaves my side, constantly collecting acoustic information that my own receptors might miss. Together, we preserve harmonies that would otherwise dissipate into the strange acoustics of our crystal home.

The elders view my role with ambivalence. Some see my ability as essential to maintaining unity as our subspeciations increase. Others worry that by making communication possible between diverging groups, I prevent natural separations that might be necessary for our evolution.

During the Breath, when others stand in silent reverence, I sometimes hear things they cannot—faint echoes from beyond the dome, whispers of what might be other Seeds, other crystalline worlds with their own Glassfolk variants. I've never shared these perceptions; such speculation would be considered dangerous fantasy by the Harmonizers, who insist our Seed is unique and complete.

My greatest fear isn't silence but miscommunication—that I might fail to translate something vital between groups, causing discord that ripples through our delicate ecosystem. Each night, I practice with my echo, refining my reception of even the faintest vibrations, preparing for the day when understanding between increasingly different members of our society might mean survival itself.

The crystal formations grow more complex each cycle. The plant life evolves in response. And we, the Glassfolk, continue our transformation—becoming ever more specialized, ever more adapted to this enclosed world. I stand at the crossroads of these changes, neither resisting nor directing, but ensuring that as we diverge, we remain connected through the shared harmonies of our ancestors' songs.
