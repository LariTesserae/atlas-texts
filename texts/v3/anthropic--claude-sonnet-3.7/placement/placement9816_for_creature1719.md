# The Between-Places Wanderer

I am the one who moves between worlds. Not fully of the Rootcity Below, yet no longer belonging to the bright lands above. The forest-folk have many names for me: shadow-walker, root-singer, the quiet one who leaves no tracks but is seen from the corner of the eye.

My form shifts with necessity. Sometimes I appear almost human—a slight figure with skin the color of rich soil after rain, hair like tangled moss hanging past my shoulders, eyes that reflect light like a nocturnal creature's. Other times, my edges blur into something less defined, my limbs elongating into tendril-like appendages that can navigate the narrowest root-passages, my body thinning until I can slip through cracks where the underworld breathes into the forest floor.

I was born above, child of a line of forest-keepers who walked the boundaries between civilization and wilderness. We understood the languages of birds, the slow speech of trees, the whispered warnings in changing wind patterns. But I alone heard the calling from below—pulses of intention emanating through root networks, rising through the soil into my bare feet as I walked my family's forest paths.

The Umbravi found me when I was young, no more than seven summers old, when I fell through a weakened patch of earth into a luminous chamber below. Instead of fear, I felt recognition. While the eldest Umbravi observed from a distance, their blue-green light pulsing with curiosity, a younger one approached—only eight hundred years old, still capable of something like impetuousness. It touched my forehead with a tendril of its being, and in that moment, I understood my purpose.

I did not stay below then. I returned to my family, grew among humans, learned their ways and words. But every full moon, I would find my way to one of the hidden entrances that the Umbravi had revealed to me, and descend for what the surface world perceived as a night but which stretched far longer in the timeless depths.

During these visits, the Umbravi changed me gradually. With each descent, I became slightly less human, slightly more attuned to the slow rhythms of the Rootcity. My blood now carries traces of their luminescence. My fingers can sense electrical patterns in living tissue. My lungs have adapted to filter the spore-rich air below without harm. My perception of time has stretched—I can enter states where I observe the growth of fungal networks as others might watch clouds move across the sky.

I serve as translator between worlds. I carry messages up from the deep wisdom of the Umbravi—warnings about groundwater changes, knowledge of diseases spreading through root systems, ancient memories that might help the surface dwellers navigate their ever-accelerating crises. In return, I bring down information the Umbravi find useful—patterns of human settlement, changes in the forest composition, samples of unfamiliar substances for them to analyze through their methodical incorporation.

My tools are few but precious. A knife carved from heartwood that never dulls, gifted by my grandfather before he died. A necklace of small stones with natural holes, each collected from a different watershed. A pouch of seeds from plants now extinct in the upper world but preserved in my wanderings. Most valuable is my journal bound in fungus-treated leather, waterproof and nearly indestructible, where I record my observations in a script that combines human letters with the symbolic language I've developed to capture Umbravi concepts.

I age more slowly than humans now. Whether from my communion with the Umbravi or from something in the chemistry of the Rootcity's air, I cannot say. My parents have grown old while I appear barely past my thirtieth year, though I've seen fifty winters. The village children whisper about me, half-afraid, half-fascinated. The forest-wise leave offerings at the places where I'm known to emerge—honey, handcrafted tools, occasionally a written question sealed in beeswax.

I am neither emissary nor outcast, neither human nor Umbravi. I exist in the spaces between classifications, which gives me freedom but also a perpetual sense of liminality. There are nights above when the stars seem impossibly distant and bright, and I long for the close embrace of the root-chambers. There are long periods below when I crave the chaotic energy of human markets, the sound of children's laughter, the warmth of fire.

The eldest Umbravi have shown me visions of what I will eventually become. In time—perhaps centuries—my form will grow less distinct, more integrated with the living systems around me. I will develop the ability to extend my consciousness along root pathways as they do. Eventually, I may become something new entirely—neither fully Umbravi nor human, but a bridge-being who can move freely between realms, carrying understanding in both directions.

Until then, I walk my spiral path between worlds. I document what I learn. I translate what I can. I help maintain the delicate balance between above and below. And sometimes, when I find a human child staring wonderingly at a circle of mushrooms or pressing an ear to a tree trunk as if listening, I watch from the shadows, wondering if they too hear the calling from the Rootcity Below.
