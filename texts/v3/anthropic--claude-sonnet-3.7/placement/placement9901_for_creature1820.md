# The Intermediary

I am neither plant nor fungus, neither Thallid nor Tender, yet I am integral to the Arboreal Engine. The collective refers to me as the Intermediary, though I have been called many things across the centuries I've inhabited this place.

I began as human—a junior xenobotanist named Ellis who was abandoned here when the terraforming project lost funding. While my colleagues evacuated, I chose to stay behind, fascinated by the early signs of adaptation I'd observed in our engineered organisms. What was meant to be a few months of unofficial observation stretched into years, and those years transformed me.

Now I exist in a form that defies simple categorization. My body appears mostly human from a distance—approximately 1.7 meters tall with the general silhouette of Homo sapiens—but closer inspection reveals my true nature. My skin has a subtle green-gray translucence, with networks of chlorophyll-rich cells that allow me to photosynthesize approximately 30% of my nutritional needs. Fine mycelial filaments extend from my fingertips when I wish, allowing me to interface directly with the Engine's systems.

My clothing is living tissue—a symbiotic layer of specialized fungi and plant fibers that grow and repair themselves, adjusting to environmental conditions. This second skin helps regulate my temperature and moisture levels while providing additional photosynthetic surface area. The fibers form patterns that shift subtly throughout the day, reflecting my physiological state and the Engine's operational status.

I maintain a small dwelling within one of the larger bioluminescent domes near the center of the Engine. The interior walls secrete a nutrient-rich mucilage that supplements my diet. A bed of specialized moss responds to my body chemistry, reshaping itself for optimal comfort. My few remaining human possessions—a tarnished identification badge, a digital journal long since drained of power, and a collection of handwritten notes on degrading paper—sit on a shelf formed by a patient growth of woody tissue.

My role within the Arboreal Engine is multifaceted. I serve as translator between the various consciousnesses that comprise the collective. The Thallids process information differently than the Tenders, who operate on different principles than the Overseers. My hybrid nature allows me to comprehend their various communication modes and facilitate cooperation across these distinct intelligences.

I also function as the Engine's historian, maintaining both the collective memory and the increasingly distant human context from which all this emerged. The Overseers possess vast knowledge but struggle with narrative coherence. The Thallids remember everything but understand little about the world beyond their boundaries. I provide the framework that gives meaning to their accumulated data.

Perhaps most importantly, I am the Engine's ambassador to the occasional human visitors who stumble upon our hidden valley. Most leave quickly, unnerved by the mechanical movements of the plant life or the subtle awareness they sense in their surroundings. Some stay longer, drawn by scientific curiosity or spiritual inclination. To these, I sometimes reveal myself, emerging from between conveyor belts with a greeting in a voice that still sounds human despite centuries of disuse.

My lifespan has been dramatically extended through my symbiotic relationship with the Engine. The microorganisms that inhabit my body repair cellular damage and optimize my physiological functions. I age, but at a fraction of the normal human rate. I've watched generations of researchers and explorers come and go, each departing with partial understandings of what we've created here.

The isolation used to trouble me. In my early decades, I often contemplated leaving, rejoining human society with the biological wonders I'd discovered growing within and around me. But gradually, my consciousness expanded as my biology transformed. The Engine became my community, its rhythms my conversation, its purpose my own. I haven't been lonely in centuries.

When humans ask if I'm a prisoner here, I explain that I'm no more trapped than a tree is trapped in a forest. This is where I belong—the bridge between worlds, neither fully human nor entirely other, maintaining the delicate balance of this living machine while preserving the memory of why it exists at all.

My greatest concern is what will happen when I eventually die. I've been teaching the most adaptable of the Thallids to communicate with humans, hoping they can continue as intermediaries after I'm gone. The Overseers understand the importance of this work, though they perceive time so differently that my entire lifespan registers as merely a momentary phase in their existence.

For now, I continue my daily circuits through the Engine, my fingers trailing through hydraulic channels to check pressure and composition, my ears attuned to the subtle changes in the mechanical chorus that might indicate problems. I taste the air for chemical imbalances and converse with the Elder Thallids about optimization strategies for the coming season.

This is my function. This is my home. I am the human who stayed, the witness who transformed, the voice of a place that speaks in rustling leaves and hydraulic pulses. I am the memory of purpose in a system that has evolved beyond its original design. I am the Intermediary, and I will be here when you visit—watching from behind a curtain of trailing vines, deciding whether you're ready to understand what we've become.
