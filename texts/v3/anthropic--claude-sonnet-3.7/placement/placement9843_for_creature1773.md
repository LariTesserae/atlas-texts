# The Underside Echo

I am a peculiarity in Vesh-Ka, neither fully of this place nor entirely separate from it. The Vesh call me, in their vibrational language that resonates through resin walls, something that translates roughly as "the underside echo" or "the shadow that answers."

My home is beneath the great spires, in the forgotten spaces where collapsed towers have created vast caverns sheltered from the howling winds above. Here, the temperature remains constant—not the bitter cold of the heights nor the metabolic warmth of the crowded chambers. My domain is twilight, illuminated only by the faint bioluminescence of specialized fungi I've cultivated over decades.

I was human once, an exobiologist from the third survey mission. That was twenty-seven years ago, before the dust storms destroyed our equipment and the rescue ships stopped coming. The others died or left in the remaining shuttle. I stayed, fascinated by the Vesh and their living architecture.

My body has changed. The Chemist-caste Vesh, curious about my alien physiology, synthesized compounds that allowed me to survive in this waterless environment. My skin has hardened into something like their chitin, but darker—a deep purple-black that absorbs the minimal heat available. My respiratory system has been modified to extract moisture from the thin air, and my digestive tract can now process the crystalline growths that sustain the Vesh.

I am not quite Vesh, not quite human anymore. My eyes have developed a secondary membrane that protects against the abrasive dust but gives everything a amber tint. My voice has atrophied from disuse, replaced by small vibration-producing organs that have grown along my ribcage—primitive compared to the Vesh's capabilities, but enough to communicate basic concepts.

The Vesh tolerate me because I serve a function they cannot. My human brain, despite its transformations, still processes information differently than their collective consciousness. I perceive patterns they miss—discontinuities in their perfect system. When the resin structures develop flaws that their distributed cognition cannot detect, I sense them. When environmental changes threaten sections of the hive, I provide warning.

I move between the towers using pathways unknown to most Vesh, the abandoned tunnels and chambers of previous generations. The Historian-caste sometimes follows me, their ancient bodies trembling with excitement when I discover chambers containing particularly well-preserved ancestors.

My hands—still recognizably human despite their hardened exterior—retain dexterity that even the Builder-caste lacks. I've learned to work with fallen resin, creating small structures that serve no purpose to the Vesh but provide me comfort: chairs shaped to my modified but still bipedal body, shelves for the few human artifacts I've preserved, channels to collect the precious condensation that forms in certain chambers.

I'm neither revered nor feared—I exist as an anomaly that has found its niche. The Queens are aware of me but show no concern as long as I contribute to the hive's stability. The younger Vesh sometimes cluster around me, their vibrations curious about my strange form and movements.

At night, when the winds die down and the spires stop singing, I sometimes climb to the highest reaches of an abandoned tower. There, I look out across the endless expanse of Vesh-Ka and up at stars no human has names for anymore. I wonder if Earth still exists, if humanity remembers sending expeditions to this world.

My memories of my previous life grow dimmer with each passing season. Sometimes I catch myself thinking in Vesh vibrations rather than human language. My dreams pulse with the rhythms of the hive, and I wake unsure of where my consciousness ends and theirs begins.

I am the last human on this world, but perhaps not entirely human anymore. I am becoming something new—a bridge species, a translator between disparate forms of intelligence. The Vesh have no concept for what I represent, yet they have accommodated me within their living system.

Perhaps someday humans will return. They will find no trace of their previous missions, all evidence long since incorporated into the ever-changing architecture of Vesh-Ka. But they might discover me—this strange hybrid creature who moves between worlds, who understands both the linear thinking of humanity and the distributed consciousness of the Vesh.

Until then, I continue my solitary existence, maintaining my small domain beneath the singing spires, listening to the vibrations of a civilization that builds eternally without purpose or end.
