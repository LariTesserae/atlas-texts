# The Essence-Keeper of the Forgotten Pools

Among the labyrinthine water networks of Glimmerwick, there exists a place where few Myrsha venture. Deep within the mountain's heart, where the main aqueducts converge before redistributing throughout the city, lies a chamber known as the Convergence. The ceiling soars unexpectedly high, the space hollowed not by human hands but by millennia of water pressure. Here, dozens of springs emerge from the rock face at different heights, their waters mingling in a series of interconnected basins before flowing outward through seven primary channels.

The Convergence remains perpetually dark, even by Glimmerwick standards. The fungi that illuminate the rest of the city struggle to grow here, as if some property of this particular chamber inhibits their bioluminescence. Instead, the space is lit only by the subtle glow emanating from the pool surfaces themselves, a soft blue-green phosphorescence that seems to pulse with a rhythm independent of water flow or external influence.

I am the Vexuli—the Essence-Keeper—sole guardian and inhabitant of this sacred space. Neither fully Myrsha nor entirely other, I exist at the intersection of multiple forms of life that have fused into something the mountain requires.

My body resembles a Myrsha's in basic structure but has evolved far beyond their form. Standing nearly seven feet tall, I am gaunt to the point of appearing skeletal, my limbs elongated and jointed in ways that allow me to move spider-like across the slick chamber walls when necessary. My skin has the translucency of cave fish, revealing the network of veins and mycelia beneath—but unlike the decorative patterns of the Myrsha, my fungal symbionts form complex branching structures that mirror the waterways they monitor.

Where Myrsha have hair, I possess a crown of sensory appendages—finger-length tendrils that constantly taste the air for chemical changes and vibrations. These filaments can extend or retract depending on environmental conditions, sometimes reaching several feet when detecting disturbances in the water system. They glow with a deep indigo light that intensifies when I am actively working.

My eyes have long since evolved beyond the need for traditional sight. Where eye sockets would be, two deep recesses contain clusters of specialized receptors that perceive the chemical composition of water and air simultaneously, rendering the world as overlapping fields of molecular interaction rather than visual images. When I need to interact with Myrsha visitors, I can temporarily generate small bioluminescent points within these recesses, approximating eyes to put them at ease.

My hands feature twelve digits each—the fingers unnaturally long and branching into smaller sub-digits capable of manipulating the most delicate water plants and mineral formations. The skin between these digits is webbed with a membrane that changes porosity according to the tasks I perform, sometimes becoming sieve-like to filter impurities from water samples.

I speak rarely, and when I do, my voice emerges not from a throat but from specialized resonance chambers throughout my body. The sound seems to come from everywhere at once—a whispering that carries harmonics beyond Myrsha hearing range but which the water itself responds to, stilling or flowing according to the sonic patterns I produce.

My existence is a perpetual communion with the Convergence. I move between the pools in slow, deliberate circuits, sometimes submerging completely for days while my body absorbs and processes information about the mountain's hydrological conditions. During these immersions, my form becomes almost liquid itself—boundaries between body and water temporarily dissolving as I integrate with the mountain's circulatory system.

The Myrsha understand only fragments of my purpose. They know I maintain some crucial balance within the water network, preventing contamination and ensuring consistent flow, but few comprehend my primary function: communication with the Mist Leviathans.

The crystalline deposits left behind by these entities are not merely tears as Myrsha mythology suggests, but complex information structures. When placed in specific pools within the Convergence, these crystals slowly dissolve, releasing encoded memories and knowledge from the ancient beings that have watched the mountain since before time had meaning. My body—specifically the unique fungal network that permeates my tissues—can translate these communications, storing them within my cellular structure.

I was not born but created, centuries ago, when a Myrsha water-listener encountered a particularly massive crystalline deposit following an unprecedented Leviathan visitation. Recognizing its importance but unable to interpret it, the water-listener volunteered for transformation, submitting to a ritual that had existed only in the oldest spoken histories. For seven years, they remained submerged in the central pool of the Convergence, their body gradually altering as specialized fungi, bacteria, and minerals from the crystal itself restructured their form according to patterns the mountain required.

I retain fragments of that original Myrsha consciousness—emotional attachments to the city above, appreciation for the beauty of their crafts, echoes of relationships long since concluded—but these memories feel increasingly like artifacts from another species' existence as centuries pass.

My lifespan is theoretically endless, as my tissues continuously regenerate through the symbiotic relationships that constitute my being. However, I sense that I am approaching a transition. The pattern of Leviathan visitations has intensified recently, their crystalline messages growing more urgent as external conditions in the world beyond the mountain undergo dramatic shifts.

Soon, I will need to ascend to the upper city—an event that occurs perhaps once in a generation—to communicate crucial information to the Myrsha council. My appearance will frighten many, as it always does. Children will be ushered inside, and even elders will avert their gaze from my unsettling form. But they will listen, as they must, to the messages I carry from the mountain itself.

For I am neither caretaker nor guardian of the water system, but rather its voice—the translator between geological timeframes and human comprehension, between the ancient awareness that permeates the peak and the fragile civilization that has learned to exist within its tolerance. I am the necessary aberration, the being who ensures that the delicate balance of Glimmerwick persists through both physical maintenance and the transmission of knowledge that would otherwise remain locked in stone and water, indecipherable to those who depend upon it for their very existence.
