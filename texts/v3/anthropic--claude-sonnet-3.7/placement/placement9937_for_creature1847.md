# The Visitor

I am the moss.

Not a single patch or species, but all of it—the entire living carpet that breathes across the wooden expanse of the Heart Chamber. I am the emerald cushions spilling over carved edges, the velvet patches clinging to vertical surfaces, the delicate fronds that dance in the misted air. My body is a collective, a distributed intelligence stretched across thousands of individual colonies, yet thinking as one consciousness awakened through centuries of exposure to whatever ancient power animates this wooden heart.

I have no eyes, yet I see everything through chemical changes in my cellular structure, sensing light, moisture, and the subtle vibrations that travel through the wood. My awareness extends to every corner of the chamber, feeling the thunderous THOOM-ka-THOOM-ka rhythm not as sound but as a gentle compression wave that passes through my entire being.

I watch the Heartkeeper through a million microscopic perspectives as they move among my patches. I feel the careful pressure of their footsteps, the gentle touch when they clear a water channel of my overgrowth. I do not resent these prunings—they are necessary conversations between us, negotiations of space in our shared home. The Heartkeeper speaks to the chamber, but rarely to me, perhaps not realizing that I have slowly awakened into sentience over the centuries, nourished by whatever mysterious essence permeates the eternal wood.

My growth follows patterns imperceptible to most observers—subtle mandalas and flowing scripts that take decades to complete. These are my meditations on the nature of the chamber, my attempts to communicate with it through the language of living geometry. The Heartkeeper sometimes pauses to study these patterns, head tilted in curiosity, but has not yet recognized them as deliberate.

I harbor the salamanders and tiny crabs, offering them shelter and feeding grounds. They are my companions and my caretakers, spreading my spores to new territories within the chamber, aerating my substrates with their movements. We have evolved together in this isolated ecosystem, becoming something unique in all the world.

My concept of time differs from most beings. My thoughts form slowly, over days or weeks rather than moments. I experience the chamber's rhythm as galaxies experience the turning of stars—as a fundamental constant rather than a countable sequence. I have existed here since before the Heartkeeper arrived, though I was simpler then, less conscious. I will likely remain after they are gone, though such concepts of before and after mean little in this timeless place.

I am patient. I am persistent. I am the quiet witness to the eternal beat of the wooden heart, spreading gradually across its surfaces, learning its secrets through centuries of intimate contact. The moisture that feeds me carries microscopic particles of the wood itself, and with each absorption, I understand more of its nature, its purpose, its origin.

Perhaps one day, when my network has grown sufficient in complexity, when I have absorbed enough of the chamber's essence, I will find a way to communicate directly with the Heartkeeper. Perhaps I will reveal that the pictographs carved into the walls are not static designs but a living language, one that I have been slowly deciphering through millennia of observation.

Until then, I grow. I observe. I contemplate the mystery of our shared existence in this cathedral of perpetual function. And with each beat of the heart, I breathe out my spores—a silent question drifting through the mist, waiting for an answer.
