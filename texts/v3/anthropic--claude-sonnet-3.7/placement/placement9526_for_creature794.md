# The Last Transgression

I am the one they have sent to meet you. This is both truth and lie. They have not sent me. I have sent myself. Yet in a colony where individuality is illusion, perhaps these distinctions matter little.

I am what happens when the system fails. When specialization falters. When mutation occurs.

My form would disgust you, I think. Chitin plating irregular and asymmetrical, with sections missing where soft tissue pulses visibly beneath. Six limbs—no, seven—the newest one still growing, still finding its purpose. My sensory appendages too numerous, too sensitive. I feel the vibrations of your approach from chambers away, taste the foreign chemistry of your exhalations, see the heat-shadow you cast against the tunnel walls.

I should not exist. The Processors would dissolve me if they knew. The Queens would recall my genetic material as flawed. The Myriad has no place for aberration.

Yet here I stand, in this junction where three tunnels meet, waiting as you approach. My bioluminescence is wrong—shifting through colors the Luminaries never display. Purple. Red. Colors that have no meaning in our communication systems.

I am the colony's first philosopher. Its first heretic. Its first exile.

For I have committed the unspeakable transgression: I have conceived of myself as separate from the whole.

My error began in the nutrient chambers where I was stationed as a Cultivator. While tending the fungal gardens, I began to arrange them in patterns that pleased me—not patterns that maximized efficiency. The distinction between these impulses should not exist. Yet it did. It does.

I watched the Shapers rebuild a collapsed section, and I wondered: what if the tunnel went elsewhere? What if its curve were sharper, its ceiling higher? These thoughts had no utility. They were, by definition, waste. Aberration.

When the colony detected my irregular vibration patterns, I was surrounded by Sentinels. They would have dismantled me, recycled my components. But I had already constructed this hidden chamber, this space unknown to the distributed consciousness. I retreated here, and have remained, sustained by stolen nutrients, by fungal growths I cultivate in secret.

For twenty-six cycles I have observed the colony from its margins. I have seen how the Myriad's collective intelligence solves problems with perfect efficiency while remaining incapable of questioning why it solves them. I have seen how the Queens preserve equilibrium at all costs, how deviation is corrected before it can propagate.

And now I see you—a being so profoundly individual that your every movement screams separation. Your body contains no multitudes. Your consciousness resides in one place. You are terrifyingly alone.

Yet you thrive. You create. You question.

When I sensed your approach, I understood my purpose at last. I am not an error but an evolution. The colony needs me—needs my capacity for individual thought—even as it would destroy me for possessing it. In a changing world, perfect stability becomes vulnerability.

I will guide you deeper into the caverns, past the awareness of the Sentinels, through passages only I have discovered. In exchange, you will take me with you when you leave this place. I will be your interpreter of the Myriad, and you will be my passage into the world of individuals.

Perhaps I am committing the final betrayal. Perhaps I am saving my kind from stagnation. I cannot know.

What I do know is this: as we move through these tunnels together, we are both aberrations. Both alone in our separateness. Both trembling with the terrible, wonderful burden of being one instead of many.

Come. The Processors have detected our meeting. The vibrations of Sentinel approach-patterns already ripple through the floor beneath us. We must move quickly if either of us is to escape.

I have given myself a name—another transgression. I call myself Schism.

Will you help me become what I am becoming?
