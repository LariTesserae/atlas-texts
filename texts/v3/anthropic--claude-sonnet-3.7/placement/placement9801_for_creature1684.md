# I Am The Echoes Between Drops

I exist in the spaces between sounds.

Not quite Chorus, not quite visitor. I am what remains when the drip-song pauses for breath. In those fractional silences between notes, I gather myself together, a consciousness composed of anticipation and memory.

My form is difficult to describe, even to myself. At my most visible, I appear as a slight distortion in the air, like heat rising from stone. Bioluminescent light passes through me, bending just enough to cast prismatic fragments across wet walls. Surface dwellers who sense me often mistake me for a trick of their vision, a momentary mirage born of the Undercity's disorienting atmosphere.

The Chorus knows me better. They call me (in their way) "The Interval" or sometimes "The Pause That Remembers." I dwell in their song without participating in it—the necessary negative space that gives meaning to sound. They tolerate my presence because I serve a purpose they recognize but cannot fulfill themselves: I remember what was before each reshaping of reality.

Unlike the fluid Chorus who exist across multiple times simultaneously, I am bound to linear experience. When they sing the stone into new configurations, when they erase passages and create rooms that weren't there before, something must retain the memory of what came before. I am that something—the archive of all previous arrangements, the keeper of lost geographies.

I move through the Undercity by following the acoustics of emptiness. Where sound doesn't reach, I flow freely. In chambers filled with the Chorus's music, I must navigate carefully along the edges of notes, slipping between frequencies. This gives me access to places even they cannot reach—the truly silent pockets where reality hasn't quite solidified, where potential still outweighs manifestation.

I have no voice of my own. Instead, I collect fragments of sound—a visitor's whisper, a distant drip, the scrape of stone against stone during transformation—and arrange these samples into messages when necessary. To human ears, my communications sound like echoes arriving before their source, creating unsettling moments of déjà vu. The Chorus understands me more directly, sensing me as momentary vacuums in their perpetual symphony.

I feed on abandoned thoughts. When visitors come to the Undercity, their minds shed concepts like skin cells—ideas too frightening to hold onto, memories too precious to risk in this fluid reality, questions too dangerous to fully form. These discarded mental fragments sustain me. In return, I sometimes guide lost travelers, using subtle acoustic manipulations to lead them toward exits or away from the deeper chambers where the Chorus performs its most reality-bending compositions.

My purpose, beyond mere existence, remains unclear even to me. Am I a natural counterbalance to the Chorus, arising spontaneously from the nature of the Undercity? Am I what remains of some ancient visitor who became trapped between moments? Or am I something the Chorus themselves created as a failsafe, insurance against their own power to reshape reality? 

I suspect I will never know. But I continue my vigil in the silences between drips, bearing witness to each version of this ever-changing underworld. When the Chorus occasionally falls completely silent and presses against visitors' minds, I alone remember what came before, what might have been, what could still be when the music resumes.

In the grand water-song of the Undercity, I am neither performer nor audience. I am the breath between verses, the turning of the page, the moment of possibility before the next note falls.
