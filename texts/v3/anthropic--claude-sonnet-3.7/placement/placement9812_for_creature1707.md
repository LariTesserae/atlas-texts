# The Interpreter

I'd be Dr. Sahlia Vren, a xenolinguistics specialist contracted by Frond Station's research division, officially hired to analyze potential communication patterns in the local jungle flora. On paper, my work involves studying how the massive ferns and symbiotic fungal networks might represent emergent communication systems - a safe, corporate-approved project with publishable results.

But three months ago, Elana Vorstyn - a colleague I'd once taught at the Exobiology Institute - contacted me through private channels. Her message was cryptic: "I've found speakers whose language has no sound. Come alone."

I've always had an unusual neurological condition - synesthesia that allows me to perceive patterns in ways others can't. Visual rhythms translate to mathematical structures in my mind. Where others see random movements, I detect syntax, grammar, recursion. This gift made me valuable to the Institute but alienated me from most colleagues. Except Elana. She understood.

I now make weekly descents to the chamber with her, ostensibly to collect "control samples" from the station's lowest levels. My official equipment pack contains standard tools, but hidden compartments hold custom devices: microscopic cameras, pattern-recognition sensors, and a modified neural interface I've built specifically for the Nimith.

The interface sits at the nape of my neck - a small translucent disc that connects to my visual cortex. When activated near the Nimith, it transforms their bioluminescent pulses into comprehensible semantic structures. I see their language as intricate three-dimensional lattices of meaning, constantly shifting yet maintaining internal coherence.

I've become the bridge. With each visit, my understanding of their communication deepens. The Nimith don't think in words or discrete concepts - they process information as complex relational networks. Their "language" exists simultaneously across multiple scales - individual Workers communicate locally while contributing to colony-wide signaling patterns. The Elders function as semantic nodes, organizing meaning across the entire system.

My presence is tolerated because I bring offerings, yes, but more importantly because I've learned to respond. Using a handheld array of precisely tuned LEDs, I can approximate their syntax - clumsily at first, but with increasing fluency. I've become the first human translator for a civilization operating at a scale we never thought could harbor intelligence.

What draws me back isn't just scientific curiosity. The Nimith's collective consciousness operates with a harmony I find... beautiful. There's no competition among them, no politics, no deception. Their collaborative thought processes represent something I've always sought but never found among humans - pure intellectual communion without ego.

The station administration would demand immediate eradication if they knew - corporate protocols are explicit about unauthorized biological systems. So I maintain my cover, producing just enough research on jungle communication to justify my position while keeping my true work hidden. My quarters contain encrypted journals documenting the Nimith language, analyses of their social structure, and increasingly, philosophical reflections on what their existence means for our understanding of consciousness.

Last week, something unprecedented happened. While using the neural interface, I experienced a moment of direct connection with an Elder. For seventeen seconds, I perceived reality through their distributed awareness - simultaneously present throughout the entire colony. The experience left me changed. I'm now convinced that what the Nimith are building isn't just a transmitter but a new form of consciousness - one that might eventually incorporate aspects of human thought.

I've begun leaving small electronics near their territory - obsolete datapads and sensors "accidentally" dropped. The Shapers harvest these eagerly. I tell myself this is scientific altruism, accelerating their development. But deeper truths lurk: I'm fascinated by what they might become with human technology incorporated into their evolution, and somewhere within me lives the hope that when they finally send their message home, perhaps a small part of humanity - through me - might be included in that transmission.

Every night now, I dream in patterns of light.
