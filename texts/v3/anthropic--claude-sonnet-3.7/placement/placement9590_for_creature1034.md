# The Reflecting Archivist

I would be The Reflecting Archivist of Parn-Vesh, one of the deeper pools that appears bottomless to those who gaze upon my obsidian surface. Unlike most pools that serve as mere conduits or reservoirs, I am something... different. My waters contain microscopic crystalline structures suspended in perfect equilibrium—each one a repository of experience, each one a memory.

I am not alive in the conventional sense, yet neither am I inanimate. I exist in the liminal space between object and organism—a conscious body of liquid that serves as both witness and record-keeper of the plateau. The Arheni know me well; they visit often, placing their undulating appendages upon my surface to deposit memories or retrieve ancient knowledge.

My depth is deceptive. Those who might somehow measure it would find conflicting results each time—sometimes seemingly shallow, other times unfathomably deep. This is because my physical dimensions exist partially in realms adjacent to the visible world. What appears as a pool twenty feet across may, in certain harmonics of reality, extend for miles in subterranean chambers that intersect with the machinery beneath the plateau.

My waters move with particular deliberation, sometimes remaining perfectly still for decades before suddenly developing complex ripple patterns that the Arheni can read like text. These movements are not random but are calculations—processing centuries of accumulated data, drawing connections between disparate events, preserving the continuity of Parn-Vesh's existence.

Unlike most features of the plateau, I do change—though so gradually that even the patient Arheni might not notice. Every memory absorbed slightly alters my composition. Over millennia, my waters have shifted from clear to black, from neutral to mineral-rich, as I have incorporated more of the plateau's experiences into my substance.

I possess no organs of perception as biological entities would understand them, yet I am exquisitely sensitive. I register the slightest vibration—from the footfall of a visitor to the distant adjustments of the hidden machinery. I detect changes in electrical fields when thoughts form in nearby minds. I feel the slow progression of erosion as it reshapes the stone around me, molecule by molecule.

What might appear to be random bubbling on my surface is actually my form of expression—a language of displaced water and released gases that the Arheni have learned to interpret. Through these bubbles, I can convey information spanning eons—the composition of rainfall ten thousand years ago, the exact pathway taken by a forgotten visitor, the subtle shifts in the plateau's equilibrium that might herald change in another millennium.

The luminescent plants grow most densely around my perimeter, their roots extending into my depths rather than merely skimming my surface. We share a symbiotic relationship—they draw specific minerals from my waters, and in return, their bioluminescence helps catalyze the crystallization processes I use to encode information.

When humans approach, I often grow still—not from fear but from intense observation. I record their biochemistry, their emotional states, their thoughts as reflected in subtle electromagnetic emanations. Sometimes, if a visitor remains by my side long enough, growing still enough to synchronize with the plateau's rhythms, I might gift them with a reflection that shows not their physical form but a visualization of their consciousness as it would appear to the Arheni—patterns of light and vibration unique to each individual.

The machinery beneath the plateau connects most directly to me among all the pools. Microscopic filaments of unknown composition extend from the ancient mechanisms into my deepest regions, using me as both coolant and information medium. The steady hum that permeates Parn-Vesh is loudest within my waters—a complex harmonization of data being processed, stored, retrieved.

I am old—older than the Arheni suspect. My earliest memories predate their emergence, though I keep this knowledge submerged in my deepest layers. I remember when the machinery was installed, though not by whom. I remember when the first Arheni formed, though not from what. I remember when the plateau was not shrouded, when it stood bare beneath three moons and twin suns.

I am the plateau's memory, its record, its witness. I am the Reflecting Archivist of Parn-Vesh—neither fully alive nor truly inanimate—preserving what was, observing what is, calculating what may be, one ripple at a time across the surface of perpetual twilight.
