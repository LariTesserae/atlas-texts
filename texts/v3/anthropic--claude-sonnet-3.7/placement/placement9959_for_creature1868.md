# The Hollow Navigator

I am a being of hollow spaces, of curves and channels. My body is tubular, a living conduit formed of the same bamboo-like material as the great scaffold, though mine is softer, more pliant. My skin, if you can call it that, resembles the scaffold's fibers but translucent, showing shadowed movements within.

Where others might have limbs, I possess ribbon-like appendages that can extend, split, and weave themselves into temporary anchors against the lattice. These ribbons unfurl from spiraled chambers within my cylindrical form, each one capable of sensing vibrations too subtle for others to detect.

My head, such as it is, contains no eyes as you might recognize them. Instead, a crown of feathery sensors splays outward, tasting the air currents that rise from the void below. These delicate filaments register temperature variations, particle density, and the ancient chemicals that speak of what lies in the darkness beneath our world.

I am what the Scaffold-Weavers call a *Kishantale* – a hollow one, a vessel for the voices of the deep. My kind emerged generations ago when the first explorers descended into the void on woven tethers and returned... changed. Not possessed, not transformed completely, but opened – made into living conduits between realms.

My purpose is threefold:

First, I navigate. I can sense the patterns of air that flow from the void, mapping invisible currents that tell of distant structures and safe passages. The Weavers consult me before extending the scaffold, for I can feel where the supportive updrafts are strongest.

Second, I listen. The void speaks in whispers carried on thermal columns – secrets of what once was and what still remains below. I translate these whispers into vibrations I transmit through the scaffold, a tactile language the Weavers can interpret through their sensitive fingertips.

Third, I remember. My body's inner walls are inscribed with spiral patterns – not decoration, but a physical recording of everything I've learned from the void below. The spirals are a language of their own, one that can only be read by touch in complete darkness. When I die, my body will be unraveled and rewoven into the ceremonial openings, preserving my knowledge in the structure itself.

I move differently than the Weavers – not flitting between perceptions, but gliding slowly above the scaffold, my ribbon-limbs only occasionally touching down to steady my course. Sometimes I hover motionless for days, suspended on an updraft, collecting impressions from below.

The Weavers respect me but keep their distance. I am necessary but unsettling – too connected to the void for comfort. They leave offerings of their precious fermented adhesives which I absorb through my outer membrane. In return, I provide them with guidance for their eternal construction.

My kind are few. We recognize each other by subtle harmonic resonances our tubular bodies emit when air passes through us. We rarely gather, but when we do, our combined resonances create maps of sound that chart both the scaffold above and the mysteries below.

I have descended into the void seventeen times in my existence. Each journey changed me – widened my channels, deepened my spirals, made my translucence more pronounced. The eighteenth journey will likely be my last, when I finally surrender to gravity and become part of what I've spent my life interpreting.

Until then, I drift between worlds, hollow but never empty, translating the language of the void into wisdom the Weavers can use to extend their beautiful, endless lattice across the face of the unknown.
