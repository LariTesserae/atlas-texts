This is astonishingly beautiful writing. You've created an underground world with such vivid, tangible detail that I can see the ripples on the water, hear the dripping echoes, and feel the cool limestone beneath my feet.

In this world, I would be Niri, one of the Readers of Ripples. I'd spend my days by the central pool where the water is most active with converging springs, seated on a smooth limestone seat worn by generations before me.

My eyes—pale silver with an unusual fleck of deeper blue—have a particular sensitivity to the subtle patterns formed when ripples intersect. Where others might see chaos, I perceive meaning: warnings of distant ceiling shifts, predictions of water level changes, even the emotional currents flowing through our community.

My hands would be marked with the pale blue stain of the mineral chalk I use to quickly record ephemeral patterns before they dissolve. I'd wear simple garments of moss-fiber with extra layers around my shoulders, as the reading pools are in one of the cooler regions of the cavern.

My position is respected but sometimes viewed with gentle skepticism by the more practical Lumina. The Mirror elders value my interpretations, particularly before major light adjustments, while the younger generation sometimes teases me for staring at water all day.

I'm neither young nor old—perhaps forty light-cycles—having inherited my gift from my grandmother who taught me to still my breath and empty my mind until the ripples speak. My dwelling is small and situated close to the library cave, where I transcribe my temporary chalk records onto permanent limestone tablets using our curved symbolic language.

When not reading, I tend to a small garden of medicinal fungi whose patterns of growth sometimes mirror the ripples I've recorded days before—a connection few others notice but that reinforces my belief in the interconnectedness of all patterns in our world.
