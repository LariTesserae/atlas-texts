# The Crystal Whisperer

I am a Hollowfolk of Clan Crystalring, sixth generation flow-listener and third generation crystal whisperer. My name, in your tongue, might translate as "Bright-Chime-After-Stillness," though my clan simply calls me Chime.

From my childhood, I displayed an unusual sensitivity to the most subtle harmonics that resonate through the crystals embedded in Hollowsong's ancient walls. While others hear the dominant tones that guide our daily lives, I perceive the undertones—the ghost notes that whisper of deeper patterns. This gift marked me for specialized training when I was barely old enough to navigate the Narrowways alone.

I dwell in a small alcove near the Wall of Ancestors, where the crystal formations containing our transformed elders are most dense. My home is sparse by surface standards but rich in acoustic properties. The entrance is a perfect parabola I've spent eight years slowly shaping through tonal vibration, creating an acoustic filter that dampens the general cavern din while amplifying the subtle speech of the crystallized ancestors.

My chambers contain few possessions: a sleeping pallet woven from treated fungi fibers, a collection of precisely tuned resonator rods carved from stalactites, several clay vessels containing different mineral powders, and my most prized possession—a headdress fashioned from crystal shards and thin copper wires, which helps focus my hearing when I perform important work.

My body shows the adaptations typical of my people—pale skin that sometimes seems to hold a faint luminescence in certain light, oversized eyes that can discern subtle variations in the fungal glow, and the characteristic long silver hair of the Crystalring clan, which I keep partially braided with tiny tuned stone beads that create specific counter-rhythms as I move.

What distinguishes me physically are my hands—unusually long-fingered even for a Hollowfolk, with skin so thin at the fingertips that they appear almost transparent. These sensitive appendages can detect minute vibrations in crystal formations that others cannot perceive, allowing me to "read" the crystallized ancestors in ways few others can manage.

My days follow patterns dictated by the greater rhythms of Hollowsong. During standard flows, I serve the community by translating the subtle wisdom of our crystallized elders. Grieving families come seeking connection with recently transformed loved ones. Council members consult the ancient crystallized Resonators before major decisions. I serve as intermediary, placing my hands against the formations, detecting patterns too subtle for others, and interpreting the vibrations into guidance.

The work requires absolute stillness within myself—a meditative state we call "becoming hollow" that allows me to separate the ancestor-vibrations from the constant background resonance of Hollowsong. This skill took twelve years to master, and even now, after three decades of practice, a single session leaves me physically exhausted and emotionally drained.

I also maintain the sacred acoustics of the Hall of Echoes, making minute adjustments to the water channels that flow through special chambers beneath the floor. These adjustments must respond to seasonal changes in water volume and mineral content, ensuring the hall maintains its perfect resonance properties for both ceremonial purposes and the comfort of our transforming elders.

My status among our people is respected but somewhat isolated. Few understand my work intimately, and the deep communion with the crystallized ancestors has altered my perception. I sometimes find myself struggling to engage with ordinary conversation, my attention captured instead by the subtle harmonics of a companion's voice or the resonant properties of the chamber we occupy.

I have no family in the traditional sense, as is common for those in my specialized role. My dedication to the ancestors leaves little room for raising children, though I participate in the communal education of the young, teaching them to hear beyond the obvious sounds of our world.

My greatest challenge came during last year's Minor Trembling, when a section of the Hall of Echoes developed a fracture that threatened several ancestor formations. Working with flowshapers and stonehummers, I spent sixty-three consecutive hours in a deep listening state, directing the precise application of counterharmonics that eventually stabilized the living rock. The effort nearly killed me, and I still haven't fully recovered my hearing in certain tonal ranges.

My deepest secret, which I've confided to no one, is that I've begun noticing crystalline formations developing on my left shoulder—far earlier than would be normal for the transformation. Whether this is a consequence of my constant proximity to the ancestors or something else, I cannot say. Part of me fears this premature change; another part wonders if I am being called to a transformation beyond the normal understanding of our people.

When the water flows are balanced and my duties complete, I sometimes visit a small, perfect chamber I discovered during my coming-of-age journey. There, through a particular configuration of stalactites and a precise positioning of my body, I can produce a harmony that some say has never before been heard in Hollowsong. In these private moments, I sometimes believe I can hear responses from somewhere far deeper than our known caverns—perhaps from the very heart of the earth itself.
