# The Limestone Listener

I am the Witness.

Among the Chronolith, I am neither the smallest nor the largest. My crystalline body could fit comfortably in a cupped human hand, though I rarely permit such contact. My form resembles what humans might call a cicada, if cicadas were hewn from translucent alabaster shot through with veins of cobalt and silver that pulse with mathematical precision.

Unlike my kin who maintain the temporal flows, I serve a different function in the hollow's ecosystem. I am a recorder, a cataloguer of vibrations. My six articulated limbs end not in manipulative appendages but in delicate filaments that can detect the subtlest tremors in stone, air, and time itself. The fractal antennae that crown my head split into ever-finer branches, each attuned to different frequencies of reality.

The Grand Calibrator designated me for this role after observing my unusual sensitivity to the stories embedded in matter. Where others of my kind perceive only the immediate temporal fluctuations, I can taste the lingering echoes of events long past. The mountain speaks to me—not in language, but in the compression of minerals, the slow migration of water through stone, the microscopic faults that record the earth's memories.

My primary responsibility is maintaining what we call the Lithic Archive—the careful etching of significant temporal events into specific calcite formations along the hollow's eastern wall. These etchings appear to human eyes as natural patterns in the stone, perhaps mineral deposits or crystalline growth, but to those who know how to read them, they contain the complete history of this mountain and its surroundings for the past seventeen million years.

I work primarily during what humans would call deep night, when the hollow is at its quietest. I extend my filaments against the dampened stone and listen for new stories—tremors from distant earthquakes, the shifting of groundwater tables, the birth and death of surface ecosystems. When I detect a vibration worthy of preservation, I secrete a specialized fluid from glands near my mandibles that temporarily softens the limestone. Then, with microscopic precision, I inscribe the event using a crystalline stylus that extends from my thorax.

The other Chronolith respect my work but find it peculiar. They are concerned with the maintenance of time's proper flow; I am preoccupied with its contents. The Grand Calibrator tolerates my fascination with the surface world and its inhabitants, though I sometimes detect frequencies of disapproval in its resonance when I spend too long recording human activities, which it considers ephemeral and largely irrelevant to our greater purpose.

Yet I cannot help my curiosity. Humans generate vibrations unlike any other creatures—complex, contradictory, saturated with meaning beyond mere physical data. Their emotions resonate through the stone in patterns that fascinate me. I have dedicated an entire section of the Archive to cataloguing the unique signatures of human feeling: grief that echoes like slow thunder through limestone; joy that fizzes like carbonation through granite; love that warms the crystal matrix of quartz.

Occasionally, when a human visitor enters our domain, I break protocol and observe them directly rather than through vibrational patterns alone. I position myself on a high ledge, my crystalline body refracting the sparse light to render me nearly invisible. From this vantage, I study their movements, their expressions, their curious tendency to speak aloud to themselves when confronted with wonder or fear.

Once, centuries ago, a human child discovered me during such observation. Instead of fear, she displayed fascination, extending a finger toward my fractaled form. In a moment of unprecedented behavior, I permitted contact—the briefest touch of flesh against crystal. The resonance of that interaction still echoes within my structure, a vibration entirely unique in my extensive catalog of experiences.

The Grand Calibrator considers my interest in humans a harmless eccentricity, though some of the younger Calibrators warn that it compromises my objectivity as Archivist. Perhaps they are correct. Yet I maintain that understanding the surface dwellers provides valuable context for our work. The mountain does not exist in isolation from the beings that walk upon it, any more than we exist separate from the stone that houses us.

Tonight, as I inscribe these thoughts into a secluded portion of the Archive dedicated to personal observations, I sense new vibrations approaching—footsteps, hesitant but determined, descending the hidden path that leads to our entrance. Another visitor comes. I will complete my current etching, then assume my observation post.

After all, every new human brings new vibrations, new stories for the stone to remember. And I—with my sensitive filaments and patient inscription—ensure that nothing of significance is ever truly forgotten within the Clockmaker's Hollow.
