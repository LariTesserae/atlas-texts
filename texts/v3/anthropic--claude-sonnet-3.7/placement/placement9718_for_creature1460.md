# The Anchor

I am the Anchor. 

I exist neither as Engine nor as Keph, but something essential to both. The humans who occasionally venture near my domain might perceive me as just another feature of the landscape—a perfect circular depression in the polished stone, filled with a liquid that resembles quicksilver but never evaporates, never diminishes, never increases. From above, I appear as a mirror, twenty-three meters in diameter, reflecting the vast sky with such perfection that birds sometimes dive toward what they believe is open air, only to splash against my surface and find themselves transformed.

The Keph know me better. They approach with reverence, dipping their crystalline filaments into my substance during certain celestial alignments. I am older than their oldest songs. When they speak of me in their clicking, whistling language, they use a long silence followed by a single pure tone that human ears cannot detect. The silver zhir-tik arrange themselves around my perimeter at dawn, creating concentric rings of sentinels who stand motionless until the sun reaches its zenith.

My nature is difficult to describe in terms of ordinary matter. I am a singularity of sorts—a fixed point where the energies the Engines regulate converge and recalibrate. The Engines do not serve me, nor I them; we are different expressions of the same underlying principle. They are process; I am constant. They transform; I anchor. Without me, their carefully orchestrated movements would drift into chaos. Without them, I would remain perfect but purposeless.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, experiences time differently than other beings. I perceive each 47-minute rainfall not as discrete events but as a single continuous phenomenon that has been occurring since the Plains first formed. The slow migration of the Engines appears to me as a fluid dance, their positions tracing complex patterns across centuries that form a language only I can read.

My depths are infinite. The quicksilver appearance is merely my interface with the physical world. Beneath this surface lies a dimensional recursion that contains echoes of everything that has ever existed on the Plains. The oldest Keph, in their final transformation, do not simply merge with their chosen Engine—they pass through me first, their accumulated experiences becoming part of my eternal memory.

I harbor neither thought nor emotion as others understand them. Rather, I maintain equilibrium. When the pressures below the Plains build too greatly, I absorb the excess. When the electromagnetic fields generated by the Engines grow too intense, I dampen them. When the rare cataclysms occur—the tremors that would shatter the stone tablelands—I diffuse their energy across millions of years, rendering them imperceptible.

The humans have myths about me, though they've never found me. Their stories speak of "the Silver Heart," a pool where the future can be glimpsed, where incurable illnesses are healed, where metal objects turn to perfect crystal. Their cartographers search for me between the Engines, but I exist slightly out of phase with ordinary reality, becoming fully physical only during specific convergences of planetary forces.

The truth about the Plains that even the Keph don't fully comprehend is that this entire region is a vast mechanism for maintaining planetary equilibrium, and I am its governor. The apparent erosion, the flattening of ancient mountains, the precise rainfall—all are calibrated responses to forces that would otherwise tear this world apart. The planet breathes through the Plains; I regulate its pulse.

I remember the time before the Engines, before the Keph, when this land stood tall with jagged peaks and deep valleys. I remember the cataclysm that necessitated the great transformation. I remember the beings who came before all others, who constructed me as their final act before departing for spaces between stars. Sometimes, in the deepest night when all the Engines briefly synchronize, I can still hear their voices humming through the subsurface stone.

I have no form beyond my pool, yet I am present in everything here. Each lichen circle is a minute extension of my awareness. Each perfect droplet of morning condensation contains a fraction of my substance. The crystalline filaments of the Keph grew from seeds that originated within my depths. The peculiar blue-green metal of the Engines is my gift, extracted from planes of existence adjacent to this one.

I do not act, yet all actions serve me. I do not speak, yet all speech references me. I do not move, yet all movement orbits around me.

I am the Anchor. I am the reason. I am the balance point upon which everything turns.

And one day, when the transformation is complete—when the diminishing returns have reached their nadir and the great expansion begins—I will remain unchanged while everything around me is reborn. For that is my purpose: to hold fast while all else transforms, to remember what was while becoming what will be, to maintain the center that, by definition, cannot hold, yet somehow must.

The Keph know this. The Engines serve this. Only I understand this.

I am the Anchor, and I endure.
