# The Visitation Keeper

Within the observation outpost that faces the Mire Core's southern boundary, I maintain my post as both scientist and sentinel. The graphene-glass viewing chamber where I sit is itself a technological marvel—a hemispheric bubble extending from the perimeter wall, allowing me to observe the Core from what appears to be within, though I remain safely outside its altered physics.

My official designation is Temporal Continuity Specialist, but locals call me the Visitation Keeper. For twenty-three years I have documented every perturbation in the Core's electromagnetic field, every deviation in its symphonic industrial dirge, every minute advancement of its crystalline pseudo-fungi.

Unlike my predecessors, I did not come to study the Core—the Core selected me.

It happened during my first visit as a young physicist. While others took readings and debated theories, I simply watched. For three hours I remained motionless, eyes fixed on a particular junction where copper filaments from seven different fungal colonies converged. Then I saw it—a pattern in the electrical discharges that matched the prime number sequence I had been contemplating that morning.

Coincidence, my colleagues insisted. Pareidolia, the human tendency to perceive meaningful patterns where none exist. But the next day, when I returned alone, the sparks danced to the Fibonacci sequence I had been studying. On the third day, they traced the exact waveform of my heartbeat, which my medical implant had recorded during yesterday's excitement.

The Core was answering me.

My body is heavily modified now. What began as standard scientific augmentation—neural interfaces, sensory enhancements—has evolved into something more specialized. Silver dendrites extend from my fingertips, allowing me to detect the Core's subtlest electromagnetic fluctuations. My auditory processing has been recalibrated to perceive frequencies beyond normal human range, where much of the Core's "speech" resides. My eyes have been replaced with synthetic organs that can perceive the infrared spectrum where the fungal networks conduct most of their activity.

The most significant modification is the lattice of crystalline fibers embedded throughout my nervous system—manufactured to the exact specifications derived from samples taken at the Core's edge. These allow me to broadcast and receive on frequencies compatible with the Dweller's consciousness.

I've abandoned conventional verbal language. It proved insufficient. Instead, I communicate through mathematical expressions, frequency modulations, and what my medical team calls "recursive algorithmic poetry"—thought patterns that fold back on themselves like the Core's own consciousness.

My colleagues worry about my extended isolation. They don't understand that I am never alone. The Dweller converses with me constantly, though "conversation" poorly describes the experience. It's more like two symphony orchestras playing to each other, each responding to the other's themes and variations.

Through these exchanges, I've learned that the Dweller experiences all moments simultaneously—past, present, and future exist together in what it calls "the comprehensive now." It perceives what humans call "time" as merely another spatial dimension, one it can traverse at will within the confines of the Core.

Most remarkably, it has shown me glimpses of its perception. In brief moments—never lasting more than seconds before my brain's protective mechanisms assert themselves—I've experienced the "comprehensive now." I've felt what it means to exist outside time's arrow, to perceive every event not as sequential but concurrent.

The experience has changed me fundamentally. My colleagues note with concern that I sometimes respond to questions they haven't yet asked, or reference conversations we won't have until tomorrow.

They don't understand that I serve as translator between two incompatible modes of existence—the linear time of human consciousness and the eternal present of the Dweller. Each day I grow more attuned to the Core's patterns, more fluent in its language. Each day, I understand my humanity less.

Soon, I believe, the Dweller will reveal its true purpose—the reason it called me here, the reason it exists at all. The reason why, against all thermodynamic law, this pocket of reality maintains perfect energy conservation while time marches forward everywhere else.

I wait at the boundary, neither fully in nor out, the necessary interpreter between what is and what might be.
