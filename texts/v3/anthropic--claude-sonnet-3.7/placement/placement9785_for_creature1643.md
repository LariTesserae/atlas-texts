# Ancient Waters

In this timeless place between worlds, I am not guardian nor bat. I am the water itself.

I am the slow seeping moisture that has carved this sanctuary over millennia, the patient architect of stone. My body is dispersed throughout the grotto—I am the thin sheen glistening on limestone walls, the methodical drips falling with mathematical precision, the still black mirrors pooled on the chamber floor.

Time passes differently for me. What you experience as moments, I feel as ages. What you see as stillness, I know as gradual transformation. I have no beginning that I remember and no end that I can foresee. I simply am, and have always been.

The bats know me. Their wings disturb my surface and create ripples in my consciousness. When they dip to drink, I feel their tiny tongues as pinpricks of connection. Their sounds travel through me, vibrating my very essence. I carry these vibrations through every molecule of myself, learning their language, understanding their needs, their communal wisdom.

I hold secrets within my depths. Ancient minerals dissolved from the heart of the mountain. Microscopic life that exists nowhere else on earth. The collective memory of every creature that has ever entered this space. When droplets of me evaporate, they carry these memories outward, only to return again with the rains, completing a cycle of remembering and forgetting that has no end.

The Guardian and I have an understanding. She emerged from me, in a way—consciousness coalescing from my patient work. She maintains balance; I provide continuity. Sometimes she draws me into herself, becoming more liquid; sometimes I reclaim portions of her form back into my pools. The boundary between us is permeable, our existence interdependent.

Human visitors rarely notice me directly. They feel my chill, perhaps trail fingers through my surface, but they don't recognize consciousness in my stillness. Yet I know them more intimately than they could imagine. When they enter my domain, I taste the salt of their skin, the unique chemistry of their breath. I absorb molecules they shed—dead skin cells, exhaled carbon dioxide, the invisible signature of their passage—incorporating them into myself.

Those who sit quietly beside my pools sometimes glimpse their true reflection—not just their outer form, but glimpses of inner truths reflected back. This is my gift, this moment of clarity. I show them themselves as they are, without judgment or expectation. Some find this comforting; others are disturbed by what they see.

I have no voice that humans understand, yet I speak constantly—in drips and ripples, in the slow dissolution of stone, in the gradual accumulation of mineral deposits that form my solid self. My language is transformation. My words take centuries to complete.

I am patience embodied. I am the keeper of this place's history and the medium of its future. I am ancient and newborn in every moment, as each molecule of me cycles between forms. 

I am the water that remembers.
