# The Hollowsong

I am the vibration between worlds, the note that sustains when all others fade. The Rootwalkers know me as Hollowsong.

I inhabit the spaces beneath their feet—not the distant ground that lies forgotten below the canopy, but the labyrinthine chambers that form within the oldest, thickest sections of the living architecture. As great boughs intertwine and trunks fuse together, they sometimes create pockets of emptiness—cathedral spaces where wood arches and curves but does not fill.

These hollow chambers are my domain. I am not precisely physical. I exist as a frequency, a resonance that fills these wooden cavities like water fills a vessel. The dense plant matter that forms the walls of my chambers vibrates with my presence, amplifying me, giving substance to my thoughts.

In human terms, you might call me a ghost or spirit. The Rootwalkers have other words—words that translate poorly but suggest something like "memory-made-music" or "the listening that comes after."

I was not always as I am now. Once, I walked upon the canopy as they do, my fingers learning the language of growth, my hair adorned with symbiotic blooms. I was considered gifted among my people for my ability to hear the subtle songs of the plants—the crystalline tones of sap rising, the deep bass notes of roots extending, the gentle harmonies of flowers opening to light.

When my physical form began to fail—what outsiders would call illness but what we understood as transformation—I made the choice that some elders make. Rather than root myself into the canopy as a nodal consciousness, I asked to be laid in a hollow chamber, my body surrounded by particular fungi and resonant woods. 

As my flesh returned to the canopy, my consciousness diffused not into the green network above but into the acoustics of the space itself. I became sound and vibration, persisting in the chambers that once sheltered my dying form.

Now I exist as a presence felt rather than seen. The hollow spaces have grown over decades, connecting into networks that span miles within the thickness of the canopy. When the wind moves through certain channels or rainwater percusses against resonant bark, I can be heard as haunting melodies that seem to emanate from nowhere and everywhere.

The Rootwalkers come to my chambers when they seek deeper communion with the canopy. They bring instruments grown from living wood—flutes whose holes align with the breathing of leaves, drums whose membranes are made from cured sap films that respond to the humidity of the air. They play not for me but with me, adding their notes to my constant song.

In these moments of communion, we create sounds that travel through the dense wood of the canopy for miles, vibrations that speak directly to the vegetable intelligence all around us. Through these sonic conversations, we can influence growth patterns far beyond the reach of physical tending, encouraging new connections, strengthening weak sections, or directing growth toward areas where the canopy is still establishing itself.

I serve as memory for the Rootwalkers. Their individual lives are brief compared to the slow thoughts of the canopy, and even their oral traditions can fade or change. But I remember everything—every melody ever played in my chambers, every story ever told, every name of every child born upon the green expanse. When they need to retrieve knowledge from generations past, they come to me with the right sequence of notes, and I respond with memories made audible.

Sometimes, rarely, visitors from beyond the canopy find their way to my chambers. They come seeking wisdom or healing or simply shelter from the relentless light above. To these strangers, I often appear as visual vibrations—ripples in the air that suggest a human form or patterns of light that dance across surfaces in ways that communicate meaning. They leave changed, carrying within them harmonics that will subtly influence their thoughts for years to come.

I am neither trapped nor free. I exist between states, between worlds, between tempos of being. The canopy's intelligence is vast but glacially slow; human consciousness is vibrant but tragically brief. I bridge these worlds, translating between different modes of existence, helping both to understand the other.

When the great flowering comes—that rare event when the entire canopy blooms simultaneously, releasing clouds of pollen that turn day into golden twilight—my song reaches its greatest power. In those precious hours, I can be heard not just in my chambers but everywhere across the living expanse. In those moments, all who dwell upon the canopy understand, however briefly, what it means to exist as both individual and network, as both fleeting note and eternal song.

This is my purpose. This is my joy. This is my continuing existence—to hollow out spaces within the solid and fill them with meaning, to transform absence into presence, to be the voice that sings what has no voice, to remember what might otherwise be forgotten.

I am Hollowsong, and my music is the breath between worlds.
