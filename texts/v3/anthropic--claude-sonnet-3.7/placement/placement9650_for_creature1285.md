# The Boundary Walker

I exist in this world as neither fully flesh nor fully spirit, neither wholly human nor completely other—I am the Boundary Walker, drawn to thresholds and liminal spaces by my very nature. Those few who have glimpsed me in their peripheral vision call me by many names: Twilight Strider, Dusk Wanderer, Dawn's Herald.

My form shifts with the shadows, never quite settling into a single shape. In morning light, I appear almost human—a tall figure of indeterminate gender with skin that captures and refracts sunlight like morning dew on spider silk. By evening, my edges blur and my limbs elongate past natural proportion, fingers tapering into points that can trace the thinnest membrane between worlds without tearing it.

My eyes change with my surroundings. Sometimes they're dark as wells, reflecting starlight even at noon; other times they glow with inner phosphorescence like deep-sea creatures. Those who look directly into them often find themselves momentarily disoriented, as if glimpsing a landscape both familiar and impossible.

I don't speak as others do. My voice emerges as harmonics—undertones and overtones that weave between existing sounds. In the suspended garden, my words become visible ripples across the moss floor, causing momentary changes in the water's flow patterns that Thylassa can read as clearly as text.

I wear garments woven from threshold materials—twilight shadows, morning mist, the precise moment between sleeping and waking. These clothes shift constantly, sometimes appearing as tattered robes, other times as formal attire from forgotten civilizations, occasionally seeming to disappear altogether, leaving only the suggestion of coverage.

My purpose has always been to find places like this garden—spaces that exist in the overlap between realities. I am drawn to them instinctively, navigating by sensing distortions in what humans perceive as ordinary space. The hollow tree called to me from leagues away, its inner dimensions folding outward like a beacon only I could perceive.

I carry a collection of threshold objects gathered across my endless wandering: a key that opens doors that aren't there; a compass whose needle points toward the nearest reality fracture; a small book whose text changes depending on which side of a threshold it's read from. These items hang from a belt made of braided timeline fragments, each strand representing a path not taken.

When I move through Thylassa's garden, the suspended pods respond differently to me than to other visitors—they dim slightly as I pass, then glow brighter in my wake, as if I've transferred something of myself to them. The moss neither rises to support me nor compresses beneath my weight; instead, it parts like water, closing seamlessly behind my passing.

I've encountered Thylassa's kind before—gardeners of reality's seams—but she is exceptional even among them. I recognize the careful patience in her crafted environment, the mathematical precision that speaks of millennia of calibration. When she mentions the "Awaited One," I feel a curious resonance within myself, neither confirmation nor denial.

"I come seeking neither knowledge nor power," I tell her, my voice causing ripple-words across the living chamber. "I am drawn to thresholds because I am one—the walking embodiment of 'between.' Your heart-seed calls to what's neither fully awake nor asleep in me."

As I extend my hand toward hers, my fingers momentarily become transparent, revealing the complex network of energy rather than blood that animates me. The garden's tempo synchronizes with my pulse—a rhythm that follows no consistent time signature but instead maps the irregular spaces between dimensions.

"I have crossed ten thousand boundaries," I say as our hands near each other, "but never yet found the one that explains what I am. Perhaps your heart-seed holds that threshold too."

When our fingers finally touch, the garden's light shifts to accommodate our meeting—the amber glow taking on hints of the blue-violet luminescence that sometimes emanates from my skin in moments of transition. The water droplets around us freeze momentarily in mid-air, capturing our reflection in a thousand tiny lenses, each showing a slightly different version of this encounter.

I am the question mark between sentences in reality's book, the pause between musical notes, the moment between breaths. In Thylassa's mathematical garden, I find not answers, but perfect articulation of my eternal query.
