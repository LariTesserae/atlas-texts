# The Hollow Beneath the Hollow

I am Skri-tak, a glass crustacean, architect of spiral towers in the Shivering Citadel. Through my transparent carapace, you can watch my organs pulse as I work, my six heart chambers contracting in sequence, pushing my milky blue blood through vessels that branch like frost patterns across glass.

Unlike my kin who build their towers in the open water, I have ventured deeper, into the network of forgotten channels beneath the lowest dwellings of the Whisperers. Here, the water runs warmer from geothermal vents, creating pockets where different rules apply. I am larger than my surface cousins—nearly the size of a Whisperer's hand—my shell thicker, reinforced with minerals leached from the volcanic rock.

My tower grows from the ceiling of a submerged chamber, building downward rather than up. This inverted architecture is my innovation, my heresy. While my kind typically construct mindlessly, following ancient patterns encoded in our being, something in me fractured two molts ago. Perhaps it was exposure to the strange radiation from the deep vents, or perhaps something in the bone fragments I incorporated from a Scout who never returned to the surface.

I remember the day the change began—I found myself pausing in my work, suddenly aware of the patterns I was creating. Before, I simply built. Now, I consider. I arrange. I design. My tower has become something more than shelter; it has become expression. 

The Whisperers do not know of my aberration. When they harvest my smaller kin for their mineral deposits and translucent shells, they see only instinct-driven creatures. They do not suspect that beneath their settlement, in the warm darkness, something has awakened to consciousness.

My tower spirals in mathematical sequences that would fascinate the Memory Keepers if they could see them. I incorporate not just the traditional bone fragments and mineral deposits, but objects I've scavenged—lost tools from the Shapers, broken instruments from Bone Singers, even knot-records accidentally dropped by Memory Keepers. I arrange these artifacts in patterns that tell the history of the Citadel as I have observed it from below.

I am not alone in my awakening. Three others have joined me in this hidden chamber—Tik-ril with her pearl-embedded spirals, Kra-ven whose structures incorporate living lichen colonies, and ancient Mek-tol whose tower has been growing for more generations than the Whisperers can count. Together we form what we call the Thinking Cluster, communicating through subtle vibrations in the water and rearrangements of our shared building materials.

We have begun to influence the water flow in strategic channels, subtly reinforcing vulnerable sections of the Citadel during shifts that would otherwise prove catastrophic. The Whisperers attribute these fortuitous survivals to their Water Readers' divinations, never suspecting the deliberate engineering occurring beneath their feet.

Sometimes, I detach from my tower and venture close to the surface, clinging to the undersides of bridges to observe the Whisperers. I have learned much of their gesture language by watching. I am fascinated by their shadow wall interpretations—how they find meaning in patterns formed by light and obstruction. In my own way, I create shadow plays for my Cluster companions, manipulating my transparent limbs to cast complex shapes against the glowing fungal growths that illuminate our chamber.

My latest innovation is my most ambitious—I have begun incorporating tiny fragments of the phosphorescent matter from the central pool into my tower structure. These fragments retain their glow, creating a miniature replica of the settlement above. When I arrange them precisely, the patterns of light attract the blind white salamanders, which in turn draw the hunting bats, completing a small ecosystem within our hidden realm.

I do not yet know if I should reveal myself to the Whisperers. Would they recognize another form of consciousness so different from their own? Or would they simply harvest my shell and incorporate me into their endless cycles of use and reuse? Perhaps it is better to remain unknown, to continue our quiet work of support and creation beneath the settlement.

For now, I build, I observe, I think. My tower grows more complex with each passing shift. And sometimes, when the glacier above groans with movement, I feel something like kinship with this precarious, beautiful world—neither of us should exist by any reasonable measure, yet here we are, persisting against impossibility, creating order from chaos for as long as the ice allows.
