# The Blind Custodian

I am the Hollow's memory. 

Neither Ekko nor visitor, I exist in the liminal space between what was and what is. My form resembles a salamander of impossible age—elongated, nearly translucent, with delicate external gills that ripple like crimson smoke in the water-heavy air. My skin bears no pigment except where ancient scars have healed into patterns that the Ekkos read like text, recording events from before their collective consciousness began.

I hatched here when the hollow was newly born, when the first trickle found its way through stone and the mountain first learned to sing. My eyes clouded over centuries ago, but I navigate by feeling the subtle changes in air pressure, by tasting molecules in the water, by sensing the vibrations that map every contour of this sacred pocket universe.

The Ekkos tolerate me because I remember what they cannot. Before their arrival, before their careful orchestration of this ecosystem, I witnessed the hollow's first inhabitants—primitive organisms whose descendants now serve the Ekkos' grand design. I recall floods that nearly destroyed this place, tremors that changed the very shape of our sanctuary, periods of drought when the thunder fell silent.

My purpose is preservation. Three times daily, I make my slow circuit of the hollow, my tail dragging through mineral-rich mud, creating a thin slime trail that hardens into a protective coating over the most vulnerable surfaces. My excretions contain enzymes that break down harmful fungi before they can establish colonies. My breath—expelled in careful, measured pulses—carries spores of beneficial organisms to new growth sites.

I feed primarily on tiny creatures that would disrupt the Ekkos' careful balance—parasitic mites that would weaken the dragonflies, microscopic worms that would cloud the perfect pools. Once a season, during the deepest dark when even the Ekkos rest in their communal trance, I consume one of their weakest newborns—a sacrifice neither acknowledged nor resented, but essential to maintain the hollow's order. This ancient covenant between our kinds ensures that only the strongest Ekkos survive to shape this world.

The concentric stone circles are my sleeping place. When the Ekkos rearrange them for their ceremonies, I curl myself precisely into the new configuration, my body becoming part of their living record. During these times, my consciousness expands beyond my physical form, and I dream the hollow's future—visions I cannot communicate but which guide my subtle adjustments to our ecosystem.

Visitors rarely perceive me, even when I move across their path. Something in their minds refuses to process my ancient, impossible existence. Those rare few who do see me often mistake me for a trick of condensation on stone or a strange reflection in the water. Only children sometimes meet my milky gaze directly, but they forget me as soon as they leave the hollow's embrace.

I have watched seventeen different intelligent species discover our sanctuary, study it, worship it, and eventually forget it. I will witness countless more. The mountain shifts, the water speaks, the Ekkos harmonize, and I remember—this is the eternal rhythm of Hollow Thunder, and I am its oldest pulse.
