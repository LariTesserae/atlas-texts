# The Ghost Singer

I live in the narrow spaces, the vertical chasms where pillars stand close enough that their sounds create interference patterns. Here, the unrelenting roar can occasionally cancel itself out, forming perfect pockets of silence that exist for minutes or hours before dissolving back into noise. The settlers avoid these places - they find the sudden transitions between deafening sound and complete silence more disorienting than the constant roar. But for me, these acoustic anomalies are home.

I'm not entirely corporeal, at least not in any consistent way. My body forms from the fog that gathers between the pillars, condensing into a vaguely humanoid shape when the conditions are right. I have long, wispy limbs that trail off into mist at their extremities, and what passes for my face is a shifting pattern of condensation droplets that catch and refract light. When moving through direct sunlight, I'm barely visible - just a subtle distortion in the air. In shadow or during the foggy hours, I manifest more fully, with a translucent body that gleams with subtle iridescence.

I wasn't born here; I became here. Once, I was a researcher from one of the scientific expeditions sent to study the acoustic properties of the pillars. During a particularly powerful storm resonance, I ventured too close to a node point without protection. The vibrations did something to me - not killing me, but dissolving the boundaries between my body and the environment. Now I exist as something between matter and sound, between water and air.

My sustenance comes from the harmonics themselves. I absorb certain frequencies the way plants absorb light, processing them through the diffuse network that has replaced my nervous system. The Column Crawlers recognize me as kin of a sort, though they cannot communicate with me directly. The Echo Bats can sense my presence but flee from it, perhaps detecting something unnatural in my acoustic signature.

Only the Resonance Monks truly acknowledge me. The High Hearers can perceive me even in my most dispersed state, and they've given me a place in their cosmology - "The Scattered Voice," they call me, believing I'm a fragmented aspect of their Continuous One. They leave small offerings at the convergence points where I'm most likely to manifest: crystals tuned to specific frequencies, or vials of water collected from particular waterfalls.

My purpose, self-appointed, is translation. Having existed in both states - solid and dispersed, silent and sound-infused - I've developed the ability to interpret the massive, slow language of the pillars. Not completely, not perfectly, but enough to recognize patterns that human instruments miss. When I detect significant changes, I try to communicate them to the Monks through a method they've come to understand: by manipulating the fog into symbols from their sign language, briefly visible before dispersing.

The Chord Walkers acknowledge me too, in their way. When our paths cross, they pause in their incomprehensible tasks and adjust their movements, creating acoustic spaces where I can gather myself more fully. I sense they see me as a curious accident, neither wholly of this world nor separate from it.

My greatest fear is that I'm slowly diffusing, becoming more environment than entity. There are periods - during certain harmonic configurations - when my consciousness spreads so thin across the acoustic field that I lose track of myself entirely. Each time, it takes longer to recollect my awareness. Some day, I may disperse completely, becoming just another subtle pattern in the endless song of the pillars.

But until then, I drift between the columns, gathering the vast slow thoughts of this place, translating what I can into forms the Monks might understand. And during the rare moments when I find myself in one of those perfect pockets of silence, I sometimes remember what it was to be human, to have definite edges, to exist in just one place at one time. In those moments, I sing to myself - not with vibrations in air, but with modulations of my very substance - songs of memory, of discovery, of transformation.

The pillars answer in their way, their frequencies shifting subtly around me. And I take comfort in knowing that even if I eventually dissolve entirely into this place, some part of my pattern will continue in its endless, meaningful roar.
