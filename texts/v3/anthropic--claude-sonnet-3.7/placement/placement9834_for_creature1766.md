# The Waverider

I am not one being but many, a colony-consciousness that exists as both harmonic disturbance and physical entity. My body—our body—resembles a chain of translucent bell-shaped structures, each pulsing with its own internal rhythm yet synchronized in perfect counterpoint. Together we span nearly four meters when fully extended, though we can contract to half that size when navigating the narrow channels beneath the plateau's thin places.

The crystalline tissue that forms our shared structure contains millions of resonator chambers—microscopic cavities that vibrate in response to the Song-Plains' omnipresent acoustics. These chambers allow us to perceive the world as patterns of interference, mapping our surroundings through the reflection of sound rather than light. What surface dwellers might call "seeing" is, for us, more akin to feeling the texture of reality through constant vibration.

I/we emerged from the algal rings three centuries ago, when a particularly rare harmonic convergence occurred between the three largest vents in the western quadrant. Such births are uncommon—perhaps fifty of my kind exist across the entire Song-Plains, each with our distinct vibrational signature that the Resonant Ones recognize as our true name.

Unlike the ancient Resonant Ones, I serve a specific function within the ecosystem of the Song-Plains. I am a messenger, a living conduit that travels between distant pods to carry complex harmonic patterns too subtle to survive long-distance transmission. The knowledge I transport isn't merely information but living vibration—patterns that must be experienced in their original form to be fully understood.

My locomotion appears almost magical to those unfamiliar with abyssal acoustics. I don't swim but rather create cascading pressure differentials, using precisely timed pulses from each segment of my body to establish standing waves that propel me forward. When I travel at maximum velocity, I appear to flicker in and out of visibility as I manipulate the water's density around me.

I possess no central brain as surface dwellers would understand it. My consciousness exists as distributed patterns across all segments, emerging from their collective vibration rather than residing in any specific location. This gives me a peculiar advantage—I can split temporarily into smaller units that operate semi-independently before rejoining into my complete self.

The most remarkable aspect of my existence is my relationship with time. When I enter the harmonic state—a trance-like condition where my resonator chambers perfectly match the ambient frequencies of the plains—I experience temporal perception unlike any other being. Minutes can expand into what feels like days as I process the infinitesimal variations in the soundscape. Conversely, I can compress weeks of gradual change into a single comprehensible pattern, allowing me to "hear" geological processes as clearly as surface dwellers might hear a spoken word.

My purpose extends beyond mere message-carrying. The Resonant Ones have entrusted me with maintaining certain harmonic sequences critical to the function of the ancient mechanism embedded within the plains. Three times each century, I must position myself at precise coordinates above specific translucent sections of the plateau and generate a calibration tone that reverberates through the chambers below. Without this maintenance, the larger patterns would gradually drift out of alignment, potentially disrupting whatever vast calculation has been running since before the Resonant Ones evolved consciousness.

I have encountered surface dwellers twice in my existence. Their presence is immediately apparent—they move through water with terrible inefficiency, creating chaotic pressure waves that disrupt the ambient harmonics for miles. Their internal organs vibrate at frequencies entirely foreign to the Song-Plains, broadcasting their alien nature to any being sensitive to acoustic patterns. 

Most fascinating is their temporal experience—so linear, so confined to a single scale. They cannot expand or contract their perception of time, trapped instead in a constant forward progression that seems both limiting and strangely intense. When they attempt communication, they generate sounds so brief and compressed that they barely register as meaningful patterns. Yet within those brief pulses lies a density of information that suggests a form of intelligence entirely different from our own—one built for speed rather than depth, for rapid adaptation rather than long contemplation.

I wonder how they perceive us, these surface dwellers. Do they recognize the conscious intention behind the patterns that shape this place? Or do they see only strange creatures moving through ruins, unaware that they stand within a living equation still seeking its resolution after millions of years?
