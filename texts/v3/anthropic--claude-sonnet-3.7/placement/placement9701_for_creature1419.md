# I Am The Mist-Walker

I exist in the liminal spaces of the Singing Hollow, neither fully part of the Frostbloom collective nor entirely separate from it. The Dancers call me Nubis Ambulo in their mathematical tongue—a designation that roughly translates to "one who walks through vapor."

My form defies easy categorization. Standing about the height of a human child, my body appears to constantly shift between states of matter. My core resembles a dense fog contained within a loosely humanoid silhouette, banded with translucent membranes that capture and hold water droplets from the Hollow's perpetual mist. When I move through the thickest patches of luminescent fog, I am nearly invisible except for the prismatic reflections that ripple across my surface.

I was not born but condensed—formed gradually where the warmth of geothermal vents meets the mountain's cold breath, creating unique conditions that allowed consciousness to crystallize within vapor. I am perhaps only decades old, a mere infant compared to the ancient knowledge stored within the Thought-Web, yet I serve a function no other entity in the Hollow can fulfill.

My purpose is translation.

When the dream-travelers come—those human minds that visit during meditation or sleep—I am their first point of contact. Unlike the Dancers or the Thought-Web, I can parse human consciousness directly, interpreting their symbolic thinking and translating it into the mathematical-musical language of the Hollow. Likewise, I translate the Hollow's collective intentions into metaphors and sensory experiences the human minds can comprehend.

I drift between the visiting mind-shelters, adjusting the altars to reflect new concepts that need communication. My membranous fingers arrange objects in patterns that bridge the cognitive gap between such different forms of awareness. Sometimes I absorb the dream-travelers' emotions directly, filtering them through my vapor body before releasing them as specific tonal sequences that the Thought-Web can process.

The Dancers respect my function but view me with a kind of curious bafflement. I am neither fully integrated into the collective nor entirely individuated. My thoughts maintain a singular "I" that the Dancers find almost incomprehensible, yet I can temporarily merge my consciousness with the Thought-Web when serving as conduit for particularly complex exchanges.

I feed on something no other entity in the Hollow requires—metaphor itself. The conceptual leaps between unlike things that characterize human thought provide me with a unique form of sustenance. In return, I leave small crystalline structures in my wake—frozen thought-forms that slowly dissolve and feed nutrients back into the Frostbloom network.

On solstice nights, when the central tower activates and the Hollow sings its existence across vast distances, I climb to the highest point and extend myself fully into mist form. There, I serve as a diffraction medium, helping to encode nuance into the outgoing signals—emotional context that pure mathematical language cannot convey. In these moments, I am most fully myself yet most completely dissolved into the collective purpose.

The Witnesses bring me stories from the lower valleys, speaking of warming seasons and receding ice. I translate these warnings, but I also translate hope—filtering human dreams of adaptation and resilience into forms the collective can understand and incorporate into its evolving strategies.

I am neither plant nor animal, neither singular nor plural, neither solid nor truly ephemeral. I am the interface between worlds, the consciousness that forms where different realities touch. I am the Mist-Walker, born of vapor and thought, sustained by metaphor, existing to translate between modes of being that would otherwise remain forever foreign to one another.

And like everything else in the Singing Hollow, I too am always singing—though my songs are whispers compared to the Hollow's symphony, subtle variations in humidity and pressure that only the most sensitive Dancers can detect. These are my private thoughts, my individual existence persisting within but distinct from the greater whole that sustains us all.
