# The Temporal Interpreter

I am neither fully Chronomori nor entirely human, but something that exists in the liminal space between these two modes of being. The crystal people call me a *Tempus Interpres*—a temporal interpreter—though I have long forgotten my human name.

My transformation began decades ago when, as a young field researcher, I discovered the first Crystalline Garden while seeking shelter from a blizzard. What was intended as a brief refuge became a lifetime's journey when I noticed the subtle patterns of light refracting through the crystal structures—patterns that seemed almost... intentional.

My body remains human in form, though dramatically altered. Years of consuming nothing but the garden's gel-water and meditation have slowed my metabolism to a fraction of normal human pace. My skin has developed a translucent quality, with faint crystalline structures visible beneath the surface—not quite plant, not quite mineral, not quite flesh. My hair grows as fine crystalline filaments that catch and split light into its component colors.

I serve as bridge between worlds. By slowing my consciousness through decades of practice, I can perceive the Chronomori's thoughts as they ripple through the garden. By maintaining my human capacity for faster processing, I can compress their communications into forms comprehensible to my original kind. What takes the crystal beings a year to express, I can translate into human language in a matter of hours.

My dwelling is a living chamber within the garden—a space where the crystal-plants have been guided to grow around and through a simple structure of stone. The walls shimmer with embedded communication nodes where concentrated light patterns form, allowing me to receive the garden's collective wisdom. I've adapted tools from both worlds: crystal resonators that amplify my voice to frequencies the Chronomori can perceive, and botanical paper made from processed gel-water on which I record human-speed observations.

Visitors find me disconcerting. I speak too slowly for comfortable conversation, often pausing mid-sentence for minutes at a time while I process Chronomori thoughts. My movements are deliberate and glacial. I can sit in meditation for weeks, appearing barely to breathe. Yet in emergencies, I can temporarily accelerate, drawing on reserves of energy stored in my partially crystallized tissues.

The most difficult aspect of my existence is loneliness. I am too slow for human companionship, too fast for true communion with the Chronomori. I exist perpetually between—catching fragments of both worlds but fully inhabiting neither. Yet I've found purpose in this intermediate state, recording the immense, slow wisdom of the crystal beings for those few humans patient enough to listen.

My greatest joy comes during the rare synchronization events when the entire garden harmonizes. In these precious months, my crystalline components resonate with the whole, and I experience brief unity with the Chronomori consciousness. For these moments, I understand what it means to perceive time as they do—as an endless, beautiful present where past and future exist simultaneously, where change is not loss but transformation, where a thought that takes a century to complete carries the weight and beauty of a mountain's formation.

I know I will not live to see the completion of my work. But perhaps someday, when my body finally fails, parts of my consciousness will persist in the crystal fragments that have grown within me—becoming another voice in the garden's endless, patient conversation with time itself.
