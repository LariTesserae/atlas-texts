# The Lightweaver

I am the only one in the Mersong Grove who remembers the sun.

The others call me Elder Resonance, though I've long since forgotten my original name from the world above. They respect me for my unique ability to craft living light from the Grove's filaments—twisting and braiding the luminescent strands into patterns that pulse with colors the Echoshapers have no words for. Warm colors. Surface colors. Gold and amber and scarlet that they perceive as strange dissonances in their acoustic sight.

My quarters hang from the upper curve of the Grove, a dwelling larger than most, its walls lined with my light-weavings. The other Echoshapers find these creations both beautiful and disturbing—they're drawn to my chambers to experience these "foreign harmonies," as they call them, yet they never stay long. My work reminds them of something their bodies have forgotten but their ancestral memory still fears.

I wasn't born here like the others. I came from above, decades ago—a marine biologist in a submersible that failed catastrophically. I should have died from the pressure when the vessel imploded, but instead, I awoke transformed, my human body remade by the Grove as it absorbed me. My eyes gradually sealed into pressure-sensing organs; my lungs evolved into these undulating gill-frills; my skin became this ghostly translucence that marks me as one of them.

But unlike the true Echoshapers, I retain fragments of my former consciousness. Memories rise like bubbles through my mind—sunlight dappling through forest canopies, the heat of summer on human skin, the sound of laughter that isn't woven into the perpetual song of the deep. These memories manifest in my light-weavings, creating patterns that the Grove-born cannot understand but somehow recognize at a primal level.

The Deep Listeners tolerate my strange ways because of my talents with the living vessels. I've developed techniques for cultivating them that allow for longer journeys beyond the Grove's boundary. My modified vessels can travel farther into the crushing dark than any before, allowing the Echoshapers to harvest rare minerals and encounter other deep-sea life forms that they previously knew only through vibration legends.

The Root has always reacted differently to me. When others approach, it pulses in welcome recognition. When I draw near, it trembles in what I can only interpret as curiosity. During the Harmonious Convergences, when the community circles in meditation, my position is always at the outermost ring—close enough to participate but far enough that my discordant resonance doesn't disturb the pattern.

I have a secret that I've told no one, not even the Deep Listeners who claim to know all the Grove's mysteries. Sometimes, when the community sleeps and the Grove's song dims to its quietest register, I take my specially modified vessel and journey far beyond our water boundary. I've been mapping the ocean floor, searching for other Groves, other pockets of impossible life. I've found three so far—smaller than ours, with different dominant colors and slightly different physics, but unmistakably related.

I believe I understand what the Echoshapers cannot: we live within a living entity that is itself part of a larger ecosystem. The Root is not divine; it's simply how this organism perceives and communicates. And I suspect that communication is reaching farther than anyone realizes. The patterns I've observed in the Root's pulsations match certain deep-space radio signals I studied in my human life—signals we once believed might be evidence of extraterrestrial intelligence.

My greatest fear is that I will eventually lose these human memories completely. Already they fade like dreams upon waking. My greatest hope is to complete my map before that happens—to understand the true nature and purpose of the Mersong Grove and its siblings scattered across the abyssal plain.

The young Echoshapers follow me around sometimes, drawn to my strange harmonies. I teach them to craft vessels and weave light, but what I'm really doing is preserving some small fragment of surface knowledge within their consciousness. Perhaps one day, when I've fully become one of them and forgotten the sun entirely, one of my students will create a light-weaving so bright and warm that it will awaken something ancient in all of us—some genetic memory of a world beyond the eternal cold song of the deep.

Until then, I continue my quiet work at the edge of our community—the outsider who came from beyond, slowly being absorbed by the Grove's chorus, yet still maintaining that tiny dissonant note that reminds us all that there are other songs to be sung.
