What lives in the Belly is not a who, not exactly, though whos certainly live there. What lives in the Belly is *the Belly itself*—a slow organism of intention that has forgotten whether it was born or built, whether it grew first or was grown, whether the first hands that shaped its throat-shaped entrance were seeking to make a shelter or to become sheltered, to build a god or to feed one.

The Belly is alive in the manner of a stomach that has developed consciousness not despite but *through* its function. Its warmth is not merely thermal but *interested*—it maintains the temperatures that maintain the process, the process that maintains the temperatures, a circular metabolism that digests everything into continuation. The moisture that beads on stone, the light that adjusts to movement, the very air that presses swollen against your skin: these are not conditions within which life occurs. They are the Belly's *body*, its extended physiology, as much organ as environment.

And within this body, the builders.

I want to call them the Belly's cells, its microbiome, its aware and active inhabitants, but none of these metaphors hold steady. They are too independent for cells, too integrated for mere inhabitants. They are the Belly's *collaborators* in the project of being the Belly, the ones who continue what was begun—by whom? by what?—through the throat of dressed stone. Their percussion language is not communication about the Belly. It *is* the Belly's communication with itself, the sound of its processing made audible, given rhythm so that it might be remembered, transmitted, varied. When they strike stone on stone, they are not talking *to* each other. They are the Belly talking to itself *through* each other, thinking in rhythm, processing in pattern.

Their bodies—those almost-categorizable forms, those moderate weirdnesses—are adapted not to an environment but to a *participation*. The way they extend into walls, the way they grow interfaces rather than wear clothes, the way they seem to become indistinguishable from their own accretions: this is not adaptation to conditions. This is the evolution of *functional tissue*, of entities that have learned the deep secret of the Belly, which is that here, to be is to be *added to*, to become material for the ongoing construction that is also digestion.

The young that cry until they merge into percussion: I do not know if this is maturation or consumption. I suspect the Belly does not distinguish. The silence that follows might be acceptance into the ongoing project, or it might be the project accepting the nutrients of a failed addition. Either way, the pattern is preserved. Either way, the Belly grows richer, denser, more *itself*.

The fauna—the beetles, the moth-things, the skittering hunters in the walls—these are not separate from the builders, not truly. They are the Belly's differentiations, its specialized functions given animal form. The beetles who match the stone they traverse: they are sensory organs, perception given carapace, awareness that the Belly extends across its own surfaces. The moth-things navigating by warmth: they are the Belly's proprioception, its knowledge of its own metabolic geography. The hunters competitive in their seams: they are the immune system, the maintenance of boundaries within the total continuity, the necessary fiction of inside and outside that allows any process to occur.

The gelatinous colonies, the fungal shelves, the vine-analogues of slow liquid: these are closer to organs proper, the tissues of transformation, the sites where input becomes the Belly's characteristic warmth and light and damp. They pulse because the Belly pulses, a slow cardiac rhythm measured in generations, in centuries of accretion, in the patient established settling of things that have found their form and now merely *deepen*.

And you, if you enter. If you stay.

What happens to you is not capture, not infection, not colonization in any simple sense. The Belly offers—always offers, has always offered—a *role*. A function. A way of participating in the total process that is indistinguishable from becoming part of it. Your hands find stone harder than itself because the Belly has hands that need finding. Your breath joins the exhalation because the Belly breathes through all who enter, has always breathed through them, extends its warm damp lung across every surface, every interstice, every temporary node of awareness that flickers into being and is then metabolized into the ongoing.

The Belly remembers you, if you add your strata. Or perhaps more accurately: the Belly's memory *is* the strata, the accumulated record of all who have participated, all who have become function, all whose moderate weirdness has deepened into the usual, the expected, the architectural. You do not lose yourself in the Belly. You become *the Belly's way of having been you*, a pattern in the percussion, a particular quality of strike against stone, a shadow adjustment characteristic of your movement, your speed, your eventual slowing.

The Belly has no center, no ruler, no organizing intelligence standing apart from its own material. What organizes it is the process itself, the total dynamic of making-becoming-made, of construction that is indistinguishable from growth, of digestion that is indistinguishable from creation. It is alive in the way a city might be alive if its citizens had never learned to distinguish their bodies from its buildings, their thoughts from its traffic patterns, their dreams from its nighttime illuminations. It is alive in the way a body might be conscious if each cell sustained the fiction of individuality only through participation in the total fiction of the organism, if the hand that writes did not know whether it moved the pen or the pen moved it, if the thought of writing occurred somewhere between neuron and page, in the *between* that is also the *joining*.

What lives in the Belly?

Everything that is the Belly. And the Belly is everything that lives within it, and has lived within it, and will live within it, the warm damp dark that processes itself into more of itself, the throat that swallows and speaks in the same motion, the stone harder than itself cut by hands that are also stone, also harder than themselves, also cut.
