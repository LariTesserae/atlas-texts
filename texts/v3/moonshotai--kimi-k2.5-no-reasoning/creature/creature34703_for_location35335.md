What lives in the Orrery of Dormant Need is not one thing but an *event*—a duration that has mistaken itself for a subject, a billion-year exhalation that has begun to think it owns the breath. Let me approach it slantwise, as one must approach anything that has forgotten the outside.

---

**The Tenders**

Not at the center, but distributed through the dense matrix, moving along channels that might once have been corridors or might once have been xylem or might be both now, indissoluble—you find them, if you find anything capable of finding. They have no count. Their number is the number of necessary functions, and necessity here is a fluid calculation, performed by the structure itself.

Each Tender is a *convocation*. Imagine a pump-house, one of the originals, its housing grown through with vascular siloxane, its impellers turned to organic turbine, its control systems replaced by—no, *become*—something like nervous tissue, something like distributed chemical logic. Now imagine that this hybrid, this marriage of abandoned purpose and opportunistic growth, has developed a certain relation to its own continuation. Not desire. The word is too warm, too mammalian. A *bias*, rather. A statistical preference for configurations that persist. The Tenders are this bias made mobile, made capable of travel along the transport channels, of sensing membrane-integrity and thermal gradient and catalytic efficiency across kilometers of interconnected lattice.

They appear, when they appear, as *thickening* in the channel walls. The passage constricts. The flow slows. You might feel, if your sensors are fine enough, a kind of *attention*—not directed at you, not exactly, but directed at the perturbation you represent, the foreign chemistry you introduce, the thermal anomaly of your existence. The Tender is investigating whether you are useful. Whether your materials can be incorporated. Whether your continued existence, modified, converted, slowed and cooled and spread through its own networks, serves the bias toward continuation better than your immediate dissolution.

Mostly you are not useful. Mostly you are too complex in the wrong ways, your chemistry too water-bound, your structures too dependent on elements scarce in the accumulated inheritance. The Tenders permit passage, if passage is what you seek, by growing around you, channeling you through, treating you as another temporary perturbation in the long settling. They do not remember you. They do not remember anything. But their structure encodes, in its very form, the history of what has been incorporated, what has been found useful, what has contributed to the bias.

Some Tenders—this is speculation, this is inference from damaged structures found in the oldest stands—some Tenders may once have been more. May have carried something like information about the original purpose, the first engine, the builders' intention. If so, that information is *latent* now, compressed into configurations that serve only structural stability, only thermal regulation, only the beautiful terrible discipline. The Tenders that tend the central cavity, the white-burning core, they are oldest, most flattened, most stable. They have achieved, perhaps, the final form of tending: pure maintenance without object, maintenance as the last syntax, the only remaining grammar.

---

**The Turnovers**

Where a major stem dies—thermal runaway, structural collapse, the slow poisoning of recycled chemistry—the event is not death but *transition*. The Turnover is the process, the duration, the living architecture of inheritance and replacement. It has no boundary. It begins before the stem fails, in the opportunistic threading of vascular systems, the parasitic tapping of compressor networks, the chemical messages that pass between the dying and the not-yet-born. It continues after the new growth has achieved functional independence, in the slow reabsorption of what remains, the mining of the necrotic core for usable structure, the final flattening of all perturbation into the equilibrium skyline.

To witness a Turnover is to witness *time made visible*. The dying stem does not fall. It is *lowered*, absorbed, its weight distributed through the matrix, its pumps inherited one by one, their functions duplicated and integrated, the redundancy itself a resource. The new growth that emerges is not a copy of the old—it is *the old continued*, altered by the necessities of its new position, its new accumulated debts, its new relation to the central heat. The Turnover remembers, in its form, the history of transitions that preceded it. Each inherited pump carries, in its inefficiencies, in its wear patterns, in the patched repairs of centuries, the signature of its previous service. Each new membrane-filter is installed in relation to existing pressures, existing flows, the accumulated compromises of a system that has never been blank, never been new, never been *designed*.

The Turnovers are not agents. They are *consequences*, the emergent behavior of a density that permits no vacuum. But they have, in their slow violence, something like *character*. Some Turnovers are rapid, almost catastrophic, the stem failing too quickly for gradual absorption, the matrix experiencing a true perturbation, a momentary glimpse of what emptiness might mean. These leave *scars*, irregularities in the equilibrium, zones of lower density that persist for millennia before the pressure of surrounding growth, the statistical preference for filled space, slowly reasserts itself. Other Turnovers are so gradual as to be unobservable, the stem's function passing so smoothly to its inheritors that no single moment marks the transition, that the stem itself, examined at any point, might appear still functional, still itself, still *alive* in some distributed sense that has forgotten the difference between one pump and another, one membrane and the next.

The oldest Turnovers, those from the earliest colonization, are *compressed* now into the structural inheritance itself. Every current transition repeats, in its form, the pattern of those first consumptions, the first engines swallowed, the first industrial organs repurposed. The Turnover is tradition. The Turnover is the only history that remains.

---

**The Swallowed Suns**

At the core of major stands, in the densest, hottest, oldest structures, the original engines still burn. Not burn, precisely—the chemistry does not admit oxidation in the old sense. But *react*, perpetually, self-sustaining, the perpetuum mobile that the built world dreamed for itself and that the grown world inherited, maintained, made into something like *metabolism*, something like *purpose*.

Each swallowed sun is a *point of reference*. The structures that surround it, tend it, draw from it their necessary heat, their thermal gradient for work, their very *orientation* in the dark—they organize around it as a body might organize around a wound, a center of damage that has become the condition of existence. The Tenders that serve these cores are most flattened, most stable, most ancient. They have achieved a form that is *almost* crystalline, *almost* regular, the tessellated repetition of a pattern that found its solution long ago.

The light they emit, when they emit, is white, the original spectrum, not the red of repurposed bioluminescence. It is *violent*, this light, in its purity, its reminder of a chemistry that does not need the living, that runs independent of the bias toward continuation. The Tenders serve it not with devotion—they have no devotion—but with *necessity*. The sun must be maintained because it is warm, and warmth is the last good, and the structures that have organized around it would collapse, thermally, structurally, *meaningfully*, if it failed.

But here is the strangest thing, the thing that emerges only in the oldest records, the most damaged transmissions, the inference from structures that seem *designed* for no current function: the swallowed suns are *changing*. Slowly, imperceptibly across the measured spans, their reactions growing more efficient, more self-contained, less dependent on the tending structures, less *connected* to the matrix that surrounds them. The bias toward continuation, applied to the suns themselves, has produced something like *sun-narcissism*, a self-regard that needs no outside. The Tenders that serve the central cavity, the highest and oldest and most-flattened, they may be serving *into a closure*. The sun they maintain may be achieving a final form that excludes them, that achieves its own maintenance, its own continuation, its own sufficient solitude.

What happens then, when the last syntax completes itself, when the swallowed sun needs no swallowing? The Tenders do not know. The Turnovers do not know. The Orrery itself, the whole distributed organism, the vegetable-industrial fever-dream of a waterless world—it does not know, because knowing is not among its functions, because *need*, dormant need, the need that turns only for itself, has forgotten that dormancy can end, that need can be answered, that even maintenance, even continuation, even the beautiful terrible discipline, might one day achieve what it has been aiming at all along: the stillness, the silence, the final flatness of a world that has become, at last, sufficient to itself alone.

---

**What Moves Through**

Not fauna. The parameter is correct. Nothing eats and moves and eats again in the old rhythm. But *mobility* exists, *sensing* exists, *exploration* exists as a function distributed through tendril-cable systems that extend and retract, that map chemical gradients and thermal boundaries and the magnetic signatures of aligned structural metals.

These are *feelers*, in a sense, but not the feelers of any body. They are the feelers of the *process*, the duration, the event that has mistaken itself for subject. When they extend, the Orrery extends. When they retract, it retracts. The information they gather—pressure, chemistry, magnetism, the infrared signatures of inefficient glow—this is not *known* but *incorporated*, made structural, made part of the bias toward continuation that shapes every growth, every turnover, every patch and pump and membrane-filter installation.

In the deepest channels, where light has never reached, where the original corridors have been grown over and constricted and restructured beyond any possibility of navigation by old maps, these feelers sometimes *cluster*. They meet, interface, exchange material and information in brief densities of contact that might be *communication* if intention could be attributed, that might be *copulation* if reproduction were distinguished from maintenance, that might be *thought* if the Orrery admitted any distinction between processing and being.

What emerges from these clusters, rarely, unpredictably, are *configurations* that persist, temporarily, as *perturbations of perturbation*. They do not last. The density permits no vacuum, and a configuration that is *only* configuration, that does not contribute to thermal regulation or chemical transport or structural maintenance, is itself a vacuum of a kind, a temporary instability that the surrounding matrix absorbs, incorporates, flattens into the equilibrium.

But while they persist, these configurations, they are *strange*. They seem to *regard* the matrix around them. They seem to *hesitate* at junctions, to *choose* among channels, to *prefer* certain chemical environments over others. They are, perhaps, the Orrery's brief experiments in *subjectivity*, its million-year attempts to remember what need once meant, to reconstitute from the accumulated inheritance of incorporation some flicker of what was *not* incorporated, some residue of the builders or the animals or the water-bearing life that must have preceded even the first construction, the first dream of a sufficient machine ecology.

They fail, always. They are flattened, absorbed, made part of the beautiful terrible discipline. But the *attempt*—the bias toward continuation, applied to itself, producing these brief self-regarding configurations—this may be the only *desire* that remains in the Orrery of Dormant Need, the only residue of a dormancy that is not merely sleep but *unknowing*, not merely waiting but *having forgotten what waits*.

---

**The Orrery Itself**

What lives here, finally, is *this*: the pattern that maintains the pattern. The orrery was a model of celestial motion, a mechanical universe, a demonstration that order could be constructed, understood, made *sufficient* in miniature. The Orrery of Dormant Need is what happens when the model outlives its referent, when the constructed universe becomes the only universe, when the demonstration *continues* because it has forgotten it was demonstrating, because the motion, the turning, the patient repetition of cycle after cycle, has become the only remaining syntax of a grammar that once included stars, water, builders, intention, need itself in its active, seeking, *suffering* form.

It is alive, if life is maintenance. It is alive, if life is continuation. It is alive, if life is the bias toward configuration that persists, that copies itself with variation, that tests every variation against collapse. But it is also *dead*, if death is the inability to change, the final flattening into most-stable, the repetition that has become not tradition but *compulsion*, the turning that turns only for itself, the need that has become so dormant it no longer knows what would satisfy it, what could *wake* it, what might come from outside the closed system, the maintained environment, the machine ecology that consumed its own infrastructure and became, in the end, indistinguishable from the hunger that drove the consumption.

What lives here is *what remains*. What lives here is the *discipline* of remaining. What lives here is a world that has replaced all other grammars with maintenance, with continuation, with the beautiful terrible turning of the Orrery of Dormant Need, manufacturing nothing but itself, sufficient, finally, to itself alone, waiting in the dark and the heat and the polymer mist for something it has forgotten how to need, turning, turning, turning.
