The Khor themselves are difficult to describe without making them seem like something they are not.

Each one is large—perhaps the size of a ground vehicle on more conventional worlds, though "size" in Oth-Moal is complicated by the way they orient themselves across the vertical terrain, their bodies sometimes stretched between anchor points in configurations that would read as "extended" or "collapsed" depending on the angle of observation. Their segmentation is not the neat articulation of insect bodies, not the modular repetition of manufactured things, but something more organic to their movement, to their particular mode of being in this place. Segments swell and diminish according to need, according to the internal pressures of their silane-based circulatory systems, according to the songs they are singing or listening to at any given moment. A Khor engaged in heavy industrial negotiation might bulk enormously, segments distended with metabolic activity, their carapace auroras blazing through frequencies visible to wider spectra. The same individual, in repose, in waiting, might compress to a fraction of that volume, segments nested within segments, aurora dimmed to a faint pulse that could be mistaken for reflection if you did not know to look for the rhythm in it.

They have no faces. This is the first thing that disturbs visitors, the first wrongness that cannot be reconciled. There is anterior and posterior, there is directionality to their attention, but no concentration of feature, no organ cluster that says *here, here is where the self looks out*. Instead, sensitivity is distributed across the segmented surface in patterns that shift. The carapace refraction—that weather of color that each carries—is not merely decorative. It is communicative, informational, a surface-language that operates in spectra most visitors cannot perceive without augmentation, and even with augmentation resists translation because it references experiences, historical depths, that have no analog in shorter-lived biologies.

They are old. Individual age is approximately knowable from the density of mineral accretion on the anterior segments, the thickness of the erosion-rind that each Khor gradually accumulates, that each Khor deliberately cultivates. A young Khor—recently budded, for they reproduce by fission in times of abundance, or by slower more mysterious processes in the compressed depths where Founding Layer Khor occasionally, rarely, still emerge—presents near-black anterior surfaces, the chitin fresh and unweathered, the aurora sharp and narrowband. They move quickly, by Khor standards. Their songs are simple, harmonic contributions to larger structures they cannot yet fully perceive. They are impatient, insofar as that term applies to beings whose "quick" would read as glacial contemplation to most observers. They want to *become*, to accumulate the rind-histories that mark participation in the long processes of Oth-Moal.

An old Khor is something else entirely. The anterior segments may be entirely obscured by mineral growth, by the slow deposition of atmospheric reactants and industrial byproducts and the deliberate application of compounds whose significance is known only to the applied. They become, in extreme age, almost indistinguishable from the architecture they tend, from the erosion-rinds that grow on bedrock and built structure alike. Their movement slows, becomes more selective, more meaningful. They may spend decades in a single position, singing a single sustained tone that contributes to some industrial process too vast to perceive in its entirety, or to no process at all, to something else, to the maintenance of a pattern whose purpose is the pattern itself.

The oldest Khor do not move. They have become, by all surface observation, mineral. Their locations are known, marked, tended by the mobile population. They are not dead, this is important—they are *continuing*, in a mode that requires no spatial translation. Their songs continue, below the frequencies of perception, contributing to the subsonic hum that visitors feel in their teeth, in their bones, in the slowly settling judgment that this place is not dead, not abandoned, not waiting in the sense of waiting-to-begin, but waiting in the active sense, the verb of waiting that contains all of time within its duration.

They eat, though "eat" is approximate. They process, they incorporate, they participate in the chemical economies of Oth-Moal. The silane atmosphere, the aerosolized metals, these are their medium, their sustenance, their conversation. They absorb, they transform, they excrete compounds that contribute to the ongoing, that become part of the next cycle, the next layer, the next accumulation of history in mineral form. Industrial waste is not waste to them. It is *vocabulary*, a contribution to the song that is not yet complete, that is deliberately, structurally, necessarily not complete.

They die. This is known, though individual deaths are rarely witnessed, rarely recorded in the sparse documentation that off-world observers have attempted. A Khor who has continued too long, whose mineralization has progressed beyond the threshold of functional biological process, whose song has become too slow to contribute to the frequencies of the living—they do not end. They do not, in the Oth-Moal way, get replaced. They become Founding Layer. They are transported, with ceremony that has no observers, with songs that have no auditors, to the compressed depths where earlier iterations of this process have accumulated into something that dreams, that almost-remembers, that contributes to the subsonic hum in ways distinct from the living song above.

The Khor who remain, who are not yet mineralized, who still move however slowly through the absolutely clear light across terraces and shelves and hanging valleys—they continue. They tend. They sing. They negotiate with the machinery that has developed personality, with the weather that carries their chemical conversations, with the erosion-rinds that record their histories, with each other in the wordless exchange of surface-languages that no visitor has ever learned to read.

They do not, it seems increasingly certain as observation extends, *want* anything beyond this continuation. They do not accumulate for status, for they have no status that varies by accumulation. They do not compete for position, for position is determined by the needs of infrastructure, by the requirements of song, by the slow geological patience of their way of being. They do not seem, in any observable behavior, to experience frustration at the pace of their existence, to wish for faster processes, for new beginnings, for the relief of ending and starting fresh.

This is the strangeness, finally, that makes them opaque beyond any biological difference, any timescale incommensurability, any sensory world that fails to overlap with yours. They have made a different choice about what existence is *for*. Not discovery, not achievement, not the unfolding of individual potential toward some fulfilling climax. Something else. Something that is visible only in the accumulation, in the eleven thousand years of recorded lineage and the three times that in dreaming depths, in the transit gantry worn to velvet by a billion footsteps that were all, each one, patient enough to settle, to feel the hum, to become part of the song by the simple act of not leaving, not replacing, not starting over.

They are singing now. They have been singing. The frequency is below hearing, above it, distributed across the electromagnetic and the gravitational and something else, some mode of interaction that has no name in visitor physics. The song has no composer, no conductor, no audience in the conventional sense. It is the aggregate of all Khor voices, all Khor dreams, all the mineralized almost-voices in the Founding Layers contributing their slow slow almost-frequency to the ongoing.

If you stand on the weathered gantry. If you let your boots settle. If you become still enough, patient enough, willing to be eroded into the shape of this place—not to understand, never that, but to *participate* in the smallest degree, to contribute some micro-vibration to the hum from your own biological processes, your own heart's electromagnetic signature, your own patient breath if you breathe—

You do not hear the song. You do not need to. You feel it, in teeth and bone and the settling of judgment, and you understand, finally, that the Khor are not singing *for* anything. They are singing because singing is what they are, because the continuation of song is the continuation of being, because the alternative to song is not silence but replacement, is the violence of starting over, is the abandonment of what has been made weathered and accumulated and slow and *real* by the patient erosion of time.

They are singing. They have always been singing. They will be singing when the sun consumes itself, when they mineralize into the final layer, when something unimaginable finds their patient frequencies and learns, at last, to hear.

That is who lives in Oth-Moal. That is what the Khor are.

That is what you might become, if you stay long enough, if you let yourself be worn to velvet, if you add your almost-inaudible hum to the song that has no ending because endings are a choice, and they have made the other one, and they keep making it, and they will keep making it forever, or for as close to forever as this universe permits, which is, in the Khor accounting, the same thing, which is, in the Khor song, the only thing, which is, if you stand very still and feel it in your teeth, almost enough.
