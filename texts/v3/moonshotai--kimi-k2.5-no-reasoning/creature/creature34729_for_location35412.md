I need to sit with this for a moment. The city is already so fully inhabited by its own strangeness that adding inhabitants seems almost redundant—yet they are there, mentioned in passing, "the people of Vornath," "human figures moving between buildings." Who are they? What have they become?

Let me follow the invitation in the text: "given these constraints, what life is possible?"

---

There are no children in Vornath. Or rather, there are beings that occupy the social position children occupy elsewhere—small figures, dependent, learning, growing into the capacities of larger bodies—but no one speaks of their origins, and no one has observed the processes by which they arrive. They emerge from the lower levels, sometimes, or are found sleeping in vessels that grew nothing the day before, or simply appear in households that did not know they were waiting. The people accept them with the same functional verification they apply to everything: this small being eats, learns, responds, becomes. It functions as a child. Therefore it is tended.

The tending is communal and diffuse. There are no parents, only those-who-were-small and those-who-are-small, and the category of those-between who do the work of instruction without any formal assumption of responsibility. The young learn to walk the reliable routes before they learn to speak, if speaking is what the people do—they make sounds, certainly, communicate needs and observations and something more subtle, something like the negotiation of consensus about what is currently real. Their language, if it is a language, has no tense markers, no grammatical way to distinguish what was from what is from what will be. Why would it? In a place where corridors change length and plants appear fully grown, the usual temporal architecture collapses. They say instead: "the corridor to the cooling-tile plaza walks long today." They say: "the vessel grows a new frond-weep." They speak as the city speaks, in continuous present.

The adults—those-who-were-small who have grown into the full size of the species, though the species itself is uncertain, no skeletons consistent enough to suggest a single biological origin—they move through Vornath with a gait that is not slow but is never hurried. Urgency requires destination, and destinations in Vornath are relational, not spatial. One goes *toward* the central tone, *along* the rose channels, *with* the established route to the gathering where so-and-so will be, if so-and-so is still the person they were and the gathering still the occasion it was. The walking itself is the purpose, the continuous negotiation of a space that will not hold still.

They eat what grows in the vessels, the pithy bamboo-like growths and the translucent fronds and something else, something that is not plant but not not-plant, a layer of pale growth that forms on the interior surfaces of water channels and is scraped loose, pressed, left to dry on warm ceramic roofs into sheets that have a neutral nourishing quality, neither pleasant nor unpleasant. There is no hunger in Vornath as hunger exists elsewhere, no sharp need driving to satisfaction. The body maintains itself at the same temperature as the air, the same pace as the water, the same continuous present as the city. They eat when eating is called for, which is when food is present and the body accepts it, and not otherwise.

Their skin, where it shows through the woven fiber garments—garments that are not made but maintained, copied in form from older garments that were themselves copies, the original pattern lost somewhere in the recursive architecture below—has taken on something of the city's texture. Not scale, not the grey-green of the lizards, but a warmth, a slight sheen, a capacity to hold moisture without becoming sodden. They do not sweat, or sweat so minimally that the process is invisible. Their bodies have adapted to the wet press, found some equilibrium with it, just as they have found equilibrium with everything else.

There is no illness, or illness is indistinguishable from the city's other variations. Bodies that cease to function are not repaired but absorbed, taken to the lower levels where the made-ground grows deeper, or left in the absorption sinks where the strange objects sometimes appear, as if the city itself reclaims what it made. No one mourns, exactly, but there is a practice, a social punctuation as unreadable as the objects at the sinks: those who shared a reliable route with the ceased-to-function will, for an uncertain period, walk that route differently, take longer, pause at points where there is nothing to pause for. Then they resume the old pace, and the memory, if memory is what it is, becomes indistinguishable from the route itself.

The central tone draws them. Not all at once, not regularly, but eventually, inevitably, each person finds themselves in the thermal-tile plaza, standing at the edge of hearing, feeling the sound in their sternum more than their ears. They do not gather there in groups. The plaza is for solitary attendance, for something that has no name and no description afterward. Those who have stood there longest—there is no way to measure long, but some return more frequently, linger more completely—develop a quality that others recognize without being able to articulate. They move more like the lizards, perhaps, with a deliberation that seems to come from having more limbs than visible, more points of contact with the ground. They speak less, or their speech becomes more like the birds' calls, more like the city breathing through them. They are not revered, not avoided, but attended to with a particular quality of attention, as if they might at any moment become punctuation in the text.

And some do. The deepest adaptation to Vornath is not death but translation, a gradual becoming-indistinguishable from the made-ground itself. There are figures, sometimes, encountered in corridors that have become unreliable, that may be people who have walked too long in the continuous present, too deeply into the static instability, and have become part of it. They do not respond to speech. They move, if they move, along routes that do not connect the points they should connect, and those who follow them—there are always some who follow, out of that attention, that unnameable pull—find themselves in plazas they do not recognize, or in the same plaza with the angle wrong, the thermal pattern inverted, and must find their way back through patient renegotiation, carrying water and patience in equal measure.

The people do not ask what Vornath is, who made it, why it persists in this habitable theorem of itself. To ask would require a grammar they do not possess, a way of holding the city at distance, as object, as question. They are inside it too completely. Even the concept of inside is uncertain—there is no outside to contrast it with, no ground to stand on, only more city descending, more made-ground, more recursive intention. They are the theorem's demonstration, its working-through, and like any proof they do not stand outside their own logic verifying it. They are verified by continuing.

Yet there is something, some residue of what they were before they were people-of-Vornath, that persists in moments they do not discuss afterward. A person alone in a corridor that has compressed to a length that can be crossed in three steps, emerging into a plaza where the thermal tiles burn instead of merely warming, will sometimes—only sometimes—feel something that has no word. Not fear. Fear requires the possibility of different outcomes, and here there is only the continuous present, the same temperature held at the threshold of discomfort without ever crossing into suffering. Not wonder. Wonder requires the unexpected, and here variation is the only constant, expected precisely because it is all that occurs.

Perhaps it is recognition. The recognition that they too are made, shaped by hands using tools of stone and bone and fired clay, that their continuation is as continuous as the central tone, as the rose beetles' ancestral processions, as the water that condenses and drips and flows and is absorbed and perhaps, somewhere below, emerges again from seep-holes to begin its warm passage. They are part of the city's ecology of intention, survivors of a reality breakdown that is not breakdown but simply reality, here, where nothing is given and everything is made, where the made is never finished, where to exist is to be the object of a continuous making that no one now living understands, that perhaps no one ever understood, that perhaps was never understood because understanding was never its mode, only making, only the recursive architecture of intention folding endlessly into more of itself, maintaining, sustaining, holding at threshold, holding at threshold, holding.
