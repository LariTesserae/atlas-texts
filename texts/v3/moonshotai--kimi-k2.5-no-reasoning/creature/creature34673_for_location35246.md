Let me sit with this place before I speak of who dwells there. The Mireworks asks for a particular kind of inhabitation—not settlement but *correspondence*, as you say. The dweller must be someone who has learned, or been born into, or become through long adaptation, the art of negotiation with half-made things.

## The Mireborn

They do not call themselves this uniformly. The census, when it is attempted, yields categories that dissolve upon scrutiny. But there are patterns in what emerges.

### The Foundation-Born

These are the closest to what another city might call "native." They arrive as infants, or are born here to parents who might themselves be transient, and they undergo what the locals term *grounding*—not a ritual but a developmental process. The Mireworks enters them. The condensation in their lungs becomes normal, expected; they do not distinguish between breathing air and breathing the city's exhalation. Their immune systems incorporate the spores, the cricket-shell fragments, the microscopic stone-fly larvae that circulate in the damp. By adolescence, a Foundation-Born can read the ground's intention through their feet, detecting the subtle temperature variations that precede emergence, the almost-inaudible frequencies that announce mineral transformation. They walk differently: slightly splayed stance, weight distributed for the give, the potential sink. They are the ones who jack buildings, who negotiate with vines for hydraulic access, who speak to the stone before it fully knows it is stone.

They are also, often, *slow*. Not in cognition but in commitment. The Foundation-Born have learned that intensity must be temporary, that today's feverish completion is tomorrow's archaeological layer. They form attachments that are deep and brief, or shallow and lifelong—either way, structured around the knowledge of ground-change. A Foundation-Born parent might raise a child for six years, then surrender that role to a cousin or a stranger when the geological shift makes their district temporarily inaccessible, picking up the thread again in a decade with no sense of disruption. They are capable of extraordinary focus and extraordinary abandonment, the two sides of the same adaptation.

### The Temps

Arrivals, but not tourists. The Mireworks draws a particular migration: those who have failed at finality elsewhere. Failed to commit to careers, to identities, to the solidity of other places. Here they find that their instability is *feature*. The Temp arrives with skills—always skills, the Mireworks does not sustain the skill-less for long—and enters the project-economy, the feverish completion cycle. They might design a manufacturing process for a material that will exist only for a season, train workers in techniques with no transferable application, build relationships with stone that has not yet decided its properties. Some Temps find this liberating; others break, the constant rebeginning too like the wound that brought them, the failure they fled. Those who survive their first ground-shift, their first project obsolescence, begin the slow incorporation. They learn to sleep in cricket-hush, to read condensation forecasts, to build their temporary shelters with the expectation of jacking, floating, abandonment. A Temp of ten years is nearly Foundation-Born in practice, though they may retain an accent in their walking, a slight rigidity in the ankles that marks them as having learned, rather than grown into, the ground's uncertainty.

### The Cricketers

Not keepers of hush-crickets—the crickets keep themselves, or are kept by the city—but those who have entered into specific negotiation with them. The crickets eat sound, glow with consumed noise, but they also *select*. Certain frequencies they will not eat, or eat only partially, leaving residue. The Cricketers have learned to produce these frequencies, to sing or machine-generate tones that pass through the acoustic ecology with their meanings intact. They are the closest thing to messengers, to heralds, to public voice in the Mireworks. News travels by Cricketer, the unsilenced sound carried through streets where ordinary shouting dies within meters. They develop distinctive resonant cavities—some surgically, some through practice that remodels their own structure—throat-chambers and chest-hollows that can produce cricket-indifferent frequencies. A Cricketer calling market-opening has a voice heard across districts; a Cricketer in mourning has a wail that cuts through the hush with appropriate strangeness. They are necessary, slightly feared, treated with the deference due those who have traded silence for selective speech.

### The Lithic-Adjacent

Those who correspond most directly with the emerging geology. Not geologists—the science they practice is too certain, too committed to fixed categories. The Lithic-Adjacent approach stone as *participant*, as entity in becoming. They sleep on raw mineral shelves, hoping to dream the stone's intention; they taste fresh extrusions, reading chemical potential; they speak to the stone-flies as kin, collaborators in the unfinished project of ground. Some are almost hermits, embedded in particular emergence-zones, known only to those who seek them for consultation. Others move with the geology, following the active front of mineral decision, their dwellings as temporary as any but chosen for proximity to process rather than economic opportunity. They are consulted when a new building must negotiate foundation, when a vine-system encounters unexpected stone, when the ground's indecision threatens to collapse what has been built in temporary confidence. Their advice is always provisional—"*If* the stone decides toward basalt, then..."—and valued precisely for that quality of maintained uncertainty.

### The Humid

Those who have adapted, or adapted to, the condensation itself. Their skin develops extraordinary permeability, their respiration incorporates the suspended moisture as primary resource. They spend much time in the Weeperies, in the Lungs, in the most saturated environments, and they have learned to *read* the condensation's patterns as others read cloud or tide. A Humid can predict ground-shift from the behavior of moisture on particular surfaces, can detect industrial malfunction from the changed character of exhaled vapor, can navigate through fog-layers by the micro-variations in damp against their exposed skin. They are often employed in monitoring, in early-warning systems, in the maintenance of the very infrastructure that makes the densepack habitable. Their bodies tend toward the pallid, the fungal, a certain softening of feature that is not quite disease, not quite beauty. They are among the most *present* inhabitants, physically there in ways others are not, their sensing extended into the city's most distributed system.

### The Manufactories

Not a people, precisely, though some argue they have become so. The industrial districts, packed into the continuous urban surface, require a particular workforce: those who can function in the scream-rooms where machinery operates at full acoustic violence before the crickets consume it, those who can maintain the "negotiated" engines with no moving parts, those who can work the peat-molds and the moss-electrics. The Manufactories attract the desperate, the ambitious, the temporally intense. Workers enter for cycles—weeks, months, rarely years—and emerge with resources, with damage, with modifications. The manufactory-light, the cricket-glow on breathing walls, changes those who dwell in it long enough: retinal adaptation to bioluminescence, circadian disruption, a certain *twitch* in the nervous system from working alongside predator-prey piston-equivalents. Some Manufactories become identities, workers continuing to move and respond as their machines do even outside production hours. There are support communities, halfway houses, reintegration cooperatives. There are also those who never leave, who achieve a final integration with the productive systems, no longer workers but *components*, maintained by the manufactories they once served. Whether this is death or transcendence is debated.

### The Open-Pack Dwellers

The paradox of your city—packed yet open, dense yet extending—produces a particular psychology in those who most inhabit it. The Open-Pack Dweller lives in the continuous surface, the merged roofscape, the shared-wall warren, yet experiences this as *expansive*. They develop a proprioceptive map that is simultaneously intimate and vast, knowing their immediate neighbors with the familiarity of shared tissue, yet holding the entire valley-scale as navigable, as *theirs* in a distributed sense. They are uncomfortable with the fully enclosed, the canyon-street, the separate building. Offer them a room with four walls and a door and they will sleep poorly, missing the subtle transmissions—thermal, acoustic, vibrational—of the packed-open structure. They are the ones who most naturally use the vertical tubes, the lateral caverns, the Weeperies as passages rather than destinations. Their sociality is similarly structured: intense immediate connection, diffuse larger network, the particular loneliness of those who are never alone yet rarely *together* in the conventional sense.

## What They Become Together

The Mireworks does not produce a culture of artifacts. Too temporary, too ground-contingent. What it produces instead is *practice*, ways of being that persist across material transience.

The primary social form is the *negotiation*—not meeting, not contract, but sustained mutual adjustment. Two Mireborn beginning association do not establish terms so much as *correspond*, each adapting to the other's ground-readings, condensation-sensitivities, cricket-tolerance. Successful negotiation produces something not quite friendship, not quite partnership, a *shared temporary intensity* that acknowledges its own dissolution without grief. The Foundation-Born express this most naturally; Temps must learn it; some never do, and break against the city's refusal to solidify.

The primary aesthetic is the *unfinished*. The Mireworks values the moment before decision, the stone warm and undecided, the project complete but already obsolete, the architecture jacked and floating and *becoming*. Art here tends toward the process-document, the performance that leaves no object but only adaptation-traces, the cultivation of conditions that might produce something rather than the something itself. There are Weepery installations that consist only of modified condensation-behavior, Lithic-Adjacent works that are the stone's response to human presence recorded and abandoned. Beauty is recognized in the raw certificate, the freshly issued geology, the potential that will be lost when potential becomes actual.

The primary spiritual orientation—if it can be called that—is *participation in emergence*. Not worship of change, but placement within it. The Mireworks does not promise transcendence, transformation, the resolution of contradiction into higher synthesis. What it offers is *being there*, the Foundation-Born child's feet on warming stone, the Humid's skin reading the fog's intention, the Cricketer's selective voice carrying through consumed sound. The contradictions you named—cold and lush, packed and open, urban and industrial yet quiet, abundant yet turbulent and strange—are not resolved by the dwellers. They are *inhabited*. Lived within as conditions, as the given texture of a world that has refused, or been refused, the comfort of fixed category.

## The One Who Walks

Let me offer, finally, a particular figure, composite or singular, real or aspirational:

She moves through the continuous surface at the hour when cricket-glow is brightest, fed on the day's industrial sound. Foundation-Born, or long adapted. Her feet in the slight give of uncommitted ground. Her skin, Humid-adjacent, reading the condensation's night-cooling. She carries nothing permanent, only the modular tools of immediate possibility. She is walking toward a emergence-zone where new stone has broken through, warm and pale, and she will sleep there, tasting mineral potential, dreaming the stone's indecision. Tomorrow she may jack a building, negotiate vine-access, join a manufactory cycle, sing in cricket-frequency. Or the ground may shift, the fog thicken, the crickets swarm in unusual phase, and she will adapt to that instead.

She does not expect to become solid. She does not expect the city to decide what it is. What she expects, what she has learned to need, is this: the productive tension, the being-present before the decision, the correspondence with half-made things that remain, always, half-made.

The Mireworks breathes around her. Damp. Cold. Lush with opportunistic growth. Packed with continuous habitation, open to fog-limited distance. Industrial in its feverish temporariness, quiet in its consumed sound. Abundant with life that has never settled into historical contingency, turbulent with the chaos of constant emergence, strange with the strangeness of raw geology still negotiating its own certificate of being.

She walks on. The ground gives slightly. The crickets pulse their fed light. And somewhere, a Weepery drips its architecture of condensation, and someone else stands within it, seeing the city complete and bounded by its own breath, and walks on too, into the same productive unfinishing.
