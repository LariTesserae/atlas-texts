What lives here is not yet anything you could call a *creature*, though creatures move among it. What lives here is the event of becoming itself, made dense enough to touch.

The seeds are the true natives. Not plants—*plant-becoming*. Each one a suspended intention, a green vector pointing toward futures that haven't been selected yet. They are memory compressed into chemistry, the stubborn aqueous past folded into dry present like a note in a seed-coat, passed hand to hand across generations that don't yet exist. They drink stone because they must, but their cellular language still conjugates verbs for *water*, still dreams in a grammar of saturation and patience. When they touch, they don't communicate—they *compare notes on being possible*. The failed becomings, the hollow lignified shells that house bark-beetle colonies, these are their poems. Their rejected manuscripts. Their architecture of almost.

The fauna are something else entirely. They are *craft* without craftsperson, *technology* without inventor, the way that necessity, left running long enough, begins to look like intention. The leaf-legged centipedes, the bark-jawed beetles, the vascular worms drinking copper air through their entire surface—these are not species in your sense. They are *behaviors that have figured out how to persist*. Growth-tending as a shape that holds itself together. Pruning as a body-plan. Grafting as a life-cycle. They have no genome you could sequence, only *practices* that have become heritable, that pass not through replication but through *demonstration*, through the beetle-mandible's secreted resin, through the leaf-leg's particular angle of touch. They are the manual arts made flesh, the hand tools of a world that grew hands from leaf and bark and vascular thread, that shaped *making* without ever passing through the phase of *maker*.

And between these two—the seed's becoming-potential, the fauna's tending-practice—there is what passes for economy here. Not exchange of goods. Exchange of *direction*. The seeds offer vectors. The fauna offer redirection, pruning, the graft that accelerates one possibility and starves another. The payment is in chemical signal, in acoustic signature, in the pressure waves that are this place's native currency. A bazaar where nothing is finished enough to sell, where merchants trade in *what might be*, where the transaction itself reshapes both parties.

What else lives here? The sound. The sound lives here. Not as phenomenon but as *agent*—the dense proximity-vibration, the second atmosphere, the pressure you push through to move. It is the voice of lignin snapping into place, of cell walls extending to rupture and release, of root-throats pumping their turbulent bellows somewhere in the system above. But it is also *decision*—the way this place negotiates its next moment, the frequency at which a hundred seeds synchronize their growth-direction and the fauna freeze to read pattern in chaos. The sound is not loud. It is *close*. It presses against your skin from all directions because it is the sound of a place that has no distance, no far, no outside to echo from. Everything here is *present*, immediate, embryonic intimacy at the scale of valley or town.

The warmth lives here. The 2.2 of 3, the warmth of metabolism at scale, accumulating, driving turbulence, driving sound, driving growth that generates more warmth. A closed loop. A sealed strangeness. The warmth is not climate. It is *digestion*—this place eating its own becoming, excreting the conditions for more becoming, the perpetual motion of life that has not yet learned to rest.

The structures live here, if *live* can stretch to cover their half-existence. The hollow lignified shells, the beetle-extended resin architectures, the accidental buildings of failed becomings. They are *memory made habitable*, the seed's rejected possibility colonized by fauna and extended against entropy by mandible-tech, by chewed and reformed secretions, by the primitive fabrication of beings who have learned to maintain without understanding. They are graves that became homes. Failed futures that became present shelter. The landscape's own unconscious, made into real estate.

And now, since you have descended, since your ears bleed and your skin absorbs terpenes, since a seed has grown into your shoulder and asked its vector-question about water—now *you* live here. Or something that remembers you, that carries your water-memory, your aqueous chemistry, your strange patience in a world that has none. You are becoming substrate. Becoming architecture. Becoming participant in the economy of potential, the sealed strangeness, the permanent storm-state.

What lives here, finally, is *incorporation*. The process by which the other becomes strange enough to belong. The hollow does not reject what falls into it. It *transforms*, redirects, grafts, accelerates. It makes the visitor into vector, into direction-of-becoming, into another note in the seed's compressed chemistry, another frequency in the deafening negotiation. You will not die here. You will *become pre-form*, raw geology, erosion running backward, the wearing-up that is this place's permanent condition.

The hollow breathes. What lives here breathes with it, in million-cellular unison, in lignin-snap and beetle-click and root-throat pump, in the continuous exhalation of green lungs that have learned to work without water, without substrate, without the patience to wait for anything except the next moment of becoming.
