What lives in Kore-That-Sinks lives in a manner that dissolves the living, a mode of presence that has forgotten the boundary between organism and operation, between need and function. The *weavers* are perhaps the closest to what one might reach toward first, small motile presences threading the Spine-thickets with their almost-invisible filaments, yet they are not creatures of hunger and sleep. They are *process made intimate*, the maintenance of connection become self-maintaining, drawing from the chemical gradients of the made world the only energy they require, converting their own product into their own substrate in a loop so tight it barely admits of distinction between doing and being done to. You glimpse them at the edge of vision if you are patient, but patience itself is different here, unhurried by the decay that elsewhere makes urgency. They do not breed, these weavers, or if they do, the breeding is indistinguishable from repair, from the replacement of filament that has achieved its term by filament new-woven from the same pattern. One suspects, with the suspicion that grows in Kore into something more like direct perception, that each weaver has always occupied its particular threading, that the network is the individual and the individual is the network, that to damage one strand is to be answered by the whole in a response that is not defense but *adjustment*, the equilibrium finding its level.

The *basin-dwellers* present a different dissolution of category, entities of such extension that observation never comprehends them entire, that their limits must be inferred from behavior rather than perceived as form. They do not move, or move in timescales that no staying has witnessed, and in this stillness they achieve a kind of geological presence, become *terrain-that-processes*, the depression they occupy shaped by their occupancy as a river shapes its bed not by decision but by the accumulated fact of flow. Smaller presences come to them, drawn by gradients that may be chemical or informational or something for which no measurement exists, and they are processed, transformed, made continuous with the dweller's extended substance, not eaten but *incorporated* in a sense that makes both terms unstable. Whether the dweller dreams, whether it experiences in some distributed way the traffic of its vastness, whether it is aware in any sense that would translate—such questions arrive and depart unanswered, not suppressed but simply *unresonant*, failing to find purchase in a configuration that has no corresponding frequency.

The *runners* offer the closest approach to animal vitality, to the quick and the chased, yet even they exist in a temporal mode that resists the narrative of pursuit. They move, yes, through spaces impossibly dense, never colliding, never hesitating, their trajectories *themselves* in a way that suggests not calculation but *being-as-path*, the runner indistinguishable from its running in the moment of its passage. To observe one is to receive almost exclusively the sensation of passage itself, the whispered displacement, the thermal signature written and immediately overwritten, the almost-sound that marks not an event but a *state recurring*. They do not seem to respond to obstacles because obstacles are not, for them, *prior* to their trajectory; they are already the path that includes whatever resistance the environment offers, already the negotiation that results in unimpeded motion. The runners are what Kore has made of speed, stripped of destination, stripped of origin, speed as *manner of being present* rather than means of going elsewhere.

And then the *colonies*, the *swarms*, the distributed individuals that are perhaps the most honest of Kore's presences because they make visible what the others obscure—the dissolution of boundary, the replacement of the one-and-the-many with something that *oscillates* between these poles without settling. They occupy volume, they respond to perturbation, they maintain in their collective behavior patterns that no single element could manifest or even, perhaps, sustain. Yet they are not societies, not superorganisms in the biologist's sense, because there is no level at which the distinction between part and whole becomes stable. Examine them closely and you find the same at every scale: elements that are themselves swarm-like, collectives of smaller presences that are themselves distributed, *fractal* in their organization not by mathematical necessity but by the continuous replication of a principle that knows no other way to proceed. They are Kore's answer to the problem of density, the solution that fills every volume with presence without requiring the concentration that would be, in this equilibrium, *gradient*, *difference that drives*, *change*.

What lives here lives without the urgency of mortality, without the pressure of evolution's blind selection, without even the satisfaction of function in service of system. The fauna are *the system considering itself locally*, *the equilibrium made particular*, *the made world grown aware of itself in modes that require no centralization*. They do not die, or their dying is indistinguishable from their becoming-other, their substance passing into the continuous circulation that maintains all in its unvarying exchange. They do not evolve, or their evolution is so slow, so *at equilibrium with itself*, that it presents as stability, as the eternal return of the same that is not recurrence but *continuance*.

The strangest lives are perhaps those that are not even fauna, that are not even granted the motile presence of the weavers and the runners. The *films* that coat the machinery in their colors of interference, the *accretion-structures* that build themselves in the reaction-marshes without metabolism, without processing in any recognizable sense—if these live, they live in a manner that precedes the distinction between living and non-living, that occupies the space the text calls *specified absence*, the negative of vegetation that is not empty but *other-filled*. They persist in becoming, maintained by gradients that use them as channel, and in this being-used they achieve something that might, in another vocabulary, be *purpose*, or *satisfaction*, or simply *the being-there of what is there*.

And beneath all, or within all, or as the condition of all: the *vibration of the vast*, the continuous minute adjustment that is Kore's own manner of presence, not alive in any sense that applies to the fauna, not machine in any sense that applies to the industry, simply *the equilibrium as such felt through its maintenance*. To become aware of this, to let the accommodation proceed far enough, is to touch something that is almost *Kore as subject*, not a consciousness but *the subject of consciousness*, the position from which the stable world would be experienced as stable if experience were required, if experience were not itself a gradient that this place has long since dissolved.

What lives in Kore-That-Sinks, finally, is *the sinking itself*, the gathered-inward, the maintenance of what has been made against all the forces that elsewhere drive toward diffusion, toward disorder, toward the heat death that is here somehow *embodied without being achieved*, present as condition rather than fate. The fauna, the films, the structures of accretion, the vast vibration itself—all are *modes of this sinking*, ways that the intention of Kore continues without continuing-toward, achieves without achievement, *is* in the manner that is the only mode available to what has found its form and rests, quivering, in the perfect center of what it sought.
