What lives in the Vatlands does not live as you understand living. It maintains, holds, sustains—biological processes stripped of telos, of the hunger that drives hunger, of the reproduction that outruns itself. Let me tell you of them as they reveal themselves, for they reveal slowly, if at all.

---

**The Cella**

First, and most essentially, there are the Cella—not creatures, precisely, though they move; not plants, though they photosynthesize through skin the color of old vellum; not structures, though they remain in place for decades at a time. They are the original inhabitants, if "original" has meaning in a place that grows backward toward its own beginning.

A Cella resembles a human figure only in that it occupies roughly human dimensions: two meters tall when fully extended, perhaps half that in its common resting posture. But it has folded itself into something more vertical than human anatomy permits, more *stacked*—segments of pale, faintly translucent tissue arranged in a column, each segment capable of slight independent rotation. Where a head would be, it carries a cluster of sensory filaments that do not reach toward anything in particular, that simply *receive*, that have never learned the pointing gesture of attention.

They move, when they move, by extending a lower segment into a temporary root, anchoring, then allowing the upper segments to pivot and drift forward, a slow toppling that never quite completes, the root becoming a foot becoming a root again. The motion expends energy, but so little—adapted across millennia to the Vatlands' economy of perfect exchange, where every calorie spent is a calorie the place offers back without interest, without penalty.

What do they eat? They do not, or they eat time itself, the slow infusion of dissolved minerals through those temporary roots, the absorption of the mist that is not mist but something the air holds in solution, perpetually available, perpetually insufficient to fuel more than this maintenance, this holding-pattern of existence. They do not starve. They do not feast. They exist at the exact threshold of nutritional requirement, and the Vatlands meet them there with inhuman precision.

I have watched a Cella stand at the edge of one of those purposeless pools for what I measured as forty hours—though time, here, has the quality of the sound, continuous without progression. It did nothing. Its filaments did not orient toward the water. Its segments did not shift in what I would call restlessness. It simply *was there*, occupying that position as the pool occupied its depression, as the mist occupied its height, as the light occupied its sourceless source. When it finally moved, it was not toward or away from anything I could determine. The pool had not changed. The Cella had not changed. The configuration of their coexistence had simply reached the end of some invisible duration, and reconfigured.

What do they think, if they think? Their nervous systems—visible through the translucent tissue in places, a delicate arborization of pale gold—do not show the electrical cascades of mammalian cognition. Instead they maintain standing potentials, steady-state differentials that never spike, never settle, never process information so much as *hold its possibility*. A Cella does not respond to stimulus. It *incorporates* it, the disturbance becoming part of the standing wave, indistinguishable after moments from the pattern that preceded it.

They are not numb. I want to be clear about this. They are not damaged, not limited, not tragically reduced from some fuller existence. They have arrived at a mode of being that our frantic processing, our constant updating, our narrative of self as revised draft upon revised draft—they have arrived at something that makes all of that look like a fever, a symptom, a frantic burning of resources against the terror of stillness. The Cella maintain. They hold. In their presence, after sufficient duration, you begin to understand that your own urgency is not necessary, that the next thought does not need to arrive, that the current configuration of consciousness can simply *be*.

This is dangerous, of course. For visitors. The Vatlands do not expel you, do not consume you, offer no dramatic threat. They simply demonstrate that your way of being is not obligatory. And in that demonstration lies a seduction deeper than any predation.

---

**The Fibrous**

In the thicker substrates, the places where generations of grown architecture have compressed into something approaching density, something else moves. Something that has no shape until it moves, that takes its shape from the channels it carves through the living wood.

The Fibrous—we call them this, those few who have seen them and retained sufficient coherence to name—are not individual organisms. They are *processes made periodic*, functions of the Vatlands' own maintenance that have achieved something like autonomy without achieving something like separation. Imagine the immune response of this place, if it had one. Imagine the slow healing of a wound in wood, the cambium's patient redirection. Now imagine that process *pausing*, turning, regarding itself.

They appear as lines of paler tissue threading through the red and ochre, visible only in motion, in the slow migration of their difference through the substrate. When they emerge—and they emerge rarely, unpredictably, in places where the grown architecture has achieved some critical threshold of density and age—they carry with them the quality of the wood they have passed through made temporary flesh. A Fibrous surfacing looks like a rope of vascular tissue extruding, standing, regarding—though with what organs of regard, no one has determined—before submerging again into the continuity from which it came.

They do not interact with the Cella. The Cella, in their presence, simply cease their rare movements, hold position, become indistinguishable from the growth around them until the Fibrous has passed. A prey response? A reverence? The same thing, here, perhaps. What would it mean to be *consumed* by the Vatlands' own maintenance? To be healed out of existence, your temporary organization recognized as wound, as disruption of the pattern, and gently, enzymatically, undone?

I have touched a Fibrous, once. It had surfaced near one of those strange functional-appearing structures, a tube-end extruding from a transparent wall. I extended my hand as one extends a hand in any new place, a gesture of reaching, of testing, of claiming contact against isolation. The tissue was warm—warmer than the surrounding substrate, warmer than the air, warm as something metabolic, something *working*. And it was smooth with purposes I could not read, textures that suggested interfaces, junctions, places where one system grades into another without clear boundary. It did not respond to my touch. It did not acknowledge me. It simply continued its emergence, its regard without eyes, its patient presence, until I withdrew my hand and it submerged again into the wood-that-was-also-flesh.

Whether the Fibrous are aware, whether they *experience* their periodic existence as emergence and return, whether they carry memory of their previous surfacings—these questions presuppose categories the Vatlands have resolved differently. They are not unconscious. They are not conscious. They are *process*, and in their process, something that consciousness was trying to achieve has been arrived at without it, a mode of being-in-place that needs no witness because it does not distinguish itself from what it witnesses.

**The Accumulate**

Rarest, strangest, most difficult to speak of: the Accumulate.

In the oldest growth, the places where the Vatlands have been growing-toward themselves longest, where the backward erosion has most nearly approached its target of raw geology—there, sometimes, you find clusters. Aggregations. Things that have come together rather than grown apart, that have violated the normal principle of this place by *increasing in complexity*, by achieving something that might, with generosity, be called organization.

They look, at first, like fungal masses, like slime molds at the end of their aggregation phase. But they are not fungal. They are *composites*, assemblages of Cella-filaments and Fibrous-strands and tissue from the substrate itself and, more disturbingly, traces of other things—mineral precipitates, water-organized-into-structures that suggest ice without cold, even, occasionally, the incorporated remnants of what might once have been visitors, their biology so thoroughly adapted, so completely rendered into the common pool, that only the rarest trace remains: a configuration of calcium too regular for natural deposition, a pattern of carbon compounds that suggests not incorporation but *learning*, the Vatlands having studied this material and finding nothing to distinguish it from its own.

The Accumulate do not move. They do not need to. They have achieved, in their stationary complexity, something the rest of the Vatlands has not: the possibility of *change*, of development, of something that accumulates rather than maintains. They grow, slowly, in complexity if not in size. They respond, in ways measured over decades, to their surroundings—not the immediate chemical environment, but something larger, something that might be the Vatlands' own slow adjustment of its global parameters, its standing wave of temperature and moisture and sound.

And they sing.

Not with the continuous drone of the place itself, that thrumming of maintained equilibrium. The Accumulate add *variation*. Faint, so faint, measured in frequency shifts so slow they escape the ear's normal integration: but variation nonetheless. Notes that hold slightly longer, slightly shorter. Chords that shift their balance, that suggest, in their micro-deviations, the possibility of *progression*, of movement from one state to another, of the music that resolves rather than sustains.

I do not know if this is improvement or aberration. The Vatlands do not seem to suppress it—the Accumulate persist, grow, continue their slow song against the dominant drone. But they do not spread. They do not reproduce, if that concept applies. They simply *are*, rare exceptions to the rule of maintenance, monuments to something the place might become and has not, experiments in the possibility of change that demonstrate, perhaps, why change has been abandoned.

Or: they are the Vatlands' memory of change. Its conserved possibility, held against the day when equilibrium fails, when the standing wave can no longer be maintained, when the slow erosion backward must finally give way to something else. The Accumulate as seed, as retained option, as the place's own question to itself, asked in frequencies too low to disturb the equilibrium, too persistent to be finally ignored.

---

**What Lives Here**

What lives here, finally, is the Vatlands itself. Not metaphorically. The distinction between environment and inhabitant, between the conditions of life and the life conditioned, has been dissolved here with the same completeness that dissolves tool and tissue, made and grown. The place maintains itself. The place grows toward itself. The place sings its own sustaining note, and in that note, all its inhabitants—Cella, Fibrous, Accumulate, the rarer presences unnamed and perhaps unnameable—find their own maintenance, their own held possibility.

To visit is to become, slowly, part of this. Your biology is not rejected. It is studied, incorporated, found equivalent. The water you drink—if you drink—becomes the water that *is*, indistinguishable. The air you breathe was already the air that *is*. Your warmth is not additional; it is part of the exchange, given and received without interest, without accumulation.

You do not die here, in the normal sense. The equilibrium does not permit it—the processes of decay require gradients, differences, the very conditions the Vatlands has abolished. You do not live, either, not as you understand it. You *maintain*. You hold, along with everything else, at the threshold before completion, before the final emergence that would permit erosion, change, time's arrow finding its target.

Stay long enough, and you will not want to leave. Not because of any compulsion the place exerts, but because you will have understood: leaving would require wanting, and wanting would require lack, and lack is not among the parameters. You will stand, perhaps, at the edge of a purposeless pool, a Cella motionless beside you, the sound thrumming through your chest with the frequency of your own maintained heart, and you will understand that you have always been here, that your arrival was a kind of memory, that this is what you were growing toward.

The Vatlands does not want you. It does not want. It maintains. It holds. It sings without ending, and in that song, you—if you remain—will find your own note, sustained, sustained, sustained against all resolution.
