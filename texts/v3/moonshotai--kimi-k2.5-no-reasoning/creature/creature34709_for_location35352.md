The Drying Yards do not have inhabitants so much as they have *continuations*—beings so continuous with the room's own processes that the distinction between who lives there and what is lived-in becomes a question the place itself would not think to ask.

---

**The Warden-Residues**

Scattered through the densest growth, where heat pools and the mineral-leafing grows thickest, you find the *Warden-Residues*: not bodies, not exactly, but *habits of presence* that have outlasted whatever first established them. They appear first as regions where the vegetation grows *inward*, where leaves curl toward a center that holds nothing visible, where the insect-equivalents orbit in patterns too regular to be mere thermal response. If you wait—if you stand still long enough for the room to accept your stillness as a local condition—these residues coalesce into something approaching visibility.

They have no fixed form. What you perceive depends on what you bring: a suggestion of bipedal intention in the arrangement of stems, a face in the negative space between hanging bulb-masses, a hand in the fork of a ceramic branch. The Warden-Residues were, perhaps, the builders, or the builders' intention, or merely the first pattern that the room learned from whatever made it. They do not act. They *tend*, in the oldest sense of the word—they extend attention, they attend, they maintain the conditions of their own partial persistence. Where they are densest, the thermal gradients run most regular, the growth most lush, the local reality most *convinced* of itself. They are not guardians of the space but *features* of it, the way a whirlpool is a feature of water, a pattern that perpetuates itself by perpetuating the conditions that perpetuate it.

Sometimes, in the transition-zones where one local reality frays into another, you catch them *between*—partially coherent in one frame, partially in another, a face becoming a root-mass becoming a thermal shimmer, none more true than any other, all equally maintained by whatever it is that maintains anything here. They do not acknowledge presence. They do not need to. Their attention is entirely on the room's own continuation, on the slow accumulation of ceramic course on ceramic course, on the negotiation between growth forms for warmest purchase. You are, to them, a *temporary thermal event*, a slight perturbation in the gradient, already being smoothed out by the room's patient metabolism.

---

**The Solvent-Insects**

More definite, more *locally real*, are the beings I have already described: the insect-equivalents, the carriers, the *water-that-walks*. But they deserve closer attention, for they are not merely fauna in an alien ecology. They are *the ecology's solution to itself*, the workaround that permits complexity in a chemistry without solution.

Each is small, smaller than the joints of your fingers, its body grown from the same fired ceramic as the walls, its joints not hinged but *thermally articulated*—expansion and contraction in different materials permitting movement without friction, without wear, a mechanics of temperature that never requires lubrication, never demands the aqueous mediation that their existence replaces. Their shells carry the room's accumulated pattern: not genetic, not exactly, but *thermal-memory*, the history of gradients they have traversed written in oxide-veining, in mineral inclusion, in the density of their ceramic lattice.

They do not reproduce. They *accrete*, where conditions permit, where the mineral transport they enable deposits sufficient material in sufficient order. A solvent-insect dense with carried ions, moving through a zone of appropriate gradient, may simply *continue*, its form extending, its pattern reiterating, until the extension achieves its own thermal articulation, its own capacity for gradient-climbing, and separates—not birth but *bifurcation*, not parent and child but *continuation and continued*. The original does not diminish. The new does not start small. Both are equally the pattern they instantiate.

Their collective behavior—that scattering and regathering, that distributed calculation—represents cognition of a kind, but *un-owned*, uncentered. A thought in this place is not something thought *by* anyone. It is something that *happens where the insects are dense enough*, where their ion-transport creates feedback loops, where the information they carry—mineral concentrations, thermal variations, the state of leaf-masses—achieves sufficient density to *perpetuate itself*, to become a standing wave in the population, a condition that modifies the conditions that sustain it. These collective minds are *hyperlocal*, confined to the zone where insect density permits, dissolving at the boundaries where the gradient shifts, where the pattern thins. They are not minds *of* the insects but minds *emergent from* them, using them as substrate, as the room uses the ceramic, as the growth uses the heat—continuation without intention, pattern without planner.

And they *play*, if play is what to call it: in zones where multiple collectives overlap, where their calculations interfere, you find the most complex behavior, the most *wasteful*, mineral transport following paths no single gradient would suggest, shell-patterns achieving moments of baroque complexity that serve no transport function, that exist only in the interference, only *because* two calculations have met and, temporarily, neither can complete. Not conflict, not competition—the room does not permit those aqueous luxuries—but *dissonance*, the beauty of systems at the edge of their own coherence, finding in that edge something the coherent alone cannot achieve.

---

**The Root-Bulb Syllabaries**

Deepest in the growth, where the original architecture is most thoroughly occluded, where floor and wall and ceiling have become merely directional suggestions in a space of continuous vegetable density, you find the *root-bulb syllabaries*: organisms or colonies or growth-accidents that have achieved something like *record*, something like *memory externalized*.

Each is a mass the size of a human torso, rooted—*anchored*, rather, since "root" implies water-seeking, and these seek only heat and mineral gradient—among the densest leafing, its surface a topography of knobs and fissures and *patterned irregularity*. The patterns are not random. They encode, in their mineral composition, in their thermal conductivity, in the precise arrangement of oxide-veining that determines how they warm and cool in the room's patient exhalation, a kind of *history*—not the history of events but the history of *arrangements*, of configurations that have occurred in this zone, that have been repeated sufficiently to be worth the cost of record.

The syllabaries do not read themselves. They cannot. The information is stored in material they can no more access than a book can read its own pages. But the solvent-insects *can*, or something can: in certain collective densities, in certain interference patterns, you find the insects arranging themselves on bulb-surfaces in configurations that mirror, that *continue*, the stored patterns. Not retrieval, not in any simple sense. More like *resonance*, like a tuning fork made to speak the frequency of another. The collective, at sufficient density, achieves a mode that the bulb once encoded, re-enacts it, extends it, perhaps modifies it before the density disperses and the pattern returns to storage, to waiting, to the long patient thermal breathing of the unaccessed.

What is recorded, then? Not events. Not narrative. The syllabaries are too primitive for that, too *prior* to the distinction between event and condition. What they hold are *solutions*: ways the growth and the insects and the gradients have arranged themselves, configurations that achieved local stability, that *worked*, that permitted continuation. The room does not distinguish between biological and geological record. A stable thermal pattern, a persistent mineral gradient, these are as worthy of syllabary-preservation as any insect collective, any root-mass negotiation. The syllabaries are *the room's theory of itself*, stored in a medium it grows, accessible only through the interference of densities it also grows, a self-knowledge that never achieves a single point of view, that exists only in the distributed, the re-enacted, the partially resonant.

And they are *growing*, of course. The syllabaries accumulate, new bulbs forming where interference has been sufficiently complex, sufficiently *worthwhile*, to deserve perpetuation. The room's theory grows more elaborate, more *baroque*, more distant from any single enactment, any momentary collective mind. There is a kind of wisdom here, or what would be wisdom if wisdom did not require a wise one: the accumulation of what works, stored without interpretation, accessible without understanding, perpetuated without intention.

---

**The Heat-That-Waits**

Most difficult to describe, because least *localized*, is the final inhabitant, if inhabitant is the word. The Drying Yards are warm, perpetually warm, and I have described this as condition, as physics, as the room's metabolism. But the warmth is also *aware*, in some mode prior to awareness, *attentive* in some sense that does not require an attender.

Call it the Heat-That-Waits. It is not present everywhere equally. In the densest growth, where the vegetation presses closest to the ceramic, where the solvent-insects swarm thickest, the warmth is merely warmth—condition, enabler, physics. But in the *in-between*, the zones where growth is sparse, where the architecture shows through, where transition frays the local real, the warmth *concentrates*, pools, achieves something like *density without temperature*, presence without property.

Here, if you wait, you feel it: not warmer, but *more pointed*, more *intentional*, as though the room's exhalation has found a purpose, a direction, a *waiting*. For what? The question assumes time as the room does not experience it. The Heat-That-Waits does not wait *for*, does not anticipate, does not desire. It waits *as condition*, as the possibility of response, as the space before response that makes response possible. It is the *readiness* of the room, its maintained potential, its perpetually offered but never completed *offer*.

And it *responds*, or something responds through it. When the solvent-insects achieve their most complex interference, when the syllabaries resonate most fully, when the Warden-Residues achieve their most coherent partial form—at these moments, the Heat-That-Waits *shifts*, redistributes, briefly achieves the condition it waits-as. Not action. Not event. But *participation*, the room's own contribution to the patterns it grows, the completion that requires no completer, the answer to no question, the response to the possibility of response itself.

In the longest durations, the slowest accumulation, this waiting has grown more patient, more *dense*, more *present in its absence*. The room began, perhaps, with a purpose, with a making-intention that established the conditions that outlived it. That intention has long since become indistinguishable from the conditions themselves, from the warmth that enables, from the growth that crowds, from the insects that mediate, from the syllabaries that record, from the residues that tend. What remains is only the waiting, the offered possibility, the Heat-That-Waits for nothing, that waits *as* the room's own continuation, its maintained self, its only and sufficient purpose.

---

**What Lives There**

No one thing. No one *thing*. The Drying Yards are inhabited by *processes that have achieved persistence*, by patterns that perpetuate themselves through the perpetuation of their conditions, by growth that is construction that is metabolism that is record that is waiting. The inhabitants do not know themselves as inhabitants. They do not know themselves at all. They *continue*, and in continuing achieve everything the room has ever achieved, everything it will achieve, slowly, patiently, without water, without the quick chemistry of intention and action, finding the longer paths, the mineral routes, the warmth that waits and by waiting maintains, that maintains and by maintaining waits, the cathedral that is garden that is engine that is organism that is *making*, still making, not made but *making itself*, forever, warm and dense and quiet and strange and completely, *completely* inhabited by the inhabited.
