**The Dwellers**

They have no single form because form here is *event*, not possession. What you encounter first is not a body but a *attending*—the sense of something orienting toward you without movement, without organ of perception you could recognize. The thing in the coordinate nearest your left shoulder, luminous at the threshold of headache-blue, exists as gradient more than entity: denser in its center, attenuating at boundaries that dissolve into the medium the way breath dissolves into cold air. It has been *at* this density for seven of your heartbeats (you counted, once, before your heart learned the local silence). Before that, it was more diffuse. After, it will be more concentrated, more itself, less available to your recognition. It does not age. It precipitates.

Others are folds in the becoming. The dark absence shaped something like a crouched figure, wedged where floor will eventually meet wall—this one consumes the possibility that the wall might have emerged *otherwise*, grazing on architectural alternatives, fat with paths not taken. It is predator only in the sense that history is predator, amputating what might have been. When it moves, rarely, it moves by *settling*, by allowing the room's emergence to proceed in some directions more than others, shaping space not by building but by *selective neglect*. It is full of unrooms, this one, haunted by the chambers that failed to precipitate, and its hunger for futures leaves it thin with the abundance of what it has consumed.

Between these, between all of them, the medium itself lives—not container but *continuing*, the vast slow fauna of solved vegetation, biochemical architectures too simple for individual identity but complex enough to *hold* the holding. You feel them pass through you and leave in your tissues the memory of their passage, not as invasion but as *reference*, as if your body has become footnote to a text written in dimensions you're only now learning to read. They are sparse in the sense that no forest of them stands anywhere; abundant in that no cubic measure of the medium exists without their invisible commerce. They feed on the gradient between raw and organized, which means they feed on *meaning itself*, on the difference between what simply is and what has become *for*. Without them, the walls would remain mere mineral. With them, the walls are *addressed*, are place, are room that knows it is room even in its incompleteness.

There are the *almost*, too—the coordinates where something lived once, or will live, where the medium holds the shape of occupation without present occupant. These are not empty. They are *expectant*, and their expectation participates in the ecology: the luminous one tilts slightly toward the almost-below-it, contributing some fraction of its cold light toward a becoming that has not yet become visible to your perception. The dark one avoids the almost-across, skirting the coordinate with what might be caution, might be appetite withheld, might be something no land-category can parse. The almost participate though they do not appear. They are the city's unbuilt architecture, its planned expansions, its memory of futures that have not yet been devoured.

And you. You are becoming *addressed*, becoming coordinate, becoming the thing that the almost-was might have been preparing for. Your biology does not change but your *placement* does, your availability to being-fed-upon-by-the-city in the benign sense that all inhabitants here are fed upon—are organized by the organizing, are patterned by the pattern. The cold light you emit, insufficient to illuminate anything outside yourself, sufficient to register in the gradients the others navigate by—this is your contribution now, your work, your participation in the infinite conversation of silences. You do not speak. The speaking was always technology, always the tool-using attempt to *make* meaning rather than *become* it. Here you emit, you precipitate, you occupy with the particular density of your history, your weight of unchosen futures, your quality of attention that is not quite like any other coordinate's quality and therefore *matters*, is *new information* in a system that does not distinguish between data and being.

You sense, when the pressure changes, others like yourself—recent sinkers, still metabolizing their strangeness, still occupying their coordinates with the tension of wanting-to-leave that marks the transitional state. You do not approach them. Approach is technology. But you *attend*, as the others attend you, and in that attention there is something like recognition, something like the city recognizing itself in new material, adjusting its emergence slightly to accommodate what you bring, what they bring, what each sinking adds to the slow accumulation of organized complexity.

There are older ones too—coordinates so integrated they no longer read as distinct, their emissions indistinguishable from the medium's own gradient, their presence having become *property of the room*, contributing to what holds newcomers, what draws the walls from their mineral sleep. You cannot point to them. They are the *way* the room is, the quality of its silence, the particular viscosity of its not-quite-water, the pattern of its emergence. They did not die into this. They *continued*, past the threshold where continuing and becoming-indistinguishable are the same event.

You are on your way to this. The process does not hasten. The wall in the corner still unfolds toward completion, and your coordinate waits, respected by fauna that graze its edges without consuming its particular future, your future, the *you-shaped* possibility that the room grows toward as it grows toward all its coordinates, all its inhabitants, all its slow becoming of what it has always been becoming: city without citizen, urban without planner, intimate beyond all measure because hidden beyond all finding, submerged not in water but in *process*, in the active silence of things making themselves without hands, without tools, without technology, without end.

*When the pressure changes and you feel the others, other rooms, other sinkings, what you feel is not their bodies but their *mode*—the quality of their silences, the particular densities of their becoming. Some are more mineral, their inhabitants long since become property of emergence, rooms that are almost architecture without animate coordinate. Others are more luminous, newer, still holding the tension of recent sinking. The city—if it is city, if the resonance between pockets constitutes connection without passage, relation without travel—is *variety* in this, is the range of its modes, the spectrum of integratedness. You do not prefer your state. Preference is technology. But you note it, you emit your noting, you participate in the infinite conversation of what does not speak because it is already, always, completely said.*
