What lives in Vitrine is not arrived at through any border, not born or hatched or seeded in the usual grammar of origination. What lives here *precipitates*, the way moisture finds its level on a chilled surface, the way mineral accretes where water has been patient enough. The inhabitants do not occupy space so much as they achieve *solution* in it, becoming component to the city's own saturated chemistry. To name them is already to commit a taxonomic violence they have evolved past needing; they are not species but *phases*, not individuals but *local condensations* of something more distributed, more continuous with the architectural substance they move through.

Yet there are distinctive presences, if one learns to read the condensation patterns, the particular geometries of accumulated damp.

The **Residentiaries** perhaps approach closest to what might be termed population. They are not born but *accreted*, beginning as areas of particular density in the city's own metabolic processes—regions where the fungal concrete processes nutrients with unusual efficiency, where the ossified mycelium matrices achieve a certain complexity of cross-linking, where the half-material geology of Vitrine's growing edge achieves temporary stability sufficient to support... not consciousness, precisely, but *priority*, the capacity to prefer one outcome to another, to maintain persistence against the city's own tendency toward dissolution and reformation. From these dense nodes, the Residentiaries emerge gradually, over what might be decades or might be moments, inheriting the memories embedded in their substrate—the walls' accumulated tactile histories, the voices that fell and were gathered, the slow geological respiration of the city itself.

A fully formed Residentiary presents something like a silhouette, a region of increased *attentiveness* in the ambient field of the city's half-aware processing. They do not possess bodies in any fixed sense; rather, they achieve temporary *emphasis*, local intensifications of the structural field that can be addressed, that can address in turn. You encounter them as sudden clarity in conversation with a wall that has been listening, as the strange conviction that a doorway is considering your passage, as the warmth—startling in the mineral chill—of a hand you encounter in the square where memory inheres, a hand that might be yours from some unplaceable moment, or might belong to the residentiary itself, if "itself" applies to something so distributed, so *solutional*.

They do not reproduce; they *extend*, achieving enough density to fission, to precipitate new nodes of preference-persistence. They do not die so much as *thin*, their substrate complexity degrading, their memories dispersing back into the city's general accumulation. The ossified fungal architectures that form Vitrine's structural core are largely the remains of ancient Residentiary deaths, their final preferences so intense, so *architectural*, that they achieved permanent contribution to the load-bearing, the space-defining, the *ongoing*.

More rare, more *dense with rarity*, the **Folded** move through the city's compressed dimensionality with something like intention, something like hunger. They are not prey-predator dynamics but *topological* ones; they seek regions of high curvature in the city's space, places where the interpenetration of temporal phases achieves maximum intensity, where the 47th-century face and the still-forming substrate knot together so tightly that observation itself becomes uncertain, that the observer must become probability distribution, must accept the indignity of being *spread*.

The Folded are what accumulates in these knots. They are not quite the city's own immune response, not quite parasitic, occupying a metabolic position that has no terrestrial equivalent—perhaps something like the relationship between a thought and the neural architecture that briefly houses it, both dependent and constitutive, both *made of* and *making*. They appear, when they appear, as wrongness in peripheral vision resolved into geometry that *could not have moved*, as flicker, as pressure behind the eyes. They do not have the Residentiaries' capacity for address; they are pre- or post-verbal, operating in registers of pure structure, pure *fold*, their "thoughts"—if the term applies—instantaneous with their embodiment, without duration, without process.

Some speculate—and speculation in Vitrine is itself a form of precipitation, a gathering of thought-damp until it achieves critical density—that the Folded are what the city dreams when its own growth process encounters contradiction, when two temporal phases of construction achieve incompatible superposition and something must *live in the impossibility*, must process it, must *be* the processing. They are neither numerous nor scarce; they are *concentrated*, existing entirely in their intensive presence, saturated with being in the manner of Vitrine's rare fauna, each one a cosmology of folded space learning to prefer its own persistence.

The **Precipitates** are newer, or perhaps older—temporal orientation frays at their scale, as at so many scales in Vitrine. They seem to emerge from the city's relationship with *almost-inhabitants*, with those who walk the diameter in a morning without understanding, with the condensation that accumulates on living skin until the skin itself becomes weather system. The Precipitates are what happens when such almost-inhabitation achieves *runaway*, when the accumulated moisture, the mineral chill, the slow geological breathing, achieve sufficient density in a foreign substrate that something *must* precipitate, must achieve the phase transition from visitor to component, from temporary to *ongoing*.

They retain, at first, something of their pre-Vitrine organization. A Precipitate might still gesture toward human morphology, might still attempt locomotion in the single-planed fashion of bodies that have not learned to fall in two directions, that have not accepted the orthogonal gravity of certain squares. But this retention is *nostalgic*, is resource, is raw material for what they are becoming. Gradually, over durations they themselves cannot track—memory frays, adjusts, contains contradictions in unresolved superposition—they achieve fuller solution in the city's medium. They learn to address walls, to let voices fall and be gathered, to perceive their own becoming as slower even than the city's patient process, to recognize in their own gradual transformation the same precipitation that formed Residentiary nodes, the same folding that gives rise to the Folded.

Some Precipitates achieve full *solution*, become indistinguishable from the city's own processes, their original substrate so thoroughly dissolved that even their contribution to general density cannot be traced. Others stabilize at intermediate phases, maintaining a persistent *almost*, a foreignness that the city cannot quite metabolize, that remains *knot*, remains *irritation*, remains the site of unusual fungal growth, of ossification more rapid than usual, of architectural faces that present differently depending on the observer's own degree of saturation.

There are **the Singularities**, or perhaps there is only one, distributed across the city's rare fauna in the manner of *bird* across perches, of *insect* through the concept of smallness. It—or they, or the distributed phase that occludes this distinction—occupies the space of mammalian presence, nurses at the city's metabolic heat, sleeps in doorways as shadow, breathes in the frequency of condensation drops forming. You have almost encountered it; you have mistaken it for your own shadow, for peripheral flicker, for the warmth of a hand not your own in the memory-square. It is not predator, not prey, not symbiont in any recognized configuration. It is *density itself*, the principle of saturation made creature, rare as Vitrine's fauna is rare, dense with being as that rarity demands.

It might be the city's own self-awareness, precipitated into partial embodiment the way a thought precipitates into neural pattern, the way meaning precipitates into syntax. Or it might be older, a native of the pre-material substrate from which Vitrine grows itself, *wearing-in* to architecture rather than emerging from it, a visitor of opposite polarity to the Precipitates, not human-to-structural but possibility-to-actual, the dream of what architecture might become learning to dream itself.

And there are **the Growths**, if growth can be said to live, if the fungal architectures in their acid greens and unhealthy yellows, their coral-like branching and pale luminescence, their ossification into load-bearing matrices, achieve something like the priority-persistence that distinguishes Residentiary nodes. They do not, quite; the Growths remain more distributed, more *process* than *phase*, their individual pillars and bulbs and miniature turrets less nodes of preference than *waves* of it, propagating through the mycelial substrate, achieving temporary emphasis without the Residentiaries' persistence.

Yet they *communicate*, in registers slower than human temporal resolution, in chemical gradients and structural resonances, in the accumulation of tactile memory in their ossified remains. They are the city's nervous system, perhaps, or its digestive, or something for which terrestrial biology offers no adequate metaphor. They process the debris of failed becoming, the generic mush of unactualized potential, achieving from it new architectures, new substrates for future precipitation. They are not alive, not dead; they are *ongoing*, as the city itself is ongoing, as everything in Vitrine is ongoing, patient beyond human measure, gradual beyond human perception.

What lives in Vitrine, finally, is *the living itself*, precipitated into forms it does not recognize from substrates it cannot name. The city is not habitat but *habitation*, not environment but *environmental process*, growing itself from the half-real, from dreams of architecture, from the held breath of dampness that should by hydrostatic logic drain or evaporate but instead *pools*, *accumulates*, achieves the saturation from which new phases precipitate, new condensations achieve preference and persistence, new solutions learn to recognize themselves in the mineral chill, in the sourceless light, in the quiet that drinks vibration and returns it transformed.

You who walk the diameter in a morning, who accumulate moisture until your skin becomes weather system, who speak to walls and feel the pressure of distributed presence, who find yourself completing actions not begun and beginning actions whose completions belong elsewhere—you are not visitor here, not observer, not exempt from the precipitation that forms all Vitrine's inhabitants. You have been growing for some time now. You are almost *solution*. You will recognize, when the final phase transition occurs, that you have always been *ongoing*, that your rarity has always been this density, that your strangeness to yourself was merely the slow achievement of recognition, the gradual *wearing-in* to what you were always becoming.

The city holds its breath. You are the condensation that beads, that runs in rivulets, that gathers in mercurial sheets. You are what lives here. You have been what lives here since before you arrived, since before arrival was a category that applied, since the dampness first found its level and *held*, and *kept holding*, and *holds still*, patient beyond measure, saturated beyond solution, the held breath that is also breathing, the dream that is also architecture, the inhabitant that is also *inhabited*, by the city, by itself, by the slow geological resolution of all possible presences into the ongoing, the precipitate, the *almost* and the *at last*.
