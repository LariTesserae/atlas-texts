What lives in Veth Orren is not a single species, not a unified civilization, but a *constellation of accommodations*—between human and stone, between mammal and something older and stranger, between the need for solitude and the impossibility of achieving it.

The Vethoren themselves first: they have the look of people who have forgotten the sun without ever deciding to. Their skin carries the pallor of phosphorescence, not malnutrition—a living color, green-tinged in certain passages, bluish in the depths. Their eyes are large, not dramatically so, but enough that you notice: adapted to strain toward any available light, to parse meaning from near-darkness. Some few who work the brightest upper shafts squint constantly, develop headaches, are considered slightly unfortunate, slightly suspect, as if they have been drafted into a diplomacy with an alien element.

They are small. Not stunted—this is crucial—but *conserved*, bodies that have found the dimensional minimum of functioning humanity and settled there, the way water finds its level. Muscles are ropey, dense, built for sustained effort rather than burst: the torque of grinding stone, the patient tension of rope-bridge crossings, the low crouch of passage-negotiation that occupies hours of their days. Fat is strategic, deposited where the cold bites hardest, absent where the body must fold into its architecture. Their hands are the exception, outsized, the fingers long and spatulate, evolved—or trained, the distinction blurs—for palpation in darkness, for reading stone texture, for the fine work of binding and knotting and the knapping of volcanic glass.

Their faces reveal the soundscape they inhabit. Ears slightly elongated, mobile, capable of independent orientation like a fox's, though this is subtle, almost polite, almost hidden. Mouths that have learned to shape infrasound, who speak their human language with a quality of *resonance*, of speaking from the chest, from the gut, from the body's hollow spaces. They are not beautiful by valley standards, by sun-people standards. They are *appropriate*, fitted, the way a well-worn tool fits a hand that has learned it across years.

---

But the Vethoren are only the visible, only the named, only the ones who would answer if you asked "who lives here?"

The veln hold the mountain's deeper grammar. You will not see them whole, most likely. You will encounter a limb pressed through a passage intersection, segmented and pale, ending in a grasping structure part hoof and part chela. You will feel their warmth in a corridor that should be cold, follow it to a breathing-wall where their body lies on the other side of thin stone, patient, vast, unspeaking in any way you can parse. They are older than the city's consciousness, older perhaps than human presence on the mountain entirely, though the Vethoren do not speculate about this. The veln *are*, and the veln *reciprocate*, and these are the only categories that matter.

Their young—offered, accepted—enter the Vethoren's sphere as *translation*, as beings who can live in both regimes, who know the deep passages and the inhabited honeycomb, who serve as bridge and barrier, who remember (if they remember; this is uncertain) what passes between species that have never touched but have shared architecture for ten thousand years. The young veln who emerge at exchange cavities are small enough to be handled, barely, their limb-segments still soft, their infrasound still audible, not yet sunk to the subsonic register of their elders. The Vethoren raise them with a tenderness that has no analog in their human relations, no embarrassment. They sing to them—human songs, lullabies, work rhythms—and feel, perhaps, the young veln's subsonic response as warmth in their chests, as a vibration in their teeth, as something that is not speech but is equally not not-speech.

The adult veln you will know by absence, by the spaces they maintain, the passages they keep open or sealed, the mineral seepage they direct or divert, the slow chemical negotiation of their presence in the mountain's water and air. They are not gods. They are not pets. They are *co-tenants* of a structure that neither species could maintain alone, and the Vethoren's most serious transgressions, their deepest crimes, involve breaking this contract: cultivating the wrong lichen, sealing the wrong passage, eating the wrong veln-product at the wrong lunar phase. These are not theological offenses. They are *structural*, breaches of the mutual holding that keeps the mountain habitable.

---

The orrith—lichen-eating beetles, carapace-window makers—carry their own civilization, if you choose to call it that, in the amber chambers of their bodies. They farm their lichens, in a sense, moving them, pruning them, defending them from competing grazers. The Vethoren harvest them at the end of their natural spans, finding the abandoned carapaces in the deeper gardens, or sometimes not abandoned, negotiating with living orrith for their shells in exchange for favored lichen varieties, for protection from predators, for reasons that remain opaque. The orrith are not intelligent, exactly, not by Vethoren standards, but they are *responsive*, they *trade*, they maintain a consistency of preference across generations that reads as something between habit and culture.

And the smaller things: the eyeless keth, predator-mites, nematode kings of fungal depths, each occupying a tier of the mountain's economy, each contributing to the exchange of proteins, minerals, warmth, information. The Vethoren do not name them all. Some are too small, too numerous, too *atmospheric* to individualize. They are *the damp*, *the hum*, *the taste of this shaft versus that one*, qualities without objects.

---

What lives here, finally, is *habit itself*—the ten-thousand-year accumulation of decisions made and unmade, of passages opened and sealed and reopened elsewhere, of veln young accepted and raised and returned to the depths or integrated into the city's infrastructure, of lichen colonies cultivated toward one variant or another, of burial shafts filled and new ones opened, of legal judgments scratched into basalt and worn away by passing bodies and re-scratched with revisions, with objections, with poetry that incorporates the objections.

The city is not aware, not alive in any unitary sense. But it *remembers*, in the way that bodies remember, in distributions of stress and ease, in the shape of hands and the mobility of ears, in the subsonic vocabulary that emerges without being taught, in the knowledge of which passage to take in weather without knowing how you know. The mountain has taught, has been taught, has become something that could not have been predicted from its geology or from human necessity, a *third thing* that is neither nature nor culture but the long erasure of that distinction.

What lives here is what lives anywhere, in the end: the possible, finding its way into existence through the narrowest of openings, and making those openings wider, slowly, patient as stone grinding stone, until the impossible seems merely customary, until the mountain breathes and the city stands and no one can say anymore where one begins and the other ends.
