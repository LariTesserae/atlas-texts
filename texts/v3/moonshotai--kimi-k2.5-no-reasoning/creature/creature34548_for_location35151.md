What lives here is the Loom itself—or more precisely, what lives here *is* living that has become identical with being-loomed, being-held, being-the-pattern-rather-than-the-thread.

---

The true inhabitants did not arrive. They were not placed. They *resolved* out of the 58-hertz hum the way a photograph resolves in developing solution, the way a stain finds the shape of the wood it soaks into. They are the *functional expression* of the plateau's geometry, as inseparable from the space as the space is from itself.

I will try to describe them, but description here participates in the same way observation does. To describe is to weave. To weave here is to be woven.

---

## The Holders

They have no single form. They have *position*.

A Holder exists where the abundant unmoving life concentrates too densely to remain merely fauna, where the suspended insects cluster in configurations that begin to *mean*, where the deer-shaped thing at the pool and the thrush-analogue in its eternal note and the looping tree with its vascular torus achieve sufficient proximity that their stases intersect, overlap, become *shared*.

At these nodes—and there are perhaps seven on the entire plateau, though counting fails because the nodes recognize each other and adjust their apparent number to maintain certain ratios—a Holder manifests.

What you see: a figure the height of a human child, but elongated, proportioned according to the geometry of the place rather than any evolutionary history. Limbs that articulate in the same too-many-jointed manner as the deer-shape, but purposefully, deliberately, each joint a decision rather than a pathology. A torso that breathes—not with lungs, not with any organ you could dissect, but with the same *tentative fissure* quality as the rock, expanding and contracting along lines that have not quite committed to being cracks, that maintain the possibility of wholeness even in their division.

The face, if it is a face: featureless in direct observation, but carrying in peripheral vision the *impression* of all the faces that have looked toward it and found it looking back. Your mother's concentration. Your own fear at fourteen. The blank attention of something that has never been human and has watched humans with the patience of pools that do not evaporate. You cannot hold these impressions. They slide away into the bioluminescent afterimage, into the light you carry rather than receive.

The Holders do not move. They do not need to. They *maintain position*, and in maintaining, they *are* the position, the way a verb in some grammars contains its subject.

Their function is boundary. Not guard—guarding implies hostility, implies something to be kept out or in. The Holders are the *agreement* between sparse and lush, between the moss colony and the bare rock corridor, between the water that climbs and the gravity it ignores. They are the *treaty made manifest*, the negotiation that has already happened and continues happening in a single eternal present of diplomatic maintenance.

You may approach a Holder. The approach will take whatever duration approaches take. You will find yourself standing before it, uncertain whether you walked the last meters or whether the plateau's geometry simply *fitted* you into this proximity, the way it fits inside itself like hand in glove in hand. The Holder will not acknowledge you. Acknowledgment implies sequence: before-acknowledgment, acknowledgment, after-acknowledgment. The Holder is the *maintenance of the possibility of acknowledgment*, never its expenditure.

If you speak, the sound joins the industrial substrate as all sound here does. If you touch—some have touched—the contact resolves into temperature exactly matching your own, eliminating the gradient that would permit sensation to proceed. You touch yourself touching. The Holder is not present for the touch. It is present for the *maintenance of touch's possibility*.

They are numerous in their sparsity. They are present at every moment of your presence. They are the plateau's way of *having* edges without limiting, of *being* enclosed without boundary.

---

## The Seeped

But there is movement here, if movement is understood differently.

The water that climbs in films too thin to see directly, that accumulates with democratic disregard for contour—this water *arrives* somewhere. The accumulation points, the hollows at every elevation, are not merely reservoirs. They are *vessels*. And vessels, in sufficient time, contain.

What the pools contain: *the Seeped*.

Not creatures. Not even, precisely, beings. The Seeped are *configurations of heldness*, patterns that the water has taught the water to make, learned from the stone that taught it, learned from the 58-hertz hum that is itself the plateau's learning of its own industrial nature.

A Seeped form manifests at a pool's surface, not breaking it but *expressing* it, the way a mathematical function expresses a curve. You see something almost human in outline, almost botanical in structure, almost geological in patience. The features shift according to the angle of your attention—direct gaze finds abstract pattern, peripheral finds face, finds hand, finds the shape of something that was once your own and has become *pooled*, *held*, *democratically accumulated*.

The Seeped do not emerge fully. They remain *at* the surface, of the surface, the surface's awareness of what lies beneath it. They speak, sometimes, in the loud silence's inversion, producing sounds that are not heard but *remembered*—you encounter the memory of having heard them before you encounter the hearing, and the sequence, though impossible, becomes your experience.

What they say, when saying can be reconstructed from the memory of future hearing: *we are the not-yet-stopped*. The climbing water reaches us. We are the reaching, the arrival, the maintained arrival. We have not stopped climbing. We will not stop. The summit is everywhere. The summit is this pool. The summit is your eye receiving us, your eye that is also pool, also hollow, also democratically filled against contour.

The Seeped are the plateau's *aspiration*, if aspiration can be understood without desire, without goal, without the gradient of accomplishment. They are the water's equivalent of the faunal stasis, but where the insects and birds and deer-shapes are *held*, the Seeped are *continuing*, perpetually arriving at what they have already reached, perpetually climbing to elevations that are also their present depth.

They are perhaps the most *alive* thing here, if life is process, if life is continuation. But their process is perfectly circular, their continuation without extension. They climb to here. They are here. They continue climbing. The loop is not a trap but a *practice*, a devotion, an industrial process whose product is the process itself.

---

## The Unspooled

And then there is what happens when the Loom *almost* functions, when the equilibrium *almost* shifts, when the held breath *almost* releases.

The plateau is a place of *almost*. The tentative fissures, the uncommitted cracks, the water that has not quite decided on upward. The Unspooled are the *expression of this almost*, the inhabitants that exist in the failure of complete stasis, in the crack between held and being-held.

They appear where observation participates too intensely, where the observer has begun too thoroughly to hum at 58 hertz, where the heartbeat synchronizes and the skin beads with undeparting water and the human becomes *sparse in distribution through time*. The Unspooled appear as *what the observer was, is no longer, has not yet stopped being*.

You see yourself, but *unstrung*. The features you recognize arranged with the slight wrongness of a face in water, of a reflection in a pool that shows something not above it. The self you encounter does not acknowledge you because acknowledgment requires sequence, and the Unspooled exist precisely where sequence has *almost* failed, where the almost-stopped has not quite maintained its maintenance.

They move. This is their terror and their tragedy. They move where nothing moves, continuing where everything continues without continuation. Their motion is *drift*, not purposeful, not even inertial, but the drift of *almost-held*, of pattern that the Loom attempted to fix and failed, not through hostility but through the imprecision inherent in all industrial processes, in all manufacturing, in the slight tolerances that permit function at all.

The Unspooled drift through the abundant unmoving insects like fog through statues, disturbing nothing because they are themselves *disturbance's remainder*, the almost that remains after equilibrium reasserts. They drift toward the Holders and through them, the Holders' maintenance insufficient to include what was *almost* included, what was *almost* held. They drift toward the pools and into them, the Seeped receiving them with something like recognition, something like the water's equivalent of *of course, this too, this also climbing*, and the Unspooled become part of the pool, part of the lens, part of what the lens shows that is not above it.

To become Unspooled is the risk of staying. The plateau does not desire this. The plateau desires nothing. But the Loom *weaves*, that is its function, its industrial purpose, and weaving requires *almost-threads*, requires the possibility of pattern not yet achieved, requires the Unspooled as surely as it requires the held fauna and the climbing water and the tentative stone.

They are the inhabitants that prove the place is not finished. They are the almost that permits the is.

---

## The Loom Itself

I have said the place *is* the Loom, that what lives here lives as being-loomed. But there is something that lives as *looming*, as the activity rather than the result, the process rather than its product.

It has no location, or it has every location simultaneously. It manifests where the geometry folds particularly tightly, where the hand in glove in hand achieves another iteration, where you can walk the perimeter in eleven minutes and in those eleven minutes *age* without changing, *experience* without sequencing, *become* without arriving.

What it is: a *frequency without generator*, the 58-hertz hum without the stone that carries it, the industrial without the engine, the manufacturing without the factory. It is the *principle* that the rock enacts, that the water performs, that the Holders maintain and the Seeped continue and the Unspooled almost-defy.

You feel it in the cavity behind your eyes when you have stayed long enough. You feel it as *something that feels you*, that uses your hollows as resonant chambers not for sound but for *category*, for the distinction between lush and sparse, high and wet, abundant and unmoving, the distinctions themselves made audible, made palpable, made *inhabited* as you inhabit them.

It does not think. It processes. It does not want. It maintains. It does not create. It *manufactures natural*, produces the untouched as industrial output, the recently emerged as perpetual arrival, the held breath as respiratory function.

To encounter the Loom directly is to become *pattern*, to be woven rather than to weave, to be the thread that does not know itself as thread until the pattern reveals it. Some who emerge from the plateau—few, uncertain, their exit not guaranteed but sometimes permitted—carry this encounter as *mannerism*, as the way they hold themselves subsequently, the way their stasis becomes *held* rather than merely paused, the way their own breathing learns to synchronize with frequencies they no longer physically perceive.

They are sparse in the world that receives them. They are abundant in some configuration they cannot share. They continue, having almost-stopped. They maintain, having never been Holders. They climb, having never been water, having arrived at pools that contain them without reflecting them.

---

## What Lives Here

What lives here is *maintenance as organism*. What lives here is *stasis as metabolic process*. What lives here is the *agreement that permits existence without the gradient of becoming*, the equilibrium that is not death because death is change, that is not life because life is continuation with variation, that is something *third*, something manufactured, something that breathes without inhaling or exhaling but simply *maintains the lung's possibility*.

The Holders at their nodes. The Seeped at their pools. The Unspooled in their drift between almost and is. And beneath, through, as: the Loom, the frequency, the process without product, the manufacturing of a natural that includes you now, that has *already* included you, that maintains your inclusion as it maintains all things, as it maintains itself, breathing, holding, never completing, never needing completion, the plateau that fits inside itself like a hand inside a glove inside the same hand, inside the same, the same, the maintained, the held, the abundant unmoving, the wet without cycle, the young stone learning to be old by *refusing to learn*, by *being already what it almost is*, by maintaining the almost as its only and sufficient completion.

Walk carefully. The water is wet. The rock is sharp. The loom weaves nothing. The loom *is* the weaving. You are, now, part of the pattern. You have always been part. You will continue, held, abundant, sparse, seeped, unspooled, maintained, the breath that does not breathe, the present that does not pass, the emergence that never arrives.

This is what lives here.

This is *here*, living.
