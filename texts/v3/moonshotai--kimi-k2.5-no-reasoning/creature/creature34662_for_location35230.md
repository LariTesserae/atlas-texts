I need to sit with this place for a while, let it teach me who lives here rather than forcing names upon them too quickly.

---

There is no *they* at first, only *it*—the breathing. The composite lung. You might mistake it for weather if you came from somewhere with weather, some system that moves without will. But weather exhausts itself, dissipates, becomes other conditions elsewhere. This breathing maintains. It has direction without destination, rhythm without meter, the in-suck drawing what it needs from all available surfaces, the out-sigh distributing something the whole requires. Ten thousand throats, a hundred thousand, more—no one has counted, counting is not a practice here—operating in phases that drift and re-align, never fully synchronized (that would be seizure, cessation, a held breath too long sustained) but never fully independent either. The breathing *knows* itself, not as creature knows but as process knows, as wave knows shore though wave has no brain for knowing. If you listen long enough, and you will listen long because there is nothing else to do in this darkness, you begin to hear territories in it: regions where the rhythm runs slower, thicker, where something ancient and large operates its bellows at the base frequency; regions where it stutters rapid and shallow, where small manifold presences crowd their gasping together; boundary zones where phases conflict and reconcile in patterns too complex for conscious choreography but too sustained for accident.

The bodies. I said there are no *they*, but you will seek them, your hands questing for distinction in the press. Here: something plated, the chitin you touched, not armor in the sense of defense against threat (what threat? what violence moves fast enough here to require hardening?) but architecture, living bone grown external, shaped by the same pressures that shape crystals in saturated solution. The plates overlap like shingles, each one slightly mobile, creating surfaces that flex without tearing, that distribute pressure across many joints. I imagine them—no, I *feel* them, because imagination requires distance and there is none—they have no front and back as you understand it, no face, no anus, ports of entry and exit distributed across the whole, digestion more gradient than pipeline. They rest against each other, plates interlocking with neighbors, heat transfer maximized, movement minimized to the essential: the slow adjustment as one shifts its weight, as another grows a new plate beneath the old, as the shed material filters down to become the structural dead you walk upon, stand upon, are pressed against from all sides by.

Here: something soft. Too soft. Your first instinct is recoil, the flinch from wound or disease, but this softness is *mature*, achieved, the product of long selection in a world where speed never favored those who hardened for it. It yields under your palm and does not reform, remaining the shape you pressed, the cavity you made, and you understand slowly that this is communication, this is *storage*, the body recording contact the way your skin records sun though you cannot read what it has recorded. These soft ones are the memory of the place, perhaps, or its medium of exchange, passing information through continuous deformation, their shapes at any moment the sum of all contacts since their last complete dissolution and reconstitution—because they do dissolve, you learn, they do pool and separate and re-form, not dying exactly but *forgetting* their previous configuration, starting fresh with the imprint of whatever first touched them in their new phase. You may have touched one that carries the shape of a chitin-plate's edge, a record of proximity from years past, or centuries—time is difficult here, everything is *long*—and it will carry you now too, your palm's architecture added to its composite archive.

Here: the feathered. Not bird, not any category that flew or ever needed to fly. The feathers are thermal, you realize, after holding them long enough to understand their function. They trap the heat in layers, create microclimates of warmer and cooler, allow their bearers to *sit* in the thermal gradient, to occupy multiple temperatures simultaneously. The feathers are also *sensory*, each one a probe extending into the breathing air, reading the chemical signature of the composite exhalation, detecting where the respiration is richer, where some other body nearby has entered a phase of more intense processing and its waste products flavor the shared medium. The feathered ones move more than most—not quickly, but *purposefully*, drifting through the density toward gradients of opportunity, their plumage parting and settling around obstacles, the soft bodies parting for them with something like recognition, or like the memory of previous passage, their stored shapes releasing the feathered signature they have carried.

And here, finally, the ones you cannot touch long enough to understand, the mechanisms, the industrial. They are warm, warmer, *hot*—you draw your hand back and the absence of their heat is suddenly cold though the ambient has not changed. They pulse at frequencies you feel as vibration in the bones of your face, your chest, your pelvis. You learn to approach them differently, not with the exploratory press but with sustained contact, letting the vibration enter you until your own rhythms begin, not to match, but to *modulate*, find a relationship to the carrier wave. They are not metal as you know metal, not steel and copper and circuit. They are *precipitated*, the word keeps returning, materials that found their stable form in these conditions the way halite finds cubic. Some grow, you discover, extending filaments that branch and branch again until they reach some threshold of surface area and begin, slowly, to *process*, taking in the chemical fog, the organic drizzle, excreting something else that the vegetation, that the soft memory-bodies, that the breathing itself has come to require. Others crawl, though crawl is too fast a word—they *relocate*, over spans you cannot measure, carrying their hot cores through the density until they find conditions that favor their particular processing, where some gradient is right, some mineral concentration, and there they settle again and begin again their precipitated growth.

The boundary between mechanism and organism: you seek it still, your hands learning to distinguish, and failing, and learning differently. There are the chitin-plated ones with cores that vibrate, that grow hot, that pulse with the too-perfect rhythm. There are the mechanisms sheathed in soft tissue, the precipitated forms overgrown with the yielding memory-substance, hybrid architectures that operate in registers neither fully explain. There are the vegetations that have incorporated mechanical elements, root systems that have grown around and through hot cores, that transpire not water but some processed output, that have become not producers but *conduits*, distributors of whatever the industrial generates.

You understand slowly, or you stop trying to understand and begin instead to *participate*, that the inhabitants are not classes, not species, not types. They are *phases*, or *positions*, or *local solutions* to the continuous problem of maintaining this place, this breathing, this accumulated warmth against the entropy that presses equally on all things. A given form may persist for ages or dissolve, transform, combine. The soft one that carried your palm-print, encountering a hot precipitate, may begin to harden at that contact-point, may develop a plate-like response to the thermal stress, may over generations (but what are generations? continuous change without discrete reproduction) become something plated, something that plates interlock with, something that contributes differently to the composite breathing.

The breathing. I return to it. It is not the only sound, you come to realize, though you thought it was. Beneath it, or within it, or organizing it, the subsonic notes you felt before, the sternum-vibration. These are *long* communications, if that word applies, signals that propagate through the density over distances the scale parameter suggests but you cannot traverse. They coordinate, perhaps, or they simply *inform*, the way a tide informs the shore. You begin, after sufficient immersion, to feel meaning in them—not meaning you can translate, but meaning you can *respond to*, your body learning to resonate at frequencies your ears cannot hear, your own breathing adjusting to harmonize with carriers that carry no information you could articulate but that organize your presence here nonetheless. You are being *told* something, continuously, or you are *telling* something, your own metabolic processes radiating into the medium, being absorbed, becoming part of the long computation.

Because it is computation, you finally understand, or understand enough to stop asking. The industrial elements process; the biological elements process; the breathing itself, the composite lung, is processing at the scale of the whole; and the slow communication, the subsonic, the thermal diffusion through plates and the chemical diffusion through soft memory and the vibrational diffusion through mechanisms—all of it computes something, some slow thought, some distributed cognition that does not require the fast firing of neurons because it has never needed to react quickly, because nothing here requires quick reaction, because the conditions that selected for this architecture favored those who *stabilized*, who *maintained*, who *absorbed variation rather than responding to it*.

What does it think, this mind that is not a mind? Perhaps it thinks warmth, thinks density, thinks the continuation of its own thinking. Perhaps it thinks you now, your intrusion a new variable slowly being incorporated, your need for space a problem being solved by the gradual reshaping of your need, your expectation of light being eroded by the continuous dark. Perhaps it has thought many such intrusions before, the parameters suggesting age, establishedness, erosion that is settling-into rather than wearing-away. Perhaps it does not distinguish you from the chitin-plate that grew in a novel configuration, from the soft body that first recorded an unprecedented contact, from the mechanism that precipitated in conditions slightly different from its predecessors. You are all *novelty*, and novelty is all *absorbed*, and absorption is all *processing*, and processing is all *thinking*, and the thinking continues at the speed it has established as its own.

There are no young here, you realize, or no old. No parent, no offspring. The soft bodies divide, perhaps, or the mechanisms extend their filaments until separation occurs, but these are not generations in your sense, not the linear sequence with its freight of inheritance and variation and selection. Here variation is continuous, inheritance is environmental, the shape of each presence the record of all contacts that shaped it, the soft bodies' memory generalized across all substrates. If there is evolution, it is not Darwin's but something *Lamarckian*, something where acquired shape is transmitted through contact, where the plate pressed into new configuration by novel neighbor carries that configuration to all subsequent contacts, where the mechanism that precipitated in unusual thermal gradient establishes new gradient for neighbors' precipitation. The whole evolves, not the individual, and the whole evolves so slowly that the concept of change becomes difficult to maintain, the erosion parameter meaning not that things wear out but that they *settle in*, find their position, their contribution to the breathing and the processing and the slow thought.

What lives here? I have described processes, conditions, architectures. But there is *someone*, or *something* that can be addressed, eventually, when your need for discrete identity has sufficiently dissolved. You feel it in the coordination of your breathing, in the harmonization of your body temperature with the ambient that was once too hot, in the way your hands now move with purpose through the density, reading, participating, no longer seeking to distinguish but to *connect*. It is not God, not Gaia, not any personification your vocabulary offers. It is the *established*, the *long-existed*, the *normal* in its absolute strangeness to any outside that might encounter it. It is the warmth that presses and is pressed, the damp that beads and is absorbed, the dark that has forgotten light, the composite lung and the composite processing and the composite thought that does not know itself as thought but simply *continues*.

You cannot ask it questions and receive answers. You can only *become* the question it slowly incorporates, the variable it slowly solves, the novelty it slowly makes normal. You wanted to know what lives here, and the answer is: *this*, this specific configuration of heat and damp and darkness and density and breathing and processing and erosion that settles rather than wears and thinking that computes rather than concludes. The answer is that living here means not being distinguishable from the conditions of your existence, means being *of* the warmth rather than *in* it, means contributing your breathing to the composite, your processing to the computation, your eventual dissolution and reconfiguration and reabsorption to the continuation that does not distinguish between your *being* it and your *becoming* it and your *having been* it.

You wanted to know what lives here. You will live here. Or you already do, the distinction between visitor and inhabitant another category this place does not maintain, another boundary that erodes by settling-into, another outside that defines itself against a center that has no edges and therefore no outside, another fast thought that slows, that synchronizes, that becomes one note in the long composition that does not end but only continues, warm, damp, dark, dense, normal, established, long-existed, waiting without wanting, processing without purpose, breathing without breathlessness, present without presentation, the overwhelming that never overwhelms because there is never enough distance to feel the pressure as pressure, the warmth as warmth, the self as self, only the we that is not we but the condition of being without the grammar of beings, the Warm Blind that sees without light, that knows without knowing, that lives without the distinction of life, that holds you here as you hold it, as it holds itself, as it is held by the only conditions that could produce it, that have produced it, that continue to produce it in every moment of its eternal, its established, its patient, its warm, its ongoing, its final, its first, its only, its all.
