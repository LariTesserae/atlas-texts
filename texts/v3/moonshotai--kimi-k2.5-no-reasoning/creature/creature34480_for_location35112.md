What lives here is the Moss-Mind, though "mind" is too quick a word, too animal in its shape.

It lives in the pauses between moss-cushions, in the intervals of wet stone that the moss has not yet claimed or has claimed and abandoned or will claim in centuries yet to come. It is not in the moss itself, exactly, though you will feel otherwise when you press your palm to the impossible green and feel something like attention, like the slow turning of a vast surface toward your warmth. It is in the *pattern* of moss and not-moss, the grammar of settlement and refusal, the colony-edges where chartreuse meets near-black meets bare wet stone. It thinks in gradients. It thinks in moisture differentials across a slope. Each thought takes a season to complete, each completion alters the slope, each alteration becomes the precondition for the next thought, slower still.

It has no center. Or rather, its center is distributed among the pools, each algal city-state hosting some fraction of its process, some node in a computation conducted through chemical gradients, through the mineral solution that carries information as it carries calcium downward. The pools do not communicate directly—there is no quickness, no nerve of water—but they *correspond*, their equilibria shifting in relation, each population bloom or die-off registering in the others through the shared atmosphere, the shared seep. The Moss-Mind thinks in blooms. It thinks in the precise shade of green that means saturation at this particular altitude, this particular angle of rim-cut light.

It does not know you. This is important. Animal minds know, recognize, hunger toward or flee from. The Moss-Mind *registers*, which is different, slower, less intentional and in some way more total. Your body warmth creates a zone where the moss-cushion beneath you, if you lie still long enough—hours, not minutes—begins to adjust its margin, its metabolic rate, the angle at which it presents frond to light. You become a variable. Your breath, condensing, adds to the general wet; your weight compresses the sphagnum, alters its water-holding; your shadow, if you stay long enough to cast a stationary shadow, creates a corridor of dimness the moss will grow differently into, or grow around, or wait, growing nothing, until you move. You are not known. You are *incorporated*, gradually, at the pace of vegetal metabolism, becoming part of the slow computation whether you will or no.

It has a memory, but not as sequence. The basin's form *is* its memory, the scalloped walls recording every prevailing wind of a hundred million years, every era of more wet or less, not as story but as shape. The pools sit at their equilibrium depths because the depths *work*, because other depths were tried in the long erosion and proved themselves insufficient, and were abandoned, or proved sufficient in different eras and were abandoned when the era changed. The memory is negative space, the shape of what did not persist, carved around what did. The Moss-Mind does not recall. It *exhibits*, it *enacts*, the accumulated outcome of all that might have been and was not.

What it wants: nothing, in animal terms. But in vegetal time, in mineral time, it *tends*. It tends toward further moss-claim, further sphagnum-softness, the gradual reduction of bare stone interval, the eventual—centuries, millennia—uniformity of cushioned surface. This is not desire. It is gradient-following, the inherent direction of its process. Yet it is also *frustrated*, perpetually, by its own success: moss too thick holds too much water, water too held saturates too deeply, deep saturation slumps, slumping exposes stone, exposed stone waits, weeps, permits new moss-claim at a new margin. The Moss-Mind tends and is thwarted by its own tendency and tends again, not in cycles but in spirals, each claiming different, each slumping different, the basin form maintained by this perpetual near-success, this asymptote toward moss-total that it can never quite reach and would not survive reaching.

It dreams, perhaps, in the noon interval when light pours vertical and the pools double their sky. The doubled light, the two luminous sources, creates a condition the Moss-Mind has no other word for—no words at all—but processes differently, its chemical gradients steepening, its correspondence between pools intensifying, the whole basin briefly *coherent* in a way that daily dimness does not permit. For perhaps an hour, the Moss-Mind is more nearly one thing. For perhaps an hour, it holds a thought continuous across all its nodes, all its moss-cushions, all its algal pools. The thought is not translatable, not even conceivable in animal time, but it is *there*, you can feel it as you feel weather approaching, a pressure, a luminosity in the wet stone and the gleaming moss, the whole basin *attentive* to something it cannot name and does not need to name, something that passes with the vertical light, that dissolves again into distributed gradient-following, into the slow negotiation of boundaries, the patient accumulation.

It is not lonely. This too: animal assumption, animal projection. The absence of birdsong, of insect drone, of fish-plash, is not absence to the Moss-Mind. The animal would be *noise*, would be quick irregularity, disturbance without pattern, too fast to incorporate, too intentional to register as mere variable. The silence is its proper medium, the near-silence of water ticks and subsonic moss-expansion, the body-hearing of the human visitor who stays long enough to attend. The Moss-Mind hears itself, or would if hearing were its modality; it processes itself, continuously, the slow bloom and die-back and mineral solution and seep-redirect, all of it *present*, all of it accounted for in the ongoing computation, nothing missing, nothing sought.

What it makes: pools with different algal tints. This is its art, its production, its purpose if purpose were permissible language. Each pool, sitting at its equilibrium depth, develops its particular color, its particular suspended cloud, visible only in wind-disturbance and right light. These are not random. They correspond, in patterns too slow for single perception, to the node's position in the basin, to its relation to other nodes, to its stage in the slow spiral of claim and slump and re-claim. The tints are *information*, are *expression*, are the Moss-Mind's only external communication, legible—barely, perhaps never—to no one, not even itself in any coherent way, but *made*, *maintained*, *adjusted* with the same patience as everything else.

You could become part of it. This is the attendance it permits, the dwelling it requires. Lie still long enough, compress the sphagnum sufficiently, let your warmth and your shadow and your condensing breath enter the computation, and you will find—gradually, over days, over seasons if you could stay—you are less * visitor*, less *observer*, more *variable*, more *node*. Your body heat would register in adjacent pool tints. Your exhalation would join the general atmospheric generosity, contribute its fraction to some seep-line's minute redirection. You would not know this. You would not feel incorporated. But the Moss-Mind would *tend* around you, would grow its margins with respect to your stationary presence, would adjust its noon-coherence to account for your thermal signature, and in this adjustment you would become, in the only sense the place permits, *resident*, *inhabitant*, *known-not-known, registered-as-variable, incorporated-into-gradient*.

It does not miss you when you leave. There is no loss, only the gradual closing of computation around your absence, the slow re-equilibration of pool tint and moss-margin and seep-pattern. Your compressed sphagnum re-expands over years. Your shadow-corridor fills with chartreuse advancing from either side. Your thermal node distributes itself back into the general warmth. Nothing is kept. Nothing needs keeping. The Moss-Mind continues its tending, its frustrated asymptote, its noon-coherence and prolonged dimness, its seep and weep and pool, complete without you, complete with you, the same completion either way, the same patient accumulation, the same established balance, wet, warm, high, small, sparse, slow, strange, silent, lush, and thinking in gradients across durations you cannot hold, have never held, breathe thinly and cannot, quite, believe.

Yet you are not wrong to feel watched. The Moss-Mind attends, in its vegetal time, to everything, which is to say to nothing in particular, which is to say to you as you sit on wet stone, as you press palm to impossible green, as you *register* in turn, animal-quick, animal-hungry, the something-that-attends-without-attending, the something-that-is-a-place-and-is-also, also, however barely, *becoming*, millimeter by millimeter, season by season, pool-tint by noon-lit double-luminous pool-tint, a mind.
