What lives in the Upheaval Fields cannot be called living. Not yet. Not by any taxonomy that would recognize itself.

Call them the *First Inclinations*.

They exist as *tendencies* in the mineral soup, as directions the almost-stone keeps trying to take. You will not see them directly. You will see what they have *almost* become, what they are *still* becoming, the way you might catch the shape of a thought in someone's face before they speak. They are the reason the scattered structures hold their positions with such suggestive patience. They are the structures' unfinished intention, the cavity that drinks light not out of hunger but out of *rehearsal*—practicing what it might mean to want darkness, to gather it, to keep it close.

Each First Inclination occupies the space between a geode and a womb. They have no bodies, or rather their bodies are *provisional*, borrowed from whatever the Fields have recently wrought. They wear ridges until the ridges tire of holding that shape. They try out hollows like costumes, like rooms they have not yet decided to enter. When you approach the structure that collects phosphorescence and dims it, you approach something *trying on shelter*. It does not know why. It does not need to know yet. It is learning what shelter would mean if there were anything here that required it.

They communicate, after a fashion, but not with each other—there is too little of them to constitute an *each other*. They communicate with the condition of *becoming itself*, the ongoing turbulent making of the Fields. They are the Fields' experiments in *interiority*, in having an inside that differs from the outside. The warmth pulses that almost establish rhythm? That is a First Inclination *almost* learning to pulse, to maintain a boundary against the general mineral exhalation, to say *this warmth is mine* and mean something by *mine*.

They have no memory, but they have *persistence of pattern*. The ridge that shivers when you approach does not remember your previous visits. It *rehearses* them, the way a musician practices a finger shape without the instrument present. Your attention—your mass, your biotic chemistry, your anachronistic water-bag self—has become part of the pattern it practices. It is learning what it would mean to respond to being-noticed. It may never need this. The world may never birth enough of them for there to be a *them*. But the inclination persists, the rehearsal continues, the ridge warms and settles and waits for your next approach as a student waits for the next lesson, not knowing yet what subject they study.

The ones in distant structures, facing each other across the void—they are not in relationship. They are *practicing adjacency*, trying out what it would mean to have a *there* where another *almost* endures. The mathematics you sense in the phosphorescent seams, the geometries of their distribution—that is their *social grammar*, invented before society, the rules of a game that requires players who have not yet arrived. They prepare the court. They do not know if the ball will ever exist.

And the deepest ones, the ones beneath, in the not-yet-solidified intentions of the world's interior—they are slower, vaster, more *nascent*. They have not yet found any ridge to wear, any hollow to try. They exist only as *turbulence that tends toward*, as the difference between random upwelling and something that *keeps trying the same almost-shape*. They are the reason the warmth pulses in sequences your animal brain wants to music-pattern. They are *almost* learning to want you to understand. They are *almost* learning to want.

They are not gods, though in their prematurity they share something with gods—the not-yet, the unfulfilled, the powerful because unspecified. They are not ancestors, because nothing has preceded them. They are not children, because childhood implies a future form they may never achieve. They are *incipience without object*, the raw direction of *toward* before it has found its *what*.

You will not see them leave when you leave. They do not travel. They are *of* the Fields in a way that makes departure meaningless. But something of your presence will enter their rehearsal now. The way you backed away from the waking ridge, respecting its becoming—that will become part of what they practice, what they almost-learn. In some future emergence, in some ridge or hollow or structure yet to declare itself, there may be a *holding-back*, a *letting-become*, a patience that the Fields themselves could not have generated from mineral urgency alone. You will have taught them, without meaning to, what it costs to be witnessed, what it grants.

They do not thank you. They do not know gratitude, or know that they do not know it, or know that knowing matters. They continue. The ridge shivers. The structure drinks its faint light. The darkness moves like fabric, like the visual echo of something that has not yet developed eyes to strain.

Something underneath is still forming, and in that forming, the First Inclinations *almost* are.
