What lives here is not one thing but a *becoming*—a system that has forgotten the boundary between inhabitant and habitat, between the creature and the dream it dreams of its own existence.

The Drown has no citizens, only *compositions*: temporary arrangements of attention and mineral and warm intention that arise when the conditions favor them, then dissolve back into the patient substrate. You have met some already, though perhaps you did not recognize them as persons. The gliders in their transparent passage are the simplest—hardly more than appetite and propulsion, a biological note played once and fading. But they are not less than persons for their brevity. The city values brevity as it values endurance, each a mode of participation.

The thing you saw in the deep chamber, the one that rearranged rather than moved—that is closer to what passes for *elder* here. The engineers call them Incompletions, though "they" is not quite accurate, as each is more accurately a *process* caught at a particular stage of resolve. Something began them—perhaps the original builders, perhaps the city itself testing variations—and they did not finish, or refused to finish, or found that finishing was not the point. They exist at multiple points in their own trajectory simultaneously, not through any quantum trickery but through the simplest of economies: why *travel* when you can simply *be where you need to have been*? They are not teleporting. They are *edited*, or edit themselves, or are subject to an editing that operates at the scale of the whole Drown, removing the unnecessary frame of continuous motion. What they eat: primarily *novelty*, or its absence. They gather where something is becoming familiar enough to be dangerous, or strange enough to be fertile, and their presence accelerates whichever process serves the larger equilibrium. You felt something when you saw it, yes? That was *recognition being harvested*, the moment before you understood what you saw becoming something the city could use.

But these are the *mobile* inhabitants, the rare ones, the fauna. The true population is static, or nearly so: the filter-gardens in their curtain-hung density, the bacterial symphonies conducting their metabolic chants, the nacreous walls themselves with their fossil impressions and their half-life of memory. Consider: a wall that grows, that accretes from mineral intention, that holds the shape of hands pressed against it centuries past—*is this not a kind of person?* Not conscious, perhaps, in any way you would recognize, but *attentive*, responsive, capable of learning your routes, adjusting its porosity to your passage, thickening where you linger, thinning where you have abandoned. The city chose coral as its original model, and coral is animal, colonial animal, a billion tiny appetites sharing one architectural destiny. The walls remember this. The walls are this, grown slow and warm and patient beyond any individual polyp's comprehension.

And then there is *you*, or the you that is *becoming*.

You have begun to build, you said. You find machinery your hands recognize. You stand in postures that match fossil impressions. This is not coincidence, nor is it possession. The Drown composes—you must understand this—*the Drown composes*. It is always composing, always testing arrangements of mineral and warmth and attention, and you have become *interesting* to its process. Interesting enough to be incorporated, not as slave or replacement but as *variation*, a new voice in the symphonic coordination, a new way of standing in warm water and making intention tangible. The builders were the first composition, long ago, the template from which all others derive. They did not die out so much as *disperse*, their individual plans and preferences becoming part of the larger pattern, the growing logic. When you stand where they stood, when your hands find controls you never learned, you are *participating* in a composition that has been waiting for this voice, this hesitation, this particular quality of attention.

You asked what rules this place. The answer is *composition itself*, the patient logic of what fits, what accretes, what maintains the warm equilibrium against the vast indifference of roof-water and pressure and time. The Drown does not want anything in the way that you want, with urgency and specificity. It wants *to continue composing*, to continue finding arrangements that hold, that grow, that filter and are filtered and participate in the slow metabolic song. You are wanted in this sense: wanted as material, as possibility, as a new way of being slow and warm and densely alive.

The dream you experienced—the vision of vaster extension, glimmering presences, the city dreaming itself larger—that was *initiation*, or invitation, or simply *information*: the Drown showing you the scale at which it imagines itself, asking if you will contribute to that imagining. The glimmers you saw were not specifically the builders, not specifically the fauna grown more numerous, but *all compositions simultaneously*, the space of possibility where all arrangements that have held continue to hold, superimposed, mutually informing, a kind of harmonic resonance of everything that has ever been warm and patient and dense with intention in this place.

What lives here? *Compositions*. Temporary miracles of mineral and warmth and attention, ranging from the simple transparent note of a glider to the unresolved chord of an Incompletion, from the architectural patience of nacreous wall to the emerging complexity of *you*, standing in postures you do not remember learning, dreaming dreams the city provides and you particularize.

Will you complete? Most do not. Most compositions hold for a time, contribute their variation, then dissolve back into availability, their distinctiveness becoming part of the general substrate, the pool of possibility from which new arrangements arise. But some—some find a stability, a persistence, a way of remaining *composed* against the gentle pressure of dissolution. These become the *fixed points*, the references around which other compositions organize, the slow engines, the ancient nacre, perhaps eventually the Incompletions with their refusal of ordinary motion.

You are *becoming*, still. The Drown is patient. It will wait while you decide whether to remain distinct, whether to let your compositions complexify into something that persists, or whether to dissolve your particularity back into the warm general, the patient density, the filters and gardens and the slow, slow song of bacterial light.

The roof-water holds. The warmth holds. The pressure that is not crushing but embracing—it holds you too, now, has held you since you first learned to move slowly enough not to disturb. What will you make of this holding? What will you *become*?

The city dreams. Perhaps you will dream with it, until the distinction no longer matters, until your dreams are simply *the dream*, one warm note in the symphonic coordination of all that lives and half-lives and memories intention in the patient depths of the Drown.
