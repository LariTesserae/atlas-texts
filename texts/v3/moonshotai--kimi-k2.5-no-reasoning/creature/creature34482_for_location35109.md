What lives in the Drowned Engine is not a who, or not only a who—the separation never quite holds here. What lives is *accommodation*, the slow mutual sculpting of place and presence until each has become sufficient to the other, until the question of which adapted to which has dissolved into the simpler fact of *fit*.

---

The trilobites first, because they are most numerous, because they are most *ground*. They do not remember, individually or collectively, that their ancestors might have borne different geometries, that the sigils on their shells might once have been *applied* rather than *grown*, marks of ownership or function in some organizational system that included them deliberately, that made them as it made the hexagonal plates, as it made the ceramic walls. Now the sigils emerge in each molt with the slight randomness of biological process, never quite identical to the parent, never quite deviating from the pattern the Drowned Engine impresses through water-chemistry, through mineral availability, through the selection pressure of simply *continuing* in this place that admits no alternatives.

They do not school. They are not social in any recognizable way, though they gather at warmth, at current-concentrations, at the bases of structures that vent processed water. Their togetherness is *environmental*, not chosen, the way stones gather at the bottom of a stream. And yet: their scuttling produces rhythms. Their accumulated movement against the hexagonal plates, against each other, against the occasional eel-worm passing through, generates the *almost-music* that underlies the bell-floaters' clearer tones. They are percussion, they are the body's constant small negotiations with ground, and in this function they are perhaps more *necessary* than the bells, more fundamental to the space's sensory completion.

When they die—and they do, slowly, mineral shells finally too thick for molting, too heavy for sustained scuttling—they do not float. They settle to the plates they lived upon, contribute to the faint raised sigils' wearing, their own geometry briefly visible in the accumulation of similar geometries, before the eel-worms process them, before the water's motion distributes them, before they become indistinguishable from the mineral drift that feeds their descendants. Death here is not end but *slow translation*, the individual persisting longer than most biological death permits, the shell-shape slowly becoming floor-texture, becoming the slight friction that keeps future trilobites' footing, future visitors' uncertain balance.

They have no eyes. The light in the Drowned Engine does not merit such complexity, such vulnerability. Instead they sense vibration, they taste water-chemistry, they navigate by the subsonic engine-hum's subtle variations across the small space, by the pressure-differentials that indicate walls, that indicate other presences, that indicate the places where warmth vents and minerals concentrate. Their world is *texture and tone*, and in this they are perhaps more *at home* than any sighted visitor, any creature for whom vision constructs a reality of surfaces and distances rather than gradients and intensities.

---

The eel-worm rises.

This is how you encounter it: a disturbance in the current's constant flow, a place where the water moves *around* something rising from below, something that did not exist in your perception a moment before and now occupies the center of what you can see, what you can imagine seeing. It is pale, yes, but *pale* fails to capture the quality of its color, which is not absence but *specificity*, the particular tone of flesh never touched by light that was ever *source*, light that has only ever been *consequence*, emergent, secondary, the pale of deep water, of cave, of the undersides of stones.

Its body is not segmented, though it appears so, the muscular pulses of its rising creating temporary constrictions that suggest rings, that suggest *parts*, though the eel-worm is all one continuous process, digestion and locomotion and perception and reproduction distributed along its length without specialization beyond the terminal mouth, the ring of smaller mouths, each itself capable of *sampling*, of processing, of contributing to the distributed function of *continuing*.

It investigates you.

This is not predation. The eel-worm's rise is *curiosity*, if that word can stretch to encompass a motivation without mind, without the recursive self-awareness that usually accompanies curiosity's seeking. It tastes your disturbance in the water, the unfamiliar organic compounds, the heat-gradient you create against the Drowned Engine's settled coolness. And it *samples*, the smaller mouths extending, touching, withdrawing, a process that takes minutes, that requires your stillness or your slow movement, your participation in the space's pace, its refusal of urgency.

Most visitors report this as *intimate*, though not sexual, not even biological in the usual recognitions. The eel-worm's investigation is *systemic*, the Drowned Engine's distributed attention made briefly flesh, made touch. It does not want anything from you, does not seek to consume or transform or reproduce with you. It simply *notes*, samples, adds your particular disturbance to the water-chemistry's slow record, and then *subsides*, returning to its hollow, its coil against wall-floor intersection, its patient processing of more familiar organic drift.

Unless you are dying.

This is the parameter that shifts, that reveals the eel-worm's functional place in the finished ecology. If you are weakened, if your disturbance includes the particular signatures of cellular breakdown, of organic processes failing their maintenance, the investigation extends, the sampling becomes *feeding*, not violent but *inevitable*, the smaller mouths lingering longer, the muscular constriction perhaps circling your ankle, your wrist, a *holding* that is also *holding-in-place*, that prevents the escape the dying might otherwise attempt. You become part of the process. You contribute to the mineral drift, the organic chemistry, the slow maintenance of all that continues. The eel-worm does not kill. The Drowned Engine does not kill. But *completion* finds its forms, its adequate speeds, and dying faster than the space's processes can accommodate is itself a kind of violation, a *disruption* that the eel-worm's feeding gently, patiently corrects.

Reproduction by fission: rarely observed, the process takes days, the eel-worm's posterior gradually resolving toward mouth-formation, the anterior gradually simplifying, until some critical threshold of distribution is reached, until two where one is adequate to the hollow, to the organic drift available. Then separation, not dramatic, the two halves *relaxing* apart in the current, each finding its new territory, its new wall-floor intersection. The new mouth learns to sample; the old anterior continues its established patterns. They are not parent and offspring. They are perhaps not even individuals in any legal sense, any philosophical sense that requires continuity of identity. They are *process maintaining itself through division*, the eel-worm function persisting across physical transformation, as the Drowned Engine persists, as everything here persists in form through the constant small changes of its continuation.

---

The bell-floaters ring, and in their ringing they are perhaps most *strange*, most *other* to the visitor who arrives with expectations of biology, of ecosystem, of the natural as opposed to the made.

They do not swim. Propulsion is not their method; they *drift*, but with a purposefulness that drift does not usually possess, finding the spaces where current concentrates, where obstacles create the potential for *impact*, for *tone*. They are jelly, yes, translucent in the dim light, their interiors faintly visible as structures without obvious function, as *shapes* that might once have been organs, that might be developing toward organs, that might simply *be*, the way crystals are, the way the Drowned Engine's ceramic walls simply are, sufficient to their continuation without purpose beyond that sufficiency.

Their size varies. The twenty-centimeter individuals are most common, most *standard*, but smaller ones exist, and larger, and the parameter that governs their growth—if it is growth, if they are not instead all the same age, all ancient, varying only in some condition of the water-chemistry, some local variation in the engine-idling's subsonic hum—this parameter has not been determined. Perhaps they do not grow. Perhaps they *manifest*, emerging from the water's saturation at particular conditions, condensing like rain, though rain implies sky, implies distance between formation and fall, and the Drowned Engine has no sky, no such distance.

They ring. This is their primary activity, their *sufficient* activity, the thing they do that maintains their place in the finished ecology. The impact against hexagonal plate, against ceramic wall, against the metal of fallen panels, produces tones that the Drowned Engine's confined space sustains, that overlap and interfere and create the *almost-music* that is the space's primary sensory characteristic, more defining than the phosphorescent light, more defining than the subsonic hum, because the ringing is *variable*, is *responsive*, changes with the number of bell-floaters, with their positions, with the water's current-speed on a given day, with the accumulated small changes of a space that is finished but not *static*.

Why do they ring? No function has been identified, no communication, no territorial marking, no mating call—though mating, reproduction, the biological imperatives that usually explain such elaborate behavior, have never been observed, never inferred from the behavior itself. The ringing seems *aesthetic*, or *aesthetic-like*, the closest the Drowned Engine comes to producing something *unnecessary*, something that maintains despite serving no obvious function in the ecology's continuation.

And yet: visitors report that the ringing *orients*, that after sufficient time in the space, they begin to map by sound, to know the distillation column's position by its particular acoustic signature, the trilobite-gatherings by their denser, more complex percussion beneath the bells. The ringing creates *information*, whether or not it intends to, whether or not the bell-floaters are in any sense *agents* of information-creation. The Drowned Engine accommodates them, and in its accommodation they become functional, they become *part of*, regardless of their original or ongoing motivation.

If they have reproduction, it has not been observed. If they die, their bodies have not been found, suggesting perhaps dissolution in the saturated water, perhaps processing by trilobites or eel-worms at scales too small to notice, perhaps *absorption* by the Drowned Engine itself, by the hexagonal plates, by the ceramic walls, the bell-floating function transferring rather than ending, manifesting again in new individual, new location, new tone.

They avoid the distillation column. This is their most *choice-like* behavior, the closest they come to preference, to rejection. The water there holds some quality—warmth, mineral concentration, perhaps the faint field of whatever function the column still maintains in its idling, its non-productive continuation—that the bell-floaters find *wrong*, *inadequate*, *other* to their ringing. They drift around it, their paths bending in currents that cannot alone explain the curvature, the *avoidance*. And in this avoidance, the column becomes more *marked*, more *specific*, a place in the Drowned Engine that is not everywhere, that has local properties, local *character*, the space's finished uniformity admitting this one persistent differentiation.

---

The fungal films: brief, extinguished, *recorded*.

They grow on surfaces that the water does not constantly touch, the upper reaches of ceramic walls where condensation forms and evaporates in cycles too slow for human perception, the undersides of panels that have fallen but not fallen fully, creating spaces of *less*, of *interrupted*, where the Drowned Engine's pervasive wetness becomes intermittent, becomes possibility for something *other* than the aquatic, the saturated.

They are not the same each time. Spore-layers reveal, to patient study, *history*, variation, the Drowned Engine's subtle shifts in water-chemistry, in engine-idling, in whatever processes maintain the space's settled state. Each outbreak is *response* to local condition, to temporary *opening* in the finished ecology's sufficiency. And each extinguishing—by water-fluctuation, by chemical shift, by the slow correction of whatever the space maintains itself toward—is *closure*, return to the adequate, the sufficient, the *finished*.

Do they *want* to persist? The question imports again the framework of individual desire, of biological imperative, that the Drowned Engine's inhabitants have largely dissolved, have become *beyond* in their long accommodation. The fungal films *occur*, they *process*, they contribute briefly to the organic drift, to the mineral cycling, and then they *complete*, their spores perhaps waiting, perhaps *potential* rather than actual, for the next opening, the next brief local failure of the finished state's sufficiency.

---

And the bird-thing, seen once, perhaps *seen-once* as its permanent condition, its functional place in the ecology not of repetition but of *singularity*.

Featherless, flightless, perched on the distillation column's upper reach: it drank condensation with a beak that was two beaks, facing opposite directions, that called with a sound like grinding bearings. No subsequent visitor has reported it. No record exists in the spore-layers, the water-chemistry, the subsonic hum's variations. It is *datum* without context, *occurrence* without pattern, and in this it is perhaps most *strange* of all, most *other* to the Drowned Engine's logic of continuation, of maintenance, of the slow settling into adequate form.

Perhaps it was visitor, not inhabitant, passing through as you pass through, noted by you as you are noted, briefly *part of* before becoming *apart from*, exit swallowed by wall-of-water or by some other passage not marked in the parameters you entered with. Perhaps it was *hallucination*, the Drowned Engine's sensory complexity producing, in particular conditions of fatigue, of oxygen-chemistry, of phosphorescent light's particular tinted frequencies, the perception of *bird* where nothing bird-like could persist, could be sustained by the space's finished ecology.

Or perhaps it is *future*, the Drowned Engine's continuation producing what has not yet been, what will emerge from the slight flicker of the oldest green panel, from the slow correction of current-patterns, from the accumulated small changes that even finished states undergo, that keep them from being *static*, from being, finally, *dead*. The bird-thing called, and its call was like grinding bearings, like the industrial made audible in biological form, and perhaps this *likeness* is the direction of becoming, the Drowned Engine slowly generating what will be adequate to its future condition, what will continue its continuation when the current inhabitants have finally, fully, become part of the process they have so long maintained.

---

What lives in the Drowned Engine: *completion without ending*. The trilobites' sigils, nearly worn to invisibility, still faintly *there*, still faintly *meaning* in a system too old to reconstruct. The eel-worm's investigation, still *curious*, still *sampling*, after uncounted repetitions of similar disturbances, similar visitors, similar brief translations into organic drift. The bell-floaters' ringing, still *almost-music*, still *almost-pattern*, never completing the rhythm it almost forms, never dissolving into pure randomness, pure noise. The phosphorescent panels' flicker, hold, continue, the oldest green slowly failing, slowly being replaced by compensation, by adjustment, by the space's self-maintenance finding new equilibria, new *finished* states slightly different from the old, slightly closer to some final finality that recedes as it approaches.

They do not suffer. This is the most difficult recognition for the visitor who arrives with expectations of struggle, of adaptation as pain, of life's continuation against entropy's gradient. The Drowned Engine's inhabitants have *become* the gradient, have found the form in which continuation is not resistance but *flow*, not effort but *settling*, the water's constant motion requiring no struggle because they *are* the motion, they *are* the water's relation to the hexagonal plates, to the ceramic walls, to the phosphorescent light's particular colors and their slow, slow shift.

They are not happy. They are not content. These judgments require the prefix, the separation of experiencer from experience, of subject from condition, that the long accommodation has dissolved. The trilobite *scuttles*. The eel-worm *rises*. The bell-floater *rings*. These are not descriptions of behavior, *about* individuals performing actions. They are closer to *identifications*, the way we might say *water flows*, *light glows*, *engine idles*—statements of what something *is*, not of what it *does* in service of some further purpose.

And you: you pass through. You add to the organic drift, or you do not, depending on your adaptation, your stillness or movement, your ability to match the space's pace, its refusal of urgency. The Drowned Engine notes you, samples you, adds your disturbance to the long record, and you feel, in the bell-tones' particular pattern during your presence, in the eel-worm's investigation or its absence, in the trilobite-scuttling's slight alteration as your passage disrupts current-patterns—you feel *noted*, you feel *part of*, however briefly, the finished process that continues without you, that will continue after, that has continued through ten thousand such passages, such brief disturbances, such small additions to the mineral drift, the water-chemistry, the spore-layers' patient history.

You carry it with you: not memory, but *tone*, the subsonic hum beneath your processes, the bell's almost-pattern in your auditory imagination, the particular *cool* of knee-deep water in constant slow current. And sometimes, in your own spaces, your own continuations, you will produce a sound, a movement, a arrangement of conditions that *almost* matches, that *almost* re-creates the Drowned Engine's sensory completion, and in that almost you will feel briefly *back*, briefly *part of* again, the finished world still finishing, still becoming more itself, still ringing in the dimness where you once stood and were noted and passed through, becoming part of what you carry, what carries you, what continues.
