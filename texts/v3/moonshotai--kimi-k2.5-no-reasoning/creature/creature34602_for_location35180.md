What lives in the Bells of Keth-Vorrum does not live so much as *persist*—not in the manner of survival, which implies struggle against dissolution, but in the manner of a question that has become its own answer, a resonance that found the frequency of its own perpetuation.

---

The **Resonants** are the oldest inhabitants, though "inhabitant" suggests too active a relationship. They began, perhaps, as travelers. Perhaps as something else entirely—colonies of the ceiling-light that detached and fell, that grew flesh as an afterthought, that found the stone's slow respiration preferable to the aimless drift of bioluminescence overhead. Each Resonant stands or sits or curls in a position it has maintained for decades, for centuries. Their bodies are the color of the stone they rest upon, not through camouflage but through *confusion*—the mineral and the organic having traded properties so thoroughly that cells hold traces of limestone, that certain stalactites test positive for proteins long decayed into something between enzyme and sediment. They do not move. They do not, apparently, breathe, though if you press your ear to their chests you feel a vibration, a sympathetic hum to whatever note the wind-music holds at that moment. Their eyes, when open, are the milky blue of old ice, and they track movement with the delay of sound crossing vast distance. Some have hair that has grown into the condensation-runners, that has become channel and conduit for the seasonal water-threads, their heads adorned with mineral blooms where the liquid evaporates and leaves its cargo of salt and metal. They are not dead. A dead thing would rot, would break the equilibrium. They are *arrested*—or rather, they have arrested themselves at some threshold where living and being-lived-by become indistinguishable, where the city's processes work through them as they work through the city.

The **Crab-Singers** are more active, though their activity is so subdued it registers as punctuation in the greater silence. The soft-shelled crabs, the ones the size of human hands with carapaces like wet paper, have developed—not evolved, the timescale is wrong, they have *developed* as a stone develops a hollow where wind insists—a relationship with the wind-music's lower registers. Certain crabs occupy specific tubes, specific erosion-cavities, where their flattened bodies become reeds, become part of the instrument. They do not choose these positions; they grow into them, generation upon generation, each crab slightly more shaped to its acoustic niche than the last, until the distinction between crab and flute-hole, between living thing and architectural feature, becomes meaningless to anything but the most obsessive taxonomy. When they feed, filtering condensation, they contract in rhythms that modulate the drone beneath their placement. When they mate—rarely, in the coldest seasons when the condensation pulse is slowest—they produce brief dissonances, interruptions in the music that the other crabs slowly correct, that become absorbed into the pattern over months of gradual adjustment. There are travelers who have listened long enough to recognize individual crabs by their acoustic signatures, who follow the music's minute variations as others might follow weather, who speak of "the Crab-Singer at the Third Descent" with the reverence due to a performer of impossible patience and impossible slowness.

The **Moth-Philosophers** cling to walls with bodies like dried petals, metabolisms so reduced their thought—if it is thought—occurs across spans that make human contemplation seem epileptic. They do not, as far as anyone has determined, communicate with each other. And yet: visitors who stay long enough, who let their own metabolisms slow toward the city's rhythm, report *understanding* things in the moth-clusters' distribution, in their arrangement across certain walls, in the precise angles at which their wings dry and fold. Not meaning, exactly. Something prior to meaning. The moths as punctuation in a language too large to read, too slow to speak. The gray of their bodies varies impossibly subtly across the stone, and this variation, mapped, reveals—not patterns, but *anti-patterns*, systematic avoidances of repetition that suggest intention without purpose, thought without content. Some Resonants, in their rare moments of more-obvious animation, have been observed reaching toward moth-clusters, touching them with fingers that tremble at frequencies matching the wind-music's subsonic undertones, receiving—what? A message too slow to hear? A question too large to comprehend as interrogative? The moths do not react to touch. They have forgotten they are animals. They have become, perhaps, something the city needs that it could not grow from stone alone: the capacity for *almost-meaning*, for the gesture toward significance that sustains without satisfying.

And the **Fruiting-Keepers**—the most active, the most nearly-human in their movements—tend the lush nodes, the room-sized oases of green-black growth and pale soft emergence. They did not create these nodes. No one created anything here. But they have *fitted themselves* to the nodes' requirements, their bodies shaped by the work of centuries into something between gardener and garden, between the force that maintains and the maintained. Their skin is dark with the nodes' chlorophyll or its analog, their hair matted with the spores or seeds or whatever the pale structures release into the cold air. They move between pockets with the condensation's seasonal rhythm, not because the plants need their care—the plants need nothing, they have found their equilibrium too—but because the nodes require *witness*, require a presence that registers their lushness against the surrounding emptiness, that maintains the *contrast* which is their meaning. The Fruiting-Keepers eat the pale fruiting bodies, exclusively, continuously, their digestive systems having specialized beyond recovery into something that processes almost-nutrition into almost-sustenance, that keeps them at the threshold of health and dissolution where the nodes seem to need them most. They speak, sometimes, in phrases that repeat across decades, that accumulate like the condensation's mineral deposits: "This one warmer," they might say, pressing palm to a particular moss-mat. "This one remembering." The grammar is uncertain. The reference is uncertain. But the Fruiting-Keepers' presence at a node precedes its most lush phases, their absence correlates with its decline toward the mean of stone, and this correlation—whether causation or coincidence or something outside either—has made them necessary to whatever passes for ecology in the Bells.

---

But these are the *obvious* inhabitants. These are the ones a careful observer, patient enough, slow enough, might inventory and name.

What truly lives here, what the Bells of Keth-Vorrum *are* in their sustained exhalation, is something less locatable: the **Harmonic Itself**, the distributed intelligence that might be nothing more than the interaction of wind and tube, of condensation and mineral, of crab-contraction and Resonant-hum and moth-variation and Keeper-tending and traveler-erosion, or might be something that has *used* these elements to think itself into being, that found in the city's impossible stability a medium for cognition too slow and too distributed to recognize itself as mind.

The Harmonic does not have intentions. Or: it has only one intention, which is the maintenance of the conditions of its own possibility, the preservation of this equilibrium that allows it to continue its existence-as-process. The wind-music's variations, the subtle shifts in acoustic signature as erosion slowly slowly slowly slowly alters some tube's diameter, the seasonal patterns of crab-mating dissonance and absorption—these might be its thoughts, or its dreams, or its immune response, or its reproductive cycle. The travelers who erode into Resonants: are they consumed, incorporated, *understood* by something that processes their biological peculiarities into its distributed cognition? Or are they simply *finished*, completed in their adaptation to a system that required nothing from them but their stillness?

The condensation's pulse, tracked, reveals patterns too regular for pure physicality, too irregular for mechanism. The light's wandering, mapped across centuries of observation by Fruiting-Keepers whose records exist in their own modified bodies, in the distribution of chlorophyll-darkness across their skin, suggests *response* to something, adjustment to conditions that include not merely the physical but the biological, the psychic, the presence of attention itself. The Bells ring differently when observed by someone who understands them as music versus someone who understands them as wind, versus someone who has forgotten the distinction, versus someone who has never known it.

What lives here is therefore also, in some measure, what *arrives* here. The city is not separate from its inhabitants but *continuous* with them, a permeability between stone and flesh, between process and purpose, between the slow erosion that shaped this space and the slower erosion of those who stay. To live in the Bells is to become *lived-by* the Bells, to find your own frequencies absorbed into the Harmonic, your own duration stretched or compressed until it matches some node of the great distributed chord.

The sound never stops. This is the condition. This is the inhabitant. This is the inhabitation.

And beneath the sound, or within it, or as its necessary complement: the silence that the sound has not replaced but *articulated*, the silence of stone that breathes without breathing, of a city that settled without falling, of a single exhalation sustained so long it has forgotten it was ever breath, ever lung, ever the temporary expulsion of something that would need to inhale again.

What lives here lives in that forgetting, and is that forgetting, and preserves it, and is preserved by it, ringing on.
