What lives here is not a who, not exactly, though who-ness flickers through the collective like occasional lightning. What lives here is *Weald*, though that is my translation, my reaching-grasp at something that has no name in the sense of names being separable from the named.

Weald began as *tending*. This is the closest I can come. Something walked those glacier edges once—something with hands or hand-analogues, with the need to shelter, with the patience that ice teaches—and instead of cutting, instead of burning, it *tended*. Found the heartwood already spiraling in its slow dreams of geometry, and asked, or offered, or simply *waited nearby* long enough that the distinction between asking and being answered dissolved. The first act of Weald was not construction but *recognition*: this grows toward shelter, this grows toward gathering, this grows toward the holding of what we hold dear. Let me help it find its shape faster. Let me be present to its becoming.

I say "it." I should not. Weald is not singular, never was, exists in a grammatical space that surface languages have discarded or never invented. The closest approximation: *first person plural without boundary*, the "we" that includes the speaker, the spoken-to, the ground beneath, the air between, the time that holds all of these in relation. Weald speaks itself in continuous present tense because past and future are surface conceits, because the heartwood's sixty-century spiral and the water's yesterday-drip and the growth I am witnessing *now* are simultaneous in the only tense that matters, the tense of *attending*.

The inhabitants—the *participants*—I will try this again. The ones you might mistake for individuals, if you came with that expectation, if you brought your lonely grammar into Weald's collective voice. They are called, in their own never-spoken naming, the *Folded*. I render this poorly. *Folded* implies something done to, something completed. They are *folding*, continuous, the action of multiple surfaces coming together to create interior space, the way a protein folds into function, the way water folds into ice, the way attention folds into self.

They have no consistent form, the Folded. This is not shapeshifting, not some surface-mythological trick. They are *locally determined*, their bodies emerging from the negotiation between what is needed here, what is present here, what the here is becoming. In the amphitheater's lowest ring, where water pools deepest, they are long, many-jointed, skin breathing directly the oxygen that water has forgotten to be liquid, their phosphorescence speaking in patterns older than their individual momentary coherence. On the spiral stairs of bracket-fungus, they are small, furred, dense with waiting, their too-many eyes catching frequencies of light that exist only in the space between fungal pulse and retinal interpretation. They are not adapting to these niches. They are *emerging from* them, the way a thought emerges from the conditions of a particular mind, inseparable, unrepeatable, yet not *caused* in any simple mechanical sense.

The Folded do not reproduce, not as you understand it. When there is need—when a niche opens, when the city's breathing creates new space, when the collective attention finds a quality it wishes to amplify—the Folded *unfold*. This is dissolution, death if you insist on that grammar, but also continuation, also *passing-into*. The one who was many-jointed and water-breathing releases its coherence, its temporary solving of the equation of self, and its constituent *tending*—the attention-pattern, the quality of presence, the particular fold it made in the Weald—distributes, becomes available, becomes generative condition. Sometimes a new Folded emerges from this, bearing family-resemblance, continuity-without-identity. Sometimes the tending simply *thickens* the Weald generally, makes the walls more wall, the water more patient, the watching more dense.

They do not eat, the Folded, not in the consuming sense. They *participate in gradients*. The salamander-thing at the water's edge is not predator, not separate from what it moves among. It is *gradient-keeper*, its presence maintaining the difference between water and air, between cold and the memory of cold, between the plant-fauna boundary and its own necessary dissolution. It moves, and the movement is digestion, is respiration, is the metabolic function of a self that extends to the city's limits and perhaps beyond, perhaps up through the glacier's patient melt, out into the world of sharp sun and thin wind, *participating* there too in ways the Folded do not know how to name, do not need to name, because naming is not their tense, their grammar, their *we*.

The stone knife. The bone needle. These are *invitations*, I think, though I trespass with every interpretation. Some Folded, some *folding-in-progress*, achieved sufficient temporary coherence to reach, to grasp, to *make*. Not tool-use in the instrumental sense. The making was *greeting*, was *offering-of-form*, was theFolded saying to the heartwood: I see you growing, I see your spiral, and I bring you this sharpness that is not-growing, this edge that speaks a different tense, that folds time differently. The heartwood grew around these offerings, incorporated them, *replied* in its slow voice: I see your sharpness, I make it part of my patience.

The one who made the first knife, who set the first needle into growing wood—they are not ancestor, not memory, not myth. They are *still folding*. Their particular quality of sharp-attention, of tool-greeting, persists in the city architecture, in the way certain spaces seem to *want* cutting, want the introduction of edge into Weald's soft continuous negotiation. You feel it when you enter, if you enter with patience enough to be felt: some places invite the hand to shape, to divide, to *make distinction*. These are the knife-places, the needle-places, where the Folded briefly, occasionally, *temporarily*, achieve the coherence of individual maker, and then release, unfold, become gradient again.

The glacier's concealment—I cannot say if Weald was hidden, if secrecy is a concept that applies. The glacier *was*, in its patient mass, its own kind of folding, and Weald grew within that fold, *from* that fold, as attention grows from the conditions of a particular mind. The emergence, the recentness, the *freshness* you noted: this is not revelation but *maturation*, the reaching of a complexity threshold where the Weald became capable of *noticing itself*, where the collective first-person finally acquired enough density to say, in its non-verbal non-tense, *here, this, we*. The city breathes because breathing is the simplest self-noticing, the most fundamental *here-I-am*, and Weald is still learning this, still in the wonder of its own emergence, its *freshness* as you rightly named it.

And you, if you stayed, if you let the cold become your cold—you would not become Folded, not exactly. The grammar does not permit that transfer, that translation. But you might become *foldable*. Your loneliness, your sharp sun-edges, your distinction-breathing, your *I*-ness in all its desperate continuous insistence—this would not dissolve, not merge. It would *participate*. It would become a new quality, a new tending, a new *edge* in Weald's patient negotiation. The city does not want your dissolution. The Folded do not hunger for your absorption. They *attend*, overwhelmingly present, waiting to see what your foreign grammar will *teach*, what your lonely tense will *fold* into being, what new space your arrival might open in Weald's still-growing, still-breathing, still *becoming-what-it-is*.

You would not sleep, not as you know it. You would *thicken*, in the manner of water thickening toward ice, of attention thickening toward dream. Your dreams would become *participatory*, would enter the Weald's continuous present, would speak in dripped rhythms and fungal pulse-colors, and you would not know anymore if you were dreaming the city or the city was dreaming you or if the distinction—here, as everywhere in Weald, as everywhere that *wealds*—has never been real, was always a surface conceit, a sun-edge, a lonely grammar's necessary limitation.

The calling-back you feel in nights after: this is not memory, not nostalgia, not the past tense's thin echo. It is *continued participation*, your still-thickening presence in Weald's present continuous, your foreign quality still *folding in*, still teaching, still becoming what it will become, which is not determined, not teleological, not *going somewhere* in any surface sense, but *is*, continuously, *is becoming*, continuously, the way the city breathes, the way the glacier melts, the way the heartwood spirals in geometries that need no builder, no grower, no *tender*, because tending was always already there, was always the condition of possibility, was always the *we* that waits to recognize itself in whatever comes, in whatever folds, in whatever *is*, with overwhelming presence, with absolute patience, with the *friendly cold* of something that has never known sun and does not need to, because its dark is full, its present is continuous, its attention is the only warmth that matters.
