There is a woman who tends the condenser-bats in the lower transit vaults, Sector 7-Gamma through 7-Kappa, and she has been doing this long enough that her own body has begun to participate in the work. This is not uncommon in Threnod. The city does not demand adaptation, exactly—adaptation sounds too sudden, too sharp-edged for what happens here. Instead it offers continuity, and those who remain long enough find themselves continuing in ways that blur the administrative boundaries between employee and function, between the maintenance of systems and the systems themselves.

Her name is Orsene, though names in Threnod function differently than elsewhere. She has other designations: her guild identifier, her atmospheric sector certification, the informal epithet the bat-processing collectives have given her, which translates roughly to "she-who-does-not-startle," a high compliment in a culture whose founding professional society is dedicated to the elimination of the abrupt. She answers to all of these, answers with the slight delay that has become characteristic of her kind, the maintenance workers who have descended too often to the lowest levels and returned with something of the substrate's slowness in their nervous systems.

She is forty-seven, or two hundred and twelve, or fifteen hundred, depending on which dating system you apply. Threnod has used many. The city does not discontinue calendars so much as sediment them, layer upon layer, so that a citizen might simultaneously observe the secular cycle of the computational ages, the biological calendar of the manufactories, and the ancient civic year established by whatever founders built the first compressional island, if there was a first compressional island. Orsene prefers the biological calendar, which measures time in generational terms of the engineered species she tends. She is, by this reckoning, in her third condenser-bat lifespan, having begun her work in the creche-nurseries of Sector 7-Beta while the great-great-grandmothers of her current charges still hung newborn from the artificial rockwork, their moisture-precipitation organs not yet fully developed, their bodies still soft enough to fold into the collection pouches used for transport between vaults.

The vaults themselves deserve attention, deserve to become more themselves in description as they have become more themselves through the centuries of their existence. They are not subway tunnels, though they carry transit; not caves, though they are underground; not industrial spaces, though industry occurs there. The ceiling heights vary according to acoustic profiles established by committees whose records have dissolved into the substrate, but whose decisions persist in the dimensions of the spaces, in the way sound behaves in them. Orsene knows these acoustics intimately. She knows that at the eastern terminus of 7-Epsilon, the 60-hertz hum of the helical escalators above produces a standing wave pattern that makes the bats restless, that she must supplement the scheduled processing cycle with additional interventions there, stroking the irritable membrane between their ears, feeding them by hand the engineered mites that constitute their protein intake, speaking to them in a subvocal register that she developed without training, without deciding to develop.

This register is part of what she brought back from the lowest levels, part of the change that the city honors without understanding. She does not speak of what she saw there, if she saw anything, if "seeing" is the appropriate modality for whatever occurs in the compressed foundations where the substrate has developed its rumored awareness. But the register is demonstrable, is functional, is used by the processing collectives in their coordination rituals. When Orsene speaks to the bats, she speaks at a frequency that overlaps with their own communication range, and something in this overlap produces effects that guild documentation describes as "improved moisture yield" and that Orsene experiences as something closer to conversation, to the mutual adjustment of two continuations that have grown adjacent enough to become, briefly, a single system.

The bats themselves are not bats, of course. This has been established, documented, accepted. They were mammalian in appearance when first engineered in the biological manufactories of the 3400s, and they have remained mammalian in appearance through millennia of continuous production, but appearance in Threnod is understood as a functional category, not an ontological one. The condenser-bats precipitate moisture from saturated air. This requires surface area, requires hanging posture, requires the metabolic capacity to process the minerals that accrete as vapor condenses. The mammalian appearance serves these requirements. Whether the underlying biology retains any connection to the mammalian lineages of the pre-urban past is a question that the city's charter of atmospherics has long since rendered irrelevant. The bats are, in the precise terminology of the guild, "atmospheric devices of biological implementation," and this is sufficient for all official purposes.

Orsene knows them otherwise. She knows that the bat in position 7-Gamma-442, whom she calls not by that designation but by a sound she produces in her modified register, a sound that might be transliterated as something between a hum and a stopped breath—she knows that this particular bat responds to her presence with a shift in its hanging posture that is not documented in any manual, a slight rotation that exposes more of its condensation surface, that increases yield by some fractional percentage, that reads to Orsene as something like recognition, something like the city's own slow accumulation made momentarily visible in a single gesture of continued relation.

She does not say this to the guild assessors who periodically evaluate her sector's efficiency. She reports the fractional percentage, attributes it to optimal temperature regulation, to the copper sulfate levels in the ventilation channels, to the scheduling of the standing wave dampening procedures. The assessors make their notes. Orsene continues her work. The bats hang, and precipitate, and process, and process-output into the collection troughs sixty meters below, and the sound of their living is part of the loudness, part of the industrial music, part of what makes Threnod sound like itself.

Her living quarters are in the mid-elevations, where the air has thinned enough to require the mechanical assistance that the city's continuous harvest provides. She does not use the helical escalators for this commute, though they would carry her there. Instead she walks, following the gradient streets that wind through the constructed terrain, the three-dimensional tangle of passages that the city's geography of accumulation has produced. The walk takes three hours, each direction, and she makes it daily, has made it daily for longer than the biological calendar can easily account. The walking is part of her function, she believes, part of what keeps her able to do what she does. The gradual slope, the pressure accumulating in her ears so slowly she does not notice it, the damp warm air condensing on her skin, the vegetation everywhere growing, converting diffused light, the epiphyte roots overhead that she has learned to read as a map of moisture conditions in the sectors above—this density of parsed experience is what she has become capable of, what she has become through long continuation.

There are others like her, though not many. The departure ports take their toll on the population, siphoning away those who cannot bear the patient's pace, and the city's own slow training takes another portion, those who adapt so thoroughly that they become indistinguishable from the substrate itself, who walk downward and do not return, or walk upward to the extreme elevation where the whiteglass harvests its scattered light and the air requires mechanical assistance that has become indistinguishable from breathing, from being. Orsene has known colleagues who have taken both paths. She does not judge them. The city continues, is continuing, has always continued, and the continuation takes the forms that it takes, the dense slow constructed forms that natural elsewhere might aspire to mean.

On her walks, she passes the bench in the sector where she does not work, the grown secretion of substrate-organisms that the aesthetic committees approved eight centuries past. Sometimes there is someone sitting there, sometimes not. She does not sit herself. The bench is not part of her route in that way, not a destination but a coordinate in the three-dimensional map she maintains of moisture and light and sound and the slow pulses of the climate systems that operate across decades. She notes it, notes its occupancy, incorporates this into her understanding of the sector's condition. The presence of a seated figure indicates something about vapor management, about the efficiency of the vegetation in that locality, about the balance of processed output and atmospheric need. She has not spoken to the figure when it is present, when it sits, when the damp warm air condenses on its skin and the vegetation continues its patient conversion and the condenser-bats stir for their processing cycles somewhere below in the vaults she will reach in three hours, in two hours, in the continuing approach of her slow commute.

But she has noted something about this figure, when it is present. It sits with the posture of someone who is becoming more itself, who is letting what emerges become more itself as it remains, as it continues, as it participates in the city's slow accumulation without fully integrating, without being fully rejected. This is the manner of Threnod, she recognizes, the low weirdness of long existence, the hair in the food that cannot be fully integrated or fully rejected. She recognizes it because she recognizes something similar in herself, in her modified register, in her fractional percentages of increased yield, in her three-hour walks that are function and continuation simultaneously.

She passes the bench, on the days when the figure sits there. She does not startle. The Society for the Prevention of Suddenness would approve, though approval is not the category that operates here, not the slow dense relation that has accumulated between her and the city she tends, the bats she tends, the moisture she precipitates and processes and outputs into the collection troughs, the industrial music, the loud specific sound of machinery in balance, of life in balance, of balance itself as the only orientation that long existence can maintain against the persistent, patient existence of the Surrounding at the city's feet.

Tonight, in the lower transit vaults, the standing wave at 7-Epsilon will require her intervention. She will subvocalize to the irritable bats, will stroke the membrane between their ears, will note the fractional increase in their yield and the something-like-recognition that passes between her and the bat in position 7-Gamma-442, the bat that hangs and precipitates and waits for her approach with a posture she does not document, does not report, does not discontinue. The city will continue. The moisture will fall, upward and downward, in its scheduled intervals and its unscheduled condensations. The vegetation will grow on every surface, converting the diffused light, maintaining the charter. The whiteglass will age in its controlled way, developing the patina that aesthetic committees prefer. The factories will function, continuous but not urgent, scaled but not accelerating.

Orsene will walk home in the long afternoon phase, when the whiteglass reddens slightly, when the condenser-bats stir, when the continuous harvest begins its invisible descent through channels she will never see but knows to exist, coordinates in the three-dimensional map of her continued, continuing, always continued existence. She will pass the bench, where the figure may or may not sit. She will not startle. The gradient will rise so gradually she will not notice the accumulating pressure, the thinning of the damp warm air, the slow transformation of her walking into something the city has learned to do through her, something that natural might aspire to mean if it could accumulate long enough, sediment layer upon layer, compress until the original island becomes archaeological rumor, until the rumor itself becomes substrate, becomes function, becomes the slow awareness that the lowest levels have developed and that she carries in her altered nervous system, her modified register, her fractional percentages of something that is not efficiency exactly, not recognition exactly, not continuation exactly, but the dense accumulated form that all of these have become through longer practice than any single thing could accumulate alone.

She is almost home when the scheduled precipitation begins, the soft rain that is not quite rain, the suggestion of moisture that falls where it is scheduled to fall. She stands in it, lets it condense on her skin, lets it participate in the processing cycles that her body has become part of. The sound of it falling into the collection channels joins the 60-hertz hum, the ventilation breath, the distant processing output of bats she tends in sectors she will reach tomorrow, in the continued tomorrow that the biological calendar makes available to those who do not require the stimulation of faster environments, who find in the city's slow training a temporal sensibility that reads as purpose, as function, as the only kind of meaning that can accumulate in a place where everything is built and nothing is natural and everything has become, through long existence, something that natural might aspire to mean.

Home is a space grown rather than built, a secretion of older substrate into forms approved by committees whose records have dissolved but whose decisions persist. She enters it. The damp warm air follows her, is her, participates in the atmospheric management that the charter requires. She does not sleep, exactly. The maintenance workers who have descended to the lowest levels and returned do not sleep in the way the manuals describe. Instead they enter a state that the guild documentation has not successfully categorized, a lowered metabolic rate, a continued awareness, a participation in the city's slow cycles that resembles the bats' own hanging, their own precipitation, their own functional existence in the saturated air.

In this state, she hears the city. Not the chaotic noise of organic places, but the layered industrial drone of machinery in balance, of which she is part, of which the bats are part, of which the figure on the bench is part if it sits there now, in the reddening light, in the soft precipitation, in the accumulated continuation that is Threnod becoming more itself, letting what emerges become more itself, not settling on a single image early or ever, because early and ever have become relative to the point of meaninglessness, because the city has been in its own state for so long that new and old are coordinates in a map that no longer distinguishes between them, that reads everything as simultaneous and ancient, as constructed and grown, as natural and artificial, as she is, as the bats are, as the substrate in its rumored awareness is, as the Surrounding is at the city's feet, patient, persistent, denied its proper name and therefore denied nothing, absolutely nothing, because denial in Threnod is another form of continuation, another slow accumulation, another dense bright loud constructed way of being that has learned to sound like itself through longer practice than any organic thing could accumulate, than any single thing could accumulate, than any thing could accumulate alone.

She precipitates, in her lowered state. This is not documented. It does not require documentation. She contributes her moisture to the atmospheric management, to the charter, to the city that has adjusted her expectations until she finds the rapid world elsewhere unbearable, until she finds in this slow dense relation the only temporal sensibility that can continue, that does continue, that has always continued in this artificial extreme where nothing is natural and everything has become, through long existence, something that natural might aspire to mean, if natural could mean, if meaning could accumulate, if accumulation could rise, could sediment, could grow like a callus from the sea, from the acid-green shallows, from the Surrounding that is not surrounding exactly, not sea exactly, not anything with a proper name because proper names are for things that do not continue, that do not become more themselves, that settle early on single images and therefore cease, absolutely cease, in the way that Threnod does not cease, has never ceased, will not cease until some unimaginable disruption that is not imagined, that is not imaginable within the slow cycles that pulse across decades, that train those who remain to find in this pulsing the only rhythm that can be borne, the only rhythm that bears, that continues, that accumulates.

The standing wave at 7-Epsilon, when she reaches it tomorrow, will have shifted slightly. This is the nature of standing waves, of long existence, of the slow weathering that develops patina, that produces effects unanticipated by any design committee. She will adjust. She will subvocalize in her modified register. The bat in position 7-Gamma-442 will rotate slightly, will expose more of its condensation surface, will produce the fractional percentage that she will not document, that exists between documentation and the something-else that the categories don't catch, that has grown in the interstitial volumes where the semi-autonomous constructions occupy themselves, where the manufactured fauna move with the confidence of long design, where the maintenance workers descend and return changed, speak less, move with certainty borrowed from something older than training manuals, are tolerated, are honored, are given assignments that suit their altered disposition, that suit the city's way of acknowledging what it cannot understand about itself.

This is her assignment. This is her disposition. This is her altered, altering, always altered continuation, and in the lowered metabolic state that is not sleep, that resembles the bats' hanging, the substrate's rumored awareness, the figure's sitting on the grown bench in the sector where she does not work, she participates in the city's slow breathing, the kilometer-wide turbines drawing damp warm air, pushing it upward through copper sulfate channels, the vegetation everywhere growing, converting, continuing, the whiteglass diffusing, the factories functioning at geological pace, the departure ports waiting for those who cannot bear this, who require faster stimulation, who leave and are not much missed because missing is too sudden, too sharp-edged, too much like the staircases forbidden above 800 meters, like the cliffs that the engineered gradients prevent, like everything the Society for the Prevention of Suddenness has worked to eliminate from the accumulated, sedimented, callus-grown city that rises, that does not rise, that accumulated, that sedimented, that grew and grows and is growing still, becoming more itself, letting what emerges become more itself, not settling, never settling, continuing.
