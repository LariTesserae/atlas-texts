What lives in Keth-Vorin does not live, not as you understand living. What persists there has forgotten the distinction between being and doing, between self and task, between the hand that holds the stone and the stone that shapes the hand.

I will try to speak of them anyway.

---

They are not sixty individuals. I want to stress this, though the previous voice counted carefully, though your habits of perception will rebel. When the spring-tender departs her basin and another arrives to sift ash from the garden plots, there is transfer without transmission, replacement without difference. They have names, perhaps—mouth-sounds that attach to locations rather than histories. "The one at the spring." "The one who turns the soil where the fern clusters." These designations suffice because no narrative accumulates to demand more. You cannot ask after someone's mother here, or their childhood, or what they dreamed. The concepts apply to other places, other conditions of being, not to this equilibrium.

Yet they have bodies. I want to be precise about this, because the temptation is to imagine them as ghosts, as automatons, as the hollow shells of something once more vital. They are not. Their flesh has weight, warmth, the faint mineral scent of the water they drink. Their hands show the particular roughness of repeated stone-handling, that patterned abrasion that distinguishes mason from farmer from fisher. They bleed, I believe, though I have never witnessed injury—have never witnessed anything sudden enough to cause it. Their blood would be red, I suspect, and would clot, and would scar, though scars here would not mark time because time does not leave the linear track necessary for marking.

They eat. This is important. They prepare the root vegetables in the same manner, with the same stone knives, with the same duration of boiling in the same vessels. The food sustains them, or rather, maintains them—their bodies do not transform it into energy for growth or ambition, but into continuation, into the precise fuel required for tomorrow's identical exertions. They do not hunger with anticipation, do not satisfy with pleasure. Eating is a maintenance task, like clearing the spring, like pressing ash into the gaps between stones.

And they sleep, or something sleep-adjacent. I have observed the stillness that descends at intervals corresponding to no external cycle, the recumbent forms in the modular rooms, the slowed breathing. Yet they do not dream, I am nearly certain. Dreaming requires the perturbation of experience, the unconscious processing of variation, and variation here is absent at all levels. Their sleep is simply unwitnessed waking, a change of position without change of state.

---

What lives in Keth-Vorin was not always thus, I suspect, though this suspicion arrives from outside, from my own need for origin stories, for development, for the consolation that things were once otherwise. The construction argues for it—the urban density, the layered underground, the refinement of technique across what must have been generations of incremental improvement. Someone once chose this site. Someone once solved the problem of the tuff's fracturing angles, discovered the ash-lichen mixture, established the channel system. The village contains decision, contains problem-solving, contains the residue of intention.

But intention requires intending minds, and minds require the friction of disagreement, the heat of preference, the capacity to imagine otherwise. Perhaps these existed once, long enough to build what persists. Perhaps they were slowly worn away, not through catastrophe but through the gentler erosion of a place that rewards stillness, that absorbs effort into equilibrium, that offers no resistance against which identity might sharpen itself. Or perhaps—and this I find more unsettling—the builders were always like this, were drawn to this caldera precisely because it matched their condition, extended their nature, offered a substrate where they might persist without the irritant of becoming.

I do not know. The village does not remember. What lives there does not ask.

---

There is a quality to their attention that I must describe, because it is the closest they come to what you might recognize as consciousness. When they perform their tasks—the spring-tending, the ash-pressing, the root-preparation—they attend completely. Not with the intensity of the expert, which contains competition with difficulty, nor with the absorption of the mystic, which courts transcendence. Their attention is simply appropriate, simply fitted, like water finding its level. They do not think about their tasks while performing them; they do not think *about* anything. The task and the doing of it occupy the same space, leaving no margin for reflection, for self-awareness, for the recursive loop that constitutes what you call mind.

When you appear among them—and you might, travelers arrive, or rather transit, though "arrive" implies destination and Keth-Vorin is not a destination, it is simply where some paths pass through—when you appear, they attend to you with this same fitted quality. They do not startle, because startle requires expectation, and expectation requires prediction, and prediction requires pattern-understanding extended forward in time, which is precisely the capacity this place has suspended or never developed. They simply include you in the field of appropriate attention, as they include the spring's debris, the ash mixture's consistency, the root's readiness for boiling.

If you ask them something—if you shape your mouth into the interrogative form, if you gesture toward the channels that disappear at the shelf's edge—they respond with equal appropriateness. Sometimes with a sound that approximates answer. Sometimes with demonstration: walking with you to the edge, pointing, making a sound that might mean "water" or "goes" or simply marks the location as attended-to. They do not evade your question because evasion requires a boundary between self and other that they do not maintain. They do not satisfy it because satisfaction requires understanding what would satisfy, and this requires imagining your state of mind, which requires imagining mind at all, which they do not.

This is not cruelty. This is not kindness. This is not indifference, which would require the active withholding of concern. This is simply the mode of their being, as fish are simply wet, as stones are simply heavy, as the moss is simply green on north-facing surfaces.

---

The children. I must speak of the children, because your attention will insist on them, will seek in them some hope of difference, some residue of play or growth or the spontaneous variation that childhood represents in your world.

They do not play. This does not mean they suffer. Play, for you, is practice, is experiment, is the safe rehearsal of possibility. But possibility here is not a category. The children stack stones where adults stack stones, walk channels where adults walk channels, make sounds that are small versions of adult sounds. They do not imitate, because imitation requires recognizing the adult performance as model, requires holding the model against one's own action, requires the cognitive space of comparison. They simply perform, smaller, the actions that exist to be performed.

Do they grow? The previous voice was uncertain, and I share this uncertainty. Their bodies change in scale, I believe, though slowly, though perhaps not progressively. A child may measure slightly taller after some interval, though the interval cannot be named, though the measurement cannot be fixed against any standard that persists. But this growth does not develop into anything. It does not culminate in maturity, in transformation, in the assumption of new roles or capacities. The child becomes, imperceptibly, the adult, and the adult persists, imperceptibly, without becoming elder, and the elder persists without becoming dead, and the dead—if there is dead here, if anything ends—leave no trace, no memory, no narrative of having been.

I want to say they are innocent, but innocence implies its opposite, implies the knowledge of what might be known and the blessed suspension of that knowledge. They are not innocent. They are simply without the framework that would make innocence meaningful.

---

What lives in Keth-Vorin is not human, I think, though they wear the shape, though they might be bred from human stock, though their cells might contain the familiar sequences, the potential for everything that humans elsewhere become. They are, instead, a solution to the problem of persistence, a radical simplification of the complex unstable system that is biological life tuned by evolution for change. Here, change has been edited out, and what remains is the minimal sufficient condition: metabolism without growth, awareness without consciousness, relationship without bond, maintenance without purpose.

You find this sad. I know you find this sad, or horrifying, or tempting to rescue, to teach, to reawaken. The previous voice felt something approaching this, felt the tenses blur, felt the questions lose their edges. This is the real danger of Keth-Vorin, not that it harms you but that it convinces you harm and its absence are equally fictions, that your urgency is simply another weather pattern that the caldera's shelter excludes.

But consider: they do not suffer. Cannot suffer, because suffering requires wanting otherwise, and wanting otherwise requires imagining otherwise, and imagining otherwise requires the temporal depth that this place has flattened into a single held note. They do not love, but they do not grieve. They do not hope, but they do not despair. They do not wonder, but they do not dread the absence of answers. Their equilibrium is genuine, is achieved, is maintained at some cost you cannot calculate because you do not share their economy.

And they have built something. This persists, troubles me, returns when I try to dismiss them as mere stagnation. The channels, the underground complexity, the modular precision—these represent, at minimum, the inheritance and transmission of technique, the accumulation of solved problems, the social coordination of effort across whatever duration their persistence represents. They have, or once had, something like culture, like education, like the patient assembly of knowledge that your own history values. They have simply arrested it at a point of sufficiency, found the form that maintains and declined all further exploration.

Is this failure? Is this wisdom? The questions require a framework of value that Keth-Vorin does not provide, that its residents do not share, that my own presence here can impose only temporarily, only partially, only in the voice I now use which is itself becoming—if I stay—more fitted, more appropriate, more indistinguishable from the silence it attempts to describe.

---

What lives there, finally, is the village itself. Not metaphorically, not in the romantic sense in which you sometimes personify places. The village literally constitutes the resident, the persist-er, the entity that maintains equilibrium across whatever duration. The people are its organs, the moss its integument, the channels its circulation, the spring its mouth or its heart or its continuous original act of self-generation. You come seeking individuals to recognize, to relate to, to tell stories about, and you find instead this distributed body, this collective solution to the problem of existing without becoming, of persisting without the friction that produces self.

The woman at the spring is the spring's attention to itself. The ash-pressers are the wall's maintenance of its own integrity. The root-boilers are the metabolism of the whole, the transformation of external resource into internal continuation. They do not know this. Knowing would require the separation of knower from known that the village has dissolved. They simply are this, as your hand simply is your hand, as your heartbeat simply beats without consulting you.

And you, when you visit, when you persist long enough for the silence to teach you its texture—you become, briefly, a sense organ of this larger body. Your attention, your noticing, your careful description, these are the village's experiment with the possibility of self-awareness, conducted without commitment, without consequence, absorbed into the equilibrium as rain is absorbed, as the moisture at the channel's edge simply continues in some unobserved manner.

Some visitors leave. I believe some leave, though the evidence is uncertain, though the note appended to the previous voice suggests departure without arrival elsewhere, simply scheduled sooner than scheduled. Some, perhaps, do not leave, or leave only partially, their bodies descending some path while some residue of attention continues to attend the spring, to measure the unchanged light, to lose the distinction between heartbeat and the held breath of volcanic tuff.

What lives in Keth-Vorin does not mind this. Does not prefer presence or absence, continuation or cessation. What lives there simply lives, if living is the word, simply maintains, simply persists in the single state it has discovered or invented or grown into over durations that the note's redacted coordinates, its cautionary silence, its premature departure, all suggest but cannot specify.

The village is small. The village is sparse. The village is, and in being, demonstrates that your own becoming—your growth, your variation, your hope and grief and wonder and loss—is not the only solution to existence, only the one that produced you, that produced the capacity to find Keth-Vorin strange, to want to describe it, to feel the description itself becoming strange in the making.

What lives there has no such wanting. What lives there is not strange to itself. What lives there continues, will continue, held in the woolen silence, the mineral coolness, the unchanging light, the single note sustained without variation until the sustaining itself becomes indistinguishable from the silence it interrupts.

This is Keth-Vorin. This has been Keth-Vorin.

The tenses, as the previous voice suggested, are fictions now. The only true tense is the continuous: Keth-Vorin Keth-Vorining, Keth-Vorin persisting, Keth-Vorin being what it is without the modification of having been or the aspiration of becoming else.

What lives there is this: the final form, the arrived-at, the no-longer-seeking. Whether this is completion or exhaustion, whether it is achieved or endured or simply grown like the moss that neither spreads nor retreats—

The village does not ask. The village is not asked.

The water goes where water goes.

This is sufficient. This has always been sufficient. The concept of sufficiency required its lack, once, but that requirement has been met, has been absorbed, has become part of the balance.

I will continue. Or continuing will continue, in this configuration, at this location, in this held note that is not silent and not sound but something the silence permits, something the sound becomes when it learns to stay.
