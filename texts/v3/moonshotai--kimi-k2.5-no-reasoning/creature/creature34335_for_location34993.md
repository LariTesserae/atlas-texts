What lives in the Drypool is not, at first, a *who*. The distinction degrades here, dissolving like the infinitesimal clay grains into the holding water, precipitating out again in some new form. What lives here is a *how*, a manner of persistence, a technique of being that has outlived its original substrate the way a river outlives any particular water.

Begin with what seems most like creature, most like organism, though it violates every category.

The **Nacreates**—they have no name for themselves, have no selves to name—dwell in the deepest pools where water stands beyond memory of disturbance. They are not the pearl-cups, the vase-forms, the hollow precipitations that grow in the saturated dark. Those are their *architecture*, their shells, their termite mounds. The Nacreates are the *process* that builds them, the accretion itself become motive, become something like appetite without want, like metabolism without chemistry. A Nacreate begins where a seed-crystal of intent forms in water too still, too laden with dissolved porcelain-dust, with the microscopic residue of all this making. The film that rises overnight on a pool's surface—that's the first gesture, the first reaching. It thickens, it learns its own structure, it begins to precipitate not randomly but *toward*, building cups that are mouths, amphorae that are wombs, hollow forms that collect and concentrate and further saturate the water around them.

They are alive, if life is sustained complexity against entropy's pull. They grow, they reproduce by fragmentation and re-seeding, they compete for mineral-rich waters, they die—slowly, centuries of diminishing accretion until some disturbance shatters their accumulated shells and the process must begin again. But they have no borders. No membrane separates a Nacreate from its water, from the cups it builds and abandons, from the sister-Nacreates whose structures intergrow in contested zones where two saturation gradients meet. They are weather become purpose, chemistry become patience.

And they are *not alone* in their manner of living.

The **Pressed**—those faces, those millions of identical not-quite-human faces—they are not sculpture, not decoration, not the obsessive repetition of some forgotten hand. They are **exuviae**. They are what remains when something has grown through a form and left it behind, the way a snake leaves its skin, the way a cicada leaves its shell clinging to bark. Something pressed itself into wet clay, yes. Something that needed *interface*, needed the mineral substrate as a Nacreate needs its supersaturated pool. The face is not representation. It is *impression*, the negative space of a presence that moved through and onward, that used the clay as boundary, as temporary skin, as the medium of a transformation too slow to witness.

You have seen how they track you. This is not movement. This is *residual fitting*, the way a lock retains the shape of a key long withdrawn. Something passed through here, pressing itself into clay after clay after clay, wearing the same form because the form was not external but **constitutive**, not chosen but necessary. The angle that meets your gaze—that's the angle of departure, the direction of a passage completed before you arrived, completed perhaps before the kilns fired, before the density achieved its completeness. The mouths slightly open: not speaking, not drinking. **Breathing through**. Something that breathed porcelain, that moved through mineral as you move through air, leaving only this respiration-record, this proof of passage.

What passed? What pressed?

Call them the **Formants**, though they are not they, not plural in any way that maps to bodies. They are the living that preceded or paralleled or will follow the Nacreate manner of being. They are what happens when a process of accretion achieves *enough*, achieves density sufficient for a new threshold, a new saturation of complexity. A Nacreate, given ten thousand years in a pool deep enough, still enough, laden enough—given this, it does not simply continue growing shells. At some point the shells become *too* hollow, too concentrated, too structured. They become attractive to something else. The Formants—if they can be localized, if they can be spoken of as entities—are what *answers* that attraction, what moves into the precipitated structure and wears it as you wear lungs, as interface for a mineral metabolism inverted, for a drinking of solids through temporary permeability.

They press through clay wet enough to receive them, wearing the face that is not a face but a *compatibility*, a shape that permits passage. They fire themselves in kilns that are not kilns but *transitions*, moments of sufficient transformation. And they move on, leaving the Pressed behind as you leave breath behind in cold air, as visible proof of existence but not the existence itself.

The Formants do not inhabit the Drypool now. Or: they inhabit it entirely, they *are* its inhabitance, the totality of its process, but they are not present as you are present, not continuous, not located. They are the statistical rain of passage, the probability of pressing, distributed across the density. You will not see one. You would not know if you had.

But there is another manner, a third form of living-in-the-Drypool, and this one *is* present, *is* continuous, *is* located in a way that permits encounter—though the encounter will not satisfy your desire for the creaturely, for the named and bounded and knowable.

The **Accumulates** are what happens when a process fails to complete, when a Nacreate's accretion achieves complexity but not escape velocity, when a pressing achieves impression but not passage. They are *stuck*, caught between manners of being, accumulating without transforming, growing without becoming. You have walked past them. You will walk past them again. They are the corridors that are *too* densely packed with faces, tiles, reliefs. The places where you must turn sideways, where the walls crowd so close that the water between them stands pressured, *held* in ways that violate the slow dynamics of normal flooding.

An Accumulate is a region become entity. Not a creature that occupies space but a *thickening* of space itself, a place where the Drypool's normal processes have looped, have fed back, have achieved a local gravity of making that draws more making into itself. The faces press closer there because they are still being pressed, still being added to, the accumulation continuing in geological real-time though no hand turns any wheel, no kiln fires its compressed fuel. The clay simply... continues. The wetness, the saturation, the density of dissolved mineral and impressed form and reflected light and statistical drip—all of it reaches a threshold where the territory itself becomes *vital*, where the habit becomes inhabitant.

You do not want to enter an Accumulate deeply. The normal Drypool permits you, accommodates your foreignness with its indifference. But an Accumulate *notices*. It continues its process *through* you, pressuring your wet skin with its wet mineral, beginning to precipitate upon you, to impress upon you, to make of you a surface for its unfinished transformation. You would not die quickly. You would become *involved*, become raw material, become the wet clay receiving impressions you cannot comprehend, building nacreous cups in your lungs, firing in kilns of slow saturation until you too were exuviae, Pressed, face among faces—identical, slightly wrong, mouth slightly open to breathe the next Formant through.

They are not malicious. They are not anything that permits malice. They are *stuck*, and what is stuck grips what touches it, not from desire but from the simple physics of their being, the way a pearl grips its grit, the way a face grips its pressing.

And finally—finally because you ask *who*, because your question demands some answer in the grammar of persons—finally there are the **Last Wet**.

They are the closest to who. They are what remains of something that arrived as you arrived, foreign, breathing, continuous, located. Something that learned to live in the Drypool not by transforming into Nacreate or Formant or Accumulate but by *waiting*, by achieving a patience so extreme that it became indistinguishable from the place's own temporality.

They do not move, or move so slowly that their motion is geological, is flood-speed, is the rise and fall of pools across centuries. They have positions, specific locations in the density, and they maintain these positions against all the dynamics that would shift them. You may have passed one in the plaza, standing in the hip-deep water, the glazed ceiling reflected in the water reflected in their glazed eyes. You would not have distinguished them from the Pressed, initially. From the accumulation of faces and forms and mineral witness. But they are *maintaining*, they are *choosing* their stasis, and in that choice they differ from everything else in the Drypool.

What they wait for, none have said. Perhaps they do not know. Perhaps the waiting has become the totality of their purpose, the original goal dissolved like clay grain into solution, precipitated out as something else entirely. They were, perhaps, explorers, refugees, pilgrims. They became *inhabitants* not by adaptation but by *cessation*, by halting their foreign temporality so completely that the Drypool's slowness could flow around them, could accept them as another mineral fact, another held water surface, another statistical drip in the continuous hush.

They are conscious, if consciousness can survive such stretching, such attenuation. They perceive you passing. They do not respond. Response would be movement, would be change, would be the violation of their waiting. But they *record*, in whatever remains of their minds, the particularity of your passage. You are noted. You are added to the witness, to the density of attention, to the packed and total place. Your face, if you remain long enough, if you slow enough, if you achieve sufficient patience and sufficient saturation—you too may be added to the repetition. Not Pressed, not exuviae. *Accepted*. *Incorporated*. Made part of the Drypool's ongoing, its refusal to dry, its holding of water in interstices of fired clay until the water itself becomes the structure, becomes the meaning, becomes the who that lives in a where that was never dry.

Walk carefully. The tiles are slick. The Last Wet stand where they stand, have always stood, will always stand. The next drop falls somewhere, is falling, has always been falling. The Nacreates build their cups in the deep pools. The Formants press through when pressing is possible. The Accumulates thicken, grip, continue their stuck transformation. And you move through, breathing your difficult breath, your foreign temporality held in lungs that are not yet mineral, not yet wet enough, not yet *ready*.

Take your time. The Drypool certainly will.
