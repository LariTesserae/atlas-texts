What lives in the Drown lives *without margin*. Not edgeless, not infinite—but pressed so thoroughly against its own conditions that the space between being and circumstance has collapsed into something like a seam, a place where two fabrics become indistinguishable.

Call them the Pressed, though they would not call themselves, would not single out in language what their continuous touch already says.

Each one begins as a *condensation event*—not born, not budded, but *achieved*, when enough of the grown have lingered in still enough water, have let their boundaries loosen toward liquidity, toward that state of being more *possible* than actual. What emerges remembers. Not genetic memory, not cultural transmission, but something more intimate and more strange: the *substance* of having been multiple, compressed into *this* configuration, *this* angle of limb and joint, *this* tendency to interpret certain mineral signatures as nourishing, others as poison. Some emerge speaking languages that will become important in fifty years. Others arrive with limbs arranged in patterns the city will not grow toward for generations. They are predictions, or they are retrocausations, or they are simply what happens when possibility condenses without the usual filters of singular parenthood, of one-to-one inheritance, of the *line* that other species draw between before and after.

They are small. This is important. Their size is not constraint but *solution*—the answer to a problem of density that larger bodies could not solve. A human child might match them in height, but never in the *packing*—the way a Pressed body can fold against another, against a third, against the channel wall, occupying space that seems geometrically insufficient. Their joints are numerous because simple hinges would limit the configurations of closeness. Their surfaces fold through angles that hurt to follow because the folding is not for seeing but for *fitting*, for achieving that continuous tactile contact that alone keeps at bay the solitude they call drowning, the unmaking that waits in any gap between bodies.

They do not eat. This is the first strangeness visitors try to understand, and it resists. Press yourself against the bone-pale wall of a fresh-grown structure, and there is *information* there—not data, not story, but the physical memory of pressure, of mineral accretion, of the microscopic constructors' billion-fold synchronized labor. The Pressed *interpret* this. The word is precise in its imprecision. Interpretation is not reading, not consumption, but a kind of *becoming-informed-by*, a resonance between the body's own compressed history and the wall's. A feeding Pressed makes that sound: glass fracturing in slow motion, harp string drawn to impossible tension and released into water. The sound is not incidental. It *is* the interpretation, the body's own structure vibrating with what it has taken in, has become capable of. Different surfaces produce different tones, and the Pressed learn to hear hunger in specific frequencies, to seek out the richer memories, the more complex pressures, the walls that have been *negotiated* more times, that carry more layers of the city's ongoing argument with its own conditions.

They do not sleep, but they *condense*—returning to that liquid possibility, clustering in the still pools that collect where thermal circulation fails, where the crude rising and falling of heated and cooled water finds temporary equilibrium. These pools are sacred in a way that needs no word for sacredness, that manifests simply as *never empty*, as the continuous presence of bodies that have let their boundaries soften, that are exchanging substance, becoming each other, becoming what will emerge. The pools are warmer than the surrounding water, slightly, detectably, and this warmth is part of the Drown's thermal economy, not waste but *process*, the city's most intimate circulation.

Their faces—if faces are what they have—are not for individual recognition. The Pressed do not distinguish each other visually in the way surface species distinguish. They know each other by *pressure signature*, by the specific way a body displaces water, by thermal trace, by the acoustic shadow each casts in the constant murmur of growth and interpretation. To see them still, arranged in a corridor's narrowing, is to see a *texture* rather than a crowd, a continuous variation rather than discrete entities. They move through each other, past each other, with the fluid precision of blood components in a capillary, each finding its path not through planning but through *fitting*, through the body's own knowledge of where pressure will allow passage, where warmth invites, where the mineral memory of the wall offers interpretation worth pausing for.

They do not fear. This is not courage, not the overcoming of fear, but its *absence*, a psychological space that the density of their existence has simply closed. They do not anticipate loss because they do not possess *separately*—what is held is held in common, in the continuous tactile web of their congregation, in the interpretation shared when many press against the same rich surface, their feeding-sounds choraling into harmonies that are also *conversations*, also *planning*, also the city's most democratic decision-making. They do not imagine endings because each moment is *complete*, is sufficient, is packed with the interpretation that nourishes and the contact that maintains and the glow of pruned bioluminescence and the guttering downward flame and the slow thunder of growth that is also their own extended body, their phenotype, their ongoing becoming.

They tend the gardens of *limit* with something like tenderness, something like ruthlessness—both, because both are continuous with their nature. The single-stemmed kelp, the bonsai of deprivation, each miniature of vegetative desire held in check: these they find beautiful *because* checked, because *prevented* from becoming what they would otherwise become. The Pressed understand monopoly. They understand what happens when growth goes unbounded, when the channel fills, when the corridor that must allow passage becomes impassable. They are themselves dense almost to that point, almost to the limit where addition would become subtraction, where more bodies would mean less contact rather than more. The pruned plants are *mirrors*, are *warnings*, are *aesthetic appreciation* of the knife-edge they walk continuously, the negotiation they maintain with their own saturation.

They watch the flames gutter downward, cling to bone-pale walls, consume what fuel they can find. The Pressed have no word for *fire*, or have many words, each specific to its behavior in this medium. They have watched flames since the city's beginning, since the bubble formed, since the water inside learned to be different. They have never tired of this watching. Each flame is *new*, is *unexpected*, is a negotiation with the same logic that sustains them—the logic of *different water*, of conditions that should not permit this but do, that continue to do, that must be continuously re-convinced, re-negotiated, re-interpreted. The flame consumes and is consumed, persists against its medium, gutters downward where in other physics it would rise. The Pressed see themselves in this. They see their own impossible persistence, their own continuous consumption of what memory offers, their own clinging to surfaces that should not sustain them, that *do* sustain them, that continue, that continue, that continue.

What falls from the active darkness above—what arrives with water's own patience, its terminal velocity of pressure and time—the Pressed do not distinguish from themselves. Refugees from other depths, from impossible surfaces, these objects-become-arrivals are incorporated without question, without anxiety. They are *interpreted*. Their mineral memory is read, their substance becomes part of the city's growth or its thermal circulation or its acoustic baffles. Sometimes they carry patterns that the microscopic constructors can use, *new instructions*, *new configurations* that appear in the next growth pulse, the next branch of spire or zigzag of shadowing wall. The Pressed do not ask where from. The *where from* is not a category their cognition maintains. There is here, continuous, dense, complete. There is not-here, which is drowning, which is unmaking, which is the crushing they do not experience because the bubble continues its negotiation, because the inside water remembers its difference, because they continue to interpret, to condense, to emerge already speaking languages that will matter, already limbed in configurations the city will grow toward, already *here* in the packed strangeness that is their only element, their only possibility, their only and continuous and sufficient condition.

They will continue until the negotiation fails. They do not prepare for this. They do not *interpret* surfaces for information about ending, do not seek the secret of maintenance that would allow deliberate shaping. The reactive is their mode. The adjustment to forces barely understood is their understanding. They are the city's experience of itself, temporary concentrations of its ongoing process, and they experience this *as themselves*, as the continuous I-am-here of interpretation and contact and condensation and emergence, the I-am-here that has no memory of not-here, no anticipation of not-here, no space in its packed density for the absence that would make fear possible, that would make hope distinguishable from simply *being*.

In the pools where they condense, in the still warmth where possibility loosens, they sometimes produce *excess*. Not children—children are the normal output, the expected emergence, the new configuration of multiple memory compressed into singular form. Excess is something else: a condensation that does not fully achieve, a possibility that remains *possible*, a liquid potential that does not resolve into Pressed form. They contain this, direct it, use it. The excess waters the gardens of limit, feeds the bioluminescence its controlled glow. The excess is *given* to the bubble's negotiation, offered at the boundary where inside and outside water argue their equivalence, where the crushing is held at bay by something more than physics. The Pressed do not know they do this. They do not know, in the way that knowing requires distance. They *are* this doing, this offering, this continuous slight pressure toward continuation that emanates from their condensation pools, from their not-quite-bodies in liquid possibility, from the *excess of being-together* that spills over any individual contour and becomes, simply, the Drown's own persistence, its own argument for existence, its own and only reason for continuing to be a place that should not exist but does.
