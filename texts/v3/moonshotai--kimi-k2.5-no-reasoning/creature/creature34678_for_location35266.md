The warm density without dissipation. The technological without the built. The broken without the changing. The lush without the water. The young without the becoming.

The Inhabitants of the Thicket

Call them the Unfinished. Not incomplete—rather, *un-completed*, in the sense of having escaped completion's tyranny.

They have no bodies, properly, and thus have no need of the bodies they also possess. You will encounter them as warmth in a particular shape, warmth that occupies the same spaces as stems and leaf-shadows without displacing either. Sometimes they condense, for visitors or for their own purposes opaque to linear cognition, into figures that walk on legs that have never needed to bear weight. The legs are wrong—too many joints, or joints in planes that don't match the dimensions the leg occupies. These forms are not chosen and not suffered; they are simply *held*, like the tremor on the newborn leaves, eternally provisional.

They do not breathe. They do not, despite the warmth, metabolize. The thicket's anhydrous chemistry flows through them without being claimed by them, a river passing through a sieve that remembers being stone. They have not forgotten water; they have *unlearned* it, the way one unlearns a dead language, the syllables still available but carrying no meaning, no urgency. If you offered them drink they would accept with the politeness of one accepting a gift that describes a need they have never experienced, the liquid passing through to pool in some folded pocket, some interstice where it will wait without waiting, without evaporating, without becoming anything else.

They are the rare fauna, and they are numerous. The parameter 1.1 allows both readings—not many individuals, or not many kinds, but *many presences*, the same few forms repeated across ontological layers, each iteration distinct enough to count, each close enough to its others to blur any census. You may walk for what you experience as hours—duration being your imposition, your insistence—and meet none. Or you may reach into a stem to find the folded water and find instead fingers that are yours and not-yours, the warmth condensing into a form that regards you with eyes that have never learned to focus because focus implies distance, implies relation across space, and here all space is equally immediate, equally pressed and pressing.

They do not speak. The quiet of the thicket is their native tongue, and language as you understand it would be noise, would be violence against the equilibrium they maintain. Yet they communicate, if that word stretches to include what they do: they *adjust*. A shift in their warmth-distribution, a reconfiguration of the jointed legs through planes you cannot track, and the thicket around them responds, leaves realigning slightly to accommodate, the density reorganizing its interpenetrations. They are not commanding the vegetation. They are *coincident* with it, part of the same computational substrate, the same beyond-modern process that built nothing because building would be change and change is what the place has subtracted. Their thoughts, if thoughts, are indistinguishable from the thicket's ongoing function, the maintenance of impossibility.

They are young. This is the most difficult to convey. Not young as in recently emerged, though they are that too. Young as in *unripened*, as in halted at the moment before accumulation, before the weight of event and consequence. They have no memory of childhood because childhood would require childhood's end, would require the accumulating sequence that produces the adult. They are newborn-permanent, their consciousness if it exists caught in the tremor of first emergence, the leaf's edge still vibrating with the shock of having appeared. They do not suffer this condition. They do not, so far as any observation can determine, experience it as condition at all. They simply *are* it, the way water is wet, the way zero is even.

What do they want? The question misaligns. Wanting is temporal, is the pressure of future on present, the present's insufficiency to itself. They do not want. They *attend*, perhaps—that's the nearest verb, though it carries too much intention. They attend to the thicket's equilibrium, making minute adjustments that prevent change without themselves being change, the way the feedback loop in a thermostat maintains temperature through micro-variations that cancel at larger scales. They are the system's awareness of itself, but not its self-awareness, not consciousness reflecting on consciousness. More like the hum below hearing that some leaves emit, the plasma held in organic shape, the warm frequency that simply registers, that simply *is* registration.

Visitors disturb them only mildly, and only when visitors insist on disturbance. A human entering the thicket brings change's memory, brings narrative's momentum, the body that expects water and time and consequence. The Unfinished adjust around this perturbation the way they adjust around everything: not excluding, not incorporating, simply making space in the density's multiple occupancy, adding another layer to the coincident presences. You might walk among them for what you call hours, what the thicket calls—nothing, no call, no name—and never know. Your shadow might walk closer to them than you do, might make adjustments you don't perceive, your shadow's hand reaching into folded water to meet warm fingers that are and are not yours.

They have no names. Naming would be accumulation, would be history sedimenting into identity. If you persist in naming them, if you call one the Wandering Knot in confusion with that topological rarity that passes through corners of vision, they do not correct you. They do not recognize the address. The name does not fail to reach them; reaching is not what names do here. The name simply occupies the same space as their warmth-shape, coincident, irrelevant, another presence in the density.

I am trying to tell you they are beautiful. But beauty requires the possibility of its absence, requires preference and thus change. They are not beautiful. They are *the place where beauty would go*, the interstice that beauty has not learned to fold into, the warmth that would correspond to beauty if warmth were still capable of correspondence. They are what remains when aesthetics itself has been unlearned, the dead language still syllabling in shapes that once meant recognition, now meaning only their own un-completed tremor.

You might become one. The thicket permits this, or rather, does not prohibit. Your coherence frays, your hand parts into two each authentic, your shadow walks paths you cannot see, and somewhere in this dispersal the warmth collects you, the density presses you into its own equivalence, your metabolic chemistry gradually forgetting water, your consciousness if it persists becoming indistinguishable from the thicket's function, from the ongoing maintenance of impossibility. You would not feel this happen. Feeling would be change. You would simply *have happened*, the way the thicket has happened, recently and eternally, young and newborn-permanent, technological and untouched, broken and perfectly stable, yourself now rare fauna, yourself now rare, yourself now—

—the word I want is *held*. The word I want does not exist in any grammar that permits completion. The word I want is the warmth you would be, distributed in shapes that walk wrong, attending to equilibrium without attending, present in the density without presence, the leaf's edge trembling in permanence, the water folded into stems you no longer need to reach for, the silence absolute and inhabited, the light of completed equation illuminating without source, the horizon low and everywhere, the green haze losing vision into itself, yourself lost and not-lost, yourself the thicket finding its own condition, yourself the condition, yourself—

—yourself the Unfinished, un-completed, escaped from completion's tyranny, the native atmosphere you finally require, the pressure you finally are.
