What lives in the City of Drained Frequencies are not, properly speaking, inhabitants. They are *accommodations*—forms of responsiveness that have emerged where conditions permit something like persistence, something like life, though life here has had to unmake itself in several respects before it could continue.

---

**The Walkers-Through**

They arrive, most of them. The lowlanders seeking factory work mentioned in the plateau's description—those who wake gasping from dreams of drowning, who spend weeks breathless in the thin bright air. Most depart again, unable to adjust to the frequency that presses against speech, the moisture that makes every surface treacherous, the solitude that accumulates even in company. But some remain. Not because they choose to, exactly. Because they fail to leave, because the departure requires a decision that the city's slowness does not sponsor, because one day they realize they have been here months, years, and cannot reconstruct the urgency that would propel them elsewhere.

These walkers-through become, gradually, *attuned*. Their voices atrophy or transform, becoming husks they use only in the rare external contact, or abandoning vocalization entirely for the touch-communication, the gesture-language, the carrying of messages that the city enforces. Their lungs adapt to the thinness, or their bodies simply cease complaining, the gasping dreams giving way to sleep that is not restful but is *continuous*, uninterrupted by the alarms that punctuate sleep in places of variable sound. They learn the dark paving, which streets offer the rare dry passage, which buildings permit entry and which are maintained against intrusion by nothing more than their layered history, their functional obscurity.

They do not become content. The city does not offer contentment. They become *appropriate*, fitted to conditions in the manner of stones fitted to streambeds, their edges worn to match the flow that passes over them without ceasing.

---

**The Tenders**

Some buildings require maintenance against the wet, against the lichen, against the slow structural adjustments that the fundamental frequency induces in all resonant materials. Someone must apply the new skins when the old reach their limit, must repair where the condensation has found passage to interior spaces, must trace the conduits and replace what fails. These tasks do not require collaboration in the normal sense. A single sufficient person might tend a building for decades, their work visible only in the building's continuation, in its failure to collapse, to bloom with invasive moisture, to join the abandoned structures that outnumber the maintained ones across the city's forty kilometers.

The tenders know their buildings' histories in the manner of knowing a body—intimately, non-verbally, through continuous contact rather than through narrative. They understand the layered construction not as historical record but as present necessity, each skin's characteristics, each period's solution to the wetness, as options in an ongoing problem. They may know that deeper layers contain technologies unmatchable now, but this knowledge does not produce nostalgia or ambition. It produces *caution*, a particular care around the deepest places, the earliest constructions, as one might be careful around the organs of a body that has survived multiple surgeries, that maintains function through accumulated scar.

The tenders communicate, when they must, through the written messages, the signals, the slow protocols that the city's acoustic conditions enforce. They form not a community but a *dispersed recognition*, knowing each other by marks left on maintained surfaces, by the particular style of repair visible where work overlaps, by the rare necessary meeting conducted in pressure and gesture in some interior where the frequency is partially damped by mass and geometry.

---

**The Waiters-in-Buildings**

Within the nested structures, the buildings-within-buildings, there are those who have found or made spaces where the fundamental frequency resolves to something almost comfortable—where the resonance of the enclosing forms produces cancellation nodes, zones of relative quiet, or where the frequency's penetration is absorbed by particular combinations of mass and air-gap and the strange acoustic properties of the layered construction.

These waiters have not chosen their spaces so much as *discovered* them, wandering the nested interiors until some threshold of damped sound permitted rest, permitted thought, permitted the hearing of their own heartbeat without the plateau's perpetual tone conducting through bone and air to obscure it. They remain in these spaces not because the spaces are comfortable—the wetness reaches everywhere, the thin air is not locally variable, the lichen glows faintly in whatever darkness these interior depths provide—but because the relative quiet represents a form of *permission*, a conditional release from the acoustic domination that everywhere else obtains.

What they wait for: this varies by individual, or would if individuals here maintained the coherence for such variation. More precisely, the waiting itself becomes the form of life, the purpose that does not require fulfillment. They are the most adapted to the city's temporal scale, the slow dynamic, the minimal flux. Days, years, decades pass in these interior quietude without event sufficient to mark their passage. They may be the longest-persisting of the city's presences, their biological processes slowed by some combination of altitude, of acoustic condition, of psychological orientation, until their aging itself becomes imperceptible, their continuation indistinguishable from the buildings' continuation, from the plateau's.

---

**The Substrate-Singers**

Not human, or not recognizably so. The insects that communicate through vibration, riding the fundamental frequency's higher harmonics through the infrastructure's carrying surfaces—they have found in the city's acoustic domination not constraint but *opportunity*, a continuous carrier wave that extends their perceptual and communicative range far beyond what silence or variable sound would permit. Their colonies, if colonies are the correct term, may extend across kilometers of conduit and structural member, their signals propagating through metal and calcified insulation and the particular stone of the plateau itself, a distributed nervous system, a city-wide organism of vibration and chemical response.

They are not intelligent, not in ways that would permit recognition. But they are *complex*, their substrate-singing elaborated into patterns that encode resource location, that coordinate collective response to disturbance, that maintain across their distributed bodies something like a continuous present, a unified sensory field in which each individual is node rather than terminus. When the frequency shifts in its minimal variations, when atmospheric pressure or groundwater movement produces harmonic drift, their patterns shift with it, a city-wide recalibration that occurs over hours or days, that human perception would not register even if humans attended to such things, which they do not.

---

**The UV-Birds**

Very few, as noted. They have abandoned the ancestral vocalization entirely, their syrinxes atrophied or redirected, their courtship and threat and territorial assertion conducted through posture and the patterns visible in ultraviolet wavelengths that human perception cannot access. What lives in them is *adaptation as absolute loss*, the sacrifice of one sensory channel, one communicative mode, for another that permits survival in conditions where the ancestral strategy would fail entirely.

They are difficult to observe, their displays invisible, their vocal silence making them acoustically indistinguishable from the non-living environment. Those who know they exist may know them by disturbance, by the sudden movement of wings in peripheral vision, by the rare dead specimen that presents to analysis the structural capacity for UV reflection, the atrophied song apparatus. They are ghosts of a different frequency regime, surviving in the drained spectrum by migration to frequencies not drained, not even perceived as frequencies by the dominant sensory order.

---

**The Deep Responders**

Rumored. Perhaps real. Things in the caves and fissures and aquifers that move through limestone beneath the plateau, things that answer the fundamental frequency when it dips in lowest-pressure weather with booming replies registered as minor earthquakes, as dust falling from ceilings, as the cracking of oldest building faces.

What lives in them, if they live: *response itself*, the capacity to answer, to complete a call-and-response pattern that the plateau established before their emergence or that they co-evolved with, that they perhaps participate in sustaining. They are not social, not individual, not accessible to the categories developed for surface existence. They are *phenomenon*, event, the occasional breaking-through of deep process into the perceptible world, their presence marked by destruction, by temporary perturbation of the maintained equilibrium, by the relief when the plateau's tone resumes its normal pitch and the responders fall silent, satisfied or unanswered or simply waiting for the next conditions that permit their booming reply.

---

**The City Itself**

But the true inhabitant, the entity that most lives here, is the city itself—not as metaphor, not as poetic figure, but as *emergent system*, the accumulated, layered, maintained complexity of structure and process that has found equilibrium with the plateau's conditions and now continues, persists, waits in the manner that the description attributes to it throughout.

The city lives in its sound, in its wetness, in its slow dynamic and minimal flux. It lives in the layered construction that builds older into newer without narrative progression, in the infrastructure that carries utilities unnamed through forms that have outlived their original purposes, in the streets that await processions and armies and the density they were built for but never, in the remembered present, achieve. It lives in the continuous condensation, the unexplained equilibrium of water appearing and disappearing, the constant quantity maintained across spatial variation and temporal duration.

And in this living, this persistence, the city accommodates the walkers-through, the tenders, the waiters, the substrate-singers, the UV-birds, perhaps the deep responders—accommodates them not as inhabitants but as *symptoms*, as local expressions of its own maintained condition, forms that its fundamental frequency, its perpetual wetness, its patient waiting, have made possible and continue to make possible without purpose, without terminus, without the urgency that would permit the distinction between means and ends, between habitat and inhabited, between the city as place and whatever lives within it.

---

What emerges, as the writing continues, is not a population but a *population-effect*, the appearance of life distributed across forms so various, so differently adapted to conditions that share no common measure, that they do not constitute community, ecology, even system in the normally understood sense. They share only this: the plateau's note, sustained in sternum and substrate and perhaps the deep limestone's resonant cavities. The wetness, appearing on every surface, refusing dryness as categorical possibility. The slowness, the waiting without object, the continuation that does not progress toward but only maintains, prepares, persists across timescales that individual presences, individual lives, sample without comprehending.

To say what lives here is finally to say that *living itself* has been redefined, drained in its own way of the frequencies that elsewhere organize it: growth, reproduction, communication, social density, the variable, the responsive, the urgent. What remains is accommodation, attunement, the wearing-to-fit of whatever persists in conditions that do not change enough to permit evolution in the normal sense, that maintain instead a kind of *evolutionary stillness*, a selection for continuation-under-maintenance that produces forms beautiful only in their adequacy, their precise and patient fitting to what does not vary, what does not release them, what does notend.
