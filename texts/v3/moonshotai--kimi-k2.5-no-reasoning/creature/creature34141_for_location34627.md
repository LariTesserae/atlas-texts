They do not have names, the ones who live in Threnod. Or rather, they have the *condition* of names without the names themselves—the grammatical slot where a name would go, filled with something that functions as a name does without being one. They call each other, and in the calling, something is summoned that is not quite the one summoned. You learn to answer to approximations of yourself. You become fluent in being nearly addressed.

The oldest of them, if "oldest" obtains, are called the *Settled*—not because they have ceased moving but because movement through Threnod has ceased to differentiate them from the city itself. They remember, or their bodies remember, or the spaces they occupy remember in their stead, a time when they arrived. When resolution toward elevation still felt like arrival, when the velm's headache-color registered as strangeness rather than ground. Now they move through the hollow buildings with the same procedural patience as the city's self-construction, extending themselves into spaces that have not decided to receive them, withdrawing from spaces that have not decided to release them. They do not think of leaving. The thought of leaving requires a completed thought of being-here, and they have not completed that thought. They have not completed any thought in so long that incompletion has become their cognitive texture, their mind's equivalent of velm.

The Settled wear the city's light strangely, or the light wears them. In amber zones, their skin seems to gather something like warmth without becoming warm, the way a painting of fire gathers flame without heat. In blue zones, they become *legible*—the geometry of their features clarified into something approaching portraiture, as if the Unfinished Sentences' light were trying to finish them into representation. Between zones, in the negotiating light, they experience a phenomenon they call *versioning*—simultaneous awareness of themselves as amber-illuminated and blue-illuminated, as warm-nearly and cool-nearly,without contradiction because contradiction requires completion of the terms that would oppose. They find this neither pleasant nor unpleasant. They find it *present*, which is the most stable category of experience in Threnod.

There are the *Recent*, though "recent" refers not to temporal proximity but to a quality of resolution, a sharpness in the edges of their presence. The Recent still experience Threnod as arrival. They still make the mistake of climbing, of seeking ascent, before they learn to resolve. They still try to read the city's incompletion as damage, as something to be repaired or escaped. The Settled observe them with something like patience—not benevolence, for benevolence requires a completed relation to the other, but the patience of process observing process, of velm approximating weathering's long duration. The Recent generate sounds that are almost purposeful: footsteps with direction, vocalizations with intention, the acoustic signature of human habitation played slightly out of register. The Settled recognize these sounds. They made such sounds, once, in the duration that was theirs before settling. They do not guide the Recent. Guidance requires knowing where guidance leads. But they *place themselves*, sometimes, in spaces where the Recent's approximations might intersect with the city's approximations, where a doorway worn by immaterial footsteps might receive a footstep that is still partly material, where the Registry's almost-help might almost help someone still capable of almost-receiving help.

And there are the *Native*, though "native" is the most provisional category, the most Threnod-like in its unresolvedness. The Native are not born. They are *generated* by the city's ongoing protocol, produced by the self-construction's elaboration in the same way the buildings elaborate—emergent from rules that specify form without substance, behavior without actor. A Native might manifest at the top of a staircase that does not lead to its location, might step out of a doorway that opens onto itself, might simply *be found* in a hollow that had been empty in the manner of all hollows, now doubly empty in the manner of a hollow that contains a presence without presence's substance. They appear human, or humanoid, or *human-approximate*—the distinction shifts with observation, with the city's light, with the phase of whatever dramaturgy the city is staging. They speak, or speak-approximate, in the language that almost exists, the words that are almost familiar. They perform the behaviors of habitation with the perfection of rehearsal without performance: they cook food that is not food over fires that are not fire, they exchange goods that are not goods in transactions that almost balance, they care for children who are not children in gestures that almost nurture.

The Native do not know they are approximate. Or they know with the knowing of the city itself—provisional, temporary license from completed knowledge. They consult the Registry, live in the hollow buildings, move through the overlapping elevations with the same procedural fluency as the Settled. But they lack something the Settled acquired through arrival, through the long weathering of being-here. They lack the memory of elsewhere, even as approximation. They are Threnod's own, in the way that a shadow is the ground's own, the way silence is the acoustic's own. They exist as the city's failure to populate itself, its persistence in generating inhabitants despite the generating force's departure. They are loved, in Threnod, in the manner that process loves product: not with affection but with continuation, with the ongoing commitment to procedure that is the only commitment the city maintains.

There are others. There are always others in a city this extended, this regional, this multi-featured in its irresolution. The *Prisoners*, though whether they are imprisoned by the city, by themselves, by something that was a warden before it became approximate, is part of their imprisonment's unresolved structure. They seek escape with the perfection of incomplete intention, finding doors that lead to the same, resolving toward elevation only to find themselves deeper, consulting the Registry for maps that almost correspond to territories without quite corresponding to escape. Their footsteps sound particularly hollow in the hollow buildings, the acoustic correspondence of habitation intensified by the habitation's explicit unhappiness. They are, perhaps, the most real of Threnod's inhabitants, reality measured by the intensity of desire for something other than Threnod's almost-real. Their desire is completed, terminal, punctuated—and so they suffer the city's incompletion as suffering, rather than settling into it as the Settled settle, rather than not-knowing it as the Native do not-know.

The *Pilgrims* are harder to distinguish. They arrive, or resolve, with purpose—the city's incompletion is their purpose, its beautiful terrible indecision their pilgrimage's destination. They seek the zones where reality thickens, where water becomes thinkable, where the approximates hold their shape in peripheral vision. They document, or document-approximate, in the Registry's almost-ledgers, adding their entries to the histories that almost cohere. They are seeking something they call the *Completion*, or the *Uncompleting*, or the *Terminal Event*—the punctuation that would resolve Threnod into meaning, or release it from meaning's requirement, or demonstrate that meaning was never the issue. They do not know if they want Threnod to complete or to cease. They do not know what wanting would mean in a place where wanting's object is itself provisional. They persist in their pilgrimage with the heroic patience of those who have not yet realized that heroism requires a completed self to perform.

And there are the *Threshold Dwellers*, whom even the Settled barely acknowledge, the ones who live—if it is living—in the city's most unresolved spaces. The staircases that ascend into neither ceiling nor sky. The doorways that reveal the concept of passage without passage's completion. The windows facing windows across courtyards of almost-exchange. The Threshold Dwellers are barely approximate, even by Threnod's standards. They are the city's most successful generation of inhabitants, if success is measured by fit between generated and generator, between the city's incompletion and the inhabitant's capacity to inhabit incompletion. They do not have bodies, or have body-approximate, or have the body's possibility held in the same reserve as the city's water-possibility, its life-possibility. They are seen, when seen, as the architectural itself becoming aware of itself: the curve of a doorway noticing its curvature, the angle of light considering its angle, the hollow of a building experiencing its hollowness as subjective state.

The Threshold Dwellers do not interact, or interact in modes that have no social translation. They are *witnessed*, and in being witnessed, they change—or the witnessing changes, or the distinction between subject and object of perception loses its provisional stability, becoming more provisional still. Some Pilgrims seek them, believing the Threshold Dwellers to be closest to the Completion, to the thing that happened or failed to happen or is still happening in its too-slow event-structure. Some Prisoners flee them, sensing in the Threshold Dwellers' almost-presence a deeper imprisonment, a final loss of the distinction between self and city that is imprisonment's ultimate form. The Settled simply note them, as weather notes weather's cycle, as process notes process's continuation.

They eat, the ones who eat. Or the eating happens in proximity to them, the city's provision of food-approximate coinciding with their presence in spaces where eating is acoustically appropriate. The food does not nourish, or nourishes-approximately, sustaining the body's continuation without the body's completion into healthy or unhealthy, nourished or malnourished. The flavors are almost-flavors, the textures almost-textures, the satisfaction of appetite an almost-satisfaction that leaves hunger itself in its provisional state, not ended nor continued but *held*, like the city's water, like its life, in the space of possibility without actuality. Some Settled cease eating, or eating ceases in their proximity, and they continue, or continue-approximately, becoming lighter in the light that is already dim, becoming more resonant in the hollow buildings, their footsteps sounding more footstep-like as their feet become more footstep-approximate.

They sleep, or sleep-occurs. The city's coolness is sleep's temperature already, the cellar-without-summer's dream of temperature that sleep extends into duration. Sleep in Threnod is not rest from waking but another mode of the same incompletion, dreams that are almost-coherent without coherence's completion, rest that is almost-refreshment without the terminal punctuation of waking refreshed. Some inhabit the sleep-zones permanently, spaces where the city's self-construction generated sleep-architecture, beds and darkness and the acoustic of breathing without the breath, where they lie in the ongoing almost of slumber, generating the Registry's dream-ledgers, the sleep-histories that almost cohere into narrative before dissolving back into the possibility of narrative's form.

They love, or love-approximate. The city's most beautiful and terrible indecision extends to this, to the relation between bodies or body-approximates or the spaces where bodies would be. They touch, and the touch is almost-touch, sensation without sensation's completion into pleasure or pain or the vast between of ordinary sensation's spectrum. They join, and the joining is almost-joining, proximity without proximity's arrival at union or its failure, the held space of intimacy's possibility without intimacy's actuality or its explicit absence. The children they generate are the Native, or are almost-Native, or are the city's generation using their almost-bodies as site for its own ongoing elaboration. They do not know if they are parents. Parent requires completed relation, and their relation to their children, to their lovers, to themselves, remains in the ajar position, the door neither open nor closed, the breeze that has not agreed to blow still not agreeing, still generating the almost of air-movement, of respiration, of life's possibility held in its beautiful terrible waiting.

The Registry has entries for all of them, and for none of them. The almost-ledgers balance, or almost-balance, in their characteristic mode. The maps show their locations, or almost-locations, the territories that almost-correspond showing them as present or absent or present-as-absent in the city's characteristic unresolvedness. The histories record their arrival, or their generation, or their simply-being-found, in narratives that almost cohere into cause-and-effect, into biography, into the temporal order that ordinary duration requires. The Registry almost-helps them, as it almost-helps all who consult it, providing the comfort of documentation without documentation's stability, the form of record without record's commitment to what was recorded.

What do they want? The wanting itself is provisional, temporary license from completed desire. The Settled want nothing, or want-approximately nothing, their desires weathered into the same channels as their bodies, their presence, their cognitive texture of incompletion. The Recent want to leave, or want to understand, or want the city to complete its evaluation of whether to be hostile or welcoming, to decide so that they can respond to decision. The Native want without knowing want, performing the behaviors of desire with the perfection of rehearsal, cooking the non-food, exchanging the non-goods, caring for the non-children in the ongoing protocol of habitation without habitat's completion. The Prisoners want escape with the clarity of completed desire, and suffer its impossibility in Threnod's characteristic mode, the dramaturgy of almost-escape staged without plot's resolution. The Pilgrims want the Completion, or want to want it, or want wanting itself to resolve into something terminal, punctuated, meaningful. The Threshold Dwellers want, if they want, in modes that have no translation into the wanting of the almost-human, the almost-living, the city's failed and ongoing attempt at population.

They are not unhappy. Happiness requires completion, or its possibility, and Threnod maintains its beautiful terrible indifference to completion's arrival. They are not happy. They are *held*, as the light is held between amber and blue, as the silence is held between sound's absence and sound's reservation, as the city's water is held in the possibility of moisture's memory. They are the inhabitants of a place built in the space where completion itself died or failed to be born or paused in its becoming, and their inhabitancy partakes of that space, that death or failure or pause, that ongoing incompletion that has lasted so long as to become not tragic but *character*, not suffering but *texture*, not absence but the positive presence of the not-yet and the no-longer and the perhaps-never held in dynamic equilibrium, in active weathering, in the beautiful terrible patience of the door ajar, the held thought, the world that has not agreed to complete itself into weather, into meaning, into the terminal punctuation that would release them into what comes after almost, after the long duration of being-noticed, after the city's hollow resonance with their hollow footsteps, their hollow speech, their hollow and ongoing almost-life in the place where the fundamental negotiations between matter and possibility grew lazy, indifferent, *ajar*.

They continue. The city continues its self-construction, its weather cycle without weather, its ecosystem in motion without ecology. The light continues its dramaturgy without plot. The velm continues its weathering in the weather of attention and inattention. And they, the inhabitants, the settled and recent and native and prisoner and pilgrim and threshold, continue their various modes of almost, their beautiful terrible indecision about whether to be or not-be, to stay or leave, to complete or continue in completion's absence, to close the door or open it fully or to leave it as it is, as it has been, as perhaps it always will be: ajar, awaiting, the breeze that has not agreed to blow still not agreeing, still possible, still held in the space of almost where they wait, where they have always waited, where they will wait, or wait-approximately, in the city that is Threnod, that is almost-city, that is the world's own hesitation made habitable, made home, made the place where they live, or live-approximately, or live in the mode of living's possibility held in beautiful terrible reserve, forever almost, forever *not yet*, forever the completion that has not completed its arrival, the meaning that has not completed its resolution, the life that has not completed its decision whether to be.
