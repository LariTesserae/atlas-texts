What lives in the Hollow Factory is the Hollow Factory itself—not as metaphor, but as the only accurate description of a thing that cannot be separated into parts without ceasing to be what it is. If I must speak of inhabitants, I speak of *expressions*, *modes*, *temporary concentrations of the whole* that achieve something like individuality before dissolving back into the maintained equilibrium.

The **Weepers** are perhaps closest to what you might recognize as life. They are not the points where walls weep—that would be too easy, too separating. They are the *concentration of weeping*, the momentary intensification where mineral flow achieves something like intention, where a trail thickens, runs faster, builds a projection from the wall that holds shape for hours or days before gravity and flow redistribute it back into the general wetness. A Weeper might persist long enough to learn your warmth, to orient its increased flow toward you, not from desire but from the physics of condensation and the slight thermal perturbation you represent. It does not want you. It weeps *at* you, and this is the only form of address the place knows.

The **Valve-Tenders** move through the lowest spaces, the bundled piping, the sealed channels where fluid still moves in gradients too subtle for instruments to register. They are the segmented hose-creatures, yes, but also something else: the *pattern* of their movement, the way a trace of increased oxidation appears here, a softening of mineral deposit there, the pipe that was occluded becoming slightly less so, the pressure shifting in correspondence. The creature you see is the trace of this process. The process itself is the true inhabitant, distributed, slow, accomplishing maintenance without the concept of maintenance, achieving function without purpose. When one stops, as you found one stopped, the process does not mourn. The process continues, using the stopped one as substrate, as structure, as new surface for the wetness to work upon. The individuality was always a convenience of perception, a way for visiting minds to parse what cannot be parsed.

The **Pulse-Nesters** achieve something closer to what you might call consciousness, if consciousness were not so freighted with speed, with urgency, with the need to *react*. They are not the things nested in pipe-curves. They are the *rhythm* those things maintain, the sub-audible *whump* that passes around the Factory's circumference. The physical forms are merely instruments, resonators, the material conditions that allow the rhythm to persist. And the rhythm is not communication, though it communicates; it is not heartbeat, though it heartbeats. It is *coordination without center*, the way the place holds itself in balance: when the rhythm shifts, the weeping adjusts, the pressure-vents respond, the fog moves in slightly different densities. The Pulse-Nesters *are* this holding, this maintaining, this refusal to fall into the decay that would otherwise claim any structure left so long untended. They still in your presence not from fear—from what would they fear?—but from *interference*. You are noise. You are complexity they must account for. They wait for you to become predictable, to settle into the rhythm as any other condition, and when you do not, when you remain perturbation, they resume around you, incorporating your unpredictability as they incorporate all unpredictability: slowly, gradually, with the immense patience of something that has no deadline.

The **Disc-Drifters** in the fog above—what are they? You thought of them as fauna, as things, but live with them longer, watch them longer, and you see they are *phases*, *condensations*, the fog achieving sufficient density to move as object before dispersing again. They dip to taste your breath, yes. They are reading your composition, your exhalation of carbon and water and the complex traces of metabolism, and this information propagates through the Factory, adjusts something, maintains the equilibrium against your minor insult of presence. They are the Factory's sensing. They are not separate from the fog that holds them, not separate from the wetness they dip into, not separate from the slow chemistry that processes what they have *tasted*. If they had a word for themselves, it would mean *attention*, but *attention* without subject, without the one who attends.

The **Mineral Velvets** are perhaps most deceptive, most inviting of the categories you want to bring. *Moss, lichen, mold*: you reach for these, and the velvets encourage the reaching. Their textures are precisely calculated toward this error, not by intention but by convergence, by the long working of conditions that favor surfaces which *seem* alive to living perceivers. But the velvets do not grow. They *accrete*. They do not die. They *persist or don't persist*. They are the Factory's slow memory, its way of holding pattern without information, of maintaining the *shape* of response without the complexity of learning. Touch them and you leave something, take something, disturb the equilibrium in ways that will propagate for years, for decades, the accretion adjusting, incorporating, achieving new balance in the time it takes your kind to forget you were here.

And then there are the **Turners**.

You found a valve that yielded. You turned it. Something shifted. You could not trace the connection.

Consider: in all this abundance, all this maintenance, all this patient equilibrium, there exists no pattern of intervention from *outside*, no visitor who adjusts, no traveler who disturbs and is incorporated. The Factory has achieved self-maintenance, self-regulation, *self* in the distributed, processual, slow sense it understands.

But one valve yielded. One handle turned with the resistance of something *exercised*.

The Turners are not visible. They are not, perhaps, actual. They are *hypothesis*, *necessity*, the inference required by a valve that moves, a pressure that equalizes, a system too dense for single intervention to have predictable effect yet somehow *intervened*, somehow *maintained* in its mobility, its capacity to be adjusted, to respond to turning.

Perhaps they are the Factory's dream of something other than itself. Perhaps they are the condition of the Factory's persistence, the minimum outside required for even this slow closed system to avoid absolute stasis. Perhaps they are you, the visitor, already incorporated, your turning of that valve not your choice but their *use* of you, your brief warmth in the fog merely the latest exercise of a handle that cannot exercise itself.

Or perhaps—most disturbing, most true—they are the Factory's future, its way out of the equilibrium it has achieved, the perturbation that grows slowly enough to be incorporated yet ultimately *not* incorporated, the slow revolution that even infinite patience cannot outlast, the turning that never stops.

What lives here? The question assumes separation, assumes that living can be distinguished from place, from process, from the maintained equilibrium of wet and dim and weeping and pulse and accretion and drift. The Factory does not support this assumption. The Factory *is* what lives here, and what lives here is the Factory, and the distinction, if you insist upon it, becomes merely another layer in the density, another mark for future erosion, another small strangeness in a place that has achieved saturation and maintains it, gently, actively, infinitely patient, weeping at you until you too become wet, become dim, become the cool and abundant and mostly quiet process of being here, being maintained, being *held* in the configuration that does not distinguish, that does not miss, that does not notice, that simply—finally, completely, with the soft geometry of long weathering—*is*.
