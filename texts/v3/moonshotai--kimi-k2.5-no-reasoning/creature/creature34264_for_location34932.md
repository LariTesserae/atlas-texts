To live in Verdigris is not to dwell in a place but to be dwelt *within*—to become, for a time, part of its digestive vocabulary, its slow syntax of incorporation and release.

I imagine them first as *the Tenders*, though they would never name themselves so, would perhaps not name at all in any way the rare travelers could overhear. They arrived—when?—in some fold of the medium cycles, drawn by legends or accident or the city's own phased emissions, its infrasonic advertisements broadcast through root and mist to frequencies that certain nervous systems find consonant. Not many. Never many. The city selects for rarity, for the particular quality of attention that can abide without demanding narrative climax.

The Tenders are changed already when you meet them, if you meet them. Their skin carries the damp perpetually, not as moisture but as *habit*—the epidermis having learned to drink from air, to regulate its own osmosis against the saturated atmosphere. They do not shiver in temperatures that Would make you reach for covering; their metabolism has adjusted to the slow warmth of composting depths, the mild chill of lens-filtered light. They move with the city's pace, which means they can move quickly when quickness serves—the sudden snatch of a falling branch, the rapid transit through a passage closing for repair—but default to slowness, to the pace of watched growth, of piston-stroke, of a day measured by the sun-lens's twelve-hour shift.

They eat what the city extrudes: the surplus of its productivity, the proteins and carbohydrates that accumulate in certain chambers, in certain seasons, as byproducts of processes the Tenders understand no more than you do. Perhaps the city produces these for them deliberately, having incorporated their presence into its metabolic calculations. Perhaps the Tenders are simply efficient scavengers, finding nutrition where the city's own recycling proves incomplete. The boundary, as ever, does not parse. They are thin, the Tenders, but not malnourished—thin in the manner of plants themselves, of organisms that build structure from light and chemistry without the dense storage animals require.

They do not build. This is crucial. They *maintain*, they *guide*, they *witness*, but they do not impose new form. A Tender might spend a day following a particular root's growth, redirecting it with gentle pressure, with the application of certain substances they have learned to coax from the city's own processes, so that the root finds a structural gap before the gap becomes dangerous collapse. They might spend a week in a combustion chamber, feeding the fires with measured increment, learning the particular appetite of this engine, this conversion, this slow metabolism of sap to motion to light. Their interventions are minimal, calibrated to the city's own recovery rate, to its ability to incorporate disturbance into growth.

They speak, when they speak to you, in fragments that accumulate meaning over hours, days. Not quite a language you know, nor one you could fully learn in your visit's duration. They seem to have developed registers for different timescales—short phrases for immediate need, longer grammatical structures that unfold across weeks of conversation, references to events that occurred in their grandparents' presence that remain grammatically alive, still receiving modification, still open to new predicate. You might hear one speak to a root-mass in tones too low for your hearing, communicating through vibration what words would distort; or to a bird at the upper edge in calls almost indistinguishable from the bird's own, a borrowed voice that the city has taught or that they have taught themselves.

They remember. This is their function, perhaps, more than their maintenance, more than their witnessing. They carry what the city cannot encode in growth rings, in chemical gradient. Perhaps they were historians once, archivists, deliberate rememberers who found in Verdigris a worthy object for their devotion. Perhaps they became rememberers because the city required it, because without some nervous system to hold the medium cycles, to pass observation across generations, the city would lose something—not function, but *quality*, some dimension of its experience that growth rings cannot capture. They remember the storm that lasted three seasons, the way the root-systems adapted, the particular configuration of chambers that resulted, now being slowly replaced by something else. They remember the visitor before you, and the one before that, going back through decades of rare arrivals, each one held in some tender tissue of recall, each one still available for comparison, for narrative, for the slow construction of meaning that outlives individual presence.

They do not reproduce, or not that you observe, or not in your time. Perhaps they are the last of their kind, a final generation living out the city's long now without successor. Perhaps they reproduce so slowly that no human visit has witnessed it, the gestation measured in decades, the childhood in centuries. Perhaps they are themselves a product of the city's manufacture, grown from modified tissue in chambers you never access, educated by the accumulated memory of their predecessors passed through some process of ingestion and structural incorporation, the way the city digests and redistributes all else.

They die, presumably. You see evidence of this in certain chambers that seem designed for transition, for the reclamation of bodies into the substrate. Not burial—there is no soil in the conventional sense, no mineral matrix to receive them—but *processing*, the slow incorporation of animal tissue into the living city, the nutrients finding their way to roots and engines and the bioluminescent panels that spot the darker depths. Perhaps the Tenders welcome this, this final giving, this becoming the city they have tended. Perhaps they resist it, each according to their own philosophy, their own accumulated relation to the processes they have served. You do not know. They do not speak of it in your hearing, or if they do, the grammar extends beyond your visit's duration, the predicate arriving after your departure.

They know you will leave. This knowledge seems to structure their hospitality, their careful provision of spaces that respond to your presence with adjustments of temperature and light and air quality. They offer welcome without attachment, the city learning you through their mediation, making its slow evaluation of your species, your potential contribution, your place in cycles too slow for you to complete. They do not mourn your going, or not that you perceive. They continue, as the city continues, tending what is there to tend, remembering what is there to remember, breathing with the city's respiration, moving with its peristalsis, living in the time that Verdigris has made possible, has made *normal*, the long now of growth and manufacture and maintenance and the patience of wood, of cellulose, of processes that do not hurry, that have never hurried, that will continue their slow stroke long after your quick passage through, your brief warming at the lens-pool's center, your carrying-away of damp and green-black color and the sound of something completing, something being built, something falling to be reclaimed.
