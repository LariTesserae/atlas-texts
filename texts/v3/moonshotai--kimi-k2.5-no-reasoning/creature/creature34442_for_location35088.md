The one who lives here—a particular one, not a type, though she is also a type—has no name in the sense that names are used elsewhere. She is designated by a sound-pattern: a sustained tone at the frequency of her cavity's resonance, modulated by the particular way she taps her work-stone when entering from the Ascending passage. Others produce this pattern when they wish her attention. She recognizes variations—her mother's version, her neighbor-below's, the merchant who comes every third thermal cycle—that carry emotional information she could not articulate if asked, is not asked, does not ask.

She is middle-aged, which here means she has survived the first beetle-population crash of her lifetime, the one that killed her sibling and nearly killed her, the one that taught her the taste of true hunger and the sound of families collapsing in the channels below, their tapping becoming irregular, then stopping. She carries this as body-knowledge, as a slight tightness in how she draws her head down when the cultivated niches seem too quiet.

Her moss-fur is thick, well-tended, a deep verging-to-infrared that in her people's range reads as healthy, as established. She tends it herself in the warm minutes after waking, before the vertical commerce begins, picking out dead patches, feeding it with the paste she makes from her own waste and the plain's residue, a cycle of intimacy with her own body that is also maintenance of self. The symbiotes have been with her since birth, inherited from her mother in the smear of first contact, and they remember things she does not—her mother's warmth, her sibling's chemical signature, the particular quality of light in the cavity where she was born, now occupied by another family, another pattern, another life.

She works in sound. Not the message-calling of the full resonance-learners, not that dedication, that risk of spending too long in the deep channels where listeners gather and feed on what sustained tone produces in the stone. She makes tools for sound: the struck-stones that produce clear overtones, the hollows carved to specific dimensions, the beetle-shell resonators that amplify in particular ranges. Her cavity is known for this, visited by merchants, by the young who wish to learn, by the occasional traveler from a distant level who has heard of her work in the cumulative rumor that passes for news.

She lives alone now. This is not unusual but not standard; most maintain vertical families, the cavity-chain structure, living above and below relatives or negotiated partners. She had this once. She does not now. The transition was slow, patient, the way things end here—not a rupture but a gradual modulation, her partner's tapping becoming less frequent, then ceasing, the space above hers emptying, then filling with another whose pattern she does not choose to learn. She does not think of this as loss, or thinks of it in a way that has no translation to the categories I am forced to use. It is *settled*. It is *complete*. The anti-erosion that the stone achieves, she has achieved in her living arrangements, a form that further weathering only polishes.

Her days: waking in the warmth that rises from below, the stone beneath her never cooling, never quite the same temperature. The moss-fur tending. The work, the tapping and testing and the patient adjustment of stone to stone, searching for the tone that a particular commission requires. The eating, alone, the beetle-paste or the mineral-bread or the occasional cultivated glider-meat that marks a thermal festival. The listening, always the listening, to the spire's vast drone, extracting from it information about weather (thermal stability, the plain's breathing), about social events (disputes, couplings, deaths, the illegal ceremonies on the surface she has never attended but recognizes in the changed patterns of those who do), about the listeners in the deep channels, their feeding cycles, their dangerous approach toward the inhabited spaces.

She has a particular relationship with one listener. It has developed over years, patient as everything here, and she does not know if it is friendship, threat, symbiosis, or some category that exists only in the negotiation between her kind and theirs. She discovered it during a period of grief—not the sharp grief of her sibling's death, long past, but a slower grief, the accumulated weight of living, that made her seek the deeper channels, that made her produce sounds she did not understand, sustained drones in frequencies that hurt her own ears. The listener came. It fed, she thinks, on what she produced. It did not harm her. Now she goes to the same depth, the same narrow channel, on no schedule she could explain, and produces her drone, and waits. Sometimes it comes. Sometimes it does not. She has never seen it clearly—only pale suggestion in the absolute dark, only the way sound behaves differently when it is near, absorbed or amplified or changed in ways that have no name.

She dreams of drowning, sometimes, though she does not sleep on the plain. The warmth induces it, occasionally, even within the spire, when thermal events push the temperature above the usual stable range. She dreams of breathing liquid stone, of the pressure that is also sustenance, of a vastness that is not the plain's empty extension but something else, something before or after or parallel to this existence. She wakes from these dreams with her moss-fur fully extended, vibrating, producing unconscious sound that takes minutes to subside. She does not speak of this. Others do not speak of their similar dreams. It is part of the spire, part of the negotiation with heat and stone and time.

She is making something now, has been making it for years, will be making it when she dies unless she finishes, which seems increasingly unlikely. It is a resonator of unprecedented complexity, incorporating channels and chambers at scales from beetle-shell to her own body-size, designed to produce a tone that she heard once—where? in a dream? in the deep channel with the listener? in the accidental combination of spire-sounds on a particular thermal morning?—a tone that seems to her to contain something necessary, something the spire lacks, something that would change the negotiation if it could be produced, shared, heard.

She adds to the resonator when the work comes easily, sets it aside when it does not, has modified her cavity twice to accommodate its growing size, will modify again.
