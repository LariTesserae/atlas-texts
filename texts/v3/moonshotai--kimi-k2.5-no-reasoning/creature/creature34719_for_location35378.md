What lives in Hush lives *with* Hush, not upon it. This distinction matters because the inhabitants do not stand apart from their world; they are mineralogical events in the same continuous precipitation, biological accidents that the supersaturated chemistry has not yet decided whether to permit or dissolve.

---

Call them the **Muffled**, though they have no spoken name for themselves, though the word is wrong in every register except the descriptive. They are human-shaped, mammal-warm, breathing the saturated air with lungs that have learned to extract oxygen from moisture so dense it approaches liquid. Their skin has adapted to the perpetual condensation: a modified integument, neither scale nor hide nor exactly the skin of their unremembered ancestors, but something *interstitial*, something that handles the wetness by participating in it. Their surfaces are faintly slick, not with oil but with a biofilm, a living moisture that merges indistinguishably with the atmospheric weeping. To touch a Muffled person is to touch Hush itself, the boundary between body and world so eroded by continuous contact that neither can claim priority.

They are born from each other, though "born" requires its own grammar here. The Muffled do not couple in the familiar rhythm of pursuit and completion; they press together in the dense vertical shafts, in the ladder-descents, in the pockets of thunder-cancellation where vibration through stone becomes possible, and they *remain* pressed. The fetus, when it forms, is not sheltered in one body but *distributed*, a shared mineral-biological precipitation that occurs where their mutual touch persists longest. The mothers—there are always multiple, the concept of singular parenthood meaningless in a society where bodies pass hand to hand hourly—carry the developing form as a thickened region of their shared slick integument, a bulge that passes between them, that hardens slightly, that eventually (no one counts intervals, no one could) separates as a new body, already knowing stone, already deaf, already reading thunder-patterns through thickened palms.

---

The **children** emerge knowing the most important thing: how to hold stone without being held by it. The fresh precipitates are hungry for shape, for the organic residue that their bodies shed continuously into the wet air. A child left motionless too long would find mineral growth beginning at the contact points, the biofilm serving as nucleation site, the body becoming scaffold for new accretion. They learn movement before they learn feeding, a constant slight shifting, a dance of position that keeps all surfaces intermittently exposed, that prevents any single point from becoming *too* available to the chemistry of emergence.

They do not cry. The thunder would swallow such sound, but more fundamentally, they have no physiological reflex for acoustic expression. Their throats can shape air, can produce vibrations that they feel in their own chests, in their palates, but these are private experiences, unshared, unsharing. The closest they come to vocal community is the *hummed vibration*, a low frequency conducted through stone when many press close in the thunder-quiet nodes, a felt rather than heard chorus that serves some of the functions of lullaby, of protest, of ritual acknowledgment.

---

The **adolescents** are the explorers, if exploration has meaning in a world without edges. The city's growth, the slow extension of emergent geology, creates new volumes faster than the established population can occupy—and "faster" itself is tentative, a comparative without absolute measure. Young bodies, still flexible in their mineral-biological interface, venture into the fresh formations, test the youth of stone with their still-sensitive palms, find the paths where thunder nodes cancel sufficiently for extended touch-communication. They are the scouts of position, the cartographers of the instantaneous, mapping not change but *reachable configuration*, the momentary stable arrangement of climbable pillars, of traversable platforms, of spaces where the accretions have not yet grown too sharp, too brittle, too *new*.

This is their danger, their romance, their mortality. The young stone does not discriminate between organic and mineral opportunity; it grows through any surface that holds still, that offers the biofilm, the moisture, the complex chemistry of biological persistence. Adolescents vanish sometimes, found later as architecture, as strange incorporation, their bodies scaffold for especially complex accretions that the Muffled come to treat with reverence and avoidance—the **hardened**, the ones who became structure too young, whose exploring stilled at the wrong instant.

---

The **adults**, the established, the ones whose movement has become sufficiently patterned to be called *occupation*, perform the maintenance of density. They are the rope-makers, the platform-lashers, the carvers of soft stone, the processors of organic residue into edible precipitate. Their hands know the precise moment when volcanic glass cools to usable edge, when pillar-stone passes from carvable to irreversible, when a fresh accretion has grown too brittle to touch but still too young to safely ignore. They trade in *position-knowledge*, the accumulated tactile cartography of the city's vertical volume, passed hand to hand through the stone-mediated communication that replaces speech.

Adult sociality is continuous, unavoidable, *suffocating in its sufficiency*. They sleep in pressed masses, the minimum safe distance from stone observed but not from each other, body heat their only temperature regulation in the unchanging warmth. They eat in contact, passing the soft mineral nodules directly from mouth to mouth or more commonly from hand to mouth to hand, the biofilm serving as partial digestive pre-processing, the boundary between self and sustenance as eroded as every other boundary in Hush. They eliminate where they are, the waste immediately available to the precipitation, to the continuous chemical emergence, their bodies contributing without ceremony or disgust to the mineral-biological cycling that recognizes no category of "waste."

Their conflicts are positional, territorial in the most immediate spatial sense. Disputes arise over access to young stone, to thunder-quiet nodes, to the rare platforms where multiple bodies can gather without the crowding that becomes, in certain acoustic configurations, actively painful. Resolution comes not through violence—the density prevents the extension violence requires—but through *repositioning*, through the slow negotiation of who presses where, who shifts to new volume, who accepts the incorporation into accretion-risk that someone else refuses.

---

The **aged**, if age is visible here at all, are those whose movement has become too patterned, whose biofilm has thickened to mineral-symptomatic crust, whose palms have lost sensitivity to thunder-pattern, to stone-youth, to the subtle vibration-communication that maintains social coherence. They do not die in the familiar sense; they *stabilize*, become too still for safety, begin the incorporation they have spent existence avoiding. The other Muffled recognize this transformation early, treat the stabilizing with a combination of care and preemptive mourning, feeding them, shifting them, maintaining their minimal motion as long as possible.

But eventually—eventually, which means eventually in position, eventually in accumulated contact-hours, eventually in the biological clock that Hush does not stop but does not acknowledge—the stabilization completes. The body becomes scaffold, becomes *hardened*, becomes architecture. The other Muffled do not remove these incorporations; they integrate them, build against them, carve them when still carvable, occupy the cavities that form around their mineralized shapes. The dead are the city's growth medium, its organic residue source, its justification for continued emergence. Every pillar, every platform, every ladder-descent carries within it the maintained positions, the final pressed configurations, of those who stabilized before.

---

And the **strange ones**, the ones who do not fit the parameter of normal, who read as low-weird only in aggregate, individually *something else*—they exist, they must exist, in any population this dense, this pressed, this continuous.

There are the **listeners**, the ones born with insufficiently scarred eardrums, who retain some sensitivity to the thunder's pressure if not to its frequency. They suffer; the continuous acoustic weight is pain to them, is *information overload* in a system that cannot process what it receives. Some stabilize young, seeking the relief of incorporation. Others find peculiar niches, thunder-cancellation points so precise that they can function, can offer a service to the rest: the location and maintenance of communication-nodes, the cartography of acoustic safe-space. They are valued, these listeners, and pitied, and slightly feared for their access to experience the rest cannot share.

There are the **dry ones**, whose biofilm fails, whose integument somehow repels rather than merges with the atmospheric weeping. They are cold, perpetually, the warmth of Hush insufficient without the participating moisture. They press more desperately than others, seeking thermal contact, seeking the friction that substitutes for biofilm function. They live briefly, usually, stabilizing quickly, their bodies reluctant precipitate, eager scaffold.

There are the **mineral-eaters**, who consume not the processed organic residue but the fresh accretions themselves, the raw precipitate, the stone-in-formation. Their digestive systems manage, somehow, this mineral diet; their bodies incorporate the emerging chemistry directly. They grow heavy, slow, their own biology shifting toward the accretion they consume. Some become, in time, indistinguishable from the stone they ate, walking mineralizations, stabilized while still mobile, ambivalent category, revered and shunned.

There are the **edge-believers**, the ones who take literally the stories of Hush's termination, who dedicate their positioned existence to pursuit of gradient, of change, of the diminishment of thunder or the failure of amber or any of the terminal phenomena the stories describe. They move always in one direction, as far as the unchanging dimensionlessness allows, pressing through fresh formation after fresh formation, never staying, never building, never incorporating into the city's density. Some vanish, presumably stabilized in remote emergence. Some return, eventually, their directional obsession spent, their bodies marked with travel-wear, with mineral scars from stone too new or too old for safe passage. They bring no news, if news requires novelty; they bring only the confirmation of extension, of sameness, of Hush's unending present.

---

What lives in Hush lives *as* Hush, the distinction between inhabitant and inhabited never resolving, never needing resolution. The Muffled are not adapted to their world; they are *precipitated* with it, co-emergent, the biological and mineralogical sharing a chemistry that does not discriminate. Their urbanism is not imposed on geology but *grown with it*, the same processes of accretion, competition, stabilization, replacement operating at all scales from the microbial film to the vertical city to the extending declivity itself.

They do not wonder at their condition. Wonder requires contrast, requires the experience of alternative; Hush offers none, has never offered none, will not offer none. Their stories of edge, of termination, of something else—these function not as desire but as *structural necessity*, the negative space that lets the positive density cohere, the imagined silence that makes the continuous thunder bearable by giving it something to not-be.

They are there now, if now is the word. Pressed together in the amber insufficient dimness. Moving slightly, constantly, to prevent incorporation. Passing hand to hand the mineral nutrition, the position-knowledge, the accumulated tactile cartography of emergence. Feeling through thickened palms the thunder's fixed yet positionally variable pressure. Becoming stone, eventually, always, their final stillness the city's growth, their mineralized forms the architecture of others' continued slight motion.

They do not whisper the world's name, Hush. They have never heard it, will never hear it, would not recognize the acoustic shape if thunder permitted its audition. But they know it, in the vibration through stone, in the shared pressure of continuous contact, in the biofilm's merging with atmospheric weeping, in the complete participation in a world that has not decided what it wants to be and that holds them, warm and wet and deafening and dense, in the extended suspension of that decision.
