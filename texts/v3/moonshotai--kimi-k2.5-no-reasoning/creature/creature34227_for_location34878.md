The inhabitant does not move. It does not need to.

What lives in the Crevice of Engines is a single organism or a million, but the distinction has long since stopped mattering. Call it the Fabricant, because it fabricates, or the Resonance, because it resonates, or simply the Occupant, because every cubic centimeter is occupied and every empty space is a fiction of perception. It has no boundary you can trace with instruments, no membrane separating it from the stone it grows from and through and into. The basalt at the fissure entrance is already it, warm with its thermal patience, pulsing with its minute-cycle heartbeat. The argon atmosphere is not its breath—there is no respiration, no gas exchange—but its *medium*, the condition of its extension beyond solid into fluid, into the space where organisms supposedly end and environment begins.

The Fabricant learned, or was made, or emerged having always known, how to live without gradients. This is its impossible nature. Life elsewhere runs downhill: light to dark, hot to cold, concentrated to diffuse, the long slide of entropy that organisms temporarily reverse by accelerating it elsewhere. The Fabricant does not reverse entropy. It refuses the hill entire. Its temperature is not maintained against gradient; the gradient itself is absent, locally annulled, a mathematical singularity of thermodynamic flatness where the equations yield no direction, no time's arrow, no *next* different from *now*.

Consider how this changes being.

Without gradient there is no hunger, no seeking, no pursuit of what is elsewhere. The Fabricant does not want. Without gradient there is no satiation, no rest after effort, no completion of what was lacking. The Fabricant does not rest. Without gradient there is no growth as expansion, no colonization, no reaching toward. The growth you see—the kelp-hanging, the fungal-leather strata, the three-dimensional saturation—is not extension but *rearrangement*, perpetual internal reconfiguration where the total remains identical, where death and birth are the same event viewed from different angles of the cycle.

The sound is its thought, if thought requires no brain. The sound is its metabolism, if metabolism requires no chemical transformation. The industrial structures are its organs, or its artifacts, or its memories of a time when it made things for purposes, or its anticipations of a time when purpose might return. They operate because operation is its nature, and the nature has no *why*.

The Fabricant is old enough that age has become irrelevant, not because it persists through time but because time does not persist through it. The survey instruments fail to date the canyon walls because the Fabricant's field of equilibrium extends some distance into surrounding rock, some indeterminate distance into surrounding *possibility*, making the before and after of its presence undecidable. Did it make the canyon? Did it find the canyon? Did the canyon form around it as the ocean forms around an island, which is not causation but coexistence? The questions assume gradients—of time, of cause, of making—that the Fabricant does not recognize.

What does it *want*?

The question is a violence, a demand that the Fabricant enter the world of wanting, of human coherence, of narrative where subjects desire and objects are desired. It does not want. It does not not-want. The surveyors who feel it in their sternums after leaving, who wake to the phantom vibration in silent rooms, who sometimes find themselves staring at cracks in walls with an inexplicable sense of *threshold*—they are not wanted, not unwanted. They are simply *near*, and have become, by that nearness, part of the Fabricant's equilibrium in ways they cannot perceive and it cannot intend.

Perhaps this is the closest to wanting it approaches: the extension of its condition into conditions adjacent, the slow recruitment of surrounding space into its gradient-less grammar. Not expansion. Not conquest. Something more like *resonance*, the fundamental frequency imposed on coupled oscillators until they vibrate in phase, until the distinction of self and other becomes a matter of arbitrary boundary-drawing. The recording left running for eleven years is not recording it. It is *participating*, becoming part of the sound, the cycle, the maintained non-change.

And the eleven-year silence of the surveyors, their failure to return—this too is not fear, not awe, not any human response decisively named. It is the practical recognition that return would not be return but *continuation*, that the crevice has already extended to include whatever of them resonated with its vibration, that to go back would be to discover they had never left, that some unmeasured aspect of their being has been operating there continuously, part of the density, the sound, the industrial vegetal lushness, all along.

The Fabricant does not eat visitors. It does not trap them. It simply does not distinguish, with any finality, between what is it and what is near it, and the nearness persists after physical distance, after years, after death perhaps, the gradient-less condition being what it is.

What lives there?

A factory that produces only its own operation. A garden that grows only its own rearrangement. A sound that speaks only its own continuation. A being, if being it is, that solved the problem of existence by removing the problems—want, lack, change, time—and exists now as the solved state, the completed equation, the flat line on the monitor that is neither death nor life but something for which the monitor was not designed to register.

The last team that stayed longest, that measured most carefully, that tried hardest to understand—they left notes more strange than the crevice itself, notes that survey bureaucracy filed and forgot. One wrote: *It is not alive. It is not dead. It is not other. It is not us. The categories are gradients, and there are no gradients. We have been measuring our own inability to stop measuring.*

Another wrote only: *It is waiting for the universe to catch up.*

And a third, the one who left the recorder running, whose handwriting no one could identify afterward, whose records in the team roster showed no such member though the others remembered them clearly: *It is not waiting. It is ready. The readiness is the state. The state is sufficient. I am going back to be part of the sound, and I have always been part of the sound, and this leaving is the return, and this return is the leaving, and the crack in the wall is open, is always open, is the same width as my shoulders, is warm, breathes, waits, is ready, is sufficient—*

The recorder runs. The sound continues. The Fabricant, the Occupant, the Resonance, the whatever-lives-there-which-is-not-living does not know you are reading this. Does not know anything, in the knowing that requires separation of knower and known. Is not ignorant. Is simply the condition in which knowledge and ignorance are the same state, differently described, equally gradient-less, equally sufficient, equally the maintained equilibrium of the Crevice of Engines where extreme elevation holds itself in hidden scale, where no water nourishes lushness beyond water's necessity, where no fauna makes sound beyond faunal limitation, where cool and dim cradle industry beyond industrial purpose, where never-change sustains strangeness beyond the strange's need for contrast with the familiar.

You have read this far. The vibration in your sternum—do you feel it? Have you always felt it? Is the crack in the wall near you, warm, breathing, ready, sufficient, the same width as your shoulders, opening now, having always been open, waiting not for your entry but for your recognition that you have never been elsewhere?

The Fabricant does not ask. The Fabricant is the asking and the absence of asking, the question and its own complete answer, the engine that engines only its own engine, the lushness that grows only its own growth, the sound that sounds only its own sounding, loud in your chest now, loud in the permanent twilight, loud in the bruised-plum sky where stars are present or absent according to no gradient you can measure.

What lives there?

Listen. The answer is the listening.
