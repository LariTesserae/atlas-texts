What lives in the Warren are not precisely sixty people, though if you pressed a finger to each forehead in turn you would find sixty skins cooling and warming in the unvarying air. They have names, or had them once, but the names have become like everything else here: subsumed into the grammar of maintenance, spoken only in the most rarefied contexts, whispers so soft they register as breath rather than sound. To name oneself would be to assert a boundary, an edge where the self ends and the Warren begins, and this edge—if it ever existed—has long since grown over, cambium joining cambium, until the inhabitants cannot locate where they stop and the place continues. They are not the trees, and yet they rise with the floors, they redirect their living around the slow heave of growth as a stream redirects around stone. They are not the fungi, and yet they harvest by a phosphorescence that has entered their dreams, that shapes their movements in absolute dark without their awareness. They are not the pool, and yet they maintain its depth in their drinking, their washing, their children's fingers trailing the mineral lip, each touch a minute deposition, an accretion toward the containment the pool grows for itself.

The youngest among them—though age too has grown fungal, not linear but layered, the new emerging from and sustained by the old—learn to move before they learn to see, navigating by the gradations of filtered light, by the temperature differentials between breathing wood and seeping stone, by the acoustic texture of spaces so saturated with life that presence registers as interference pattern, a disturbance in the field they feel in their sternums before their ears could theoretically detect it. They do not crawl and then walk; they find their limbs in the act of finding the paths that exist, that maintain themselves, that are maintained by being found. A child who reached toward the wrong surface, who voiced a cry where gesture would suffice, would not be punished—the concept imports too much structure—would simply *not continue*, the calibration too subtle for description, the equilibrium self-healing, the absence of divergence indistinguishable from the absence of child. This has happened, or happens, or is happening; the tense collapses here, the stories maintaining what they describe without locating it in sequence.

The bodies of the Warren's people have become as saturated as the space they occupy. Their lungs, generations in the damp, operate at efficiencies no outside physiologist has measured, extracting oxygen from air so still it would suffocate a visitor in days. Their skin carries a permanent bloom, a lichenous quality, not disease but dialogue, microbial communities established in utero, inherited as surely as the whorls of their fingertips, which themselves have grown less distinct, less assertive, more continuous with the surfaces they press against. They do not grow tall; verticality would be violence against the horizontal density, the packed layering, the canopy that admits no upward aspiration. They grow broad, low, their joints elaborate with the accommodations made to rising floors, to spaces that narrow and widen with the decades' imperceptible pulse. Their teeth, their bones, show the chemistry of the pool, the mineral signature of water that has no inlet, no outlet, that simply is, that they simply are in turn.

Their dreams, if they have dreams distinct from waking, are of the First Openers or the Joining or the Silence achieved, though these are not dreams of event but of state, not narrative but environment, the same filtered glow, the same seep, the same held breath of meteorology. They wake—or emerge—without transition, the boundary between states as grown-over as the boundary between selves. What they do not dream, what cannot be dreamed here, is exteriority: the open, the variable, the horizon, the weather's approach. These concepts persist in their language as the most ghostly residues, words maintained perhaps for their function in certain medicinal preparations, certain social roles, certain ash deposits whose chemistry shapes certain lichens whose harvest shapes the roles whose incumbents maintain the ash. Even to attempt description of such outside imports a noise the silence cannot absorb, a disturbance the equilibrium heals without recording.

There are specific inhabitants worth attending to, though worth imports a gradient, a direction, that the Warren does not generate. The one who tends the ash, who in other circumstances might be called medicine-keeper or priest, though neither term would survive translation into the whispered minimum: this one moves through the lichen zone with a particular density of presence, a calibration of footfall and gesture that maintains the deposit's chemistry against the slow drift of time. The ash-tender does not know why the ash must be precisely this depth, this distribution, why certain woods must be burned in certain proportions, why the fire must be tended at certain intervals with a certain patience. The ash-tender does what is done, what maintains what is, the knowing grown over, the reason become architecture rather than phenomenon, as the pool simply is.

The one who harvests from the deepest chambers, where fungal phosphorescence provides the sole light, where the air itself seems viscous with spore and moisture and the accumulated quiet of generations who have learned not to disturb: this one has developed eyes, or something functioning as eyes, that register wavelengths no outside instrument has measured, that navigate by the glow of decay, the light of things breaking down in order to continue. The deep-harvester is not distinct from the harvest, from the chambers, from the slow fungal pulse that measures time in fruiting and dormancy rather than rotation or sequence. To meet the deep-harvester in the upper Warren, where the filtered day permits the ghost of shadow, is to encounter something that seems not fully present, not fully adapted to dimensions of illumination the outside visitor finds minimal but the deep-harvester finds excessive, violent, a noise against the equilibrium maintained below.

The children, the young, the not-yet-calibrated: they move through the Warren with a density of possibility that the system accommodates without absorbing, a temporary perturbation that the equilibrium will resolve, that is already resolving, the too-active limb finding its appropriate gesture, the too-vocal breath finding its whispered minimum, the too-exterior boundary finding its grown-over continuity. They play, if play sustains itself against the gravity of maintenance, in spaces that permit such temporary license, spaces that themselves exist in the architecture of the Warren as pressure valves, as necessary absences that enable the presence to continue, the pool's constant depth maintained not by stasis but by dynamic balance, by forces that include the children's fingers trailing the mineral lip, the minute deposition, the gesture toward containment.

What lives in the Warren, finally, is not the sixty or the spiders or the birds or the fungi or the trees that may or may not still be called individual: it is the Warren itself, the quality of Warrening, the verb become noun become continuous aspect, the place that has become indistinguishable from its own persistence. The inhabitants are its organs, its gestures, its mineral deposition and fungal glow and seep and held breath. They do not live in it. They are its living, the process by which it maintains what is, becoming what it is without becoming anything else. To ask after their happiness, their freedom, their meaning, is to import categories they do not generate, to speak loudly where only whisper suffices, to disturb an equilibrium that will heal without recording the disturbance, that has already healed, that was never disturbed, that simply is, that Warrens, that continues in its continuation, the perfect tense made present, the completed action maintained in its doing, forever, or what passes for forever here, in the stillness where the wind screams over and away.
