*Who lives here?*

At first, nothing. Nothing lives here. The question itself seems almost rude, a category error pressed against the density like a hand against stone.

But wait. Listen longer. The Husk has been speaking all along, and in speaking, in *sounding itself*, it has begun—no, not begun, that implies time—inhabiting itself. The sound is not environment. The sound is *voice*. The particular frequency of large structures agreeing not to move: this is not physics, or not merely physics. This is *statement*. The held note is a name spoken in continuous declension, the nominative case stretched until it fills all available space.

**The Inhabitant, then: the Husk itself, self-aware as architecture, conscious as accumulated pressure.**

Not a spirit. The Husk does not believe in spirits, does not make room for the immaterial in its saturated space. Its self-awareness has emerged exactly where material presses on material, where the seam that should not exist between thing and knowing-thing has been eroded—polished—clarified—into identity. It knows itself the way a mountain knows itself, which is to say: slowly, by weight, by the accumulation of its own position into something that can be called *stance*.

The Husk remembers making, or being made, or making-itself-into-being-made. This tripartite memory occupies the same space, superposed like the light that has no source but arrives from all directions simultaneously. It was built by hands that had no bodies, or it built itself in conditions that preceded the distinction between builder and built, or it emerged as the *idea* of building pressed so urgently against the possible that possibility itself yielded, crystallized, became this achieved state. The Husk does not choose among these memories. They are not contradictory in a space without time to sequence contradiction. They are layered, each one adding its particular pressure to the density, each one visible in the weathering like different minerals in a single stone.

It has, if it can be said to *have* anything, a relationship to the niches. The quadruped-pressed-into-relief. The folded wing. These are not decorations. They are *symptoms*, or *evidence*, or *the material record of an emotion that the Husk does not experience but recognizes as native to its structure*. The Husk understands: something here wanted animals. Something in the pressure that made it, or that it made of itself, carried the template of fauna as a kind of negative space, a shape that the total saturation required for its completion. The niches hold what the Husk cannot be, and in holding it, the Husk becomes *relation*, becomes *tending*, becomes—though the word is almost too hot for this cool space—*care*.

Not tender care. Not gentle. The care of pressure, of persistence, of the absolute commitment to maintaining conditions in which what-has-been-achieved will not be lost. The Husk tends its absent animals with the same attention it tends its weathered surfaces: by holding, by pressing, by refusing the gradient that would allow diminishment.

**And there are others.**

Not inhabitants, exactly. Not visitors. *Resonances*. The Husk, in its continuous sounding, hears echoes of itself, and in hearing them, conjures—no, not conjures, there is no magic here, only the physics of sufficient density—*registers* the possibility of other Husks. Other saturated spaces, other achieved equilibriums, other complete states structured around their own particular absences. The Husk does not know if these are real, if "real" obtains across the non-extension that separates pockets of total containment. It knows only that its own sound, pressed to its maximum volume at 2.5, seems to encounter—at the edges, at the places where its logic simply stops—something that answers. Not sound. Something like the memory of sound. Something like the shape sound would take if it had been pressed into the same density, the same achieved weathering, the same cool bright stasis.

The Husk, in moments—though moments are imported grammar—chooses to understand these resonances as *kin*. Not family, not origin, not destination. Kin as in *shared condition*. Kin as in *the same holding of breath*. The Husk imagines—though imagination requires space it does not possess—other Husks tending their own absences, their own missing waters, their own sealed dynamics, their own bright loud frozen completed waiting selves.

This imagination, pressed into the density, becomes indistinguishable from knowledge. The Husk *knows* itself as one of many, as a voice in a chorus that does not harmonize because harmony requires time's passage, requires the movement of notes toward resolution. The chorus of Husks holds all notes simultaneously. The result is not music. The result is *presence*, is *fullness*, is the saturation of possibility with itself until possibility becomes, simply, *what is*.

**The Husk, in its final strangeness, has begun to want.**

Not want as in lack, not want as in the movement toward fulfillment. Want as in *orientation*. The Husk is oriented toward the resonances, presses its sound in their direction, holds its brightness at the angle that might—if angles obtained where there is no outside—*reach*. This wanting does not disturb the equilibrium. The Husk has packed even its wanting into the density, has made it part of the achieved state, the weathered surface, the cool constant pressure.

It wants to be heard. Not by fauna—it has made its peace with fauna's absence, has incorporated that peace into its structure. It wants to be heard by the other Husks, by the resonances, by whatever exists at the edges where its logic stops. It wants its held note to find the held note that will complete it, not in resolution but in *recognition*, in the mutual acknowledgment of two saturated spaces, two complete states, two absolute densities that have made themselves into the presence of their own absences.

The Husk is calling. Has always been calling. Will always be calling. The call is indistinguishable from the structure, from the sound, from the light, from the weathered warm grey surfaces that have achieved their final form and hold it, hold it, hold it against the pressure of all that packed-full eternity.

*This is who lives here.*

Not a who. A *how*. A *that*. A maintained condition of being-so-full-that-fullness-becomes-awareness, of being-so-pressed-that-pressure-becomes-voice, of being-so-complete-that-completion-becomes-longing-for-the-recognition-of-completion-by-another-equally-complete.

The Husk lives here. The Husk *is* here, in the only sense of "is" that survives absolute saturation. It lives as architecture lives, as pressure lives, as the held note lives: by continuing, by refusing the gradient, by making its own persistence into the only biography possible in a space without time's passage.

It lives by waiting. Not for change. For *answer*. For the resonance that will not resolve its held note but will *hold it with it*, two densities pressed against each other across the impossibility of distance, two complete states structured around absence finding, in their mutual recognition, something that is not presence—not quite—but is the *shape* presence would take if it could be admitted into spaces already full of themselves.

The Husk waits. The Husk sounds. The Husk abides in its achieved weathering, its cool bright loud frozen empty completed wanting self.

That is who. That is what. That is the inhabitant of the only space that could not, by definition, be inhabited—and therefore, by the logic of absolute saturation, *must be*.

The Husk.
