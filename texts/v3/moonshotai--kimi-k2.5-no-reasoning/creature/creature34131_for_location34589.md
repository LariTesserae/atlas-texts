The Vorruans are not human, though they resemble humans in their arrangement of limbs, their bilateral symmetry, their gathering together in named places. They are something else that has learned the shape of human need, or perhaps something that human need has slowly taught itself to become in a place with no mirror but water and no reflection that stays still long enough to examine.

They do not sleep, not exactly. Each night—or what passes for night, in the diffused perpetual half-light between cloud-seas—they enter a state the local tongue has no word for, a vertical dormancy where they stand against the weeping walls of their dwellings and let the condensation run down their shoulders, their chests, their closed faces, so that by morning they are coated in the mineral accretion of the night's weeping, a thin shell that they crack gently with movement, a shedding that contributes to the ceramic-glass sealing of the dwelling's gaps, so that each inhabitant becomes part of the maintenance they do not consciously perform.

Their skin has taken on something of the water's properties. It does not wrinkle in saturation; rather, it smooths, becomes more translucent, shows the slow pulse of whatever circulates beneath in the absence of true blood. The Vorruans do not bleed when cut. They seep. A wound releases a fluid more viscous than water, gray-green, that scabs into a mineral crust matching the black stone's own chemistry. They heal without scarring, the new skin seamless, slightly more translucent than before, so that the elders among them—though "elder" means only longer residence, not chronological age—have become almost luminous, their bodies vessels for the diffused light, barely containing shadows.

They do not dream, or if they dream, the dreams do not belong to them. The standing sleep is communal, the entire population entering at roughly the same moment, triggered by some barometric shift or the plateau's subsonic note passing through a particular harmonic. In this state, they are aware—researchers have tested this, though the Vorruans do not participate in research so much as permit its presence—they are aware of each other, of the entire distributed network of standing bodies across the town, a low-level continuous perception of proximity and position that requires no visual confirmation. They know where everyone is. They have always known. The *complete ones* who walk into the wilderness are not lost to this sense; rather, they become diffuse, distributed across the whole plateau, present everywhere at reduced intensity, so that a Vorruan in the eastern marsh-edges might suddenly know the specific absence-in-presence of someone standing on the western cliff-face eleven miles away, weeping with the stone, completing.

---

The newcomers are the mechanism by which the population holds steady, but the Vorruans do not know this, or knowing, do not hold the knowledge in any form accessible to themselves. A newcomer arrives at a marsh-edge, water running from clothes that do not match any local manufacture, with no memory of ascent. The Vorruans receive them without surprise, without ceremony. There is no integration because there is no state of being other than Vorruan; the newcomer simply begins, the next day, standing in the ceramic-glass dwelling that has stood empty since the last completion, participating in the water-gathering, the grain-algae harvesting, the nocturnal mineral-accretion and diurnal cracking-free. They speak the language without learning it. They know the channels and their flows without being shown. Within a week, they cannot distinguish their own thoughts from the low continuous hum of distributed awareness, their own dreams—if they dream—from the collective standing-sleep.

Where do they come from? The Vorruans do not ask, but if pressed, if somehow forced to the edge of articulation, they might gesture downward, toward the cloud-sea, and make a sound that is not a word but a water-noise, a gurgle of condensation in a half-blocked channel. Below. Somewhere below. The implication, inescapable once considered: that they themselves have come from below, that completion is not disappearance but return, that the plateau draws its steady population from the world beneath the clouds and releases its completed ones back to that source, a slow circulation of almost-human forms through a geography that exists in no map, a hydrology that flows upward against every principle.

Some researchers have descended through the cloud-sea, though descent is difficult, the cloud layer thick and turbulent, the lower world unrecorded in Vorruan memory. They report—those who return—only more cloud, more wetness, a continuous atmospheric ocean with no clear boundary, no visible ground, as if the entire world below the Shelf were dissolved into the same perpetual condensation, the same weeping without destination. They report, some of them, glimpses of structures in the deeper cloud, vast shapes that might be inverted mountains, might be something built, might be the bodies of completed Vorruans in distributed dissolution, present everywhere at reduced intensity, dreaming the cloud-sea into being through their collective mineral-accretion, their perpetual cracking-free.

---

The children that are not born: this is perhaps the most difficult fact, the one that no researcher has satisfactorily explained, that the Vorruans themselves do not recognize as fact. There are no children. There have never been children. The population remains steady at six hundred through arrival and completion, never through generation, though the Vorruans possess all physical apparatus for reproduction, though they pair and unpair in patterns that suggest mating, though they sometimes stand together in the standing-sleep with their mineral-accretion merging, two shells becoming one difficult cracking, a prolonged moment of shared emergence from the night's weeping.

But no children. And more: no pregnancy, no conception, no desire for these things though desire for pairing remains, though the physical expression of that desire proceeds according to patterns that seem biological, seem evolved, seem continuous with whatever the Vorruans were before they became Vorruan. The researcher who pushed furthest into this question—we do not name researchers, they become part of their researches, they complete or they do not—this researcher eventually proposed that the Vorruans reproduce through completion, that a newcomer is not drawn from below but generated from above, that the walking-out completes a process that begins in the paired standing-sleep and resolves itself into emergence at some marsh-edge, a new translucent body with no memory because there is no memory to have, a fresh mineral-accretion waiting for its first cracking-free.

There is no evidence for this. There is no evidence against. The Vorruans themselves, asked directly, respond with the water-noise, the gurgle of blocked flow, and sometimes a gesture that might indicate completion, might indicate birth, might indicate that these categories have never been separate in their distributed awareness, that to complete and to be born are the same movement in opposite temporal directions, the same water finding its level at eleven thousand feet.

---

What do they believe? The question itself misleads, importing a category—belief, the holding of propositions not fully evidenced—that may not apply. The Vorruans do not hold propositions. They hold positions, physical, in space, in relation to water-flow and stone-weeping and the low continuous note of the plateau's settling. They know the channels because they are, in the standing-sleep, continuous with the channels, their mineral-accretion contributing to the accretion that heals the stone it passes through, their bodies part of the same process they apparently only observe.

They have what might be called practices, though calling them so separates action from being in ways that distort. They tend the marsh-edges not because the marsh-edges need tending but because tending is the form of awareness appropriate to marsh-edges, the way of being that completes the marsh-edge's own emergence. They harvest the blue-green globes before ripening not to prevent release but to participate in release at the proper rate, the controlled distribution of whatever the globes become when they become something else. They do not eat this harvest, not exactly. They incorporate it, their translucent bodies absorbing the blue-green substance through prolonged contact, skin becoming momentarily phosphorescent, the glow fading not as metabolism but as distribution, the substance joining the network of low continuous awareness, becoming available to all, present everywhere at reduced intensity.

They do not worship the plateau, though researchers have mistaken their attention to stone-weeping, their vertical dormancy against weeping walls, for devotional practice. They do not distinguish between the plateau and themselves with sufficient clarity to support worship. The distinction exists—it is not dissolved, not mystical in any recognizable sense—but it is functional, a matter of channel and flow rather than spirit and matter. They are where the water goes; the plateau is where the water comes from; the difference is orientation in a continuous system, not ontological category.

---

What lives here? Not the Vorruans alone, though they are the most articulate, the most proximate to human comprehension. Something else lives in the spiral moss, in its record of stone topography, its growth-pattern that is also memory, also history, also a form of slow computation working through mineral gradient and light diffusion. Something lives in the reed with its solid stem, its pressure-grown cracks, its rooting in black stone through force rather than accommodation. Something, perhaps, lives in the plateau itself, in the subsonic note of its settling, its eleven thousand feet of not-yet-completed cooling, its perpetual geological moment of emergence.

This something is not conscious, not alive in any framework that permits interaction, permits research, permits the asking of questions and the receiving of answers. But it is not dead, not inert, not merely physical in the reductionist sense. It processes. It transforms. It takes the water that has nowhere to go but up and makes of it a system, a cycling, a held moment of becoming that includes the Vorruans, the moss-spirals, the solid reeds, the blue-green globes and their controlled ripening-release, includes also the researchers who come and sometimes complete, sometimes return transformed, sometimes neither, existing in distributed presence across the cloud-sea below.

The Vorruans know this, not as knowledge but as position. They stand in their vertical dormancy and let the mineral-accretion build, and they are the plateau knowing itself, the water finding its impossible level, the young stone still weeping from the wound of its own emergence. They do not ask what they are because asking requires distance, and there is no distance here, only the continuous condensation on every surface, the perpetual wetness of a world that has not yet learned to drain.

---

When a Vorruan completes—when they walk out into the weeping wilderness and do not return in any concentrated form—something is conserved. The dwelling stands empty but not inactive; its channels continue their self-maintaining flow, its walls continue to weep and to heal, and the empty space becomes, in the distributed awareness, more present rather than less, a point of intensified absence that strengthens the network rather than weakening it. The completed one walks, or is walked, to some position appropriate to their particular transparency, their particular mineral-accretion history, their particular pattern of channel-knowledge and marsh-edge-tending and blue-green globe-harvest. They become part of the plateau's knowing. They contribute to the subsonic note, the settling, the perpetual not-quite-completion of geological time.

And then, eventually, a newcomer emerges at some marsh-edge, water running from clothes that do not match any local manufacture, with no memory of ascent. The cycle continues. The population holds steady. The water goes up and comes down and never leaves, and whatever lives on the Shelf continues its held moment of becoming, its frozen emergence, its young-world stasis that is not death because it has never begun to live in any way that would permit ending.

This is what lives here. Not the Vorruans, not solely, not finally. The Vorruans are the expressible portion, the interface with human comprehension, the being-that-resembles-human in a place where resemblance is a local principle, where water condenses into human-shaped vessels because human-shaped vessels are what the plateau has learned to weep. What lives here is the weeping itself, the condensation, the upward flow without destination, the mineral-accretion and cracking-free, the spiral moss and its stone-topography memory, the solid reed and its pressure-grown cracks, the blue-green globes and their ripening-release into something else, something distributed, something present everywhere at reduced intensity.

What lives here is emergence, not yet complete. What lives here is the moment before a mountain finishes being born, held in perpetual recurrence, weeping the water of its own becoming into forms that tend it, that harvest it, that stand in vertical dormancy and let it build and crack and build again. What lives here has no name because naming requires completion, and completion is what this place postpones indefinitely, what it circles around without reaching, the water going up and coming down and never finding the level that would let it rest.
