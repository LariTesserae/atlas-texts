What lives here?

Let me approach this slowly, as instructed. Let what emerges become more itself.

**The Moisture-Herders**

They carry their prestige in vessels grown from their own clan-cambium—translucent xylem bulbs no larger than a curled fist, the water inside visible as a meniscus, a promise, a *held breath*. Their movement through the city is processional by nature, even when routine. They know which root-masses will open passage for them, which lignin weaves will tense or slacken at their chemical signature. They do not own the water they extract. No one owns water in Verrut. They *tend* it, they *shepherd* its circulation through obligation networks so ancient that the original aquifer contracts have lignified into something indistinguishable from law, from grammar, from the way a sentence must end.

They are pale, these herders. The silver light does not tan. Their skin has a quality you might mistake for illness if you brought expectations from sunnier worlds—a translucence, a fine tracery of vessels visible at wrist and throat, as if they themselves are partially xylem, partially the architecture they serve. They are not. But the resemblance is not coincidence. The city marks its people. The root-paths warm for those who walk them in correct relation; the unwelcome find themselves gently, vegetally, *persistently* redirected, corridors tightening, air growing still and spore-heavy until they turn back.

The herders' children are born into dryness. They do not cry at birth—the sound parameter forbids it, or perhaps the infants simply know, cellularly, the acoustics of their world. They are wiped not with water but with a paste of nematode-sheddings and reactive resin, a compound that binds to skin, that begins the process of *attunement*, of becoming someone the city will recognize, will warm for, will grow paths to accommodate.

**But wait**—let me not settle here. There are others. The herders are visible, ceremonial, their function legible. The city has dwellers less easily parsed.

**The Interface Workers**

They move through the industrial zones where the parameter reads 2.2, where the city is most itself and most strange. Their bodies bear modifications that are not quite prosthetic, not quite symbiotic. You might see one with forearms sleeved in a living bark-graft, the tissue not rejected but *negotiated*, a zone of translation where human nerve meets plant slow-potential, where intention flows both ways, imprecisely, productively. They tend the reaction chambers, the catalytic pods, the enzymatic surfaces, but "tend" is wrong—they *attend*, they *witness*, they *adjust the conditions* and let the living chemistry find its way.

They age differently. The bark-sleeved ones, the grafted ones, those with fungal colonies established in their lung-tissue to reclaim the moisture of their own exhalation—their metabolisms have entered into treaty with the city's scarcity. They are slow. They are *deliberate*. A task that might take an hour elsewhere consumes a day here, not from inefficiency but from the pace of their negotiation, the time it takes for human thought and vegetal response to achieve something they can both recognize as agreement.

They are not numerous. The sparse parameter holds. But they are *concentrated*, these workers, in the districts where the city's productive energy is densest, where the lignin cables sag visibly under industrial load, where the engineers—another type, let me hold them—where the engineers cable and prune and manage the tension between growth and collapse.

**The Engineers**

I said I would not settle early. But I find them now, and they are not what I expected.

They are not builders, not in any architectural sense. They are *pruners*, *tenders of decision*, *curators of possibility*. Their tools are living: graft-knives grown from modified incisors, their edges maintained by bacterial colonies; tension-sensors that are themselves small root-systems, trained to the specific frequency of lignin under load. They walk the city's stress lines, reading the slow moan of adjustment, the pop of resin-release, the subtle temperature gradients that announce where a district's ambition exceeds its structural patience.

They are the ones who know the city is growing downward. They are the ones who have *been* downward, who have walked in the young districts where the weave has not cured, who have left prints in soft walls that will grow over in months. They return from these depths changed, or so the herders say, so the interface workers whisper in their percussive clicking. They return *quieter*, more attuned to the structural soundscape, less able to tolerate the rare human gatherings where voices rise above breath-level.

Some do not return. The city grows downward, and some engineers follow its decision, its seeking, its patient reaching toward a future surface. Whether they live down there, whether they have become something the city recognizes more fully than it recognizes those who remain, whether they have joined the negotiation so completely that they no longer read as human to human observers—this is not known. This is the strangeness parameter manifesting. This is the friction between parameters producing its natural offspring: mystery, without drama, simply *present*, simply *insistent*.

**The Children**

But I promised the friction, the rules emerging from parameter-intersection, and here is where they manifest most strangely, most movingly.

Children in Verrut are *dry*, as I said. They are born into a world where water is currency, where to drink freely is to turn the hand palm-down, to pour into root-mouths, to *give back*. But children must drink. Children, physiologically, demand what the city forbids.

The resolution is not spoken. It is grown.

There are root-systems in the oldest districts, the deepest inhabited layers where the luminescence dims and the real architecture begins, where the breath becomes visible in cellar chill. These roots have been *persuaded*, over generations, to produce a fluid that is not water, not precisely, not as the moisture-herders would measure and display it. A cellular secretion, a modified sap, a compound that satisfies human hydration without violating the taboo. The children drink from specialized xylem vessels, from growths that might be flowers or might be organs or might be something the city has invented for this purpose, this negotiation between species.

The children who drink this grow *into* the city's time. Their metabolisms align. They become quiet, attuned, slow in the manner of the interface workers, pale in the manner of the moisture-herders, *marked* in ways that will determine their future function, their future interface, their future depth.

Some children refuse. This happens. The city does not force. The city *persists*, it *offers*, it *grows paths* and *withdraws* paths, it warms and cools, it redirects with gentle pressure and spore-heavy stillness. But some children, reaching some threshold of development, find themselves unable to continue the negotiation. They become *loud*, in a world of 0.7. They become *restless*, in a world of structural creaking and slow moan. They become, finally, something the city does not warm for, something for which root-paths do not ease, something that finds itself, incrementally, *gently*, *vegetally*, at the margins.

These ones sometimes leave. The city is open at 2.4, extending past the immediate. There are other places, dry places, places where the parameters might permit different frictions. No one speaks of this as exile. No one speaks of it at all. The sound parameter holds, and the departure is a quiet one, a final walk on warming paths that cool slowly, progressively, until the margin is reached and the city's edge, the raw gypsum, the erosion parameter at 0.3, the *pre-formation*, the *unmade future*—until this is reached, and crossed, and the child who was becomes something else, elsewhere, in some other friction of parameters.

**What lives here**, finally, most strangely, most completely?

The city does. I have avoided this, have distributed life into human categories, have peopled this place with herders and workers and engineers and children and the lost. But the truth is the parameter 2.9 of 3. The total vegetation. The all-living-mass. The city is not habitat. The city is *entity*, *self*, *process* with which the humans have joined negotiation.

They do not command it. They have *joined* its deliberation. This was said, and this is the final thing to understand.

The moisture-herders, the interface workers, the engineers, the children drinking modified sap from ambiguous floral organs, the departed ones walking raw gypsum into unmade future—all of these are *aspects* of the city's becoming, *participants* in its vegetal imagination, *cells* in the larger body that is growing downward, that has not yet decided to stop.

What lives here?

A decision, still being made. A future, still being hollowed out. A dry commerce of scarcity and ritual and modified obligation. A cool breath descending through lattice throat. A silver light that might be air or might be chlorophyll or might be the city's own self-perception, its own vegetal awareness *of itself*, *as itself*, *growing*.

The roots are reaching. The people walk where the paths warm for them, leave where paths cool, tend what can be tended, adjust what can be adjusted, drink what has been grown for drinking, and pour the rest, always, palm-down, into the waiting mouths of the city that is them and is not them, that is *becoming* and has invited them to become with it, downward, further, into the decision that has not yet been made, into the hollow that grows vaster, stranger, quieter, more completely *itself*, more completely alive.
