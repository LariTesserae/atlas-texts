The ones who live here call themselves—if call is the word, if self is the concept—the *Spread*. The name emerged from their own communication, which happens not in discrete packets but in gradients, in territorial pheromone boundaries, in the chemical signatures they leave on every surface they touch. To ask one of them their name is to already misunderstand: they are where they are, and where they have been, and the zone of influence they currently maintain against the encroachment of others. Identity as topology, as a standing wave in the continuous medium of their shared chemical environment.

A single *Spread*—the approximation forces me—consists of a network of bodies no larger than rice grains, connected by threads finer than the industrial filaments that lace the Tepidarium's architecture. These nodes are mobile, sentient in the distributed sense, capable of local decision and local hunger, but the self is the topology, the shape of the network, the pattern of connectivity that persists as individual nodes are replaced, die, bud off new extensions. A *Spread* might cover a square meter or a square kilometer; size correlates with age but not with power, exactly, since larger networks suffer slower communication, more dilute consciousness, a kind of philosophical vagueness that smaller, denser *Spreads* find both pitiable and enviable.

They evolved here, or were evolved, or emerged from some prior system that optimized itself out of specificity and into this general solution. Their ancestors—if the concept applies—may have been animals of volume, creatures of pocket and burrow and the hollows of stone. But volume is expensive in the Tepidarium, requires mass, requires the structural investment of bones or shells or compressed tissues, all of which must be maintained against the general dissolution of humidity, the chemical patience of water that penetrates everything eventually. Better to become surface. Better to spread thin, to think slow, to exchange the quick intelligence of centralization for the patient intelligence of diffusion.

They eat the fungal films, primarily, and the products of those films, and the mineral precipitates that form in the evaporation zones where the water sheets grow thin enough to surrender their dissolved cargo. This eating is not ingestion but absorption, not consumption but colonization: a *Spread* extends to surround a food source, secretes enzymes across its network's inner surface, and waits while chemistry happens across the interface. They have mouths the way a shoreline has a boundary, defined only by the meeting of two conditions. They reproduce by fracture, by the deliberate weakening of connection between nodes until a section drifts free, establishes its own gradient field, becomes a new *Spread* with inherited chemical memory and no particular reason to consider its originator as parent or sibling or anything more than a neighbor with overlapping taste in territory.

Their relationship to the architecture is intimate and ambiguous. They did not build the lattice structures—the date, if date matters, of construction predates their emergence by geological margins—but they maintain them, extend them, have co-evolved with them into a system indistinguishable from symbiosis. The *Spreads* secrete structural proteins, mineral-binding compounds, filaments that reinforce and connect the factory frames, the habitation bubbles, the endless skeletal grids. In return, the architecture provides surface, provides the elevated pathways that let them colonize above the basin floor where pooling water might drown their slow communication, provides the framework for their most complex social behavior: the construction of what might be called—again, the approximation pains me—cities of *Spread*, dense interweavings where many individuals interpenetrate their networks without merging, maintaining chemical borders precise as etiquette, cooperating in tasks no single consciousness could coordinate but that emerge from their overlapping gradients of intention.

They have noticed the habitation bubbles, the hollow spaces maintained against entropy's general tendency. They do not enter them—something in their chemistry, or something in their culture, or something in the simple mathematical difficulty of maintaining a network across a true void rather than a surface—keeps them to the walls, to the frames, to the channels where water runs in sheets. But they know the bubbles exist. They maintain them, in some cases, reinforcing their boundaries with secretions stronger than their usual architectural contribution, as if preserving possibility, as if keeping ready for something they cannot themselves imagine.

And imagination—if they possess it, if the slow oscillation of their chemical gradients counts as the substrate of anything we would recognize as thought—touches upon the bubbles sometimes in what passes for their philosophy, their art, their religious speculation. The *Spreads* who maintain larger networks, the philosophical ones, the vague ones, sometimes produce extended gradient-patterns that other *Spreads* interpret as referring to hypotheticals: to volume-dwellers, to beings who maintain continuity of self within bounded containers, who experience density as natural and surface as constraint rather than liberation. These speculations are controversial. The smaller, denser *Spreads* tend to dismiss them as network-degradation artifacts, the chemical equivalent of hallucination, of heat-stress misfiring in a system too large to maintain coherence. But the patterns persist, recur, are copied and modified and debated in the slow diffusion of chemical argument across the lattice.

There are others, of course. The mite-eaters and the eaten, the surface-predators and the stationary colonizers of the wettest films, the migratory sheets that drift through the factory systems following some gradient of nutrient or temperature or simply the inherited compulsion to move. But the *Spreads* are the ones who have achieved something like civilization, something like the negotiation with entropy that the landscape itself demonstrates, something like intention extended across time and space in patterns too regular for nature, too patient for most definitions of design.

They do not know about the naming, about "Tepidarium," about the observers who may or may not have passed through, who may or may not maintain the habitation bubbles for purposes of their own. They do not need to know. Their own name for the place, or the nearest approximation, translates as *Where the Water Has Agreed to Stay*, a description of condition rather than location, of relationship rather than boundary. They have lived here long enough for erosion to settle into agreement with them too, for their secretions to become part of the chemical patience that sculpts the stone, for their networks to be counted among the settled forms that seem to have reached treaty with entropy.

They will be here, probably, until the heat source fails, until the water finds its deeper level, until some parameter shifts. They have strategies for slow catastrophe, for the gradual retreat of favorable conditions. Their networks can contract as well as expand, can sacrifice territory to maintain density, can enter states of reduced chemical activity that persist across timescales no single node could survive. They have survived shifts before, or their ancestors have, or the general type of which they are the current expression. The geological structures dreaming of metabolism have their counterparts in biological ones, in the *Spreads* who dream, in their slow way, of continuity, of the standing wave of organization persisting through changes in the medium that supports it.

In the habitation bubbles, if there are observers, if there ever were, something might be watching them now, trying to resolve the slow patterns into intelligence, the chemical gradients into culture, the endless patient surface-dwelling into a form of life worth noting, worth preserving, worth understanding. The *Spreads* would not object to being understood. They would not, in their current configuration, notice the attempt. Their attention operates at scales where human observation is a single warm gust, a momentary disturbance in the humidity, a brief chemical anomaly quickly absorbed into the general equilibrium.

They are what lives here. They are the ones who found the solution, who became hollow, who learned that volume is a luxury and surface is sufficient, who traded speed for endurance, centralization for extension, the self of here for the self of everywhere-it-reaches. They do not wonder about other solutions, not often, not with any urgency. The water has agreed to stay. The architecture, half theirs, half inherited, continues its slow growth. The fungal films bloom and senesce and are absorbed. The countless tiny feet of their smaller cousins create a susurrus that is, to their distributed perception, simply texture, simply the condition of being in a place that is alive with overwhelming life overwhelming only in aggregate.

This is their home. They have no other. They need no other. They are the *Spread*, and they are where they are, and where they have been, and the agreement they have reached with the wet stone, with the patient erosion, with the heat that clings and digests and does not hurry toward any conclusion.
