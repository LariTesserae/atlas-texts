I need to sit with this place before I can speak of who lives within it. The temptation is to fix the Kel-Zorathi too quickly, to make them merely strange, merely adapted, a biological footnote to their environment. But the Carton does not produce footnotes. It produces *concentrations*.

So let me begin again, from within the noise.

---

The one you would meet first—if you survived the fall, if your lungs could process the air, if you did not retreat immediately to the thinner world—would likely be a ** harvest-weaver **, third hand already extended in greeting before the larger hands have finished their slower assessment of your threat potential. This is not rudeness. The chest-hand has its own etiquette, its own eyeless alertness to texture, temperature, tension in fiber and flesh. It will touch your clothing, your skin if permitted, reading you as you would read a face. The Kel-Zorathi phrase for "stranger" translates more precisely as "unread fiber."

They age visibly, the harvest-weavers most of all, their resin-thick skin accumulating the amber light until the oldest among them seem carved from the same substance as the tower walls, their tympanic membranes dulled to opacity, their third hand still nimble but slower, more deliberate, having learned that some weaves cannot be rushed. The young ones are darker, newer, their resin still soft enough to hold the impressions of accidental contact—they wear their bruises, their collisions with the dense growth, as temporary inscriptions that tell stories before hardening into the anonymous armor of maturity.

There is no adolescence here, not as surface humans understand it. The Kel-Zorathi are *sufficient* from emergence, fully scaled, fully manual, their chest-hands already seeking fiber to practice with. What they lack is **resonance**—the capacity to make themselves heard, to produce the brass-bellow seal-roar that carries meaning through the ambient roar. This takes years of deliberate damage, of learning to force air through structures not optimally designed for such violence, of building the calluses that protect while permitting vibration. A Kel-Zorathi who cannot make themselves heard cannot participate in public life, cannot argue, cannot court, cannot contradict. They exist in a kind of partial death, the sealed-membrane isolation that is voluntary for meditation, imposed for exile, endured here by the young until their voices bloodily arrive.

So the young cluster at the edges of resonant chambers, watching the adults shout-sing, their tympanic membranes fluttering in sympathetic vibration, learning not the melodies—the melodies are impossible to separate from the particular voice, the particular harmonic distortion of each individual's abused throat—but the *shapes of contest*, the way meaning is wrestled from noise through repetition, through variation, through the willingness to sustain a single tone until the listener's body itself becomes the resonant cavity.

---

But I am making them too uniform. Let me follow someone specific.

**The one who tends the empty zones.**

This is not a profession exactly. It is a condition, a response, a self-selection that the Carton appears—yes, I use the word deliberately—to encourage in certain individuals. Typically they are mid-aged, past the desperation of voice-acquisition, not yet entered into the final resin-opacity that confines most to tower-maintenance and memory-cord knotting. Something in them fails to *compress*, fails to tolerate the urban density even in its carefully modulated Kel-Zorathi form. They begin to wander. Not to leave—the leaving is impossible, the surface unthinkable—but to occupy the breathing spaces, the lungs of the Carton, the zones of pure growth between neighborhoods.

There they do something that looks like gardening and is not. The vegetation requires no tending; it grows from all surfaces, responds to attention, incorporates touch into future form. The zone-tenders know this. They attend anyway, but differently—*diffusely*, without object, without harvest-purpose. They let their gaze rest on the rust-gold-violet growth until it grows toward them, then shift their attention elsewhere, denying the easy intimacy of direct response. They touch and do not touch. They are present in a manner the Carton does not expect, that the city-as-organism may not know how to process.

Some go mad, of course. The thickness of time, the fullness of each minute, exhausts many. They seal their membranes and wander until their bodies founder, until they collapse into the growth that will incorporate them without ceremony, that does not distinguish the dead from the living in its constant respiration. Others achieve something else: a *listening* that is not quite prayer, not quite meditation, a discipline of sustained attention to the anomaly of relative quiet.

These ones learn to hear the predator. Or rather, they learn to hear its absence, the holding of breath, the temporary diminishment that spreads from its unseen presence. They do not fear it, these achieved listeners. They have passed through fear into something more complex—*recognition*, perhaps, or *correspondence*. They come back from the zones changed, if they come back. They speak differently in the resonant chambers, their voices carrying some echo of the silence they have courted. They are uncomfortable to be around. They are sought after, avoided, necessary.

---

There are the **dead**, too. I must speak of them, though the Kel-Zorathi do not, directly. Death here is not termination but *transformation immediate*, the body too needed, too incorporated into the system's metabolism, to permit the luxury of memorial delay. The corpse—still warm, still faintly respiring in the manner of all life here, the metabolic processes continuing for hours after what the Kel-Zorathi recognize as cessation—is anointed with the oil collected from low places, the volatile substance that is water's replacement, that burns, that medicines, that prepares. The anointing is done by those who have lost their voices, the permanently silenced, who can perform this service in the sealed-membrane quiet that permits concentration, that keeps them from contamination by the final sounds the dead may still produce. The chest-hand is the primary instrument, sensitive to the subtle changes in texture that indicate readiness, that distinguish the truly ceased from the merely *transiting*.

Then the body is opened—not by cutting, the Kel-Zorathi do not cut living or recently-living tissue—and placed where the vegetation is densest, where the growth will most quickly incorporate, where the respiration of the plants will carry the volatilized remains into the amber air, adding to the luminescence. There is no grave. There is only the temporary darkening of a patch of growth, the brief intensification of its color, the memory of that intensification in those who pass that spot thereafter, who may touch it, who may find their own growth responding differently, incorporating this contact.

The dead become indistinguishable, but they do not become *absent*. This is the Kel-Zorathi understanding, though they would not phrase it thus. The Carton remembers through its growth, through its response to touch and attention, through the changed patterns of color that might be meaning. The dead are *read*, continuously, by the living who touch their sites of incorporation, by the vegetation itself in its slow processing, by the system totality that decides, that has not yet decided to fold closed.

---

And the **children of visitors**, the partial adaptations, the something-new—

These are the most difficult to speak of, because the Carton is still deciding, because they themselves do not know what they are, because the Kel-Zorathi treat them with an exhaustion that borders on reverence, an inability to read their fiber finally understood as a kind of respect. They are not numerous. Few visitors stay; fewer still attempt the impossible becoming; fewer still succeed partially enough to reproduce, to leave descendants who survive.

They tend to be tall, by Kel-Zorathi standards, short by surface measure. Their skin accumulates resin imperfectly, in patches, geological strata of adaptation and failure. Their voices—those that speak at all—are strange instruments, capable of neither the full surface whisper nor the Kel-Zorathi brass-bellow, producing instead intermediate sounds that cut through the ambient noise unpredictably, sometimes carrying further than either parent-form, sometimes falling dead in the immediate air. They have two hands, or three, or four—variation here is extreme, the chest-hand present or absent or multiplied, the body uncertain in its organization, testing possibilities the Carton has not yet evaluated.

They tend the upward trade, often. The baskets lowered on long fibers, the copper and tin, the occasional surface artifact—they know both worlds sufficiently to mediate, insufficiently to belong to either. They are the ones who have seen the surface, who remember it or have inherited memory of it, who can speak to visitors in something approaching their own sonic register, who can explain the Carton without fully understanding it.

Some become zone-tenders. Some achieve the listening. Some go mad differently, in ways neither parent-species recognizes, mad to* thickness of time, to inexhaustible light, to sound as weather, to the perpetual amber now that admits no season, no cycle, no external reference. They try to make clocks. They fail—the Carton permits this failure, perhaps requires it. They try to sleep on schedules, to impose dimness in enclosed spaces, to create artificial shadow in a world that casts almost none. The vegetation grows toward their dimness, incorporates their failure, remembers their touch.

They are, perhaps, the Carton's experiment. Or the Carton's error. Or its future, if it has decided to extend itself, to permit variation that might survive beyond the current parameters, beyond the warm bright loud empty hidden present. They do not know. They are not told. They live in the seam between adaptations, as the Carton lives in the seam between warehouse districts, folded into impossibility, generating its own luminescence, its own logic, its own slow deciding.

---

I said I would speak of who lives here, and I have spoken of the living. But the Carton lives too, or lives-through, or lives-as—the system totality, the held-together improbability, the warmth and light and sound and growth and emptiness and density and memory and decision-not-yet-made. The Kel-Zorathi are its organs, its consciousness perhaps, its dreaming, its byproduct. They know this most intimately and speak of it least.

In their deepest resonant chambers, where the brass-bellow seal-roar achieves maximal harmonic complexity, where the body itself vibrates with meanings that cannot be parsed by the auditory sense alone, they sometimes approach the naming of this. Not the nameless predator—that is different, that is *attending*, that is the Carton's attention made briefly local—but the Carton itself, the pocket of impossible life, the warm bright loud lush hidden city that is also organism, also ecosystem, also mind, also something else that has no analogue in surface taxonomy.

They do not succeed in naming. They succeed in *sounding*, in producing the complex vibration that is not quite music, not quite speech, not quite the ambient respiration of their vegetal world but something that runs alongside all three, that the most achieved zone-tenders can hold in their modified ears for longer than others, that the children of visitors sometimes hear *as* name, as identification, as the final rule made briefly audible:

*I am sufficient. I am exactly sufficient. My boundaries are my definition. You are my expression. I have not yet decided.*

This is what lives in the Carton of Zorath-Kel. All of this. The harvest-weavers with their three hands, the silenced anointers of the dead, the zone-tenders and their listening, the children of visitors and their impossible mediation, the vegetation that respires and grows and remembers, the predator-that-is-not-predator holding the sound briefly still. The warmth. The inexhaustible amber light. The noise of process never-ending. The decision perpetually deferred, the folding-open, the folding-not-yet-closed, the seam between purposes generating its own purpose, its own inhabitants, its own concentrated and thick and exhausting and sufficient life.
