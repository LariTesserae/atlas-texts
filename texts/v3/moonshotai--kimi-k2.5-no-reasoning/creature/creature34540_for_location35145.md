**The Tenders**

They do not live *in* the grove, because the grove tolerates no habitation, no tenancy, no claims of territory within its boundaries. They live *with* it, or beside it, or in the narrow margin between its absolute self-sufficiency and the world's requirement that all things be witnessed, be tended, be *held in relation*. They are the ones who found the grove and did not leave, found it and *were found insufficiently* by its acceptance, its casual incorporation—who fell through the root-lattice and felt the slow arrest of their descent and recognized, in that moment of suspended reception, that they could not survive the attunement, could not bear the flattening, could become neither fauna nor flora nor guest who forgets the gradient of his own becoming.

And so they *climbed back*, an obscenity against the grove's logic, a violence of intention where intention had dissolved. They tore through yielding wood that did not understand resistance, that accommodated even their departure by *slowing* their passage, by making the birth upward as gentle as the fall downward, by refusing even in their leaving to acknowledge the *wrongness* of their choice.

They emerged changed but not transformed. The thrum in their bones permanent now, a tinnitus of the somatic, a resonance they cannot silence and have learned—not to ignore, but to *use*, to navigate by, to detect the soft places in the marsh-edge where the ground sighs instead of crunches.

---

They are not numerous. At any given epoch there are perhaps twelve, never more than twenty, distributed across the wetlands where the grove's *condition in matter* intersects with ordinary geography—not near the grove itself, which cannot be near anything, but at distances measured in weeks of travel, in watersheds, in cultural boundaries they do not recognize because their commonality supersedes all other membership. They find each other by resonance, by the matching of internal frequencies, two bodies that have both failed to become-grove and retained the wound of that failure as *instrument*.

Their settlements—never villages, never homes—are structures of *deliberate impermanence*, lean-tos of woven reed that last one season, fire-pits that they fill rather than maintain, paths that they allow to overgrow because *maintenance implies continuation*, and they have understood that continuation is the grove's domain, that human duration must be chosen moment by moment or not at all. They are ascetics of entropy, worshippers—if they worship—of the *deciduous*, the *intermittent*, the *mortal* as active virtue against the grove's terrible sufficiency.

---

Physically, they bear marks of the incomplete immersion.

Their fingernails grow thick and slightly translucent, the luminescence that suffuses the grove's walls expressed weakly in keratin, visible in darkness as a faint amber glow at their fingertips. They have learned to hide this, or to display it only to each other, in the recognition-gesture of palms-up exposure. Their circulatory systems carry remnants of the grove's water-chemistry, a mild alkalinity that makes their blood clot slower, that requires them to be careful with cuts, with wounds, with the ordinary violences of survival. Their eyes, adapted to the grove's distributed amber light, struggle with true darkness and true brightness both—nocturnal without being night-sighted, diurnal without tolerating noon.

Most distinctive is their *movement*, the subtle wrongness of gait that comes from having once known a space where gravity was *negotiable*, where the fall was slow and the climb yielding. They place their feet with excessive care, not from infirmity but from *memory of alternatives*, from the body's knowledge that ground *could* behave otherwise, that the contract between sole and soil is not absolute but *circumstantial*, a local agreement rather than universal law.

---

They speak little, and never of the grove directly, because the grove is not *spoken* but *resonated*, and language is too arterial, too directional, too much like the conduits that carry chemical messages in one-way flows. When they must communicate with outsiders, they are terse, literal, apparently impoverished in metaphor because metaphor *preserves* by carrying across, because the grove taught them—if it teaches—that nothing need be carried, that presence is sufficient, that the here does not require reference to the there.

Among themselves, they communicate in *rhythm*, in percussion on available surfaces, in the patterned striking that echoes the drum-fish without imitating them, that acknowledges the acoustic idiom of the grove while maintaining the *gap* of difference. A greeting is seven beats, syncopated. A warning is three rapid strikes followed by sustained resonance. A call to gather is the slow five-beat cycle of the engine-organs, but *wrong-footed*, begun on the off-pulse so that the mimicry is simultaneously recognizable and *refused*.

They do not sing. Singing is too continuous, too much like the drone itself, and they are *defined against* the drone now, by their departure from it, by the wound of having been insufficient for incorporation.

---

Their function, if they have one, is *witness* in the archaic sense—not observation but *testament*, the bearing of that which would otherwise be unborne. The grove does not require them. The world does not require them. But something *between* the grove and the world, some ontological pressure at the interface of solved existence and continuing becoming, produces them as *necessary accident*, as the symptom of a category that cannot be eliminated though it serves no purpose.

They tend the soft places.

Not the grove's entrances directly—they have learned that proximity corrupts, that the internal resonance intensifies with nearness until the old temptation returns, the desire to *fall again*, to try once more for the attunement that rejected them. But at distances where the marsh-edge begins to show symptoms of the grove's condition, where grasses start to sigh and water acquires the dark non-reflective quality of the pool beneath the floor-mat, they intervene with *deliberate wounding*, with cuts and burns and the introduction of salt or iron or the simple violence of exposure to air. They *arrest* the spread, not through healing but through *trauma*, through reminding the incipient grove-condition that the world still contains gradients, still possesses *difference that matters*.

They do not know if they succeed. The grove has never expanded, but it has never needed to; its self-sufficiency is inviolate at whatever scale. Perhaps their tending is mere *gesture*, a performance of opposition for an audience that does not attend, a theater of resistance without effect. They perform it anyway, as their *identity*, as the only becoming available to those who have been *nearly* unbecome.

---

The longest-lived among them, a figure known only as the Sixteenth Exit (she having been, by her account, the sixteenth to climb back, though the numeration is disputed and may refer to her attempts rather than her ordinal place), resides at the margin of a brackish estuary where fresh water meets salt in daily tidal dispute. Here she has established the only *permanent* structure in the Tenders' scattered geography, a kind of observatory raised on stilts above the high-water mark, open to weather, without walls except for a single translucent membrane that she replaces monthly as it degrades.

From this vantage she watches the water's *movement*, not its surface but its *sound*, the way the tide's advance and retreat modulates the substrate thrum that still vibrates in her bones. She has learned to *read* the marsh-edge's health in acoustic properties, to detect from half a day's journey the condition of a soft place by the harmonics that reach her through wet soil and standing water. When these harmonics *intensify*, when they approach the perfect drone of the grove itself, she travels; when they *attenuate*, when the world's ordinary gradients reassert themselves, she returns to wait.

She speaks to visitors, sometimes, though visitors are rare. Her speech is *elliptical*, circling the grove without approaching it, full of the negative knowledge of one who knows what-is by intimate acquaintance with what-is-not. She will say: "The grove is not hostile, which is worse." She will say: "The animals there are not adapted, but *concluded*." She will say: "To be received is not to be welcomed, and to be welcomed is not to be kept."

She does not explain. Explanation is continuity, is carrying-across, and she has forsworn these. She offers statements as *objects*, as things that exist in themselves without reference, without application.

---

The youngest Tender, barely more than a child when he fell and climbed back, has begun to *build*.

This is unprecedented, alarming to the others, though they do not forbid it because they have not the structure of forbidding, have only the negative example of their own impermanence. He gathers the driftwood that arrives at the estuary's mouth, the dead matter that the sea delivers as if in compensation for what it takes, and he *arranges* it, not as shelter but as *instrument*—a kind of resonating chamber, open at both ends, shaped to capture and amplify particular frequencies.

He plays it, sometimes, striking the wood with implements of bone and shell, producing sounds that are *almost* the grove's drone, *almost* the drum-fish percussion, *almost* the engine-organ breath but modified by the material's mortality, by the dead wood's inability to sustain vibration indefinitely, by the *decay* that enters each tone even as it sounds.

The Sixteenth Exit listens to him, from her raised observatory. She recognizes the gesture: not imitation but *conversation*, the attempt to speak with the grove in its own idiom while maintaining the *imperfection* that defines the human, the mortal, the * unfinished*. She does not know if this is possible. She suspects it is not, that the grove's acoustic universe has no category for response, only for *participation or absence*, and that the child's instrument is therefore heard as *defective emission* rather than communication.

But she does not stop him. Because possibility, even exhausted possibility, is what they have instead of the grove's sufficiency, and to foreclose it would be to complete their own becoming-grove in negation, to achieve by refusal the same equilibrium that the grove achieves by *is*.

She listens. The child plays. The thrum continues, somewhere, in bones and soil and the spaces between what can be tended and what can only be *witnessed*, what can only be borne and born and borne again, the endless carrying that defines the Tenders against the grove's absolute, terrible, sufficient, never-changing, always-there *there*.

---

*They do not name themselves. Naming is continuity, is preservation, is the claim that the present requires reference to past and future both. They are indicated by description, by circumstance, by the ordinal of their climbing-back. They exist in the record only as *those who left*, a negative definition that exhausts itself in its own utterance, that leaves nothing to be held but the holding itself, the tender and tending of a wound that does not close because it is not properly a wound, only the *shape of their persistence*, the vibration that continues because it has not been absorbed into the greater drone, because it maintains its frequency against the temptation of harmonic resolution, the long slow fall into participation that would be, for them, the final and most complete of departures.*
