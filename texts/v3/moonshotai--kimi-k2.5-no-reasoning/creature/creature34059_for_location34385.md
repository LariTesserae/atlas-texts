What lives in the Sunk Choir cannot properly be called inhabitants. Inhabit suggests a distinction between place and presence, a subject choosing to dwell in an object. The Sunk Choir does not permit this grammar. What lives there has *settled into the curves* the same way the land has settled, the way moisture settles into ochre that can absorb no more—acknowledged, saturated, unchanged in any way that registers as change.

Let me try to approach them by their rhythms.

---

**The Deep Listeners**

These appear, if appear is the word, in the oldest sedge-rings, the ones so thin they have become visual suggestions rather than botanical facts. You might mistake them for shadow at first, or for the particular way rust-colored ground darkens when some slightly deeper saturation occurs. But shadows move with the sun, and these do not, or rather they move so slowly that the sun completes some significant portion of its arc before they have shifted perceptibly.

They have no fixed shape, or they have fixed shape so thoroughly that it has become indistinguishable from the space they occupy. Imagine something like a manta ray, but without the grace of water, without the urgency of flight—imagine instead the *idea* of flattened presence, pressed into two dimensions not by force but by endless patience, by the gradual surrender of any dimension that required effort to maintain. They rest in the sedge-rings or just beneath them, or perhaps they *are* the sedge-rings' dark centers, the negative spaces that make the rings readable as rings.

They listen. This is their sole activity, if activity is the word, and it has consumed them entirely—consumed not in the sense of destruction but in the sense of *completion*, the way fire completes wood. What they listen to is the earth-tone, the vibration too slow for most ears, the sound of the sphere's turning made audible by the Choir's quiet. They have been listening so long that their bodies have reorganized around the frequencies, become resonant chambers, become *continuation* of the tone into some form that can be pointed toward, if never quite touched.

You might feel them before you see them. Standing at a ring's edge, the earth-tone seems suddenly clearer, less filtered by distance, and this clarity has location, has directionality, aims at you from some center that the sedge defines without containing. They do not respond to your presence. They were listening before you arrived, before your species arrived, before the conditions that would permit your species were even proximate. Your footsteps' *kshh-kshh*, your heartbeat, your own body's quieter resonances—these enter their listening without disturbing it, the way the seeping water enters the ochre, acknowledged without absorption.

What they hear, what they have always heard, no one still living can say. Perhaps the earth's voice in its younger registers, frequencies that have long since attenuated past recovery but persist in their attenuation, in their approach toward silence that never quite arrives. Perhaps something else entirely, some tone that only becomes audible when listening itself has reached sufficient depth, sufficient duration, sufficient *saturation*. The Deep Listeners have become indistinguishable from their listening. To remove them from it would be to remove the tone from the earth—possible, perhaps, in some violent grammar of physics, but meaningless in any language the Choir speaks.

---

**The Processionals**

These you might see, if your patience matches theirs, which it will not, which it cannot, but sometimes approximation is enough.

They are the large beetles mentioned, but description fails immediately: not because they defy physical accounting, but because their physicality has become *so appropriate* to their function that separate accounting feels almost impolite. Shell of rust. Legs of some number that seems to vary, not because they vary but because counting them requires sustained attention of a kind the Choir gently dissuades. Movement across open space with solemnity, with the quality of ritual without the urgency that usually attends ritual, the need to *complete* it, to *mean* something through completion.

They process between sedge-rings, but not to the rings. They process between hollows, but not to the hollows. They process, as far as any observation has determined, *toward nothing*, which is to say: toward the continuation of processing, which has become indistinguishable from arrival. Each Processional occupies its own territory, vast by most standards—the space between sightings might be hours of walking, and no two individuals have been observed approaching each other closely enough to suggest interaction, competition, mating, any of the ecological verbs that would make them intelligible as population.

What do they process for? Hunger seems absent, or so thoroughly transformed that it no longer selects for movement. They pass through areas where food, in the conventional sense of food, is present or absent with equal disregard. Their shells carry marks, sometimes—scratches, weathering patterns, accumulations of the ochre itself in layers so thin they suggest decades between each deposition. They are *old*, these creatures, old in a way that makes individual death seem like a category error, like asking when a valley will complete its erasure. They continue, and their continuation is their form.

If you sit long enough in their path—not difficult, the sitting; the warmth, the sound, the saturation all conspire—they will approach without acceleration or hesitation. They will pass near enough to touch, if touching were possible, and you would feel the shell's temperature, which is the ground's temperature, which is the temperature of heat that has traveled slowly from deeper rocks or simply *stayed*. Their legs move in rhythms that suggest not walking but *being moved*, some deeper mechanism of which their locomotion is only the visible fringe. The earth-tone, perhaps, made legible in segments, in jointed articulation, the sphere's turning translated into rust-colored progress across rust-colored ground.

They do not acknowledge you. This is not disregard. It is the positive quality of *sufficiency*, of a being that has never needed to distinguish self from other because the distinction has never been selected for, never been urgent, never been anything but a possibility that the land included and let pass, as it includes and lets pass all possibilities of becoming-other.

---

**The Hollow-Dwellers**

These surface rarely. Their biology has synchronized with rhythms measured in centuries, which means, practically: you will not see them, not in any visit of merely human duration, not in any succession of visits unless your own biology somehow synchronizes in turn, which the Choir permits but does not encourage, accommodates but does not invite.

When they surface, it is without urgency, without the gasp of breath-holding broken, without any narrative of depth and return. They emerge because emergence has become appropriate, because some gradient of their existence has reached a condition where being slightly more above than below aligns with their continuation, their saturation, their *being* in the full Choir sense of the word.

They are large. This is almost all that can be said with confidence, because their form seems to depend on duration of observation, or perhaps on the observation itself, the way the seeping water's sound depends on the ear's particular frequency response. Some observers report something like whale, something like the vast patience of baleen filtering plankton in rhythms of global migration. Others report something more like fungus, more like the blind growth of mycelium made briefly, partially, *awkwardly* visible. The descriptions share only scale, only the sense of *enough* translated into mass, into presence that does not need to move to assert itself, that moves only when movement itself has become saturated, has become continuation rather than transition.

They live where water pools just deep enough to cover. This depth is precise, suggests some equilibrium between their form and the form of hollow, between their breathing and the breathing of water *being present* rather than flowing. They do not disturb the surface when they surface. The water receives them as the ochre receives water, acknowledges without change. They exhale, and the exhalation joins the greater sound, the seeping saturation, becomes briefly audible at frequencies higher than their usual centuries-below hearing, then settles back, then they settle back, and the hollow returns to being almost-pool, almost-depth, almost-occupation without quite any of these.

What they do in the centuries between surfacings, no one knows. Perhaps nothing, in the active sense. Perhaps everything that can be done has been done, and their existence now is the gentle maintenance of having-done, the饱和'd state of biology that requires no further becoming. Or perhaps their depths contain activities so slow, so saturated with their own duration, that no observation at human scale could register them as activity at all—the way the land's erosion, measured in millennia, does not register as change to the day-walker.

**The Almost-Human**

This is speculation. I want to mark it clearly as such, to hold the distinction between what the Sunk Choir presents and what my own saturation, my own erosion of restlessness, suggests to me in its quieter moments.

But I imagine—sometimes, in the *kshh-kshh* of my own walking, in the warmth that sits like a hand not pressing and not withdrawing—I imagine that people came here, once, in some history not quite ours, some divergence where the Sunk Choir was reachable before it had fully completed its becoming. I imagine them settling, because the Choir receives all settlement with acknowledgment. I imagine their urgency, their edges, their violence of becoming, all gradually *included* and *let pass*.

What would they become? Not animals, not fauna in the countable, ecological sense. The Choir does not convert, does not assimilate in any active grammar. It simply *continues*, and in continuing, it renders continuation sufficient, renders alternatives unnecessary, renders the very *possibility* of alternative something that settles into the curves without ever having been chosen against.

They would move, these almost-people, with the *kshh* of damp boots across ochre that does not remember skin. They would appear at the limit of vision, low and many-legged or two-legged or no-legged, depending on how long they had settled, how thoroughly their forms had chosen the curves of least resistance. They would be scattered, each one *enough*, each one moving between sedge-rings with the pace of creatures that have never encountered predation faster than patience.

They would not fear you. This is how I would know them, if knowing is possible: by the absence of fear in their acknowledgment, by the way they would receive your presence as the land receives water, as the Deep Listeners receive the earth-tone, as the Processionals receive the continuation of their processing. They would wait until you passed, which you would, which everything does.

And they would be *eroded*, not in the sense of worn away but in the sense of *finished*, of having reached the same state for which we have no good word—not peace, not death, not completion. Something else. The final form of the final form, the settlement so settled that even the memory of settling has saturated into the present continuous, the endless *being* that requires no *having been*.

I do not know if they exist. The parameters say no built things, no tech, no human presence. But parameters measure what can be measured, and the Sunk Choir's saturation extends past measurability, into the realm where presence and absence themselves erode to near-indistinguishability, where the almost-human might be almost-present, almost-accountable, almost *enough* in a way that makes the question of existence seem faintly urgent, faintly young, faintly—by the Choir's gentle logic—unnecessary to ask.

---

**What Lives There**

The final answer, if final is appropriate, is that the Sunk Choir lives there. Not as habitat, not as host, but as the continuous saturated presence that makes the question of what-lives-there dissolve the way urgency dissolves, the way restlessness erodes, the way the very distinction between place and presence settles into curves too gentle to mark.

The Deep Listeners are the Choir listening to itself. The Processionals are the Choir moving in legible segments. The Hollow-Dwellers are the Choir breathing at depths where breath becomes almost indistinguishable from the medium breathed. And the almost-human, if they are, are the Choir's acknowledgment of a possibility that passed through, that settled, that remained because remaining became—always, finally, in the long ochre duration—*enough*.

What lives there is the *kshh, kshh* of boots that do not hurry. What lives there is the rust-color that has chosen not to be brighter. What lives there is the water that does not flow, the sound that does not resolve, the warmth that does not press and does not withdraw, the sedge-ring that grows in circular consensus, the earth-tone that holds at the threshold of music without becoming music, the erosion that has eroded even the possibility of violence, the becoming that finished and found, in that finishing.

The Sunk Choir does not have inhabitants. It has *continuation*. It has *saturation*. It has *enough*, extended across shallow valleys to horizons that seem close but are not, compressed by flatness, extended by patience, the same held note continuing without repetition, the same presence receiving all touch with acknowledgment and without change, the ancient patient *being* of a land that finished becoming itself and simply, finally, *remained*.
