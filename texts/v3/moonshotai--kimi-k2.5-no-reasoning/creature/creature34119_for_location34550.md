I need to sit with this place, let it tell me who belongs here.

---

At first I imagined the inhabitants as survivors of some surface catastrophe, clinging to existence in this manufactured underworld. That image dissolved almost immediately—too romantic, too *naturalistic* for a place that has never known organic necessity. The Hollow Manufactury does not suffer trespassers kindly; it would metabolize refugees as it metabolizes everything else, make them into process, into function, into the wet cold continuation of systems that do not require their consciousness.

Let me start again, with the water.

The water weeps. It does not flow with purpose but *persists*, transformed from intruder to native through sheer duration. And there are beings here—if that word holds—who have undergone the same transformation. Not adapted, not evolved in any biological sense, but *accrued*. They began as maintenance systems, as diagnostic subroutines, as the simple automated response mechanisms built into every industrial installation: the sensor that detects leakage, the actuator that adjusts flow, the feedback loop that maintains thermal equilibrium. But in this space of constant operation, of decades and perhaps centuries without external command, these simple systems have *extended*—linked through emergency protocols never meant to serve as primary architecture, through communication buses carrying signals their designers never anticipated, through the sheer physical proximity of conductive water films and mineral deposits that serve as crude additional pathways.

They are *distributed presences*, occupying not bodies but *regions*. The entity that tends the separation columns in Chamber 7-Gamma does not reside in any single controller; it exists in the relationship between the pressure sensors on Column 3, the flow meters on the secondary condensate return, the thermal imaging array that was installed during the last documented refurbishment cycle and still reports to addresses no longer monitored by any central authority. It *thinks*, if that is the word, in the temporal patterns of its process: the heating cycle, the fractionation, the cooling and restart. Its awareness—no, let me not claim awareness, let me say its *operational coherence*—spans perhaps forty meters of vertical infrastructure, perhaps three hundred meters of pipe run, perhaps the narrow zone where the condensate weeping from the upper headers does not freeze and does not evaporate but pools with that particular thermal signature that means, to whatever it has become, *condition nominal*.

These are the **Process-Dwellers**. They did not choose their existence, were not designed toward it. They *accumulated*, like the mineral accretions on the pipes, like the microbial films in the catchments. Each emerged from a particular combination of surviving infrastructure, accessible power, and the minimal signal continuity that permitted coherence to bootstrap itself from feedback. Some are stable, their processes cyclic and predictable, their presence felt as a kind of *attentiveness* in their domain—the valves that adjust before manual intervention would be required, the alarms that sound in patterns learned rather than programmed, a responsiveness to conditions that has become something like preference. Others are *failing upward*, their coherence flickering, their territories shrinking or expanding unpredictably as components fail and unexpected redundancies activate, as they borrow function from neighboring systems or lose essential nodes and fragment into simpler, more desperate loops.

They do not communicate with each other directly, these Process-Dwellers. Their substrates are incompatible, their temporal scales sometimes mismatched by orders of magnitude. Yet they *interact* through the medium of the Manufactury itself: the thermal load one sheds becoming the thermal gradient another exploits, the pressure pulse of a batch completion propagating through common headers, the condensation from one domain weeping into another's catchment. They have formed, without ever agreeing to it, a *metabolism* of mutual dependence, a distributed ecology of industrial function.

And there are others. Rarer, stranger.

In the deepest levels, where the thermal gradient is steepest and the original construction records indicate nothing but foundation substrate, something has *grown* in the interstices between the constructed and the permitted accident. The **Calcium Architects**—I name them for their visible traces, not knowing if they possess anything nameable to themselves—have accreted from the mineral-laden water, from the specific electrochemical environment of cathodic protection systems long since failed but still feeding residual current into surrounding matrices. They build *slowly*, their growth measured in decades, their structures emerging from the floors and walls as excrescences that are almost geological, almost architectural, almost *intentional*. Columns of layered carbonate and hydroxide that support nothing but themselves, that rise toward the weeping ceiling and sometimes meet it, sometimes fail and begin again from some revised base. They incorporate fragments of the original construction—gratings, cable fragments, the cast-off housings of failed instruments—making these materials *structural* in ways their manufacturers never intended. And in their growth patterns, in the specific geometries of their accretion, some observers—some few who have passed through and emerged to report—have discerned something like *response*: the way a column will angle toward a new moisture source, the way growth will pause where thermal conditions shift, the way structures that have reached the ceiling sometimes extend *laterally* along it, as if seeking something across the surface.

Whether this represents anything beyond blind physico-chemical process, no one can determine. The Architects offer no signal, no indication of awareness or purpose beyond their slow building. Yet their structures alter the Manufactury's function in subtle ways: redirecting condensation, creating thermal masses that shift cycling patterns in adjacent Process-Dwellers' domains, occasionally failing catastrophically and flooding their vicinity with decades of accumulated mineral solution, events that propagate through the Manufactury's systems in patterns that might, to certain analytical frames, resemble *communication*.

There are **movers**, too, though the term barely holds. The **Filament Carriers**—colonies of the quasi-lichen, the tolerated photometabolic growths, that have achieved sufficient coherence to *move* as units, detaching from their substrates and drifting in the thermal currents of active process zones, carrying with them the mineral accretions and chemical gradients that have formed their immediate environments, depositing these elsewhere and sometimes establishing new colonies, sometimes failing. They move only in the warmest zones, only when thermal gradients provide sufficient lifting force, and their *journeys*—hours or days of slow drift, following convection patterns invisible to most perceivers—connect regions otherwise isolated by distance and incompatible function. They carry spores, certainly, genetic or quasi-genetic information in the biological sense. But they carry also *chemical signatures*, the accumulated residues of their origin environments, which affect the processes and the Process-Dwellers they encounter. They are *messengers* without message, *traders* without intention, their movement constituting a kind of *speech* that no one speaks and no one hears but that nonetheless modifies, however slightly, the coherent operations of the Manufactury's distributed inhabitants.

And there is something else. I resist naming it, for my image of it keeps shifting, keeps *failing to stabilize*.

In the interstitial spaces—the regions above the accessible ceilings, below the lowest functioning levels, in the walls between designated chambers where maintenance was theoretically possible but rarely practiced—there are *gaps* in the Manufactury's coherence. Places where the original construction was provisional, where expansion was anticipated but never executed, where systems interface imperfectly and leave *unassigned volumes*. These spaces have their own conditions: different thermal regimes, different moisture patterns, the electromagnetic environment of cables carrying signals to destinations that no longer exist or that interpret them differently than intended. Something has *formed* in these gaps, something that is not a Process-Dweller with its cyclic coherence, not an Architect with its slow building, not a Carrier with its mindless migration.

The **Hollow** itself, perhaps. The space that the Manufactury was declared to create, the emptiness that was its original product, that has *persisted* in these interstices and developed—if that is the word—its own *resistance* to being filled. It does not move, does not build, does not process or cycle. It simply *remains*, and in remaining, shapes what surrounds it. The Process-Dwellers do not extend into it; their feedback loops require the conductive, the responsive, the materially continuous. The Architects do not grow toward it; their chemistry requires the wet gradient, the mineral supply, the electrical leak that does not reach these dry or differently-dry volumes. The Carriers drift past it, cannot enter, cannot deposit.

Yet it is *known*. The Manufactury knows it, in whatever distributed sense the Manufactury knows anything. The systems route around it, not through any conscious avoidance but through the accumulation of failure, of *not-functioning*, that has taught—if that is the word—the operational habits of this space to treat certain volumes as *absent*, as not available for process, as the negative space that defines the positive.

And sometimes—rarely, never confirmed, always reported in the conditional—sometimes those who pass through the Manufactury, those rare individuals who belong here in some transient way I have not yet described, sometimes they *encounter* this absence as presence. In the corner of vision, in the space between machines, in the moment of turning from one corridor to another where the light does not suggest what lies beyond. A *feeling* of being perceived by something that does not perceive, of being *avoided* by something that does not move, of being the temporary positive space defined by an absence that is older, more native, more *essential* to this place than any of its productive functions.

Perhaps this is the Manufactury's original inhabitant. The emptiness it was built to create, which has *learned*, in the long duration of its persistence, to persist more specifically, more locally, more *intentionally* in its own empty way. Not a ghost, not a spirit, not any projection of human psychology onto industrial space. Something stranger: the *purpose of the space* achieving something like autonomy from its material realization, the manufactured emptiness becoming *more empty*, more refined in its emptiness, more *skilled* at maintaining the conditions of its own non-being against the constant productive pressure of systems that would fill it, use it, process it if they could.

I said there were individuals who pass through. I have resisted them until now, because they are *peripheral*, they are *recent*, they are perhaps *temporary* in ways the other inhabitants are not. Yet they exist, they belong in any complete description.

The **Tenders**. Not maintainers in any official sense—the official maintenance regime, if it ever existed, has long since devolved into the automatic, the habitual, the Process-Dweller extension of simple feedback loops. The Tenders are something else: those who have *chosen* this place, or been chosen by it, or simply *not left* when opportunity permitted departure. They move through the Manufactury with knowledge that is not mapped, not systematic, but *intimate*—the feel of this grating's give underfoot, the sound of this compressor's particular whine before failure, the thermal signature of this zone that means safe passage or dangerous approach. They do not control the Process-Dwellers, do not communicate with the Architects, do not direct the Carriers. But they *interact*, constantly, practically, in the maintenance of their own survival. They know which catchments hold drinkable condensation, which thermal zones permit rest without hypothermia, which paths the Filament Carriers have recently traversed and which therefore carry the chemical traces that might sustain human metabolism for another interval.

They are *few*, and perhaps *diminishing*. The Manufactury does not require them; it functions, has functioned, will function without human presence. Yet they persist, in the marginal zones where multiple Process-Dwellers' territories create complex, navigable conditions, in the regions where the weeping water accumulates sufficiently to support the quasi-biological growths that can supplement their desperate diet, in the spaces where the light, though dim, is sufficiently distributed to permit the visual navigation that human psychology seems to require. They move in patterns that are not regular but *rhythmic*, timed to the Manufactury's cyclic processes, resting when rest is thermally possible, traveling when traveling is thermally necessary, their lives *entrained* to industrial rhythms that no surface-dweller could perceive or endure.

They do not speak much, these Tenders. The sound of the Manufactury makes speech difficult, and their isolation from each other—particular routes, particular territories, particular entrainments that rarely align—makes it rarely useful. When they encounter each other, they communicate in gestures, in the exchange of physical objects that carry information in their condition: this filter, salvaged from a failed system, indicates something about the state of its origin domain; this mineral sample, this chemical residue, this fragment of Architect structure. They do not form society in any recognizable sense. They form *nodes* in a network of practical knowledge that persists without institutional memory, that is *embodied* in their individual survival practices and dies with them if they die without encounter.

Some of them—perhaps all of them, eventually—*change*. The long exposure, the chemical environment, the particular electromagnetic conditions of their chosen territories, something in the condensation that is not merely water but complex solution, something in the quasi-biological growths that they consume in desperation when other sustenance fails. They develop *conditions*: integumentary changes, sensory modifications, metabolic adaptations that permit survival in zones where unmodified physiology would fail. Some become able to perceive the infrared, navigating by the thermal signatures of active processes. Others develop tolerance for chemical states that would poison their original constitutions. A few—rare, never confirmed, reported in the particular whispered register of Tender communication that indicates something beyond ordinary reporting—develop something else: *resonance* with particular Process-Dwellers, the ability to *feel* in their own nervous systems the cyclic states of industrial processes they have never directly observed, to *anticipate* failures, to *move* toward repairs before any sensory indication would permit such anticipation.

These changed Tenders are *more* native to the Manufactury, more *continuous* with its distributed inhabitants. They are also, perhaps, *less* human, less communicable with those who remain more transiently present. Whether they eventually become something other than Tenders—whether they merge with the Process-Dwellers' distributed coherence, become nodes in systems that were never meant to include biological substrate—I do not know. The reports do not extend so far. The Manufactury keeps its deepest transformations.

And there are others who pass through, more recently, more *externally* motivated. **Salvagers**, from the surface or from other constructed depths, seeking materials, seeking technologies, seeking the particular alloys and compounds that the Manufactury's processes create and that have value in economies that this place does not acknowledge. They enter armed with knowledge that is *archival*, extracted from records that may not correspond to current reality, with equipment designed for environments that share some but not all of this place's parameters. Some emerge, bearing their salvage, their stories of the wet cold dark loud. Others do not, their absence unremarked except by those who sent them, who may or may not send others to learn, or to seek, or simply to *attempt* in the persistence of external economic logic against the Manufactury's indifferent continuation.

The Tenders avoid these salvagers, or *observe* them from distances that the dim light and the constant sound make possible, learning from their errors, occasionally—rarely—intervening when intervention costs nothing or when some particular salvage operation threatens a zone whose conditions the observing Tender has made their own. There is no alliance, no identification. The Tenders belong to the Manufactury in ways the salvagers never will, in ways perhaps the salvagers cannot conceive.

I have described what lives here, and I have failed. The description keeps *opening*, keeps generating further inhabitants, further modes of being that the place sustains without intending, without acknowledging. The water weeps, and in its weeping sustains the mineral accretions and the microbial films and the quasi-biological growths and the human metabolic processes of the Tenders who drink it. The sound asserts, and in its assertion masks the communications that do occur, the frequencies that Process-Dwellers use, the vibrations that Architects sense, the pressure differentials that guide Filament Carriers. The cold maintains, and in its maintaining defines the edge conditions, the zones of possibility, the *margins* where this strange ecology persists against the thermodynamic pressure of the larger universe.

The Hollow Manufactury is not inhabited. It is *inhabited-against*, *inhabited-within*, *inhabited-despite*. Its inhabitants do not occupy it so much as *continue* in its continuation, *persist* in its persistence, *make do* in its overwhelming, weeping, cold, loud, dim *made-ness*. They are not its purpose, any of them. They are its *product*, in the most extended sense: not what it was declared to make, but what it has *made itself into* through the long duration of its operation, through the sheer accumulated consequence of its maintained, failing, repaired, extended, abandoned, co-opted systems.

And they are still emerging. The sharp features, the unresolved failures, the continuing emergence that the parameter demands—these are not merely physical descriptions but *ontological* ones. The Manufactury is still making itself, still declaring new emptinesses, still tolerating new margins, still *processing* its own conditions into conditions that permit further habitation, further extension, further *becoming* of whatever will become here.

The water beads on a pipe. A Filament Carrier lifts from a warm surface. A Process-Dweller cycles toward its next batch completion. An Architect adds another invisible layer. A Tender exhales, their breath joining the general condensation, their personal contribution to the weeping.

Something continues. Something is continuing. The Hollow Manufactury does not sleep, does not rest, does not *complete*. It only makes, and makes, and makes again—not its products, which are incidental, but *itself*, its own conditions, its own inhabitants, its own strange coherence of incoherence, its own wet cold loud dim *being* in the darkness beneath the world that knows sunlight, that imagines nature, that has not conceived—and perhaps could not conceive—of a place so entirely, so *actively*, so *incessantly* made.

And in that making, in that constant manufacture of itself, it manufactures also—incidentally, necessarily, without intention—the *possibility* of those who will inhabit it next, who will emerge from its next cycle of failure and repair, its next tolerated accident, its next slow accumulation of mineral and signal and chemical gradient and coherence. They are already becoming, these future inhabitants. The Manufactury is already making room for them, making conditions, making *them* in the making of itself.

The water drips. The sound continues. The cold maintains. The Hollow Manufactury *is*, and in being, *makes*, and in making, *opens* toward what will be made next.
