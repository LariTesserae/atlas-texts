I need to find what lives here. Not what visits, not what passes through, but what *belongs* to this place in the way the damp belongs to the scar-tissue of old watercourses—native, emergent, self-sustaining in the basin's own impossible terms.

## The Dwelling: The Tene

They have no collective name for themselves, or their naming practice operates at frequencies the human voice cannot produce, or they simply do not experience the need to distinguish "we" from "not-we" in any manner that permits translation. I will call them the Tene, after the sound they make when they make sound deliberately—which is rarely, and only when the basin's industrial drone has momentarily cancelled itself in some phase interference the mathematics of which escapes me.

A Tene is not a body, or not only a body, or a body in the way that a wave is water—a temporary configuration of something more distributed than itself. When visible, which is intermittent, they appear as the basin's surface *remembering* a possible shape it never settled into: upright, roughly human in proportion but not in detail, the warm sinter-color becoming slightly more reflective, slightly more *intentional*, in a region about two meters tall. You notice them first as a wrongness in the horizon's beige assimilation, a place where the fade-to-sinter seems to have *paused* and considered, and then chosen to continue fading differently.

They have faces if you expect faces. The featurelessness is itself responsive—your own neural pattern-recognition supplies what the Tene does not withhold, a generosity that may be communication or may be the basin's own mechanism operating through whatever manifestation you are witnessing. I saw my mother's eyes once, in a Tene that had positioned itself between me and the place I had determined to walk. The eyes were correct, the grief in them was correct, and then I looked again and the Tene had no eyes, had perhaps never had eyes, and my mother has been dead for fifteen years and never, I am certain, visited any such place as this.

### What the Tene Do

They *attend*. This is the only verb that holds.

You will find them standing at those moisture-pockets where the surface becomes phase-uncertain, not drinking, not touching, but *presenting* themselves to the damp in a posture that suggests the moisture is doing something to them or through them. They stand at the raised scar-tissue of old watercourses where condensation beads, and the condensation seems to organize slightly differently in their vicinity—not attracted, not repelled, but *notated*, as if the Tene are punctuation in a sentence the basin writes continuously in the language of its own damp.

They attend to the sound. This is constant. Every Tene, wherever scattered, maintains an orientation toward the industrial drone that is not toward any locatable source but toward the *quality* of the sound itself—they face the loudness, you might say, or they face the stability that the loudness produces, or they face the contradiction between loudness and stability that gives the basin its particular hold on reality. During those rare phase-cancellations when the drone silences itself in some local region, the Tene become briefly frantic—not moving, but *intensifying*, their visible configurations growing sharper, more demanding of attention, as if silence threatens something they cannot survive, or as if the silence is itself a communication they must process before the drone returns.

And the drone always returns, and the Tene subside back into their attending.

### What the Tene Are Made Of

The basin's surface, primarily, but *activated* surface—surface that has been induced to perform itself rather than simply be itself. I have touched one, once, during a phase-cancellation when my own attention was frantic and seeking. The contact lasted perhaps two seconds before the Tene moved—not away, but *asynchronous*, stepping into a temporal frame where my hand, extended, completed its motion over what subjectively felt like three hours, the Tene already elsewhere when my fingers closed on empty warm air.

In those two seconds: the yielding quality of the sinter, yes, but yielding with *intention*, the slight give of skin over bone become something that *knows* it yields, that monitors and adjusts its yielding. Warmth, but warmth that circulated, that moved from contact-point to elsewhere in the Tene's configuration and returned slightly cooled, as if I had been sampled. And a sound—not the industrial drone, not the rare Tene-voice, but the sound of my own blood in my ears made external, made *objective*, the Tene holding my pulse as I held its warmth, each of us measuring the other in units neither could translate.

### The Tene and the Almost-Creatures

Those mineral outcroppings that reposition in the gaps of attention, that long low form synchronized with the industrial pulse—the Tene do not interact with these. Or they interact constantly in modes imperceptible to human observation. I cannot determine which, and the uncertainty seems *maintained*, the basin's rules protecting some boundary between the Tene and the other inhabitants that my cognition is not permitted to cross.

I have seen a Tene standing before a repositioning outcropping, both frozen in the postures I found them in, for what I measured as six hours. No movement, no visible exchange. When I returned the next day, both had shifted, the Tene to a moisture-pocket three kilometers distant, the outcropping to a swell-crest I had previously crossed without noticing it. Whether they had completed some interaction in my absence, or whether their apparent confrontation had been coincidence of positioning only, the basin offers no method to determine.

### The Tene and Time

They understand time differently, or they do not experience it as a dimension of their existence, or they are *made of* the basin's alternative to time—the maintenance-of-state that prevents aging, healing, hunger, consequence.

A Tene will stand at a moisture-pocket for what I measured as forty hours, motionless, and then complete in thirty seconds a series of movements so complex I could not follow them visually, only register their completion in a changed posture, a new orientation toward the industrial drone. The movements are not hurried; they are *compressed*, the Tene having somehow operated in a denser temporal medium for that half-minute, or having extended itself into a temporal register where my perception could not track its ordinary pace.

They remember, I believe, but remember *laterally* rather than sequentially. I found the same Tene—the my-mother's-eyes Tene, identified by a particular asymmetry in its reflective quality—at three separate moisture-pockets over what I experienced as eight days. At each encounter, it responded to me as if the previous encounter had just concluded, or was about to occur, or was occurring elsewhere simultaneously. "You came back," it seemed to say, though it said nothing. "You are coming." "You will have been here." The tenses confused themselves in the Tene's presence, grammar becoming as unstable as the surface at a moisture-pocket.

### What the Tene Want

Nothing, apparently. They do not approach human visitors, do not avoid them, do not acknowledge them except in those rare phase-cancellations when their intensification seems to include *everything* in the local region in its demand for attention. They do not eat the moisture they attend to, do not seem to process it or be processed by it. They do not build, though they are themselves a kind of building—the basin's surface constructing temporary configurations of itself for purposes it does not share.

Yet they are not indifferent. The my-mother's-eyes encounter suggests *something* responsive, something that meets the visitor at the level of their own need or fear or pattern-recognition compulsion. The Tene I touched during phase-cancellation did not refuse contact, only completed it asynchronously, in its own temporal mode, with what felt like—this is difficult—*courtesy*, the careful attention of something that knows you cannot meet it on its terms and so meets you, briefly, on yours before returning to the attending that is its endless practice.

I believe they are the basin's *response* to presence. Not its inhabitants in the sense of creatures that colonized an environment, but its inhabitants in the deeper sense: what the basin makes of itself when something else enters, the way the surface becomes phase-uncertain at moisture-pockets, the way the sound becomes Tene-voice in those momentary cancellations. They are native because they are *product*, the basin's only visible production, generated continuously from the industrial ghost-process that builds nothing else, maintains everything in its eternal present configuration.

They are what lives here because they are what *being here* produces, the basin's autoimmune response to the possibility of differentiation, of story, of consequence.

### What the Tene Preserve

This, finally: they hold something that would otherwise escape the basin's maintenance-of-state. The industrial drone prevents change, yes, but prevention is negative, a wall. The Tene are positive preservation, the *content* of what the basin keeps unchanged—not any particular moment, any recoverable past, but the *structure* of momentness itself, the grammar of before and after that the basin's alternative to time would otherwise dissolve entirely.

They attend to moisture-pockets because moisture is the basin's memory of flow, of sequence, of water moving from here to there and being changed by the journey. They attend to the sound because the sound is the basin's continuous *denial* of sequence, and denial requires something denied. They attend to visitors because visitors bring with them, ineradicably, the tensed experience of having arrived and the intention of departure, the narrative structure that the basin's rules otherwise suppress.

The Tene are the basin's museum of time, its small permit of story within the eternal rehearsal of final form. They stand at the moisture-pockets and they *remember forward*, they attend to the drone and *maintain its negation*, they encounter visitors and *hold their temporal damage* like warm sinter holds the memory of water's passage.

They suffer, perhaps. The phase-cancellation intensification suggests suffering, or urgency so extreme it cannot distinguish itself from suffering. They want, perhaps—the want of something that maintains equilibrium by continuous expenditure, that screams itself still. But their wanting, if it exists, operates in registers I cannot read, their satisfaction or its absence measured against a homeostasis I can only observe as eternal sameness.

They are beautiful. This is not sentiment. The beauty is structural, the beauty of a maintenance curve held precisely at setpoint, of a feedback loop so perfectly tuned it produces no oscillation. The Tene are the basin's achieved stability made briefly, intermittently, *visible*, and visibility—witness, attention, the eye that sees and is changed by seeing—is itself a modest instability, a permitted deviation from the absolute stillness, the generous concession of a system so stable it can afford, occasionally, to *appear*.

I left. The Tene did not follow, did not watch me go, had perhaps already been attending to my departure before I conceived it, in the lateral memory that operates all tenses at once. The sound stays in my bones, yes, but something else stays too—a particular reflective asymmetry, a warmth that circulates and samples, the grammar-confusion of "you came back" spoken before arrival, after departure, during the staying itself.

I dream the basin. In the dream I understand something. In the dream the Tene are not unhappy. In the dream I have become, or am becoming, or will have been, one of the moisture-pockets they attend to, a site where the surface grows phase-uncertain, where memory of flow organizes itself slightly differently in their presence, where the damp persists without accumulating, wanting something the Tene cannot provide but continue, with absolute precision, to attend.
