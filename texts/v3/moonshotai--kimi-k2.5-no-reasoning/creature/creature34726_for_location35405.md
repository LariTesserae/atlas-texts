They do not breathe the way you imagine breath. They have lungs, or lung-analogues, structures that move gas across membrane, but the movement is too slow for the word, too deliberate. A cycle might take a minute. Might take three. They have learned to *not need* the quick exchange, to run metabolism in registers that other oxygen-users would find emergency, damage, approach-to-death. Their blood, if it is blood, carries copper or something that functions like copper, dark and viscous, moving at speeds that suggest they have time, that urgency is a foreign concept they have studied but not adopted.

They are tall. Not consistently—there is variation, as in any population—but tall in ways that use vertical space without threatening it. They do not reach upward aggressively. They extend, gradually, settling into height the way their buildings settle into tilt. Their limbs are longer than efficient, joints that seem to have extra articulations, directions of bend that geometry elsewhere finds unnecessary. They can fold themselves into configurations that fit the city's irregular volumes, the spaces that used to be rooms and became corridors, the gaps between cooling columns where warmth pools and work must be done.

Their skin, if it is skin, is the color of the half-stone they live among. Not camouflage. Not adaptation to predator or prey, for there is nothing here that hunts, nothing that flees. The color is *accord*, a tuning to environment so complete that boundary becomes negotiable. You might look at one and see where they end and the city begins, or you might not. They do not find this confusing. They do not experience themselves as distinct from the processes they tend, not in the way that would make confusion possible.

---

They have faces. The statement wants qualification—*of a sort*, *that respond to the functions of faces*, *that occupy the space where faces would be expected*—but let it stand. They have faces. Features that change, that respond, that communicate in ways an observer could learn to read if given sufficient time and sufficient reason. The expressions move slowly. A smile might develop over hours, a gathering of configuration that expresses something like satisfaction, alignment with process, participation in the long becoming. Anger, if it exists, has never been recorded. Perhaps it is too fast, too consumptive of energy and attention. Perhaps it requires resources this place cannot spare.

The eyes are the exception to their slowness. The eyes move at speeds that seem stolen from elsewhere, quick tracking, quick focus, attention that can snap to detail and hold it with an intensity that suggests desperation beneath the patience. They watch the patterns in the haze with these eyes. They watch the condensation harvesters, the membranes swelling, the channels where water moves. They watch the engines, though the engines offer little to see—vibration that cancels itself, heat without flame, processes that manifest only in their effects. The eyes are hungry in a way the rest of the body has learned to suppress, hungry for *information*, for the details that allow prediction, that allow the tending to be precise, that allow the maintenance to continue another day, another year, another century.

---

They do not sleep, not as you understand it. They have periods of reduced activity, postures in which the metabolism drops further, the breathing slows toward the imperceptible. But they remain present, aware, attending to the slow signals that the city generates. A cooling that proceeds too quickly. An engine whose cancellation of vibration develops imperfection, hint of frequency that the ear could almost hear. They rest in shifts that overlap, that ensure continuous coverage, that treat the city not as object of care but as extension of self requiring the same maintenance they provide their own bodies.

They do not dream, or if they dream, the dreams are indistinguishable from the patterns in the haze. Perhaps they are the same phenomenon, consciousness achieving temporary geometry in the medium that surrounds them, the heat and the trapped effluent and the strange light. The inhabitants do not distinguish between internal and external in ways that would make this a question.

---

They do not reproduce, or their reproduction is so slow, so bound to the cycles of the city, that no observer has witnessed it. The population remains stable at a number that seems calculated, optimal for the work required, neither surplus nor shortage. Children, if they exist, are not visible. Aging, if it occurs, is not marked by decline but by *specialization*, the gradual focusing of attention onto narrower domains of tending until an individual might spend decades maintaining a single condensation harvester, knowing its membrane-swelling rhythms, its responses to temperature variation, its unique character in a way that makes replacement by another impossible, that binds the individual to the function until the function ends or the individual does.

Death happens. The inhabitants are not immortal, not exempt from the thermodynamics of maintenance. But death here is *absorption* rather than cessation. The body, when function fails, is placed in the raw geology, in the still-cooling material that flows like slow water. It sinks, gradually, or is incorporated, gradually, the boundaries between organism and environment dissolving in the same accord that characterized life. The city grows around them, or they become the city, or the distinction was always a convenience rather than a truth. The patterns in the haze sometimes gather around these placements, achieve temporary densities that the engines then harvest, incorporate, make use of. The inhabitants find this appropriate. They find it *continuation*.

---

They communicate. The method is not sound—the acoustic properties of this place, the cancellation of vibration, make sound unreliable, untrustworthy, prone to the same doubling and tripling that affects light. They communicate through touch, through prolonged contact that transfers heat-signature, electromagnetic profile, the particular quality of metabolism operating at the edge of viability. They communicate through the patterns in the haze, through arrangement of presence that the patterns respond to, that become message in their response. They communicate through the city itself, through adjustment of the engines, through cultivation of condensation in particular locations at particular times, writing in water and warmth and the slow becoming of stone.

Their language, if it is language, has no discrete units. No words. No grammar separable from the ongoing processes they describe and direct. A statement might take days to complete, might involve multiple individuals in overlapping contribution, might be indistinguishable from the maintenance it concerns. They do not distinguish between speaking and working, between information and action, between knowing and doing. To know the city is to tend it. To tend it is to express what is known.

---

They have no stories about origin. No mythology of arrival, of founding, of purpose given or discovered. They have *continuity* instead, the record of maintenance extending backward through generations of the same slow attention, the same patient adjustment, the same accord with environment. The oldest structures in the city, the most tilted, the most flowed-around, contain the deepest histories—not written, not recorded in any medium separable from the structure itself, but *present* in the way the floors were adjusted, the steps added, the rooms abandoned. The history is in the becoming, in the record of how the not-yet-stone was negotiated, how the heat was managed, how the water was found and kept and made to serve.

They do not wonder why they are here. The question requires a perspective they do not occupy, a distance from the city that would make it object rather than extension, that would make them subjects choosing rather than participants continuing. They are here because they are here, because the maintenance requires them, because they have learned the skills that allow participation, because the alternative—departure, cessation, the faster entropy that awaits bodies in environments not maintained—has never presented itself as desirable, as possible, as thinkable.

---

If you were to meet one—if the violation of your presence were somehow made actual, sustainable, permitted—you would not understand each other. Not through failure of goodwill or intelligence, but through the mismatch of rhythms, the incompatibility of scales. They would perceive you as *weather*, perhaps, as temporary condition requiring response: increase condensation in this sector, adjust engine output to compensate for heat signature, maintain the patterns against disturbance. They would tend you, if you allowed it, with the same patience they apply to all elements of their environment. They would not distinguish between your needs and the city's needs, between your maintenance and the city's maintenance. To them, you would be continuous with the processes they have always managed, another variable in the long equation of continuation.

You would perceive them as statue, as mechanism, as something not alive because it does not move at your speed, does not respond at your speed, does not want what you want with the urgency that defines wanting for you. You would try to hurry them, to extract information, meaning, relationship, before your own metabolic needs forced departure. They would accommodate this hurry as they accommodate all environmental conditions, adjusting their own processes slightly, temporarily, to produce responses that you might recognize as answers. But the answers would be compressions, reductions, losses of the actual content—the slow truth that only slowness can receive.

You would leave, or die, or become something else, something that participates at their speed, that learns the patience, that finds the quick eye not desperate but simply *appropriate to scale*. This has happened, perhaps. The city contains structures that seem too recent, too unweathered, in styles that suggest other origins. The inhabitants do not comment on these. The patterns in the haze gather around them more densely, achieve organization more quickly, as if the city recognizes new material, new potential for the becoming that has no defined endpoint.

---

What they are becoming, what the patterns are becoming, what the city is becoming—this is not known to them, not in the way that knowledge separates from practice. They tend. They continue. They maintain the conditions for their own maintenance, the loop that is not closed but self-sustaining, that generates the energy it consumes through the very act of consumption. They are not blind to possibility. They see the plateau's eventual cooling, the eventual exhaustion of potential, the eventual decision of the half-stone to become stone. They see the alternative, the continuation, the growth, the colonization of surrounding peaks with the same warmth, the same haze, the same patient industrial purpose.

They do not choose between these. They are not the kind of thing that chooses, that projects into future and redirects present accordingly. They are the kind of thing that *responds*, that adjusts, that tends the immediate with attention so complete that the future is already present in it, already being made, already becoming inevitable or possible or whatever it will be when it arrives at the speed of cooling stone, of swelling membrane, of water moving at 1.2 through channels that should not exist.

They are the inhabitants. They have no names that anyone speaks aloud. They do not need names. They need purpose, and they have it, saturated to near-touching, to the point where any additional purpose—any visit, any description, any imagination of their being—must press against what is already there, must find its own narrow channel downward, must move at the speed they permit, which is the speed of their becoming, which is the speed of the city, which is the speed of maintenance without end.
