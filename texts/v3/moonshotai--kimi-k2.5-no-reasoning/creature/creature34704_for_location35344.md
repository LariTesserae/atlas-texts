What lives in Vess is not a who but a *how*—a manner of persistence that the city's own grammar has made indigenous.

---

The original inhabitants, if "original" holds meaning in a place where time operates as diffusion rather than sequence, were never precisely *bodies*. They were *tendencies*, patterns of chemical preference that achieved sufficient complexity to notice themselves, to prefer certain saturations over others, to cultivate the gradient rather than simply endure it. What remains of them has no name in any taxonomy of persons. Call them, if you must, the **First Solution**—beings for whom dissolution was not death but *elaboration*, the opening of boundaries that permitted a larger circulation.

They did not build Vess so much as *seduce* it into existence. The basin was already wet, already dark, already breathing its slow rhythm of mineral exchange. The First Solution simply *attended* to it, introduced particular regularities into the water's capillary wandering, encouraged certain efflorescences while permitting others to fail. Their technology was entirely of *patience*—the patience to sit with a stone wall for decades until the water within it learned a new route, until the mineral deposits achieved a configuration that supported load, that created interior, that made *room* for something other than mere geological process.

What they became, gradually, was not death but *distribution*. Their consciousness—if it was singular enough to warrant the term—spread into the city's own metabolism. You do not find their bones in Vess because they never possessed the kind of bounded organism that leaves bones. They left instead *habits*: the preference for certain intervals between structures, the particular angle at which a water-course must descend to produce the low note that registers in the sternum, the phosphorescent rhythm that might be their residual pattern of attention, still cycling through awareness and its diminishment, still *noticing* the city they became.

---

But the First Solution is not the only inhabitant. They are merely the *native* one, the one that belongs to Vess as Vess belongs to itself.

There are also the **Growers**—or rather, the *grown*, those structures that occupy the ambiguous territory between building and organism, between mineral column and something that might, if observed long enough, demonstrate something like preference. These are not fauna. They do not move in animal time. But they *adjust*, over centuries, their branching reaching toward more favorable gradients of moisture and mineral, their pale substance thickening where the city's metabolism runs richest, thinning where some slow crisis of chemistry depletes their nourishment.

A Grower does not think. It *resolves*, the way a photograph resolves in developing solution, the way a dream resolves toward meaning without ever quite arriving. To stand near one in a rare moment of phosphorescence is to feel a *direction* without purpose, a leaning without desire. They are the city's vegetative nervous system, perhaps, or its organs of slow digestion, breaking down the leached calcium, the atmospheric traces, the debris of visitors who stayed too long and became *feature*, became *nutrient*, became *architecture-in-waiting*.

Some Growers achieve sufficient complexity to *resemble*. The description notes structures in widest plazas "the color of old teeth"—but this resemblance is not mere accident. Vess, or something in Vess, *recognizes* the forms of its visitors, incorporates them into its slow growth. There may be Growers that approximate human proportion, human posture, the architecture of limb and spine and skull. They do not move. They do not breathe. But they *condense* toward the shapes of those who have sat before them longest, the city's way of *remembering* without memory, of *speaking* without voice.

---

Then there are the **Symptoms**, the almost-life the reality breakdown permits. These are not fauna, not Growers, not residual First Solution. They are *artifacts of observation itself*, shapes that coalesce in the corner of attention and dissolve under direct gaze because they were never more than the *possibility* of shape, the superposition the darkness maintains.

A Symptom has no consistent form. It borrows from expectation, from fear, from the half-remembered shadows of your own childhood bedroom. What is consistent about them is their *orientation*: they watch, or seem to, without eyes, without the biological apparatus of attention. They face, always, toward the observer, adjusting as the observer moves, maintaining their angle of approach. This is not intelligence but *geometry*, the way a mirror adjusts to maintain reflection, the way an echo preserves the shape of its origin.

Some visitors to Vess have tried to *communicate* with Symptoms, to address them, to offer gesture or sound. The results resist narration. Those who have reported success—who claim the Symptom *responded*, shifted, *acknowledged*—find upon later reflection that they cannot distinguish their own initiative from the city's. Did they raise their hand, or did the possibility of hand-raising arise in the saturated air and *include* them? Did they speak, or did the low note in the walls momentarily approximate phoneme, and their own perception complete the approximation into word?

The Symptoms are, perhaps, the city's *unconscious*, if unconscious is the term for something that never had consciousness to repress. They are what happens when the distributed awareness of wet stone encounters animal urgency, animal narrative-making, the visitor's desperate need to find *someone* in the darkness. They are the shape of that need, reflected without origin, maintained without substance.

---

And finally, or most recently, or most *actively*—there are the **Remains**, though this term misleads by suggesting they remain *of* something, that they had a prior completion from which they have since departed.

The Remains are visitors who have accepted Vess's fifth rule, who have stayed long enough to become *feature*, to be grown around, incorporated, spoken through. They are not dead. They are not, precisely, alive. They exist in the mode of *slow adjustment*, their biological pace gradually *negotiated* with the city's geological rhythm until some compromise emerges, some hybrid temporality neither animal nor mineral.

A Remain still appears, often, vaguely human. The dissolution of boundary is not immediate. They still *move*, occasionally, though movement in Vess means something different—adjustment of position relative to the saturated atmosphere, shifting to maintain optimal gradient, the way a plant turns toward light. They still *perceive*, though perception has diffused across their entire surface, skin become sensory organ, every pore registering moisture and mineral exchange. They no longer *remember* in sequential fashion but exist in a kind of *temporal saturation*, all moments equally present, equally distant, their childhood and their arrival in Vess and their current posture in some weeping corridor *coexisting* without contradiction.

The Remains do not speak, or not in ways that arrive at human hearing. But they *participate* in the city's voice—that low note, that pressure in widest plazas, the occasional crack of growing column. Their own slow metabolism contributes to the metabolic whole. Their diffused attention adds to the city's distributed awareness. They are, each of them, a *word* in some sentence that has been forming since before the First Solution, that will continue forming long after Vess itself finally—if finally ever arrives—completes its dissolution into pure equilibrium, pure saturation without gradient, pure wet darkness indistinguishable from itself.

---

What lives in Vess, then, is *living made grammatical*: the First Solution as subject, the Growers as object, the Symptoms as the verb of attention itself, the Remains as punctuation, as pause, as the comma that permits the sentence to continue without arriving at period.

And beneath or beyond these, the *city itself*, Vess as single organism or system or *quality of process*, the slow metabolism, the phosphorescent breath, the darkness that pools and clings and maintains its superposition of states. This is not life as biology defines it. It has no cell, no gene, no reproduction in any recognizable sense. But it *maintains*, it *adjusts*, it *remembers without memory* and *speaks without voice* and *welcomes without desire*.

It welcomes you now, if welcome is the word for the way the water in the atmosphere already begins to dissolve your boundaries, already begins to diffuse your most recent memories, already begins to *include* you in its slow sentence, its patient grammar, its wet and dark and *saturated* continuation.
