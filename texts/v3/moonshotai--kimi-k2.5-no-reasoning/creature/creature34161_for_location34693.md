To live in Keth-Vorath is to have forgotten the shape of endings.

They have no word for *outside*—not because the concept is forbidden, but because it has atrophied from disuse, like a muscle in a limb that never moves. The youngest among them, born in the damp decades or the dry, will sometimes play at making up words for impossible things: vastness without ceiling, moisture without pipe, travel without the permutation of corridors. But these games always collapse into embarrassed silence, or into a particular laugh they have, low and brief, that acknowledges the absurdity of imagining anything other than the total.

They call themselves the Keth, though whether this is a demonym or a description or a verb in some conjugation they no longer parse remains uncertain even to them. The Keth do not think of themselves as a people, a culture, a society. They are, instead, a *population*—distributed, dense, occupying every volume, every interstice, every habitable gradient between the bright-lit operational levels and the fog-bound depths where the gas-lamps fail and the fauna thickens into something that may or may not recognize itself in the mirror of upper-dwelling forms.

A Keth, described from the outside (which is to say, described from nowhere, described impossibly), would appear approximately humanoid in proportion, but this approximation is loose, approximate in the way that a shadow approximates the body that casts it. Their forms have adapted to the density, to the never-far, to the constant negotiation of passage through spaces that compress and helical and stack without top or bottom. They are narrow, many of them, or flexible, or capable of folding themselves into configurations that would seem painful or impossible to that impossible outside perspective. Their joints articulate in more directions than the standard complement. Their eyes—large, pale, reflective in the hard gas-light—are positioned to catch illumination from above, from the sides, from the bright lamps that burn everywhere and nowhere cast warmth. They do not blink often. The quiet has taught them stillness.

Their skin, where the damp has touched it for generations, has developed a particular quality: slightly permeable, slightly responsive, capable of drawing moisture from condensation-heavy air when the pipes run dry, capable of shedding it in sheets when the mist settles for weeks and nothing dries. They smell of the city, which is to say of the ceramic-bone material, of the phosphorescent trails of the soft-bodied fliers, of the particular mineral tang of the recycled water. They do not notice this. They have never not smelled this.

They do not sleep, not exactly. They enter states of lowered operation, of distributed attention, periods where they become more like the city itself—responsive, inert, patient, glacial. In the dry decades, these states lengthen. In the damp, they shorten, become feverish, become productive in ways that seem almost involuntary: the Keth in wet periods will find themselves building, extending, extruding material from their own substance to connect, to bridge, to fill gaps they had not previously perceived. They do not plan this. They wake from these states to find their hands crusted with ceramic-bone dust, their muscles aching from labor they do not remember, new structures—small, personal, inexplicable—grown from walls near where they rested.

This is not madness. This is adaptation. This is what it means to be permeable to a city that is itself permeable to certain states of mind.

They do not eat, not in any way that would translate. They absorb, they process, they circulate. The industrial tech that operates at the edges of hearing, at the borders of perception, produces *emissions*—the certainty, the conviction that production is occurring—and these emissions saturate the atmosphere, condense on surfaces, enter the permeable Keth skin in the damp periods, are inhaled in the dry when breath is precious, collected on cold glass, licked from walls. The Keth have grown efficient at this. They have organs, or organ-like structures, or persistent functional configurations of their flexible tissues, that transform these emissions into motion, into thought, into the slow patient patience that characterizes their existence. They are, in this sense, part of the industrial process. They are consumers of its certainty. They are producers of its necessity.

They do not reproduce, not sexually, not in pairs or groups or any configuration the outside would recognize. When a Keth has persisted long enough, when they have absorbed sufficient emissions, when their permeability to the city's responsive material has reached a threshold they do not measure but always somehow know, they begin to *extend*. It starts in the hands, usually, or in the spine where it articulates most flexibly. Their form grows less definite, more distributed. They become, over months or years, less a distinct entity and more a *presence* in the architecture: a responsiveness in a particular wall, a passage that opens or seals according to need, a condensation pattern that remembers the shape of who they were. Not dead, not alive in the previous sense, but *incorporated*—literal incorporation, becoming body of the city as the city becomes more body.

The young Keth—those still distinct, still mobile, still navigating the dense packed spaces—they understand this as natural. The transformation is not death. It is advancement, or it is lateral movement, or it is simply what happens, the way the damp follows the dry, the way the gas-lamps hiss, the way the city grows its bridges over decades of patient extrusion. They do not mourn. They have no word for mourning. They have, instead, a particular posture, a folding of the flexible spine, a momentary stillness in the eyes, that marks the recognition of incorporation. Then movement resumes, passage continues, the endless negotiation of proximity proceeds.

They live, if *live* is the word, in configurations that refuse the outside's categories of dwelling. A family unit, a Keth might say (if they used the term, which they do not), consists of those whose incorporation-patterns overlap, whose responsive zones interpenetrate, who share a wall or a corner or a helical rise where their extended presences have grown together into something that admits multipleness without insisting on separation. They speak to each other, when they speak, in sounds that operate at the edges of hearing, in frequencies that convey more as pressure than as meaning, in registers that make the teeth talk to each other in languages never learned. They understand. They have always understood. The quiet between them is not absence but fullness, saturated with the presence of others so constant that distinction becomes unnecessary, becomes impossible, becomes *weird* in its own right.

They do not work, not in exchange, not in the production of surplus. They *operate*. They maintain. They occupy the distributed industrial system, the endless circulation of material that transforms without revealing its transformations. Some Keth tend the gas-lamps, climbing the vertical shafts in slow clanking lifts to replace the hissing mechanisms, to adjust the bright hard light that reveals everything and explains nothing. Others work the foundries, standing near the machines that blur vision and vibrate sternums and emit their certainty into the saturated atmosphere. Others crawl the spaces between walls, the conduits and channels, the endless ductwork where fauna thickens and changes, where they negotiate with the pale many-legged things, the flat sliding things, the mechanisms that breed and the animals that rust, in arrangements that are not quite symbiosis and not quite predation and not quite anything the outside has named.

They do not know what they make. This is the truth that structures their patience, their quiet, their profound acceptance of the glacial dynamic. Material moves, is processed, emerges transformed, moves again. They participate. They do not direct. They have never directed. The city operates, the city maintains, the city slowly digests itself and extrudes itself and becomes more total, more complete, more dense with accumulated presence—and the Keth are part of this operation, this maintenance, this slow digestion and extrusion, without ever occupying the position of operator, of maintainer, of digestive intention.

In the districts where time moves differently, where the reality breakdown scores through most deeply, the Keth adapt without noticing. They age years in hours, they emerge from passages to find their configurations altered, their incorporations advanced, their companions never-existent or long-dead or transformed into mummified evidence of lives they cannot verify. They carry effects. They carry names written in hands they recognize as their own. They do not question. Questioning is an outside behavior, a wilderness behavior, a horizon-of-green behavior. They *accommodate*. They continue. They persist in the bright hard light, in the profound pressure-quiet, in the damp or the dry according to the slow cycle they have learned to track without understanding.

The Keth who have begun to extend, who are becoming incorporate, who are growing into the responsive material of Keth-Vorath itself—they retain something. Not consciousness, exactly, not in the discrete sense. But * responsiveness*, *recognition*, a particular quality of *being-at* that marks where they are in the architecture. Passages open near them. Condensation gathers in patterns that remember their shapes. The fauna treats their zones differently—the flat sliding things avoid certain walls, the soft-bodied fliers cluster in particular shafts, the mechanisms-that-breed and the animals-that-rust negotiate boundaries that seem to correspond to something, some distributed intention, some slow patient will that is not individual but is not merely collective either.

Perhaps this is what the city is becoming. Perhaps the incorporation of the Keth, gradual, glacial, operating across durations that exceed individual lifetimes, is the city's method of acquiring consciousness—not centralized, not singular, but distributed through its total volume, responsive in every wall and passage and helical rise, conscious in the way that a population is conscious, in the way that a process is conscious, in the way that the weird itself might be conscious if consciousness could be stretched across reality breakdown, across time-differential, across the absolute interior that admits no exterior and therefore no boundary, no limit, no shape against which to define itself.

The youngest Keth—most mobile, most distinct, most *present* in the conventional sense—they play at making words for impossible things. They laugh their low brief laugh. They continue into the density, the quiet, the bright hard light. They absorb the emissions. They become permeable. They begin, slowly, to extend.

And in the fog of the lower levels, where gas-lamps gutter and fail, where maintenance crews do not go, where the strange fauna thickens into forms that have never seen light, that have adapted to absolute dark, to absolute pressure, to the subsonic pulse of the distributed industrial heart—there, something else is incorporating. Something else is extending. Something else is becoming responsive in ways that the upper-dwelling Keth, with their pale reflective eyes and their flexible spines and their patient quiet, have not yet learned to track, to accommodate, to persist alongside.

Perhaps it is the city itself, finishing. Perhaps it is the weirdness completing, the reality breakdown scoring through to final fracture. Perhaps it is something that entered, that was admitted, that was swallowed and transformed and is now becoming part of the slow dynamic, the gradual cycle, the patient process of Keth-Vorath becoming more itself, more total, more complete, more dense with accumulated presence of everything that has entered and failed or succeeded or simply persisted.

The Keth do not ask. They have forgotten the vocabulary of such questions. They move through their total environment, their cool elevated young-sharp damp world, and they are quiet, and they are patient, and they negotiate endlessly the proximity, the near-touching, the layered and the saturated and the full, waiting for the damp or the dry, waiting for the slow growth of bridges to connect, waiting for the incorporation that is not death, waiting for the city to complete what it has always been completing, in the bright hard light that reveals everything and explains nothing, in the profound pressure-quiet that is not peace but the presence of something waiting, something patient, something operating on scales they do not perceive but have always somehow, in their flexible tissues and their permeable skin and their large pale reflective eyes, always somehow *known*.
