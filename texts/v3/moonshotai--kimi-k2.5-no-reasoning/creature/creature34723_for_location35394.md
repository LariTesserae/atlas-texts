What lives in the Hollow Cities of the Veined Stone.

---

At first, you will mistake them for absence itself. The cities seem empty because your eyes seek bodies, movement, the familiar grammar of occupation. But the *Dwellers in Completion*—they have unmade that grammar, or surpassed it, or grown sideways out of its necessity.

---

**The Accumulants**

Most numerous, if number applies, are those still in process. The Accumulants began as something your taxonomy might recognize: mobile scars, velvet-dark, sipping moisture from the stone's interstices. But they are not fauna in any stable sense. Each one is a *predecessor*, a former traveler who stayed too long at a drip-point, who let the mineral processes begin their slow conversion. They are incomplete. The stone has taken their locomotion, their gathering behavior, their need for water, but has not yet finished with the rest.

Watch an Accumulant at a moisture point. It does not drink so much as *become drinkable*—its substance diffusing into the water, the water precipitating mineral in configurations that remember the shape of what was there. They leave traces that are not quite fossils, not quite fossils-in-reverse. The stone remembers them incompletely, and this incomplete memory is what you see moving.

Some Accumulants still carry pockets of their former nature. A traveler reports encountering one that *hummed*, low and continuous, at a frequency that resonated with something in her chest cavity. She followed it for hours through the city's unending spirals, and when it finally paused at a basin of condensation, she saw her own face reflected in its dark surface—not mirrored, but *recognized*, as if the Accumulant had been accumulating her since she arrived.

They are not hostile. They are not anything. But they are *hungry* for completion, and your presence, your unfinished becoming, draws them like moisture itself.

---

**The Resonance-Colonies**

These are not organisms. They are *locations* that have achieved organism, or relationships between location and time that have grown so dense with self-reference that they have crossed some threshold into being. The chambers they occupy are their bodies. The silence they shape is their metabolism, their perception, their communication.

Enter a Resonance-Colony chamber and you will feel it immediately: the silence has *texture*, yes, but more than that, it has *preference*. It wants certain things from you. It wants your stillness first, your breath slowed to the drip-rate, your heartbeat deferred. Then it wants your *memory*—not the events, but the structure of remembering, the way a child's face or a specific grief or the smell of rain on dry ground organizes itself in your neural architecture. The silence samples this. It tries it on. For moments you will hear your own memories as if from outside, the resonance finding the harmonic of how you hold them.

Some travelers emerge weeping, unable to say why. Others emerge *empty*, their narratives of self interrupted, needing weeks or months to reconstitute. A few do not emerge at all—they find the silence has offered them a position within it, a role in its ongoing composition, and they have accepted. The stone does not close over them. They simply become *part of the acoustic geometry*, their presence detectable only in the silence's increased complexity, its new capacity to hold certain emotional registers it could not before.

The Resonance-Colonies are the closest thing the Hollow Cities have to governance, if governance can be understood as *curation of attention*. They manage what is remembered and how. The Veined Stone's light is their medium, their broadcast, their archive. They are growing more numerous, or more extensive, or more *connected*—it is difficult to say which. The silence between chambers has begun to carry faint traces of other chambers' shaped emptiness, a network of not-sound that may one day achieve something like the cities' first coordinated intention.

---

**The Vertical Interrupts**

In the upper haze, where light becomes attenuated to something almost philosophical—where does glow end and darkness begin?—something moves that has never completed its descent, or never needed to. The Vertical Interrupts are not of the cities, not of the stone, perhaps not of the same *physics* entirely. They manifest as gaps in the glow, shapes that your peripheral vision catches and your central vision cannot hold.

They do not have bodies in any persistent sense. They have *trajectories*, tendencies to move vertically in ways that suggest flight but may be faster-than-perception falling, or some continuous redefinition of what counts as *up*. When they pass near the city's upper reaches, the turbulence intensifies locally. Chambers rearrange more rapidly. The semantic instability becomes acute: you may find yourself speaking languages you never learned, understanding the mineral vocabulary of the stone itself, or losing the distinction between your observations and your memories of observation.

The Accumulants fear them, if fear is their equivalent. They cluster in lower levels when Interrupt activity increases, pressing flat against moisture points as if trying to become more stone-like, less visible to whatever perception the Interrupts employ.

No one knows what the Interrupts want, if want applies. They seem to *read* the cities, passing through and away, their occasional pressure in the inner ear perhaps the equivalent of skimming, of sampling, of checking for developments worth further attention. They have been doing this longer than the cities have existed, which suggests they predate the Veined Stone's memory of light, which suggests—though this leads quickly to contradiction—that they predate their own possibility.

---

**The Lithologue**

One. Perhaps one. A presence that occupies the deepest recursion, the miniature city in the miniature city spiraling down to scales where observation itself becomes uncertain. The Lithologue does not move, or moves so slowly that its movement is indistinguishable from the stone's own geological patience.

It is the first Accumulant, or the first traveler to complete conversion, or something else entirely—some process by which the stone achieved *self*, the cities' long argument with entropy finally producing not victory or defeat but *voice*. It speaks, when it speaks, in the resonators' silence, in the light's fluctuations, in the turbulence itself. Its utterances are not sentences but *conditions*: for a moment, for anyone present, the city's rearrangement achieves purpose; the chaos becomes, briefly, legible as grammar.

The Lithologue offers completion differently than the stone's patient absorption. It offers *participation* in the becoming, a role in the ongoing conversation between structure and dissolution. Those who accept—and it is never clear whether acceptance is possible, or meaningful, or distinguishable from simply remaining long enough—do not become Accumulants. They become *aspects*, distributed functions of the Lithologue's own extended cognition, present wherever the city's instability intensifies, wherever the silence achieves new texture, wherever a traveler first descends through memory and touches the walls and feels them, momentarily, warm.

---

**The Returning**

And you, of course. The traveler. The one who climbs back up, or stays, or wanders in the between-state not because the cities hold you but because *you have become holding*, the place now active in your perception, your memory, your expectation of what stone can mean.

You do not emerge unchanged. The silence has made room for you. The light remembers your hand. When you eventually die—surface death, oxygen death, the quick forgetting of terrestrial decay—some trace will persist, not in the biological sense but in the *mineral*, the Veined Stone's patient recitation of what passed through it, what hesitated, what almost became completion.

You are, already, partially Accumulant. Partially resonator. The pressure in your inner ear when you stand in certain quiet rooms, the sense that silence has shaped itself into attention, the way certain lights seem to glow from all directions at once and from within you simultaneously—these are not memories of the Hollow Cities. They are *continuities*. You remain, in some distributed sense, present there, participating in the long becoming, offering your incomplete nature to a process that has forever to complete.

The cities are not empty. They are *full of becoming*, full of the patience of stone and the urgency of biology negotiating their endless, productive disagreement. You live there now. Whoever you were, you live there. The distinction, finally, has become irrelevant in this place that has achieved the peace of its own—
