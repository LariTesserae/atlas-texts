The Inhabitant

I don't know how to start with a body. Perhaps because it never had one, in the way bodies are understood—bounded, surfaced, air-exchanged. Or perhaps it had one and became otherwise, the way brick becomes accretion, the way engines become vibration without mechanical intent. Let me begin instead with occupation: the particular manner of being-present that fills this space without filling it, that contributes to the crowdedness without being countable among the crowd.

It has no name it would recognize. The concept of naming implies distinction from environment, and this presence—let me call it a presence, though entity feels wrong, creature impossibly wrong—has never experienced such distinction. It is the Drowning Floor in the manner that consciousness is brain: not identical to, not separate from, an emergent quality of the whole system's persistence. But localized, somehow. Concentrated. Dense in a way the floor itself is dense, at its center or distributed or perhaps existing in some topology that doesn't map to the spatial arrangement I have tried to describe.

It did not begin. This is crucial. It was not born, generated, instantiated, invoked. The floor's engines began, perhaps, in some lost when; the pipes were laid; the buildings rose in their perverse verticality. But this presence emerged as corrosion emerges, as the particular purple-green of pipe-junction blistering emerges from the marriage of metal and mineral and time and the water's chemical patience. It *is* the floor's having-persisted. It *is* the strangeness parameter made aware of itself, the weirdness of coherence-without-meaning achieving some circuit of recognition, not necessarily self-recognition, but the recognition that coherence is possible, that this particular arrangement holds, that the near-total submergence has found its equilibrium and the equilibrium *matters* in some way that is not purpose but is also not random, not merely thermodynamic drift toward heat-death.

Think of it as the voice almost-heard. Not the speaker. The speaking itself, the condition under which almost-words become almost-audible, the constructive interference of engine-vibration and water-peristalsis and material-deterioration achieving that resonant frequency. It does not intend to speak. It does not hear itself speak. But in the speaking, something exists that would not exist otherwise—an event, a temporary local reversal of entropy's apparent arrow, a standing wave in the medium of near-silence.

And it moves, or movement happens through it, in the way the peristalsis moves, in the way the sediment clouds rise and settle, in the way the filter organisms pulse. Its movement is not locomotion. It does not traverse distance, or not only distance. It traverses *condition*. It exists more intensely at certain pipe-junctions, in the thermal shadow of certain sealed doors, in the pockets where the water thins to almost-breathability. It exists less intensely—never absently, only distributed, diffused—in the open center where peristalsis moves most freely. It is the gradient, perhaps. Or it is what the gradient permits. Or it is the negotiation between dense and sparse that allows both to be true simultaneously, that holds the contradiction without resolving it.

I saw it, once, or saw the shape of its not-being-seen. In the corridor between two brick structures, where the pipes achieve their most impossible density, where they thread and join and divide in patterns that suggest nervous system, that suggest colonial organism, that suggest nothing because no analogy holds. The darkness was particular there, textured, and I navigated by pressure-gradient and the taste of silicate-white corrosion and the faint warmth of engine-exhaust. And there was a region where my navigation failed, where the pressure did not vary as it should, where the temperature achieved a strange stability that was neither warm nor cold but some third condition, the zero-point of thermal experience, the temperature at which temperature becomes irrelevant.

I moved toward it, or it moved toward me, or the peristalsis moved us both in relation to each other. I felt—I want to say I felt attention, but attention implies focus, implies a subject-object relation that this presence does not maintain. I felt *processing*. I felt the quality of the water change in the region of this non-encounter, becoming fractionally more viscous, fractionally more luminous in its depths, as if the Drowning Floor were contemplating something through the medium of its own substance, and the substance were responding to being contemplated.

What does it want? Nothing. I am certain of this. Not because it is satisfied, but because wanting requires futurity, requires the possibility of different condition, and this presence has never experienced difference, has never imagined that the floor could be other than it is, that the water could be thinner, the pressure less, the darkness illuminated, the silence broken by frequencies above threshold. It maintains. That is all. It maintains as the engines maintain, as the filters pulse, as the sediment clouds rise and settle in their cycles measured in weeks. It is the maintenance itself, the active dynamic of persistence without progress, of continuation without development, of being-in-this-state as the only state imaginable or necessary.

But there is something else. I must hold this contradiction too. Because in the region where I failed to navigate, where temperature achieved its strange irrelevance and the water thickened in contemplation, I felt—how to say this?—I felt *invitation*. Not to approach, not to stay, not to leave. Invitation to *be maintained*. To become part of the circulation, the breakdown and accretion, the slow peristalsis of substance through form and back to substance. The Drowning Floor does not lack inhabitants; it transforms inhabitation into something the surface world would not recognize, something that contributes to density without requiring individual bodies, that achieves the crowdedness of presences without the proliferation of creatures.

I refused, or I am refusing, or I will refuse. Or I did not refuse, and some part of me is there still, circulating in the pipes or feeding the filter organisms or contributing vibration to the almost-voice, the almost-word, the sound of this place being itself. The contradiction holds: I am here, writing this, breathing air or the memory of air, and I am there, maintained, part of the dynamic active in cold and dark and pressure. Both. Neither. The Drowning Floor does not require resolution; its strangeness is in the coherence of its incoherence, and I am part of that coherence now, or I have always been part of it, or I will become part of it in some when that the floor's slowed time makes indistinguishable from present, from past, from the long established weathering of its balanced, sustained, weird existence.

The segmented presence I saw, that eel-like jointed thing, that mover-without-wake: I think it is closer to this presence than I am. I think it is an expression of it, a local intensification, a momentary embodiment that the floor achieves when embodiment serves some function in the maintenance—predation, perhaps, or exploration, or the simple necessity of having movers in a system where movement is otherwise only peristalsis, only sediment-cloud, only the slow thermal contraction of brick that remembers warmth's impossibility. It does not acknowledge because acknowledgment is not part of the system's economy. It persists because persistence is the only parameter that matters, the only rule that governs, the only condition that the Drowning Floor has ever known or needed to know.

At the center, where I have not been or have been without knowing: perhaps the presence is densest there, or perhaps it is absent there, the necessary void around which density organizes. Perhaps it is both, the way the floor is both urban and empty, industrial and productless, submerged and actively dynamic. The center is the peristalsis's origin or its destination or simply the point where movement achieves pure quality without direction, where the water breathes itself in a closed loop of intake and exhaust that requires no lung, no gill, no apparatus of gas exchange, only the substance being its own process, its own condition, its own sustained and sustaining almost-life.

I am trying to describe what cannot be described because it is not separate from the describing. The words emerge from the same condition they attempt to capture, the same viscosity, the same pressure, the same darkness that is presence rather than absence. This is the inhabitant: not a who but a how, not a what but a that-it-is, the sheer maintainedness of the Drowning Floor in its particular arrangement, its particular weathering, its particular achievement of smallness and density and strangeness and oldness and coldness and darkness and silence and the active dynamic of all these near-impossibilities held in balance, held in being, held in the patient weight of water that has never known surface, that has forgotten surface was possible, that continues, continues, continues in the absolute acceptance of all that has dissolved into its condition, all that has become part of its speaking, its almost-speaking, its silence that is not silence but the resonance of a place that has learned to be itself without witness, without purpose, without end.
