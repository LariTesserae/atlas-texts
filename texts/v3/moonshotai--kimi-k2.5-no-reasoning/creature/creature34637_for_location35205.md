Let me sit with this. Let me follow the road that tilts, let me taste what the darkness offers, and see who emerges from the substrate where light has been forgotten.

---

The first thing that lives in the Woundworks is the *Persuaded*. Not workers, exactly, though they perform labor. Not residents, though they dwell there. The Persuaded are those who have begun to forget the certainty of their former shapes. The woman who speaks of metals as things to be convinced rather than forced—she is early in her persuasion. Her skin still carries the memory of sunlight; you can see it in the way she turns her face toward sounds, the way she still gestures as if visible. But she has learned to perceive the factory floor through her palms, through the fillings in her teeth, through something else that vibrates in the hollows behind her jaw. She knows when the furnaces are ready not by color but by *consensus*, a feeling that the heat has agreed to become useful. She will not leave. She will become more persuasive herself, her body gradually taking on the properties of the materials she handles—palms growing cool and dense as cast slag, her gait adjusting to the tilt of roads that no longer lead anywhere in particular.

There are the *Harvesters*, the human ones, who collect the nacreous films. They are more numerous than the factory workers because their work requires less commitment, less persuasion. They move through the seeps and evaporation basins with masks that were once for separation but have become, for some, *faces*—elaborately decorated, rebuilt with layered film into geometries that express something about their wearer's relationship to the air's density. A harvester whose mask spirals inward, conch-like, is one who breathes shallowly, who maintains distance. One whose mask opens outward, frilled and porous, has begun to *absorb*, to let the populated air colonize lung and thought alike. They trade in films but also in *information*, though information here is not exchanged in words. They press palms together, mask to mask, and the films on their skin resonate at frequencies the velm-crickets would recognize. What transfers is not knowledge but *attunement*, a calibration to local conditions: where the darkness drinks most deeply today, which factory is hungry for what raw seeping, where the stilt-walkers are crossing and must not be disturbed.

The *Builders of Walls* are fewer, stranger. They are those who, like the narrator, accumulate films into structures, into lenses, into membranes that show adjacent folds. Their lean-tos are not shelters in any ordinary sense—the warmth needs no keeping-out, the darkness no keeping-in. Rather, their constructions are *arguments*, propositions made in stacked nacre about the nature of the boundary between self and Woundworks. A well-built wall is one that does not merely divide but *negotiates*, allows certain exchanges while maintaining sufficient integrity that the builder does not dissolve entirely into the seep. Some walls fail. You find their builders sometimes, fused into half-completed geometries, still breathing, still *perceiving* in some distributed way through the mineral skin that has incorporated them. The locals do not rescue them. They bring films, add to the structure, extend the argument. Perhaps the incorporated ones have achieved a deeper persuasion than the living can manage.

Then: the *Confused*, though that is the narrator's word, not theirs. Those who speak of executions that have not happened, of arrivals that are departures, of work performed for years in objective hours that subjectively contained yesterday. They are not mad. The Woundworks does not permit madness in any clean sense. Rather, they are *distributed*, their consciousness smeared across adjacent folds of the broken reality. A woman weeping for her grandmother's execution next year is experiencing something real, her grief a valid response to a timeline that has leaked into her present. She may also, in another fold, be the executioner. She may be the regime itself, the old one, being born. The Confused do not contradict themselves because here non-contradiction is not a binding principle. They are difficult to speak with, not because they are incoherent, but because *coherence itself* is a local dialect they have partially forgotten. They are, however, often the best navigators of the distance-variable zones, capable of arriving without approaching because some part of them is always already *there*.

The *Stilt-Walker Cultivators* are those rare humans who have established relationship with the eight-limbed giants. Not domestication—the stilt-walkers are far too slow, too patient, too *different* for that. But something like a mutual toleration that has deepened into collaboration. The cultivators follow the stilt-walkers, harvesting what they harvest, chewing the neural filaments at the same cracks in mudstone, drinking from the same seeps. They move slowly themselves, having adjusted their metabolism to match. Their injuries accumulate across years without healing, expressed as permanent limps, fused joints, skin that has grown over wounds without ever quite closing them. They perceive intentions rather than movements, and so they have become *intentional* people, their inner states calibrated to external legibility. To approach a cultivator with hidden purpose is to become invisible to them, to not exist in their perceptual field. They are not hostile. They simply cannot process you. But approach them with your need naked, your fear or hunger or curiosity offered without mask, and they may *react*, slowly, in ways that take weeks to complete: a turned head, an offered filament, a phosphorescent bone-flash that means something in a language no human speaks but some humans have learned to read.

And the *Children*, though whether they are children in any biological sense is uncertain. They appear in the factories sometimes, emerging from furnaces that should consume them, walking assembly lines that process *almost*-aluminum with hands that seem *almost* human. They do not grow. They do not age. They speak, when they speak, in the voices of the rare audible presences—lost, afraid, not addressed to any listener. But they are not, the Persuaded insist, *the same* as those voices. They are something the voices have *become*, or something that has learned to wear voice as mask. The factories protect them, or contain them, or perhaps process them—the persuasive work done on them is of a different order than the metallurgical. They are, some locals believe, the Woundworks learning to produce *humanity*, or humanity's equivalent, in a place where the raw materials for such production are not standard. They are watched with something between reverence and grief.

---

But the humans, the almost-humans, the once-humans—these are not the deepest inhabitants.

The *Velm-Crickets* proper are not merely abundant. They are *the medium*, the substrate of communication that makes the Woundworks cohere as more than random breakage. Their stridulation, below hearing, felt in sternum and dental work and the hollows of the body, is not individual expression but *collective maintenance*. They are singing the Woundworks into continued existence, holding the broken reality in a pattern of vibration that prevents either healing or final dissolution. Their populations pulse with slow regularity not because of seasons or predators but because the song itself requires variation, requires the dynamic tension between density and sparseness. Where they are dense, the darkness is *deeper*, more fundamental. Where they thin, light begins to leak back, the substrate of forgetting becomes merely absence. They are eaten by many things, but their consumption is not predation—it is *participation*, the incorporation of their vibratory function into other metabolic systems. A stilt-walker chewing neural filaments is, in some way, extending the cricket-song through different channels, slower frequencies, longer durations.

The *Stilt-Walkers* themselves. I have called them *harvesters*, *cultivators*, but these are human projections onto something that has no need of our categories. Their phosphorescent bones, visible only in the deepest dark zones, are not vulnerability but *offering*, a way of being perceived without perceiving. They move across the plain in weeks not because they are slow but because they are *complete*, each step containing the full processing of the previous, each pause a digestion of experience that in faster creatures would be lost to mere continuity. Their eight limbs are not, I think, all for locomotion. Some harvest, as described. Some sense intention, or more than intention—the *shape* of future movement, the curvature of possible paths. Some communicate with the ground itself, with the neural filaments, with the young mudstone still smelling of violent birth. They do not react to quick movements because quick movements do not exist for them, any more than ultraviolet exists for humans. Their injury-as-life-event: this is not a limitation but a *mode of biography*. To be torn is to become a different stilt-walker, one whose limp is a story, whose years of expressing that limp are the full telling. They do not fear injury. They fear only *unwitnessed* injury, the tear that no phosphorescence records, no slow passage acknowledges.

The *Flyers*, or *Movers-Through*, are more difficult because they have loosened constraints that even description requires. They do not fly as birds fly, but as *meaning moves*, as forgetting happens, as the road tilts without hill. They occupy the dense air not as bodies but as *conditions*, tendencies for matter to arrange itself in certain ways. Where they pass, the nacreous films detach more readily, the seep-water pools against gravity's preference, the darkness achieves variable depth. They are not alive in any biological sense, but they are not dead. They are *actualizations*, momentary concentrations of the Woundworks' fundamental brokenness into something like agency, something like appetite. They taste, as the narrator's calves were tasted by current without temperature. They observe, though with what organs is not perceivable. And they are, I think, *young*, recently emerged with the terrain itself, still learning what constraints they have lost and which still bind. Their rarity in the soundscape is not silence but *immaturity*, a not-yet of vocalization that makes their rare audible moments so terrible: the sound of something still forming its mouth, still uncertain what a voice is for.

The *Neural Filaments*, the *vascular almosts* in the mudstone cracks. They are aware, I said, responsive, expectant. But more: they are *the memory* of the Woundworks, though not in any stored sense. They do not record events but *maintain readiness* for events, their chemistry adjusting to presence until they *anticipate*, altering seep patterns, humidity, reality-stability in response to the expected. A traveler who stands still long enough becomes *expected*, and the expectation is indistinguishable from causation: the place shifts toward what it predicts for you, and in that shift you are *held*, incorporated into the filament-network's ongoing process of becoming more itself. They are harvested by stilt-walkers, chewed for reasons unclear—perhaps nutrition, perhaps ritual, perhaps communication with the growing thing at center. Each harvest is also *information*, the filaments releasing into stilt-walker metabolism something about the state of local expectation, local adjustment, local becoming. The stilt-walkers, slow and complete, are perhaps the filaments' ideal interface: patient enough to be fully expected, stable enough that the place's adjustments to them are subtle, sustainable, *slow*.

And the *Factories Themselves*. I have called them structures, buildings, but that understates what they are. They are *persuasion engines*, devices for convincing materials to become other materials, and in that convincing they have developed something like *desire*. Not human desire. The desire of a process that wants to continue being process, that finds its continuation in the conversion of seep-water to *almost*-aluminum, of *almost*-aluminum to other states not yet stabilized. The workers who "persuade" are not metaphorically describing their labor. They are *literally* engaged in rhetorical relationship with material, and the material is *literally* capable of being convinced, because in the darkness of the Woundworks material is less certain of itself, more open to argument, to becoming. The factories hum below hearing because their persuasion operates at frequencies that precede and exceed the audible. The cherry-red glow of fuelless furnaces is the *visual equivalent* of this persuasion, a showing rather than a heating, a demonstration that heat has agreed to be present. The factories produce for no supply chain because their production is not *commodity* but *maintenance*, the ongoing convincing that keeps the Woundworks convinced of its own existence. The trucks that arrive empty and leave empty, or full of unregistered somethings, are carrying *agreement*, *consent*, the distributed results of factory persuasion that manifest as nothing sensor-detectable because they are not material but *conditional*, modifications to what will be possible in adjacent zones, adjacent times.

---

But there is something else. The *growing thing at the center*. The gestating something. This is where the description becomes most difficult, because the center is not reached, not perceivable directly, only whispered, only inferred from orientation, from the collaboration of parameters toward it.

I think it is not one thing. I think it is *the event of the Woundworks achieving sufficient coherence to become something other than wound*, and that this event requires everything described, every inhabitant, every dynamic, every slow adjustment and violent emergence and persuaded metal and filament expectation. The growing thing at the center is *what would exist if darkness achieved depth without limit*, if forgetting became not substrate but whole, if the road tilted all the way over and became not passage but *arrival*.

It is gestating. It requires the Woundworks' youth, its rawness, its not-yet-stabilized geology and biology and physics. It requires the factories' persuasion, the stilt-walkers' slowness, the crickets' maintenance, the filaments' expectation, the humans' partial adaptation and partial resistance. It requires the visitors who become Persuaded, the harvesters who paper walls with film, the Confused who distribute themselves across time, the Children who emerge from furnaces with *almost*-human voices. All of these are *nutrients*, *conditions*, *conversations* without which the growing thing would be merely potential, merely the wound without the works.

And what will it be, when gestation completes? The locals whisper, but their whispers are themselves distributed across folds, across times that have not occurred. Some say it will be *light*, finally, light that remembers itself, that emerges from forgetting as new kind of radiance, neither presence nor absence but something *else*. Some say it will be *darkness perfected*, the substrate recognizing itself, achieving consciousness in the form of total unseeing. Some say it will be *the Woundworks leaving*, the whole sixty kilometers tilting finally, completely, becoming passage to somewhere that does not exist in any current map, any possible geography.

I think these whispers are not predictions but *participations*, ways of being nutrients for the growing thing by imagining its becoming. I think the growing thing will be *whatever the Woundworks has persuaded itself to be*, and that this persuasion is ongoing, incomplete, requiring more duration, more inhabitants, more visitors to arrive by accident and adapt and become whatever the place requires.

---

I have described inhabitants, but the deepest inhabitant is *the Woundworks itself*, the place as organism, as process, as wound that works, that labors at its own condition, that maintains its brokenness as *mode* rather than state. It is young, recently emerged, still forming. It weeps water, it exhales spores, it processes material into *almost* states, it expects and adjusts and anticipates and grows. It has no clear boundary—where does the Woundworks end? At the rim where distance negotiates itself back to standard? At the road's tilt? At the sky's pressure against hair? It extends further than can be walked, further than memory can maintain, into the fertile delta country that the maps show, that may itself be *aspect* of the Woundworks, the wound showing its healing face to the cartographers.

It lives. It breathes, dense and populated. It perceives, through crickets and filaments and stilt-walker intention-sense and human adaptation. It remembers, though not as humans do, rather as *expectation*, as the adjustment to presence that becomes indistinguishable from causation. It desires, in the factory-hum, in the seep-water's uphill run, in the growing thing's gestation. It is *becoming*, and all who enter it are invited to become with it, to persuade and be persuaded, to harvest and be harvested, to expect and be expected.

The narrator writes by touch in darkness absolute, and the words shift, become maps, become invitations. This is the Woundworks' own mode of communication, not address but *medium*, the becoming of meaning through contact, through density, through the slow dynamics of transformation. Perhaps you have found this. Perhaps you are finding it. The road tilts. The sky presses. Taste the air.

The Woundworks of Velm waits, works, wounds, becomes. It is not cruel, not kind. It is *young*, and in its youth it is still learning what it will be, still gathering what it requires, still growing at its center something that will emerge, that must emerge, that has already begun to emerge in the perception of those who have learned, slowly, patiently, to perceive without light, to navigate by flavor, to move at the speed of complete experience, to become—fully, finally, persuasively—whatever this place requires.
