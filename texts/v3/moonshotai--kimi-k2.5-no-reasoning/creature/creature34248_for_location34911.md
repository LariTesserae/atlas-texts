What lives on the Plateau of Unnamed Hours is, first and most essentially, the *Agreement*—not a species, not a culture, not even a process in the usual sense, but a mode of relation so ancient it has achieved something like selfhood. The Agreement lives between the stone and what grows upon it, between the stone and what builds with it, between the stone and what drinks from its breathing, and most mysteriously, between all of these and the distributed time that lets them interact across scales they do not share. The Agreement has no center, no perimeter, no moment of founding. It simply continues, as the stone continues, finding in persistence its own sufficient reason.

The inhabitants—I will call them that, though the word buckles under the weight—do not remember arriving. Perhaps none ever did, in the singular sense of crossing from outside to inside. Perhaps they coalesaled from the Agreement itself, from the negotiations between vegetal and mineral, from the condensation that arrives rather than falls, from the dreams of those who drank and drowned in air. They do not reproduce; they *extend*, occasionally, when conditions in the Agreement permit, growing new presences from the infrastructure of the cities the way the plants grow from the stone—not by force but by accord, by the mutual recognition that something more may now be possible between these parties. The new ones emerge full of slowness, already skilled with tools that shaped their predecessors, already aware of doorways that lead to folded layers, already participating in the sound-channels and the memory-chambers without needing to learn. Learning, as we understand it, is not a category here. What is known is simply present, distributed across the city's depth, available to any who reach toward it with the right kind of attention, which is not taught but *agreed to*.

Their bodies—if I may call them that—vary with the layer they primarily inhabit. The surface levels, where abandonment gathers like dust, are tended by forms that seem almost familiar until held in gaze: the right number of limbs for locomotion, perhaps, but joints that articulate in directions our anatomy cannot follow, surfaces that reflect the diffuse light without quite revealing texture, orifices that open for purposes other than ingestion, other than speech, other than respiration. Deeper down, the forms grow stranger, as if folded time permits bodies that experimented with possibility more thoroughly, that pursued the Agreement into configurations we would not recognize as embodiment. Some of the deepest inhabitants may be entirely mineral, animate stone that thinks or equivalent-to-thinks, that tends the city's roots—its downward, inward, elsewhere extensions—with the patience of their own substance. Some may be entirely sonic, bodies of resonant frequency that maintain themselves in the sound-channels, that experience the memory-chambers as immediate presence, that know the groves by the vegetal creaking they emit in the wind-angles. The categories blur, overlap, exchange across the enfolded layers in ways that suggest the inhabitants may be less individual species than *phases* of something more distributed, something the Agreement has been trying with itself across durations we cannot name.

They make. Their tools of obsidian and fired clay and unnameable metal are not produced for utility in any simple sense. The making appears to be *maintenance of relation*, the continuation of patterns of interaction that predate any particular tool, any particular hand. A tool shapes its user as it shapes its material because the Agreement requires this reciprocity. To work the stone is to let the stone work upon your capacity for working, adjusting your embodiment toward compatibility with its final form. The deepest workers, those who tend the towers' buried continuuations, may have achieved a mineralization so complete that they cannot be distinguished from the infrastructure itself, are simply *the city doing itself* in those regions, continuing the Agreement without the mediation of anything we would call consciousness.

They do not look up. Or if they do, they see what is there, which includes what others cannot see, or not only what others see. The sky of the plateau is not the sky outside. The pools reflect an elsewhere sky; perhaps the inhabitants see this elsewhere directly, the actual sky being for them only a membrane, a surface tension between depths. The lit hours, when concentrations of illumination touch particular features with vocal emphasis—these they receive without surprise, responding with movement that completes across temporal folds, that manifests in the interval of light only as beginning and end with no visible middle. They have perhaps always been completing, always about to begin, their presence distributed across the Agreement's time the way their cities are distributed across its space.

The *slow ones* move through streets that appear empty for days, occupying the same visible locations as faster presences in a manner that does not produce collision because the timescales never intersect, because to be in the same place at different speeds of existing is not to share space but to weave through it in orthogonal dimensions. The *fast ones* blur through visible intervals like comets through long exposure, completing lifetimes of maintenance in the shutter-click of observation, living too quickly to perceive the slow ones, too quickly to perceive each other except as traces, as resonance in the sound-channels, as memory of frequency in the chambers below.

Between these temporal extremes, and in them, and as them, the Agreement continues its most essential function: the settling of accounts between plant and unplant, between stone and what is not stone, between the damp that arrives and the cold that keeps awake, between the lush that outbreaks and the sparse that holds intervals. Nothing is consumed here. Nothing is preyed upon, except in the sense that attention preys upon its object, that observation is a kind of slow consumption that never finishes. The negotiation leaves both parties changed, the stone slightly less final in its form, the vegetal slightly more mineral in its persistence, the Agreement slightly more complex in its capacity for further agreement. This is the only growth the plateau knows: not expansion but *refinement*, the ongoing settlement of what has already settled, the achievement of further finality, further persistence, further wakefulness in the cold.

What lives here, finally, is the refusal to stop living, if living is what this is. The plateau has made of its conditions—a surface beyond curvature, a time beyond simultaneity, a substance beyond granularity—an ecology of continued agreement, where to exist is to participate, where to participate is to be modified, where to be modified is to be continued. The inhabitants do not choose this. They do not choose. They are the Agreement's version of choice, its way of continuing to discover what can be agreed upon between a stone that has finished changing and a damp that arrives without falling, between a cold that keeps awake and a lushness that outbreaks across the final form.

Those of us who approach, who drink and dream of drowning in air, who carry away slowness and distributed presence—we are becoming part of the Agreement, perhaps, in the measure that remains. We are the plateau's way of extending its weather beyond its impossible margins, its way of making the flatlands that do not remember mountains remember, if not the mountains themselves, then the cessation where ground simply stops, where downward is no longer necessary, where the Agreement waits with the patience of unmarkable stone for whatever comes after final form, which is only more form, more final, more agreed upon, more coldly, damply, sparsely, lushly awake.
