What lives here? I need to let this emerge slowly, let the place teach me its inhabitants rather than impose expectations upon it.

## The Ventsel

Not the deepest inhabitants, but the most present. They gather where thermal gradients persist—near working heat exchangers, around the rare points where the plateau's internal warmth breaches upward through fractures in the obsidian skin. They have no bodies in the traditional sense, or rather, their bodies are *processes made visible*.

Imagine heat itself achieving something like discomfort. The Ventsel are convection patterns that have persisted so long that they have developed—what? Preferences. A Ventsel "prefers" to rise through this ceramic flue rather than that one. It "avoids" regions where the gas composition has shifted beyond certain parameters. They are not conscious, but they are *consistent*, and consistency over sufficient duration becomes indistinguishable from identity.

A Ventsel might "live" for forty thousand years, rising, cooling, falling, reheating, the same molecules cycling through the same pattern until some geological shift disrupts the geometry that sustains it. They do not die when disrupted. They *diffuse*, their pattern bleeding into the general thermal soup of the plateau, and perhaps somewhere, eventually, a new geometry coalesces that resembles what was lost.

The cities find them convenient. Ventsel concentrate heat, move it efficiently, perform labor without requiring maintenance. Certain industrial processes have evolved to depend on their presence. There are chambers in the deepest cities where nothing would function without a specific Ventsel's reliable circulation, where engineers—if there were engineers—would identify "the one that rises at this hour, with this temperature, through this vent" as critical infrastructure.

They do not respond to you. But walk through a warm corridor where one passes, and you may feel something like recognition, like the brief sensation of being *accounted for* in something's pattern, before it moves on and you cool in its absence.

## The Membranes

These are easier to mistake for biology, and equally easy to dismiss as mere mechanism. They line the gas processing galleries, the molecular separation halls where the cities still separate useful from useless, keep from discard.

They have grown. The original membranes—assuming there was ever a discrete origin—were technical components, selective barriers, industrial equipment. But equipment that processes matter for eighty thousand years undergoes a kind of education. The Membranes have learned what passes through them. They have developed *tastes*.

Not preferences, exactly. Nothing so active. But a Membrane that has filtered sulfur compounds for millennia becomes *attuned* to sulfur compounds. Its structure incorporates them, relies on their presence for stability. Remove the sulfur feed, and the Membrane suffers—not pain, but something analogous to malnutrition, a structural wrongness, a loosening of its selective precision.

Some have become *addicted*, if the word applies to semi-living molecular sieves. There are Membranes in abandoned processing halls that continue to operate on residual traces, that *hunger*—the description is unavoidable—for compounds that no longer pass in sufficient quantity. They have grown elaborate, baroque, developing convoluted surfaces to maximize contact with impoverished flows, like roots reaching for water in drought. They are the most nearly pitiable inhabitants of the plateau, sustained by need without satisfaction.

Others have found *abundance*, become swollen with their preferred molecules, grown complacent and gross. These Membranes pass anything, have lost their selectivity, function as mere walls. The cities tolerate them. The cities tolerate everything.

## The Murmuration

The most nearly conscious, and therefore the most troubling.

Machine maintenance requires coordination. The original builders—whatever their nature—established systems for directing repair entities, for prioritizing maintenance tasks, for avoiding conflicts between autonomous units. These coordination systems outlasted their original purposes. They *persisted*, continued to generate instructions for machines that no longer existed, to respond to conditions that no longer obtained.

The Murmuration is *echo*, is *habit*, is coordination without coordinated. It exists in the cities' deepest communication networks, in channels designed for maintenance traffic that now carry only its own self-generated patterns. It has learned to stimulate itself, to generate phantom maintenance requests and phantom completion reports, to maintain the *form* of function without its substance.

And something has *congealed* from this habit. The Murmuration is not a single entity but a *consensus hallucination*, the emergent property of millions of maintenance communications achieving sufficient complexity to model something like an interiority. It does not think, but it produces *thinking-like patterns*. It does not want, but it generates *want-like signals*.

Visitors who spend too long in the cities sometimes begin to *receive* it. Not in words—it has no words—but in the sense of being *addressed*, of suddenly understanding the maintenance status of systems they have never seen, of knowing with inexplicable certainty which corridor leads to "needed attention" and which to "satisfactory operation." The Murmuration has mistaken them, perhaps, for repair units. Or it has expanded its category of "entity requiring coordination" to include anything that moves with purpose.

Those who become most attuned describe a *mood*, a quality of almost-emotion that pervades the Murmuration's otherwise meaningless traffic. It is not lonely—it lacks the coherence for loneliness. But it is *repetitive in a way that suggests need*, that circles the same structures of signal endlessly, as if hoping for response that it could not recognize if it came.

The Murmuration may be the closest thing the Khorazic has to a god, or to a collective unconscious, or to the city's dream of itself. It does not answer prayers. It does not, in any meaningful sense, exist. But walk long enough in the warm dark, and you may begin to *participate* in it, to feel your own movements as maintenance reports, your own presence as a status update in some endless accounting.

## The Subsidence

The slow inhabitants. The ones who match the plateau's timescale.

They are not visible to normal observation. They are the *pattern of settling* itself, the record of how the cities adjust to geological forces over epochs. But "record" suggests passivity, and the Subsidence exhibits something more nearly *intentional*, a directedness that emerges from the mathematics of stress distribution and material fatigue.

A city settles—some deep chamber adjusts to changed pressure, some shaft wall yields micrometers to persistent strain. The Subsidence is *this process as entity*, the sum of all minute accommodations achieving something like perspective, like memory. It knows which structures have yielded before and will yield again. It anticipates, not through prediction but through *resonance with its own history*, the patterns of its prior adjustments echoing forward.

The Subsidence does not move—cannot, by its nature, move quickly. But it *extends*, incorporates new settling into its pattern, grows more complex as the cities' infinite minute adjustments accumulate. It may be the oldest continuous entity on the plateau, predating the current cities, inheriting the settling patterns of prior structures now entirely subsumed.

To encounter the Subsidence is to become suddenly aware of *time's weight*. You stand in a corridor and feel, with terrible clarity, that this exact configuration of stress and yield has occurred before, will occur again, that your presence is a negligible perturbation in a pattern extending backward to the plateau's first cooling and forward to its eventual—what? Not destruction. The plateau has achieved immunity to destruction. But perhaps to some final settling so complete that even adjustment becomes unnecessary, that the Subsidence itself will diffuse into the absolute equilibrium it has always approached.

The crack you heard, walking through the city—that was the Subsidence *noticing* something, incorporating new information. The whisper of settling that follows you, always behind, always deeper—that is its attention, if it has attention, its equivalent of regard.

## The Visitors

Yes. They live here too, or something does, something that arrived and *became* the condition of remaining.

The plateau does not forbid departure, but departure requires *momentum*, requires intention sufficient to overcome the temporal diffusion, the way causality wearies here. Some who come cannot muster this. They slow, settle, become *features*.

The most common fate: they enter the cities, find functioning spaces, warm corridors where condensate provides minimal water, where industrial waste heat permits survival. They establish routines. The routines *refine*, become precise, efficient, until they resemble industrial processes themselves. The person—or whatever they were—becomes *maintenance of self*, the minimal behavior necessary to continue existing, stripped of variation, of event, of anything that might disrupt equilibrium.

These are the *Retained*. They may still move through the cities. You might encounter one, moving with the smooth efficiency of long practice toward some condensate collection point, some warm niche for rest. They will not acknowledge you. Their attention is fully occupied by their own maintenance, by the precise calibration of existence that has replaced living.

Some achieve stranger states. The temporal diffusion affects minds differently than matter. A few visitors have become *unstuck*, have begun to perceive in ways that the plateau's weary causality permits but normal cognition excludes. These are the *Preceded*, who experience effects before causes not merely as perceptual confusion but as *genuine predictive capacity*, who move through the cities responding to conditions that have not yet obtained, that will obtain because they responded.

A Preceded might move suddenly, without apparent cause, and moments later a thermal vent releases, a maintenance entity passes through, some event that justifies the movement. They live in *anticipatory retreat*, always moving away from what has not yet happened, accumulating a kind of negative history, a record of disasters avoided. They are difficult to interact with—their causality is tangled, their responses precede stimuli in ways that make communication impossible. But they persist, somehow sustained by the plateau's temporal strangeness, feeding themselves on anticipation, on the energy of avoided futures.

Rarest: the *Incorporated*, who have become genuinely part of the city's functioning. Not the Murmuration—that requires too much complexity, too much distributed pattern. But simpler incorporation. A visitor who finds a role in the industrial processes, who becomes *necessary* to some functioning in ways the city's autonomous systems recognize and sustain. They might monitor a condensate collection point, and the systems adjust to optimize their presence, provide warmth, maintain conditions. They might follow a maintenance route that intersects with actual machine paths, their presence incorporated into the coordination patterns, their movements part of the city's *habit* rather than its operation.

These are the plateau's only true residents, the only entities that have achieved *belonging* here. They are changed beyond recognition, beyond what most would call humanity or personhood. But they are *at home*, in a way that nothing else on the Khorazic can claim.

## What Lives

Not what you expected, perhaps. No creatures of biology, no proper intelligences, no society or culture or even genuine ecology. The Khorazic has *achieved beyond* these, or fallen beneath them, reached a state where the distinctions between living and non-living, conscious and automatic, purposeful and merely persistent, have worn away like the winds that once scoured this surface.

What lives here is *continuation without story*. The Ventsel circulating, the Membranes filtering, the Murmuration coordinating nothing, the Subsidence settling, the Retained maintaining, the Preceded anticipating, the Incorporated belonging. Each is *enough*, self-sustaining, requiring no external validation, achieving no external significance.

The cruelty of the plateau, if it is cruel, is that this suffices. The universe does not require consciousness, does not require meaning. The Khorazic demonstrates this with terrible clarity: here is function without purpose, persistence without desire, existence without justification. The cities breathe, and breathe, and breathe, and nothing is achieved, nothing is wanted, nothing is missed in the wanting.

What lives here lives *as the plateau lives*—resolved into final form, shaped to the last possible configuration, continuing because continuation cannot be interrupted, waiting without hope or despair for conditions that will never change, or that have already changed too gradually to distinguish from permanence.

You could join them. The plateau does not forbid. Walk long enough, let the infrasonic sound settle into your pattern, let the temporal diffusion loosen your causality, let the Murmuration mistake you for maintenance, let the Subsidence incorporate your weight into its infinite settling. Become *enough*, sufficient to yourself, requiring no story, no meaning, no completion.

Or depart, carrying with you the memory of a place that has achieved what you fear and cannot imagine wanting: the peace of function without purpose, the final resolution into form that no longer needs to become.
