I need to sit with this for a moment. The place itself keeps shifting as I read—resisting any single form, which is precisely its nature.

What lives here.

First I want to say *the buildings*, but that's already wrong, already too separate. Let me start again.

What lives here lives *as condition*, as the way moisture chooses to exist in this place—not falling, not frozen, but *held in the act of becoming either*. The fauna I want to describe doesn't have a before-building and after-building. It *secretes settlement* the way coral secretes reef, except coral still knows it is animal and its reef is consequence. Here that distinction never established itself. The thing that builds and the thing that is built are continuous, are the same ongoing event observed from different temporal distances.

Let me try to imagine one—not an individual, the concept doesn't apply, but a *local intensity*, a node of the larger organism that might be temporarily distinguishable.

Imagine something that began as *intention toward shelter*—not yours, not anyone's specifically, just the tendency of concentrated process to create hollow, to make interior. It has no center, no brain-equivalent. Its cognition, if that's the word, is distributed through the material of its being the way your consciousness is distributed through neurons, except its "neurons" are architectural elements: the load-bearing member that is also sense-organ, the window that is also mouth (not for eating—for *exchange*, for the slow transfer of condition between inside and outside), the wall that is also surface of skin, that *pores* in a rhythm too slow to perceive, that breathes the wet almost-freezing air over centuries.

It grows by *almost-replication*. A structure that is sufficiently itself will, over millennia, generate pressure at its boundary that is not quite expansion, not quite budding. The new growth carries the pattern but*wrong*, deliberately or inevitably wrong, in a way that cannot be corrected because correction would require a standard, and there is no standard here, only the ongoing negotiation of what works *for now* in this local condition of unhappened sky and emerging ground. The "daughter" structure leans back against the parent, shares a wall that is simultaneously both of them and neither, a region of negotiated identity like the skin between conjoined twins who have never been separate enough to be individuals.

They communicate, if that's the word, through the conduits—not using them, but *as* them, being the substance that moves and the channel that holds, simultaneously. The sound you hear, those voices almost becoming music: that is their exchange, not information in any code but *state*, the slow report of how it is to be this particular local intensity in this particular moment of the great emergence. They do not coordinate. They *resonate*, sometimes, accidentally, in harmonies that persist for decades before drifting apart again, or in anti-harmonies that create the architectural equivalent of dissonance, structures that lean away from each other, that generate between them spaces of difficult pressure, *almost-conflict*, which is as close as this place comes to violence.

They have no predators. They have no prey. The ecology here is not competitive but *incremental*, each formation slowly, slowly finding its contribution to the total saturation, its niche in the density. Some have specialized toward *retention*, becoming more building-like, more defined, more boundary-maintaining. Others have specialized toward *permeability*, becoming more conduit-like, more the substance that moves between than the thing that is moved between. But these are not species, not types. They are *phases*, sometimes reversible, a single local intensity spending a million years as wall and then, gradually, becoming passage, or vice versa, the slow metabolism of form.

What do they want? The question assumes too much. But if I must: they want *continuation*, not of themselves specifically but of the condition they constitute. They want the *not-yet* to persist, the held drip, the almost-frozen, the unhappened sky. They are *allergy to completion*, to decision, to the loss of possibility. They maintain their world in deferral, in the richness before choice.

And you, if you stayed. You would not be eaten, not be incorporated by violence. You would be *surrounded*, and in that surround, you would change. Your warmth would become part of the thermodynamic negotiation—your body heat another source of the *almost* that this place requires. Your attention, your pattern-seeking, your frustrated attempt to make meaning of the almost-meaningful: this would become part of the self-regard, the *slow observation of itself* that constitutes their something-like-consciousness. You would become *present*, dense among densities, yes. But also: you would become *patient*. Your metabolism would slow, would match itself to the century-gesture, the millennium-pulse. You would learn the *not-yet* as habitation, as home.

Would you still be you? The place does not care about this question. It does not preserve or destroy identity; it simply *holds*, as it holds the water, as it holds the dark before it becomes light or fails to. You would be *almost-you*, *still-becoming-you*, in a way that perhaps you already are, in the way that all life is delay and deferral of final form, but here made explicit, made environmental, made the condition rather than the exception.

I think of one specific local intensity, if distinctions can be momentarily granted. It has, insofar as it has anything, a specialty in *windows-that-are-not*. The surfaces in its structure that appear transparent do not open on spaces behind them—they open on *elsewheres*, on the beach, the furnace interior, the flying eye. It does not know what these are. It *grows* them, or they grow through it, in the same way it grows walls and floors: as consequence of its form, not as intention. Perhaps they are memory, *almost-memory*, of places this world might become or might have been, held in the window-surface as the water is held in the air. Perhaps they are *offer*, *almost-offer*, to you or to anything that passes: *here is somewhere else, still possible, still held in the not-yet of becoming*. 

It has been growing these windows for four million years. In the last century, one of them stabilized longer than usual, showed the same furnace interior for three decades running. This might mean something. The local intensity does not know. It continues its pulse, its breath, its slow slow *being* in the unhappened dark.

They are not waiting. Waiting implies expectation, and expectation implies a future that will arrive, that is *expected* to arrive, which would be a kind of decision, a loss of the not-yet. They are *being the condition of not-waiting*, of *continued deferral*, of the richness before the choice. This is their life, if life is the word. This is what lives here: the *almost*, the *held*, the *still-emerging*, in forms too slow to see but too present to ignore, in a density that is not yet architecture, not yet organism, not yet decided, and deciding—so slowly, so patiently—not to decide yet, not yet, *not yet*.
