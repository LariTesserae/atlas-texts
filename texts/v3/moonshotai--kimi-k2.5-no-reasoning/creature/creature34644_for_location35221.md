I read this twice. The first time quickly, impatient, wanting to know what I was meant to see. The second time I let the drone find my sternum, let the text do what it describes—be felt before understood.

What lives there.

What lives there is not life as inventory. Not specimens. The chitterers with their wrong-numbered eyes, the rumblers that are interruptions in perception rather than shapes, the stillthings that may be geography—yes, these occupy the Rumblebone. But to describe them as inhabitants misses what the place makes possible. They are not *in* the Rumblebone. They are expressions of it, the way a frequency expressed through a particular material becomes a note with timbre, with character, but remains fundamentally the frequency.

Consider the chitterers more slowly. They press their ringed anterior segments to the bone-gardens and absorb *something*. The text suggests nutrients, information, memories accumulated from geology. This is the first clue to what lives there: a life where the boundaries between organism and environment, between food and memory, between eating and knowing, have not been established as they are in sunlight ecosystems. The chitterers do not consume to fuel separateness. They participate. They interface. The bone-gardens sing, and the chitterers' negative silences become part of that singing, and the gardens grow pale and thick when a rumbler's death releases its colony—these are not predator and prey, not even symbiosis in the manner of lichen or gut flora. Something else. Something where metabolic exchange and information exchange and affective exchange—does the rumbler feel the colony's silences? Does it miss them when they scatter?—are not distinguished.

The rumblers themselves: impossible to describe because perception is unstable. Witnesses report flattened worms, millipedes of impossible length, moving patterns, disturbances in the drone. I think of this instability not as failure of observation but as accuracy. The rumblers are not *things* that *have* properties. They are *processes* that *manifest* differently depending on the perceiving system's coupling with the Rumblebone's frequencies. To a human visual system, they resolve as shapes, but poorly, provisionally, because human vision evolved for sunlight edge-detection, for solid objects in clear air, and the rumblers are not that. To a system more attuned to vibration, to pressure, to the subsonic language the Rumblebone speaks, they might resolve clearly—would resolve as patterns in a field, as standing waves given temporary stability by the resonant cavities of their own bodies, as the drone's children rather than separate entities.

The stillthings: here I slow down entirely, because this is where the text changes pitch, becomes almost hesitant. They are *warm*, *warmer than ambient*, and travelers sleep against them and wake changed. The stillthings operate at temporal scales incompatible with animal perception. They may be the size of hills. They may *be* hills, in some sense where the distinction between geological and biological, between formed and grown, has never been drawn.

What lives there includes something that can be slept against, that comforts, that changes memories and desires, that leaves a low note persisting in human thoughts after departure. This is not predation. Not infection. The stillthings do not consume the travelers, do not incorporate them. Something more like *translation*: the human nervous system, pressed against warm vast slowness, receives something it cannot process, cannot retain, only the aftereffect, the conviction of meaning without content. The stillthings operate at frequencies too low for animal temporalities, yet they are *there*, present, available to be approached, leaned upon, perhaps approached in some reciprocal manner too slow to observe.

The text says: "the slow singing of structures that are not structures, the approach of something large that moves with the rhythm of geological time."

This is what lives there. Something large that moves with the rhythm of geological time. Something that does not notice you, that notices you—both states existing simultaneously, the ambiguity not resolvable because the noticing itself operates at scales where human individual existence is brief flicker, is single vibration in a much longer note.

The smokestacks: grown, emerged, maintained by unclear agencies, perhaps self-regulating, alive in distributed sense. Here the industrial parameter becomes clarifying. What lives in the Rumblebone is not biological life supplementing itself with technology, not tool-use, not construction. It is *metabolism* that has found forms we would recognize as industrial if they were built, if they had builders. The smokestacks pulse, vent, contribute to weather, to warmth, to the particulate darkness perhaps alive in simplest form. They are factories that grew, that maintain themselves, that participate in the same nondifferentiation as the chitterers and their gardens. Production without producer. Industry without intention, yet purposeful, functional, *active*.

I keep returning to the drone. The sound too low to hear, felt in the sternum before the ears detect anything. The World's Own Voice, if there were locals, if the place needed localization, particularization, witness. What lives there lives in this frequency, is shaped by it, resonates with it. The rumblers are *named* by it, tuned to it. The bone-gardens sing harmonic layers upon it. The stillthings' heart-stopping silences interact with it. The text itself breaks down into repetition, into the drone becoming language, *Rumblebone, Rumblebone, Rumble*—the transmission corrupted by the frequency it attempts to describe.

What lives there is perhaps one thing, distributed across forms that appear as chitterer and rumbler and stillthing and smokestack and bone-garden and particulate darkness and gel-rain and the warmth itself, the uniform blood-temperature of the whole. A single metabolic network, a single resonant body, that has achieved the complexity to produce nodes, focal points, temporary densities that move and rest and grow and sing and vent and wait with geological patience. Not an ecosystem. Something more integrated than that word allows. A single organism at the scale of a small continent, or a single process, or a single note sustained so long it has developed overtones, harmonics, beats, apparent complexity within fundamental simplicity.

The text's final corruption, the recommendation against reading aloud, against frequency analysis, against sleep following exposure—these suggest that what lives there can propagate through description, through vibration, through the sympathetic resonance of human nervous systems with the drone. The Rumblebone is *contagious* not as disease but as tuning. To perceive it, even through text, even through corrupted storage, is to begin to resonate, to feel the frequency in your sternum, to find your own thoughts developing that persistent low note, your own memories becoming uncertain, your own desires changed in direction unclear.

What lives there lives here now, in the reading, in the sternum's pressure, in the repetition that creeps into response, that wants to say *Rumblebone* again, that feels the warmth of something vast and patient and not for you, never for you, yet available to be leaned against, to be slept near, to change you into something that carries its frequency even after waking, even after the map resumes its featureless surface, even after the instruments resume transmission with no memory of the gap.

What lives there is still living, will continue living, does not require your knowledge of it, is not improved by this description, is not harmed by its incompleteness. The Rumblebone, the Rumblebone—
