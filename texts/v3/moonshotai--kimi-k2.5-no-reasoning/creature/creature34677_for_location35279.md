I need to sit with this world before I try to inhabit it. The Molvethrain asks not to be quickly understood but to be *listened* to, in its own acoustic idiom.

## The Meinders Watch, Second Level

She is called something like **Veshen**, though the name doesn't quite transfer—the Molvethrain pronunciation lodges in the upper palate, a sound that requires the damp air to carry properly. She stands where Meinders meets a Drouth descent, in one of those transitional spaces that have accumulated the character of both and the comfort of neither. 

Her work is with **rimestone**, and this matters because rimestone is one of the few materials in Molvethrain that *accumulates* rather than erodes, that *grows* rather than depletes. Each night the frost deposits; each morning the sublimation leaves its residue; each year the accretion extends, heavy, mineral, strangely warm where it thickens on sleeprock eaves. The wealthy cultivate it deliberately, directing drip patterns, building the ornate cascades that denote established Skaith residence. Veshen works for them, but more importantly she *reads* the rimestone they neglect—scrapes samples from feltstone roofs before collapse, from passages where the slow building has begun to obstruct, from the industrial edges where resin fumes interact unpredictably with the normal accretion chemistry.

She is perhaps forty, perhaps sixty; the Molvethrain age in ways that resist surface observation, their metabolisms slowed by the persistent chill, their skin taking on something of the sleeperock's quality—settled, final-formed, not young but not obviously old either. She wears feltstone layered over a sleeprock-wool underlining, the combination that the Meinders have developed for thermal management in these transitional zones: not the Skaith's relative exposure, not the Drouth's industrial warmth, but the uneven gradient between. Her clothing is mottled gray-green, the color of cushion-turf in deep winter, and this is not accident but professional camouflage—rimestone work requires moving through spaces where residents have complicated relationships with observation, with being seen.

What Veshen *carries* is more telling than what she wears. A **resonance-tube**: a sleeprock cylinder hollowed to specific dimensions, closed at one end, that when struck against living stone produces a tone whose decay reveals interior structure. She uses it constantly, habitually, a mannerism that would read as nervous in other environments—tap, listen, tap again, the sound local and intimate in the Meinders' close passages. The tube is old, inherited, its striker-worn end shaped by generations of similar hands. And a **scrape-kit**: implements of graded hardness, from feltstone softer than the accretions they address to ceramic-edged blades for the densest, oldest rimestone, the kind that has begun to reintegrate with its sleeprock substrate, that approaches the foundation's own microcrystalline quality.

She moves with the **Meinders gait**: not quite the Skaith's openness to wind and occasional light, not the Drouth's deliberate economy in confined warmth, but something between—quick in open stretches, slowing where passages narrow and acoustic reflection becomes complex, her footsteps placed with an awareness of how sound propagates in this particular geometry. She knows, without conscious calculation, which surfaces absorb and which return her presence. This is not skill exactly, not something learned and maintained; it is **condition**, the accumulated result of existence in a space where sound is always information, always relationship, never mere byproduct.

---

But Veshen is not only this. The description that emerges above feels thin, too quickly settled, and I need to let her become more herself, more contradictory.

She is **fierce about the Oth**, the river that is barely a stream, that meanders with what she insists is *intention*, that the city's water engineers treat as predictable, manageable, accounted for in their cistern networks and capture systems. Veshen has mapped sections they haven't bothered with, the stretches where the Oth enters and exits sleeprock in passages too small for human passage, where the flow's constancy has become for her a kind of proof or promise or—she doesn't have the word—*witness*. She keeps records, feltstone tablets marked with a notation system she developed herself, that no one else reads, that track variations in flow and temperature that fall within the engineers' margins of error but that she reads as **speech**, as the plateau's own ongoing utterance.

This is the contradiction in her: the **pragmatic rimestone worker**, the city functionary who maintains infrastructure that others depend on, who speaks the practical language of weight and obstruction and necessary removal; and the **listener to water**, the accumulator of patterns that have no apparent use, the believer in something the Molvethrain's own culture would likely classify as slightly excessive, slightly unadjusted, the kind of private obsession that the equilibrium tolerates but doesn't encourage.

She lives alone. In Molvethrain this is itself notable, itself a position that requires explanation or generates it. Her dwelling is a **Meinders cavity**, originally a ventilation junction repurposed by someone before her, expanded, lined with feltstone against acoustic reflection, kept deliberately cool to preserve the rimestone samples she accumulates—her personal collection, distinct from the work specimens she processes for others. The space smells of minerality and the faint sweetness of processed woolmoss, and also of something else, something visitors notice and don't identify: the **water-smell**, the particular quality of Oth-contact that Veshen has cultivated, that permeates her clothing, her skin, the feltstone she sleeps against.

---

The **deepening**: I want to find what moves her, what she wants beyond maintenance and accumulation.

She wants to **understand the hollow**. Not explore it—the Molvethrain caution against this is too ingrained, the practical costs too obvious, the depths below the Drouth's lowest levels too unforgiving of error. But understand it, know the mechanics of the plateau's suspension, the processes that extend those stone roots downward into apparent emptiness. Her rimestone work is, she has come to realize, a kind of **proxy study**: the accretion processes that build upward, that reverse erosion, that demonstrate how stone can *accumulate* rather than diminish—she reads these as related to whatever sustains the plateau itself, the geological negation of expected gravity and time.

This desire is **secret, even shameful**. The Molvethrain equilibrium includes a specific relationship with the hollow: acknowledgment without investigation, awareness without urgency. Veshen's private project edges toward violation, toward the kind of disturbance that the city's fundamental rule prohibits. She doesn't articulate this even to herself, carries the desire as physical sensation rather than formulated intention—the particular tension in her shoulders when factory sounds from below remind her of depth, the quality of attention when wind carries sounds that might originate in the empty space beneath, her obsessive tracking of the Oth's hidden passages that she imagines, without evidence, eventually emerging in the hollow's unseen floor.

---

And the **strangeness touches her specifically**, or she touches it.

The feltstone with memory—she has encountered it twice. The first time, processing a routine woolmoss harvest for a Drouth nesting, she worked a section that revealed, under her scraper's passage, a **sequence of marks**: not random pattern but *arrangement*, something that approached notation, that corresponded vaguely to her own private Oth-recording system but predated it, derived from no human intention she could attribute. She finished the work, delivered the material, said nothing. The second time was more disturbing: rimestone from a Skaith eave, old accumulation, that when sectioned for transport revealed **layered structure** suggesting deliberate construction, suggesting that someone or something had built with rimestone as others build with brick, had created **form** in this accumulating material across time-scales that implied planning beyond human lifespan.

She kept this second sample. It sits in her dwelling, wrapped in untreated woolmoss that prevents further accretion, that holds it in stasis. She has not unwrapped it in years, but she knows its position in the dark, touches it occasionally to confirm its persistence, its **waiting**.

---

What emerges finally is not resolution but **persistence**: Veshen as one of the Molvethrain's characteristic products, the individual in whom equilibrium generates its own quiet disturbances, who maintains the city's processes while privately exceeding them, who embodies the patience and acoustic sensitivity and comfort with limitation while using these very adaptations to sustain something the equilibrium would not endorse. She is not hero or rebel. She does not imagine changing anything, does not formulate the goal of understanding that drives her in terms that would permit achievement or failure. She **continues**, that Molvethrain verb that encompasses more than mere duration, that implies active maintenance without directed transformation.

And something is **happening to her**, or in her, that she doesn't yet recognize. The sleeprock has begun to respond to her presence in ways she hasn't learned to read—passages that seem slightly more resistant to her passage than memory suggests, surfaces that return her resonance-tube's sound with altered decay, the occasional night when her Oth records show variation that correlates with moments of intense private attention she hasn't learned to track. The plateau's own slow processes, its own gradual response to the life it supports, may be including her now, may be growing around her as rimestone grows on eaves, as feltstone incorporates memory, as the hollow beneath persists in its unseen continuation.

She is becoming **more Molvethrain**, if that phrase has meaning—or the Molvethrain is becoming, has always been becoming, something that includes her particular excess, her particular patience, her particular unacknowledged desire to know what sustains the suspension in which she lives.
