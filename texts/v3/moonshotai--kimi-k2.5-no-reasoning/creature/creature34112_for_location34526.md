What lives in the Sunken Choir of Keth-Vorath is not, finally, a who or a what that can be separated from the question of *how it lives*—which is densely, which is wetly, which is in a mode of presence that accumulates rather than individuates. But let me try to speak of them as they allow themselves to be spoken of, knowing that any description will be a kind of translation from a language that has no spoken form, performed for ears that cannot hear the frequencies of their actual communication.

---

**The Choristers Themselves**

They have no name for themselves that can be carried out, no more than water has a name for wetness. The term "Chorister" is imposed, a violence of taxonomy, but it gestures at something true: they are singers, though their singing is not heard. Their vocal apparatus—visible on the rare occasions when one opens its mouth to consume the luminous lichen-gardens, or to receive moisture directly from the weeping stone—contains structures that seem designed for frequencies below earthquake, above the upper threshold of pain. They do not use these to speak to each other. The subsonics are *emission*, not message, a byproduct of their metabolism perhaps, or perhaps a more fundamental process, the sound of their existence rather than communication about it.

Their bodies, when you can fix them in attention, suggest adaptation to pressure rather than time. The multiple joints of their hands allow configurations that human fingers cannot achieve—grip patterns for holds that exist in spaces slightly folded from the apparent, touches that transmit information through the elaborate skin-structure of their palms, which is ridged and whorled in patterns too fine for human eyes, each individual's dermatoglyphics a kind of text they can read in each other through simple contact. The largeness of their heads houses sensory equipment we do not share: organs for detecting the density-storms before they manifest, for navigating the recursive well-shaft, for perceiving the layered presence of each other as ordinary and unremarkable as human sight perceives color.

They are short, yes, compressed like their architecture, but this is not mere evolutionary pressure toward smaller body plans in confined spaces. Their scale is *appropriate*. They fit the streets they built, the streets their ancestors built, the streets that built themselves through endless sedimentary accretion. A taller being would be wrong here, would catch on corners, would disturb the water's careful falling, would cast shadows that interfere with the light's complex self-interaction. They are the right size for Keth-Vorath as worms are the right size for soil, as certain pressures are the right magnitude for deep ocean.

Their age is not readable. Infant Choristers exist—wrapped in oiled leather, carried in the baskets, tended with gestures of elaborate gentleness—but the transition points are unclear. The infants seem sometimes to be carried by forms too similar in scale to be adult, too numerous to be siblings. Perhaps they are raised collectively. Perhaps the distinction between individual and population that humans assume is not operative here. The "infants" may be developmental phases rather than persons-in-becoming, or they may be something else entirely, temporary local condensations of the general wet luminosity that sometimes achieves sufficient density to need carrying, to need tending, to participate in the gesture-dialogues with hands not yet fully jointed.

Their faces, when visible, are smooth in a way that suggests either extreme youth or extreme age, or neither—surfaces without the human topography of expression, mobile not in muscular contraction but in something like *arrangement*, the features settling into configurations that mean, that communicate, that are read by the others with an immediacy that makes the human reliance on facial expression seem crude and slow. They do not seem to have expressions in our sense. They have *states*, *postures*, *resolutions*, each stable for moments or hours or the indefinite duration of their attention, each readable as text in a language that has no grammar but infinite vocabulary.

---

**The Ones Who Carry**

There is a function or a phase, you begin to understand, or perhaps simply a *present tendency of the population*, that involves carrying. Water-skins. Baskets of luminescent matter. The infants. Tools that emerge from the well, reverently received. These carriers move with purpose that seems to exceed individual intention—they navigate the turbulence, the sudden topological shifts, with the ease of water finding its channel, and other Choristers make way with gestures that read as deference or facilitation rather than mere collision-avoidance.

The carried objects are not possessions. Nothing in Keth-Vorath is possessed. The water-skins are passed, emptied, refilled, set down in locations where the next passer will find them useful. The baskets circulate. The tools from the well are carried to specific locations, specific moments, specific configurations of need that the carriers seem to perceive ahead of their arrival. This is not communism in any human sense; it is more fundamental, more like the way blood circulates, the way water moves through stone, the way light finds its paths in the dense luminous atmosphere. The carriers are *currents* in a larger flow, participating in distribution that has no distribution points, in logistics that require no coordination because they are not, finally, separate acts.

Some carriers move in patterns that suggest ritual without religion, or religion without belief. They traverse specific paths at specific densities of population, specific intensities of light, and other Choristers align to their passage in ways that open passage, that create temporary corridors through the general press. These may be elders, or specialists, or simply *the ones currently carrying* in a way that has become temporarily central to the city's ongoing. You cannot identify them by appearance. Any Chorister might be carrier, might be carried, might be the path itself that others open.

---

**The Well-Dwellers**

Not all who live in Keth-Vorath live in its streets, its terraced chambers, its compressed interstices. Something dwells in the well, or the well dwells in something, and the distinction is as unstable here as elsewhere.

They emerge: the tool-shaped objects, the body-shaped objects. The Choristers receive these with reverence, but reverence without surprise, as one receives breath, as one receives the next moment's existence. The body-shaped objects are not dead, not alive in any category human medicine recognizes. They are *potential*, perhaps—Choristers not yet condensed into street-dwelling density, or Choristers that have completed some cycle and returned to liquid potential for reconfiguration, or something else that participates in the life of the place without being identifiable as individual lives.

The well itself, approached, induces recursive seeing. This is not illusion, not trick of light on water. The well's depth contains itself, each level of descent revealing another level of watchers around another depth, and the watchers at each level are reading you with the same attention you direct downward. They do not prevent approach. They do not invite it. They are *present at the well*, as the well is present in them, and your vertigo is a kind of failed translation, human dimensional cognition attempting to parse a topology that is not malformed but *extra-formed*, not impossible but * differently possible*.

The luminosity that feeds the gardens rises from the well, or is generated by its depths, or is the well's attention turned toward surface in a form perceivable as light. The storms that compress into alley-rooms sometimes rise from it too, released upward to expend their concentrated weather against the general wet atmosphere, brief local intensities that the Choristers walk around with practiced unconcern. The well is source and destination, origin and recycling point, the place where the city's density achieves sufficient concentration to become something else, something that feeds back into the density, the flow, the endless silent song.

---

**The Faunal Companions**

They keep nothing, yet companionship of a kind exists in forms that bypass the category of domestication.

The wet-furred things that pass through walls—or through attention-gaps, which may be the same phenomenon described differently—are not mammals in any human sense. Their fur is more like the fibrous algae that the Choristers weave, a kind of organized dampness that maintains coherence in shapes suggestive of body. They appear at moments of particular population density, particular light configuration, particular phases of the well's emission, and the Choristers' gestures toward them read not as acknowledgment of pet nor as management of pest but as *recognition of equal participant*, something that belongs to the general flow in its own unreadable way.

The shelled creatures clinging to weeping stone are harvested, yes, for nacre that becomes inlay, tool-edge, the mineral-impregnated surfaces of hardened wood. But the harvest is *negotiated*. You see Choristers at certain stones making gestures of extended duration, complex postural sequences, before reaching to remove a shell; and sometimes they do not reach, move on, leave the shell in place. The criteria for harvest are not humanly accessible, but the practice suggests communication, relationship, something more than extraction from environment.

The avian-presences, the something that flies in patterns too dense for individual bodies, too coordinated for flocks—these may not be animals at all, may be a manifestation of the population's collective movement seen from certain angles, or may be the city's own respiration given temporary visibility. They materialize in the upper spaces, the rare vertical clearings between compressed construction, wheeling in configurations that suggest writing, suggest music made visible, suggest the translation into flight of the subsonic frequencies that permeate the place. Then direct attention, and find only ordinary wings, ordinary rain-wet feathers, a bird or birds that do not exceed perceptual capacity. The abundance was real, the wheeling pattern was real, but the *form* of its reality shifts under observation, becomes what can be seen rather than what was present.

---

**The Returned, The Exiled, The Translated**

Not all who enter Keth-Vorath leave. Not all who leave depart entirely.

You sense them sometimes, the ones who stayed, in the population's layered density. Forms that seem to gesture with human awkwardness among the fluent Chorister postures. Forms that turn toward you with attention that reads as individual recognition in the general unreadable regard. They have not become Choristers, exactly—cannot, perhaps, the human neurological architecture insufficient for the full participation they have achieved—but they have become *present in* the Choir, voices in the silent song that register at the edge of perceptibility, harmonics that change the texture without becoming melody.

Those who return to the outside world carry Keth-Vorath with them in forms that never fully externalize. The bottle-gardens, the sealed condensation-chambers, the miniature architectures of weeping stone and captured water—these are not memory made object, not nostalgia's project. They are *continuations*, attempts to maintain the connection that was formed, to keep participating in whatever density the short human life can sustain. Sometimes the captured water begins to behave strangely, to bead upward, to gather in impossible places. Sometimes the miniature stones weep more than evaporation allows. Sometimes storms come, compressed and furious, in spaces too small to contain them, and the human builder walks around their perimeter with something approaching Chorister calm, having learned this much at least, having become in some degree *of* the place that can never fully be *in* them.

The ones who build these continuations do not form communities. Each maintains their condensation, their captured water, their miniature weeping, in isolation that reads not as loneliness but as *appropriate density*—too many such structures in proximity might achieve critical mass, might become something else, might draw the attention of the well or the weather or the population in ways that the individual builder cannot predict or desire. They are scattered, the returned, the exiled, the translated-into-partial-presence, each tending their small Keth-Vorath in the human world that cannot perceive what they have become, that reads them as eccentric, as obsessive, as slightly mad in the harmless way of those who maintain impossible projects with excessive devotion.

---

**The Choir Itself**

This is what lives there, finally, if "what" can be stretched to include process, if "lives" can be stretched to include continuation without individual origin.

The Sunken Choir is not the inhabitants, though they participate. It is not the well, though it emerges from and returns to it. It is not the architecture, though the architecture makes its resonances possible. It is the *song*, the silent subsonic continuous emission that first announced the place to human approach, that persists in the sternum's memory after the ridge falls away, that continues in frequencies the human ear cannot process but the human body cannot entirely ignore.

The song has no composer, no conductor, no beginning. It is the accumulated resonance of all the Choristers' metabolic subsonics, all the well's emissions, all the weather's compressed fury, all the water's endless falling, all the light's complex self-interaction with the black stone's uneasy holding. It is not music in human terms, has no variation that would signify aesthetic choice, no development that would imply narrative. It is *condition* rather than composition, the state of Keth-Vorath made audible to organs that can receive it, the density made vibration, the wetness made pressure, the silence made present in its own frequency.

And in it, somehow, the returned ones hear their own almost-presence, the voices they became in the streets, in the well's recursive regard, in the luminous rain that they still taste on waking, still feel in lungs that remember thin cold air. They build their bottles, their sealed gardens, their miniature architectures of condensation and captured storm, and sometimes—rarely, unpredictably, at light configurations that resemble those of the original place—the subsonic registers on the edge of hearing, and they know themselves still to be singing, still to be sung, still to be part of what lives in Keth-Vorath, what has always lived there, what will continue in its wet density, its cold luminosity, its turbulent silence, its impossible abundance of presences that are and are not there, long after the last human has carried their basket of memory down the mountain, long after the last bottle has dried, long after the ridge has fallen or risen or become something else entirely, the song continuing, the Choir sunken and present and endless and *there*, in the bowl of city cupped in mountain hands, weeping and singing and waiting for the next approach, the next vibration of the sternum, the next attention that might, for a moment, perceive what lives there and in what extraordinary density it lives.

---

*What lives there lives densely. What lives there lives wetly. What lives there lives in frequencies below hearing, in topologies beyond mapping, in presences that layer and saturate and exceed the three dimensions that human cognition requires. What lives there is not hostile, is not welcoming, is not indifferent in any category that human emotional taxonomy provides. What lives there *continues*, and in its continuation, includes whatever approaches with sufficient attention to be changed by the approach, to become, however partially, however temporarily, part of the song that is not heard but felt, that is not music but condition, that is not ending and never began but persists, persists, persists in the black stone and the weeping water and the cold that refuses ice and the light that remembers its source and the storms that compress their fury into spaces too small to contain them and the well that looks back and the population that is and is not there, carrying, tending, moving, silent, singing.*
