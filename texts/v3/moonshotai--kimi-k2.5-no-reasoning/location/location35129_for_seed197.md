**The Song-Husk of Verrith**

It begins with pressure in the inner ear, before you see anything at all. A sustained tone that seems to have always been sounding, that you have somehow only just begun to *notice*, as if your consciousness needed time to calibrate to this frequency of existence. Then you crest the low rise—elevation here means only that you are not *submerged*, that the warm air can still move against your skin—and you see it below you, or rather, you see where it *is*, because the thing itself keeps not resolving into normal vision.

Verrith is a city grown from a single organism, or a single organism that has *become* city, or—and this is where the reality breakdown announces itself—both states existing in unresolvable superposition, observed only through the flickering of your own inability to hold both concepts. The walls are bark-thick, chambered, rising in organic analogues of towers and avenues, but when you look directly at them they are simply *tree*, enormous tree, and when you glance sideways they are masonry, settlement, planned space with the memory of intention. The warm damp air carries spores that glitter in the "normal" light—neither day nor artificial but something that simply *illuminates*, without source, the color of witnessing—and these spores are words, almost words, phonemes of a language that plants speak in their sleep.

The vegetation is total: 2.6/3. This is not "many plants." This is plant-as-substrate, plant-as-medium, plant-as-the-condition-of-being-here. Every surface capable of supporting life is *living*, and many surfaces that should not be capable. The streets—if they are streets—are compressed root-mass, worn smooth by processes that may or may not include the passage of feet. The "buildings" are hollows grown within the architecture of enormous, interlocking species: chamber trees that have evolved, or been cultivated, or *dreamed themselves into* spaces of habitation. Ceiling mushrooms bioluminesce in rhythms that might be communication, might be computation, might be the city's metabolism made visible.

But the density is empty. Almost nothing per volume. The city grew for dwellers who are not here, or who are here in forms the concept "fauna" only partially describes. 2.3/3 abundant life. Life pressed into every available ecological niche, yet the niches themselves are vast, cathedral-hollow, grove-chambered. You walk for minutes between presences. A creature the size of your torso, all articulated legs andlistening-antennae, crosses a "street" that spans forty meters of root-platform. It takes seventeen seconds to traverse. You watch it. Then it is gone, and there is only the sound again.

The sound.

Deafening. Absolute. The sound is the city breathing, but breathing is not the right word—the sound is the *wrongness* of the city breathing, the acoustic signature of a organism the size of a town inhaling and exhaling in a medium where sound should not propagate this way, at this intensity, without source or directionality. It is root-pressure in thousand-ton vascular systems. It is the friction of growth, continuous growth, the erosional here of 2.0/3 meaning *settled into its own form* but that form is still *becoming*, weathered into balance yet actively balancing, a dynamic equilibrium of 2.4/3 that includes weather cycles happening *inside* the architecture, biologically generated pressures, predator-prey relationships at the scale of architecture: something that lives in the high chambers hunts something that lives in the lower root-networks, and their population cycles manifest as doors that open and seal, corridors that breathe wider and narrower, the city *participating* in their ecology because the city is not separable from it.

And underneath, or overtop, or permeating: the reality breakdown. The sound is so loud it becomes a kind of silence, a white-noise totality, and in that totality perception frays. You see a tower—glance away—look back—and it is a still-living stump, ancient, rotted at the core, yet structurally identical to what you saw before. The "normal" light does not cast consistent shadows. Time moves differently in chambers with different acoustic properties; you may spend hours in a root-arcade and emerge to find the spore-glitter has shifted in patterns that suggest minutes, or days, or that time is simply another substrate here, another thing the vegetation has grown into and made its own.

Water at 1.4/3: intermittent. Condensation beads on warm bark and falls in slow, unpredictable rain. Cisterns exist—grown cisterns, pitcher-plant architecture—collecting moisture, but they are scattered, unreliable. The damp is more atmosphere than resource. You are always slightly wet. The air itself carries water in suspension, and your own breath adds to it, your own presence participating in the humidity economy of this place.

There is no technology. Nothing built by tools. Yet the organization is unmistakably *urban*: infrastructure-density without infrastructure, layered construction that is simply the history of growth, the city stratified in rings you can read like a core sample, older chambers compressed, absorbed, built-around by newer growth, yet still *present*, still acoustically active, contributing their own frequencies to the deafening whole. The city has a memory, expressed in wood density and chemical gradients and sound-damping characteristics. It remembers being smaller. It remembers being different. It is still remembering, still learning, still urbanizing itself in slow botanical epochs.

The fauna: abundant, but distributed through such emptiness that each encounter startles. Creatures have adapted to the sound, to the reality-flicker, to the warm low press of plant-city. Some resonate with particular frequencies, their bodies tuned to acoustic niches. Others have learned to move through the perceptual instabilities, slipping between the city's unresolved states, present more in one version of Verrith than another. You may see something out of the corner of your eye that cannot exist in the version you are currently inhabiting, and when you turn, it is gone, but the *trace* of its passage remains—a humidity disturbance, a spore displacement, a sudden harmonic in the deafening tone that your ear, improbably, isolates and recognizes as *footstep*, as *breath*, as *someone was here*.

The rules of this world: causality is local but not contiguous. The city resolves into coherence for each observer, but different observers may resolve different cities, and the space between resolutions is not empty—it is *growing*, it is substrate, it is the plant filling every possible space including the spaces between versions of itself. You can navigate if you accept that your path is also the city's growth-ring, your destination also a memory it is having about itself. The sound protects and disorients: at this intensity, no individual sound can be distinguished, which means all sounds are equally present, which means *intention* becomes audible, *meaning* becomes navigable, if you can learn to listen for it without being overwhelmed into the static.

It has been here a long time, this small place, this grove-scale urban absence. Eroded into itself, weathered into balance, yet active, dynamic, breathing loud enough to blur the boundaries between what is heard and what is simply *the condition of being present in Verrith*. It does not need inhabitants. It may have never had them. The urban form is not for us. The chambers, the streets, the cisterns, the acoustic properties that make language possible and impossible—they are ecological features, grown by and for processes that include but do not require the animal, the conscious, the perceiver.

Yet you are here. Listening. Damp and warm and low, in a city that grows around your attention, that may only *be* city when observed, that flickers between tree and building, organism and architecture, memory and present growth. The sound continues. It was continuing before you arrived. It will continue, you understand, in versions of this place you cannot access, where the light is different, where the water has collected elsewhere, where the fauna moves in patterns shaped by entirely different resolutions of the underlying wrongness.

Verrith is not one place. It is the interference pattern of many places, grown together, plant-substrate binding them into apparent coherence, the sound the sound of their overlap, their friction, their impossibly simultaneous existence. Small enough to walk across in an afternoon. Empty enough to hold nothing but itself. Loud enough to drown the distinction between walking and being walked, between observing and being grown around, between the city you are in and the city that is, in its warm damp silence-beneath-deafening, learning you.
