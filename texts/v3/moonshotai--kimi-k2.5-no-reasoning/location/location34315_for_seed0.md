The place has no name in any human tongue, though the fauna here have their own calls for it—vocalizations too complex and layered for unmodified ears to parse, sounds that function as both navigation and narrative, a living map updated in real-time by thousands of throats.

You arrive, if you can call it arrival when there is no border marked, during one of the darkness periods. The light parameter of 0.3 does not mean pure absence. There is phosphorescence everywhere, bio-luminescence having evolved into ecological dominance, the primary visual currency of this world. Fungal networks thread through the dense vegetation like neural tissue made visible, pulsing soft greens and violets in slow metabolic rhythm. The plants themselves—lush to the point of obstruction, vegetation at 2.5—have adapted to darkness not by retreating but by developing light-generating capacities, competing for visible spectrum the way surface flora compete for sun. Their leaves are broad and dark, nearly black, optimized for photon capture at the lowest thresholds, but their flowers, their seedpods, their defensive thorns all glow. You move through a garden of self-illuminated geometries, shapes emerging from black only where life has made itself seen.

The terrain is low, elevation 1.3, but youthfully so—erosion at 0.8 means the land has not yet learned patience. The ground feels provisional. You walk on volcanic glass sometimes, sharp-edged, recently enough formed that its fractures still catch the phosphorescent glow and throw it back fragmented. Other times the footing gives slightly, peat accumulating in hollows where water at 0.8 has gathered without permanence, seasonal or diurnal pools that appear and evaporate, leaving salt rinds and mineral crusts, then return with the next condensation cycle. The dampness is intermittent, atmospheric, a humidity that seems to originate from the vegetation itself, transpiration on a collective scale that creates local weather. It does not rain so much as precipitate, moisture falling from the canopy in slow, continuous drizzle whenever the biomass has exceeded some metabolic threshold.

But the sound. The sound at 2.6, deafening, requires its own taxonomy. There are no quiet moments. The fauna—overwhelming at 2.7—have filled every acoustic niche until the niches themselves have become indistinguishable. Insect analogues, though nothing here maps cleanly to Terran categories, generate baseline frequencies, mechanical droning from rubbing organs or vibrating membranes. Larger creatures, megafauna of the perpetual twilight, produce calls that seem to serve no communicative function, territorial announcements perhaps, or mating displays, or something else entirely—notes so low they register in the chest before the ear, so high they trigger instinctive flinching. Between these extremes, a middle layer of mid-sized animals produces what you might mistake for language if you listened without analysis: sequential, varied, responsive. The calls overlap, interrupt, harmonize accidentally, create standing waves in certain clearings where multiple sources align. You find yourself shouting to hear your own thoughts, then stop, because thought itself feels different here, subsumed into the sonic texture, become something collective and reactive rather than interior and deliberate.

The density parameter of 2.3 manifests not as simple crowding but as stratification. Life occupies every available volume with near-complete efficiency. The canopy, fifteen to twenty meters up in most places, is itself multi-layered—emergent giants rising higher still, their crowns lost in darkness above, middle-story light-generators, understory specialists in dim spectra, then ground cover, then the substrate itself alive with rhizomorphic networks, then below that, in the young volcanic soils, biomes of chemotrophic organisms processing mineral nutrients, their own slow glow visible through cracks in the glassy stone. You sense presences near-touching, each layer pressing against the next, competition so immediate and constant that cooperation has become obligatory, symbiosis the default survival mode. Plants grow on plants. Animals nest in living architecture. Parasitism blurs with mutualism until the categories lose meaning—every relationship is extractive and supportive simultaneously, each organism both host and guest in recursive chains.

The dynamism, 2.6, turbulent, means the place is never the same when you return to where you began, even if you have not moved. The volcanic youth of the terrain extends to its behavior. Geothermal vents open and seal, creating transient hot springs that boil their surrounding vegetation before cooling and becoming new colonization sites. Sinkholes form where organic accumulation becomes too thick, too heavy, too metabolically hot, dropping whole sections of canopy into subterranean cavities that become, within days, new biomes with their own light signatures, their own acoustic signatures. The phosphorescent networks pulse faster in some seasons, slower in others, but "season" is a provisional term—the cycles seem partly geothermal, partly biological, partly something else, perhaps even responsive to the fauna's own chorus, feedback loops of sound and light and growth and decay running at speeds that make the landscape appear to breathe, to think, to be in perpetual creative argument with itself.

And the weirdness, 1.3, unusual but not total—this is not a place of arbitrary physical law, but of extended, unexpected consequence. You notice it first in the way light behaves, the phosphorescence not merely illuminating but somehow *caching*, leaving afterimages that persist in retinal perception long after the source has moved or dimmed. You see trails where things have passed, ghost-luminescence marking recent paths. The acoustic standing waves in clearings do not merely resonate—they seem to *record*, to replay with variation, calls from hours or days before mixing with present sound in temporal collage. The fauna appear aware of this, using specific clearings as archives, reference libraries of vocal history, returning to particular resonance nodes to hear what was said before.

Technology here, 1.2, primitive, exists but belongs to the fauna, not to any hypothetical human presence. You find tools—stones fractured by deliberate percussion, though by what appendage you cannot determine, woody materials shaped by abrasion, fibers twisted into cordage abandoned in nests or feeding sites. No metal. The volcanic glass provides cutting edges that need no improvement. The light-generating organisms provide illumination that needs no artificial replication. The acoustic environment provides, for certain species, something like distributed computing, problem-solving through networked call-and-response that achieves complexity no individual intelligence could manage. The tech is biological, social, acoustic, luminescent—a technology of relationship rather than artifact, primitive not in simplicity but in fundamentality, rooted in capacities shared across many species rather than concentrated in tool-making specialists.

There is nothing built. 0.2 on that scale means absolute absence, not even ruin, not even the memory of construction. The place has never known architecture in the human sense. Shelters exist—woven enclosures, excavated burrows, cavities grown-by-influence rather than carved—but nothing that separates inside from outside with finality, nothing that resists the dynamic turbulence by fixed opposition. Structures here are always provisional, always growing or decaying, always negotiating with the dense life around them. A nest is also a food source for something else. A wall is also a substrate. Boundary is performance, not fact, enacted moment to moment and abandoned when the enactment becomes unsustainable.

The darkness has its own depths. At 0.3 light, you learn to see with more than eyes, or the fauna have. The overwhelming presence of animal life includes forms navigated by sound, by scent, by sensitivity to thermal variation, by detection of electrical fields in the humid air. Some creatures appear to perceive the phosphorescent networks directly, reading in their pulses information about nutrient flows, defensive alerts, reproductive status across miles of connected substrate. The darkness is not absence but specialization, a niche so thoroughly occupied that light itself has become one modality among many, not the default condition it is on brighter worlds.

You move through this place, if you move, as the most provisional presence of all. You carry no built thing, or you learn quickly to abandon it. You adapt to the deafening sound until it becomes a kind of silence, full rather than empty, carrying information at every frequency if you learn to attend. You follow the glow-trails, the cached luminescence of recent passage, navigating by biological light that answers to no diurnal cycle, that brightens and dims according to rules you might spend years learning. You become part of the density, your body heat sensed by creatures that have never known fire, your footsteps contributing to the acoustic archive, your exhalation joining the transpirational humidity. 

The place does not care whether you understand it. It is busy being itself, turbulent and young and lush and loud and dark and weird and overwhelmingly alive, a world before settlement, before architecture, before the human pause of building something and standing back to see if it holds. Nothing holds here. Everything continues, changing, pulsing, calling, glowing in the dark, sharp-edged with volcanic newness, damp with its own exhalations, dense with life that has never made room for vacancy, that fills every volume with the fact of itself, the noise of itself, the light of itself in darkness, the strange coherence of a world that found its own solutions, its own technologies of continuation, its own reasons for being so much, so loud, so present, so utterly and specifically *here*.
