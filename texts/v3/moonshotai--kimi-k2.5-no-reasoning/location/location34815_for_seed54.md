The sky is not black here; it is *unhappened*, a color that predates darkness, the shade of a room before you remember where the walls are. What light exists does not illuminate so much as *assert edges*—you understand the presence of a vertical surface not because you see it but because something in your inner ear insists upon it, because the pressure of shadow against your left shoulder has weight and temperature.

You are high. The air is thin in a way that seems *new*, as if the atmosphere itself is still learning how to be atmosphere, still deciding on ratios, still wet with the condensation of its own becoming. Water is everywhere here and nowhere visible: it exists as potential, as the taste before rain, as films on surfaces that should be too cold to hold liquid. Everything weeps. Crystalline outgrowths—salt, perhaps, or something that performs salt's function in a chemistry not yet fully negotiated—drip with moisture that freezes in mid-fall, hangs suspended, accumulates into chandeliers of stillborn drops that never complete their arc to the ground.

The ground. The ground is *emerging*, still in the process of deciding whether it will be ground. In places it has the consistency of cooling slag, of chocolate held just at melting point; you could press your thumb into it and it would hold the impression for hours, for days, as if considering whether to accept your violence into its permanent record. Elsewhere it has begun to assert itself, to develop fault lines and planes of cleavage, to grow the confidence of geology. But these older-seeming regions are islands in a matrix of the *becoming*, and their boundaries bleed. You can stand with one foot on stone that dreams it has already been stone for a million years and one foot in material that is still, in some sense, a liquid that has forgotten it is liquid, that has paused in its flow to experiment with solidity.

And everywhere, the city. Or: the *becoming-city*, the *almost-settlement*, which is perhaps more unsettling than any finished metropolis would be. Because it is dense—so dense that the concept of *space-between* has not yet been fully established here. Structures touch. They lean into each other like trees in wind, like sleepers in cold, sharing walls that belong simultaneously to three or four different addresses. But they are not old, these buildings. They are *premature*, built in anticipation of a maturity they have not yet earned, their surfaces smooth with nanoscale precision while their foundations rest on ground that has not finished deciding its own form.

The construction—if it is construction, if it is not something more like *growth directed by intention*—operates at scales that the eye cannot parse without technological assistance, and the technology here is not offered to assist. It is *post-modern* in the most literal sense: it has passed through modernity without quite stopping, collected none of its habits of explanation. You see surfaces that appear to be metal but flow like liquid when you do not look directly at them. You see windows that do not open onto spaces behind them but onto *elsewheres*, vistas of beaches or furnace interiors or the view from the eye of something flying, impossibly, through the unhappened sky. The infrastructure is dense with functions you cannot identify. Conduits run between buildings at every level, packed so thickly that in places they form *new* buildings themselves, hollow architectures of pure connectivity whose interiors are filled with the sound of whatever moves through them.

The sound. The sound is overwhelming—not because it is so loud, though it is loud, but because it is *specific*, because every element in this dense world produces its particular frequency and none of them quite harmonize. The drip of not-quite-freezing water. The hum of the conduits, which sounds less like machinery than like *voices trying to become music*. The groan of the emerging ground as it settles into new configurations, the crack of crystalline growth in accelerations too fast for normal geologic time. And beneath all of it, permeating all of it, the sound of the fauna.

Which is *overwhelming* in a sense that the scale does not quite capture. There is no vegetation here, no plant life, no photosynthetic foundation for an ecology. What lives here has found other arrangements. You do not see them at first—they are too dense, too *integrated* with the urban fabric, too much a part of the material that everything else is made from. They are *in* the walls, not behind them. They are *in* the conduits, not moving through them. The fauna here is not a separate kingdom; it is a *condition* of the place, the way in which this environment has chosen to be alive.

You begin to perceive them as pressure first, as the sense of being *regarded* by materials that should not have eyes. Then as movement in periphery: a flow in a wall-surface that leaves the wall changed, that leaves it *having experienced*. Then, finally, you understand that the structures themselves are *fauna*, or are *fauna-equivalents*, or are the exoskeletons, the shells, the secreted architectures of things that do not differentiate between living and building in the way your biology has taught you to expect.

They are slow. Everything here is slow, patient, operating on cycles that would test the endurance of stone. A building that is *fauna* might take a century to complete a single *gesture*, a wall-that-is-membrane might pulse once in a human lifetime. The dynamic of the place is embryonic, *barely dynamic at all* in any scale you could perceive unaided—and yet you sense the direction of it, the vector of change. It is growing more itself. It is consolidating its strangeness, its weirdness (which is not absolute, not the maximal *weird* of places that have abandoned coherence entirely, but rather the *unusual* of something that almost makes sense, that invites pattern-recognition without quite rewarding it).

You are walking through a body that is learning to be a city in a world that is learning to be a planet, and the body regards you, slowly, with the patient attention of something that has time, that has *only* time, that measures its existence not in years but in *becomings*. The water condenses on your skin and does not freeze, held liquid by some agreement between your warmth and the world's willingness. The dark presses against your eyes and does not become light, held *unhappened* by some protocol you cannot access. The sound accumulates in your ears, layer on layer, the specific unharmonious chorus of this place that is *nearly* loud enough to become music but refuses, that maintains its distance from pattern, from meaning, from the comfort of significance.

You could stay. The city-fauna would not prevent you. It would, slowly, *incorporate* you, your presence becoming part of its architecture, your warmth becoming part of its thermodynamics, your attention becoming part of its slow *self-regard*. You would not die here, not exactly. You would become *present*, dense among densities, another layer in the saturated volume of this high cold wet place that is still, in its deepest structures, *emerging*.

Or you could leave, descending through the unhappened sky, back toward light that illuminates rather than asserting edges, toward ground that has finished its argument with itself, toward cities built by beings that separate themselves from their constructions, toward sound that you could call music if you chose to, toward the *weird* that has been tamed into the merely *strange*.

The water drips, not falling, not frozen, *held*. The ground considers your weight. The fauna-city accumulates another moment of attention, slower than patience, longer than waiting. Something is being built here, or born here, or dreamed into a solidity it has not yet earned. You are present at the *emerging*, at the *pre-formation*, in the time before the place has finished deciding what it will be.

It has not decided. It will not decide soon. The dark holds everything *before* decision, in the richness of possibility that precedes the loss of all other possibilities. This is its atmosphere, its ecology, its law: *not yet*, *still becoming*, *almost but not yet*.

What would you almost become, if you stayed?
