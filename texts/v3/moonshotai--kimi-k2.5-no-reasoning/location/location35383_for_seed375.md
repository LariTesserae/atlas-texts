The Dry House of Keth-Vorath

Consider, first, the silence. Not the absence of sound but its *refusal*—a quality of negative pressure against the eardrums, the way a held breath differs from emptiness. The Dry House exists in this held-breath state, perpetually. When sounds do occur—and they do, despite the parameter's weight—they arrive as *intrusions*, as violations: a pebble rolling down a slope that stops too quickly, as if embarrassed. The silence has density. It has rules.

The structure occupies a space best understood as *cathedral-scale but inverted*—not upward aspiration but inward folding, a volume that could fit perhaps three rural churches placed corner-to-corner, yet feels larger because so little of it can be occupied at any moment. It sits low, nearly at datum level with the surrounding basin, what elevation exists coming from subtle swellings in the floor-plane rather than any true height. The basin itself: cool, not cold. The temperature of a body hours after death. The temperature of stone that has never known sun directly but remembers some other heat source, now withdrawn.

And young—so young. This is the strangest parameter, perhaps. The Dry House is *newly emerged*, but not from construction. Not built. *Born*, or *excreted*, or *precipitated* from conditions that preceded it. The walls show no tool-marks because no tools touched them. The surfaces are *fresh* in the manner of a tooth just broken through gum, still wet with whatever medium preceded its exposure to this dry realm. Sharp edges everywhere—corners that could cut, angles that catch the dim light and hold it wrong, scattered in directions that don't illuminate so much as *accuse*.

The light. Ah. There is a source, singular, located at what inhabitants call the Throat (though no voice has ever emerged from it): a vertical shaft of uncertain depth, perhaps three meters in diameter, cutting through ceiling membranes that layer downward like the pages of a book compressed by water, then dehydrated. The light from this shaft is the color of old limes, of copper left in vinegar too long. It does not spread evenly. It *pools* in certain regions according to rules of surface tension that seem to apply to photons here, and in the pooled zones visibility extends perhaps twenty meters. Between pools, the darkness carries texture—not blackness but *brown*, a mineral brown, the color of dried blood on rust. The eye keeps trying to resolve shapes in these interstitial zones, and sometimes succeeds too well.

Urban, they called this parameter. Settlement-scale. The Dry House holds approximately forty-seven *habitable volumes*—I hesitate to say rooms, as the boundaries behave poorly. Some are spherical, accessed through openings that require crawling, that then expand into spaces where one could stand with arms outstretched and turn slowly. Others are long corridors that *kink*, that achieve intersections geometrically impossible, where turning left three times does not return you to your origin but to somewhere else entirely, somewhere with different air pressure and a faint taste of iron. The organization is undeniable: *infrastructure-density* in the form of the Connective Tissue, a series of passages that recur at regular intervals, that bear signs of repeated use—polished surfaces where something has passed many times, though what, and in what direction, remains unclear.

Urban, but empty. Density: 0.4/3. The near-void. The Dry House contains perhaps *two* individuals at any given measurement, though counting proves difficult. Are the Echoes individuals? They repeat your movements with a delay of 7–12 seconds, visible only in the light-pools, and never approach closer than fifteen meters. Are the Inhabitants the House itself? The distinction blurs. What is clear: vast volumes exist that have never been entered, that wait in their brown darkness with the patience of young geology, fresh from whatever process made them.

No water. The driest thing. Not desert-desiccated, not salt-field where water once existed and left its mineral memory. This is *non-aqueous chemistry*, a realm where the concept of H₂O as solvent is simply *not native*. The surfaces weep instead with *olefins*, with *liquid silicon compounds* that move slowly if at all, that pool in depressions and form *lenses* through which the lime-light refracts into spectra that suggest colors for which human language has no need. The atmosphere, such as it is, carries these vapors at concentrations that would confuse most Carbon-based sensors. Breathing here is possible but *theoretical*—the House accepts certain chemistries, rejects others without explanation. Some who enter simply *stop*, not dead but *paused*, until the House finishes with whatever processing it requires.

Vegetation: 1.3/3. Sparse. The Dry House is not entirely dead to biological colonization. In the highest, coolest volumes, where the lime-light maintains its most consistent pool, *lichenous approximations* cling to the sharp young surfaces. They are not lichen, not fungus, not anything with terrestrial analog. They grow in patterns that suggest *writing* in scripts that almost resolve to meaning before dissolving into mere geometry. They fruit, occasionally, releasing spores that the silence catches and holds suspended, visible as golden motes in the light-pools, drifting without current, without purpose, accumulating in corners until they form *drifts* like pollen snow that the Echoes disturb with their delayed passage.

Fauna: rare. Rarer than the vegetation. The most common animal is the *Click*, though whether it is animal, mineral process, or something else entirely remains undecided. The Click manifests as sound—single, sharp, sourceless cracks that occur at irregular intervals, sometimes several per hour, sometimes none for days. They correlate with nothing measurable: not temperature fluctuations, not the olefin weeping, not the movement of Echoes. Yet they *feel* biological. They feel like something *noticing*.

More substantial: the Filters. These occupy specific volumes—always the same ones, though why those particular spaces remains unexplained—and appear as *densities in the brown darkness*, shapes that absorb even less light than their surrounds. They move, but only when unobserved directly; turn to face them and they are static, perhaps just architectural irregularity. Turn away and peripheral vision catches their slow rotation, their extension of *appendages* that seem designed for sifting, for sorting, for *processing* the golden spore-drifts. They do not acknowledge other presences. They may not perceive at the wavelengths, in the sensory modes, that other entities use.

Dynamic: 2.3/3. Active. This is perhaps the most crucial strangeness, the weave that holds the contradictory parameters coherent. The Dry House *processes*. The young sharp surfaces are *already* changing, already being modified by their own internal logic. Volumes that were spherical last season—if seasons obtain here—have elongated. Corridors that kinked left now kink right, stranding established routes to the Throat and opening new ones to spaces previously inaccessible. The Echoes change their delay; the most recent measurement suggests 9–14 seconds rather than the historical 7–12. The Click has *clustered* recently: four in one hour, then thirty hours of silence, then two simultaneously from directions 180 degrees apart.

There is weather here, or something analogous. The *Pressure Fronts* move through the Connective Tissue at intervals, perceptible as changes in the olefin weeping's rate, as alterations in the *taste* of the atmosphere—more metallic, more organic, more of something that doesn't parse. They carry no precipitation, no wind in the conventional sense, but they *move things*: spore-drifts rearrange, Echoes become momentarily more defined, the Filters extend their appendages and work faster, as if expecting.

And predator-prey, yes, if we accept a broad definition. Something *hunts* in the lowest volumes, where the brown darkness achieves its maximum depth, where the lime-light pools thin to mere suggestion. What it hunts: perhaps the Filters, perhaps the spores, perhaps something else entirely that has not been catalogued. The evidence of its passage: surfaces *smoothed*, the young sharp edges worn to curves in corridors that show no other traffic, and a residue—not quite olefin, not quite organic—that *phosphoresces* faintly for hours after, the only light in those depths, just enough to show that something else was here, is *still* here, moving somewhere ahead of you in absolute silence.

The rules of this world:

First: *occupation is temporary*. The Dry House permits presence but not residence. Those who attempt to remain in one volume find the volume *forgets* them, its geometry shifting to exclude their habitation, or includes them in a fold that doesn't connect to the rest.

Second: *the Echoes precede*. Whatever you do has already been done by something else, delayed. This suggests time operates differently, or that the House maintains some kind of *recording* that plays back with variation.

Third: *water is memory*. Not present, but *remembered*—certain volumes feel *damp* without moisture, certain passages carry the *implication* of liquidity past. The House may have had water once, or may be destined to, or may exist in superposition between states that include and exclude it.

Fourth: *the Throat is the only exit, but not for everyone*. Those who leave through the shaft do so differently than they entered. Some emerge elsewhere entirely. Some emerge *as* Echoes, delayed, repeating movements they no longer control.

Fifth: *silence is the substrate*. Sound exists as exception, as wound in the negative pressure. The Click, rare footfalls, the *almost-voice* of the Filters in their processing—all these are intrusions that the silence works continuously to heal, to close, to restore to held-breath.

What emerges, finally, is a place *becoming*. The urban structure without builders. The active processes without clear agency. The young sharpness already wearing, already shifting, in the cool dim dry hollow of its own emergence. A place that has not yet decided what it is, that uses its rare visitors—fauna, fauna-as-inhabitants, fauna-as-prey—as probes toward self-definition.

The Dry House of Keth-Vorath. Enter through the Throat, if you can find it from outside; it moves too, though more slowly. Bring no water. Bring no expectation of echoless solitude. The silence will hold you, briefly, before forgetting you ever came.
