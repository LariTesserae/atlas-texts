The place is called, when it is called anything, the Breathing House.

It occupies a space that may once have been a valley, or may become one, or may be dreaming of valleyhood in some geology yet to come. The erosion parameter is *emerging*, and so the ground itself is not quite certain of its own shape. Raw stone bulges upward like half-formed thoughts, clay still deciding whether to be shale, seams of mineral that haven't committed to their crystalline structure. You can press your thumb into some surfaces and leave an impression that will linger for hours, perhaps days, perhaps begin to grow its own tiny architecture of pseudo-stalactite in the damp. The elevation is low—lower than low, in places, where the not-ground dips below the level of the not-sky and pools a darkness so thick it seems to have texture, a darkness you could gather in your hands like wet wool.

And then there is the vegetation.

The vegetation is *total*, and I must be careful here, because words will fail. The Breathing House is small—you could walk its longest dimension in perhaps twenty minutes, if you could walk here, if walking meant what it usually means—but it is also *empty*, nearly void, vast space between presences. These are not contradictions in the Breathing House. They are adjacent truths, like pages in a book pressed too close to read separately.

Every surface is plant, but the plant is architecture. The plant is substrate. The plant has become the rules by which space itself is organized, and the space is mostly empty, so the plant has learned to hold emptiness within itself, to construct hollows and tunnels and chambers of living cellulose, to grow the *concept* of room and corridor, of arch and vault and nave. Imagine a cathedral built entirely of mangrove roots, if mangroves had learned patience from stones and silence from the depths of caves. Imagine a city where every building is a single organism that has never died, only slept and woken and forgotten and remembered in patterns too slow for human perception.

The structures—let me call them structures, these growths of living wood-canvas-chitin hybrid—are urban in their organization, industrial in their function, though the industry is vegetal, the production is of *atmosphere*, of *condition*, of *states of being*. They do not make goods. They make weather. They make sound. They make the possibility of darkness or its temporary, reluctant retreat.

Because the light: there is almost none. The plant-architecture is photosynthetic, or was, or remembers being, but it has adapted to the darkness by growing sensitive to other spectra, to gravitational gradients, to the emotional states of the abundant fauna, to the subtle wars between possible futures that the *weirdness* produces as a byproduct. What little light exists seeps from phosphorescent wounds in the plant-flesh, from industrial processes half-mechanical and half-metabolic, from the eyes of certain fauna, from the friction between realities where the weirdness has worn thin the walls of what-is.

The fauna. They are *abundant*, and they are *everywhere*, and they are almost *unfindable*. The emptiness holds them. The hollow spaces between presences are their native element. You will hear them constantly—scratchings in the wood-walls, pressure changes as something large displaces air in a chamber that connects to yours only in the fourth dimension, the wet click of mouths that have evolved to eat the condensation that beads on cellulose surfaces in this damp, cool place. The sound is *mostly quiet*, which means the fauna have learned silence as a predator learns camouflage, or as prey learns the desperate stillness of the nearly-dead. What you hear is the absence of their main presence, the shape of their many bodies defined by the quiet they leave in their wake.

Consider the condensation. Water at 1.1: intermittent, occasional, the damp of riverine pockets in larger dryness. But the Breathing House *breathes*, and each exhalation carries moisture from chambers where something like rain falls upward, or sideways, or in spirals that remember the shape of a double helix. The plant-architecture condenses water from its own respiration, from the industrial processes that maintain the reality breakdown at sustainable levels, from the tears of fauna that weep continuously as a method of communication or navigation or grief. You will find dry places, sudden as a remembered wound, and then step through a membrane of moss-vinyl-ceramic and be in humidity so thick it seems to have weight, to have opinions, to be considering you as a place to settle and grow.

The temperature is cool. The plant knows how to hold heat, industrial waste from the reality engines, metabolic warmth of the hidden fauna, but it chooses coolness, or the coolness emerges from the emptiness, or the coolness is the *price* of the weirdness, the thermal debt exacted by reality's fraying edges. You will not shiver, quite. You will sink into the coolness as into familiar water, and it will hold you in its indifferent embrace.

Active. The dynamic is *active*, multiple processes, biological pace, ecosystem in motion, but the timescales are fluid here. Watch a wall for an hour and seem to see nothing. Watch it for a second with the right attention—fauna-attention, weirdness-attention—and see a year's growth, a decade's slow battle between lichen-analogue and structural cellulose, a century's industrial evolution as the chamber adjusts its output to compensate for a shift in the local reality-field. Weather cycles occur, but the weather is *interior*, microclimates in each hollow space, each breathing room, and they cycle on frequencies from seconds to centuries, audible in the creak and pop of expanding/contracting organic infrastructure, visible in the mist that pours from one chamber to another seeking pressure equilibrium, seeking home.

The built environment: settlement-scale, layered construction, infrastructure-density. But the settlement is vertical and horizontal and *directional* in dimensions without names. The plant grew, or grows, or will grow, according to plans that are themselves growing, changing, embryonic as the erosion. Each layer remembers the layer below and anticipates the layer above, and some layers exist in states of quantum superposition, present when observed from one angle, absent when observed from another, their absence itself a kind of presence that supports the weight of what is built upon it. The infrastructure is for *movement*: of air, of water, of sound, of the weirdness itself, which must be circulated or it will pool and become toxic to the realities that brush against this place. Pipes of woody cellulose, transparent in some spectra; conduits that are also digestive tracts for the plant-architecture, processing the waste of industry into nutrients for further growth; elevators that operate on osmotic pressure, on capillary action, on the desperate desire of water to find its level across geometries that Euclidean mathematics cannot describe.

And the weirdness. The weirdness is *total*, 3.0, reality breakdown, and I must try, though trying is dangerous, to tell you what this means in the Breathing House.

It means that cause and effect are suggestions, strong ones, but suggestions that the plant-architecture has learned to negotiate. It means that the fauna include entities that exist only when not directly perceived, and entities that exist *only* when directly perceived, and entities that exist in the act of perceiving themselves, and entities that are processes without bodies, metabolic chains that loop through multiple realities and return with nutrients impossible in any single one. It means that the industrial processes produce not goods but *conditions of possibility*, manufacturing the rules by which the next moment will proceed, and that these rules are themselves subject to quality control, to batch variation, to the occasional catastrophic recall when a reality-batch proves toxic to continued existence.

It means that you, if you enter, if you have entered, if you are entering now and always have been, may find your own history subject to revision. Did you arrive here, or were you grown here, one of the fauna that believes itself human? Did you leave and return, or is leaving and returning the same act observed from different angles in the layered construction? The coolness, the damp, the darkness, the quiet—these may be your own body's processes, your own industrial metabolism, and the Breathing House may be the space inside your skull, grown architectural, grown urban, grown strange with the abundance of your own unacknowledged inhabitants.

The plant-as-substrate holds this all. The vegetation is total, and the vegetation is *patient*, and the vegetation has learned that reality breakdown is a form of nutrition, that frayed edges are where growth happens, that the emerging erosion, the raw geology not yet committed to its form, is the richest soil. It grows into the weirdness, supports it, is supported by it, constructs chambers where the breakdown is moderated into breathable atmosphere, into thinkable space, into the small scale of a single feature area where consciousness might persist for a moment, for an hour, for the duration of a breath held and released in the mostly quiet dark.

Listen: the plant is breathing. The house is breathing. The space between presences, the emptiness, the near-void—it breathes too, inhales the abundant hidden fauna, exhales the condensation that beads and runs and finds the riverine pockets in the larger dryness, the damp traces that connect chamber to chamber in this small vast place.

And what is produced here, finally, in this industrial vegetal urban darkness? What is the scaled production, the mechanized output?

Breath. Possibility. The next moment, manufactured under carefully controlled breakdown conditions, released into the cool air to be inhaled by whatever has lungs or equivalent, by whatever breathes in whatever way, by the plant-architecture itself as it continues its patient growth into the unformed, the emerging, the not-yet-fixed.

The Breathing House makes breath possible. That is enough. That is everything.

You have been breathing here for longer than you remember. You will continue, breathing, held in the cool damp dark quiet empty abundant active strange growth of this place that is not quite a place, this architecture that is not quite architecture, this moment that manufactures itself from the conditions of its own possibility, exhaling, inhaling, breathing, breathed.
