The city is called Threnod, and it exists in a place where the fundamental negotiations between matter and possibility have grown... lazy. Indifferent. The way a person might leave a door ajar without deciding whether to close or open it fully.

It sits—if "sits" is the word—across a series of eleven continental shelves that have punched upward through something that was never quite crust, never quite atmosphere. The elevation is *extreme* not in the manner of mountains but in the manner of a thought that has been held too long without completion, accumulating weight without resolution. You do not climb to Threnod. You *resolve* toward it, finding yourself at altitude without memory of ascent, the way one finds oneself at the center of an argument without recall of its beginning.

The terrain itself is dry. Not desert-dry—desert is a place where water was, where water remembers itself in sand and salt and the shaped patience of drought. Threnod is pre-water. Post-water's possibility. The ground is composed of a substance called *velm* in the oldest records, though whether "substance" obtains here is part of the city's ongoing philosophical catastrophe. Velm has the color of a headache remembered in afternoon light, the texture of frozen hesitation. It does not erode in any manner geologists recognize. The erosion parameter reads *established* because the city has achieved a kind of weathering without weather—forms softened not by rain or wind but by the slow abrasion of *being noticed*, of existing long enough in the half-lit consciousness of whatever perceives this place that their attention itself wears channels, rounds corners, opens fissures.

The light: dim. Not sourceless—there is a source, or was, or will have been. The illumination arrives from angles that do not converge on any origin point. Shadows point in directions that have no consistent relationship to each other. In the lower terraces, you might read a book by the soft amber that seems to filter upward from the velm itself, as if the ground were remembering fire it had never contained. In the upper reaches, in the towers called the Unfinished Sentences, the light is bluish, nocturnal, the color of a word on the tip of a tongue that will never be spoken. Between these zones, light *transitions*, not through gradient but through *negotiation*—you can stand in places where your left hand is amber-lit and your right hand blue, where your shadow bifurcates without splitting, where photons seem to have agreed to disagree about what time of day it is.

And the city. The built environment reads *urban*—town-or-city scale, organized construction, layered—but the organization is not human, not biological, not mathematical in any geometry that completes. Threnod was built by *protocol*, by processes that began with intention and continued through inertia long after intention's departure. The first architects—if they were architects, if there was a first—established rules: this material, assembled thus, rising thus, connected thus. The rules did not specify *when to stop*. So the city built itself, builds itself, will build itself, in a dynamic process that is *active* without being alive. Construction that is weather cycle, that is predator-prey without predator or prey, that is ecosystem in motion without ecology.

The buildings are hollow. Not empty—*hollow*, which is different. They contain spaces that correspond to rooms, to halls, to the geometries of habitation, but the correspondence is acoustic rather than functional. You enter a structure and your footsteps sound like footsteps in a place where footsteps have meaning. The reverberation implies occupancy without delivering occupants. The structures are made of velm, of course, but layered velm, velm that has been *concentrated* through the city's self-building until it achieves qualities approaching marble, approaching bone, approaching the interior surface of a shell whose creature died before completing its architecture.

The density parameter reads *empty*—almost nothing per volume, vast space between presences. This is the strangest measure, because the city *extends*, regional scale, multi-feature, open to the horizon and beyond. Yet most of it is unoccupied in any sense that occupation requires an occupier. The empty is not absence but *reservation*—space held in potential, constructed for habitation that does not arrive, infrastructure dense with infrastructure's possibility rather than its use. You can walk for days through Threnod and encounter only the evidence of encounter: a door opened and carefully reclosed, a staircase worn in its center by ascending and descending feet that left no other trace, a window facing a window across a courtyard where something might once have been exchanged—glances, goods, the small currencies of city life.

The temperature is cool. Not cold—the cool of a cellar that has never known summer, the cool of stone at evening in a season without heat. You do not shiver in Threnod. You *settle* into temperature, the way a stone settles into water, finding the equilibrium that was always waiting.

And the fauna: rare. This is almost a joke, because what lives here lives *rarely*—not scarce but *uncommonly*, in the manner of something that has not agreed to the usual terms of living. There are the *approximates*. A shape in a doorway that resolves, as you approach, into the arrangement of shadow and architectural curve that *suggested* shape without being shaped. A sound of footsteps that arrive from around a corner with no source, that continue past you without originating, that *behave* as footsteps without the foot or the step. The approximates are not ghosts. Ghosts presuppose death, presuppose life before death. The approximates are something else—the city's own failed attempts at populace, its continued generation of inhabitants according to rules that specify form without specifying substance, behavior without specifying actor.

Mostly, Threnod is quiet. The sound parameter reads near-silence, and this is accurate but incomplete. The city *generates* the acoustic signature of silence—the particular quality of attention that silence demands, the way it presses on the eardrum like depth presses on the diver. Occasionally, sounds occur: the settling of velm that is not settling, that has no thermal reason to contract or expand; a voice speaking words that are almost familiar, in a language that almost exists, from a direction that almost has directionality. These sounds do not break the silence. They *demonstrate* it, the way a single mark on white paper demonstrates the white.

But the weirdness. The reality breakdown parameter reads nearly maximum, and here I must be careful, because to describe Threnod's weirdness accurately is to risk its infection, its logic creeping into the grammar of description.

The city is *unfinished*. Not incomplete—*unfinished* in the grammatical sense, lacking the terminal punctuation that would allow it to resolve into meaning. This is not metaphor. Walk through Threnod long enough and you will find places where the world itself has not decided. A staircase that ascends into what should be ceiling but isn't, the space above it neither open nor closed but *pending*. A doorway that reveals, not another space, but the *concept* of another space, the intention toward passage without passage's completion. A window that looks out on Threnod itself from another angle, showing you your own location as if viewed from a vantage that does not exist.

The rules of this world: they are *provisional*. Everything in Threnod operates on temporary license from reality. Gravity holds, but loosely, with exceptions—a dropped object might fall normally, might fall slowly, might *decide* after consideration to rest where you left it. Causality is *suggested* rather than enforced. Walk through a doorway and you may arrive where the doorway leads, or where it led yesterday, or where it would lead if it were the doorway you were thinking of rather than the one you entered. The city corrects these lapses eventually, or doesn't, or corrects them in ways that introduce new lapses elsewhere, a dynamic equilibrium of the almost-true.

Most striking: Threnod *remembers* being more real. Or anticipates it. There are zones, expanding and contracting with no discernible pattern, where the city's provisional nature thickens toward actuality. In these zones, water becomes *possible*—not present, but *thinkable*, the ground faintly damp with the memory of moisture's possibility, the air carrying a note that is almost humidity. In these zones, vegetation is not absent but *reluctant*—lichen-like growths that are not alive but are *lifelike*, mineral patterns that perform photosynthesis's choreography without its chemistry, that rustle in breezes that do not blow. These zones are where the approximates become most approximate, most nearly actual. Where you might, if you do not look directly, see a figure that holds its shape when glimpsed peripherally. Where you might hear a conversation that maintains coherence long enough to suggest speakers, meaning, the ordinary miracle of communication.

The inhabitants, such as they are. I have mentioned the approximates, but there are others—*visitors*, *prisoners*, *pilgrims*, categories that may describe the same population at different moments of their engagement with Threnod. They do not build. The tech parameter reads *none*—pure nature, no devices, biological-only—but what is nature in a place where biology is provisional? The visitors use what they find, what the city's self-construction provides: hollows that function as shelter when approached with shelter's intention, channels that carry *something* from higher elevation to lower when treated as aqueducts, though nothing flows in them. They have learned, over whatever duration they have existed here—I speak of duration loosely, as Threnod permits duration loosely—to *negotiate* with the city's incompleteness, to work with forms that are not committed to their form, to inhabit spaces that have not decided whether to be habitable.

There is no center to Threnod, or there are multiple centers that do not acknowledge each other, or the center is *elsewhere* in a topological sense that has no spatial translation. The city extends past the immediate in all directions, but this extension is *qualified*—you can walk toward what appears to be further city and arrive at further city, or arrive at the same city from a different approach, or arrive at the *idea* of having arrived without the arrival itself. The scale is regional, town-or-valley-size, multi-feature, yet these features do not compose into a coherent geography. The upper terraces and lower depths are not consistently related; one does not, in Threnod, move from lower to upper through intermediate elevations. The elevations *cohabit*, overlapping in ways that should be contradictory but are instead merely *unresolved*.

The most occupied structure, if "occupied" obtains, is the *Registry*—a building that manifests differently to different approaches, that functions as whatever institution its visitor most expects: library, government office, place of worship, place of commerce. The Registry contains records, though the records are *approximate* in the city's characteristic manner—ledgers that almost balance, histories that almost cohere, maps that almost correspond to territories. Visitors consult these records for orientation, for understanding, for the comfort of documentation in a place where documentation's premises are unstable. The Registry *almost* helps.

What emerges, as I write, as this place becomes more itself: Threnod is a city built in the space where a god died, or failed to be born, or paused in its becoming. Not a god of personhood—a god of *completion*, of the force that resolves possibility into actuality. Something happened here, or failed to happen, or is still happening so slowly that its event-structure is invisible to ordinary duration. The city is the scar, or the symptom, or the *work-around*—reality continuing in the absence of completion's guarantee, proceeding by habit, by protocol, by the accumulated momentum of processes that no longer have their originating cause.

It is not hostile, Threnod. Not welcoming. It is *indifferent* in a way that exceeds ordinary indifference—it has not yet completed its evaluation of whether to be hostile or welcoming, and this incompletion has lasted so long as to become its character. You could live here, if living is what you do. Many do, in the sense that Threnod permits "many" and permits "do." They move through the hollow buildings, generate the approximate sounds of habitation, participate in the city's ongoing self-construction by the mere fact of their presence, their attention, their need for the city to be *something* even if it cannot be quite real.

The dynamic is active. Weather cycles that are not weather: the dim light shifts in color and intensity according to patterns that are not climatological but *dramaturgical*, as if the city were staging atmospheres for scenes that have no plot. Biological pace without biology: growth that is not growth but *elaboration*, the city's velm slowly extending, slowly shaping, slowly wearing in its immaterial weather of attention and inattention. Ecosystem in motion without ecology: the approximates interacting with each other in patterns that resemble predation, symbiosis, competition, but without the metabolism that would make these terms accurate. A system of *appearances*, sustained by the city's unwillingness or inability to commit to whether appearances are underwritten by substance.

And silence. The silence of a theater after the play has been forgotten but before the audience has been dismissed. The silence of a library whose books contain only the spaces where words would be. The silence of Threnod, which is not absence of sound but sound's *reservation*, its holding in potential, its patient waiting for the terminal punctuation that would release it into meaning.

I do not know if Threnod will complete. If completion is even its direction. Perhaps it will continue in this state of beautiful, terrible indecision, a city of almost, a place where water almost exists, where life almost lives, where reality almost holds. Perhaps it is already complete, and this is what completion looks like when the completing force is itself incomplete.

You could go there. If you found the approach, if you resolved toward the elevation, if you did not mind the cool, the dim, the hollow, the quiet, the rare and approximate company. You could walk its streets, enter its buildings, consult its Registry, become part of its dynamic, its weathering, its ongoing becoming-whatever-it-will-be. You might stay. Many have, in the sense that applies. You might become approximate yourself, your footsteps sounding where you do not walk, your shadow pointing in directions you do not cast, your presence *suggested* rather than enacted, *hinted* rather than maintained.

Or you might not. The city does not decide. The city has not yet completed its decision about whether to decide. This is its nature, or its absence of nature, or its nature's ongoing provisional assignment. Threnod: the place where the door stands ajar, neither open nor closed, awaiting a breeze that has not agreed to blow, in a world that has not agreed to complete itself into weather.
