The world is called **Hush**, though no one knows why anymore—no one can hear a name spoken here, or anything else below the thunder.

It begins with water, always water, though never quite where you expect it. The land sits low, lower than the surrounding nothing, a vast declivity cupped in still-forming palms of raw geology. The erosion parameter reads as *emerging*, which in Hush means the ground has not decided what it wants to be. Basalt pillars rise with the ridged texture of cooling, of having just now, *just this instant*, finished their phase change from liquid stone. Their sides show flow patterns still, like water frozen in the act of fleeing. Smaller formations—tables, benches, low walls that might be natural or might be the first gestures of architecture—steam at their edges, not from heat but from the simple chemical fact of their birth into warm air. Everything is condensation. Water beads on every surface, runs in threads too small for the word *stream*, gathers in depressions that have no drainage, no outlet, no plan. The atmosphere weeps continuously. Not rain, exactly, but a persistent settling, a fog that has given up on horizontal motion and simply descends, touches stone, becomes a film, becomes a pool, becomes a mirror that reflects nothing because there is no light sufficient for reflection.

---

And the light. The light is dim, but this requires qualification. The sky—if sky is the word for the pressure above—glows with a constant amber suffusion, the color of mammal fat burning in a closed room. It is warm here, consistently, bodily warm, the temperature of blood or of a fever dream. The glow has no source anyone has found. It simply persists, time-invariant, the same at all hours that might be hours, unchanging in a way that suggests not *noon held forever* but *time's irrelevance to this place*. Shadows are shallow and reluctant. They do not lengthen or shrink; they hold their positions like servants waiting for instructions that never come. What this means practically is that nothing is ever fully visible. Forms emerge from the amber, are suggested by moisture-beaded surfaces, resolve partially and withdraw. The dimness is not darkness but *insufficiency*, a failure of information to complete itself.

---

The vegetation. There is none, or none recognized.

In a place so warm, so wet, so chemically active, you would expect explosion: ferns, mosses, the first scrambling vascular plants at minimum, cyanobacteria sheening every wet stone. Instead, Hush presents a mineral consciousness. What grows here accretes. The water, supersaturated with dissolved minerals in the warm stillness, deposits. Where drops hang longest—under overhangs, in the shelter of embryonic pillars—material builds in forms that mimic organic growth without organic process. Stalactite gardens descend in blind profusion, not the patient work of millennia but the rapid precipitation of hours, days. They are fragile, these growths. Touch them and they crumble to damp powder, their crystal lattices too new, too eager, too *young* to hold structural memory. And they are everywhere, these pseudo-biological mineral excrescences, making the world into something almost vegetal in its density of forms. Shelves, fronds, cupped structures that would collect water if water were not already the continuous condition. They crowd every surface, layer upon layer, a forest of stone-in-the-process-of-becoming that creates the *density* of the parameter without life contributing.

---

The density. This is the defining experience of Hush.

The world is crowded. Not with people, though there are people, but with *presences*. The mineral growths cluster near-touching, fighting for surface area, for condensate, for position in this still-forming geology. Sound is deafening—this is the great paradox—and the density contributes. The thunder, the continuous low thunder that is Hush's only constant noise, reflects off these countless surfaces. The sound has nowhere to go, no gradient to follow, no escape into open air. It bounces, accumulates, interferes with itself. The result is a pressure, a physical weight on the chest and eardrums, a felt density of acoustic energy that matches and exceeds the visual crowding of forms. Walk through Hush and you move through pockets of differential thunder, nodes where frequencies reinforce or cancel in patterns that shift not with time—for nothing shifts with time here—but with *position*, with your own movement through the fixed acoustic field. The density is spatial, volumetric, a saturation of the possible.

---

And the people. The built environment. The *urban*.

They are primitive, these inhabitants, in the specific sense of the parameter: early tool-use, pre-metal or simple metal, manual craft. They have not developed metallurgy suited to this wetness; iron blooms to rust, copper greens, bronze holds longest but pits, develops mineral sympathy with the growing stone. They work instead with what the emerging geology provides: the volcanic glass that forms at cooling edges, sharp as any blade and impervious to the weeping atmosphere; the soft stone of fresh pillars, carvable while young, hardening irreversibly as it ages. Their tools are of these materials, and of bone, and of a curious substance they recover from the mineral accretions—a compressed organic residue, pre-life, the chemical shadow of what might have grown and did not, preserved in precipitate strata. They shape this into implements with properties no smith could predict, tools that seem to *want* certain forms, that guide the hand toward functions.

They are abundant, these people, as abundant as the fauna parameter demands. And here is another paradox, for the fauna is 2.1/3—abundant—yet the world reads as somehow empty of conventional animals. What fills the fauna slot, what makes the census ache with presence, is *them*, the people, and something else, something in the mineral growths, in the way they accrete and compete and die (crumble) and are replaced. The inhabitants of Hush recognize this. They do not distinguish sharply between the living and the growing, between the animal and the mineral-in-process. Their urban organization reflects this.

The city—if city is the word for dense settlement without agriculture, without hinterland, without the metabolic supports we assume—exists in the interstices of the emerging geology. They build against the fresh pillars, incorporate them, carve while carvable, occupy cavities that will seal and harden in geological instants around their forms. The architecture is layered, vertical, exploiting every surface of the dense mineral forest. Platforms span between pillars, lashed with vegetal substitutes (those organic residues, compressed, processed to rope), creating a three-dimensional occupancy that climbs and descends without the horizontal expansion conventional cities demand. Streets are vertical shafts, ladder-descents, the narrowest possible passages between competing accretions. The city is *in* the geology, not on it, a symbiosis with the emerging land that recognizes no difference between constructed and found.

They are deaf, most of them, the inhabitants. Not congenitally—though some are, now, generations in—but actively, protectively, the eardrums scarred and thickened against the thunder. They communicate by touch, by gesture in the amber dimness, by vibration conducted through the stone itself. They press hands to pillars and feel the thunder's shape, its local variations, read the acoustic field as others read weather. Their social density, their urban crowding, is thus intensely tactile. They know each other's bodies with a precision that would seem intimate elsewhere; here, in the pressing crowd, in the near-touching verticality of their architecture, it is simply efficient, simply *necessary*. Personal space is a concept they have no term for. The closest they come is a gesture indicating the minimum safe distance from a fresh accretion, before it hardens, before it becomes too sharp or too brittle or too *new* to touch safely.

---

The rules of Hush.

Time does not pass, or passes so slowly that all change is geological, all history compressed into the single extended moment of emergence. The inhabitants do not age in the familiar way; they accrete experience like the mineral growths accrete mass, layer upon layer without clear discontinuity between self and memory, between individual and the collective touch-knowledge of the city. A child is born, is held, is passed hand to hand through the vertical shafts, learns the thunder's patterns, the safe paths through the acoustic nodes, the locations where the stone is still young enough to eat (they consume the soft precipitates, the organic residues, mineral nutrition in a world without plants). There is no before, no after, no narrative of improvement or decline. The city is, has been, is being, will be—verbs collapse into a continuous present that matches the unchanging dynamics, the equilibrium without gradient.

What changes—for change exists, must exist, even here—is *position*. The city grows in extent as the emerging geology extends, as new pillars cool from below, as the declivity slowly, imperceptibly, widens its cup. The inhabitants explore, occupy, abandon when the stone hardens beyond utility. Their movement through the fixed amber light, through the deafening stable thunder, through the weeping unchanging atmosphere, is the only motion available, the only story. They are nomads of position, settlers of the instantaneous, a dense urban society without history, without agriculture, without the external, without the exit.

---

The weirdness is low. Normal, the parameter says. And this, perhaps, is the strangest thing.

Hush is not alien. It is *intimate*, recognizable, a nightmare of sufficiency rather than deprivation. The warmth is bodily. The wetness is like fever sweat, like tears, like the condensation on a glass held too long. The thunder is the thunder any child fears, simply continuous, simply *never stopping*. The people are human in proportion, human in their social cramming, their touch-communication, their vertical urbanism. They love, as far as love can be distinguished from the general tactile density; they quarrel over position, over access to young stone, over the interpretation of thunder-patterns. Their gods, if gods are the word, are the accretions themselves, the emerging geology that provides and threatens and is simply *there*, unchanging in its change, stable in its emergence. They do not worship these powers; they negotiate with them by posture, by placement, by the careful choice of where to sleep, where to eat, where to let their own bodies contribute to the mineral precipitation when they finally stop moving, finally become, in death, part of the growing, the dense, the urban, the emerged and emerging land.

---

At the edge of Hush, if edge is a meaningful concept, the parameters would read differently. The water would lessen or intensify, the vegetation find its chance, the light brighten or fail entirely, the thunder perhaps find its gradient and escape into distance. But no one in Hush has reached such an edge, if it exists. The world extends past the immediate, regional, town-or-valley-size, multi-feature, mid-range—and extends equally in all directions, the same warm dim weeping thunder-crowded density, the same urban primitivism, the same emergence that never completes, the same equilibrium that never shifts.

They have stories of edges, the inhabitants, told in touch, in vibration through stone. Stories of places where the thunder stops, where the amber fails, where the wetness gives way to something else—dread or desired, they cannot say. These stories function not as geography but as theology, as the structuring absence that lets the dense presence mean. Everyone in Hush carries such stories, layer upon layer, accreted like the mineral growths, indistinguishable from the self, from the city, from the warm weeping never-changing deafening emerging world.

They are there now, if *now* is the word. Pressing close in the vertical shafts. Communicating by hand on stone. Eating the soft precipitates. Giving birth, dying, becoming mineral, contributing to the density, the abundance, the urban emergence of a place that has not yet decided what it wants to be, that waits in equilibrium, that holds all parameters in suspension, that is called Hush for reasons no living ear could hear spoken anyway.
