The Woundworks of Velm

I.

There is a plain where darkness is not the absence of light but something more fundamental—the substrate from which light has been *forgotten*. You arrive there by accident, usually. The maps show fertile delta country, rice terraces, a river braiding through loam. And then the road tilts, though no hill rises, and the sky above becomes a ceiling you can feel pressing against your hair, and you understand that you have passed into Velm.

The darkness has texture. It varies in *depth*. In some valleys of this open land—spanning perhaps sixty kilometers from rim to rim, though distance here is a negotiation—you can raise your hand and see it at arm's length, a pale ghost. In other zones, the darkness *drinks* photons actively: torches gutter, batteries drain in hours, bioluminescence itself seems to drown. The locals, if they can be called that, navigate by sound, by temperature gradients, by the distinct *flavor* of moisture on the air. They say the dark tastes metallic near the factories, sweet near the seep-zones, brackish where the old things move.

II.

Water is everywhere but nowhere free. The water table sits at ankle-depth across most of the Woundworks, and it *weeps*. Not from springs—though those exist—but from the ground itself, a capillary action that seems to mourn. The soil, where there is soil, clings to your boots in ropes of gray-black paste. More often there is no soil, only a thin crust of mineral deposit over mudstone so fresh it still smells of its violent birth. The erosion parameter here is *young*, and it shows: no rounded hills, no smoothed riverstones. The terrain is raw, recently-emerged, as if something beneath had *pushed up* in geologic hastiness.

The water that surfaces does strange things. It pools in basins that ignore gravity's logic, running sometimes *uphill* to collect in depressions invisible to any survey. It carries silicates, sulfur compounds, something organic that does not match any catalogued biochemistry. Where it evaporates—and evaporation is constant in the warm, dense air—it leaves *films*, nacreous skins that flake and blow like shed snakeskin. The locals collect these. They paper their walls with them, build kites, fashion crude lenses that darken rather than focus.

III.

Warmth here is not comfortable. The temperature hovers near 28°C but the humidity—always the humidity—pushes heat into a different register. The air is *dense* with presences. Not merely moisture, though that: something else occupies the atmospheric volume. Spores, certainly. Mineral dust. But also particulate matter from the factories, and something finer still, some breakdown product of the reality-instability that gives the Woundworks its name. You breathe and your lungs feel *populated*. Two-point-oh on the density scale: near-touching, layered, high-saturation. The locals wear masks not for filtration but for *separation*, a ritual boundary between lung and world.

The warmth has its inhabitants. Fauna is abundant—2.3—and these creatures have adapted to darkness the way deep-sea things have adapted to pressure. Not blind, most of them; rather, they perceive through other channels entirely. The velm-crickets stridulate in frequencies below hearing, their songs *felt* in the sternum, in the fillings of teeth. Their populations pulse with slow regularity—dynamic near-zero here, change across long durations—and a traveler might sit for hours in apparent silence before realizing the ground itself vibrates with a billion simultaneous communications.

There are larger things. The stilt-walkers, eight-limbed and three meters tall, whose bones phosphoresce faintly in the deepest dark zones—not enough to see by, but enough to *be seen by*, a vulnerability they seem not to share. They move with the patience of the Woundworks itself, crossing a plain in a week, harvesting mineral films, drinking from seeps. Their metabolism is so slow that injury is a life-event; a torn tendon takes years to express itself as limp. They do not react to quick movements. They react, perhaps, to *intentions*, sensed through channels unknown.

And there are things that fly, or *move through* the air without flying, things for which the erosion of reality has loosened certain constraints. They are mostly quiet, these presences. The soundscape of Velm is 1.4, mostly quiet, but that *mostly* conceals oceans. The factories grind always, but their frequencies are infrasonic, felt as unease. The weeping water makes no sound. The fauna communicates in channels we do not receive. What you hear, in the Woundworks, is the occasional *crack* of crust settling, the *whisper* of nacreous film detaching, the rare and terrible *voice* of something that has found a way to speak in audible frequencies—usually lost, usually afraid, usually not addressed to you.

IV.

The factories.

They are the reason anything human persists in this broken place. Tech at 2.2: industrial, mechanized, scaled production, electrified—and yet *strange*, because the electricity here does not behave reliably, because the machines operate in darkness, because the assembly lines process materials that have no name in standard metallurgy.

The factories are scattered—built at 1.2—single buildings, partial remains, occasional artifacts. You might walk for a day through raw mudstone and suddenly find a structure: windowless, humming, lit only by the cherry-red glow of furnaces that seem to burn without fuel. They process the seep-water, extract its impossible compounds, cast them into ingots of something *almost* aluminum, *almost* ceramic. The output feeds no supply chain you can trace. Trucks arrive empty and leave empty, or full of something that registers on no sensor. The workers—there are workers—live in the structures or nearby, in lean-tos of nacreous film and scrap.

I spoke with one, once. She had no name she would share. She described her job as "persuading the metal," a phrase I have never unwound. The factories, she said, do not *make* things. They *convince* things to become other things, and the darkness helps, because in darkness material is less *certain* of itself.

V.

The reality breakdown.

Weirdness at 2.7: this is the gravity that holds the Woundworks in its orbit. I have seen it in increments.

First: the water that runs uphill. The small betrayals of physics that might be illusion, might be measurement error.

Then: the zones where distance *varies*. You walk toward a factory for an hour, resting twice, and arrive no closer. You turn, walk back, and reach your starting point in minutes. Or you walk toward it again, and arrive in minutes from a direction you did not approach.

Then: the *doublings*. Your shadow, in the rare moments when light is introduced, sometimes moves independently, sometimes precedes you, sometimes *discusses* you with other shadows. You learn not to carry light, which is why the rating is 0.0: not absence but *avoidance*, a darkness maintained by consensus.

Then: the history that *accumulates* regardless of occurrence. Structures appear old that were not present yesterday. The locals speak of events that have not happened, will not happen, *are happening* in adjacent folds of time. A woman told me of her grandmother's execution by the old regime, wept genuine tears, then blinked and said "but that is next year, I am confused."

The deepest strangeness, I think, is *purposeful*. The Woundworks is not broken randomly. It is broken *toward* something. The factories, the seep-water, the raw young terrain still settling from its violent emergence—all of it orients toward a center I have never reached, a place where the darkness achieves its maximum depth and, locals whisper, something is *growing*.

VI.

Vegetation at 1.4: sparse, yes, but what exists is *committed*. No trees. Something like lichen carpets the rare stable surfaces, phosphorescing faintly enough that you sense rather than see it. Fungal towers rise in the seep-zones, porous, warm to touch, exhaling spores that participate in the density of air. And in the cracks of fresh mudstone, something *almost* vascular, *almost* neural, networks of filament that the stilt-walkers harvest and chew for reasons of nutrition or ritual or communication with the growing thing at the center.

The sparse vegetation means no cover, in a landscape where cover might be desired. But the darkness provides its own concealment, and the fauna that might hunt you—some do—hunt by means other than sight. The filaments in the mudstone, I have come to believe, are *aware*. Not conscious, but responsive. Step heavily and they transmit vibration for kilometers. Stand still long enough and they begin to *expect* you, their chemistry adjusting, altering the local seep patterns, the humidity, the very reality-stability of your immediate zone.

VII.

I have tried to leave the Woundworks three times.

First by the road I entered, which no longer tilts in any direction, which becomes the same mudstone plain in all directions, which I walked for two days before finding myself at the same factory I had passed on entry.

Second by following a stilt-walker, reasoning that its slow patience must lead somewhere. It led to a seep-zone deeper than any I had found, where the water rose to my knees and something *tasted* my calves with currents that had no temperature. I returned, if return is the word, in hours that contained no memory.

Third by waiting for the slow dynamics to shift, for some seasonal or cyclical change to alter the Woundworks' hold. The cycles here are long, patient, weak in gradient. I have been here—*experienced* here—perhaps six weeks. The locals say I arrived yesterday. The factories say I have worked there for years. My own nacreous film collection, which I do not remember beginning, spans twelve meters of wall in a lean-to I do not remember building.

VIII.

What holds this place together, what makes it describable, is the *coherence of its incoherence*. The parameters seem to conflict: open scale yet dense atmosphere, warm yet dark, young terrain yet ancient factories, abundant fauna yet quiet, industrial yet brokenly non-standard. But they collaborate. The darkness permits the factories to operate in impossible ways. The youth of the terrain, its rawness, provides channels for the seep-water that feeds those operations. The density of air carries spores that maintain the reality-breakdown as an *ecology*, not merely a destruction. The slow dynamics mean nothing resolves quickly; the Woundworks persists in its wounded state without healing or dying.

And I persist in it. I have begun to perceive without light, to navigate by the metallic taste near factories, the sweet toward seep-zones. I have begun, I think, to *persuade* materials, to speak to metal in the darkness until it agrees to become what I need. My walls grow. My films thicken into something like windows, showing—when I press my face close—other versions of this place, adjacent folds, the executions that have not happened, the roads that tilt back to worlds where light behaves predictably.

I do not know if I will leave. I do not know if the concept applies. The Woundworks is *young*, they say, recently-emerged, still forming. Perhaps it will stabilize, the darkness lifting to reveal a new valley, fertile, mapped, ordinary. Perhaps it will deepen, the center will open, the growing thing will emerge and the parameters will shift beyond any scale's measurement.

What I know: the water weeps, and I weep with it, mineral films on my cheeks. The factories hum below hearing. The stilt-walkers pass, patient, harvesting, *almost* acknowledging my presence. And somewhere, in the maximum depth of darkness, something—I use the word for lack of better—*gestates*.

The Woundworks of Velm. Wet and warm and dense with what breathes and breaks and builds. Young as violence, old as the forgetting of light. I write this by touch, in darkness absolute, and when I read it back my fingers tell me the words have *shifted*, become other words, become maps, become invitations.

Perhaps you will find them. Perhaps you have already arrived. The road tilts, does it not? The sky presses. Taste the air: metallic, sweet, brackish?

Welcome, then. Take your time. The dynamics are slow. There is duration enough to become whatever this place requires.
