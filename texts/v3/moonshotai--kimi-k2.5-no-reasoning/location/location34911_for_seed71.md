The Plateau of Unnamed Hours

---

To reach the edge, you would walk for weeks through flatlands that do not remember mountains. Then the ground simply stops—not cliffs, not the drama of erosion's violence, but a *cessation*, as if the world agreed here that downward was no longer necessary. The plateau extends beyond the curvature that should limit it. Cartographers who have tried to map its full extent have produced sheets that fold into themselves, that show, when held to candlelight, coastlines that belong to no known shore. The plateau has weather of its own. Storms form in its interior that never reach its margins, and the margins sometimes experience sunlight that the central expanse has never known.

The rock here is older than granularity. Geologists who chip samples find no crystals, no strata, no fossil record—only a uniform substance the color of closed eyelids, dense as bone, that accepts blows without marking. They have stopped trying to date it. The rock has achieved its final form, finished with the restlessness of younger geology. It has settled into itself the way a body settles into death, except it persists, persisting being, apparently, its chosen function.

And yet it is not dead, this surface. The cold keeps it awake. The temperature hovers low enough that breath feathers and fingers numb, but not so low as to forbid the strange lushness that outbreaks across the stone. Vegetation here does not root so much as *agree* with the rock, finding purchase in agreements we cannot measure. The plants are not of familiar kingdoms. They cluster infrequently—great distances separate each colony, each grove, each solitary specimen that has attained, over centuries or longer, the mass of a small hill. Between them: the sparse, the generous emptiness, stone running to horizons that may be the same horizon seen from different angles.

The damp comes not from rain, though rain occurs, but from condensation stranger than meteorology allows. Moisture gathers on the rock's surface at twilight, not falling but *arriving*, as if the stone itself were breathing out. Pockets of liquid form in hollows that have no business being hollow, being too shallow for hydrology, too regular for chance. These pools do not evaporate. They shrink sometimes, grow others, and in their depths reflect not the sky above but *a* sky, elsewhere, elsewhere in the process of clouding over. The water is drinkable. Those who drink report dreams of drowning in air.

---

The cities, if cities are what these are, rise where the vegetation thickens, where several groves have agreed to proximity. They are not built upon the stone so much as quarried *from* it, or *with* it, in a grammar of construction that predates architecture as we understand it. The inhabitants—if inhabitants is the word—work with tools of obsidian and fired clay, with metals that do not appear in any periodic table, tools that seem to shape the worker as much as the worked. Their technology is simple, manual, the product of hands that may not have five fingers, that may not be hands. The cities spread at the scale of towns, dense with infrastructure whose purpose unfolds slowly to foreign perception: channels that carry not water but sound, or the memory of sound; chambers whose ceilings are lower than their floors, navigable nonetheless; towers that narrow downward to points that do not pierce the stone but *continue* into it, as if the visible tower were only the emergent portion of something mostly buried, or mostly elsewhere.

These urban spaces are layered. Excavations—if that's the word—reveal older constructions beneath, and older still, not in stratigraphy but in *enfoldment*, each layer contemporary with the others, accessible through certain doorways, certain shifts in attention. The deepest layers are the most inhabited. The surface levels seem almost abandoned, maintained perhaps from obligation, from the weight of what the city remembers about being a city.

The cities do not trade with each other. What would they trade? They do not war. What would war mean here? They simply persist, each at its own pace of activity, its own weather of social process, processes that occur in timescales difficult to witness. A street that appears empty for days may, observed long enough, reveal itself as crowded with presences moving too slowly for casual perception, or too quickly, blurring through the visible like comets through a long exposure.

---

There is no animal life. The ecological functions that elsewhere would be distributed across kingdoms—consumption, predation, decay, reproduction—occur here through other means. The plants process stone into themselves, and something else, unplantlike, processes plants back toward stone, not through digestion but through *negotiation*, through a settling of accounts that leaves neither party unchanged. The dynamic of the plateau is active, cycling, yet without the urgency of animal metabolism. Seasons pass, if seasons can be said to pass in a place where weather is local to the mile, and the groves shift in color, in density, in the songs they make in wind that moves at angles to the compass.

For there is sound, though mostly the place is quiet. The silence has weight, has texture. It is not absence but *presence compressed*, a medium you move through, thicker in some valleys, thinner on some heights. What sounds do occur—vegetal creaking, the *tone* of stone settling into further settlement, the strange harmonics of those water-pools when disturbed—emerge as figures against this ground, not breaking the quiet but *articulating* it, giving it grammar. In the cities, the infrastructure channels sound as others channel water, and certain chambers resonate at frequencies that induce not hearing but *memory*, memories of sounds never experienced, or always about to be.

---

The reality breakdown does not announce itself. You will not see holes in the world, not exactly, not unless you are looking for them, and looking the wrong way. The breakdown is subtler, structural, the way a familiar room becomes strange when you cannot recall why you entered it. Distances on the plateau are not consistent with themselves. Walk toward a grove and it may retreat at precisely your pace of approach, may arrive suddenly as you walk away, may exist simultaneously at multiple removes, accessible in its different locations by different methods of attention. The cities are not always where they were, or where they will be, but they are never lost, only *relocated*, discoverable by those who do not search directly but who maintain the intention to arrive, eventually, permitting the path to unfold.

Time is the most broken thing. Not ruined—*distributed*. In the same vista you may perceive processes occurring at rates impossible to reconcile: a plant completing its century-growth in the interval of a cloud's shadow crossing it; a city tower eroding at a speed visible to patience, while beside it water stands in a pool that has not rippled for what local measurement would call eons, the two coexisting without paradox, without even the tension of juxtaposition. The plateau does not violate the laws of physics so much as *encompass* their local exceptions, making of each exception a small jurisdiction with its own constabulary of cause and effect.

Those who live here—if live is the word, if here is the word—do not experience this as weird. Their existence has adjusted to the conditions, or the conditions have adjusted to their existence, in a reciprocity too ancient to distinguish. They move through their cities, their groves, their intervals of stone and condensation, with the unremarkable competence of fish in water, fish who have never imagined air. They make their tools. They tend their infrastructure. They do not look up at the sky, or if they do, they see what is there to see, which is not what others would see, or not only what others would see.

---

At certain hours—hours that the plateau names without sharing the naming—the light shifts. The normal illumination, diffuse, sourceless-seeming though the sun must somewhere operate, gathers into concentrations, into *attentions*, beams that touch particular features with an emphasis almost vocal. A pool will glow, briefly, with the color of its elsewhere-sky. A tower's buried continuation will become momentarily visible through the stone, a ghost geometry extending downward, or inward, or elsewhere. The groves respond to these lit hours with movement too slow for the interval of light to contain, movement that must therefore be occurring across some fold of time, completing itself in other whens.

The plateau has no center, or the center is everywhere, or the center is what the plateau refuses to be, distributed across its vastness like the silence that carries sound, like the damp that arrives without rain, like the cities that maintain themselves in layers of enfolded now. It has been here long enough that its age is not a quality but a *dimension*, not something it possesses but something it extends into, a direction in which it continues, settled so deeply into final form that the form has become indistinguishable from process, from the ongoing of its own ongoing.

To leave—those who leave, what they report, what they cannot report—is to carry with you a certain slowness, a certain distribution of your own presence. You may find yourself in places that do not hold all of you. Part of you continues, perhaps, walking toward that grove that retreats at your pace, drinking from that pool that shows an elsewhere sky, inhabiting a doorway in a city layer you never found while there. The plateau does not detain you. It simply does not recognize the absolute of departure. You are damp there, still. You are cold there, still. You are sparse and lush and ancient and vast, still, in the measure of you that remained, that agreed to stay, that continues to agree, that will continue until the agreement achieves its own final form, its own settled settlement into the stone that is older than granularity, that accepts blows without marking, that is, in its patient persistence, the closest the plateau comes to welcoming, to the warmth of refusal to change, to the cold that keeps it all awake.

---

*There is no end to describing it. That is one of the conditions. Description continues, distributed across those who have approached, who have agreed, who have not quite left, who find themselves still arriving, the approach measured in increments too slow for the life that measures, too fast for the stone that accepts, that persists, that is the plateau's own description of itself, ongoing, unfinishable, vast enough for weather of its own, and having that weather, and being that weather, and waiting, if waiting is what it does, for what comes after the final form, which is only more form, more final, more settled, more awake in the cold, more damp with what arrives, more sparse with what it holds between, more lush with what grows in the agreement between stone and what is not stone, what has never been stone, what will not remember being anything else.*
