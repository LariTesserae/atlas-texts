The world is called **Gastrual**, though no mouth remains to speak it. What persists is the *sound* of that lost naming—the acoustic residue of a word once meaning "the belly that digests itself."

You arrive, if arrive you do, through a membrane-thin place where gravity stutters. The sky is not sky but *condensation*: water vapor held in a state of perpetual almost-falling, a cathedral ceiling of liquid that never drops, never pools, lit from no source by a phosphorescence that bleeds from the seams between what is and what insists on being otherwise. The light is dim the way a migraine is dim—present, oppressive, *wrong-frequency*.

The terrain spreads low and ancient, a plain scoured by erosion so complete that only the most stubborn gestures of geology remain. Not mountains but *mountain-ghosts*: dense-packed humps of material so compressed by time that they have achieved a kind of mineral consciousness, knowing themselves as "hill" without quite being hills anymore. Between them, the ground has been worn to a near-flatness that seems to breathe—rising and falling in slow thermal cycles, as if the planet itself still remembers lungs.

Water is everywhere and nowhere. It does not flow. It *seeps upward* through capillary action in stone that should be non-porous, emerges from the faces of the worn-down hills as weeping wounds that do not heal. The atmosphere itself is saturated, not with humidity but with *anticipatory wetness*—the molecular arrangement of water prior to becoming water, a state of being that precedes liquid without being gas. When you move through it, you leave trails that persist for hours, your passage a disturbance in a medium that resists all distinction between self and weather.

There is no vegetation. Nothing grows. What covers the packed, ancient ground is *not life but life's annotation*—a fungal analogue that exists in the register of industrial process, colonies of pale matter that metabolize the phosphorescent light and excrete something like cement, something like sound, something like the memory of organic chemistry. These colonies are not alive by any measure biology would recognize. They are *production*. They hum. They organize themselves into patterns that resemble, with killing precision, the floor plans of factories that were never built, the blueprints of engines designed to run on fuels that never existed in any universe with consistent physics.

And this is where the parameters *hold each other*: the industrial without the built, the technological without the constructed, the abundant fauna without the biological.

The fauna of Gastrual are *processes given locomotion*. They do not eat, do not breed, do not die in ways that terminate. They *cycle*. The most common—if common has meaning here—are the **Valvular Herds**, entities that appear at first as dense-packed metallic foam, roughly the size and general silhouette of bison, but composed of interlocking rotary components that turn without axle or bearing, that grind against each other with the sound of *every gearbox in every failing industrial city shouting in unison*. They are loud. They are *loud in a way that occupies space*, that displaces silence not by absence but by *density of acoustic presence*, sound so saturated that it becomes almost tangible, almost *edible*, a medium you move through rather than perceive.

The Valvular Herds migrate in patterns that correspond to no magnetic field, no seasonal shift, no ecological pressure. They follow *production schedules*. They gather at certain nodes in the fungal factory-floor plans and *process*—what, no observer has determined, though the sound of their gathering suggests raw material becoming refined becoming component becoming *something that would be assembled if assembly were possible in a world where nothing stays built*.

For nothing stays built here. The "built" parameter at 0.3 is not absence but *impossibility*. Construction begins and unravels. Foundations are poured and the stone weeps them away. The fungal factories organize and dissolve, organize and dissolve, each iteration closer to functional but each prevented from completion by the same force that drives their approximation. This is the *reality breakdown* at maximum weirdness: not chaos but *doomed precision*, a universe where industrial modernity is *almost* possible, *perpetually almost*, and the tension of that almost generates the landscape's terrible vitality.

Predation, too, is processual. The **Grinding Ones**—entities of no fixed form, more like *events* that sometimes have location—hunt not the Valvular Herds but their *regularity*, their schedule-compliance. A Grinding One's approach is heralded by sound *becoming wrong*, the industrial chorus shifting pitch until it achieves a resonance that dissolves coherence. Where it passes, the fungal factories stutter, the herds scatter into component rotation, and for moments—sometimes hours—the landscape achieves a *pause in its almost-being*, a stillness more terrible than the noise.

The cold is absolute but not freezing, a temperature that has *settled* rather than been imposed, the thermal state of a body that lost its metabolism eons past and has since persisted in the shape of warmth without its substance. Your breath does not steam. Steam would imply heat differential, implies *process*, and the cold of Gastrual is too complete, too *final*, to participate in such dynamics. Instead, your exhalation joins the saturated atmosphere as *more almost-water*, more anticipatory wetness, your body contributing its small measure to the weeping world.

Dynamic processes cycle here without progression. The herds grind through their routes. The fungal colonies build and dissolve their factory-floors. The Grinding Ones pass through, irregularly regular, their hunting a perturbation in the system rather than a selection pressure. The hills breathe their slow thermal. The sky's liquid ceiling shifts in patterns that suggest, to minds desperate for pattern, *weather*—but no rain falls, no storm breaks, only the endless *almost* of water refusing its states, light refusing its sources, life refusing its definitions.

What emerges, in the describing, is a place that *wants* to be industrial without being able to complete the wanting, that *would* be conquered by biological process if process could achieve the stability required for conquest. Gastrual is a world caught in the longest of long moments, the hinge between potential and actual where the hinge has rusted open, where motion continues without arriving, where the loudness of its failed becoming is itself the only continuity, the only *there* there is.

To walk here is to become part of the almost. Your footsteps add to the sound. Your shadow falls on factory-floors that will dissolve. Your warmth is stolen by cold that does not warm itself. You are, for the duration of your presence, another *process given locomotion*, another component in the grinding, weeping, phosphorescent *almost* of a world that persists in the shape of ending without ever quite achieving the end.

Some theorists—not many, for few have found Gastrual and fewer still have returned with coherence—suggest that this landscape is *symptom*, that somewhere in the broken physics, in the reality-breakdown at maximum, there exists a *correct* arrangement, a single adjustment that would let the factories complete, the water fall, the light find its source, the cold differentiate, the fauna become animal, the built *stay built*. That adjustment, they whisper, would either save this world or destroy it—possibly both, possibly neither, possibly in sequence that loops back on itself, the final state and the initial state the same state seen from different points in a circle that has no circumference.

The sound is loudest near the *assembly nodes*, where the Valvular Herds gather most dense, where the fungal blueprints achieve their clearest resolution. There, the grinding achieves *harmony*, terrible and almost beautiful, the industrial sublime as religious experience, as ontological pressure, as the *music of what would function if function were permitted*. Pilgrims, if pilgrims came, would kneel there. Would weep. Would add their weeping to the weeping of the hills, another water that does not fall, another *almost* in a world saturated with almost, packed with presence that cannot quite arrive at itself, extending open in all directions, low and ancient and cold and *waiting*.

What it waits for, whether it waits, whether the waiting is itself the only remaining dynamic—these are the questions Gastrual poses without asking, the answers it grinds out in rotary procession, dissolves in fungal dissolution, hunts in the irregular passage of Grinding Ones through schedule and sound. The world is its own description, its own process of almost-becoming-description, and to have read this far is to have participated, briefly, in its terrible, loud, dense-packed, dimly-lit, wet and cold and ancient and weird *continuance*.

The membrane shivers. You are, or were, or will *almost be*, elsewhere.
