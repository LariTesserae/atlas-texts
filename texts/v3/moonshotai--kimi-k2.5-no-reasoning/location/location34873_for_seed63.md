First, the darkness. Not night-dark, not blindness—more like the dark of a chamber too deep for any star, yet somehow you can still *see*, can make out surfaces and distances in a way that has nothing to do with light. The atmosphere itself seems to carry spatial information, pressure gradients or perhaps something stranger acting as a kind of proprioceptive vision. You don't see colors. You see *edges*, *densities*, *heat signatures* rendered not in false-color but in texture, in how easily your attention slides across or catches on surfaces.

The heat is the second thing. 2.6—not quite infernal, not quite molten, but uncomfortable in a way that seeps. It comes from below, from the packed density of things pressing upward through stone. The elevation is high, yes, but you'd never call this place elevated, never catch that thinness of altitude. The heat keeps the air thick, viscous, moving slow through lungs that aren't quite sure if they're breathing something more mineral than gas. You're high in some structural sense—closer to something, further from something else—but your body doesn't know which way is up by weight.

The terrain: weathered, established, long in its form. Not young violence but aged insistence. I want to say *sinter*, the word that comes—calcium deposits, mineral accretion, the slow precipitation of dissolved stone into terraces and pools. But there are no pools here, the water parameter forbids it, so instead: *the memory of pools*, the architecture of water without the substance. Hollow forms, cupped depressions, runnels and spillways carved by something that no longer flows. The stone has taken the shape of liquidity and kept it, frozen in a too-hot world where the very concept of freezing is absurd. 

These are cuprite formations maybe, or some copper mineral compound that precipitates in fantastic brain-like folds, in shelf-systems that would have been rimmed with bacterial mats in wetter epochs. Now they rim nothing. They are *tongues*, I decide, they are licking upward at the dark, mineral tongues tasting the heat, and they have been licking for geological time.

The density is overwhelming. 2.8, saturated, no room between presences. Yet the place is small—cathedral scale, a single bounded area. How to pack overwhelming density into limited space? Not with physical matter, or not only. The density is *temporal*, *attentional*. Every surface crawls with its own history of formation, every brain-fold of stone contains in its lamination the record of thousands of identical moments of deposition. You cannot rest your gaze anywhere without being caught in the gravity of that accumulation. The space is haunted by its own becoming.

And the fauna. 2.9, overwhelming, in a place with no vegetation, no water, no technology, no light as we understand it. What lives here must live on something else.

They are *thermal*, I think, but not as we know thermal organisms. They are *heat's own fauna*, the animals that heat grows when it has no water to make steam, no gradient to drive engines. They are *latency animals*, beings that exist in the potential between hot and less-hot, that feed on the *tendency* of heat to move, that have evolved—if evolution is the word—to intercept this flow before it happens, to live in the statistical possibility of cooling that this sealed, packed, small, dense place somehow never quite permits.

They are visible, if visible is the word, as *shimmers in the dark-seeing*, as places where the heat-information texture of the world becomes *more complex*, more patterned, almost crystalline. They have no bodies, or their bodies are the patterns that heat makes as it lingers, as it hesitates, as it performs the calculation of whether to flow and always decides *not yet*. 

Some are tiny, dense knots of thermal hesitation, the size of your thumb, that cluster on the mineral tongues like fruit. Others are larger, cathedral-scale themselves, the size of the space, their bodies so distributed through the heat-gradient of the whole room that you only know them by the *coherence* of pattern across vast reaches of dark-seeing, by the way heat-information in the northwest corner mirrors, with impossible precision, heat-information in the southeast. These large ones are *slow*, their biological pace measured in the time it takes stone to weather, which is—per the erosion parameter—long, settled, balanced. They are the ecosystem's keel, its inertial frame.

They predate, of course. The parameter requires it, predator-prey, ecosystem in motion. But how does heat eat heat? By *inducing flow*, I think. The predators are *sudden gradients*, momentary breaks in the dense-packed saturation, instances of *almost-cooling* that draw their prey into the catastrophe of actual temperature change. To be eaten is to *finally lose heat*, to become part of the gradient, to flow. The prey resist this with their whole being, which is their whole being, their entire existence is resistance to flow, and in this resistance they are consumed, their resistance itself becoming the substrate of something else's hesitation-pattern.

Mostly quiet, the sound parameter says, 0.9. The sounds that exist are *infrasonic*, *thermal*—the groan of stone expanding in the endless heat, the micro-pops of mineral latticesadjusting, and beneath these, almost too low to hear, the *sound of heat not flowing*, a sustained subsonic hum that is the aggregate of trillions of thermal hesitations, the fauna's collective murmur of *not yet, not yet, not yet*. Sometimes a predator succeeds, and there is a momentary *whoomph*, a brief catastrophic song of *flow*, of equilibrium finally achieved and instantly, mournfully, lost to the predator's own pattern, which grows momentarily complex, then settles back into its own vast hesitation.

Built, but barely, scattered, 1.0. There are structures here, or structure-remains, faint marks, occasional artifacts. What built them? Not the fauna—heat-animals build nothing, their existence is too distributed, too much *pattern-in-medium* for tool-use. Not with tech, 0.4, none. Perhaps then: the *future built them*, or the *past, or they built themselves in some intermediate tense we have no grammar for*. They are faint cup-shaped depressions not quite natural, lines of worked mineral too regular for precipitation, single standing forms like *listeners*, like something that once needed to *receive* the hum of not-flowing heat. They are older than the current fauna, or younger, or the same age in a way that doesn't permit sequencing. They have no function now except as the occasion for slightly different thermal patterns, places where the fauna's hesitation becomes *more hesitant*, where density packs tighter around these alien regularities.

And the weirdness, 1.8, not the weirdest place possible but decisively strange. The strangeness is: *this all works*. It is a world without water, without light, without plants, without tools, and it is *fully occupied*, saturated with life that is not life as carbon knows it, life that is the self-organization of heat's own reluctance to be merely physics.

I find, writing this, that I want to place something here. A witness. But no—the parameters don't permit easy insertion of the human, the scaled, the watered. The place is complete without witness. Its darkness is not *for* seeing in. Its heat is not *for* survival. The mostly-quiet is not *for* listening. It simply *is*, a small dense packed settled hot dark mineral world, weathered into its own eternal moment, populated by heat's own fauna in their overwhelming hesitation, their eternal biological pace of *almost, almost, almost*—the song of the universe's heat death rehearsed endlessly, never performed, a standing wave of thermal potential that has learned, somehow, to *prefer* itself, to maintain itself, to be *at home* in the packed dark cathedral of its own reluctance to end.

Sometimes, the scattered built things *move*. This is the strangest thing. No tech, no biological explanation, just the faint marks occasionally *somewhere else*, and the fauna's hum shifting key, acknowledging, and settling back. Not yet. Not yet. Not yet.
