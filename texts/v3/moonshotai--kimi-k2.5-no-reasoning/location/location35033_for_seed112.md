The sound arrives before anything else—a continuous subsonic thunder that lives in your sternum, your jawbone, the hollow behind your teeth. You do not hear it so much as become a resonating chamber for it, your body transformed into a receiving apparatus for frequencies below and above human audition. The deafness it produces is paradoxical: not silence but *too much* sound, a saturated field where no individual signal can emerge. You are overwhelmed into a state resembling deafness, your auditory cortex flooded, chattering, then finally—protectively, desperately—quieting into a kind of numb receptive hum.

Then the light. It is not sunlight, though it behaves like light—scatters, reflects, casts what might be called shadows if the shadowed areas were not themselves faintly luminous, a different quality of brightness, silver-blue where the main illumination runs gold-white. The source: vast sheets of something between plasma and fungal bioluminescence that drape across the upper canopy, kilometers overhead, feeding on—the sound itself? The perpetual atmospheric turbulence? There is no clear directional source. The brightness arrives from all vectors, diffuse, shadow-less in the ordinary sense, yet rich in texture, in *gradation*, layers of luminosity like translucent curtains hung at different depths.

You stand on a slope. High elevation, yes, but the sense of height manifests strangely here because the perspective is so vast, the curvature so subtle, that you cannot trust your perception of angle. The ground rises toward something that may be a horizon or may be merely the point where the bioluminescent sheets thicken to opacity. The slope is covered in—this is where the parameters begin their strange negotiation—in vegetation that is total, complete, an ecosystem where plant has become substrate, architecture, atmosphere, *law*.

Yet water registers almost nothing. How?

You understand gradually, your boots crunching on surfaces that yield slightly, then firm, with each step. The vegetation is not aqueous life. These are plants of *desiccation*, of mineral metabolism, organisms that have transcended or bypassed or never evolved the aqueous chemistry you associate with green. Their vascular systems, visible through translucent outer tissues, carry not sap but something denser, slower, *redder*—oxides in suspension, liquid metals at cool temperatures, a sluggish metallurgical pulse. They draw water where they can find it locked in mineral lattices, in atmospheric traces, but they do not require it as *medium*. The water parameter here is satisfied by absence, by the desert that underlies even this total vegetative coverage, by the salt-dust you taste when the sound-waves momentarily compress air against your face.

The plants themselves: they have attained *scale* with the landscape, individual organisms measured in kilometers, their forms not tree-like but *process-like*, the visible expressions of slow equations. Here a mat of interlocked rhizome-analogues spreads across a valley floor you cannot see the far edge of, surface texture somewhere between coral and circuit-board, colors in the ochre-rust family broken by sudden outbreaks of that bioluminescent white where some metabolic threshold converts mineral energy to light. There a tower-formation rises, not growing upward so much as *precipitating*, mineral accretion around some core process, layered like a thousand years of industrial slag except each layer lives, repairs, contests with its neighbors for position. The density parameter says *sparse*—and true, there is space between these presences, you can walk for hours in regions of only ground-cover, the rhizomatous substrate, before encountering another monumental form. Yet the *total* vegetation, the *all-living-mass* quality, means even the spaces between are saturated with plant, with living process, with the slower metabolism of distributed organisms whose bodies extend far beyond what presents as individual.

The temperature: cool. Your breath does not quite show. The great sheets overhead emit their light without heat, or rather with a *different* heat, not thermal but something that warms you somewhere between skin and bone, a cellular hospitality. The wind—because there is wind, tremendous wind, part of that deafening sound, part of the dynamic turbulence—cuts with ordinary chill where it finds exposed skin. You are warm and cold simultaneously in different registers, different depths of embodiment.

Industrial tech, but nothing built. How? The vegetation *is* industrial. This is the strangeness that emerges, the weirdness parameter finding its purchase. These plants run processes we would call factories—mineral refining, alloy production, energy conversion at scales and efficiencies that human metallurgy has never approached. You pass a depression where something *boils* without water, a low-temperature smelting performed by specialized tissues, ingots of strange metal cooling in formations that are *intentional*, that will be harvested by other processes, moved, incorporated. The sound changes character here: lower, more rhythmic, the industrial metabolism audible in its subsonic signature. No hands built this. No minds designed. The *industrial* is a property of biological process here, of evolution finding niches in mineral economics, of organisms that *make* because making is their living, their being, their reproduction and their ecology both.

The fauna: abundant, but you must recalibrate your search image. The animals here are *processes*, *phases*, *functions* of the vegetative industrial. Something moves across the rhizome-mat a kilometer distant, too far to resolve visually, but you track it by the sound—different frequencies where its passage interrupts the standing waves, where its own metabolism adds to the orchestral drone. Closer, you find *mobile fruiting bodies*, organisms of temporary coherence that detach, move, perform some exchange or pollination or predation or *whatever categories fail here*, then dissolve back into substrate or incorporate into new growths. A thing like a wheel of braided cable rolls past, shedding spores or sparks or both, making a sound like a turbine bearing failing, beautiful, precise, already being absorbed into the root-network at its trail-edge even as its leading edge continues forward. Abundant life, but *distributed*, *phase-shifting*, never fully *object*, always also *process*, *relation*, *temporary crystallization*.

The erosion: established, settled, in balance. Yet this balance is *dynamic*, *turbulent*, the storm-state as equilibrium condition. The landscape *wears*, but its wearing is also its growing. You see a tower-formation *unmaking*, sheets of its outer material sloughing, being caught by wind, by mobile fauna, by the great ground-sheets, *reincorporated*. The sound peaks here, at this site of dissolution-constitution, not from destruction but from *activity*, from the maximal rate of transformation-without-loss. The weather of this place—continental weather, weather of its own physics, the scale parameter asserting itself—moves in patterns of erosion-growth, deposition-metabolism, storm-fronts of spore-exchange and mineral precipitation that cross the vastness with deliberate, systemic slowness, generations per cycle, yet *turbulent* in their micro-structure, chaotic in the mathematical sense, sensitive, creative, *living weather*.

You walk. You will walk for days, for longer, and the landscape will not repeat, will not settle into *type*. Each valley has its own mineral economy, its own dominant processes, its own quality of sound and light. The bioluminescent sheets *flake*, *reform*, driven by atmospheric processes you begin to read as *another vegetation*, aerial analogues of the grounded, light-harvesting, sound-harvesting, *harvesting as existence*. Between ground and sky, you move through a medium of continuous transformation, the sparse elements monumental in their spacing, the total vegetation a *condition* rather than a clutter, the industrial a *nature*, the built a *growth*, the turbulent a *balance*, the deafening a *silence of another order*.

What lives here lives *as* this place, not *in* it. You are the anomaly, the temporary coherence, the mobile fruiting body that will dissolve, that is already dissolving, your minerals catalogued for slow incorporation, your water—your *water*, your strange aqueous chemistry—already being processed by organisms for whom it is rare substrate, exotic resource, something to be *used sparingly, conserved, treasured in its strangeness*.

The sound peaks. The light shifts, a sheet overhead reaching some threshold, flaring, dying, being replaced by the growth of neighbors. You are warm-cool, deaf-attentive, alone-surrounded, moving through a world that has never been water's world, that built its abundance on other foundations, that *industrializes* without intention, that *grows* without water's mediation, that *roars* with the joy of its own continuous becoming.

You do not know if you will leave. You do not know if *leaving* is a category here, if your dissolution might not be your continuation, your water distributed, *used*, becoming strange to yourself, becoming *of* this place in the only way it permits, the only way it knows.
