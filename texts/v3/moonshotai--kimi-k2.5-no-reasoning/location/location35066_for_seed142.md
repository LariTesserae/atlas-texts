# The Still Cities of the Khorazic Plateau

## The Foundation

Imagine standing on a floor of compressed obsidian so ancient that it has forgotten it was ever liquid. The Khorazic Plateau rises 4,000 meters above the theoretical sea level of a world that never held seas—an elevation measured not against water but against the dense, unbreathable atmosphere that pools in the world's lower basins like heavy syrup. Up here, the air is thin enough to permit something like consciousness, something like industry. Down there, nothing functions. Nothing needs to.

The plateau stretches for seventeen hundred kilometers in its longest dimension, a continental shelf lifted by processes that concluded before the first stars in this galaxy had finished assembling their hydrogen. The erosion parameter here is deceptive: 2.7 suggests age, and yes, this place is old, but "erosion" implies a wearing away, and the Khorazic has achieved a different state. It has reached its final form. The winds that once scoured it have been exhausted. The temperature differentials that drove weather have equalized. What remains is not erosion but *resolution*—the last possible shape these materials could hold, settled into like a body into death.

## The Surface

Walk across it. Your feet (booted, presumably; the surface holds heat at 42°C average, warm but not killing) make no sound on the vitreous plain. The obsidian here is not black but *absence-colored*, drinking what little light exists. Occasionally your boots catch on seams where ancient flows met and cooled in patterns that suggest language but aren't. The surface is not flat. It has achieved a kind of textured peace—wrinkled, dimpled, occasionally rising into low waves that held their shape while the last molten seconds drained away.

No water, remember. 0.2 allows only the memory of it, or its chemical possibility. What moisture exists binds to mineral lattices, incorporated into the very structure of things. You could heat these rocks and drive out vapor, but why would you? The infrastructure for such extraction exists in the cities, but the cities have other concerns.

## The Cities

They are not cities as you understand them. The "urban" rating of 2.0 reflects density of *function* rather than population. Imagine structures that grew rather than were built, though they were built—industrial processes (1.9) so scaled, so long-running, that their products achieved a kind of geological persistence.

The Khorazic cities are primarily *ventilation systems*. This requires explanation.

The world below—let us call it the Depth—contains resources. Hydrocarbons, useful metals, energy-dense minerals. The Khorazic plateau is porous in its fashion, riddled with shafts and galleries driven downward through millennia of industrial obsession. But the Depth is hot, and more critically, it is *toxic* in ways that make the plateau's thin atmosphere seem like paradise. The cities exist to manage the pressure differential. They are pump stations, heat exchangers, gas processing facilities grown to the scale of urban agglomerations.

You approach one. In the darkness (0.1—this world orbits far from its dim red star, and the star itself is old, settled into its own final burning), you perceive it first as a *wrongness* in the starfield. The cities generate light, but they do not waste it upward. What illumination exists is functional, directional, mostly infrared. Your sensors (you have sensors, yes? in this place, you must) register it as warmth against the cold of space, as organized geometries of waste heat.

The architecture—if the word applies—is *layered construction* in the most literal sense. The oldest foundations are kilometers below the plateau surface, hollowed by extraction. Above these, the industrial phases: foundry levels, assembly levels, the strata where machines made machines until the process achieved sufficient density to become self-sustaining. The visible structures, the surface manifestations, are relatively recent. Perhaps only eighty thousand years old.

They look like *crystallized smoke*. The builders (long absent, purpose unknown, possibly never biological in any recognizable sense) favored materials that accreted rather than were placed. The cities rise in spires and vaults of sintered metal-ceramic composites, porous, breathing. They are still breathing. The industrial processes continue, maintained by entities that may or may not be conscious, that respond to conditions without requiring intention.

## The Sound

0.6. Mostly quiet. The plateau itself generates nothing—no wind, no thermal expansion, no biological process. The cities, though. Walk into one. The sound is *infrasonic*, felt in the sternum before the ear detects it. The great pumps, still moving atmosphere between levels that have not seen living maintenance for longer than your species has existed. The gas processing, molecular separation achieved through ancient membranes that have become semi-living, that *hunger* for certain compounds and pass others. The sound of need without wanting.

Occasionally, a *crack*. Thermal stress in some deep structure, relieving itself. The cities settle constantly, adjusting to pressures that shift on geological timescales. They are never changing (0.4), but they are never finished settling either. A frozen equilibrium, yes, but one that includes constant microscopic adjustment. The crack echoes through porous chambers, dies unanswered.

## The Fauna

0.8. Rare. This requires the most imagination, perhaps.

There is nothing biological. The vegetation parameter at 0.0 forbids it, and the water parameter makes it impossible in any case. But *fauna*—animals—suggests movement, suggests entities that respond and pursue and avoid. The Khorazic has these.

They are *maintenance ghosts*. Not supernatural, though the distinction may be academic. The industrial systems include diagnostic and repair subsystems that achieved complexity sufficient to demonstrate—what? Not intelligence, certainly not in any concentrated sense. But *behavior*. Patterns of movement through the city depths, responding to stimuli, avoiding damage, seeking optimal conditions for their function.

You might see one as a *flicker* in your peripheral vision, in the infrared or the ultrasonic. Something the size of your torso, made of ceramic and memory-wire and piezoelectric polymer, moving with the grace of something that has never known gravity's full weight. It avoids you. It has avoided everything for longer than you can conceptualize. Its purposes are opaque. It may be repairing something. It may be the last member of a species of machines that once bred in these depths, now solitary, now functionally immortal, waiting for conditions that will never return.

There are larger ones. Rarely seen. The size of buildings, mobile only in the deepest shafts where they can brace against the walls, crawling through spaces designed for their passage fifty thousand years past. They are not dangerous because they are not interested. They have achieved the equilibrium of their kind, whatever that might be.

## The Weirdness

2.0. This is the gift, yes?

The Khorazic defies certain expectations because it has *history without memory*. The cities function, but no entity within them knows why. The industrial processes maintain themselves, but the products—where do they go? Some shafts open to the Depth, to that uninhabitable below. Transport continues. Something receives these materials, processes them, perhaps. The plateau has trading partners, or feeding partners, or ritual recipients, somewhere in the dark.

The lightlessness is not absolute. Bioluminescence is impossible without biology, but *electroluminescence*—the cities leak it. Certain structures glow faintly, persistently, in wavelengths that suggest communication but may be mere inefficiency. Walk the plateau at night (which is always, which is the only condition) and you will see distant constellations of this waste light, cities marking themselves against the dark, but never brightly enough to see each other clearly, never confirming that they are not alone.

The strangest thing: *time passes differently*. Not relativistically—the physics here is standard. But the equilibrium state, the never-changing parameter, produces a locality where causality itself seems weary. Effects precede causes in perception, not in fact, but the distinction blurs when nothing changes enough to establish sequence. You might walk toward a city for days and find it younger than when you started, not because it changed but because *you noticed different aspects of its age*. The plateau teaches a kind of temporal vertigo. The erosion that concluded here concluded in *reversal*, in a settling so complete that past and present achieve a simultaneity uncomfortable to contingent minds.

## The Rules

What can you do here? What is permitted?

The Khorazic does not forbid. It *does not notice*. The cities will not respond to you unless you interfere with their function, and their function is so distributed, so deeply embedded, that interference would require intention beyond most capacities. You could walk through them for years, sleeping in their warm corridors, drinking the condensate that forms on certain cooled surfaces (0.2 water, remember—not zero, never quite zero), and never be acknowledged.

But you would change. The temporal diffusion, the infrasonic sound, the constant subtle wrongness of maintenance entities passing through walls designed for their permeability—these would shape you. Visitors to the Khorazic rarely leave, and those who do carry something with them: a patience, perhaps, or its opposite, a desperate urgency born of having experienced time without event. They speak of the plateau as *finished*, as a place that has achieved what all places strive for and found it wanting.

The scale (2.4) is the final cruelty. You can see forever here, in the infrared, in the absolute dark. The cities extend to your horizon in multiple directions, and beyond them, the obsidian plain, and beyond that, more cities, more plains, the repetition achieving a kind of infinite regress. You are not trapped. There are no barriers. But where would you go that is not here, that has not been here since before your kind existed?

The Khorazic Plateau waits. It has always waited. The industrial processes continue, achieving nothing, maintaining the possibility of purpose without its exercise. In the warm dark, the ancient surface holds its final shape against the weight of nothing, and the cities breathe, and breathe, and breathe.
