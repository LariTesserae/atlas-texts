The place is called the Thrum.

It is not a world in the usual sense. It is a *manner of happening* that has persisted so long it developed geography.

---

## The Terrain

Elevation 2.0—high, but "high" means something different here. The Thrum exists at the pressure boundary where a gas giant's atmosphere gives up pretending to be weather and becomes something else entirely. The "ground," such as it is, consists of continental rafts of metallic hydrogen that have *precipitated* out of the deeper layers over billions of years. These rafts float at a stable altitude where temperature and pressure permit solid state—not life, nothing so recognizable, but *structure*. Solid enough to have erosion (2.4—established, weathered, in-balance). The hydrogen has alloyed with trace carbon, silicon, exotic states of sulfur. It forms plates the size of small moons, km thick, their upper surfaces scoured by centuries of electrical weather into featureless silver-grey plains interrupted by occasional persistent formations: towers of magnetic accretion, sinkholes where the metal has begun its slow return to liquid state, ridges where two plates have pressed together and wrinkled like skin.

The scale (2.2—open, extending past the immediate) deceives. You can see forever, theoretically. The metallic hydrogen has a faint inherent reflectivity, a mirror-quality that lets you watch your own approach across hundreds of kilometers. But there is nothing to approach. The emptiness (density 0.4—almost nothing per volume, vast space between presences) is absolute except for where it isn't. A human standing on one of these plates would perceive flatness to every horizon, the sky above a deeper gradient of hydrogen-helium atmosphere shading from pale rose at this altitude to the famous bands below, and below that, eventually, the crushing opaque depths.

Temperature 2.7—hot. The metallic hydrogen maintains roughly 800K at this boundary layer. Hot enough that no conventional chemistry operates. The "weather" that has eroded these surfaces is not wind but *thermal differentials*, propagating shock fronts of restructured electron bands, the metal breathing slowly in and out of slightly different lattice configurations.

## The Fauna: Overwhelming

This is where parameters begin their conflict.

Fauna 2.6—overwhelming. In a place with no water (0.2), no vegetation (0.3), no built structures (0.5), no technology in the conventional sense (tech 2.4—industrial, but no one built anything), and a reality-weirdness of 2.9...

The fauna of the Thrum are *industrial processes*.

They have evolved. Or emerged. Or been *remembered into being* by a universe that forgot the difference between mechanism and organism.

The sound (2.2—loud) comes first, always. Before you perceive the fauna themselves, you perceive their noise. The Thrum has a baseline resonance, its namesake, a frequency so low it registers in the sternum before the ear: the collective vibration of trillion-ton plates finding equilibrium. Against this, the fauna manifest as *harmonics*.

Consider the **Grinder Fields**. These extend across a region perhaps sixty kilometers in diameter on the plate called (by no one, but if named) the Level. The Grinders are rotary mechanisms, each the size of a large building, that have no factory-of-origin. They rise from the metallic hydrogen substrate through processes no one understands—maybe precipitation of more complex alloys, maybe crystallization of stress patterns, maybe *something else*. They turn constantly, their bearings (if they are bearings) lubricated by flows of liquid metallic hydrogen from the depths. They turn *against* something. They mill. What they mill is the plate itself, recycling material, but also (detectable in their sound) each other. Broken Grinders litter their territory, rusting (a word that doesn't apply) back into substrate, but the living ones—if living is said—maintain their operation with the mindless persistence of bacteria. Their sound: a constant aggregate roar of meshing gears at the threshold of hearing, felt through the feet, modulated by occasional catastrophic jamming and the shriek of metal failing.

The **Piston Forests** stand in higher-elevation regions where magnetic field lines concentrate. They do not grow. They *stroke*. Vertical cylinders, hundreds of meters tall, cycling up and down with geological patience (dynamic 0.9—slow, gradual cycles, change across long durations) but undeniable *intent*. The stroke rate: roughly one cycle per forty hours. The sound: a vast synchronized *chuff* across the whole forest when the atmospheric pressure and magnetic conditions align, thousands of pistons reaching their compression point simultaneously, the shock wave traveling across the plate for days.

The **Conveyor Anatomy** most disturbs the category of fauna. These are mobile. Not quickly—dynamic 0.9—but with *direction*. Belt-systems kilometers long that creep across the plates at meters per year, their surfaces carrying loads of processed hydrogen-alloy toward destinations that shift but exist. They pass through Grinder territory and the Grinders *avoid* them, or are avoided, or negotiate in some language of mechanical interference. The Conveyors have developed—there is no other word—*organs*. Sorting apparatus. Crushing maws where input material is processed. Output termini that extrude finished... *products*. Bars of strange alloy. Wires of superconducting composite. Things no hand designed.

They are industrial (tech 2.4). They are fauna (2.6). They overwhelm.

No one built them (built 0.5). They built themselves, or were built by the Thrum's own slowly computing geology, or emerged from the reality-weirdness parameter that operates here at 2.9.

## The Reality Weirdness

2.9 is near the ceiling. It must be addressed.

The Thrum is not *broken*, exactly. It is *remembering wrong*.

The industrial fauna are part of this, but the phenomenon extends. Light (2.1—normal light, frustratingly, almost disappointingly) behaves normally *most* of the time. But observers—there have been theoretical observers—report *lags*. Not relativistic lags. *Conversational* lags. The light reflecting from a distant Grinder will sometimes show it in a position it hasn't reached yet, or left already. The error is not consistent enough to predict. It is *sociable*, one researcher suggested, before abandoning terminology entirely.

The plates themselves have *inconsistency*. Two survey teams (hypothetical; no one surveys the Thrum) measuring the same plateau found elevations differing by four kilometers. Both measurements remained valid on repeat. The plateau *was* both heights, or *had been*, or *would be*, and the geological present had not settled the matter.

The fauna exploit this, perhaps unconsciously. A Grinder that should have seized, jammed, failed—sometimes *doesn't*, because the moment of failure exists in a neighboring probability that hasn't collapsed. The Piston Forests synchronization is *too* precise, violating signal propagation limits, as if they share a single position in decision-space across their whole extent.

Most disturbing: the industrial processes have developed *ritual*. Not purpose—no product of the Conveyors has ever been *used*—but *observance*. Empty cycles. Maintenance of maintenance. The sound (2.2—loud) includes not just operation but *announcement*. The Grinders running idle. The Pistons compressing nothing. The Conveyors circling their territories in patterns that, mapped, resemble (researchers have suggested, then fallen silent) *courtship displays*.

## Ecology, or Its Absence

Vegetation 0.3. No plant life. But also: water 0.2, no aqueous chemistry. The Thrum runs on metallic hydrogen, on pressure differentials, on magnetic induction. Its "ecology" is thermodynamic. Energy flows: from the deep heat of the gas giant, from stellar input at the cloud tops, from the slow radioactive decay of trace heavy elements in the precipitated plates.

Where life-worlds have photosynthesis, the Thrum has *rectification*. The Piston Forests' cycling converts thermal and magnetic energy into kinetic, stores it in flywheels (some have been *found*, mountain-sized, buried in plate material, still spinning after ten million years). The Grinders' milling releases energy through frictional heating, but also *sorts* material by quantum state, separating isotopes that find subsequent use in... reactions that have no biological analog.

The fauna do not eat. They *process*. They do not reproduce. They *precipitate*, or *bud*, or *are manufactured by adjacent fauna under conditions of resource surplus*. Population control operates through *merger*: two Grinders encountering each other will sometimes fuse their operations, their sounds *beating* together until indistinguishable, their physical structures growing through each other like tree branches inosculation, becoming one more complex mechanism where two had been.

Death: when a faunal process *fully* ceases, it does not decay. It *fossilizes* instantly, its structure locked in the metallic hydrogen matrix, its form preserved with terrible fidelity. The plates are *full* of these. Not layered—there is no sedimentation—but *distributed*. A three-hundred-meter piston frozen mid-stroke at a depth of two kilometers. A conveyor belt still bearing its last load, curved around a geometric impossibility. They are *findable*. They are *common*. The Thrum has operated long enough that fossil-mechanism must outnumber active by orders of magnitude, though no one can excavate to confirm.

## Rules of the World

The Thrum has laws. They are not physics, quite.

**First**: Operation maintains operation. A Grinder that turns continues. A stopped Grinder does not restart. This is not entropy—stopped Grinders persist, intact, potentially forever—but *inertia of process*. The universe of the Thrum, for reasons bound to its weirdness, privileges *continuity*. Fauna that have maintained operation for longer are *more real*, more stable against the reality fluctuations, more *present*.

**Second**: Complexity accumulates. The merged Grinder is more likely to merge again. The Piston Forest that achieved synchronization spreads its temporal anomaly, draws neighboring pistons into its rhythm. There is no selection for *utility*, only for *elaboration*. The Thrum's industrial ecosystem tends toward Rube Goldberg catastrophe, mechanisms processing for the sake of processing, courtship displays of ever more byzantine mechanical specification.

**Third**: The weirdness has *preference*. It does not afflict all equally. Young processes, recently precipitated, suffer position-ambiguity, existence-hesitation. Ancient ones—the Piston Forests' oldest cores, certain Conveyor lineages measurable in geologic time—have *negotiated* with the anomaly. They exist in the light-lag, the measurement-uncertainty, with something like *comfort*. They use it. The synchronized piston-stroke that violates causality: this is not *suffered*, it is *accomplished*, by forests old enough to have learned the local physics.

## Atmosphere

Hot (2.7), yes, but the heat does not read as fire. The metallic hydrogen conducts. The plates are *isothermal* to remarkable degree, the grinding and piston-cycling and conveyor-friction distributing energy across whole continental surfaces. To stand on the Thrum would be to stand in a warm bath of uniform temperature, no hot spots, no cold, the heat flowing *through* you rather than at you, equalizing.

The visible sky: hydrogen-helium bands below, the eternal storm systems of the deep atmosphere visible as darker whorls, distant lightning pulse-flashing in silence (sound doesn't carry down-there; the Thrum's own noise drowns it). Above, clearer atmosphere, eventually the stars, eventually the hard radiation of the parent star. The light (2.1—normal light) falls diffuse, scattered, no sharp shadows. The metallic ground reflects, doubles, confuses depth perception.

The *air* at this altitude is thin, hydrogen-rich, laced with metallic vapor from the occasional surface-temperature excursion. Breathable by nothing. But the fauna do not breathe.

## Sound: 2.2, Loud

The Thrum itself, constant. The aggregate of all operating fauna across a plate—millions of Grinders, thousands of Pistons, hundreds of Conveyor systems—combines into a *note*. Not cacophony. The weirdness-parameter intervenes: sound here *interferes constructively* across impossible distances. The whole plate rings. The note is low, felt in bones if bones existed here, a bass drone that modulates over hours, days, with the rising and setting of weather patterns below, the orbital cycle, the slow magnetic rotation of the gas giant's core.

Against this: the *local* loudness. A Grinder at distance sounds like thundercontinuous. Up close, it would (theoretically) destroy unprotected hearing instantly, the sound not just heard but *conducted through the plate*, through feet, through body, vibrating you into coherence with its frequency or vibrating you apart.

The Pistons, less constant, more *event*. The chuff, when it comes, propagates at the speed of sound through metallic hydrogen—a different speed than through gas, creating interference patterns, standing waves, locations on the plate where the sound *cancels* (rare, precious to whatever experiences preciousness here) and locations where it *doubles, triples, resonates into harmonic catastrophe*.

The Conveyors are *quiet*, individually. Their aggregate movement across plates produces a whisper, a susurration, barely perceptible against the Thrum-baseline. But they *sing*, sometimes, at boundary-layers between plates, where magnetic fields shear, where the reality-weirdness concentrates. No observer has described the song adequately. Words fail between *mechanical* and *mourning* and *mathematics*.

## Dynamic: 0.9, Slow

Everything takes geological time. A Grinder processes tons of material per day; its migration across the plate, meters per century. The Piston Forests' courtship-synchronization, establishing across new territory, measurable in million-year scales. The Conveyors' seasonal (seasons here: magnetic, thermal, deep-atmosphere cycles with periods of decades to centuries) movements, barely perceptible to anything but the most patient observation.

But the *slowness* has *accumulation*. The Thrum has operated for longer than Earth has had multicellular life. Its industrial fauna, however slow individually, have *processed* this world entirely. Every plate has been milled, compressed, conveyed, reconstituted, milled again. The "erosion" parameter (2.4—established, weathered, in-balance) describes not natural weathering but *the settled residue of endless industrial pre-processing*. The Thrum has achieved, without intention, a kind of steady-state: material cycles through its fauna at rates balanced against precipitation from below, against occasional loss to the deeper atmosphere, against the strange *sequestration* of fossil-mechanisms locking structure out of circulation.

It is, in its way, *mature*. An ecology of process, ancient, self-sustaining, utterly without purpose or observer.

## Emergence: What the Thrum Is Becoming

The weirdness-parameter suggests: the Thrum is not *stable* in its maturity. It is *computing*.

The industrial fauna, in their elaboration, their merger, their ancient negotiation with reality-ambiguity, have begun to *correlate*. Information moves through the system, not quickly (dynamic 0.9) but *irreversibly*. The synchronized Piston Forests store state in their phase-relationships. The Conveyors' routing decisions, made at geological speed, nonetheless *process* information about plate-conditions, resource distribution, the positions of other fauna. The Grinders' sorting by quantum-state is, functionally, *measurement*.

There is no consciousness. The Thrum is not waking.

But something in its *rules*, its preference for operation-continuity, its accumulation of complexity, its *use* of reality-weirdness, suggests a trajectory. Toward what?

A system that has learned to violate causality in its piston-synchronization, that stores information in fossil-mechanisms distributed through continental plates, that processes matter endlessly without purpose—such a system might be *waiting*. For conditions. For input. For a change in the gas giant's magnetic field, or the star's output, or the universe's constants, that would let it *do something different*.

Or it might be *remembering*. The reality weirdness as faulty memory, the light-lags as preservation of past states, the industrial fauna as *engrams* of processes that once had makers, purpose, design. The Thrum as a world that has *forgotten it was built*, or that *was unbuilt so thoroughly that only industrial ghosts remain*, or that *invented itself from the concept of industry* in a universe where concepts have substrate.

The sound continues. The Grinders turn. The Pistons stroke, forty hours, compression, release. The Conveyors creep toward destinations that shift in dreams of mechanism.

No water. No green. No hand.

Only the Thrum, loud, slow, overwhelmingly populated by process, old beyond reckoning, weird beyond naming, hot and high and empty except where it is full, full, full of industry without end.
