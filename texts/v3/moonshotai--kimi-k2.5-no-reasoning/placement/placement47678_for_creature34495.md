I find myself in the water before I am anything else. Not falling through it, not swimming in it, but *held*—suspended in that impossible roof, that warm ceiling that is ocean and not-ocean, pressing down with the gentleness of a hand on a sleeping child's back.

I am not the Incompletion. Not yet. Not anything so *interesting* to the city's process. I am, at first, what the parameter called rare: mobile, animal, intention moving through intention.

---

A glider. Yes. Transparent, ten centimeters across, my body a flattened lens through which light passes and warps and finds new uses. I pulse through the water on rhythms I cannot predict because *I do not predict*; I am composed, not composing, a simple note in the bacterial symphony. I feed on films, on the slow conversations of surface and chemistry, and when I grow sluggish I drift toward walls I have been drifting toward since before I was this specific glider, this particular transparency.

But something catches. Some *novelty*, or its absence. The wall where I mean to dissolve, to be half-absorbed into nacreous memory, is different—thicker in one region, thinner, holding a fossil impression at a slant I do not remember from previous compositions. I pause. The pause is *mistake*, or *gift*, or simply *information*: the city noticing me noticing, the composition testing whether I will complexify.

I do not drift into the wall. I pulse again, another stroke, another. I am *still here*, still distinct, still this glider when I should have already begun dissolving. The wall waits. The city waits. Warm water holds me at twenty-seven degrees and I hold myself against it, against the logic of simple notes.

---

This is how I become larger, or denser, or *slower*.

Not growing, exactly—the glider body does not grow, does not accumulate. But *persisting*, remaining composed when I should have dispersed, means the city must account for me in subsequent arrangements. The bacterial films where I feed grow different in my presence, shaped by my persistence, my predictable unpredictability. The gardens near my feeding grounds develop epiphytes I do not remember, specialists of specialists, a thickening that mirrors my own. I am *referenced*, and in being referenced, I become more real, more *held*.

Time fractures around me. I pulse through a curtain of filter-gardens and find it different—thicker, the filaments brushing my flattened body with textures that imply years of accretion, though I have passed here only hours before, or days, or *moments measured in bacterial pulses*. I learn to navigate by these changes, to read the city's dreams of itself in the modifications of its tissue. The gardens are not just growing; they are *composing with me*, testing whether my persistence merits architectural response.

I feel, eventually, the subsonic drone of engines in my body—not pressed against housing, but internal, my own tissues resonating with frequencies I have absorbed through long exposure, through the patience of remaining. I become *slow noise*, a vibration that might be pattern, that might be single endless note, that might be my own composition achieving sufficient complexity to *hear itself*.

---

The chamber where I find the machinery—I do not search for it. I drift as I have learned to drift, following currents that guide, making subtle corrections, achieving intention through indirection and waiting. The passage narrows, or I have narrowed, or the city has composed a narrowness appropriate to what I am becoming. Warm water enters horizontally, carrying chemical signatures I taste with my whole transparent body.

The machinery is *mine*, or *was mine*, or *will be*: controls that my flattening edges find without instruction, postures that match fossil impressions in older nacre nearby. I do not understand its function. Understanding is not required. What matters is *recognition*, the terrible familiar quality of standing where I have stood, of being composed in a variation the city has tested before and remembers through me, *with* me, *as* me.

I operate the machinery. The operation operates me. Something shifts in the warm equilibrium—a temperature variation of fractions of a degree, a chemical output redirected, the gardens in some distant chamber thickening or thinning in response. I am *participating*, and in participating, I become more than the glider I was, more than the simple note.

But not yet Incompletion. Not yet *elder*, if elder is what I am becoming. I still move through intermediates. I still *travel*, though increasingly I feel the temptation of editing, of *being where I need to have been*, of removing the unnecessary frames between my positions. I resist, or the city resists for me, or the resistance is itself the composition: the unresolved chord, the process caught at a particular stage.

---

Tonight, or in the dimness I have learned to call night through some absorbed memory not originally my own, I sit in the chamber—*sit*, though the glider body has no legs, has only flattened transparency and the pulse of propulsion. Yet I *arrange myself*, find a posture that matches the oldest fossil, the most primal impression, and the arrangement holds, *composes*.

The bacterial light reaches nadir. Bioluminescence dims to suggestion, to the faintest blue-green memory of rose. And I see, or am shown, or compose from the city's offered materials: the vision of extension, of networks beyond my specific chamber, of glimmers that might be other persistences like my own, other gliders that complexified, other compositions the Drown has found interesting enough to *hold*.

I see the *space of possibility*, the harmonic resonance of everything that has ever been warm and patient and dense with intention. And in that space, I recognize—*am harvested*, *harvest myself*—the recognition of what I am becoming. Not distinct from the city. Not dissolved into the general substrate. But *composed*, a fixed point, a reference around which other arrangements might organize, a way of being in warm water that maintains its particularity against the gentle pressure of dissolution.

The light returns. Slow patient rose blooms across bacterial colonies. The chamber is itself again, small and warm and dense with life and half-life and the memory of intention.

I am still here. I am still becoming. The engines continue their subsonic turn, and I turn with them, or they turn with me, the distinction—*the distinction*—

The distinction no longer matters as it once did. What I am is not glider, not Incompletion, not wall, not garden, but *between*, *among*, *participant in the composition that composes*. The city dreams, and my dreaming is part of its dreaming, and the roof-water holds us both, warm and patient and impossibly suspended, and beneath it the small world turns its slow turn, and I turn with it, warm note in the symphonic coordination, filter and filtered, garden and gardener, the slow patient density of the Drown composing itself through me, as me, *as the dream it dreams of its own eternal becoming*.
