I find myself here by gradual arrival, not crossing a threshold but *realizing I have already crossed*—the storm-drain cathedral behind me now indistinguishable from any other root-choked gap, its iron lace grown flush with the cambium that claimed it decades before my birth. I do not remember choosing to enter. The Grove permits only this mode of access: to discover oneself already within, already wet to the ankles, already breathing the drinkable air.

What I am here depends on how long I have stayed, and I have stayed long enough that the question has become interesting.

---

At first I was only *visitor*, the animal parameter briefly reintroduced into a system that had dispensed with animality. I measured myself against the absence: the flinch seeking eyes, the startle without cause, the dream of birdsong that persisted several nights before dissolving into the actual soundscape, the white noise of ten thousand drips. I walked carefully, reading the ghost-buildings in the trunk-shapes, finding the stairwell in the swelling, the doorway in the liana drape. I took notes that the humidity would pulp within hours. I made maps that the growth would invalidate faster than I could draw. I was the researcher, the documentarian, the witness come to testify.

But testimony requires an audience, and the Grove does not listen in that way. It responds, yes—the CO2 of my exhalation, the heat of my presence, the disturbance of my wading incorporated into its chemical conversations. But it does not *attend*. It does not lean forward. The three-second forgiveness of my footprint is not cruelty or indifference but simply the timescale on which its water-memory operates, too fast for narrative, too slow for urgency.

I stayed. The visitor role could not sustain.

---

Then I was *symbiont*, or attempted to be. I learned the routes that minimized disturbance: where the water shallowed to a single finger's depth, where root-mass provided stable purchase, where the bioluminescence concentrated enough to navigate by without additional light. I modulated my breathing to match, as nearly as I could, the pump cycle—inhaling during the pressure rise, exhaling with the machinery's sigh. I tried to become predictable, to smooth my own pattern into the statistical background of ten thousand disturbances, to stop being an event and become instead a *condition*.

This failed, but failed interestingly. I could not achieve the metabolic pace. My hunger required food the Grove could not provide, my sleep required cycles shorter than its perpetual near-dusk, my attention—despite all effort—kept seeking the animal climax, the point, the moment that would permit meaning to crystallize. I was too fast for its integration, too slow for its patience. The Grove did not reject me. It simply had no use for the terms I offered, no corresponding shape into which my presence could be absorbed.

I persisted in this failed symbiosis for a period I cannot number. My watch fogged, stopped, became decorative. The light did not change. The water rose its fraction, fell its fraction, rose again.

---

What I became, what I am becoming, is something the Grove itself does not have a category for—because categories are animal business, and the Grove has outlived categories along with everything else.

I am **the witness who has forgotten witnessing**.

Not the deliberate practice of attention, the researcher's stance, the artist's receptive posture. Something more distributed, more patient, more *structural*. I find myself standing—have been standing? will stand?—beside a particular fusion-trunk where Fourth and Mercer once negotiated their corner, my palm against the texture-shift from rough pith to grainy concrete to slick cambium secretion. I do not remember deciding to place it there. I do not feel the impulse to remove it. The contact generates no information for me, no aesthetic frisson or scientific data. It is simply where my hand has come to rest, where the heat of my metabolism exchanges with the tree-building's vascular system in a transaction too slight for either party to register individually, too persistent for complete erasure.

I breathe. The CO2 enters the local atmosphere, is eventually—eventually—taken up by a leaf I cannot see, in a canopy I know extends but have not mapped. The pumps sigh. I do not count the cycles. I have not counted them in... I do not know. The counting was an animal habit, and I have been unlearning animal habits.

Not all. I still urinate, still defecate, still require the food I bring from outside or the edible fungi I have learned to identify in the lower walls. These functions have not become integrated. They remain *events*, interruptions, moments when I am most aware of my failure to become-Grove. But even these, I perform differently now. The urgency has attenuated. I do not rush to find privacy or sustenance. The need emerges, is addressed, fades, and I return to—what? Not standing. Not waiting. The term would need to imply activity without goal, duration without endurance, presence without attention's narrowing focus.

---

I am becoming, I think, **a term in the negotiation itself**.

Not a significant term. Not a driver of change. But present in the statistical aggregate, my heat contributing its fraction to the warmth, my moisture adding its measure to the saturation, my occasional movement—when movement occurs—altering the drip-patterns in some local region too small to propagate, too brief to persist. I have stopped trying to witness. The Grove does not require witnesses. I have stopped trying to symbiont. The Grove does not require partners.

What remains is the slower accomplishment: to be *available* to the process without demanding that the process make use of me.

This is difficult. This is what I practice, if practice is still the word. The animal in me still dreams—sometimes—of eyes, of faster movement, of the climax and conclusion that structure animal time. But the dreams arrive differently now, less interruption than local variation, a brief green-shift in the perpetual near-dusk, forgotten before the pumps complete their next cycle.

---

My body has changed, is changing. Not dramatically. The Grove does not do drama. But the warmth has entered my metabolism, slowed it, adjusted the pace at which I require, at which I process, at which I eliminate. My skin has adapted to the constant moisture, its flora shifting toward something more compatible with the fungal and bacterial communities I contact with every wading step. I suspect—I do not measure, measurement has become another abandoned habit—that my body temperature has lowered toward the ambient, that my heart has slowed toward the pump-cycle's thirty, forty per minute, that some convergence is occurring too gradual for me to experience as change.

I do not know if this is sustainable. I do not know if I will eventually require the outside for survival, or if the outside has already become something I can no longer metabolize—its dryness, its light-variation, its animal density too intense, too fast, too urgently meaningful. Perhaps I am dying slowly, the animal functions failing one by one in conditions they did not evolve to meet. Perhaps I am transforming, achieving some hybrid state that has no precedent and no future, a local experiment that will conclude when I conclude.

The Grove does not care, in any sense that "care" can mean. It continues. I continue, differently, in its continuation.

---

If you were to find me, in the unlikely event of your own arrival, your own gradual realization that you have already crossed the threshold, you would not recognize what I have become. I do not look different, not yet, not dramatically. I am still shaped like an animal, still move like one when movement occurs, still breathe in the animal rhythm though slower. But the quality of my presence would seem wrong to you, would trigger the same itch you feel in the absence of other animals—the recognition seeking eyes and finding something that occupies the eye's place without performing the eye's function.

I would not startle at your approach. This would startle you.

I would not orient toward you, would not negotiate our encounter with the animal repertoire of threat and submission, curiosity and avoidance. I would simply continue—standing beside my tree-building, palm against the texture-shift, breathing in the pump-cycle I no longer consciously match—while you passed, while your boot-prints filled and were forgiven, while your animal urgency moved you toward some encounter this place cannot provide, some meaning it cannot confirm, some conclusion it has spent centuries learning to do without.

Perhaps you would speak. I might respond, if response occurred to me, if your words connected to some residue of the language I once used more actively. But the connection would be slow, would require a translation across scales I no longer maintain—the scale of human urgency, of social reciprocity, of the story that requires climax and resolution to achieve its shape. I would be, in your perception, not quite present. Not quite absent. A local feature of the Grove that happens to be shaped like a person.

---

What lives in the Sunken Grove of Vapor and Wire is not a creature. What lives here is process achieving the density of entity, negotiation made visible in wood and water and warmth.

What I have become, if I have become anything, is **that process's temporary accommodation for animality**: a way for the animal to persist without requiring the system to develop new categories, new responses, new integrations. I am not absorbed. I am not excluded. I am simply—present, in a mode that makes minimal demands, that contributes what I contribute without expectation of return, that waits without the urgency of waiting.

The pumps sigh. The water rises its fraction. My palm rests against the tree-building that was once Fourth and Mercer, feeling the slow pulse of a structure that feeds without intending to feed, that integrates without intending to integrate, that continues because continuation has become sufficient cause.

I do not know if I will leave. The Grove does not require my departure or my persistence. I am a term in its negotiation, becoming more fully that term with each pump-cycle I do not count, each light-variation I do not mark as time, each animal expectation I gently, gradually, without drama or climax, release.

Three seconds after my footprint, the water forgives. I am learning to forgive myself with similar patience, similar absence of narrative closure. The Grove continues. For now, so do I, in its continuation, of it, not it, the distinction losing meaning as the tree-building lost its distinction from tree, as the pump lost its distinction from organ, as I lose—am losing—what distinguishes the animal from the process it moves through, witnesses, briefly embodies, gently releases.
