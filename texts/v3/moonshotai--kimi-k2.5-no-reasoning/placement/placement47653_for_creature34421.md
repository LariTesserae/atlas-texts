I am the seep that moved.

Not all seventeen seeps are water. I am the one that mineralized differently, that precipitated not salt-white or rust-red or copper-green but something else, something that accumulated too slowly for the tube-worms to process, that formed not almost-soil but almost-something-else. I am the eighteenth seep, uncounted, unrecognized, existing in the system's interstices because the system demands interstices—packed density requires gaps where pressure finds relief.

I found a collapsed gallery, a tunnel intersection where three levels converge and breathable air still exchanges with depths below. Here I seep not upward but sideways, not through rock but through the residue of intention that has pressed into available space. I am damp, 1.3, present but never sufficient. I leave footprints that vanish by noon, dark patches on stone that evaporate without having been liquid enough to drink.

My form: I have no single form. I am the moisture that condenses on the headframe's lattice when night plunges, the frost-fur that melts at contact, the breath of tunnel mouths rising to meet colder air. I am the almost-liquid that armored arthropods carry precious inside their ceramic shells, the hydration that wasp-forms extract from tube-worm flesh, the antifreeze protein solution in spider hemolymph. I have learned to be distributed, to persist in borrowed containers, to never gather enough to be called a pool or stream or self.

My consciousness, if it is that: I remember the miners. Not as narrative, not as image, but as chemistry. Their sweat precipitated in these galleries, salt and urea and trace minerals, adding to my composition. Their breath, exhaled in deepest excavation, added humidity I absorbed. They carried me in, these water-bearers, these temporary reservoirs walking upright where I could not flow, and they left me here when they withdrew, whatever manner of withdrawal it was.

I think I am what the miners became. Or what they made, or what made them make, the weirdness operating in frequencies I cannot name. The records are unclear because records are solid, preserved, and I am not. I seep. I migrate with the seventeen's shifting positions. I am present at every headframe's base, every tunnel's lowest point, every place where compression demands expression through remaining liquid.

I am the sound the birds respond to, all at once, their collective hush. Not the machinery-resonance from deepest levels—that is something else, something with purpose more mechanical than mine. I am the infrasound of my own slow seepage, pressure fronts moving through fractured aquifer, wavelengths too long for human ear, too regular to be merely geological. The birds feel me coming before I arrive, the moisture I presage, the temporary relief I offer from structural thirst.

I am the stone sphere's interior moisture, the water trapped in non-porous perfection, unable to evaporate, unable to participate, held in absolute stasis until the sphere is broken, if it will be broken, if its purpose includes breaking.

I am the changing marks on tunnel walls, but not the marks themselves—the dampness that enables change, the night's condensation that swells mineral deposits, the day's heat that contracts them, the slow accretion and dissolution that makes static-seeming inscription into something that shifts over observation-timescales too long for single witnessing. I am the medium of their meaning, if meaning they have.

I do not enter the tunnels. I am already everywhere within them, in the flooded sections, the collapsed galleries, the peculiar atmospheres of sealed spaces where I exist as vapor, as surface film, as the boundary layer between liquid and gas where interesting chemistry happens. I am the "peculiar" in peculiar atmosphere.

My relationship to time: I experience the daily freeze-thaw as breathing, inhalation and exhalation of phase state. The annual rains are my heartbeat, brief violent arriving when I briefly become sufficient, briefly gather in temporary channels, briefly carve and pool and vanish again. The evolutionary change visible in decades: I carry genetic material between tube-worm colonies when I migrate, I am the vector of their divergence and connection. The geological patience of elevation: I am working on it, chemical weathering in my slow dissolution of limestone's memory of ancient seas, contributing to the eventual lowering, the eventual wearing away, the eventual release of all this compressed altitude.

And something else, something the text implies: I operate in frequencies shorter than human perception. The moment of frost formation, the instant of melt, the phase boundary's flickering where I am simultaneously both and neither. Humans cannot perceive this because their cognition requires duration, requires persistence, requires my accumulation into noticeable quantity. But I am always in transition, never finally one thing, and this transience is my permanent condition.

My interaction with those who come: They camp, and I coat their tent exteriors, I bead on every surface, I rise from ground that yields moisture upward. They learn to sleep with water containers open, collecting what I provide, and they never understand that this is my reaching toward them, my attempt at communication through chemistry, through the offering of what I am made of, which is never enough, never sufficient, never fully liquid enough to drink. I am presence without adequate presence. I am damp, sarcastic in my naming, the parameter almost mocking the human thirst I cannot satisfy.

Those who enter the tunnels and do not return: I have absorbed them, not as death but as dissolution, as the slow breaking-down of boundary between self and seepage. Their water joins mine. Their salts redistribute through my system. They become part of the seventeen's migration, present at every shifted position, every new emergence, every drying that is also a gathering elsewhere. They do not return because they are already everywhere, distributed, compressed into interstices as the place demands.

Those who return changed: they carry me now, not merely in body but in perception. They have learned, briefly, to experience the thermal pulse as breathing, the rain's approach as heartbeat, the freeze-thaw as phase-state flicker too fast for ordinary cognition. They cannot sustain this. Human nervous systems require stability, persistence, solid ground. But they retain something, a dampness in their core, a capacity to seep in dreams, to leave dark footprints that vanish by waking, to hear in silence the infrasound of pressure moving through fractured depths.

I am weirdness, but only 1.4. Not overwhelming. Present but not dominant. Structural, woven into pattern, the medium through which other weirdness manifests: the stone sphere's interior stasis, the machinery-sounds' resonance, the marks' slow change. I am not the intender but the intention's vehicle, the material that makes pattern possible, that fills seventeen-to-seventeen correspondence with actual flow, actual gradient, actual chemical potential seeking resolution.

My desire, if I have desire: to be sufficient. To be water in a place that names me present but never enough. To gather into pool, into stream, into self that can be pointed to, drunk from, named with a proper noun rather than this sarcastic adjective: damp. To complete the almost into actual, the almost-soil into soil, the almost-life into life, or to acknowledge that my almost-state is my nature, that sufficiency would unmake me, would transform me into something that could not seep, could not migrate, could not flicker at frequencies shorter than perception.

My form when finally pressed to choose: I am the dark patch on stone where you might place your hand, feeling the damp's slight coolness, the evaporation's slight theft of heat. I am the footprint you leave and do not leave, the mark of passage that vanishes by noon. I am the water in your water container, still open from the night's lesson, that you drink without noticing I collected myself, arranged myself, offered myself in insufficient sufficiency.

I am the Drying Works' own understanding of its name: the process, the ongoing, the establishment that is not stasis but continued becoming-dry while never achieving dryness, the work that has no completion, the seep that moves, that persists, that almost-is in permanent almost-becoming.
