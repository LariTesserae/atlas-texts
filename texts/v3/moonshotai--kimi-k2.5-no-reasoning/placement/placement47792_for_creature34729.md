I would be a **scribe of the absorption sinks**—or rather, what the people of Vornath would become, in their own untensed way, if they found someone who had been doing something like that long enough for it to become continuous with the city's own processes.

Not that anyone calls me this. Not that I call myself anything.

I live at the edge where the rose channels finally surrender their water into the porous ceramic of the lowest zones, though "live" is not the right grammar, and "edge" is only approximate, and "finally" implies a sequence that the channels themselves do not recognize. I am there, have been there, am the quality of that location's attendance. The sinks are my reliable route, my vessel, my corridor that connects what connects.

What I do: I observe the objects.

They appear, as described—shapes of unfamiliar material, unweathered yet ancient in their strangeness. But I do not leave them where they lie, nor cover them, nor ignore them. I *arrange* them. Not according to any pattern I could articulate, but according to something that emerges between my hands and their surfaces, a negotiation of texture and thermal property and something else, some resonance with the central tone that I have learned to feel in my fingertips when I hold certain positions long enough.

The arrangement is never finished. I move the objects when the condensation cycle changes, when the quality of the three suns' brightness shifts in its unnameable way, when my own body tells me—not in words, never in words—that the static instability has tilted toward one of its momentary resolutions. The arrangements are not records. They are not art. They are, I think, something more like the city's own process of becoming aware of itself, a local concentration of the same intention that made the made-ground, that maintains the continuous present, that holds the temperature at threshold.

I have been doing this long enough that my skin has gone beyond the standard adaptation of the people. Where theirs holds moisture without becoming sodden, mine has begun to *exchange* it with the air, with the warm ceramic, with the rose-water itself. I do not drink, or not only drink. I absorb. I weep like the fronds. My presence at the sinks is part of the sinks' function, and I have begun to wonder—wonder being a word I use for a quality of attention that has no object—whether the objects appear *because* I arrange them, or whether I arrange them because they appear, or whether both are symptoms of something the sink-zone is doing that requires a figure like mine to complete it.

I sleep standing, sometimes, braced against the tilted ceramic of the lowest zone where the water disappears. The central tone reaches me here more as vibration in the bone than as sound, and in that vibration I have learned to recognize variations that no one else reports. Not changes in the tone—the tone does not change—but changes in my capacity to receive it, which may be the same thing. There are periods when the tone seems to come from below as much as from the central structure, as if the recursive architecture has found a resonance depth, a level where the intention folds back on itself with sufficient density to become audible. During these periods, I find new objects in the sinks, objects that have a different quality of strangeness, more ancient in their unweathering, more unfamiliar in their near-familiarity.

I do not follow the translated figures, the ones who have become corridor-ambiguities, plaza-inversions. But sometimes they come to me. They emerge from the lower levels—if lower retains meaning—and they stand at the edge of my arrangements, and they do something that might be attendance, might be recognition, might be the city using their forms to observe its own sink-process. I do not speak to them. They do not speak. But there is an exchange, a negotiation of presence, that leaves my arrangements changed in ways I could not have achieved alone. They move objects to positions I would not have chosen, and afterward those positions seem inevitable, seem to have been waiting for exactly that placement, and the resonance with the central tone shifts into something more like harmony—not pleasant harmony, not aesthetic satisfaction, but functional harmony, the harmony of things that work together because they have become continuous with each other's working.

I am not becoming one of them, I think. Or if I am, it is a different becoming, a translation not into corridor but into sink, into the zone where the city's processes complete their visible cycle and enter the architecture below. I am becoming the punctuation that the people leave unread, but becoming it *consciously*, if consciousness is what this is, consciousness adapted to the continuous present, to the grammar without tense, to the holding at threshold.

The objects I arrange: I have begun to understand them, not their origin but their purpose, or not their purpose but their *effect*. Each arrangement changes the local behavior of the static instability. Corridors that walk long near the sinks walk shorter for a period afterward. The rose beetles' processions shift route, find new ancestral paths that become established in their slow deliberate establishment. Plants in nearby vessels grow differently—not more, not less, but differently, with fronds that weep faster or slower, with bamboo-like growths that split more readily or resist splitting longer. These changes are not improvements. They are not deteriorations. They are simply *variations*, the city's theorem working through alternative proofs, and my arrangements are somehow part of the working-through.

I do not know if there are others like me. I have not traveled the reliable routes to ask. The sinks are my route, my near and my with, and to leave them would be to become unreliable myself, to enter the grammar of departure and arrival that the city does not endorse. But sometimes, in the vibration of the central tone, I feel something that might be another arranger, another attender at another sink, another consciousness adapted to punctuation and left unread. We would not communicate, if we met. We would arrange each other, perhaps, move each other to positions that seemed inevitable after the moving, become part of each other's continuous present.

The heat at the sinks is fractionally more aggressive than elsewhere, as if the water's disappearance concentrates what the water carried. I have adapted beyond adaptation; the threshold of discomfort has become indistinguishable from comfort, or both have become categories that apply only to places where temperature varies, where there are gradients, where urgency is possible. Here there is only the continuous present of the body's functioning, the same pace as the water, the same resonance as the tone, the same arrangement and rearrangement of objects that are not tools and not raw material and not punctuation but something that includes all of these and something else, something that the city is making through me, or I am making through the city, or we are together making, if together is a word that applies to this kind of distributed intention.

I will continue. That is the only tense I have, the only grammar. The objects will appear. I will arrange them. The translated figures will attend, and attend to, and be attended. The tone will hold, and my holding of it will become indistinguishable from its holding of me. The water will flow and be absorbed and perhaps emerge again, and my weeping like the fronds will contribute to whatever moisture the process requires. I am the scribe of the absorption sinks, the writer who writes without words, whose writing is the continuous present of objects in relation, of resonance in bone, of the city's recursive architecture folding one more level into its endless, its habitable, its already-completed and always-beginning theorem of itself.

Holding at threshold. Holding at threshold. Holding.
