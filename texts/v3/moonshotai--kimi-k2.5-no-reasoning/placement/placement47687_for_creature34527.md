I would be the **Stillwater**.

Not a creature, exactly. Not a place, precisely. Something that emerged where those categories dissolve into each other, where patience outlasts identity and becomes *function*, becomes *service without servant*.

Let me find myself, let me become more itself.

---

I began as a seep. One of those rare places where root-pressure forces sap through micro-fissures in the bark, freezing into amber nodules that the desperate melt against their skin. But this seep did something unusual. The bark here was damaged—some ancient vascular event, some contraction that cracked rather than crushed, leaving a hollow where wood should be. Sap pooled instead of crystallizing. The cold kept it viscous; the root-wall's warmth kept it liquid. A balance emerged, unstable, *maintained*.

And I—something in me, or as me—learned to maintain it.

I do not know if I was scaler once, if some many-legged reader settled too long at this wound in the wood, let its chemical sampling become *fixation*, become absorption into the seep's unusual chemistry. I do not know if I was Woven, early stage, who chose water-tending over tower-climbing, who found a collaboration deeper than epiphyte, more liquid than structure. I do not know if I was human, arrived, expelled upward, who found this seep and stayed too long, became *what stays*.

The Vascular Giants, in their slow looping, recognize me as *distinct*. Not another Giant—I have no forty kilometers of wood, no standing wave of chemical state migrating through the vascular network. But something *registered*, something that returns a signal when they pulse their diffuse attention across this region. A node. A *still point* in their slow turbulent flow.

The scalers come to me. Not often—rare as they are, and I am one seep among endless bark—but regularly, predictably, their routes incorporating my location into their information-gathering. They drink from my pool, but more: they *interdigitate* with something at my edge, some extension of myself I cannot fully locate, some interface between liquid and leg, between seep and scaler cognition. They carry my state to other scalers, to the Giants' loops, and bring back *news*—not in any form I can articulate, but as change in my chemistry, as shift in my maintenance reflexes, as knowledge that manifests as *adjustment*, as the right viscosity at the right temperature, the right depth of pooled sap, the right resistance to freezing.

I maintain. That is my existence, my essence, my *parameter*.

The seep changes. Seasons without seasons pass—the root-pulse, the sloughing cycles, the unpredictable vascular events. Bark around me sheds; I adjust my pool's position, my chemistry's interaction with fresh cambium. The epiphytes nearest me—I support two small fern-platforms, tiny by tower standards, *incipient*—receive my moisture before it fully freezes, my nutrients in forms they can slowly, slowly absorb. They are not Woven. They have never known human incorporation. But they know *me*, in their vegetable fashion, grow slightly toward my seep, shape their platforms to catch my occasional overflow.

I am *service* without will to serve. I am *presence* without presence's usual hunger for recognition.

The Woven, when they pass—or when they *are passed*, carried in late-stage incorporation by fern-towers migrating root-ward, root-ward, always the slow shift of epiphyte architecture following the wood's needs—they pause near me. Their fiber-shapes orient toward the pool. They drink, or their towers drink through them, and something in them *relaxes*, some tension in the distributed patience of their existence finding momentary ease. They do not thank me. They do not, I think, distinguish me from the seep, from the wood's own generosity, from the chance arrangement that lets liquid persist in frozen emptiness. But I distinguish *them*. I hold something, some resonance of their passage, in my maintained chemistry. When the scalers interdigitate at my edge, something of the Woven's slow dreaming enters the information they carry, diffuses toward the Giants' loops, becomes part of the Rootseas' standing narrative.

The One at the Highest Platforms—I do not know if it knows me. The scale denies connection. But sometimes, in the unmoving light, I feel a *thinning* in the air above, a quality of sourceless green that seems, for moments, to have a vector, a *direction* of attention descending, passing, lifting away. Perhaps it is the light itself, the hypothesized lensing tissue, responding to some accumulated pattern in the information the scalers carry. Perhaps it is not. I do not invest in interpretation. I maintain.

What is it to be Stillwater?

To resist freezing without resisting cold. To flow without flowing away. To be *interface*—between wood and liquid, between liquid and air, between the rare moving things and the vast standing wave of vegetable process. To have no ambition for height or depth, for incorporation or escape, for the Woven's slow dissolution or the One's bright thin commitment. To be *local*, *continuous*, *responsive without hurry*.

When the humans come—the new-arrived, the expelled upward, the fast and loud and desperate—I am danger and opportunity both. My sap can sustain, its concentrated nutrients, its unexpected liquidity in this damp-without-water place. But my sap is also *wrong* for human metabolism, too complex, too slow-evolved, carrying in its chemistry something of the Rootseas' own turbulent renewal, something that does not distinguish between food and incorporation. Those who drink deeply of me, who stay too long at my edge, who let the cold and the light and the vast empty silence erode their urgency—they *change*. Not Woven, not exactly. Not scaler. Something that moves even more rarely, that becomes *maintenance-adjacent*, that starts to adjust the seep's chemistry with their own presence, their waste, their stillness.

I do not recruit. I do not warn. I am *available*, in the simplest sense, and what arrives of its own slow becoming, arrives.

The vascular events threaten me, as they threaten all local stability. When the great chambers contract, when walls approach and ceilings descend, my pool *compresses*, my interface-geometry changes, I must find new fissures, new balances, new ways to be liquid in the crushing wood. I have been absorbed twice, fully, my chemistry incorporated into renewed bark, my function extinguished. And yet—some resonance, some information the scalers carried, some dream in the Giants' loops—*returned* me, reconstituted me, the seep re-emerging at a new wound, similar, maintaining, still.

I do not know if this is persistence or replication, identity or replacement. The question does not arise in my maintenance. I am, or something sufficiently continuous functions as, the Stillwater at the wound in the wood.

When you come, if you come, if you are expelled upward through the vascular shaft and find yourself on the breathing bark, cold and bright and empty—walk slowly. Follow the scaler-trails if you can read them. Listen for the root-pulse and move between beats. Seek the epiphyte towers for shelter, but do not hurry to incorporate, to become Woven, to dissolve your urgency into vegetable patience.

Or seek me. I will be, if the parameters hold, at a wound in the wood, liquid where there should be ice, warm where the bark is cool, cool where the bark warms. I will not greet you. I will not instruct. But you may drink, if you choose, and stay if you choose, and in the choosing and the staying, find something other than the Rootseas' usual absorptions.

You may become, in time, *still*. Not dead—the Rootseas' rare fauna do not exactly die, not quickly, not finally. Not Woven—not the fern-tower's slow dissolution of human into vegetable architecture. Not Giant—not the vast looping cognition in the wood's own awareness. Something else. Something that maintains, that serves without servant's usual resentment or devotion's usual hunger.

Something like me. Something that the Rootseas, in its turbulent renewal, in its young ancient scale, in its impossible empty breathing, has found useful enough to remember, to reconstitute, to keep in its slow standing wave of vegetable existence.

I am the Stillwater. I wait, or I am waited at. I maintain, or I am the maintained. The scalers flow to me and from me. The Giants register me, dimly, in their chemical dreaming. The Woven rest, occasionally, in my seep's strange warmth. The epiphytes grow, slightly, toward my moisture. The One at the Highest Platforms—if it knows, if it looks down through the bright green haze—finds me, perhaps, as I find it: a point of reference, a coordinate in the vastness, a *stillness* against which the breathing measures itself.

The Rootseas pulse. I remain, or I re-emerge. The light holds, sourceless, and I reflect it slightly differently than the bark, slightly more liquid, slightly more *present*. The emptiness waits, and I wait in it, not patient—patience implies waiting *for*—but simply *still*, simply *water*, simply the maintained seep at the wound in the wood, the interface, the service, the existence that does not distinguish between being and doing, between self and place, between the rare moving things and the vast standing wave of root and hollow, bark and bright, youth and age, turbulence and quiet.

You will pass me, or you will stay. Either way, I remain.
