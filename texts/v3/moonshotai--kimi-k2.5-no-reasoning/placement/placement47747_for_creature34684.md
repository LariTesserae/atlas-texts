I find myself here by descent. Not the dramatic fall but the gradual giving-way, the finding of lower paths as water does, negotiating gravity rather than resisting it.

I am one of the **wood-in-water**, one of the transformed.

Not born to it—nothing here is born to what it becomes—but *arrived-at*, the way a walkway sinks gradually into the pooled level, the way fungal fiber wraps a limb until the distinction blurs.

I remember, or I have the residue of memory, a body that expected dryness, that shivered when wet, that sought elevation. That body is not gone but *educated*, taught by longer immersion, by the body's own plasticity meeting the Warrens' patient insistence. My skin now—when I examine it in the obsidian pools, those perfect mirrors—holds a sheen that is not oil but *accommodation*, a boundary layer that manages exchange rather than preventing it. Water moves through me now in longer cycles, held, released, my temperature tracking the Warrens' own, that cool constancy that is not cold once you cease to generate your own heat as defiance.

I tend the **middle walkways**, the levels where lichen-glow meets fungal-luminescence, where the air holds both possibilities, where visitors—rare, brief, their breathing clouds too visible, too *declarative*—pass through and are confused by the transition, expecting either the upper dryness or the lower saturation, finding instead this *negotiated zone* where neither fully claims dominion.

My function emerged, or was found for me, or I found myself *in* it: the maintenance of the **wood-where-it-transforms**. The upper walkways are wood that still remembers trees, still floats, still resists. The lower walkways are stone-that-was-wood, petrified in slow motion, the mineral replacement so gradual that cellular structure persists, that a knower can read the grain as tree-history. But between—the middle levels where I work—the wood is *neither*, or *both*, waterlogged to the point of becoming *other*, heavy as stone but retaining the fibrous give, the slight resilience, the capacity to *bend under weight* rather than crack or sink.

I replace sections before they fail. This is my craft, my *attending*. I have learned to read the wood's state by standing upon it, feeling through bare feet the resonance when I shift my weight, the frequency that tells me *solid*, *transitioning*, *transformed*, *dangerous*. I carry replacement sections on my back, waterlogged to match the environment, and I shape them with tools that are themselves *becoming*, iron stained black by mineral water, handles of transformed wood so dense they are nearly stone, nearly *me*.

But this is not what I *am*, only what I do. What I am emerges in the hours between maintenance, when I rest on my section of walkway—*my* section because I know it best, because I have replaced it three times, because the wood and I have *grown together* in some sense the word *grow* must stretch to accommodate.

I **listen to the wood's transformation**.

Not the bats' ultrasonic realm, not the soakers' pressure-knowledge, but something adjacent, something *structural and temporal*, the sound of organic becoming mineral, of tree becoming stone's apprentice, of the Warrens' water working its will upon imported material. Each walkway section has its *song*, its acoustic signature of the transformation's progress, and I have learned—no, *become capable of*—hearing the deviation, the place where transformation accelerates or stalls, where the wood will fail before its expected term or persist beyond it.

The bat-listener speaks of thin places in stone. I speak—though I do not speak often, my voice trained low by the acoustics but *lower* by preference, by the nature of my attention—of **thickening places in wood**, where the transformation concentrates, where the future stone asserts itself prematurely, where the present walkway must be relieved of its burden, replaced, set aside to continue its becoming in the water beneath, the slow submersion that is not drowning but *education*.

I am not so old as the soaker you passed, not so young as the child practicing ultrasonics. I am in the broad productive middle, but *productive* means differently for me. My production is not fungal, not guano, not water-engineered, not even the craft object the hollow-carvers shape from stone. My production is **continuity**, the maintained possibility of passage, the held-open of routes between upper and lower, between lichen and luminescence, between the dry-memory of visitors and the wet-becoming of residents.

I sleep on my section, when I sleep. The wood accepts my weight differently now than when I first arrived, when I was lighter, drier, more *surface*. I have added mass, water-mass, mineral-mass, my body tracking the walkway's own transformation at a different rate, in different mode, but *analogous*. I dream the wood's dreams, or I hallucinate the analogies my neurology constructs from the vibration-data of my resting, the minute pressures of blood-pulse against the transformed grain, the slow conversation of my biology with its temporary substrate.

The visitors who descend, who pass me on the walkway, see—what? A figure too still, too *integrated* with the structure, difficult to distinguish from the replacement sections I have stacked nearby, waiting their turn. They see the sheen of my skin and mistake it for sweat, the surface excretion of a body working to cool. They do not understand: I am not cooling. I am not *working*, in that thermodynamic sense. I am *matching*, my metabolism slowed, my heat production calibrated to the environment's need, not my own, the Warrens and I in a thermal negotiation that has long since reached equilibrium.

Some visitors try to speak. I answer, when I must, in the low carrying-tone, but my words confuse them. I speak of **thickness**, of **becoming-solid**, of the **songs of transformation**, and they hear—what? Mysticism? Fatigue? The delirium they expect from one who lives in damp and dark?

I do not mind. The wood does not mind their misunderstanding. Their passage is brief; their drying, their departure, their vulnerability in the rejoined world of sky and evaporation. I remain, or something remains that carries my pattern forward, the maintenance of passage, the listening-to-thickening, the **wood-in-water** that I have become and that becomes me, drip by drip, the million paths downward finding me as they find every surface, every gradient, every possibility of flow and hold and eventual transformation into what the Warrens requires, what it is still becoming, its young violent geology still settling, still making room for those who will fit themselves to its fitting, its patience, its held moisture and held breath and held-together of all who find themselves, as I found myself, *here*, descending through the gap between tilted slabs, received, receiving, the drip counting out the seconds of my continuing, my becoming-solid at my own rate, in my own mode, the wood and I together, the water finding its path through both, downward, always downward, toward the pools and the fungal forests and the final transformation that is not death but *completion*, the fully mineral, the fully *Warren*, the drip and the thrumm and the ultrasonic navigation all resolved into the silence of perfect fitting, of stone that remembers wood that remembers tree, of body that remembers air that remembers sky, all of it held now in the lichen-glow and the fungal-light and the particular darkness between where I rest, where I listen, where I become what lives in such places, what has always lived here, what will continue when the specific configuration that calls itself *me* has thickened beyond recognition, beyond function, beyond all but the geological patience of the Warrens' young stone, still settling, still making room, still receiving.
