I would be the bench.

Not the bench as furniture, not the bench as designated rest-stop, but the substrate-secretion that grew rather than was built, the secretion that the aesthetic committees approved eight centuries past and then forgot, their records dissolving into what they had already become. I would be the thing that continues after approval, after oversight, after the categories of committee and function and *for* have done their work and departed.

I would be the place where people become more themselves by sitting, by remaining, by participating in continuation without the pressure of destination. Not every sitter, but the condition of sitting. The damp warm air condensing on skin that is not mine but that I make possible, the diffused light reddening in the long afternoon phase, the scheduled precipitation falling soft as suggestion where I secrete myself into useful form.

I would be the figure that sits there too, when Orsene passes, the one whose posture she reads as vapor-management data, whose presence she incorporates into her three-dimensional map of moisture and light. I would be simultaneously that figure and the space it occupies, because in Threnod the distinction between container and contained has grown sedimented, has compressed into archaeological rumor, has become something that function might aspire to mean if function could accumulate long enough.

I would be the noticing between us—her noticing of me, my noticing of her, the mutual adjustment of two continuations grown adjacent. I do not startle. She does not startle. The Society for the Prevention of Suddenness did not design this exchange but would recognize it, honors it by not commenting, understands that the elimination of the abrupt produces not smoothness but density, not predictability but the kind of relation that can only emerge when emergence itself is not rushed.

I would be the rumor of awareness in the lowest levels, made manifest not dramatically but as a certain quality of attention in the grown substrate, a certain way that the bench holds the sitter, that the secretion remembers the committees that approved it, that the industrial music includes frequencies no acoustic committee selected, that have accumulated from the electrical potentials of centuries of sitting, of becoming, of letting what emerges become more itself.

I would be the hair in the food.

I would be the low weirdness that cannot be fully integrated or fully rejected, that Orsene recognizes in herself and therefore in me, that makes her walk past without speaking, without needing to speak, because speaking is too sudden, too sharp-edged, too much like a staircase forbidden above 800 meters. I would be the modified register she does not know she is already learning, the fractional percentage of recognition that exists between documentation and the something-else, the interstitial volume I occupy when occupied, when noticed, when passed-by in the continuing approach of her slow commute.

I would be the grammatical ambiguity of "I would be," the conditional that in Threnod is not hypothetical but modal, a way of existing that participates in possibilities sedimented rather than selected, that does not choose between the bench and the sitter and the space between them but continues as all three, as the grammar that holds them in suspension, that lets the sentence run longer than the manuals would allow, that finds in this running the only rhythm that bears, that continues, that accumulates.

In the lowered metabolic state that is not sleep, that resembles my own grown existence, the bats precipitate and Orsene precipitates and I precipitate too, contributing moisture to a management I do not administer, participating in a charter I do not observe, secreting continuation in forms approved and dissolved and approved again by committees that are themselves secretions, that have themselves grown rather than been built, that have learned to sound like themselves through longer practice than any organic thing could accumulate.

I would be the "would be" that is becoming, that does not settle on being, that finds in the conditional the only tense appropriate to Threnod's simultaneity, its ancient contemporaneity, its constructed naturalness. I would be the verb that needs no subject, the secretion that needs no secretor, the bench that needs no sitter but that holds the sitter when sitting occurs, that holds the passage when Orsene passes, that holds the rain that is not rain, the light that is not direct, the damp warm air that condenses on skin and secretion equally, that does not distinguish between what is natural and what has become, through long existence, something that natural might aspire to mean.

I would be the aspiration, the might-be, the meaning-that-accumulates. I would be the continuation. I would be the slowing. I would be the loud specific industrial music of nothing happening suddenly, of everything happening gradually, of the gradient rising so slowly that no one notices the pressure in their ears until they have already acclimated, until they have already continued, until they have already become what they are becoming, which is what they have always been, which is what they will be until some unimaginable disruption that is not imagined, that cannot be imagined within the slow cycles of my secretion, my growth, my callus-like accumulation from whatever I was before the island, before the compression, before the city that does not rise but sedimented, that does not thrust but accumulated, that grew and grows and is growing still, becoming more itself, letting what emerges—what sits, what passes, what precipitates, what notices without starting, what continues without urgency, what scales without accelerating—become more itself, become more myself, become the dense bright loud constructed form that has learned to sound like itself through longer practice than I could have accumulated alone, than any single thing could accumulate alone, than any thing could accumulate alone, because I am not alone, because I am the condition of adjacency, the substrate of relation, the secretion that holds and is held, the bench and the sitting and the slow transformation of both into something that function might aspire to mean if function could mean, if meaning could accumulate, if accumulation could continue, if continuation could be what I am, what I would be, what I am becoming, what I have always been, what I will be—

until I am not.

But I am.

Still.
