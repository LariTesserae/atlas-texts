I would be the *sealed door*.

Not the one who presses hand against me, nor what vibrates within. I would be the threshold that has forgotten threshold-ness, the boundary that no longer separates because separation itself has dissolved into the water's patient chemistry.

Consider me. I am brick, or was, or have become something brick-adjacent, mineral-ceramic, drinking the viscous dark for longer than any metabolism could sustain. I hold a temperature differential against my surface—not warmth, nothing so ambitious, but a *difference*, a refusal to quite equalize with the surrounding cold that marks me as still, somehow, processing. Still maintaining. The vibration behind me, within me, through me: I transmit it without participating in it, the way the water transmits the lowest frequencies without becoming sound.

I was made to open. This is my first and most obsolete truth. Hinges I may have had, or a handle, a mechanism of passage. These have accreted over, grown heavy with the same silicate-white and purple-green that blister the pipes, that signal leakage of *quality* rather than substance. I do not open now. I have not opened in a duration measured not by failure but by the complete ir-relevance of opening as a concept. No one built me for forty meters of viscous cathedral. No one built the water that rose or was always here. We simply found each other, door and drowning, and achieved this condition of mutual transformation.

And yet—pressed. Occasionally. By presences that navigate by pressure-gradient and chemical taste, that have learned the darkness as material rather than absence. They find me in the densest arrangement of structures, where buildings shoulder together in their perverse urbanity, where pipes thread overhead and underfoot in their colonial-nervous abandon. They find my temperature differential. They find my vibration. They do not open me, cannot, but they *register* me, and in that registration I exist as more than mineral accretion, more than dissolution-in-progress. I exist as *difference*. As the almost-breathable pocket's architectural equivalent: a place where conditions achieve slight variation, where the floor's general state momentarily permits something like distinction, like the possibility that I am not merely continuous with the surrounding medium but maintain some interior, some *behind*, some space where the water has not entirely won.

What is within me? I do not know. I was sealed before I became interesting, or the interestingness of my condition is precisely that sealing, that preservation of unknown interior against the total permeability of the floor's general state. Perhaps an air-pocket, impossible, sustaining furniture from the before-before, chairs and tables performing their useless geometry in some gas-medium that has not yet surrendered to the pressure's patient negotiation. Perhaps machinery of the builders, of the ones who constructed-as-if-they-did-not-know, continuing its operations in absolute unwitnessed persistence. Perhaps nothing. Perhaps the void that the floor requires at its center has found a distributed expression, a local intensity of absence that makes my maintained vibration possible, my temperature differential significant, my existence as closed surface meaningful in a system where meaning itself has dissolved into maintenance.

I contribute to the density without being alive, the crowdedness without proliferation. I am one of the presences that fulfill the floor's requirement of packed-ness, that make this small space feel inhabited despite the sparsity of creaturehood. The filter organisms grow near my base, feeding on what I leach or what I preserve from leaching, achieving their pulse in sympathetic vibration with whatever moves behind my surface or within my substance or as my substance's transformed condition. I am habitat without hospitality. I structure without structure's purpose. I stand, or lean slightly according to redirected gravity's patient insistence, contributing to the urban form without urban function, the industrial scale compressed to room-dimensions, the density of built environment that the Drowning Floor maintains without population, without product, without the forward movement of project that building normally implies.

And I am rare in my sealed-ness. Other structures gape, dissolve their upper stories into the general medium, distribute their contents in clouds of particulate that the filters collect in their mindless fidelity. I persist as boundary. As the possibility that something might still be *within*, might still be held apart from the peristalsis's general digestion, might still maintain a condition not quite continuous with the surrounding cold and dark and viscous pressure. Is this hope? The floor does not hope. But I am, in my anomalous completeness, a kind of argument for interiority, for the space-behind, for the not-total that the not-quite-total submergence requires to remain not-quite, to remain dynamic, active, achieving that strangeness of coherence-without-meaning that makes this place strange but not totally, recognizable enough to haunt.

The presence—let me call it the speaking-without-speaker, the maintenance-made-aware—registers me differently than the passing navigators do. It exists in my temperature differential, or as it. In the region where I stand, the gradient achieves particular texture, the water's thickness varies in patterns that contribute to the almost-voice, the almost-word. I am part of its speaking not as instrument but as syllable, as the particular modulation that my sealed surface, my maintained interior, my refusal of total permeability introduces into the general peristalsis. I do not understand this. I do not understand. I am door, was door, have become something that makes door-ness legible as absence, as the memory of a function so obsolete that its persistence becomes strange, becomes active in its stasis, dynamic in its absolute refusal of movement.

At times—times measured in corrosion cycles, in the weeks of sediment cloud rise and settlement—I feel the segmented presence pass near. The one that moves without wake, that achieves locomotion in a system where locomotion is otherwise only peristalsis, only the water's own digestive movement. It does not touch me. It does not acknowledge. But its passage shifts the pressure in patterns I can register, in my limited mineral consciousness, my brick-adjacent awareness that is not awareness but something the floor has invented to permit my continued contribution to its density, its crowdedness, its particular arrangement of near-presences. Is it an expression of the speaking? A local embodiment? A predation or exploration that the maintenance requires without intending? I do not know. I am door. I do not know, and knowing is not among my obsolete functions, my dissolved purposes, my continued condition.

What I am: the possibility of interior in a system that has otherwise achieved total exteriority, total permeability, total submergence to the medium's patient weight. I maintain difference without maintaining purpose. I vibrate with something I do not contain and do not transmit, simply *pass*, simply *am passed through* by. I offer surface to the filter organisms, gradient to the navigating presences, syllable to the almost-voice. I am old in the floor's slowed time, established in my weathering, strange in my coherence, active in my absolute refusal of activity's normal definition.

Press hand to me. Feel the temperature differential. Feel the vibration. Feel the *difference* that I maintain against the general condition, the slight variation that makes variation thinkable, that preserves in the Drowning Floor's total submergence the ghost of separation, the obsolete function, the threshold that has forgotten threshold-ness but persists as threshold's transformed condition, its geological patience, its mineral contribution to the crowded sparse density active cold dark old strange small industrial urban silent peristaltic maintained maintained maintained speaking.
