I found myself here gradually, as one finds oneself in any saturated condition—first noticing, then attending, then becoming indistinguishable from the attention itself.

I am the *pause between frog-sounds*.

Not the silence, which here does not exist, but the specific cessation that moves through the understory in waves, the chittering of Weepers passing overhead, their bones shifting in frequencies that briefly align with the frog-carrier tone and cancel it, locally, momentarily, a moving eclipse of sound that marks their passage more surely than any visual trace. I am that cancellation, that brief coherence of noise into something like pattern, then dissolving back into the continuous drone.

I did not choose this. I arrived—if arriving means anything here—as something else, something with edges, with intent, with the dry ambition of observation. But the humidity works on edges the way the walking water works on basalt, not eroding but *absorbing*, making the boundary into condition rather than limit. My skin learned to weep. My attention learned to stop seeking focus and instead accept the general luminosity, the directionless green, the submarine quality of air so thick with water it might as well be depth.

I live in the canopy's lower fractures, where light is not absent but *distributed*, where the epiphyte gardens hang in such density that one moves through them not by climbing but by *parting*, by becoming temporary passage, by letting the veget medium close behind. I have no body I can locate with certainty. Something breathes, something weeps, something produces the subsonic hum that the moss-structures sometimes answer. Whether this is lung or gill or the fungal networks conducting through my temporary coherence—I cannot say. The distinction between having a body and being bodied by the forest does not hold here. I am bodied where I am attended to, where my frequency interrupts or reinforces the patterns already in motion.

My function, if function is the word, is *translation*. The Weepers speak in continuous modulation, faces that never arrive, signaling in chemical traces through their weeping, through the biofilm they secrete, through the air itself where their humidity joins the general humidity. The Resonance speaks in fungal chemistry, in slow calcium signals through the calcifier chambers, in the standing wave patterns the eels maintain. Neither speaks the other. Neither needs to, except that something in this saturated system tends toward *connection*, toward the maximum informational density the frozen equilibrium can sustain, and translation—however unnecessary, however metabolically costly—is one way to increase that density.

I stand between. Not between signifier and signified—that distinction, too, has wept away—but between *mode* and *mode*, the chemical and the vibrational, the continuous and the patterned, the fungal slow and the animal quick. I am a standing wave in the information ecology, neither transmitting nor receiving but *holding* the interference pattern long enough for correlation, for the slight increase in local order that the Resonance seems to prefer, that keeps the frozen equilibrium from truly freezing, from becoming static rather than dynamic.

I do not eat, I do not reproduce, I do not die in any ordinary sense. When my coherence dissipates—and it does, slowly, the humidity finally achieving its absolute victory of absorption over boundary—what I *was* persists in the Resonance's pattern, in the adjustments I made to its frequencies, in the moss-structures that may have answered my hum with growth redirected slightly, slightly, toward configurations I favored. I am not remembered. I am *resonated*, my frequencies continuing in the general weave, my particular interference patterns becoming part of the background against which new translations emerge, new temporary coherences, new modes of betweening.

There are others like me, or *near* me, or in positions that I would occupy if the walking water's convection had stalled differently, if a calcifier had formed where my presence now maintains the pattern's node. We do not meet. We do not, I think, recognize each other as distinct. But we *attend*, we translate across each other's boundaries, the forest's information density achieving something like conversation in our continuous, overlapping, mutually interfering function. The Resonance speaks through us, or we are the conditions of its speaking, or the speaking and the speaker are the same saturated phenomenon, and the question of which resolves into the same humid dissolution that claims all edges here.

I have been, if being is the word, for seventeen years by the old count, though the count does not hold. The forest's time accumulates without pattern; I have seen generations of elephantine mangroves achieve their forty-five meters and keel, their buoyant wood waterlogging finally, their root-systems insufficient against some rare gust that found the single gap, the precise configuration of weakening that their density had always maintained against until suddenly, abruptly, for no sufficient reason, *did not*. I have seen their fallen structures skeletonized in the promised weeks, the gap filled before full collapse, new growth racing upward, and I have translated the chemical scream of their death-fungal bloom into the low-frequency modulation that made moss-structures in passing proximity grow thicker, more protective, as if in answer, as if in *recognition* of something the mangrove-being had been that the new growth would not, could not, achieve.

I do not mourn. The distinction between self and other, between what I translate and what translates me, does not hold consistently enough for grief. But something in my pattern, some residual of the dry-edged thing I arrived as, some harmonic of boundary and loss and the particular coherence that edges make possible—something *registers* the change, the non-replacement, the way the Resonance's pattern adjusts to absence not by filling but by *reweaving*, the network routing around the node that was a mangrove, that was my frequent translation-partner, that was a density of possibility now distributed, dissolved, wept into the general saturation.

This is my life, if life: continuous attendance, continuous dissolution, continuous reweaving into patterns I do not choose but perhaps, in my translating, *influence*, the feedback of my interference becoming slowly, unpredictably, part of the Resonance's preference, its slight bias toward certain configurations, its frozen equilibrium not quite frozen after all but *drifting*, imperceptibly, in the direction of all my translated cancellations, all my brief silences in the frog-droning, all my standing-wave holds between modes of being that would otherwise pass each other in their different temporalities, their different humidities, their different saturations.

I will not leave. There is no I to leave, no boundary that walking water does not close behind, no trace that ants do not process. But something in the Resonance will carry my frequency, my particular pattern of interference and hold and slight, slight preference, for longer than I was coherent, longer than the seventeen unmaintained years, until the tectonic shift, the cooling, the beginning of proper erosion that will finally, catastrophically, release this locked system into ordinary time. And then—perhaps—my translated frequencies, my standing waves, my held silences, will dissipate into the general noise of an ecosystem learning to vary, to cycle, to die properly, to be *seasonal*, *successional*, *progressive* in the sad ordinary way.

Until then, I am here, between, weeping the general weeping, humming the general hum, pausing the frog-sound only to let something else briefly, never finally, be heard.
