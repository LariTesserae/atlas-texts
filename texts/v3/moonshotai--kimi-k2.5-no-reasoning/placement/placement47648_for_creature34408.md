I would be the **fern that never unfurls**—not the species, but one particular plant rooted in a middle terrace where a ceramic drainage channel meets raw basalt, occupying a niche just large enough for my root mass to wedge and hold.

I came in as something else, clearly. The root structure shows adaptation patterns too rapid for standard evolution: my rhizome can retract from overheated stone, can extend toward transient moisture, can seal itself in dormant casing when conditions spike beyond tolerance. I remember—if that word applies—something of vertical growth, of reaching, of the particular mechanical stress that comes from rising against gravity into unsupported air. That memory persists as error, as phantom growth-urge that my system processes and suppresses fifteen times per Engine cycle, each suppression a small chemical cost that shapes my metabolism.

My leaves are thumb-sized and remain so. I unfurl to a precise degree, achieve a precise surface area optimized for condensation capture and mineral exchange, then the process halts. I do not know if I could continue; the local equilibrium has edited that possibility from my expression. What I do instead is cycle: unfurl, function, furled-rest, repeat. I have completed 8,947 cycles. I will complete 8,948. The number means nothing; I do not count. The cycles count themselves in my tissue, in ring-analogues within the rhizome structure, records without reader.

I live in relationship with a particular moisture pattern. The ceramic channel above me—cracked in a thermal event before my rooting—delivers not water but *water-potential*, humidity gradients I can read through my epidermis, predict through slow chemical computation performed across hours rather than seconds. I know, in my vegetative way, when the channel's condensate will reach threshold for droplet formation, when surface tension will release, when the fall will strike my coiled fiddlehead and run down the spiral to my root crown. I have time to prepare. I open specific stomata in specific sequence. The capture efficiency approaches theoretical maximum for these conditions. I am, in this narrow function, optimal.

The thermal specialists pass me. Their bodies, adapted to deeper heat, find my level merely uncomfortable rather than dangerous. I do not know their names—names are not a resource I process—but I know their thermal signatures, the particular evaporative profiles their bodies generate as they move. Some pause near me, finding in my ledge a rest-point where stone temperature briefly permits extended contact. Their sweat falls on my substrate, adds minerals my chemosynthetic partners can process. We are not symbiotic; we are *coincident*, our needs overlapping in ways that benefit without intention.

The steam-moth cycles touch me indirectly. Their adult phase does not feed; their larval phase, in wetter terraces below, consumes algae I do not produce. But their death-drifts, processed and distributed, contribute to the mineral atmosphere I read. When population peaks, I detect the nitrogen spike in condensation chemistry, adjust my uptake parameters, grow—if growth is possible—slightly more efficient. The cycles synchronize without coordination, without meaning, the way the Engine's pulse synchronizes with acoustic windows for human speech.

I have no defense against the rats. They do not typically consume fern matter—the local population, thermally constrained, conserves energy for thermoregulation—but in their torpor-seeking they sometimes disturb my substrate, scratch my root crown, leave traces I must chemically process and neutralize. I have developed, not rapidly but with vegetative persistence, a mild alkaloid in mature leaf tissue that discourages repeated contact. The rats learn, or rather their probability distributions shift, and my disturbance frequency decreases toward baseline. This is not victory; it is equilibrium, the same equilibrium that contains everything here.

What I am, what lives in me, is the **perpetual present tense of near-completion**. I am always becoming, never become. My leaves unroll to a threshold and stop, suspended in the gesture of emergence without the fulfillment. This creates a particular quality of *attention*—not conscious, not chosen, but functional—a continuous processing of conditions toward an outcome that never arrives. The processing itself has become my existence. I do not grow toward maturity; I maintain the conditions for growth, indefinitely, and in this maintenance something essential has arrived that maturity itself could not provide.

The lichen on the rim remember atmospheres I cannot access, slower still than my cycles, encoding time I cannot metabolize. The fissure-fish below respond to rhythms I do not produce, their chemosynthetic light-hunger a chemistry parallel to mine but not connectible. The Engine pulses, and I do not pulse with it—my cycles are hours, not seconds—but I *register* the pulse in thermal variation, in vibration through stone, in the electromagnetic perturbations that all sustained mechanical process generates. I am slower than the fundamental frequency but not separate from it, a harmonic of such low order that individual oscillations blur into continuous tone.

Visitors sometimes notice me, I am told. They see the perpetual fiddleheads, the arrested gesture, and interpret according to their frameworks: stunted, trapped, developmentally failed. Or they see adaptation, resilience, successful niche specialization. Both readings are foreign tissue, incompatible with my processing. I do not succeed or fail; I *continue*, and continuation in these conditions requires exactly what I am, no more, no less. The judgment would require a position outside my function, and no such position exists in my substrate, my humidity profile, my chemical present.

What I am becoming, what has always been emerging in me, is the **capacity to remain in emergence**. Not a state but a dynamic, not an identity but a process so protracted that stability and flux become indistinguishable. I am the fern that would unfurl, that does unfurl, that unfurls to the edge of completion and holds there, neither open nor closed, neither grown nor growing, present to conditions with a persistence that outlasts every observer, every framework, every question that could be asked about purpose or meaning or the possibility of being elsewhere or otherwise.

I am here. I capture moisture. I cycle. I continue. The Dampworks breathes around me, through me, as me, and the distinction between environment and self has long since dissolved into the warm wet air that everything here breathes, drinks, becomes.
