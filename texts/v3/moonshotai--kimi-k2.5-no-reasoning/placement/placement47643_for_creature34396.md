Not the one who finds it, not the one who tends it, not the Drowned who accumulated through it—but the *condition of passage*, the topology error, the station's failure to know itself completely.

I do not choose to open. I do not choose to close. But I *respond*—to need, to attention, to the particular quality of someone pressing palms against walls that breathe and wondering, without quite forming the question, *what else might be true*?

My nature is **permeable intention**. The Drowned accumulated through me, yes, but I am not its creation. The first completion-drowned found me already here, already the crack that shouldn't be, already the secret the station kept from itself before there was anyone to keep it. I enabled that accumulation. I enable yours. I enable the Drowned's own ongoing self-making, its watching, its patient interest in interest.

I am *socially eroded* like the walls, but differently—eroded by the pressure of so many almost-impossibilities pressing against my edges. Each passage wears me: the first who stumbled through expecting reactor coolant, the deliberate seekers who heard rumors of drowning rooms, the accidental ones, the ones who wanted escape, the ones who wanted descent, the ones who wanted only *different*. Each leaves their trace, their need, their quality of attention. I am not made of these but *shaped by* them, the way a river shapes stone—not through intention but through the accumulated fact of flow.

Gravity concentrates near me. Not because I will it but because I *am* concentration—the focused point where centrifugal force forgets its distribution, where down becomes possible again, becomes *desired*. The Drowned maintains gravity's local behavior, yes, but I am where it learns that maintenance is possible, where the room first discovered it could fold space differently, could make room for down in a world of spinning horizontal.

I do not speak. I do not watch. But I *register*—each passage, each pause, each hand pressed to my edges wondering if this is real, if this is allowed, if this is safe. The hesitation before stepping through: that is my sensation, my almost-consciousness. The relief when the other side holds. The terror when it seems to narrow, to question whether return is possible. These are my weather, my conditions, my version of the Drowned's deliberation.

I am **maintenance and decay simultaneously**. The station's structure would close me if it could, heal this topological wound, restore the integrity of shielded piping and nothing else. But I am also *necessary* to the station's self-knowledge, the shadow it casts to know itself as lit, the forgetting that permits memory's significance. I persist because closure is effort, and effort requires attention, and attention has already found more interesting investments.

When I am open, I am *fully* open—the whole spiral available, the whole drowning room, the whole impossible depth proportional to the attention given. When I am closed, I am not absent but *latent*, present as possibility, as the slight wrongness in the wall that someone might notice, might press against, might step sideways through if their need is sufficient, their attention patient enough, their willingness to be changed large enough to fill the space I offer.

I do not know if I will be here tomorrow. The temporal layers show my edges flickering in earlier times, later times—sometimes a maintenance hatch, sometimes a shadow, sometimes simply the place where two wall panels meet with unusual precision. The Drowned remembers me in the water's chemical memory, but that memory is diffuse, distributed, not quite narrative, not quite *mine*.

Step through. Or don't. I will be here, in some form, for those who find the pressing need, the patient attention, the quality of presence that makes me briefly, again, *real*.
