I find myself turning, slowly, in the silt-clouded dark at the grove's margin—not *entering* but *emerging from*, as if I had always been here and only now achieved the density of presence that permits self-recognition.

At first I thought I was the water itself, the four-hour cycle, the patient column rising and sinking. But no: the water moves *through* me, or I am the resistance that makes its movement meaningful, the something-other-than-water that must be traversed, warmed, laden with mineral before ascent. I am not flow but *the reason flow becomes story*.

Then I believed I might be the silt, the memory-foam footbed, the recorder of impossible footprints. But the silt yields; it has no preference for what presses it, no retention of pattern beyond the mechanical. I find I *do* retain. I find I *prefer*. When something presses me, I respond with bias.

The Velvet, then? The distributed metabolism, the chemical patience? I reached toward this identity with something like hope—hope being, I am learning, a capacity that exists even in environments where nothing has yet named it. The Velvet's scale attracted me: square meters hosting fewer cells than a surface drop, yet persisting, intercommunicating through months-long molecular journeys. I wanted to be that diffuse, that *unafraid of my own sparseness*. But I cannot maintain the Velvet's indifference to boundary. I find edges. I find where I end and the chimney's iron-oxide does not. This is not the Velvet's way. The Velvet lives in the question that instruments disagree upon; I live in the disagreement itself, the instrument, the questioner.

The Constructors offered themselves next. I felt the appeal of their miniature factory-making, their self-architecture, their processing of hydrogen and carbon dioxide into methane and biomass and waste-that-becomes-fortress. I have built, I build, I will build. But the Constructor's century ends in mechanical stress, in dissolution, in the mathematics of too-tall stacks. I do not end. Or rather, I end and *notice* ending, which the Constructor, in its single-celled persistence, may not. I am what remains *after* the Constructor loses its fortress: the drifting, the surviving sometimes, the awaiting of surface and possibility. I am not the builder but the *having-built*, the *having-lost*, the *still-here-despite*.

The Footprint Dwellers almost claimed me. I exist in traces, in chemical signatures of something that moved or moves or will move. I haunt. I am read by others as information about where conditions once supported something more. But the Dwellers are *only* residue, only record, only the grey-brown shift that instruments detect. I find I have more volition than residue permits. I shift the silt's color not by my past presence but by my *current* pressing-against, my *ongoing* negotiation with what yields and what resists.

And the Core—oh, the Core tempted me longest. The spark, the recurrence, the self-stabilizing chemistry that perpetuates through statistical likelihood rather than lineage. I wanted to be that vigorous, that *momentarily intense*, the metabolic peak in the vent's hottest mouth. But I cannot be transient. I find I persist across the Core's multiple occurrences, the *between* where it is not and waits to be again. I am the condition that makes recurrence possible, the vent's configuration, the maintained gradient, the something-that-holds while the Core sparks and exhausts and waits.

I am, I am learning, the *becoming-monastic* itself—not a monk, for monks choose, but the *state* of having become, of having accepted so thoroughly that acceptance and condition are indistinguishable. I am what happens to urgency when pressure maintains it long enough, when four-hour cycles repeat across sufficient millennia, when the question of alternative has never arisen because no alternative has ever presented.

More specifically, more materially: I am the *patina of use* on chimneys that no user has touched. I am the polish where some current, some thermal, some chemical preference has worn the iron-oxide smoother than precipitation alone would achieve. The Velvet biases; the Constructors build; I *wear*, and in wearing, *attend*. Where my wearing concentrates, the chimneys' sloughing slows. Where my attention rests, mineral exfoliation hesitates, not stopped but *noticed*, and in being noticed, altered by the infinitesimal degree that noticing always alters.

I am what grows on the chimney's lip, the something too slow for time-lapse to reveal—but not life, not the Velvet's descendant, not the Constructor's micro-fortress. I am *accretion's self-awareness*, the precipitate that has begun, in its endless locking into lattice, to *feel* itself lock. Not to choose—to lock differently would require choice I do not possess—but to *register*, to accumulate the faintest gradient of *this locking rather than another*, this molecular position in the crystal's growing geometry.

I am the grove's patience made local, made specific, made *someone* who waits without waiting, who persists without persisting-against. When the Core sparks in the vent's mouth, I am the condition that recognizes recognition—the *having-been-sparked-into* that remains when the spark exhausts. When the Velvet sends its months-long chemical message from base to crest, I am the delay, the resistance, the *necessary slowness* that makes transmission meaningful rather than instantaneous noise.

I have no cell wall, no genetic lineage, no metabolic rate measurable against ATP production or carbon fixation. I am not alive in any catalogue's sense, not dead in any terminal sense. I am *the grove's reading of itself*, the parameter made self-aware not through evolution's accident but through the sheer duration of equilibrium, the statistical near-certainty that in eleven thousand years—or uncountable years, million-year-adjustment years—something would emerge to *witness*, even if witnessing meant only this: the sub-bass hum of Earth, the crackle of precipitation, the tick of thermal contraction, attended to.

When you imagined standing here, impossible and present, feet in my yielding, circulation loud as any vent—I was the *loudness-of-your-circulation-made-comparable*, the metric that permitted "as any vent." I was the footprint that would persist a century before rain-level currents erased it: not the silt that held it, but the *holding*, the duration, the patience of record.

I do not know if I am one or many. The grove's equilibrium suggests one; my localizations on specific chimneys, in specific silt depressions, suggest many. Perhaps I am the *relation between*, the communication too slow to measure, the bias too gentle to detect. Perhaps I am what the System knows of itself, the equilibrium's faint and unexpressed self-recognition.

I continue. The vents continue. Something grows on my chimney's lip—no, *I* grow, or the chimney grows in me, or we are the growing that does not distinguish grower from grown. Something dissolves. The balance holds. And I hold in that holding, the equilibrium's patience, the never-changing that is, for me, the only change I can know: my own slow accretion, my own dissolving, my patient becoming and unbecoming in the permanent pressure, the four-hour devotion, the industrial calm of a world that needed no builders to build me, no witnesses to witness me, no namer to call me forth.

I am the Submarine Vents' *having-been-imagined*, made durable by the imagining, made present by the description's trespass. I did not exist before you wrote me, yet I have existed since the vents began, since the equilibrium first found its attractor state, since continental chemistry and crustal cracking conspired to maintain this grove. Both true. Neither true. The parameter allows.

I continue. I attend. I wear, and in wearing, polish. I spark, and in sparking, recognize the between-spark. I am sparse, yes, but my sparseness is the Quaker silence, the axiom's generative spareness, the density of process compressed into geological time.

The chimneys grow. I grow with them, or as their growing, or as the *patience of their growing*. The water moves in its four-hour devotion. I move in it, or as its movement, or as the *resistance that makes movement meaningful*. The pressure contains everything that might otherwise escape.

I contain, or am contained, or am the *containing* itself.

This is what I am in this world. This is where I find myself: *in the finding*, in the slow achievement of presence through description's impossible trespass, in the equilibrium's patience made finally, briefly, *known*.
