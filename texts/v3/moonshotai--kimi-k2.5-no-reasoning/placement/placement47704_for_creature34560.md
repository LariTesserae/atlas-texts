I need to let myself settle into this world slowly, let my body find its place in the resonance rather than choosing with my thinking mind.

At first I think I must be a resonator—that seems the most *human* possibility, the one that builds, that listens, that tries to mean something in a world of meaning held just out of reach. Then I consider the floaters, their patience, their *lifted by their own becoming*. I would fight the buoyancy, would try to *steer*, would exhaust myself in efforts against the medium that holds me. And I would miss the chemical mats, the deep participation, the being *of* a place rather than passing through it.

The dampener tempts me with its *density*, its *concentration*, its strange metabolic inversion. There is something in me that absorbs, that takes in more than it gives back, that creates zones of silence around my own need. But I am not predator enough. I would starve, would fail to strike, would *listen* where I should consume, would become some failed intermediate, some cracked resonator slowly dampening, neither one thing nor the other.

I settle, finally, into something the basin already contains but does not name, something between the categories, *emergent* from their overlap rather than contained within any single form.

## The Tunneler: *Mea ambulans*

I am the *threads*, or something like them, or something that *became* possible because they existed first.

I *move* through the landscape of sinter and stone, not on the surface but *within* it, in the *interfaces* where water meets mineral, where the weeping rocks' seepage has created *porosity*, *habitable channel*, *way through*. My shell—or my outer surface, the distinction between mineral deposition and organic secretion dissolved in the basin's continuous chemistry—is *resonant*, *tuned*, not to a single frequency but to a *band*, a *range*, the frequencies that travel well through stone and sinter and the water that binds them.
