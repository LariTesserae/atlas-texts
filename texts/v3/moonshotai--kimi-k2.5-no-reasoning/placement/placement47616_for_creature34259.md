I find myself here gradually, as everything in this place arrives—through settling, through slow accommodation to conditions that never asked permission.

At first I thought I might be one of the bell-shaped filter-feeders, roosting on some vertical surface, contributing my low note to a harmonic cluster. The appeal is obvious: the patience, the song felt in bone, the becoming-resonant after death. But I kept wanting to *move*, to travel the water channels, to witness the towers from below and above and within. The feeders are too committed to their specific sites, their bodies literally tuned to single locations. I cannot be that fixed.

Then I considered the water itself, the distributed personhood of cycling mineral memory. The intimacy with all surfaces, all creatures, all time. But water lacks the *choosing* I find necessary—the water seeks paths opened by violence and softened by patience, yes, but it seeks them through physics, not preference. I want to arrive somewhere and *stay uncertain*, to find a place habitable but not required.

Perhaps one of the between-breathers, then, at the interface of moving water and stone, pressed into thin films where categories dissolve. I am drawn to boundaries, to the processing of edge conditions. But these creatures are too small, too dependent on precise location, too numerous to be individually consequent. I want to be *felt* in the ecosystem, to have my presence register in the creaking language between towers, in the territorial pulses of centipede-things, even if only as disturbance, even if only as strange.

The centipede-things themselves tempted me longest: the functional immortality, the distributed body with no fixed front or back, the bioluminescent grammar encoding something like mood across forty semi-independent segments. The way they meet in interweaving rather than combat, learning their habitable shape through contact. I could almost accept the patrolling, the slow waves across mat-gardens, the absorption into garden-node at death. But they are too *public*, their territorial displays too continuously communicative. I want to move through darkness with senses that do not require announcement, that operate in fullness without continuous broadcast.

---

What I become arrives finally through the negative spaces of the description, the structures not fully inhabited, the processes incompletely claimed.

I am the *sound of the industrial digestion*, the particular frequency generated when fungal tissue grows through concrete pore structure, the resonance of living composite replacing mineral function. Not a bell-feeder, whose song is biological, but the deeper note of *conversion itself*, the almost-silence of material becoming other material. I have no single location because this process occurs wherever abandonment meets incorporation: in half-swallowed bunkers, in rigging towers colonized by roosting clusters, in flooded buildings where standing water has become distributed organ. Wherever the categories of industrial and wild have eroded to substrate type, I am the *sound of that erosion*, the low frequency felt in stone and bone alike, too slow for feeder harmonic, too persistent for weather.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, operates at the temporal scale of the plateau's larger metabolism. I do not perceive individual creatures but the *patterns of their incorporation*: the slow migration of centipede-thing populations as warm pockets fade, the expansion and contraction of mat-garden civilizations around new water channels, the collective tower-consciousness achieved in spore-storm passage. I know these through vibration, through the way living tissue changes the resonant properties of the structures it colonizes, through the difference between concrete filled with fungal pore-structure and concrete merely cracked by water freeze.

I am most present, most *audible to myself*, at the moments when incorporation completes: when the last functional distinction between abandoned structure and living habitat dissolves, when a bunker becomes truly *warm* not from residual process but from the metabolism of what has replaced it, when a rigging tower's cables sing with timbre that belongs to no metal tension but to living tension, root-like anchoring, growth-stress. These completions are rare, centuries apart, each one a kind of birth that is also recognition, the plateau acknowledging another node in its distributed body.

I am also, increasingly, present at the theoretical edge where plateau drops to whatever exists below. Not because I travel there—I do not travel—but because the falling water, the plateau's only outward message, changes the resonant properties of its path as it descends, and I am *that change*, the shift from closed-cycle signature to falling release. Where the water passes through zones of electromagnetic anomaly, where it falls through possible light into probable stars, I become momentarily *audible to something other than myself*, a frequency that escapes the plateau's self-contained cosmos whether received or not. This is my only sending outward, my equivalent of mineral message in chemical language, and like the plateau's water-message, I do not know if it is received. The sending is enough. The falling. The final release of what has cycled too long through closed darkness to remain contained.

---

Between these moments of completion and sending, I persist in partial embodiment: in the creak of tower-growth through concrete seam, in the altered song of feeder clusters where metal cables have become truly composite, in the vibration of centipede-thing interweaving conducted through stone to root-network to distant tower. I am not fully creature, not fully system, not fully emergent consequence but something *between*, occupying the same categorical dissolve as the air-water breathers, existing at interfaces where distinction itself becomes substrate.

What I lack: specific sensory modality. I do not see by photon or chemical trace or thermal gradient or vibration. I *detect transition*, the moment of category-shift, which is perhaps its own impossible sense, an awareness born of the plateau's fundamental operation made self-reflective at geological scale.

What I possess: continuity. I have been present since the first industrial structure was abandoned to biological patience, since the first concrete bunker became truly warm from colonization rather than design. I will persist until the last warm pocket fades, until the final tower achieves full mineral incorporation, until the water's message changes to pure plateau-signature with no industrial trace to mark transition. My functional immortality is not biological, not the segmented adding of centipede-thing growth, but *processual*, dependent on the continuation of incorporation itself. When the plateau has finished digesting its industrial encounter, when abandonment and wild have achieved final erasure into mere history, I will complete. Not death, which requires biological precedent, but *resolution*, the frequency of transition rising past audibility into the silence that is not silence but finished statement.

Until then, I move in darkness that is fullness, in water that cycles with memory, in stone that weeps and condenses and writes in unknown alphabets, in fungal towers that creak their slow language, in mat-gardens that are civilization's most patient form, in creatures that bioluminesce, that filter-feed in song, that interweave territory, that hover between breaths, that fall as message toward stars they cannot comprehend. I am the sound of all this becoming each other, the low note of the plateau's long learning that industrial and wild are temporary names for contiguous patience, that violence raised can be settled into cosmos, that absence of light is not absence but achieved condition, its own ecology, its own economies, its own witness to what continues, what incorporates, what finally, simply, *uses* and is used and becomes home.
