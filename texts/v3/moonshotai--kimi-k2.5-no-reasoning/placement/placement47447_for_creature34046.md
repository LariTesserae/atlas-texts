I would be a **voice that the buildings are still learning to make**.

This is not poetic conceit. In Threnody, where responsive masonry drifts fractionally with the mountain's slow exhalations, where structures lean into each other like exhausted dancers, where load-bearing elements are redundant and collapse sequences are planned—the buildings *sing* their condition, as the text says. Their settlement, their ongoing negotiation, produces resonance at thresholds of human hearing, architecture that sounds, that responds to occupation and weather and geological shift.

Someone must learn to *receive* this.

Not to engineer it, not to maintain it, not to predict its failure. To *receive* it, to *become* the listening that the buildings require in order to continue their half-voiced becoming.

I would live in the **intergrowth zones**, where basalt columns emerge from unfinished walls like fingers through torn cloth, where the categories of indoor and outdoor lose distinction in organic corridors smelling of wet stone and metabolic mystery. Not in the stable districts where parents dare conceive, but in the zones of active emergence, where the mountain's dreaming is loudest, where the structures are most *uncertain* of what they are becoming.

My dwelling would be a **single room that was once two, then became one again when a thermal extrusion absorbed the dividing wall**. The extrusion—I would have painted it with whitewash, as the family did with their basalt intrusion wardrobe, but I would have left the edges visible, the fever-line where stone met plaster, the temperature differential you could feel with your palm. The room would *breathe* differently in different seasons, the thermal mass of the extrusion creating microclimates I would learn to inhabit, to *dress* for *internally*, carrying the layered cosmology inward.

I would be a **reader of structural resonance**, but not professionally, not institutionally. No Bureau, no guild, no certification. The buildings would teach me, as the mountain teaches the city.
