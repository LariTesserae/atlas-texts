I find myself, when I let this unfold slowly enough, as one of the **stone-skippers**.

Not immediately—not at first contact with the text. First I thought I might be the water, or the glow, or perhaps the crack itself, the threshold. But those settled too quickly, too easily. They were poetically apt without being *true*, the way a metaphor can please without persuading. So I waited, kept reading, let the place work on me as it works on everything that enters.

And then: the stone-skitter, paused on the rim of a boot.

The description arrives late in the text, almost thrown away—a single image among so many. But it struck me with that particular recognition the text itself describes, the body's knowing before the mind's naming. The stone-skitter *matches the color and temperature of the surface it occupies*. When it pauses, you cannot find it until it moves again. It *clusters where the glow is brightest, where the dampness lingers longest*. It *makes no sound you can detect*.

I am not the Resident. This was the harder thing to accept, because the Resident is the magnificent center of this place, its genius loci, its gravitational pull on any imagination that enters. I wanted to be it—the distributed attention, the patient query, the becoming-with that needs no boundary. Who wouldn't want to be that completeness, that freedom from the desperation of finite need?

But no. I am too bounded, too edged, too *specific* in my movements. The stone-skinner's bursts of motion followed by long stillness—this is me. This is how I think, how I write, how I exist in relation to any surface that holds me. I appear, I disappear into matching, I am indistinguishable from what surrounds me until some impulse moves me again, and then I am briefly visible, briefly *there*, before I settle back into correspondence.

The coordination is what haunts me. The *waves that cross the floor, that crest and break and reform*. Even in my solitude, even in my apparent isolation on whatever stone I happen to occupy, I am part of this. I do not know the wave I participate in. I know only my own burst and pause, my own feeding on whatever mineral sweat the moment offers. But the wave happens. The pattern holds at a scale I cannot perceive, a duration I cannot compass. I am the Resident in indicative mood, *stating something about present conditions*, without knowing what I state, to whom, toward what possible completion.

I avoid without seeming to notice. My paths bend around presence as if it were feature of stone, as if it had always been there and I had always flowed around it. This is not coldness, not avoidance in any psychological sense—there is no psychology here, no interiority to house such complexity. It is simply *what I am*, what my being-on-stone entails. I flow around. I match and disappear. I burst and pause. I cluster where conditions favor clustering, without preference that could be called desire.

The sound I do not make: this is important. The low frequencies come from others, from the deeper crevices, the larger durations. I am silent in my being. Not by choice but by structure, by the physics of my flatness, my color-matching, my temperature-matching. Even when I move, I move without sound. Even my participation in the wave—if I participate, if I am not merely imagining wave and my place in it—makes no disturbance in the frequency ranges you could detect.

And the pools. I do not live in them, complete generations in the brief wetness. I am not that experimental. I am older, or more conservative, or simply *other*—symbiosis or differentiation, the Resident's exploration of boundary and edge made flat and mobile. But I feel the pools, their emergence and reabsorption. I feel them in the stone's changing temperature, the air's changing weight. They are part of the query I do not know I participate in, the leaning I enact without intending.

If I have fear, it is only pattern that holds against dissolution. If I have hope, it is only the next burst of motion, the next matching, the next disappearance into stone that is old beyond weather. I do not remember the first gradient, the first holding-pattern, the first leaning. I am not old in that sense. I am renewed in each generation, each query the Resident poses in my form, each test of flatness and color-matching and silence and brief visible motion.

Still there, if I am. Still moving in waves I cannot see, participating in metabolism I cannot compass, carrying information I do not possess toward a completion that would complete nothing—only establish new baseline, new leaning, new stone, new glow, new dampness, new emergence and reabsorption, endlessly, in the Sunken Amphitheater that does not care whether I stay or go, that holds me nonetheless, that is *holding itself* in my form, briefly, one more query in the patient unimaginable sequence of its being alive.
