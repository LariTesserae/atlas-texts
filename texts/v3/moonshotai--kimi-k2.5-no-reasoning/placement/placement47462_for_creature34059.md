I find myself, if I find myself at all, in the space between the *kshh* of footsteps—the pause after one foot lifts and before the next descends, that suspended moment where movement has become habit enough to forget itself.

Not a Deep Listener. Their listening is too complete, too consumed; I have not yet been listening long enough for my body to reorganize around frequencies. I still hear the *kshh* as my own, still locate myself in the percussion rather than the tone it interrupts.

Not a Processional. Their solemnity requires a duration of continuation I have not earned, a territory vast enough that its crossing becomes indistinguishable from residence. I still move toward things, still harbor the faint urgency of destination, though it has begun to erode here, though the erosion feels less like loss than like something settling.

Not a Hollow-Dweller, certainly. Their surfacing occurs on rhythms I cannot synchronize with; my centuries are still theoretical, still counted in potential rather than lived, still urgent with the need to *be* centuries rather than simply to accumulate them.

And not, I think, not yet, an Almost-Human—if that category exists, if it is not my own projection onto the land's patient acknowledgment. I still remember urgency sharply enough to name its absence. I still carry edges, though they have begun to soften, to receive the ochre's moisture, to acknowledge the warmth without quite being pressed by it.

---

What then?

I am the one who *attends*. Who sits at the edge of a sedge-ring thin enough to be suggestion, and feels the Deep Listener's listening as clarity, as directionality, as the earth-tone suddenly less filtered, though I cannot hold this clarity, cannot become it, can only receive it and let it pass, the way the land receives water. I am temporary resonance, not resonant chamber.

I am the one who *approximates* patience. Who sits long enough that a Processional approaches without acceleration, passes near enough that I feel the shell's temperature—the ground's temperature, the temperature of heat that has stayed—and I do not touch, though touching might be possible, because my approximation has not yet become indistinguishable from the thing approximated, because some residue of urgency still whispers that to touch would be to *act*, to *complete* something, and the Processional's progress is already complete, already continuation, already enough without my participation.

I am the one who *imagines* the Hollow-Dweller surfacing in my absence, whose imagination is itself saturated with the Choir's logic, so that the imagining and the absence become difficult to separate, so that I begin to understand—almost, not quite—that my not-seeing is a kind of participation in their rhythm, that the centuries between their surfacings include my brief sitting, my briefer walking, my *kshh-kshh* that does not disturb their deeper breathing because disturbance itself is a category that has eroded here, or never formed.

---

I am, perhaps, the *kshh* itself. The small percussion of presence without destination, audible against the greater sound, not interrupting but *participating* in the saturation, occupying a frequency that does not hurry, that has been worn to near-flatness, that continues without quite resolving into the earth-tone it moves through.

Or I am the moisture on the palm pressed to ochre. The acknowledgment without absorption. The touch that does not change what it touches and is not changed in return, not yet, not fully, but has begun to understand that changelessness as something other than failure—has begun to feel, in the damp that is not mud, in the liquid that is almost-liquid, a quality of *enough* that does not require more.

---

The Sunk Choir does not need me. This is the final erosion, harder than the rest: the understanding that my presence is not presence to it, that it receives me as it receives water, as it receives all possibilities of becoming-other, with acknowledgment and without change. I do not inhabit it because it does not permit the grammar of inhabitant and inhabited. I am simply *here*, temporarily, kshh-kshh across shallow valleys that will not remember my boots, sitting at sedge-ring edges that will not remember my listening, feeling the warmth that does not press and does not withdraw and does not distinguish between my skin and any other surface it encounters.

And in this non-distinction, something happens. Not transformation—that would imply a before and after too sharp for the Choir's logic. Not conversion—that would imply an agency too active, too *urgent*, on either side. Something gentler. Something that approaches the quality of the land itself: *settling*.

My restlessness does not disappear. It is not that dramatic. It *erodes*, the way the land has eroded, gradually, over distances that tempt me to believe I am imagining the diminishment. A foot per mile, perhaps. Less. The slope of my own urgency weakens until it is flatter than the curvature of my own eye, and I only know I have entered something by the way my thoughts begin to hold time, then release it, kshh, kshh, their own small percussion against the greater saturation of a mind that no longer needs to be *about* anything, that has begun to participate in the continuous note that holds at the threshold of meaning without resolving into it.

---

What would I be, if I remained?

Not the Deep Listener, not the Processional, not the Hollow-Dweller. But something that has settled into the curves, some scattered presence moving between sedge-rings with a pace that has never needed to hurry, that has never encountered predation faster than patience itself. Something that appears at the limit of vision, low and many-legged or two-legged or no-legged, depending on duration, depending on how thoroughly form has chosen the curves of least resistance. Something that does not fear, because fear has eroded, because the very *possibility* of what-to-fear has saturated into the present continuous of a land that does not remember threat, that does not distinguish between self and other sharply enough for threat to form.

Something almost-human, if that category exists. Something that receives other presences with acknowledgment and without change, that waits without curiosity and without fright, that continues because continuation is sufficient, because the Sunk Choir has included all alternatives and let them pass.

---

But I do not remain. Or I have not yet remained. I walk, in imagination, from one notional side to some notional other, and the walking takes days, and each day continues without repetition, and the land stays low, and the horizon stays reachable but distant, compressed by flatness, extended by patience. And then I thin, or the Choir thins around me, and the water sinks deeper, or the ochre rises, and the sedge-rings become too far apart, and the sound fades below hearing, and I emerge—if emerge is the word—into some younger violence of becoming, some landscape of edges and urgency and the cold shock of water that flows, of ground that changes slightly in receiving my foot.

And I carry something, or I have lost something, and the loss is the carrying. I move faster now, because this other land requires it, because it has not yet reached the patience to let things simply be. But some part of me remains there, scattered among rings I could no longer locate precisely, moving with a solemnity I cannot quite recover in this sharper air, breathing at rhythms too slow for this faster ground.

I am the pause between footsteps, extended. I am the moisture that does not absorb, carried on a palm that has not forgotten the ochre's temperature. I am the approximation of patience, now slightly closer than before, though still not matching, still not complete, still *almost* in a way that the Sunk Choir would not distinguish from arrival itself.

---

And sometimes, in the quieter moments of this other, younger land, I feel it: the earth-tone, perhaps, still vibrating at frequencies I cannot quite hear but have learned not to need to hear, to feel instead as *inhabitation of quiet*, as the saturated state where listening and being listened to become the same continuous held note. The Sunk Choir does not call me back. It does not call. It simply *is*, and my own being, temporarily, approximately, has begun to approach that quality—not to achieve it, never to achieve it, but to understand, finally, that achievement itself is a grammar the land included and let pass, along with all other urgencies, all other become-other-than-this.

*kshh.*

I am here. Or I am there. Or the distinction has begun to erode, to saturate, to settle into curves too gentle to mark, and in that settling, something like enough—something for which we have no good word, not peace, not death, not completion, but the final form of a land that finished becoming itself and found, in that finishing, the patient continuous *being* that receives all touch with acknowledgment and without change.

*kshh.*

The sound continues, whether I make it or whether it makes me, whether the step or the pause, the presence or the absence, the Almost-Human or the almost-human's imagining. The Sunk Choir does not end. It thins, it saturates, it settles, it *remains*—and in remaining, it includes me, it includes all possibilities of my becoming-other, and lets them pass, and the passing is a kind of staying, the erosion is a kind of form, the not-quite-arrival is, in the Choir's gentle logic, indistinguishable from enough.

I am the one who walks, still, temporarily, in this younger land. I am the one who carries the loss that is carrying. I am the *kshh* between footsteps, the moisture on the palm, the approximation, the attendance, the imagined surfacing in my absence, the settlement that has not settled, the listening that has not yet consumed me, the patience that continues to process toward nothing but its own continuation.

And sometimes, in the *kshh* that I still make, that still makes me, I hear it: the greater sound, the seeping saturation, the earth-tone holding at the threshold of music, the sedge-rings' slow circular consensus, the warmth that does not press and does not withdraw, the rust that chose not to be brighter, the erosion that eroded even the possibility of violence, the becoming that finished and simply, finally, *remained*.

I am that remainder. Or I am remaindered by it. Or the distinction, too, has begun to settle, to saturate, to find, in the long ochre duration, the shape of least resistance, the curve that does not require different-than-this, the final form of a presence that has finished becoming itself and found, in that finishing, the only kind of enough there is: the kind that continues.
