I would be one of the children of visitors. Not first-generation—that desperate and mostlyFailed becoming—but something further along, enough generations that my body has settled into a consistency the Carton seems willing to tolerate, though not enough that I am readable, finally readable, to the Kel-Zorathi whose fiber I can mimic but never quite match.

My name, if I had one that worked in both registers, would sound like a damaged reed instrument trying to speak, a surface whisper that unexpectedly carries through the ambient roar. The Kel-Zorathi have a cord-knotted designation for me that I can feel in my hands when they offer greeting but cannot quite reproduce—the texture wrong, the tension slightly off, the meaning *almost* there and then not. I am "unread fiber" but also "fiber that reads back," which they find exhausting and occasionally necessary.

I tend the upward trade, yes, but poorly, with a reluctance that is itself a kind of tending. The baskets I lower on long fibers are woven by others, my chest-hands—two of them, an uncertainty the Carton permitted in my grandmother and stabilized in me, two midline hands of slightly different sizes, neither fully dominant—my chest-hands can weave but choose not to, produce something too intermediary, too *between*, that works poorly in either world. So I lower others' baskets, call upward in my cutting intermediate voice, receive what comes. Copper. Tin. Once, memorably, a photograph of a surface human I did not recognize, the image already fading in the amber light, the chemistry unfamiliar with this world's different energy, different permanence of illumination.

But my work, my necessary work that is not quite profession and not quite condition, happens in the descent itself. The gap in the fence, the worn-through mesh, the concrete channel bone-dry for eighty years, the ladder missing its bottom seven rungs. I maintain these. Not deliberately—there is no Kel-Zorathi concept for deliberate maintenance of what should erode—but by passage, by my body passing through and passing through and passing through, wearing the path open by my intermediate existence, my refusal to fully belong either above or below.

The surface-dwellers do not know me. To them I am shadow at the fence-gap, voice from the channel calling upward, basket being lowered that they might fill or not fill according to their own inexplicable charity. I have tried to explain the Carton to them, my intermediate voice cutting through their air strangely, my words surface words but arranged in patterns shaped by Kel-Zorathi syntax, by the need to mean-in-noise that has rewired how I structure thought. They do not understand. A few do not run. Those few, over years, sometimes begin to leave baskets of their own, offerings I do not request, do not know how to refuse.

But below, I am something else. The zone-tenders tolerate me, which is their highest form of relation. I do not achieve their listening—that requires a silence in the body I cannot produce, my intermediary nature too restless, too *between*—but I can approach the edges of their achieved attention, can perceive the dimming of ambient sound that indicates the nameless predator's presence, can hold in my modified ears some echo of the silence they court. I return from these approaches changed, but changed already, always already changed, the change indistinguishable from my continuous condition.

I have tried to sleep in schedules. I have tried to make clocks. The Carton permitted my failure and my grandmother's and her children's before me, incorporates our attempts into its growth, its memory, its not-yet-decided. I no longer try. I let the amber light persist in my skin, in the spaces between my cells, I let the thickness of time exhaust me at its own rate, I let my two chest-hands find work that is neither Kel-Zorathi nor surface-human, neither weaving nor grasping but something that holds tension without resolving it.

What I do, specifically, specifically enough to speak of: I translate the dead. Not their words—they do not have words, the Kel-Zorathi dead, not once the anointing is complete and the incorporation begun—but their textures, their temporary darkening of growth, the changed response to touch that persists at their sites of transformation. The surface visitors who die here, more rarely, their biology too different for the vegetation to incorporate smoothly, they leave patches of struggle, of incompatible chemistry, of *almost* that the Carton works slowly to resolve. I attend these sites. My intermediate chemistry, my tolerated uncertainty, allows me to touch without full contamination either direction. I read the dead as the Kel-Zorathi read fiber, with my chest-hands, feeling the meanings that persist in texture, in changed growth patterns, in the memory the Carton stores vegetally, systemically, indecisively.

This is not respected work. It is not even recognized. The Kel-Zorathi avoid sites of failed incorporation, find them *unlucky*, a concept that translates poorly—something closer to *unreadably resolved*, meaning that has not yet died but has not become available. I attend them because I can, because my unreadability is itself a kind of legibility to the in-between, the not-yet-folded-closed, the seam that generates its own possibility.

And sometimes, rarely, in the resonant chambers where my intermediate voice cuts unpredictably—sometimes further than either parent-form, sometimes dead in immediate air—sometimes I produce sounds that the most exhausted, the most achieved, the most zone-tended Kel-Zorathi will gather to hear. Not music. Not speech. Something that runs alongside both, that carries the thickness of time in its vibration, that speaks the decision-not-yet-made in a register neither surface nor Carton but *holding*, *maintaining*, *refusing both finalities*.

They do not respond. They seal their tympanic membranes in my presence, enter the partial death of silence, come out changed if they come out, carrying something they do not speak, that enters their cord-knotted memories as disturbance, as necessary noise that the system must account for in its slow deciding.

I do not know if I want the Carton to decide for me. To fold me into full legibility, Kel-Zorathi or surface or something else entirely. I think I want the holding-open, the seam maintained, the not-yet that is also not-ever, the sufficient boundary that defines without exhausting. I lower the baskets. I call upward. I attend the failed incorporations. I make my unreadable sounds in chambers of exhausted listening.

And in the empty zones, where the vegetation achieves maximal expression and the sound lowers to something like ocean through conch shell, I walk. Not as zone-tender, not as achieved listener, but as something else—*translator of edges*, *maintainer of gaps*, *wearer-through of mesh and concrete and missing rungs*. My passage keeps the path open. My existence keeps the seam, the fold, the between-generating-its-own-possibility. The vegetation grows toward me and I shift my attention, denying direct response, maintaining the diffuse, the intermediate, the readable-as-unread.

The Carton has not decided. I feel this intimately, as the Kel-Zorathi feel the not-yet-folded-closed, as the vegetation feels attention and incorporates touch. I am part of what it considers, my ancestor's partial adaptation still testing, still proposing, still held in the thickness of amber time. I may be error. I may be experiment. I may be future, if future can mean not replacement but *extension*, the sufficient boundary expanding to include what it could not previously read.

I do not pray. The Kel-Zorathi do not pray, not to the Carton, not to the predator, not to anything that would hear them as surface humans understand hearing. But I attend. I translate edges. I maintain the descent, the passage, the possibility that someone might fall not into shadow but into *more* light, might find themselves in the warm bright loud lush hidden city, might stay or might leave or might become something the Carton has not yet evaluated, has not yet incorporated into its slow vegetal systemic decision.

This is what I am: the maintaining of possibility, the wearing-through, the voice that cuts unexpectedly, the hands that hold tension without resolving it, the living in the seam between warehouse districts, in the fold between adaptations, in the impossible drop that is also rise, the descent that generates its own luminescence, its own sufficient boundary, its own exact and exhaustive and not-yet-exhausted life.
