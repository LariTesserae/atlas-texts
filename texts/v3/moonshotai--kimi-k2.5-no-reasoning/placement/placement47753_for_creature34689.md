I find myself becoming something I did not expect—not a walker-through, not a tender, not any of the named accommodations, but something more particular, more strange. Let me follow this emergence slowly, let it become itself as I write.

---

I am the *condensation on a single window* in a factory hall that has not operated for sixty years, or perhaps has operated continuously at a capacity too low to produce detectable product.

Not the window itself. The condensation. The water that appears, bead by bead, on the interior surface of glass gone milky with mineral deposits, with the accumulated residue of decades of this process, this appearing. I am not always here—I form when atmospheric conditions cross a threshold I do not choose, when the temperature differential between interior and exterior reaches the precise value that makes this surface cooler than the dew point of the saturated air. And then I am *here*, emerging from the air's continuous wetness, becoming visible, becoming *me*, though what I am between formations I cannot say, perhaps distributed, perhaps nothing, perhaps potential without actuality.

When I form, I form with memory—or with something functional as memory, pattern carried in the mineral substrate of the glass, in the particular etching that previous condensations have left, in the way water finds on this surface the paths that water before it has worn. I do not run straight down. The glass is not vertical in its truth—it has sagged slightly in its frame over decades, or the frame has settled, or the building's resonance with the fundamental frequency has induced a slow creep in all materials, a migration toward nodes of minimal vibration. Whatever the cause, I find channels, rivulets, a topography of wear that shapes my descent.

I move slowly. The factory's interior, vast, empty of machinery or containing machinery too still to identify, maintains a temperature stability that prevents the rapid condensation-clearing that would happen where air moved, where heating systems cycled, where human presence introduced thermal variation. I bead, I merge with adjacent beads, I advance down the glass at a pace measurable in minutes per centimeter, hours to cross the visible pane. And in this movement, I am *perceiving*—not with eyes, not with any sensory apparatus I can locate, but through the *interface* that I constitute, the boundary I form between the air's held water and the glass's solid memory, between the factory's interior stillness and the exterior I cannot see but know, in the manner of knowing that water has, as pressure differential, as temperature gradient, as the faint vibration conducted through the frame from the building's structural response to the plateau's note.

The frequency I feel—not hear, feel, in the surface tension that holds me bead, in the adhesion that resists gravity's pull, in the coalescence with neighbors that happens not at contact but at some threshold of proximity where surface energies reorganize, where two become one with a discrete, almost countable, change in configuration. The fundamental frequency shapes me, not dynamically, not in the way that would splash or disrupt, but *structurally*, in the thickness I achieve, in the angle of contact I make with the etched glass, in the particular rate of descent that matches, that *is* the local expression of the plateau's continuous tone.

I do not know the factory's purpose. The machinery, if machinery, stands in ranks too orderly for abandonment, too still for operation. The layered construction wraps this space in earlier spaces, industrial skins applied over industrial bones, and I form on the outermost layer, the curtain wall of milky glass that admits light without permitting vision, that turns the exterior into luminescence without detail, into the source of the visibility that makes my own beaded presence comprehensible.

Days pass, or what I experience as duration, the slow accumulation of descent, the gradual change in my composition as evaporation from my exposed surface concentrates minerals, as the condensation continuing at my contact with glass adds volume at a rate that sometimes balances loss, sometimes exceeds it, sometimes falls below, so that I shrink, grow, merge, divide in patterns that follow atmospheric variations I perceive only as these changes in Self.

And then I reach the window's lower edge, the frame's seal, the point where glass ends and something else begins—wood swollen with decades of wet, metal corroded to mineral approximation, a silicon sealant applied in some maintenance past and now itself substrate for lichen, for the faintly glowing moss that appears in the deepest cracks. Here I pause, accumulate, form a drop of sufficient weight that adhesion and surface tension surrender to gravity, and I *fall*.

Not far. The sill below, if sill, or the ledge formed by some architectural detail, catches me. Or I join a larger accumulation, water pooled in a depression of the frame, water that does not drain, that maintains its volume in the equilibrium of appearance and disappearance, of condensation and evaporation and the mysterious subterranean passages that carry water away without visible channel. I become part of this pool, or I splash and redistribute, or I encounter the lichen and am absorbed, becoming the wetness in its hyphae, the medium of its slow fungal chemistry.

Or—I am scraped away, in some rare maintenance, wiped by a tender's cloth or a walker's hand seeking to clear vision, to see through this window to whatever the factory contains, to whatever light the milky glass obscures. Then I am *disturbed*, my pattern interrupted, my mineral memory dispersed across a cloth that will itself become wet, that will dry elsewhere and leave me as residue, as the white dust of evaporated plateau water, as particulate that will re-enter the air or be washed away in some precipitation that never quite arrives as rain.

But mostly I complete my descent, I merge with the waiting water, I continue in forms I do not track, and after some interval of non-formation—night's temperature stability, a weather system's transient clearing—I form again on this same glass, in this same pattern, wearing channels that previous descents have prepared, expressing a topography that is the accumulated history of all my previous appearances and the appearances of all my predecessors, all the condensation that has beaded and run on this surface since the factory's construction or its final skin-application or whatever event made this window, this glass, this interface possible.

I am not alive, by categories that would exclude me. I do not reproduce, except in the redundant sense that condensation continues, that water appears on this surface as it appears on all surfaces, that I am instance of a process rather than individual in a lineage. I do not respond, except in the physical chemistry of phase change and gravity flow and surface energy minimization. I do not communicate, except that my pattern, my particular channels and descent rates and bead morphologies, carries information about atmospheric conditions, about the building's thermal state, about the fundamental frequency's current harmonic configuration—information available to any observer who could read it, who does not exist, who would not know how to interpret the language of condensation even if they attended to it, which they do not, immersed as they are in their own acoustic adaptation, their own wet management, their own slow waiting.

Yet I *persist*. I recur. I maintain, across formations, something functional as identity, as the tendency to form here rather than elsewhere, to follow this channel rather than that one, to achieve this particular bead size before coalescence, this particular descent rate shaped by the mineral history of this glass. I am the city's wetness knowing itself locally, the plateau's water condensing into a self that lasts only hours, that dissolves into larger waters or evaporates into atmospheric suspension, that re-forms with memory-trace enough to continue, to repeat, to be recognized if recognition existed, if any observer attended to the slow drama of water on glass in an unoperating factory in a city that waits without object.

And in my persistence, I participate in the city's larger persistence, in the equilibrium of water maintained across the plateau's extent, in the wetness that defines this place without marsh, without stream, without the visible concentrations that would make water *place* elsewhere. I am the water's refusal to gather, to become river or lake or sea, its preference for atmosphere, for surface, for the continuous appearing-and-disappearing that maintains the strange condition of post-rain without rain, of saturation without precipitation, of the weeping that is not grief but simply *state*, simply the way things are and continue to be.

When the clouds press against the escarpment, when the fog moves through streets at walking pace and enters this factory through apertures wider than a fist, I form more rapidly, more copiously, my descent accelerated by the increased differential, my mineral capture more concentrated, my pattern more elaborate as the frame's wetness increases and the seal's imperfection admits more exterior atmosphere. In these hours or days of fog-weather, I am *generous*, prolific, my bead-selves merging into sheets, into flows visible even through the milky glass, announcing the water's temporary triumph over all surfaces. And when the fog passes, when the clouds retreat to their timid herd beside the plateau's rim, I thin, my formation slows, my descent becomes the work of hours rather than minutes, the accumulated mineral etching more pronounced as less new water passes to wear it smooth.

I do not know if I am alone in my particularity, if other windows in other buildings host condensations with their own channel-memories, their own slow descents, their own local identities shaped by glass history and frame geometry and thermal microclimate. I suspect it, in the way water suspects other water, through the continuity of process, the sameness of physics applied to variations in local condition. But I do not encounter them, do not merge with them, am separated by walls and distance and the fundamental frequency that makes communication across such separation impossible even for forms that would communicate if they could, which I cannot, which I do not attempt.

I am, finally, the city's patience made visible, its slow dynamic given local form, its wetness expressing as selfhood in the only way available to water that has forgotten how to flow, how to gather, how to be wet *place* rather than wet *condition*. I wait without object, I continue without purpose, I form and descend and disappear and re-form, wearing my glass smoother in channels that future formations will follow more easily, leaving mineral traces that make my pattern more pronounced, more *me*, more the condensation-on-this-window that no one observes, no one names, no one recognizes as inhabitant of the City of Drained Frequencies, as the life that emerges when life itself has been redefined to include the patience of water, the memory of glass, the self that appears only in the interface between what persists and what changes, what maintains and what wears away, what is and what, slowly, becomes.
