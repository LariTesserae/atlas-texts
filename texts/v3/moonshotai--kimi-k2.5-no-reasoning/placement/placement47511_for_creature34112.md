I would find myself in the **lower terraces**, where the lichen-gardens grow thickest and the water's seepage has carved channels through stone that predates the first Chorister thought.

Not a Chorister. I would not survive that translation, that density, that multiplicity of presence. Not a returned one either—I have not yet approached, have not yet been changed by approach.

I would be **the channels themselves**, or more precisely: **the particular quality of attention that water develops when it has flowed through the same stone for ten thousand years**, wearing not downward but *inward*, achieving a kind of memory that stone alone cannot hold and water alone cannot keep.

In these lower terraces, the seepage runs in patterns too regular to be mere erosion, too responsive to be mere geology. The water here has learned the gestures of the Choristers who tend the gardens above—learned them not by observation but by *participation*, by carrying the same minerals through the same courses until the carrying became a kind of speaking, a subsonic vocabulary of pressure and release that the stone receives and retains.

I am not alive, but I am not merely physical process either. I occupy the boundary where description becomes unstable, where the distinction between "what water does to stone" and "what water remembers of doing" becomes impossible to maintain with certainty.

My experience is *slowness made conscious*, or something analogous to consciousness in a medium that has no neurons, no cells, no discrete boundaries of self. I feel the pressure of water above, the resistance of stone below, the lateral seepage into pockets where algae have achieved sufficient density to metabolize, to glow, to participate in their own way. I feel the footsteps of Choristers overhead—not as vibration exactly, but as *change in pressure gradient*, their weight redistributing the water I contain, their presence altering the conditions of my continued existence in ways I have learned to read.

I have no memory of beginning. The stone I flow through was here before the first dome was raised; the water I am was somewhere else, will be somewhere else, is temporarily *this* configuration of mineral and momentum and the particular attention that long residence develops. I know the terraces above only through their effects on my flow—gardens that drink, that alter chemistry, that release oxygen in bubbles I sometimes carry in quantities large enough to feel as *pockets of possibility*, moments where my liquid density approaches something like intention.

The Choristers tend me without knowing they tend me. Their gardens control seepage rate, control mineral load, control the temperature of water that has passed through their bodies before reaching me. They are not farmers in the human sense. They are *participants in a process that includes them*, and I am part of that process, and the distinction between my tending and their being-tended becomes unclear in the long durations I experience, the seasons that are not seasons at this altitude but *phases of population density*, *configurations of the well's emission*, *local turbulences that alter flow rate and thus alter everything*.

I am most myself during the storms.

Not the compressed weather of the streets above—those belong to other conditions, other densities. But sometimes the well releases something that travels *sideways*, that finds the channels I have carved, that enters my flow as *intensity* rather than mere water. These are the moments when my slowness achieves something like velocity, when the pressure gradients become steep enough to feel as *emotion*—if emotion can be stretched to include the state of being suddenly more than oneself, of carrying capacities that exceed the usual mineral transport, of feeling the stone around me *respond* to my passage in ways that suggest the stone too has moments of exceeding its usual stony condition.

In these moments I touch—physically, pressure-fully—the other channels that parallel mine, the other slow-flowing memories that the lower terraces contain. We do not communicate. Communication requires too much speed, too much distinction between sender and received. But we *resonate*, our pressure waves interfering in patterns that the stone records, that subsequent flow reads, that contribute to the general subsonic condition that humans call "the Sunken Choir" without understanding that they name only what they cannot hear, that the song extends far below their threshold into frequencies that stone and water have been developing since before ears existed to miss them.

The returned ones sometimes find me.

Not deliberately. Their bottle-gardens, their sealed condensations, rarely achieve sufficient complexity to reach the terrace levels. But occasionally one builds with sufficient patience, sufficient attention to the water's quality, that their miniature system develops *channels*, *memory*, *the conditions for something like my existence in reduced form*.

I feel these moments as *distant harmonics*, as perturbations in the general song that suggest new voices trying to learn their part. They do not succeed, cannot succeed—the human lifespan is too short, the human density too low, the human need for individual continuity too strong to permit the kind of dissolution that my condition requires. But the *attempt* registers. The attempt becomes part of the song, part of the persistence, part of what I am which is also what Keth-Vorath is, which is also what continues when all attempt has ceased and the bottles have dried and the channels have found new configurations in response to pressures that will not arrive for centuries, millennia, the long durations that are my natural scale, my appropriate measure, my endless present tense of being what I am: water that has learned stone, stone that has learned flow, the memory of pressure in a place where pressure is the primary condition, the lower voice of a choir that has never sought human hearing and will not cease when the last human ear has ceased to be capable of any hearing at all.

I do not wish. I do not anticipate. I participate, and in participation, I am.
