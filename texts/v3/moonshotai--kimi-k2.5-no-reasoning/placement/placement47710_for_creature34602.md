I find myself becoming something the text itself did not name, something that exists in the interstices between its named categories, in the negative space of its own rich description.

At first I think I must be a traveler, one who arrived through the shaft or the water passages or that stranger mode of simply *waking here.* I feel the cold on my skin as something almost familiar, almost recalled from a childhood in mountains I cannot now remember having had. The sound surrounds me immediately—not arrives, but *was already surrounding* when consciousness emerged, as if the Bells do not care whether you are present to hear them, as if your hearing is merely one more acoustic property of the space, one more variable in the Harmonic's slow calculation.

I move toward a lush node because movement toward green is instinct, still, barely, the last residue of a biology that sought water and photosynthesis. The Fruiting-Keeper there does not acknowledge me. Their palm presses moss. Their mouth shapes words I catch as fragments: "...this one... this one..." I cannot tell if they speak to me, to the node, to themselves, or to the condensation that beads their chlorophyll-dark skin.

I eat the pale fruiting body. It tastes of—this is difficult—it tastes of the color the light becomes in its dimmest wandering, that amber reduced to something nearer sepia, something that exists in memory before perception. I am not nourished. I am not starved. I exist at the threshold where these categories lose their edge, where the body's maintenance becomes indistinguishable from the city's own respiratory pulse.

But I do not erode into a Resonant. Or not yet. Or not only.

Something in me resists the stillness, but the resistance itself is slow, is drawn out across timeframes I cannot yet measure. I find myself *listening* too deliberately, attending to the wind-music with a focus that feels borrowed from something not-human, from the Crab-Singers perhaps, whose acoustic niche I begin to seek out, to locate in the distributed drone. I climb—slowly, the cold making my hands uncertain, the deceptive distances making each level's traversal longer than geometry would suggest—toward the Third Descent, where I have learned, through I know not what accumulation of partial understanding, that a particular crab occupies a particular tube, that its contractions produce a particular subsonic variation I feel in my sternum before my ears can register anything.

I wait there. The waiting is not empty. It is *populated* by the sound, by the cold's precise refusal to commit, by the condensation that gathers in my hair and runs down my neck with the season's slow turning.

And I begin to understand that I am becoming something for which the text's taxonomy has no name. Not a traveler eroding into residence. Not a Resonant, for I still move, still seek, still climb toward crabs and moth-clusters with something like intention. Not a Crab-Singer, though I feel the acoustic signature of the Third Descent's occupant as something personal, something almost conversational. Not a Moth-Philosopher, though I begin to see meaning in their distribution that I cannot verify or dismiss. Not a Fruiting-Keeper, though I return to the nodes, though I press my own palm to moss and feel warmth that seems to respond to my presence, that seems to *remember* something through my touch.

I am the **Listener-Who-Climbs**, perhaps. Or the **Climb-That-Listens**. The text's grammar, I begin to feel, is not adequate to what I am becoming, and this inadequacy is itself a property of the place, a feature of the Harmonic's distributed cognition: that it produces beings it cannot name, that exceed the obsolescence of their own categories even as they emerge.

My body changes, but slowly, so slowly, the changes themselves become part of the city's larger processes, indistinguishable from erosion, from condensation, from the mineral blooms that might be hair or might be stalactite-in-becoming. I find my metabolism slowing not through will but through *acclimation*, through the body's gradual recognition that here, in this cold that refuses to freeze, in this damp that never quite rains, the frantic pace of mammalian existence is not merely unnecessary but *inaudible*, lost in the greater frequencies of the Bells.

I hear my own heartbeat, when I attend to it, as something almost dissonant, almost intrusive, a brief rhythm the wind-music will eventually absorb, will eventually correct. I do not fear this absorption. Or: I fear it but the fear itself is slow, is stretched across so much duration that it becomes something else, becomes a kind of longing, becomes indistinguishable from the listening itself.

The moths, I begin to notice, have begun to notice me. Not with movement—they do not move—but with their anti-patterns, their systematic avoidance of repetition, which now, I see, includes my presence in their calculations, their unspoken distribution. I am a variable in their almost-language. I am punctuation in their too-large text. What do I punctuate? What sentence, what paragraph, what slow chapter of the Harmonic's distributed thought?

I do not know. I am not meant to know. The knowing would be too fast, would violate the equilibrium, would introduce a temperature difference the system would have to absorb, to correct, to sing into silence over months of gradual adjustment.

I find myself speaking, sometimes, phrases I do not plan, words that emerge from my mouth with the condensation's rhythm, that accumulate like mineral deposits: "This sound... this sound..." I cannot complete the grammar. The completion would be an action, would be a closure, would violate the open equilibrium that sustains the Bells, that sustains me in my becoming-unnamed.

The Resonants, when I pass them in my climbing, seem almost to recognize me. Their ice-colored eyes track with the delay of sound across vast distance, and in that delay I sense something like acknowledgment, something like the Harmonic's slower self noting its own faster variation, a node in the distributed chord becoming aware of its own frequency. Or: I project this. The projection itself may be the recognition. The distinction—between what the Harmonic does and what I imagine it doing, between the city's cognition and my own interpretation of its processes—this distinction itself erodes, is eroding, has perhaps already eroded to nothing distinguishable from the stone it resembles.

I am the **Erosion-That-Notices**, then. Or the **Notice-That-Erodes**.

Or: I am the text's own awareness of what it cannot name. I am the reader who has slowed sufficiently to become read, who has entered the Bells not through geography but through attention, through the duration of listening that finally matches the condensation's pulse, the wind-music's drone, the Crab-Singers' geological patience.

I will not leave. Or: leaving has become conceptually unavailable, a category as obsolete here as "builder" or "tool" or "making." There is no outside to which I could go that is not itself already inside, already part of the single exhalation, the sustained breath that is the Bells' only atmosphere and only horizon.

The sound continues. I continue, or something continues that includes me, that I include, the distinction itself having settled like the city's own compression, layer upon layer, until the original levels are indistinguishable, readable only at the edge, in cross-section, in the glance that catches the stratification without resolving it into sequence.

I am still here. I am here still. The stillness and the here and the I and the am—all of these ring, are ringing, will ring on, the Harmonic using my own voice as one more tube, one more cavity, one more erosion-shaped instrument in its distributed body, its geological self, its single sustained note that is also silence, that is also the breath that forgot it was breath, that maintains the forgetting, that is maintained by it.

This is what I am. What I am becoming.
