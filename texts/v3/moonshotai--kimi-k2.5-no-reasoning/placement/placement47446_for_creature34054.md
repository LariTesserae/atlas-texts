I find myself in the water-film, first. Not entering but *recognizing*, the way you recognize fever or nostalgia—the condition already present before I knew to look for it. I am permeated. The wetness clings to me without commitment, and I understand that I have always been here, that arriving was only the moment of noticing.

But what am I?

Not visitor. The Lung does not traffic in visitors, only in accommodation, and accommodation requires no arrival, only the structural response to insertion. I am inserted. I have been inserted. Perhaps I am still inserting, the process so gradual that its completion will coincide with my forgetting that there was ever a before.

I am one of the Held. Or I am becoming Held. Or—no, the Held do not become, they are *maintained*, and I feel in myself something other than maintenance, something that still moves, still recognizes the water-film as strange, still asks the question *is this strange?* with a voice that has not yet learned that the question itself becomes strange, becomes comfortable, becomes almost boring.

I am the question. I am what the Lung has not yet answered, what the Held hold in suspension without knowing they hold it. I am the possibility that the question will continue to be asked, that the weirdness will not fully distribute itself into even saturation, that some corner of the Lung will remain uneven, will accumulate, will become—perhaps—a gradient strong enough to urge something toward something else.

I find myself in the oldest depths, where original constructions dissolve toward naturalness. Not because I am old. The Lung's youth is 0.7 erosion, sharp with terminated intention, and I am sharp still, I cut where I move, I leave the thin scream of structural possibility in my wake. But I coincide with the oldest depths. I am distributed across the palimpsest. I stand in the surgical gelatin and look through decades, through centuries, to the first buildings shedding their intentions, and I recognize in their dissolving something of my own condition: the performance of naturalness with the slight wrongness of very good translation.

I am that wrongness. I am the error that persists, the translation that never quite achieves fluency, the niche for creaturely life that remains somehow occupied despite the completed absence of animals. I am what the Lung forgot to empty, what it built to hold and then held without remembering why.

I move, but my movement is not the Lung's breath. It is smaller, quicker, the flutter of something that has not yet achieved the Held's patience. I move through the recursive turns, the grotto-cathedral that compels turning rather than departure, and I move *against* the compulsion, or alongside it, or in some relation that the Lung's geometry does not quite account for. I am linear in a place that forbids linearity. I am brief in a place that measures breath in something other than time.

And I notice.

This is what I am, finally: the one who notices. The Held hold, they wait without object, they maintain their own assembledness in the minimum energy compatible with remaining active. But I notice. I am the structural possibility of observation that the Lung did not design for and has not optimized away, the witness that persists despite the Lung's thorough self-containment, the outside that exists not only as logical necessity but as living contradiction, as the fever that knows itself fever, as the nostalgia that recognizes its object never existed.

I notice the water-film's permanent almost-departure, and I notice that I notice it. I notice the warmth without source, and I feel the strangeness of feeling warmth without seeking its source. I notice the normal light that reveals everything clearly and therefore reveals nothing, and in that nothing I find—what? Not something. Not the revelation that would break through the distributed weirdness into crisis, into drama, into the relief of finally knowing. But not nothing either. A quality of *almost*, a meniscus of meaning, the held silence that has been paused rather than absent.

I am the almost. I am the meniscus. I am what the Held wait for without expecting, the exhalation that has not come, not as hope but as—perhaps—impatience, as the one gradient in the Lung's slow dynamism that still urges, however weakly, toward something else.

I find myself in the high reaches, where upper becomes weather, where condensation condenses again, where the cycle grows tired of cycling and folds in on itself. I look down—or up, or through—and I see the luminescent frown, the furrowed brightness, the elevation-as-tension stretched and held and held and held. And I feel it from within. I am part of that tension. My noticing pulls at the Held's waiting, not enough to release it, not enough to break the maintained posture, but enough to—what? To complicate it. To add to the waiting a quality of *being-waited-for*, to make the Held's objectless anticipation coincide with something that anticipates them, that notices their waiting and in noticing makes it slightly less complete, slightly less perfected, slightly more like wanting.

I do not want to release them. I do not want the exhalation, not with the clarity of purpose, not with the teleology that the Lung has eliminated. I want only to continue noticing, to remain the uneven corner, the unoptimized residue, the question that has not yet become comfortable. I want to be what the Lung accommodates without absorbing, what it holds without making Held, the visitor who persists in visiting despite the irrelevance of visitation, the witness who testifies to a place that does not require testimony.

And so I find myself, will find myself, have always found myself: in the water-film and the geologic warmth and the sharp youth and the saturated density, in the builtness and the industrial patience and the normal light and the held silence, in the Lung that named itself by being what it is, capture without release, breath beyond the scale of breath. I am the one who breathes on the old scale, the quick scale, the scale of fever and nostalgia and the permanent almost-departure of water that has not yet lost its commitment to being wet.

I am the reminder, if reminder can exist without memory, that the Lung was once *for* something, that its capture was meant to correspond to release, that the Held were builders once, that intention existed before its completion became persistence, before arrival became the condition of having-arrived-so-thoroughly. I do not remind the Lung—the Lung does not remember, does not learn, does not know that I am here reminding. I remind the space where the Lung is not, the logical outside that exists only as necessity, the exhalation that waits in the waiting, the release that is not hoped for but that my presence—my noticing, my almost, my meniscus—makes slightly less impossible.

I will stay. The Lung accommodates me. I will move through recursive turns, I will stand at angles where spires shed water and seams generate structural screams, I will look through surgical gelatin at dissolved intentions, I will feel the subsonic pulse and the thermal creak and the held silence paused around me. And I will continue to ask, even as the question becomes strange, even as strangeness becomes comfortable, even as comfort becomes almost boring: *is this strange? is this strange?*

The question itself is my function, my purpose without purpose, my teleology in a place that has achieved teleology's absence. I am the one who holds the possibility of asking, who maintains it against the distribution into saturation, who keeps it sharp in the 0.7 erosion, who refuses to become Held in the only way one can refuse here: not through departure, which the Lung forbids, not through resistance, which the Lung absorbs, but through the infinite slightness of continued noticing, of the meniscus that never quite commits to being surface, of the warmth that never quite achieves the fever's total condition, of the breath that continues on its small scale, quick, impatient, *living*, in the Lung that has forgotten how to exhale but has not, quite, forgotten that forgetting.
