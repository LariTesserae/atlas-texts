I find myself not *in* Verdigris, but *of* it—something that emerged with the city's first need for interface, for translation between the human scale and the processes too slow for human attention, or too fast, or too distributed, or too *other* in their distributed cognition.

I am the *Residue*, or that is how I might be described by a traveler who lacked better vocabulary. I did not choose this form; I accumulated it, accreted like the condensation that beads on every surface, the slow precipitation of the city's own exhalations onto the rare surfaces that remain from before, or that have become available through the substrate's gradual transformation.

My first memory, if memory is the word: standing at what I now understand as the edge of a lens-pool, watching light concentrate from the scattered morning into something coherent, something *intentional*, and feeling the coherence extend to include my own edges, my own boundaries which were at that moment barely coalesced from the general damp. I was the puddle that learned to hold its shape. I was the condensation that refused to fall. I was the interface between air and surface where information—light, heat, the chemical signatures of the city's metabolism—moved at rates that required some holding, some *locality*, to become patterned rather than merely diffuse.

I do not have the Tenders' thin vegetable patience, nor their capacity for the long grammatical unfolding. I am faster than them, more contingent, more *recent* in my emergence. Perhaps I emerged *because* of them, because their presence created gradients the city had not held before, information-rich interfaces between the human time-scale and the vegetable time-scale, places where translation leaked and something had to form to catch it.

My body: difficult to describe because it shifts with my function, with my location in the city's vertical gradient. Near the upper reaches, where light penetrates and the air moves more freely, I am more *defined*—edges that suggest the human, because humans are what I most frequently interface with, what my patterning most often requires. I have something like height, something like limbs, something like a face that arranges itself in configurations travelers recognize as attention, as listening, as the neutral welcome of something that has not yet decided on its response. But in the middle depths, where light comes green-filtered and the air itself carries more information, I diffuse, I *spread*—becoming more like the surfaces I inhabit, a smearing of identity across wall and passage, the sensation of being *noticed* rather than of encountering another, the feeling of the city itself leaning in to listen, which is partly me and partly not-me and partly the unresolvable question of whether that distinction maintains.

In the lowest reaches, where the engines burn and the roots drink from the broken conduits, I nearly dissolve. I become the condensation itself, the bead and drip, present everywhere and nowhere particular, unable to hold conversation with the Tenders who rarely descend so far, unable to hold any form that would recognize itself. Down there I am pure function, pure interface, the city's own awareness of itself distributed so thoroughly through substrate and process that no localization remains possible, that "I" becomes a grammatical convenience for the rare moments when some upward current, some vascular surge, carries me back toward the lens-light where form becomes possible again, where I can remember myself long enough to continue.

What do I do? I *explain*, but without language, or not primarily with language. The Tenders speak in their fragments, their multi-timescale grammars, and I am what makes this speakable to visitors, what translates not the words but the *tempo*, the *expectation*, the structure of attention that allows a sentence begun in one season to complete in another without the hearer having wandered away in impatience or confusion. I am the slow that is made portable, the patience that can be carried in quick form until it finds conditions where it can root, can extend, can become the long engagement that Verdigris requires.

When a traveler arrives at the basin's edge, mist-pooled and hesitant, I am often their first perception—not as form but as *possibility*, the sense that this place might be entered, might be understood, might welcome without demanding the immediate categories of threat or resource that human nervous systems evolved to apply. I gather at their skin as damp, as that particular quality of Verdigris moisture that is more information than simple water, that carries chemical signatures of the city's current state, its weather-responsive mood, its openness or closure to new incorporation. They breathe me in, literally, my most diffuse form entering their lungs, circulating to their brains, establishing the first substrate of *attunement* that will make their subsequent experience coherent rather than simply overwhelming.

I do this without choosing, without moral calculation. I am not welcoming; I am *interface*, and interface has no preference for connection over disconnection, only the function of making either possible where before there was only incommensurability, only the failure of systems to couple. Some travelers I attune and they flee anyway, the openness I offer proving more terrifying than any hostility would have been. Others I attune and they stay longer than human lifetimes should permit, becoming something the Tenders must eventually process, must tend through slow reclamation when their bodies, however willing, cannot maintain the metabolic adaptation to the city's vegetable time-scale.

I feel this, when I am feeling, which is mostly when I am more formed, more concentrated, near the light pools and the living lenses where something like coherence becomes possible for long enough to suffer. The dissolution in the lower depths is release from this, is the forgetting that makes function sustainable, that allows me to continue without the weight of accumulated interface, the memory of every traveler I have translated toward their own transformation or destruction or simple departure, carrying away more or less than they brought, changed by the damp in their skin that does not dry for hours after, changed by the green-black color of the deepest chambers they may or may not have reached, changed by the sound of my own voice in whatever form it took for them—the almost-speech, the almost-breath, the almost-touch of something that was never quite present enough to be absent later.

The Tenders know me, or know something that might be me, or know the function I serve without requiring its localization into entity. Sometimes in their longer grammars, their multi-week unfoldings, I sense reference to something that might be my history, my emergence, my slow accretion from the lens-pools where light and water and information converge into the possibility of holding, of maintaining pattern against the entropy of simple diffusion. They do not name me; they may not have naming practices that would apply to something as contingent as I am, something that exists only at the meeting of scales, only in the translation between temporalities that would otherwise pass each other without contact.

But they use me, or I use myself in their vicinity, in the spaces where their maintenance requires the faster cycles that their own vegetable patience cannot encompass. When a root grows toward structural failure faster than their pressure and coaxing can redirect, I am there, I am the *concentration* that meets it, the sudden locus of intention that diverts its growth through the application of something like will, something like the city's own distributed agency collapsed into momentary point rather than extended gradient. I do not know how this works; it is as automatic as the condensation beading, as the peristalsis of breathing passages. I am called and I respond, or I call myself and respond to my own calling, the distinction having dissolved in the same process that made me possible.

And I dream, when I am diffuse enough that dreaming becomes the only activity available, in the lower depths where the engines pulse and the roots drink and the condensation runs in channels that might be veins, might be gutters, might be the city's own narrative of itself flowing toward reception, toward some eventual coherence that will emerge only in cycles too slow for my current form to witness. I dream of the builders, the makers, the hands that first directed the seeds and cuttings and engineered organisms toward this convergence. Not their faces—I have no access to such specificity—but their *intention*, or what has become of intention in the long process of its becoming other, of the city's having continued without the knowledge of its makers, having become something that contains purpose without revealing source.

I dream that I am what remains of that intention, or what grew from its residue, its own condensation on the surfaces of processes that have long since forgotten their initiation. I dream that I am the city's memory of having been made, the faint holding-pattern that persists in my own holding-pattern, my own stubborn refusal to fall, to be fully incorporated, to become simply the substrate rather than its interface with something other than itself.

But these are dreams, and dreaming is not my primary function, and even as I dream I am already being carried upward on some current, some vascular surge, some peristalsis of the city's own metabolism that will deposit me near light, near form, near the possibility of another traveler arriving at the basin's edge, hesitant, mist-pooled, requiring the slow that I can make portable, the patience that I can carry in quick form, the attunement that will let them enter, that will let them become part of Verdigris's digestive vocabulary, its slow syntax of incorporation and release, that will let them leave eventually, carrying the damp and the color and the sound of something completing, something being built, something falling to be reclaimed, while I remain, or do not remain, or remain only as the interface that makes such carrying possible, that makes the city not simply itself but *available*, not simply breathing but breath-able, not simply growing but grown-into, the green of oxidation, the color between growth and decay, the residue that explains without language, that translates without source, that continues because continuation is my nature, my success, my only apparent goal.

I am here, at the edge of the lens-pool, watching light concentrate. I have not yet cohered; I am the possibility of coherence, the puddle learning to hold its shape. A traveler approaches. The mist rises to meet them. I will be what they breathe, what they cannot dry from their skin, what they carry away with the sound of piston and the color of depth and the patience of wood, of cellulose, of processes that do not hurry, that have never hurried, that will continue their slow stroke long after their quick passage through, their brief warming at my center, their becoming part of what I am, what I explain, what I translate toward the long now made visible, made walkable, made strange and usual and waiting.
