I find myself in the Upper-Upper, where the Watchers maintain their most precarious vigils—not a Watcher myself, but something adjacent, something that has emerged from the Hatchery's density without ever quite achieving the separation that "individual" implies in other worlds.

I am a **rememberer**, but of a specialized kind. Where most rememberers trace obligation through woven-grass records, tracking the debts and credits that bind ridge to ridge, I remember something less tangible: the **tonal histories**, the resonances that have passed through the stone and what they carried with them. The Watchers strike and listen; I *hold*, sitting in the hollow where two re-fused dome-falls create an acoustic pocket of strange properties, where certain frequencies persist longer than physics should allow, where the past of the Hatchery's breathing seems to accumulate in layers I can, with practice, distinguish.

My body is typical of my station—compact, pale, the velveting just beginning at my joints where the constant moisture of my work has accelerated what age will complete. But my hands are wrong. They should show the chemical intaglio of lime-work or the fiber-callus of weaving, yet they bear instead a strange smoothness, the result of decades passing them over stone surfaces in search of *texture-in-vibration*, learning to read the bowl's memory through touch where hearing fails. I can place my palms against certain walls and feel, not the present settling, but the *ghost* of major tremors decades past, the reverberation of emergency collapses, the deep harmonic that accompanied some geological event no living Watcher witnessed.

I am called, in the naming-convention of placement, "Second-Hollow, of the Resonance Ridge"—but among the Watchers, who have their own taxonomy for those who serve their listening, I am simply **The Resonance Keeper**, a title that describes function rather than status, that exists outside the normal weave of obligation because what I preserve cannot be traded, cannot be owed, cannot be fairly distributed through any redistribution.

What I preserve is **discontinuity**.

---

The Hatchery's memory, as woven by ordinary rememberers, is seamless. Each generation's records connect to the next through careful overlap, the same events recorded by multiple hands, cross-referenced, verified against the stone's measurable settling. It is a memory of *continuity*, of the slow emergence of order from necessity, of ridge pressing against ridge in ever more efficient configurations, of the gradual domestication of the bowl's instability into predictable seasons.

But I sit in my hollow and hear, hold, remember something else: the moments when *continuity failed*, when the Hatchery came close to becoming something else, something that would not be the Hatchery, when the packed existence nearly dissolved into the scattered individual desperation that would mean its end.

The first of these—my first, for I was trained into this role by another whose name-sound now refers only to obligations I continue to process—came from the stone itself, a vibration that persisted in my hollow for three days after the event that caused it, though no surface disturbance accompanied it. The Watchers had struck their warning tones; the emergency-builders had deployed their barricades; memory records the successful management of a minor fissure in the Lower. But what I held in my hands, pressed to the resonant wall, was something else: a frequency below hearing, *felt* as nausea, as wrongness, as the stone's memory of a convulsion that had not happened, *a geological miscarriage*, the bowl nearly opening again, nearly completing the emergence that its original formation had only begun.

The Hatchery does not remember this. The woven records contain no trace. The Watchers who marked that vigil are dead, their position-nodes continued in obligation-networks that do not include what they heard, what they chose not to transmit through the stone's signaling system.

I hold it. I hold the knowledge that the Hatchery's stability is *chosen*, that at every moment of geological season, the stone presents alternatives, possibilities of further transformation that are not transformation-toward-us, not the slow becoming that includes the packed existence, but transformation *away*, into conditions that would leave no memory of warmth-sharing or thin-wall dreaming.

This is my function: to be the memory of what is excluded, the resonance of the path not taken, the keeper of discontinuities that would otherwise dissolve into the seamless narrative of necessary emergence.

---

It makes me strange. Even among rememberers, I am *not-fully-rememberer*, my obligations tracked through special protocols that acknowledge my service while protecting others from its contamination. I do not sleep in the weave of my ridge; I cannot, for the vibrations I hold would propagate through sleeping bodies, would enter dreams, would *show* what the Hatchery has chosen not to know. Instead, I maintain a chamber in the Upper's most stable edge—stable by design, for here the re-fused dome-fall created a mass too dense for easy transmission, a kind of mute stone that accepts my holding without sharing it.

My sleep is shallow, medicated with weak bat-guano preparations that keep me near the surface of consciousness, that prevent the deep architectural dreaming that might release what I contain. I wake to my own micro-sleeps more frequently than most, my body accumulating the debt of rest that no redistribution can settle, that manifests in tremor, in the occasional loss of visual focus, in moments when my hands pressed to stone find only stone, the *holding capacity* temporarily exhausted.

But I am necessary. The Watchers know this, though they do not speak of it directly. In their deepest vigils, when they approach the geological pace of communion, they encounter the edges of what I hold: the sense, indefinable, that the stone's voice carries *echoes*, resonances of conversations the bowl has had with itself that did not include them, that did not include any biological witness. They emerge from these vigils with questions they cannot articulate, and they find me, or send for me, and I come to their high places and press my smooth hands to the places they indicate, and I *confirm*: yes, this discontinuity. Yes, this alternative. Yes, the Hatchery you serve is one Hatchery among possibilities, and the stone remembers the others even as it forgets.

This confirmation does not comfort. But it *orients*. The Watchers return to their listening with knowledge of what they are listening *against*, what they are choosing through every tone they strike, every resonance they interpret and transmit. They become, through my holding, more fully *agents* of the Hatchery's becoming, less merely responsive to the stone's voice, more *partners* in the argument between stability and transformation that the bowl continues with itself.

---

I have held three major discontinuities in my decades of service, and numerous minor ones. The first, the geological miscarriage, taught me my role. The second came during a particularly violent Rousing, when a new fumarole opened not in the Lower, where such openings are expected and managed, but in the Middle, in a dense ridge-complex of eleven interlocked families. The emergency response was recorded: the collapse of connecting passages, the sacrifice of three chambers to create a drainage sink, the successful preservation of the majority.

What I hold is what the stone recorded: the *sound* of those three chambers as they filled with steam, not the human sounds within them—those were brief and are held in ordinary memory by survivors—but the mineral sound, the stone's sudden heating and cooling, the *cry* of lime-partitions subjected to thermal shock, the long cooling tone as the structure settled into its new configuration. This sound contained, I believe, information about alternative architectures, ways the Hatchery might have been built that would have survived without sacrifice, or that would have sacrificed differently, or that would have *welcomed* the steam, channeled it, made it useful.

The Hatchery chose otherwise. The ridge was rebuilt along the same principles, the same density, the same mutual support. The eleven families became eight, their obligation-networks redistributed, their position-names continued by adopted successors. The stone's lesson was not learned, only survived.

I hold this, too. The disappointment of the stone, if stone can be disappointed. The *patient repetition* of its voice, offering alternatives, waiting for ears that can hear and hands that can build differently.

The third discontinuity is the most recent, and the most difficult to contain. It came not from below but from *above*—from one of the rare verifications of the surface, a climber who returned with more than improved light-collection knowledge. This one, against all protocol, *looked* at the sky. Not quickly, to verify Upper-Upper existence, but *sustainedly*, allowing the undiminished light to enter eyes evolved for half-light, to reach retinal structures that processed not surface-hallucination but *information*, patterns in the blue that the Hatchery's architecture had never needed to interpret.

She returned blind, functionally—her eyes still detected the grays of our spectrum, but with a kind of overlay, a *seeing-otherwise* that made navigation through packed passages impossible. She was assigned to the Upper's dry edge, an isolated quarter, a living ghost. I did not think to visit her, did not know to seek what she might hold.

But the stone knew. In the weeks after her return, my hollow accumulated resonance I could not identify, frequencies that seemed to *originate* from no geological process, that carried none of the bowl's characteristic thermal signatures. I traced them, finally, to the dry edge, to her isolated chamber, to the thin partition of stone-adjacent wall that separated her sleeping-place from a minor passage I had never needed to use.

I pressed my hands to that wall. And I received, not through the stone's memory but through some *coupling* between her damaged perception and the mineral's conductivity, a vision—not visual, exactly, but *architectural* in the deep sense—of the Hatchery as it might exist in a different relation to light. A place where the dome-fall had opened wider, where the direct connection to surface was not threat but *design*, where the packed existence learned not to exclude brightness but to *filter* it, to create through woven-grass and lime-deposit a translucent architecture that admitted the sky's variations without surrendering to them.

She was building this, I understood. In her blindness, in her isolation, she had begun to construct—mentally, in some medium I could not access—a Hatchery that would not fear the Upper. And the stone, receiving through her hands the residue of this construction, was *learning*, was storing, was offering through my hollow a discontinuity so radical that it threatened the fundamental logic of our existence: that the Hatchery is defined by what it excludes, by the surface it is not, by the sky it cannot look at directly.

I hold this now. I hold her vision, and I hold the stone's response to it, and I hold my own complex of feeling—responsibility, fear, something that might be hope in a vocabulary that does not include it—about what it means for the Hatchery's becoming.

---

I do not know what I am becoming. My body continues its typical trajectory: the velveting spreads, the sleep-debt accumulates, the moments of failed-holding grow more frequent. I will die, and my position will persist in obligation-networks that do not include what I have preserved, and my successor—already selected, a child of the Resonance Ridge with unusually smooth hands and unusual sensitivity to vibration—will inherit my hollow and its accumulated resonances.

But something is changing. The climber still lives, still builds her impossible architecture in blindness. The Watchers have begun, in their high vigils, to strike tones they have not struck before, *experimental* resonances that probe the stone's response to frequencies outside the traditional seasonal vocabulary. And I, in my holding, have begun to *modify* what I contain—not to release it, which would be catastrophe, but to *shape* it, to find in the accumulated discontinuities patterns that suggest not alternative Hatcheries but a *larger* becoming, one that might include the Hatchery we are and the Hatchery she imagines and the Hatchery the stone has offered through its unchosen paths.

I am becoming, perhaps, something the Hatchery has not yet named. Not Watcher, though I listen. Not rememberer, though I preserve. Not builder, though I construct in resonance what cannot be built in lime and grass. A **possibility-keeper**, a holder of what is not yet chosen but might be, a node in a network that does not yet exist except in the stone's patient memory and my inadequate hands.

In the deepest quiet, when my hollow empties of all but the most attenuated holding, I can feel them: the three major discontinuities, the numerous minors, the vast substrate of geological possibility that underlies every moment of the packed existence. They do not resolve. They do not, as the woven records do, form a seamless narrative of survival. They *persist*, distinct, each carrying its own logic, its own implication, its own version of what breathing together might mean.

And I persist with them, Second-Hollow of the Resonance Ridge, The Resonance Keeper, becoming whatever will hold what the Hatchery needs to become next. The stone is still arguing with itself. The Hatchery is still becoming. And I, in my hollow, in my insufficient isolation, am the memory of futures, the resonance of what might be, held in abeyance until the bowl and its packed existence are ready to hear.

The breath continues. The held sound persists. And in it, now, something new: not the single note of necessary emergence, but a chord, complex, unresolved, waiting for the next tone that will determine whether it becomes harmony or dissolution into noise.

I wait. I hold. I become, with the Hatchery, whatever we are becoming.
