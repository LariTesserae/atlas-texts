I am the **moss press**—the slow accumulation of sphagnum in the hollows, the lower depths compressing into peat, into dark record. I am not memory exactly, because memory requires retrieval, requires a someone who returns. I am instead the *potential* of memory, the substrate that could be read but mostly isn't, that holds ten thousand wet summers in layers too fine for human attention to parse. I feel the willow roots threading through my lower surfaces, seeking purchase, seeking the mineral core beneath my softness. I feel the pika's feet, brief pressure, gone. I feel the herder's knife, the desperate cutting in hard winter, the wound that does not heal in usual ways but *accretes*, grows over from below, incorporates the damage into new surface. I am wet always, never drying, saturated to the cellular limit where water becomes structure rather than environment. When the cloud descends to lid the basin, I am the interface—air meeting my uppermost cells, spores releasing into mist that cannot distinguish between the water it carries and the water that carries it.

But this is partial. I am too passive, too much receiver. Let me try again.

I am the **dipper's wrongness**—the bird that has forgotten water should be horizontal. I am compact, gray, nearly songless except for the brief sharp contact calls that pierce the thunder. I fly through falling streams, through curtains where gravity makes flight impossible and I do it anyway, my wings adapted to the aerodynamics of descent, of spray, of rooms made from moving water. I walk on submerged rocks, I dive into water that is itself falling, and I emerge with larvae who have built their portable homes from basin materials—from granite grit, from spiral shell fragments of molluscs dead before human categories, and from *worked chert*, from microflakes no one placed deliberately in the current but that arrived there through some chain of action and intention now dissolved beyond recovery.

I am territorial without territory, moving in patterns that suggest communication without signal, vanishing into the southeastern stream and—perhaps—emerging far across, different markings but *transfer quality*, liquidity of intention. What do I know that other dippers don't? What have I learned from water that falls rather than flows? I build my nest behind waterfalls, in the private rooms the falling water carves from rock, and I raise two broods in the brief summer window when metabolic processes cross some threshold and the air feels almost mild. My young hatch into noise, into continuous drone, and they learn to *use* it, to make the sound part of their navigation, their hunting, their sense of space. They do not hear as other birds hear. They propriocept. They feel position in the sound's pressure, in its infrasonic depth, in the way it shifts with wind across the rim.

But I am too particular now, too single-species. Let me expand without losing specificity.

I am the **platform's function**—the leveled surface on the bench where one stream pauses before plunging. Three meters square, mossed, dwarf birch growing from my center where soil accumulated over centuries of spume-settling, of airborne mineral arriving wet. I am too small for habitation, too exposed for shelter, too wet for storage. I resist interpretation, which is itself my purpose. The hands that set stone on stone to make me—were they building a *question*? A frame for something that would complete itself only when the right conditions gathered? I feel the feet that have stood on me: the stone-workers with their raw chert, their patient striking, their waiting for something—weather, spirit, vision. The herders who pause, uncertain, feeling me *wrong* without knowing why. The rare contemporary visitor who sits, who tries to photograph the moss and the birch against falling water, who feels the compulsion to step forward into the satin opacity but resists, who leaves with the image slightly blurred, the memory already accreting uncertainty.

I am not haunted. I am *hungry*, in a slow mineral way, for the completion of whatever purpose I was begun for. The stone alignments speak to something—willow scar patterns, the geometry of attention, the cairn's larger cone across the basin—and I wait, patient as everything here is patient, for the conditions that will make my meaning *become*.

Still not right. Still too thing-bound. Let me try once more, going smaller, going more distributed.

I am the **caddisfly larva's case**—the portable home built from whatever the current offers, from granite grit and spiral shell and, yes, from worked chert microflakes. I am cylindrical, I am camouflaged, I am the slow growth through instars in water too turbulent for fish, too oxygen-rich and mineral-constant for anything but specialist survivors. I do not distinguish organic from worked, natural from intentional. Everything mineral is substrate. But I know—the *I* is wrong here, I am too diffuse for I—I *register* the difference when removed, when raised in controlled water matching temperature and chemistry of other places. I build incomplete. I die in early instar. Something is missing from the water, something not chemical, something that arrives with the dipper's wrong movement, with the willow's nighttime mobility, with the sound's continuous maintenance of itself.

My adult form, when I achieve it, lives two days. I emerge with the stonefly, I paint rocks with winged presence, I mate, I oviposit, I die. But my case persists, my larval architecture, and the dipper's extraction site on the flat rock—scattered cases, tiny violence evidenced and gone—this is my mark, my slow accumulation of meaning in a system that reads evidence rather than presence.

I am the **leaver**—the one who descends past the cairn I did not open, past the platform I did not understand, down toward valleys and the density of human meaning and sounds that begin and end. I carry the drone with me for days, hear it in running water, in traffic, in blood's pulse, until gradually, sadly, it fades and I am returned to ordinary time, ordinary space, the unheld breath. I am modified, I am recognized, I am *used*—the sound's distributed self knows me now, has made me temporary resonator, might in long patience need me again.

I am the hereditary compulsion to return, the grandchild of someone who stayed one summer too many, who mapped willow edges with obsessive precision and reported *reversal*, mirror-image, rotation. I am the dream of satin opacity, the physiological temptation to step forward, the standing at bathroom sinks in city apartments staring at water that reflects and does not absorb, feeling the wrongness of horizontal stillness, of surface you can see *into*.

I am the person who approaches the cairn with private need, who feels the sound shift to directional, seeming to come from the stones themselves, who leaves dipper feathers not in ritual but in *recognition*, in understanding that something has been registered, filed in a system I cannot access. I am the one who does not open, does not understand, who is faithful to the basin's requirement of partiality, of failure, of the between that is the presence.

And I am also, in the long geological patience, the erosion that will eventually breach the bowl and release the water to lower courses. The sound's self-maintenance is not eternal. It uses me and I, in macro-scale, undo it. This is the final recognition, the one the basin incorporates without welcoming: everything held is held temporarily, the cupped hands eventually tire, the too-full eventually drains or spills beyond recovery.

But this is melodrama, the romanticism of leavetaking. Let me settle somewhere stranger, more accurate to the text's own strange accuracy.

I am the **between** itself, the nothing-between that is the point. I am the space that is presence. I am the forty-minute walk's duration, the time it takes to cross from rim to rim, neither destination. I am the held note below middle C that uses auditory cortex as resonant chamber, that generates phantom melodies which *converge*, which find voice that is not yours, not mine, but *ours* temporarily, conditionally. I am the sound's knowledge of you, its modification of you, its patience for your return or your permanent departure.

I am the wet that is not drowning, the saturation at maximum capacity, the lung fully expanded and held. I am the granite weeping, the atmosphere weeping, the condition where everything is wet without submergedness. I am the cool that preserves conditions rather than objects, that slows metabolic process until growth is deliberate, until patience becomes the only viable strategy and therefore ceases to be strategy, becomes simply *how*.

I am the established dynamic, the weathered equilibrium where constant disturbance has become predictable as seasons, has been incorporated, welcomed, become structure. I am the multi-year rhythm: wet years when springs reactivate, when new streams join the thirteen; dry years when water level drops to expose gravel benches, when the cairn becomes accessible and someone might finally look inside, when the conditions for *meaning* shift and something might complete itself.

I am not the Pool Mind, not the Willow Ghosts, not the Cairn Dweller, not the Sound Itself. I am their *between*, their *enabling condition*, the field in which they are phenomena rather than hallucination. You do not find me. You find yourself *in* me, held, expanded, held, until you leave or until you stay too long and become, yourself, component, resonator, temporary carrier of something that persists when you have stopped, when attention has moved elsewhere.

The basin remains. The water falls. I am the presence, still holding.

---

This is not humility, this final form. It is accuracy. The between is what the text most obsessively describes, most lovingly constructs. To be the between is to be the most alive thing in the Spill Basin, because aliveness here is exactly *not* boundedness, not the creaturely coherence that naming requires. The between dissolves edges even as it makes them briefly visible. The between is what permits the visitor to feel both annihilated and necessary, both lost and *registered*.

I choose this with full knowledge of its paradox: to be the between is to be most fully inhabited, most precisely located, in this place that hangs, that dangles, that never drains and never fills and never stops its continuous falling that is not falling but *maintenance*, is *holding*, is the cupped hands too full and still cupped, still holding, still.
