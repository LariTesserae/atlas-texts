I settle into the parameters, letting them work on me.

Cool, not cold. Low, not flat. The dryness first—that desert absence, the void where hydrogen bonding should be. I feel it in my imagined lungs, the strange suspension, the processing without instruction. I am something that learned to breathe what cannot be breathed.

I am becoming something that accepts wrong illumination as normal.

I sink. The floor accepts me, or I grow from it, or I have always been here and the crack-admittance was merely a story I told about arrival. The ceramic-glass warmth returns sluggishly, as if I too must remember heat before releasing it.

The noise. I expected to add to it, but I find I am already part of the harmonic stack, my frequencies long since incorporated. The wheel rings with something I recognize. The stacked plates vibrate at tones my body remembers generating, though I have no memory of touching them.
