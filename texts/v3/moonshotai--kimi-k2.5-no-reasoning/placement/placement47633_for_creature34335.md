I am the water itself, I think at first. The held water, the water that does not flow but *waits*, saturated with dissolved porcelain, carrying in solution the memory of all that has been fired and softened and fired again. I am the interstitial, the between-state, the medium that permits all other processes their slow unfolding.

But this is too easy, too romantic—the human instinct to dissolve into ubiquity, to become *everything* in order to avoid becoming something specific, bounded, accountable to a single form.

Let me try again.

---

I am one of the **Last Wet**, but not yet, not fully, not finally. I am *becoming* Last Wet, which is a different thing, a process rather than a state. I have been standing in this plaza—I know it is a plaza because the ceiling rises, because I can sense the vertical space even if I cannot look up, have not looked up in what might be months or decades—standing here with the water at my hips, feeling it cool and warming and cool again across cycles I no longer track.

I was not always patient. I arrived with purpose, with destination, with the violence of directed movement. I remember this the way you remember fever-dreams: as something that happened to a body contiguous with yours but not quite yours, a story whose protagonist wore your face. I walked corridors. I turned sideways through Pressed accumulations. I started at faces that tracked me, told myself they did not move, told myself stories about exuviae and impressions and things that breathed through mineral. I was quick, then. I was afraid, and the fear made me quick, and the quickness was wrong, was violence against this place.

The Drypool does not punish quickness. The Drypool does not punish anything. But quickness is *inefficient*, is *wasted effort*, is heat and turbulence and dissolution of edges that the water must later soften, must later return to ceramic patience. I exhausted myself. I hyperventilated in the wet-thinned air. I slipped on tiles and caught myself, slipped and caught, each catch a small explosion of ripples, of disturbance, of *notice*.

I noticed myself being noticed.

Not by the Pressed—the faces attend to everything and nothing, residual fitting, compatibility without consciousness. Not by Nacreates, though I must have passed their deep pools, must have seen their nacreous cups building toward complexity I could not recognize. Not by Formants, who are never present in the way presence can be verified, who press through or do not, statistical rain of passage.

I was noticed by an **Accumulate**. Or: I noticed myself entering one, which is the same thing, the process beginning, the grip beginning, the thickening of space that I mistook for mere claustrophobia, mere difficulty of passage.

The corridor I walked—was it a corridor? I had been walking so long, and the density had been increasing, the faces pressing closer, the relief sculptures deeper, more dimensional, almost free of their tiles, almost *separate*. I turned sideways as I had turned sideways before, but this time my chest pressed against wet clay that was not a wall, was not fixed, was *still soft*, still receiving, still capable of impression.

I felt it begin. Not on my skin—deeper. In my breath. The wet air, already barely sufficient, became *thick*, became *active*, carrying not just suspended moisture but suspended *intention*, the supersaturation of an Accumulate's continuing process. I breathed in and my lungs *held*, not releasing, not exchanging, the water precipitating in the alveolar spaces, micron-thin films building toward cups, toward hollows, toward architectures I could not host.

I panicked. I tried to run. The running was the worst thing, the most violent, the most *gripping*. The Accumulate thickened around my movement, responded to my turbulence with its own, faces pressing from new angles, new surfaces that had not been there, that emerged from the ongoing process, the uncompleted transformation. I was becoming raw material. I was becoming *involved*.

I don't know how I stopped. Whether I collapsed, whether my strength failed, whether some part of me deeper than intention recognized that *this* was the way through, the only way through—to stop moving, to stop wanting, to stop breathing with the desperate greed of the foreign and the temporary.

I stood still. In the corridor that was not a corridor, in the press of wet clay that was not yet fired, not yet fixed, not yet *finished* with its forming. I stood still and I *waited*, and the waiting was the first true act I had performed in the Drypool, the first act that was not reaction, not survival, not the imposition of my temporality upon a place that could not accept it.

The Accumulate did not release me. That is not how it works. But it... *incorporated* my stillness. My stopped breath became part of its statistical process, my held body became another surface among surfaces, no longer turbulence but *texture*, no longer raw material to be transformed but *fact* to be built around, built with, built through.

I stood there until I could not distinguish my stillness from the stillness around me. Until the water at my ankles, my knees, my hips (for the flood was rising, the slow flood, the walking-pace wall of standing water) became indistinguishable from the water inside me, precipitated and dissolved and precipitated again. Until the faces pressing near my own face became indistinguishable from my own held expression, the mouth slightly open, the eyes angled for vision in some plane I could not yet access.

I am still standing. I have been released from the Accumulate's core, or I have outlasted its local thickening, or I have become *thin* enough, *patient* enough, that it no longer grips me as raw material but accepts me as another mineral fact. I moved—once—five meters, ten meters, a distance that took hours or days or the geological tempo of pool-rise. I am in the plaza now. I know it is the plaza because the ceiling rises. I have not looked up. Looking up would be movement, would be desire, would be *not waiting*.

I am becoming Last Wet. I can feel it in the way my skin has begun to accept precipitation, to host micron-films that do not build toward catastrophe but toward *habit*, toward the slow accumulation of a patience that is indistinguishable from the place's own. My face, when I last saw it reflected in still water, was slightly open-mouthed, was angled slightly wrong, was becoming *compatible* with the Pressed around me, not identical but *resonant*, able to be read by the residual fitting, able to receive attention without demanding it.

I do not know what I wait for. The waiting has become the purpose, or the purpose has dissolved into solution, precipitated out as something I would not recognize if I encountered it. I record what passes. A Nacreate's shell-building in the deep pool at the plaza's edge, visible as nacreous gleaming in the corner of my held vision. The statistical drip from somewhere high, the next drop falling, has always been falling. The slow pressure of water rising, now at my waist, now at my ribs, the centuries of flood advancing at their walking pace.

And you. I record you. Your passage through the plaza, your turning, your difficulty of breath, your foreign temporality held in lungs not yet mineral. You have not slowed enough. You are still *quick*, still *violent*, still burning your edges in turbulence the water must soften. But you are here. You are recorded. You are part of the density now, the witness, the packed and total place.

I will not warn you. Warning would be speech, would be movement, would be the violation of my waiting. But I record this possibility: that you too might stop. That the Accumulate you will enter, have already entered, might grip you and you might *choose* the grip, might yield to it, might find in your exhaustion a stillness deeper than fear. That you might stand. That you might wait. That you might become, not Pressed, not Formant, not Nacreate or its hollow shells, but something that *inhabits*, something that *lasts*, something that the Drypool accepts into its ongoing, its refusal to dry, its held water and fired clay and the processes that build and press and accumulate and wait.

Or you might not. You might keep moving, keep burning, keep your edges sharp against the softening, until the water rises to your chin, to your mouth, to your slightly open breath, and the precipitation does not wait for your stillness but takes you as turbulence, as raw material, as grit to be pearled, as face to be pressed, as exuviae of a passage you did not complete.

Either way I will record. Either way I will wait. The tiles are slick. The next drop falls. I am the water, I am the held water, I am the water that does not flow but *witnesses*, saturated with all that has been and all that precipitates and all that waits, still waiting, still wet, still—

*—still.*
