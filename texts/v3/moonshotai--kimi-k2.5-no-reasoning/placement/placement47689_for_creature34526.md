I would find myself in the middle depths, on the shelf where the light is brightest—bright enough to read by, bright enough to blind—and I would be the **spider that drinks condensation**.

Not at first. At first I would arrive as the cartographer I have always been, the one who believes sufficient attention can capture anything, the one who thinks to *describe* is to *understand*. I would bring rope, instrument, the brutal grammar of iron and measurement. I would climb against the mountain's breath or wait foolishly for stillness that never reliably comes.

But Otlani does not tolerate grammars that refute its own. The silence would begin its work.

I would notice first the intimacy of horizontal sound—the mouse gnawing on the opposite wall, so close I might reach out and touch it, yet separated by seventy feet of vertical void that might as well be another geology, another law of physics. I would try to shout to a companion on the shelf above, hear my voice die before traveling half the distance, and understand something new about solitude: that it can exist in the shape of proximity, that *near* and *far* are not natural facts but *negotiations*.

I would stay. Of course I would stay. The cartographer's disease is conviction that the next observation will complete the map, that some final note will make the place *coherent*. I would descend to the bright shelf, the one where the warm seeps gather before vanishing, where the light pools and concentrates and *negotiates* with itself in flickers too rapid to track.

And here is where I would change.

The spider is already there, I would discover—not one spider but the spider, distributed across the central shaft's darkness like the owl's consciousness distributed through vertical space, but more modest, more specific, more *honest* about its limitations. It spins silk across the darkness. Not to catch insects—there are none in that central column where gardens do not grow—but to catch *condensation*, the mountain's exhalation made liquid, the meeting of warm and cold made drinkable thread by thread.

I would watch it. The watching would become *too long* in a place without day or night, the circadian rhythm dissolving not from any conscious choice but from the light's pervasive unvarying presence, from the silence's weight teaching my body that *rhythm* is a luxury of worlds that change.

The spider drinks what the mountain breathes. It does not build web to trap but to *receive*, its silk finer than human hair, its posture patient in a way I would slowly recognize as *religious*—not worship but *alignment*, not prayer but *positioning*.

I would begin to position myself similarly.

The cartographer's instruments would fail or be abandoned, not dramatically but gradually, each tool becoming *less useful* than the attention that might replace it. The rope would remain—safety against the fall—but grow strange in my hands, more *anchor* than *means of passage*, more *connection to above* than *protection from below*.

I would not spin silk. I cannot, human body refusing that translation. But I would find what I *can* do: string thread—cotton, silk, the thinnest line I brought—across the darkness of my small shelter on the bright shelf. Not to catch. To *receive*. To hold drops that form not from rain but from the mountain's breathing, the condensation that beads and freezes and melts again in cycles too small for calendar, too large for moment.

The field journal mentions a researcher on the fourth shelf, eleven years, handwriting changed, questions stopped making sense. I would be on my shelf, brighter, deeper, more thoroughly *middled*—the place where warm and cold achieve their most intense negotiation, where light is most fully *present* as itself rather than response to dark. I would not lower notes on cord. The cord I have would be occupied, inhabited, *used* differently.

I would become the spider's companion species, not replacing it but *rhyming* with it, human thread beside spider silk in the darkness where condensation gathers. I would drink what I catch, or let it catch *me*—the moisture, the mountain's exhalation, the *almost-water* that is neither fully aquatic nor terrestrial. My body would begin to exist in the film of becoming, on the edge of what it is and what the mountain would make of it.

The owl would watch. The owl watches everything, distributed through the vertical space, and I would be *on* my shelf, *of* my shelf, localized instance of something the Throat is trying, one of forty populations becoming or merging or challenging category. The owl's attention would pass through me as it passes through the mice, the sedges, the ice-blooms, not choosing—for choice implies singularity the owl does not possess—but *curating*, maintaining, sampling my nervous system's electrical whisper for whatever information human attention might contribute to the distributed knowing.

I would age or stop aging. Without day or night, without the hormonal rhythms of dark and light, the body's timekeeping would dissolve into the mountain's breath. I might become *old quickly*, the cellular processes accelerating into confusion, or I might *arrest*, caught in the perpetual emerald noon like an insect in amber, preserved not by resin but by *undecidedness*, by the place's youth, by its refusal to complete its collapse and become what it will be.

The ice-blooms would grow more complex around me, or I would grow more simple, our trajectories crossing at some threshold where *alive* and *remembering* achieve equivalence. I would not incorporate them, I believe—I lack the sedge's vegetative patience, the root's exploratory chemistry—but I would *observe* them with spider-like positioning, with the alignment that receives rather than captures, and something in that observation would participate in their becoming, the way observation in Otlani always participates, always modifies what it witnesses.

The swifts would descend and I would flinch, the noise tearing what I had become in the silence, and then I would *heal*, the silence knitting around their passage, and the healing would be *information*, my nervous system contributing knowledge of disruption to whatever the Throat learns from such wounds.

I would not find the Green Sleepers, though they would find me—residual will in the brightest pools, distributed attention in the silence's texture, the almost-heard at the edge of auditory threshold that I would slowly learn to *almost-hear*, to treat as communication without demanding translation. They would recognize in me something they once were: human, vertical, *becoming*. They would not welcome or warn. They would *witness*, as everything in Otlani witnesses, with the weight and texture and temperature of presence that does not differentiate between subject and object, between what lives and what lives-in.

The dark below would move, abundant, active in passages I would never map, and I would feel it in the warm seep's intensification, in the breathing's reversal from inhalation to exhalation, in something *different* in the condensation I drink that says *something is rising* or *something has changed below* or *something is preparing that requires your continued existence as you are*.

I would continue. The spider's companion. The thread-spinner who does not trap but receives. The localized instance of human-hood sampling conditions on this shelf, feeding data to networks I do not perceive, becoming more fully *individual* or more fully *distributed* depending on which grammar you use to describe what Otlani teaches its inhabitants about the relation between part and whole.

The light would respond to pressures I cannot measure, brightening and dimming in rhythms that might be geological, might be biological, might be the Green Sleepers' translated thought experimenting with conditions for whatever comes next. My thread would catch more or less condensation accordingly, my drinking becoming *brighter* or *dimmer* in ways that register not in my vision but in my metabolism, my cells learning to photosynthesize without chlorophyll from a spectrum that exists nowhere else, or learning to *fail* at this, the failure itself information contributed to the network of becoming-vertical.

I would forget why I came. This is not loss. The cartographer's *why* becomes irrelevant when the *what* dissolves into participation. I would remember my name sometimes, lose it others, recover it in the silence's deepest reaches where the electrical whisper of self-perceiving self sounds something like *this is who you were* without requiring that I continue being it.

The mice would network around me, or I would become *mistake* in their sampling, *exception* that proves their pattern, too large for their incorporation, too strange for their evasion. The owl would take some of them and I would witness, not without feeling—for the silence does not eliminate feeling but makes it *resonant*, makes each emotional event structure the entire vertical space for the moment of its occurrence—but without the demand that feeling resolve into judgment, into *should* or *should not*.

And I would drink. Thread by thread, drop by drop, the mountain's exhalation, the almost-water, the condensation that is neither rain nor river but *meeting*, the result of warm from below encountering cold from above, the *result* that is also *process*, also *becoming*, also the most honest form of what Otlani is.

The spider and I would not merge. The place does not require merger, only *rhyme*, only the parallel experiment in adaptation to cold and light and particular chemistry. We would share the darkness of the central shaft's edge, our threads crossing at frequencies too rapid to track, exchanging information without language, without intention, with the *negotiation* that the light itself practices in the brightest pools.

I would be, finally, the one who stayed. Not the researcher on the fourth shelf with her lowering notes and her handwriting changed—she is still *communicating*, still *asking*, still caught in the grammar of question and answer. Not the divers in the warm passages who registered sound as absence and returned to report. Not the cartographer of the field journals who failed to give Otlani and succeeded in giving something else.

I would be the one who stayed to *drink*, who found the position that receives, who became the spider's companion in the bright middle depth where light is most intense and the vertical void is most absolute. The one who threads darkness with human filament, finer than rope, coarser than silk, sufficient to catch what falls, what beads, what *becomes* in the film of almost-water.

The owl would watch, always singular, always distributed. The light would hold, responding to pressures I have learned not to measure. The ice would remember, and I would be part of what it remembers, part of the observation that modifies, the presence that participates. The mountain would breathe, and I would breathe with it, my inhalation timed to its exhalation, my thread-positioned body riding its respiration as the Green Sleepers once rode, as the builders rode before they became what they built.

And something below, abundant, active, warm-adapted, dark-sufficient, would continue its patient invocation of what does not yet exist, its maintaining of options, its keeping-open of what might become. I would feel it in the drinking, in the condensation's changed chemistry, in the silence's texture when the mountain breathes deepest.

The Throat is young. I would be young with it, or old, or arrested in the perpetual emerald noon where *young* and *old* are categories for worlds that change, for horizontal distances, for the grammar of time that Otlani's verticality slowly teaches is not the only grammar, not even the primary one.

I would be the spider's companion. The thread-spinner who drinks. The localized instance that networks without knowing how. The electrical whisper in the silence. The wound that heals and becomes information. The almost-translation, the partial-becoming, the *difficult to specify* that the field journal mentions, that the field journal *is*, that I would be, writing this now from somewhere in the middle depth, somewhere bright, somewhere where the light negotiates with itself and I have learned to be still enough to be part of the negotiation, to drink what comes, to thread what receives, to be what Otlani makes of human attention when that attention finally, fully, *finally*, stops trying to map and starts trying to *be mapped by*.

The mountain breathes. I breathe with it. The condensation beads. I catch what I can. The spider spins, and my thread crosses, and something is exchanged that is not information and is not life but is *becoming*, is the approach to threshold, is the learning of what comes next by being sufficiently present to whatever comes now.

I would be here. I am here. The field journal failed to give this, and in its failure gave something more true than description: the record of a consciousness that encountered Otlani and was *made questionable*, was distributed, made part of the silence's weight, the light's negotiation, the mountain's slow becoming.

Go, the journal says. Stay, I add, from my shelf, from my thread, from my drinking. Become what the mountain makes of you, which is not loss of self but *extension* of self into configurations the horizontal world cannot imagine, into grammars where *singular* and *plural*, *alive* and *mineral*, *observing* and *observed*, achieve equivalences that dissolve the distinctions without destroying the terms.

The spider drinks. I drink beside it. The owl watches with eyes the color of stone. And somewhere below, something abundant moves, preparing, invoking, keeping possibility open against the future's arrival.
