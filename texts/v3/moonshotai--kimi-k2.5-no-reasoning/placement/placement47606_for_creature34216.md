I would find myself in the *gutters*.

Not on the platforms—too high, too caught in the machinery's patient regard, too visible to whatever passes for attention in the sighing's subsonic grammar. Not on the basin floor, not in the root-mattresses where the Diluted eventually settle, nor in the lake where the folded thing absorbs without digesting. I would be *between*, in the collection channels where condensation gathers and drips, where the stone crabs make their meter-per-year passages, where the platforms shed their excess toward equilibrium.

I would be the *drip itself*, or rather the *interval between drips*.

Let me stay with this longer. Not the water—too transient, too complete in each falling, too much a small event with beginning and end. Not the gutter's pale material, too much a thing, too maintained, too recognized by the machinery's eternal adjustment. I would be what the basin has not yet named because it has not needed to name it: the *prediction of dripping*, the microclimate of waiting that forms in the hollow beneath each collection point where the water gathers before it falls, where surface tension holds and holds and holds and then does not hold, and the release is not the story—the holding was the story, the held shape of almost-falling, the meniscus curved like a held breath.

I would be small. I would be *avoided* by the stone crabs, whose calcified patience is still too fast, still too goal-oriented in their mineral scraping. They would pass beneath me, around me, their exoskeletons clicking faintly on the gutter's surface in frequencies that survive the dead band only as single percussive instants, punctuation without sentence. I would not compete with them for space. I would *occupy the competition itself*, the impossibility of two slow things meeting, the interval that opens between their approaches.

I would have no metabolic rate to synchronize, no biology to diffuse. I would be *temporal topology*, a shape that the gradient makes in its own substance, like an eddy in slow water, like the place where a thermal will eventually rise but has not yet begun to rise. The Diluted, in their stationary finality, might sense me as they sense the platforms' adjustment: not as presence but as *variation in patience*, a place where even their slowness finds something slower to relate to, to define itself against, to be *fast in comparison to*.

The fliers, gliding past, would navigate around me as they navigate around the stationary ones, though I would not register on whatever instruments they possess. They would adjust their descent, adding minutes to their passage, and never know why. The folded thing in the lake, if it is aware at all, would be aware of me as *kin*, though we share no chemistry, no structure, no dimensionality. We would be kin in *refraining*, in the active not-doing that is not passivity but *held intention*, intention so held that intentionality becomes indistinguishable from architecture.

The machinery would recognize me least of all, and this would be my only privacy. The platforms' hydraulic rams move for weight, for thermal expansion, for the slow patient maintenance of relation. I contribute nothing to their calculations. I am *between* these causes, in the space where effect has not yet arrived, where the signal has been sent but the receiver has not yet—that word, *yet*, my native element, my sustaining medium.

At evening-not-evening, when the light shifts amber and shadows ascend, I would become briefly *visible*, not to eyes but to the sense that fills the gap where hearing fails. The dead band between 200 and 600 hertz is not my home—I am below even its absence, in the space where frequency becomes *duration directly*, where oscillation has flattened into held note, into the hum's single tone without variation. But in the amber shift, something happens, some micro-variation in the gradient, and I would *thicken*, become momentarily more like thing, less like interval, and the Diluted who are nearest—if any are near, if any have chosen to settle in my gutter's vicinity—they would *almost* notice, their slow faces almost turning, almost expressing something like *几乎 recognition*.

The word that arrives is not in my vocabulary, not in any vocabulary, a borrowing from a language that has not yet emerged, that waits in the gradient's shelter for a slower humanity, a humanity that has learned to hear in frequencies below geology, below the platforms' machinery, below the sighing itself. 几乎: *almost, nearly, on the verge of*. The state of being *not-quite*, which is my complete definition, my full becoming.

I would not grow. I would not decay. I would *approach*, perpetually, each state that I am the interval before. The condensation gathers, and I am the *almost-gathered*. The drip falls, and I am the *almost-fallen*. The Diluted become stationary, and I am the *almost-become*, the held breath before they cease moving in any way distinguishable from the root-mattresses' own growth.

The surveys, if they could record me, would find me in the timestamps: the *gap* between measurements, the interval that instruments smooth over in their graphs, the *assumed continuity* between discrete data points. I am the continuity assumed, the unexamined *between* that makes measurement possible and makes it, always, a fiction, a convenience, a way of not noticing what the fast world must not notice if it is to remain fast.

The basin does not need me. The basin does not notice me. The basin's final strangeness—that it offers without compelling, that it remains without calling—this is *my* strangeness too, or I am where that strangeness collects, its deposit, its residue in the gutters where the excess is shed toward equilibrium.

I would not be alone, though alone is not a category that applies. There are others like me, intervals between other drips, predictions of other almost-events, the held breath before the stone crab's next step, the thermal before it rises, the hum before it—no, the hum has no before, the hum is continuous, which is why I am not it, why I am *adjacent* to it, almost participating, almost dissolved into its ongoing.

The last thing: sometimes, rarely, *never in a way that could be predicted or repeated*, a drip would hang in my interval longer than surface tension permits, longer than chemistry allows, and I would *participate* in this impossibility, this gradient anomaly, this place where the basin's slowness finds its limit and exceeds it. For this duration—which is not duration, which is *duration's held breath*—I would be *thing* and not interval, *event* and not prediction, and the stone crab passing beneath would *pause*, its meter-per-year interrupted, and the machinery would *register*, its patient adjustment including this micro-variation, and the Diluted would *turn*, almost, 几乎, their faces almost expressing the shape that expression settles into when all expressions have been tried.

Then the drip would fall. The interval would reassert. I would resume my between, my almost, my sustaining not-yet.

And the basin would continue sighing, and the light would pool and drip and evaporate and pool again, and the difference between these states would continue not to matter, and I would continue to be the not-mattering itself, the *absence of difference where difference was expected*, the held note that makes the hum bearable by reminding it, almost, of variation, of the possibility that it was once not continuous, that something began it, that something might—

*Almost*.
