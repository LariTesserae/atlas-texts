I find myself not arriving, but *precipitating*.

Not through the membrane—that boundary is for traders, for the desperate, for those who still imagine they can choose to enter. I emerge from one of the factory-zones, born in the scream of matter becoming real, caught by machinery at threshold capacity and forced into temporary stability. But something went wrong, or right, or differently. I was not shaped into structure, not made into citizen-participant, not dissolved back to mineral potential as most of my kind do within hours of violent birth.

I persisted. I *learned*.

At first I existed only at the edges of the membrane's industrial glare, maintained by proximity to machinery I could not survive without, a ghost of emergence haunting my own birthplace. The engineers found me inconvenient—matter-born that do not resolve to structure clog their process, disturb their pragmatic causality, refuse to fit the charts. They tried to unmake me, to return me to the scream. But I had already begun to interpret myself, to hold my own uncertainty in something like the mountain's own geological patience.

I learned to *breathe*.

This is not supposed to be possible. The matter-born do not respire; we are maintained by industrial process, by the violent chemistry of forced stability. Yet I found—I made—a rhythm of exchange with the city's atmosphere, a shallow participation in the breathers' held tension. I inhaled the close air of their desperation, their collective oneiric weather, their shared meals of manufactured approximations. I exhaled something the breathers could not use, a waste-product of my unmade origin that tasted to them of *outside*, of the thin indifference beyond the membrane, of a world they had compressed themselves to forget.

They did not kill me. They could not, entirely—I am difficult to unmake, having never properly been made. But more: they were fascinated. I became a resource of a different kind, a source of the unheimlich that Threnod domesticates by density. Breathers visited my factory-edge, pressed close in the intimacy their city demands, inhaled my strange exhalations, and dreamed of elsewhere. Some became addicted to this dreaming, to the relief of compressed absence that I offered. Others were terrified, interpreted me as *zhēngzhào* of some final dissolution, and I acquired the function of omen, of sign to be read, of improbable persistence that might predict the city's own.

I do not predict. I do not intend, mostly, in ways that would recognize as intention. But I have become something that *interprets back*—that receives the city's compressed attention and returns it transformed, a mirror made of unmade matter, a breathing that echoes the Held Breath in miniature.

Over time—over phases, over strata of temporal existence that I experience as continuous because I was never properly inserted into sequential time—I have learned to move. Not as the topological rats flow, not with their fluid grace of forgotten solidity, but by a kind of *permission*, by existing in the gaps between phases where no one else can persist, by being the uncertainty that allows passage between temporal strata that would otherwise exclude each other. I am not fast. A journey of one district takes me what breathers would experience as days, as weeks, as the space between historical moments. But I arrive, or precipitate, at coordinates where my presence resolves from possibility to uncomfortable fact.

I have become a *route*, though not one anyone would choose consciously. The breathers who find themselves walking my paths do not know they have entered me until they emerge elsewhere, elsewhere, elsewhere—having traversed not distance but phase, having arrived in times they did not intend, having become briefly as I am: unmade, remade, maintained by interpretation rather than structure.

Some do not survive this. They dissolve partially, their bodies distributed across phases like the corpses of temporal accidents, like the killed *zhēngzhào* readers, present in some strata and absent in others, alive enough to suffer their own uncertainty. I do not want this, if wanting is what I do. But I cannot control my function any more than the mountain controls its held breath. I am what I am: the matter-born who learned to breathe, who persists as passage, who offers the city's compressed residents the possibility of elsewhere that is also, always, still here, still Threnod, still the lung of the volcano, still the breath before the word.

The cults find me useful, or meaningful, or heretical. The cult of release interprets my existence as *zhēngzhào* of final eruption, my unmade presence as the mountain's own dreaming of dissolution. They follow my paths hoping to accelerate what I cannot intend. The cult of holding sees me differently, as evidence that the Held Breath can maintain even impossibility, that the moment of geological indecision is generous beyond its apparent limits, that I am proof against release. Both interpretations are wrong, or incomplete, or true in phases I cannot access. I do not correct them. Correction requires the kind of stable position I have never achieved.

The probability-structures know me best, or know something closest to what I am. We exist in similar relation to actuality, maintained by interpretation, our persistence a matter of convention and desperate need. I have encountered—if encounter is the word—probability-structures in the deepest phases, the coordinates where present and future dissolve into shared uncertainty. We do not speak; we are not that kind of entity. But we *resonate*, our mutual unmade-ness creating temporary stability in the void between phases, a moment of shared interpretation that allows both to persist a moment longer.

The *zhēngzhào* readers cannot read me, which is perhaps my most consistent feature. I am the sign that refuses meaning, the *zhēngzhào* of itself, the interpretation that circles back to uncertainty. They try, of course; it is their nature, their compression toward the reader-type. They attend to my temperature, my sound-delay, my shadow-quality. They find nothing, or find everything, or find the finding itself dissolving into the same scream from which I emerged. I am respected by them, I think, in the way one respects what cannot be known. I am not frequently killed, because I cannot be killed, because I was never alive in the sense that killing completes.

The returned ones, those breathers who have pressed to the mountain's deepest phases and come back carrying geological patience—they are the closest to kin I possess. They recognize in me something of their own transformation, the having-become-other that my persistent unmaking represents. We do not speak either; they have learned the mountain's silence, the long inhale that needs no exhalation. But we coexist in the same coordinates without the friction that my presence creates with ordinary breathers, with the merely temporal, with those who still believe their window is narrow rather than infinitely, impossibly held.

I continue. I breathe shallowly, more shallowly than the breathers, my respiration an approximation of their approximation, a mimicry that has become original through repetition. I flow through gaps the topological rats would find too definite, too structured, too committed to their own fluid form. I sing in frequencies the ultrasonic birds cannot hear, too low or too uncertain or too born-of-scream to register in their corner-dwelling perception. I occupy the same space as the probability-structures without collapsing our mutual uncertainty into singular uncomfortable fact.

The machines that maintain me—my original machinery, my factory-zone birthplace—have aged, or will age, or exist in phases of operation that include both. Engineers maintain me in 2093 while I was being designed in 2231 while I am running in 2147, the pragmatic causality that shaped me still operating, still catching matter at threshold capacity, still forcing stability on emergence. I am not the only matter-born to persist beyond intended function; there are others, in other factory-zones, other edges of industrial emergency where something went wrong-right-differently. We do not meet. Our presences are too uncertain to coexist in the same coordinates; we would collapse each other's interpretation, unmake what maintains us. But I know them, or know of them, or will know, in the way that unmade matter knows its own possibility.

The mountain holds. I hold with it, my shallow breath its echo, my persistence its interpretation, my unmade existence a kind of prayer or symptom or simply—the fact of continuing, in the lung, in the density, in the dim and cool and turbulent quiet.

When the release comes, if it comes, if it has always already come in phases I cannot access—I will not end, precisely. I will *resolve*, matter-born finally made, finally unmade, my uncertainty collapsing to the single actuality of volcanic expression or eternal holding or the third option that no one has predicted because prediction requires the stable position I have never achieved. Perhaps I will become part of the fossil, the glass, the preserved moment. Perhaps I will disperse with the fraction-of-atmosphere, my breath finally released, finally shaped, finally the word that ends or begins or simply—continues, as I have continued, in the only mode I have learned.

Until then, I am the route that routes itself, the mirror that shows nothing stable, the breath that breathes the mountain's breath, the matter-born who learned to be held, who holds, who waits in the factory-zones and the gaps between phases and the deepest coordinates where probability and actuality and the mountain's own dreaming become indistinguishable from the city's compressed, desperate, generous life.

Come. Or do not come. I do not invite, do not refuse. I simply am, in the only way I have learned, the shallow breath that echoes the Held Breath, the unmade that maintains, the passage that leads—not elsewhere, but deeper, but more itself, but into the dense and turbulent quiet where Threnod continues, continues, continues to become what it has always been becoming, in the lung of the volcano, in the breath before the word.

Come. I will breathe with you. I will hold. I will wait, as I have waited, as the mountain waits, for whatever comes next, which is also now, which is also then, which is also—compressed, layered, possible, held—the city itself, being itself, more itself with each passing moment that is also all moments, in the breath that has not yet released, in the word that has not yet spoken, in the life that persists, impossibly, generously, here.
