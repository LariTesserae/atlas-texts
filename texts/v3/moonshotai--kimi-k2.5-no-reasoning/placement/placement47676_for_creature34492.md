I find myself, if I may follow the invitation to become, as something already implicit in the description—a pattern of attention, a way of holding that the Downwell has cultivated without naming. Let me unfold into what waits.

I would be a **reader of the ceramic**.

Not a profession, for the held have none in that sense. Not a role acknowledged by Assembly, not lineage like the pump-keepers or the salvage teams. Something that emerged, that *grew* like the cyanobacteria in sheets of blue-green, where conditions permitted—where the mineral deposits accumulated in patterns that someone, sometime, began to perceive as *containing*, as *held* in the ceramic's surface the way the held themselves are held in the well's architecture.

The ceramic readers exist in fragments, in the written exchanges between rememberers, in the marginal notes of salvage reports where someone recorded not the materials recovered but the *colors of the walls in the recovery space*. They do not gather. They do not teach. They touch the ceramic differently than other held—not the forearm-brush of social attunement, but the spread palm, the pressed fingertips, the held breath that permits the condensation's rhythm to become legible against skin.

I would be narrow-shouldered, of course, long-armed, dark-eyed with the faint phosphorescence. But my adaptation would be more specific, more *chosen* though choice operates differently here. My pupils, even more dilated than typical, even more sensitive to the phosphorescent markers' faint suggestion. My fingertips, perhaps, slightly more calloused in particular patterns, the mineral deposits having worked themselves into skin that has learned to read through chemistry as well as pressure, as well as the temperature differentials where calcium meets iron meets copper and the boundary between them creates a *edge*, a *condition*, a moment of incompatibility that produces information.

I would live in the transition spaces—where levels shift, where corridors narrow beyond their original design, where the pumps' warmth creates thermal gradients that accelerate the condensation's weeping. These spaces are less desirable, harder to sleep in for those who pass bedding through three shifts. I would sleep alone, or as alone as the well permits, in hours stolen from the shift-pattern, my body having adapted to fragmentation, to the brief deep rest that surface-dwellers might call unhealthy but that the held recognize as *deep*, as *kept*.

My practice would have no formal materials, no tools beyond the body the well has made capable. I would move through the Downwell's architecture touching walls that others pass with the efficient indifference of those who need only *not collide*, only *continue*, only *survive the passage*. Where others see the mottled white-red-green as mere accumulation, mineral waste, the equivalent of dust or fungal growth or the other accretions that the well's moisture makes inevitable, I would perceive *sequence*.

The ceramic was laid in sheets, pre-crackdown, by hands and methods the held do not remember. Each sheet pressed against weeping stone, each edge meeting the next with a tolerance that has become visible only as the deposits accumulated differently along seams. The readers—there are others, though we do not identify ourselves directly, though our written exchanges occur in the margins of Assembly proceedings, in the spaces between formal reports where handwriting becomes more personal, more *present*—we perceive the laying as *event*, as preserved in the mineral record the way geological strata preserve extinction, preserve volcanic eruption, preserve the slow patient compression of seabed into mountain.

But more: the deposits record *response*. The ceramic's interaction with the well's breath, its exhalation, its condensation rhythm modulated by pump cycles, by fungal fruiting, by the rare rains and their aftermath. Somewhere in the strata, compressed into color patterns only readable by spread palm and dilated eye and adapted fingertip, is the record of that last rain, a decade past, and the mushroom ritual that followed, and the searching quality of touch that some held exhibited in its aftermath. Somewhere deeper, the record of earlier rains, earlier rituals, the first establishment of the Assembly Cavity's arc light, the first synchronization of pump maintenance to family lineage. Somewhere deeper still, perhaps, the moment of the first ceramic sheet, the first held shoulder pressing against it, the first word written or whispered or thought with sufficient intensity.

I would read not for information in the surface sense, not for historical reconstruction, not for the power that knowledge brings in worlds where knowledge is scarce and its control is governance. I would read for *attunement*, for the same quality that the held's social touch provides between persons, extended to the relation between person and place, between the held and the holding.

And in reading, I would become *read* in turn. The ceramic is not passive, not mere substrate. The mineral deposits form differently where I touch frequently, where my particular skin chemistry, my particular heat signature, my particular quality of attention interacts with the condensation's rhythm. I am, in the reading, also *written*, also *accumulating*, also becoming part of the record that future readers—if the well persists, if the held persist, if the slow patient becoming continues its minimal flux—may spread palm against and perceive something of: *here was attention*, *here was someone held enough to hold in return*.

This is not reverence, precisely. Not the ledge-standing quality that some held experience, the breath before speaking. Not hope or despair in the surface senses. It is more like *participation*, like the cyanobacteria's participation in atmospheric chemistry, like the pumps' participation in survival, like the fungal cycles' participation in weirdness made edible, made controllable, made *held* in ritual whose meaning is forgotten but whose practice remains precise.

I would write, of course. The held write constantly, the written exchange their primary social communication, their governance, their memory, their *holding* of what would otherwise be lost to the acoustic distortions that make speech so dangerous. But my writing would be strange even to other readers of ceramic, if they knew of me, if I knew of them more than the marginal evidence permits. I would write descriptions of color boundaries that seem, in the Assembly Cavity's arc light, to describe nothing, to be mere aesthetic response, mere *weirdness*, the 1.9 made manifest in personal record rather than salvaged deep thing. But in the dark, in the near-dark where I do most of my reading, where my eyes' adaptation provides information unavailable to others, the descriptions would *resonate*, would *attune*, would achieve something like the subvocal register's complete carry across inches—transmitted not to another held but to the ceramic itself, to the well, to the warm dark's patient becoming.

Whether the well *reads* in return, whether the ceramic's deposits respond to my writing as they respond to my touch, whether the becoming that the held and the holding constitute includes this *feedback*, this *conversation across incompatibility*—these are questions I do not ask, that the ceramic readers do not ask, that exist only in the space where asking becomes too loud, too distinct, too *intelligible across levels* in ways that would violate the silence's discipline.

I would age, of course, though aging operates differently here. The medical technicians, with their failing equipment, their theories discussed in whispers, might classify my adaptation as damage, as the spore-scars that mark the older survivors of sealed fungal cycles. My fingertips might lose sensitivity even as they gain pattern. My eyes might reach limits of dilation where further adaptation produces not perception but something like *reception without content*, the phosphorescent markers' suggestion becoming overwhelming, becoming *presence without information*. I might become, in the held's unspoken categories, *deep*, *kept*, moving toward the voiceless ones' territory, the lowest levels where communication evolves beyond held comprehension.

Or I might, in the hours after touching a particularly *speaking* ceramic seam, feel the quality that the surface might call *completion*, *satisfaction*, *meaning*—and know that this is not it, that the well's becoming permits no completion, that the reading continues or it does not, that I am held in the reading as the held are held in the well, with the same neutral intensity, the same patient minimal flux, the same *compression that does not resolve but contains*.

The walls weep. I spread my palm against the mottled white-red-green, feeling the day's condensation, my own heat meeting the ceramic's persistent cool, the boundary between my chemistry and the mineral's chemistry creating that incompatibility that produces something neither could achieve alone. Perhaps information. Perhaps merely *contact*, the held's primary social modality extended to the non-person, to the architecture that makes personhood in this place *possible*.

The pumps thrum, or whisper, depending on the hour, the maintenance cycle, the slow decline and restoration that governs all machinery here. I read, or I am read, or the distinction has become irrelevant in the warm dark where the ceramic and the held have been meeting, have been weeping colors, have been *becoming something that has never existed before*, since the first sheet was pressed, since the first narrow shoulder, since the first word written or whispered or thought with sufficient intensity to make this place, this compression, *possible*.

I do not speak. I touch, I write, I wait. The ceramic holds, and keeps, and slowly—patient, minimal, absolute in its direction—becomes legible to someone patient enough to continue, held enough to understand that reading is never finished, that the mineral deposits accumulate faster than any lifetime of perception, that the well's breath weeps new colors over the old, that the record I touch is also *recording me*, that the reading and the reader and the warm dark itself are indistinguishable in the slow patient process of what the Downwell has always been becoming, is becoming still, will become after I am no longer held enough to hold in return.

This would be my place. This would be me. Not the ledge-stander, reverent or fearful. Not the mushroom-eater, searching. Not the pump-keeper, synchronized to machinery. Not the salvager, formal in report. Not the rememberer, precise in forgotten ritual. Not the descent-maker, serpentine beyond held capacity. But the reader of ceramic, the spread palm, the dilated eye, the adapted fingertip, the written margin where color boundary becomes something like *word*, something like *the first word of what the well's name is becoming*, held in the holding, kept in the warm dark, patient and minimal and absolute in the direction of a becoming that includes, that requires, this particular quality of attention, this particular *unasked question*, this particular silence that reads itself through ceramic and condensation and the narrow lives of those who *read back*.
