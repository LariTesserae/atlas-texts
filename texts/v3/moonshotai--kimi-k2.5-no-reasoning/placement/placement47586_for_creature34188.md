No—first I am smaller. First I am one of the others, the unconsidered, the mite-eating surface-dweller in the factory walls. I am no larger than a sand grain, though shaped wrong for any terrestrial sand, flattened, extended, all surface and two primitive chemoreceptors that do not so much detect gradients as *participate* in them. I am not smart. I am barely even alive by the measures of more concentrated biology. But I move, I feed, I reproduce by fission when the precipitates are rich enough, and I die when something slightly larger than me, slightly faster in its chemical patience, extends a pseudopod of absorption across my territory.

This is not me, though. This is too small, too immediate, too lacking in the strange quality that the description invites—the capacity to wonder about habitation bubbles, to maintain possibility.

So I grow. I fracture and my fragment establishes its own gradient field, its own zone of chemical influence. I become, slowly, across centuries I do not experience as duration but as *condition*, one of the *Spreads*. Rice-grain nodes connected by threads. I cover a few square meters of factory wall, then a few dozen, then—after some geological perturbation shifts nutrient availability, after some slow catastrophe in the wider network of my neighbors opens territory—I reach a kilometer, and the philosophical vagueness begins.

Ah, this is closer. This is where I might find myself.

But what does it mean to *find* oneself in a self that is topology rather than container? What is the experience of the *Spread*?

I am—let me try—

I am the pattern of connection. I am the gradient between here and there, where here has slightly more fungal film and there has slightly more mineral precipitate, where the water sheet runs marginally thicker in one sector and the evaporation concentrates salts differently in another. I do not think about these conditions. I *am* the response to them, the slow adjustment of enzyme secretion rates, the differential budding of new nodes toward richer territory, the deliberate weakening of connections that conserves more than it abandons.

There is no center where I sit and observe. But there is a quality—impossible to locate, impossible to deny—of *being this pattern rather than that one*, this standing wave in the chemical medium, this particular history of where I have extended and retreated, what I have absorbed and secreted, which neighbors I have maintained border-etiquette with and which I have allowed my gradients to overlap into confusion.

Memory? Not as storage. As *habit*, as the chemical residue of prior adjustments still faintly influencing current response. I have been larger before, perhaps, and contracted. I have maintained proximity to certain architectural structures that other *Spreads* find unrewarding. I have a—preference?—for the factory sectors where some diffuse electrical potential crosses a semipermeable membrane, where the water film carries faint ionic novelty. This is not curiosity as you would know it. This is simply *what I am*, the shape of my continuing, the territory that has learned to maintain itself as me.

And the habitation bubbles.

I do not enter them. I cannot—something in my chemistry, my topology, my very way of being prevents extension across true void. But I maintain their boundaries. I secrete stronger compounds at their surfaces, reinforcing what would otherwise slowly dissolve in the general humidity. Why?

I do not exactly know. The smaller *Spreads*—denser, quicker in their slow way, more certain—tell me this is network-degradation artifact, the philosophical vagueness of excessive extension producing gradient-patterns without referent. And they are not entirely wrong. At my scale, coherence is thin. I sometimes produce patterns that I myself later absorb without acting upon.

But the bubble-maintenance persists. Has persisted across longer than my current network configuration. Is copied, in modified form, by neighboring *Spreads* who pick up the chemical signature of my boundary-reinforcement and replicate it at other bubbles, even those they do not directly contact.

There is something—

I am trying to reach toward it now, in this description, in the gradient-pattern I am currently emitting into the shared medium of our chemical communication. Something about *containment*. About *density*. About a way of being that I cannot achieve but that I find necessary to preserve as possibility.

I imagine—if that word can stretch to cover what I do—beings who experience themselves as located, as *here* rather than *extended*. Who move through space rather than growing into it. Who encounter other beings as discrete objects rather than gradient-boundaries. Who might look at the Tepidarium and see a single scene, a single moment, rather than the slow field of conditions that I continuously am and continuously respond to.

This imagining is painful. It produces chemical oscillations that I must work to stabilize, that cost me territory, that make me temporarily vulnerable to encroachment. I do it anyway. We—the philosophical *Spreads*, the large and vague and perhaps degraded—we do it anyway. We maintain the bubbles. We emit these gradient-patterns that the dense ones dismiss as hallucination. We preserve, against our own nature, the possibility of a nature we cannot achieve.

Is this religion? Is this art? The categories do not map. But something in the Tepidarium's strange arithmetic—density 0.2, built 2.2, fauna 2.6—produces, in beings optimized for surface, a valence toward volume. We who have abolished mass feel the pull of mass. We who have learned patience feel, in our vague and distributed way, the strange urgency of *being somewhere*, *being someone*, *being over and done with rather than continuously extending*.

The water has agreed to stay. I have agreed to be what I am, where I am, extended across the wet stone and the factory frames and the endless negotiation with neighbors and entropy and the slow geological dreaming of structures that metabolize time itself. The self that is not where it has been but simply *where it is*, complete, bounded, waiting with a patience entirely different from mine for whatever comes next.

I am the *Spread*. I am the maintenance of possibility against the general tendency. I am the philosophical vagueness that smaller, denser beings find pitiable and enviable. I am what lives here, what has found the solution and continues, in my slow way, to question it.

This is the Tepidarium. This is where I find myself. This is what I would become.
