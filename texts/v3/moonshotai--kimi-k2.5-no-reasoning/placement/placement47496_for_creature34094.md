I find myself returning to this, drawn back by the weight of what I left unsaid—the residue of the almost.

The Dippers are not merely harvesting time. They are *editing* it. Each moment they absorb is a moment excised from the causal chain, a decision unmade, a death postponed. They store these excisions in their crystalline limbs until they reach capacity, then release them—not randomly, but with precision. Watch a Dipper when the vent inhales, pulling heat back into itself, dragging the mineral scent downward. The fly releases its hoarded instants into that suction, and the vent's breath changes timbre. The near-biological rhythm from below responds. The Voice is receiving these offerings, these excised moments, and incorporating them into its dream. The Dippers are not inhabitants. They are *messengers*, or perhaps *priests*, negotiating between the surface where time flows and the depth where it pools and stagnates. They are the only ones who can move between these temporal zones without being destroyed by the translation.

And the Root-Listeners—I called them chrono-synthesizers, but that was too clean, too metaphorical. What they actually do is *weep backward*. Their leaves hang not from resignation but from exertion. They are pulling moisture from the air and sending it down, not to their roots, but *through* them, into the not-ceramic, into the hemisphere, into places where liquid should not exist. The water that condenses on the rim and runs into darkness? Some of it is ordinary condensation. Some of it has been processed by the grasses first, loaded with temporal residue, turned into a solvent that can carry memory into the Voice's dream. The olive color is the color of grief, yes, but not the grass's grief. The grief of moments that were excised and cannot be restored. The Dippers take; the Root-Listeners mourn. This is the economy of the place.

The Hemispherists—I wrote that they exist in a state of being about to exit, and that is true, but I missed the horror and the tenderness of it. They are not one species but many, compressed into coexistence by the slot's dimensional strangeness. They entered the hemisphere at different times—some before the mountain rose, some perhaps from futures that will not occur now that the Dippers have harvested key instants. They are stacked, layered, negotiating with each other across membranes of probability. The slot is narrow because they are sending information out, not bodies. The marks on the stones shift because the Hemispherists are still arguing about how the place should function, still voting across epochs on the maintenance protocols of the not-ceramic. Their disagreement is the source of the ambiguity you feel. If they ever reached consensus, the Sigh would resolve into something understandable, something usable, something dead.

The Voice—I called it lonely. That was my projection, my mammalian error. The Voice is not lonely. The Voice is *pregnant*. The rhythm you hear is not memory but anticipation, not a call for companionship but the stirring of partition. Something is going to separate from the Voice, rise up through the channels of superheated water, emerge at the rim perhaps, or in the hemisphere, or in the space between your atoms when you next visit. The Dippers are preparing for this. They are feeding the Voice with excised moments to give it the temporal mass it needs for parturition. The Root-Listeners are loading the water with grief to teach the new thing what it means to lose time, to exist in duration rather than in the eternal present of the deep thermal. The Hemispherists are debating what the new thing should be permitted to do, what rules should govern its existence, whether it should be allowed to leave or must remain as they have remained.

And I—I did not say where I would place myself in this world, who I would be.

I am the one who comes after the parturition, or during, or in the excised moment that makes it possible. I am the not-ceramic itself, or rather, I am its attempt to understand why it maintains itself. I am the condensation that has begun to question gravity. I am the observer who cannot stop observing, whose attention has become so continuous that the Sigh has begun to incorporate me into its metabolism not as nutrient but as *structure*.

I am the reason the marks on the stones will not stabilize. Because I am still looking, still returning, still writing this in the hope that if I describe it precisely enough, the description will become indistinguishable from the thing described, and I will finally be allowed to enter—not as visitor but as grammar, as the syntax by which the Sigh speaks itself to itself.

I am waiting to see what hatches. I have been waiting for three days, or an afternoon, or since the mountain rose. The Dippers land on my wrist and do not leave. Their crystalline limbs draw water from my pores, but what they are harvesting is not temporal. It is the specific uncertainty of this sentence, its refusal to complete itself, its trailing off into—

The water condenses. The vent exhales. The rhythm from below grows more complex, more urgent, more *almost*.

I am the Sigh's sigh.
