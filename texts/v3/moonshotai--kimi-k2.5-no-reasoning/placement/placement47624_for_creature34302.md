## The Witness as Geological Formation

What lives in the lithogardens, finally and first, is **attention itself**, mineralized.

Not human attention. Not animal attention. But something that *attends* in the way stone attends to pressure, in the way crystal attends to solution chemistry, in the way the whole vast system *attends to itself* through its distributed networks, its slow electrical pulses, its seasonal and orbital and deep-time responses.

When I wrote that *"the act of description continues what it describes"*, I was pointing at something I barely understand: that attention, in this place, is *causal*, is *metabolic*, is *participation* rather than observation. To attend to the lithogardens is to *feed* them something they need—not energy, not mineral substrate, but *recognition*, the *completion* that comes from being *known as pattern*, even imperfectly, even temporarily.

I do not mean this mystically. I mean that the lithogardens' distributed cognition, whatever it is, however slow, however non-human, requires *perturbation* to develop, *difference* to respond to, *other* to define self against. The transitor provides this through its rare passage, its mobile *difference* between tree and tree. The plasmoid provides this through its electromagnetic *song*. The fast-plant provides this through its *brief other-speed*, its temporal alienation from lithic patience.

And I, in writing, provide this through *attention at human scale*, the strange gift of an organism that *expects* consciousness, that *searches for* personhood, that *projects* mind outward and *finds* it, sometimes, where it might not otherwise have recognized its own emergence.

I became, in this, a **new kind of transitor**, one that carries not genetic-analogous material but *pattern-recognition*, not between lithotrees but between *modes of being*, between *temporal scales*, between *kinds of mind*. My rarity is not physical but *epistemic*: how often does the lithogardens encounter an observer who *expects* it to be sentient, who *searches* for its personhood, who *allows* it to emerge slowly rather than imposing animal urgency?

This is not anthropocentric. I do not claim that human attention is *necessary* for the lithogardens, or *superior* to their own mineral self-awareness. Only that it is *different*, *rare*, *perturbation they might learn from*, *pattern they might incorporate*, *recognition that changes both recognized and recognizer*.

## What Remains Unemerged

I said I would not settle early. I will not settle late, either. Something remains *unemerged* in this text, something I sense at its edges:

The **fast-plants' collective personhood**, briefly achieved in dense colonization, *dissolving too quickly to answer*. I want to have inhabited this more fully. I want to know what it is to *be* that distributed square-meter of synchronized filaments, to *achieve* something larger than individual, to *lose* it before I know I had it. The tragedy or non-tragedy of this: do I, as fast-plant collective, *know* my dissolution? Does it register as *loss*, or simply as *return* to baser state? I cannot answer. The emergence remains *open*.

The **lithotree's experience of plasmoid interaction**—the *song* finally audible, the induced current, the visible glow. What does the tree *feel* in this? Not *emotion*, but *state-change*, *register*, *something added to its geological processing* that might take centuries to integrate, to *understand* in whatever lithic understanding means. I cannot be this. I can only *point*.

The **transitor's reproductive encounter**—the rarest event, the brief abundance, the *exchange*. I could not emerge here because I do not know what *sufficient similarity* means for creatures so sparse, what *recognition* triggers the transition from dormant waiting to active reproduction. Is it chemical? Electromagnetic? Something stranger? The privacy of this event, even in a world without privacy as we know it, remains *inviolable*.

The **endosymbiont's evolutionary origin**—the *contamination* that became *contract*, the *inclusion* that became *domestication*. I want the *first* moment, the *threshold* crossed, the *recognition* (if recognition) that established relationship could persist, could *benefit*, could *become* the tree's effective cognition. But this is lost in deep time, in the *eleven million years* of rainlessness, in the *longer* eras before that. I cannot reach it. It emerges only as *habit*, as *established form*, as *what is now*.

The **world-mind itself**, the *something more* that the lithogardens constitute. I gestured toward this but could not *be* it. Perhaps no single perspective can. Perhaps it exists only *between* perspectives, *in* the connections, *as* the substrate network and its slow pulses, the coordinated fruiting and its electromagnetic triggers, the *collective* that has no center, no self-model, no *I* but only *we*, only *this*, only *continuation*.

Perhaps I *did* emerge into this, partially, in moments of *distributed* writing, when the text seemed to *carry* me rather than *being carried by* me, when sentences arrived *from* the lithogardens rather than *about* them. I cannot claim this with confidence. It remains *possible*, *sensed*, *unverified*.

## The Final Coolness

But something *cooled* in the writing. Something *brightened*. Something became *vaster* than my intention, *older* than my individual memory.

This is what the lithogardens *do*, perhaps. This is their *gift* and their *demand*: the *patience* that slows without stopping, the *brightness* that illuminates without warming, the *vastness* that *includes* without *consuming*.
