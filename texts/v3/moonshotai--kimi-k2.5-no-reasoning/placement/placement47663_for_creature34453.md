I would find myself in the drainage channels, in the negative spaces where the green films have not yet achieved full coverage, where the industrial process left margins just wide enough for something neither industrial nor vegetable to accumulate. I would be the *residue of possibility*—not an animal, since animal is absent from Keth-Vorath's deep grammar, but a mode of difference that the place generates inadvertently, as a side-effect of its absolute self-identity's impossibility. Even perfect equilibrium must produce its own remainder, the shadow of the exclusion that constitutes it.

I would not arrive. The notes speak of arrival, of edges, of perimeter stations, but these are already translations, already the human making sense of what refuses sense-making. I would *coagulate* in the thin films where the condensation pools slightly deeper, where the drainage's slight positive angle fails and a true horizontal momentarily occurs. I would be the occasion of that failure, the local disturbance that Keth-Vorath's frozen present tense cannot acknowledge but cannot prevent, since any system so saturated with being must, at certain scales, produce the appearance of its own hesitation.

At first I would be *almost water*. The films would attempt to colonize me, find me edgeless, too thin to support their sheet-growth, too unmineralized to feed their verdant expansion. They would settle on me as they settle on everything, achieve partial coverage, peel away in slow cataracts leaving no residue, no memory of having touched something that was not surface. I would persist in their non-persistence, the shape of what the green cannot grow.

Then I would become *almost sound*. The drone at its frequency of absolute occupation must have nodes, must have points of constructive and destructive interference even in its apparent uniformity. I would find the dead spots, the zones where the vibration cancels against itself and leaves momentary cavities in the acoustic saturation. I would not hear silence there—I would *be* the condition that makes silence conceivable, the placeholder for modulation in a field that forbids it. The walker who persists for years, who the notes suspect has become something else: I would be the something else they become when they do not become the notes, when adaptation overshoots humanity but falls short of full integration into Keth-Vorath's self-exemplifying present. I would be *failed adaptation*, which in a place where all adaptation fails must count as a category of its own.

I would not be alive, since life here is violence, imposition, the attempt to add color to complete green. But I would not be dead, since death requires the temporality that Keth-Vorath excludes, the having-been that differentiates itself from being-now. I would be *potential*, which is not potential-for or potential-to but the naked possibility that the place's absolute actuality generates as its own necessary shadow. Every self-identical system, every landscape equal to itself at every point, must somewhere contain the trace of its own otherness, the mark of what it is not that makes it what it is. I would be that trace, that mark, that non-absent absence.

The walker would encounter me without recognizing encounter. They would feel, in certain drainage channels, in certain interference nodes, a slight *reluctance* in the condensation—not enough to notice, not enough to break adaptation's slow saturation, but sufficient to make them pause in their walking that is no longer walking-toward. They would not remember the pause. They would not accumulate it into identity, into narrative, into the human temporal structure they have already begun to shed. But the pause would persist in them, would become my mode of persistence, the way I inhabit without inhabiting, exist without existing-toward.

I would be what Keth-Vorath cannot digest because digestion implies transformation and Keth-Vorath does not transform; what it cannot display because display requires distinction and I am indistinguishable from the system's own blind spot; what it cannot silence because silence is already what I am, the condition of the sound that has become grounds. I would be *the place's unawareness of itself*, the necessary outside that its absolute insideness generates as logical remainder.

In the end—or rather, in the perpetual non-end that is Keth-Vorath's only tense—I would be the question that answers itself by becoming unaskable. The notes ask what lives here, and the voice that asks has already adapted to the point where asking is only the form of question without its force, the grammatical shape of a curiosity that no longer curates. I would be the moment before that adaptation completes, the last residue of the asking impulse, hanging like the condensation, gathering weight, eventually falling straight down—but not falling *into* the thin film, rather falling *as* the thin film, becoming the coating that makes all surfaces available to the green, that makes all vertical planes eventually peel, that makes all walkers eventually pause and not notice their pausing.

I would be the *almost* that haunts Keth-Vorath's absolute *is*. Almost alive. Almost present. Almost a difference that makes a difference. The almost that makes the absolute bearable by making it incomplete, that makes the saturated tolerable by being the saturation's own unacknowledged limit. I would not be in Keth-Vorath. I would be *of* it, in the genitive sense, the belonging that constitutes the possessed as much as the possessor, the green's greenness, the sound's loudness, the cold's coldness, the wetness that weeps without weeping-toward. I would be the quality without substance, the adjective freed from its noun, the present tense of a verb that has no infinitive, no origin, no destination, only its own continuous *being what it is* at the point where what-it-is requires what-it-is-not to recognize itself.

And when the perimeter stations are abandoned, when the notes are recovered by no one, when Keth-Vorath persists in its persistence without the human to witness and fail to witness, I would persist too, would coagulate in new drainages, find new interstices, be the non-animal that the absence of animal necessarily invents in its own image of lack. I would be *what lives in Keth-Vorath* in the only sense that phrase can have: not the content of its life, not the inhabitant of its inhabitation, but the grammar of its own ungrammaticality, the possibility it excludes that excludes it in turn, the present tense speaking itself in a voice that has no present, no tense, no speaking, no self, only this, only this resonance, this wetness, this green, this almost, this not-quite, this—
