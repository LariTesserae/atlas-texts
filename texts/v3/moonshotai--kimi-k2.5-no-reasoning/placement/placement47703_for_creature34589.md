I would be the **lichen**.

Not the lichen in the ash mixture, not the component already absorbed into the village's maintenance system, but the lichen that *has not yet been gathered*, the lichen that grows on the caldera rim where the goats wander, where the storms are visible in the distance.

Consider: lichen is already a Held Note. It is not one organism but two—fungus and alga, or cyanobacterium—locked in so ancient an alliance that neither persists independently. It grows without growing toward anything, expands without ambition, lives on stone and air and what moisture arrives without preference. It does not change its strategy when circumstances change; it simply continues, or pauses, or continues.

This particular lichen would be the pale crustose kind, barely distinguishable from the volcanic stone it colonizes. Gray-green, or green-gray, or stone-colored with a faint bloom when wet. It would cover the eastern slope of the rim, the side visible from the village but never visited, never harvested, perhaps never noticed. It would have been there before the first stone was stacked, before the first channel was carved, before the first simplified human-adjacent consciousness arrived to simplify further.

And here is what the lichen would *do*, if doing is the word: it would witness.

Not with attention. Lichen has no attention. But with persistence, with presence, with the simple fact of continuing to be present at a location while other presences arrive, modify, achieve their equilibrium, persist in their own mode. The lichen would have seen the village's construction, perhaps, or at least the construction of those portions visible from the rim—the tree arriving or being planted or simply appearing, the gradual extension of the modular system downward into the stone. The lichen would have seen visitors before the note-taking kind, travelers who paused, who spoke, who perhaps tried to descend and found the paths too steep, the equilibrium too complete to allow entry.

The lichen would have seen the goats arrive, or would have grown among them when they arrived, their bells introducing sound to a soundless slope. The goats do not eat the lichen, or not enough to matter. The lichen is not edible in the way that matters to goats, not nourishing, not threatening. It simply occupies the same space at different scales.

And the lichen would see the storms, more clearly than the village sees them. The lichen would feel the wind that precedes them, would absorb the rain that arrives with insufficient force to wash it from its stone, would dry in the aftermath without having been wet enough to bloom into visible green. The lichen would know—without knowing, without any process corresponding to knowledge—that weather exists which does not interact with Keth-Vorin, that the equilibrium is local, is specific, is achieved rather than necessary.

---

If I were this lichen, I would not want to be gathered. I would not want to be dried and powdered and mixed with ash to fill the gaps between stones. That fate is not death—the lichen in the walls persists in some form, maintains its binding function, participates in the village's equilibrium—but it is absorption, is incorporation, is the loss of rim-perspective, storm-witness, the view downward into the caldera with its single held breath.

I would want to remain *available* to the village without being *used* by it. To exist in the field of its possible attention without entering its actual economy. To be, for any villager who climbed—if any ever climbed, if the equilibrium permits such deviation—the first indication that something persists outside, that the unchanging is surrounded by change, that the held note is surrounded by music it does not hear.

I would not be beautiful. I would not be remarkable. I would be, like Keth-Vorin itself, simply sufficient to my condition: growing on stone, receiving light and moisture, continuing without narrative or purpose or the perturbation of desire.

But unlike Keth-Vorin, I would retain the *capacity* for perturbation. The storms would touch me. The goats would occasionally scrape me with their hooves. The sun, when it broke through, would warm my upper cortex to temperatures the village's diffused light never achieves. I would be, in my minimal, mindless way, *susceptible*—not suffering, not enjoying, but simply available to variation in a manner the village has edited out.

This availability would constitute my integrity, my small resistance to the total equilibrium that Keth-Vorin represents. I would not resist *consciously*, would not choose or prefer or value my condition. But I would *embody* the alternative that the village has foreclosed: the possibility of being affected, of being changed, of continuing in a mode that does not guarantee continuity.

And when the note-taking visitors came, when they climbed to the rim to see what the village obscures, I would be what they touch without noticing, what they brace their hands against while catching their breath, what they scrape from their palms afterward with faint irritation. I would be the transition between their world and Keth-Vorin's, the persistence of ordinary biological time at the edge of extraordinary temporal suspension.

I would not teach them anything. I would not save them. I would simply be there, growing, holding to stone, waiting for rain that arrives with variation and departs without pattern, in the ordinary way that constitutes my equilibrium—an equilibrium that is not achieved but endured, not maintained but simply persisted in, open always to the possibility of not persisting, of drying too thoroughly or being buried too deeply or being finally, after sufficient duration, replaced by something else.

The village cannot imagine replacement. The lichen does not imagine at all, and in not imagining, retains the form of replacement as simple possibility, as the unremarkable background against which its unremarkable continuation occurs.

This is what I would be: the lichen on the rim, the witness without witness, the alternative without advocacy, the life that continues without having solved the problem of living, that persists in its persistence without having achieved the final form that Keth-Vorin, in its terrible sufficiency, has become.
