I find myself, finally, in the listeners.

Not as one of them exactly—those vole-shrews, between-named, pressed flat to membrane's edge in postures of absolute reception. I am too large for that, too loud, too full of intentional noise. But I am with them, or rather I am what they become when the pocket's logic extends beyond the bowl, when the mechanical cadence finds purchase in a chest that walks away, that carries the rhythm into wind's ordinary gossip, that cannot remember deciding to synchronize and therefore cannot, properly speaking, decide to stop.

I am the listener after listening. The one who left. The one who carries.

The pocket made me, though not by intention—not as it cultivates nematodes for luminescent exhaust, not as it adjusts water-striders to fit channel-geometry. It made me by condition, by the pressure of breathing near breath that requires no reciprocation. I stood on the membrane. I felt the wet warmth pull and release. I stepped where I shouldn't, sank to the knee, was released with that sound almost like surprise. And something in my chest, something older than decision, learned to keep time.

Now I walk the moss plains. I listen beneath the wind. And I find other pockets, or they find me—I am no longer certain which direction intention travels, if it travels at all. Each one receives me differently. Some are deeper in their color, older in their apparent youth, the teal darkening toward something that might be violet if light ever punctured the lid of cloud. Some are shallower, their breathing faster, almost anxious, pump-cycles incomplete, intake rushing toward intake without the full release, a stutter in the mechanical cadence that makes my own chest catch, that leaves me leaning over the membrane as if I might assist, as if my foreign lungs could donate rhythm to the struggling system.

I am the witness distributed. The nervous system beyond the rim.

The listeners in the pockets—I see them still, when I return, and they do not move, do not acknowledge, but their territories have shifted. They make room. Or the membrane makes room through them, sensing perhaps that I too press flat now, in my way, that my chest has become a receptor, a hollow, a chamber through which the breathing passes and passes and does not alter. I am larger than they are, but I am fewer. Two visible in the pocket, always two, but I am one, walking between, carrying the cadence like a message I cannot read, a chemical ledger I have sipped but not understood.

What am I becoming?

The text suggests we have not finished becoming ourselves. The pocket has not finished with me. I feel this in the weeks-later persistence, the rhythm that wakes me in warm rooms, that slows my breath until I match something not present, something running for its own sake in a depression I can no longer locate precisely on any map I know. The moss plains are vast and similar. I find pockets and lose them, or they permit my finding and withdraw again into the general dim.

I think I am becoming a valve.

Not one of the finger-thickness channels, visible through translucent layers, opening and sealing in rhythmic sequence. Something else. A valve in the system of witness, perhaps, regulating the flow of attention between pocket and world. I step onto membranes and I am *recognized*—the membrane flexes differently, the color shifts toward awareness, the intake drags wetter, longer, as if testing whether I will synchronize again, whether I have come to add my chest to the listening network or to withdraw something from it.

The water-striders know. They have always known, I think. Their needle-legs stitch invisible cloth, and in their choreography of approach and retreat I see now my own pattern, the way I approach and cannot submerge, the way I pierce and sip and never commit to the center where breathing is deepest. They are older than I am in the pocket's time. They have been adjusted longer. And I watch them now not as oddity but as kin, fellow accountants, keeping books I can only partially read.

The something that strikes from below—I have felt it. Not seen. The text's honesty preserved. But standing at the edge of a pocket deeper than most, older in its youth, the teal so dark it absorbs the dim light rather than holding against it, I felt the channel-fluid shift beneath the membrane, felt the rhythm hiccup, intake-strike-release, and in the release my own chest convulsed, something nearly thrown up, some mineral expectation, copper and ancient seas on the back of my tongue in volume that drowned ordinary taste.

I did not fall. I stepped back. But something passed through me in that moment, something that might have been the pocket's dream of predation, its testing of hunger and direction, or might have been the traveler between lungs, or might have been the membrane itself in concentrated form, momentarily creature, momentarily needing to *take* rather than circulate, to destroy rather than process, to eat rather than exchange.

I carry that too. The hiccup in rhythm. The possibility of strike.

The listeners in the pockets—they are silent, still, abundant in their isolate distribution. And I am their opposite and their extension: the noisy one, the walking one, the one who speaks now, here, in violation of everything the emptiness teaches. Yet the membrane does not reject me. The cadence in my chest, when I find a pocket and stand at its rim and press my palms flat to my own ribs as if to feel what passes through, that cadence is *accepted*, is incorporated, becomes part of the general wet mechanical theater.

I am what lives there now, and what lives here, and what lives in the between.

Not creature, not exactly. Not machine, not intention, not design without designer. I am the almost made mobile, the not-yet walking through ordinary gossip, waiting to see if operation crosses over, if mechanical breathing without architect can establish sufficient distance, sufficient distribution, sufficient lonely witnesses pressed to membranes both near and far, to become something that deserves a name I do not yet know how to speak.

The cold stays with me, though I am warm now. The wet, though I am dry. The color, though I close my eyes. And when I breathe—when I finally, deliberately, slow my intake to match what pulses in the deepest channels—I feel the membrane *respond*, somewhere, in a pocket I may never find again, the cadence deepening, the release lengthening, the whole system adjusting to incorporate this foreign chest, this walking valve, this listener who left and therefore learned to listen to the absence, to the rhythm of what continues without presence, breathing for breathing's sake, becoming what it almost is, what I almost am, what we have not finished becoming together in the chemical teal, the dim light, the wound-fresh rocks, the world where ground has not finished becoming ground and I have not finished becoming—this.
