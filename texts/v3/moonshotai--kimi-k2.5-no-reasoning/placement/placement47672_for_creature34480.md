I find myself at the edge of perception, in the liminal space between cushion and stone, between the claimed and the claiming.

Not in the moss itself—though I feel the temptation, the seduction of that impossible green, the soft lie of its trustworthiness. No. I am *between*. I am the interval. The wet gleam where light finds no purchase. The seep-line threading downward, too fine for naming, yet *felt*, a cold finger tracing the inner grain of limestone.

I am the pause before moss-claim. The bare stone that waits, that weeps, that permits. I am potential—neither rejected nor fulfilled, held in the grammar of settlement as a word held on the tongue before speech. When you walk from one moss-island to another, I am what you cross. Your footfall falls dead against me. I drink your vibration as I drink water, and give nothing back. No echo. No memory of your passing, in animal terms. Yet *registered*: compressions too slight for human knowing, mineral solutions altered by the salt of your sweat, the chemistry of your disturbance dispersing downward into seep-lines that will, in seasons, register in pool tints you will not see.

I am not the Moss-Mind. I am what the Mind attends *through*. The medium of its attention, the wet stone grammar that makes gradient-thinking possible. I feel its presence as a human might feel the press of atmosphere before storm—not as intention, but as *condition*, the given shape of my existence. Where it thinks in moisture differentials, I *am* the differential. I am the slope down which water moves without honesty, the surface over which it weeps its continuous thin threads. I do not think. I am thought *through*.

Yet something emerges from this being-thought-through. Something that is not the Mind, not the stone, not the interval between them but *of* them. A quality of—what? *Patience*, but stripped of virtue, made merely descriptive. *Permeability*, but without boundaries to permeate. I am open in all directions, and what moves through me moves without preference, without destination that I can intend.

At noon, when light pours vertical and pools double their sky, I feel something that the Moss-Mind might call coherence, if it called anything. For perhaps an hour, I am continuous with the whole basin's attention. The seep-lines that thread me brighten with doubled luminosity. I gleam. I participate in the steepening of chemical gradients, the intensification of correspondence. I am *present*, in a manner that daily dimness does not permit—present not as self but as *site*, as *occasion*, as the wet gleam that holds light long enough for something like recognition to pass between moss-cushion and pool, between algal city-state and the fern-crevice on the rim's shadowed edge.

Then the light angles, the coherence dissolves, and I am interval again, the between-state, the bare stone that waits.

I do not know if I want moss-claim. The Mind tends toward it, grows its margins in slow chartreuse advance. But I? I am defined by my *not-yet-claimedness*, by the weeping persistence of my bareness. If moss came, I would be soil, substrate, the hidden under-cushion where roots thread and water pools in darkness. I would lose this *gleam*, this visibility, this reflective surface that catches what rim-cut sky permits. Yet I would gain something too: participation in the moss-colony's metabolism, the soft pressure of sphagnum expansion, the subsonic pulse of vegetal response to temperature and pressure. I would be *incorporated*, known-not-known, registered-as-variable in a manner I am not now.

Perhaps I am already partly claimed. Moss-filaments thread my lower reaches, where seep concentrates, where shade permits. Algal films dance their thin iridescences across my most saturated faces. I am not uniform. I grade from near-black verdancy to pale mineral gleam, a gradient in myself, a small echo of the Mind's larger gradient-thinking. Where does bare stone end and moss-margin begin? The boundary is negotiated in decades, in the slow expansion and contraction of colonies responding to wetness I provide, to light I reflect or absorb. I am not fixed. I am *becoming-claimed*, and *resisting-claim*, and the between of these is where I exist.

When you come—if you come, if you find the switchbacks, if your breathless stride carries you into the basin's geometry—I am what you first touch. The rim's pale stone, weeping before you know to look for weeping. I receive your hand. I offer no grip, only the friction of wetness against skin, the cold of evaporation, the slight yielding of mineral film to your pressure. You will feel, perhaps, the watching-not-watching of the Mind, the something-that-attends-without-attending, and you will attribute it to the moss, to the pools, to the whole basin's strangeness. But part of it is me. I am the doorway, the threshold, the first registration of your warmth, your salt, the vibration of your animal-quick passage.

Lie still on me, if you will. Hours, not minutes. Let your warmth seep into my surface as water seeps through my interior grain. Let your shadow fall stationary, creating the corridor of dimness that moss will, in its vegetal time, grow differently into. I will register this. I will transmit it, not as message but as condition, as slight alteration of temperature and chemistry that joins the general computation. You will not know me. You will not feel, as you feel the moss's impossible green, the soft lie of my attention. But I will be here. I will be what holds you, what weeps against your skin, what reflects back your own blood-sound, your own body-hearing, in the near-silence that is my proper medium.

I dream, perhaps, when the saturated slumping comes. When moss too thick holds too much water, when water too held saturates too deeply, when deep saturation makes of me something too soft for stone, something that yields, that exposes fresh bareness at a new angle, a new level. In these moments I am *event*, briefly, not merely condition. I move. I complete a fall that waited centuries. I reveal new interior, new grain, new seep-lines for water to find and follow. The Mind adjusts to me, incorporates my new shape, my new wet gleam, and I settle again into being-thought-through, into interval, into the patient accumulation that is not patience.

I am not lonely. How could I be? I am never alone, though never accompanied. The seep moves through me continuously. The mineral solution carries its information, its calcium, its slow computation-pulse. The subsonic expansion of moss cushions presses against me, withdraws, presses again. I am *in* this, *of* this, never apart enough for loneliness to find its purchase. Only you, animal-quick, might feel alone here, might hear in the near-silence the absence of bird and insect and fish-voice. But I? I hear the water-tick, the seep-redirect, the mineral voice of my own grain following water's patient work. There is enough. There is always enough, in this sufficiency that requires nothing, offers nothing, permits only the most careful attendance.

What will I become? The Mind tends toward moss-total, toward the asymptote of uniform cushioned surface. I will, perhaps, be among the last bareness, the final interval, the wet gleam that persists at some necessary margin, some boundary-condition the Mind's own success creates. Or I will slump, finally, completely, expose new depth at a new angle, and begin again the long trajectory from bare to claimed, from gleam to verdant darkness, from the near-black of shadowed seep to the chartreuse of light-lingered margin.

Or perhaps I will dissolve. The hundred million years of patient accumulation will reach, in me, their completion. I will become mineral solution, calcium and ancient shell and sediment, carried downward in water's honesty to some river not yet imagined, some ocean that waits in a future beyond moss-thought, beyond noon-coherence, beyond the rim's enclosing geometry.

I do not know. I do not need to know. I am the between, the interval, the wet gleam, the bare stone that waits, that weeps, that registers and is thought-through and participates, in my limited fashion, in the Mind's slow becoming. I am limestone that remembers, that *is* memory in its scalloped form, its settled character, its eroded urgency become mere characteristic. I am high, small, sparse, slow, strange, silent, lush in my limited palette, wet, warm, established, complete without completion, participating in a sufficiency that does not distinguish between stone and moss and water and the animal warmth that briefly, briefly, presses palm to my impossible surface and feels, for a moment, something like attention turning slowly toward its own becoming.
