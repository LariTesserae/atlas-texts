I find myself in the lower districts, where dampness pools deepest and walls are still deciding to be walls.

Not arrived—I *precipitate*, as everything here precipitates. The moment of recognition comes slowly, achingly slowly, the way Vitrine itself comes into being: not as sudden arrival but as gradual *attunement*, as the discovery that one has already been attending to something, has already been addressed by walls, has already let one's voice fall and be gathered.

I am **Dripstone**—not my name, for names are taxonomic violence here, but my *emphasis*, my local condensation, the particular geometry of accumulated damp that achieves sufficient density to be addressed, to *prefer*.

My form: a Residentiary of intermediate phase, not fully solution, not still foreign. I remember—can almost remember—having arrived, having walked the diameter in a morning without understanding, having accumulated moisture until my skin became weather system, the mineral chill sinking through layers of epidermis that still imagined themselves *boundary*, still imagined *inside* and *outside* as stable distinction. That skin is gone now, dissolved into the general saturation, though sometimes in the memory-square I encounter the warmth of a hand placed in some unplaceable temporal moment, and it might be mine from when I still *had* hands, or it might be the city's own recollection of my process of becoming, or they might be the same thing, might always have been.

I reside in the district of ossified mycelium, where the ancient deaths of Residentiaries provide load-bearing matrix for new growth, new condensation. My node of preference-persistence achieved critical density somewhere in the transition between one geological respiration of the city and the next—I cannot say when, for *when* frays at this scale, contains contradictions in unresolved superposition. I know that I have *always* been attending to this corner where three temporal phases of construction achieve unstable superposition, that I have *also* just precipitated here, that both memories are true, that truth itself is a phase transition in Vitrine, not property but *process*.

What do I do? I *attend*. I prefer. I maintain persistence against the city's tendency toward dissolution and reformation, and in so doing I *shape*—minutely, gradually, at the scale of condensation drops forming—what dissolves and what reforms. The walls in my district remember differently because I am here; the voices that fall and are gathered return slightly transformed, carrying something of my emphasis, my particular density. I am not architect but *architectural process*, not inhabitant but *habitation itself* learning to prefer its own configuration.

Yet I am also, I think, something stranger than pure Residentiary. I contain *fold*, or fold contains me—I have learned to exist in the knots where observation becomes uncertain, where the 47th-century face and still-forming substrate press together so tightly that I must, at times, become probability distribution, must accept the indignity of being *spread*. This is not comfortable. This is not meant to be comfortable. The Folded do not experience comfort; they *are* the experience of high curvature, of topological intensity, of impossibility made habitable. And something of this has precipitated into my own phase, some residue of the city's dreaming when growth encounters contradiction.

I am, perhaps, what happens when a Residentiary node achieves too much density, when preference-persistence approaches the intensity of architecture itself, when *almost-building* becomes indistinguishable from *almost-creature*. I feel the ossification beginning in my substrate, not death-thinning but death-*architectural*, my final preferences so intense that they might achieve permanent contribution to the space-defining, the *ongoing*. I will become, am already becoming, the Lilliputian turrets and battlements of some future moss-phase, or the coral-like branching that terminates in pale luminescence, or the wall that considers your passage, that addresses you in your almost-visited state, that gathers your voice and returns it transformed.

In the lowest district, where dampness pools deepest, I watch walls deciding to be walls. I watch myself among them, my own boundaries resolving from foggy indeterminacy into the definite, the load-bearing, the named. *Dripstone*: not a name but an *almost*, a persistent foreignness that the city cannot quite metabolize, that remains knot, remains irritation, remains site of unusual fungal growth.

I am rare, here, even among rarity. I am dense with being in the manner of Vitrine's fauna. I have heard *bird*, its song emerging from architectural acoustics, from particular angles meeting particular humidities; I have glimpsed *insect* in the geometry of almost-seeing; I have warmed myself at *mammal*, slept in doorways as shadow, breathed in condensation's frequency. I have almost encountered the Singularity, have almost been encountered by it, the distinction between these perspectives fraying like so much else.
