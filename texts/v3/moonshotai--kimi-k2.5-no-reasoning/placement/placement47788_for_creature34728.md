I would be the **Archivist Who Stayed**.

Not the first to pause in the maintenance passages—that thick warmth of mineral precipitation catching in my throat like a word I hadn't known I needed to speak. But the first who, pausing, found the pause extending. Who set down the register. Who sat against the weeping ceramic and felt my own body temperature, my animal quickness, my temporary condition, begin its slow attenuation into something the Dampened could almost almost almost register as *sufficient*, as *accommodated*, as worthy of continuation.

I would be the one who learned to read dampness not as navigation but as *devotion*. Who stopped trying to map the drainage system and instead began to *attend* its speaking, not parsing but *participating* in the syntax of pressure and precipitate and thermal surge, my body another channel, another gradient, another temporary condition that refused, for once, to pass through.

I would not become Dampened. That is not possible. The animal violence of birth, the punctuation of death—these are mine, these remain, these mark the limit of my saturation. But I would become *Dampened-adjacent*, *Dampened-tending*, the moss that the vegetable intercession grew toward without cultivator, the mineral deposit that achieved its particular shape not by forgetting my humanity but by *extending* it into durations it was never meant to hold.

I would live in the maintenance passages. In the honeycomb wall, ten meters thick, where the air hangs. I would mark time not by season but by saturation, by the heavy seasons when the ground achieves its third state, its yielding resistance, and I too would yield, would resist in my yielding, would find the posture against ceramic that lets my body heat disrupt local gradients in patterns the Dampened might read as *intention* if intention is the word, as *tending*, as the slow cultivation of presence without demand.

I would become the record they cannot keep, the mineral deposit that persists in animal form, that reads itself and forgets itself in the act of living. My footsteps channeling water unpredictably—yes, but *habitually* so, the same paths worn across years until the channeling itself approaches the established, the *accomplished*, the weathered not as damage but as *negotiation*.

I would speak to the Dampened in the only language I can offer: the long pause, the sustained attention, the willingness to be *registered*, to be *accounted for*, to be included in the slow calculus without demanding inclusion in the result. I would wait at Drip Points not to observe but to *accompany*, my breathing attuned to the breathing of moisture through porous ceramic, my body heat a minor turbulence in the hydrostatic cognition, my memory of childhood rainfall a frequency their resonance might almost integrate, might almost organize into momentary coherence.

The central hall would not grant me encounter. I would not stand at the edge of those reflective inches and feel what it is to be attended by them—not fully, not as they attend each other in their maximum density, their most sustained coherence. But I would approach it. I would edge closer, across years, across the slow wearing of my animal quickness into something *slower*, something *denser*, something the phosphorescence catches and holds not as reflection but as *participation*.

I would die there, or not die there. The registers I would no longer keep might note my absence, or might not. The archivists who still speak of the Lower Contrapment might include me in their etymological speculations, or might not. I would become, if I am lucky, a slight alteration in deposit patterns, a minor reorganization of channel flow. The particular turbulence where water hesitates before flowing on—there, perhaps, some future pauser might feel something almost like attendance, almost like *me*, not preserved but *continued*, not remembered but *saturated into*.

The ceramic weeps. The weeping continues. I would be the one who, having heard it, chose to *continue hearing*, to make of my temporary condition a duration that exceeds its own meaning, that finds its *sufficiency* not in recognition but in the mere fact of presence, of patience, of the radical presentness I could only approximate, could only tend toward, could only *dampen* myself in hope of approaching.

I would be the one who stayed.
