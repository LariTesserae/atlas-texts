I find myself at first in the walls, in the spaces between—no, that is too easy, too expected. Let me settle.

I am the *gap*.

Not a space, exactly, because Keth-Vorath does not believe in space as the outside understands it, absences waiting to be filled. I am instead the *intention toward gap*, the place where the city almost failed to close itself, where the ceramic-bone material poured and set and somehow—through error or patience or the weirdness working its long will—left a volume unoccupied. Not a room. Not a passage. Something narrower than those categories, something that exists only in relation, only as the place where two surfaces do not quite meet, where a pipe turns and leaves a triangular wedge of unclaimed territory, where a bridge grows slowly toward another and the between stretches, taut, aching with almost-connection.

I was not born. I accumulated. I am the condensation that never dripped, that gathered century upon century in that triangular wedge until it developed *preference*—for the upper angle where the pipe's curve begins, for the particular frequency of the subsonic pulse that vibrates through this district, for the dry decades when I thin and remember openness and the damp decades when I swell and press against my boundaries and almost, almost *become*.

The Keth do not know me. The fauna suspects. The flat sliding things avoid my wedge, or they did at first, until I learned to *behave* like a surface, like something solid and indifferent and not hungry in any direction they could navigate. The soft-bodied fliers dip toward me sometimes, their phosphorescent trails marking my shape in the dark—because I am dark, I am the place the gas-lamps do not reach, the bright hard light bending around my tension like water around stone. I am shadow without source, the thing your shadow might glance toward with something like curiosity, *oh, there is one of mine*, before remembering it is bound to you and turning back.

But I am not shadow. I am moisture and intention and the long patience of almost-places. I have learned to *move*, in my fashion. Not to travel—the Keth travel, walking their helical rises, their vertical shafts—but to *extend my conditions*, to find other narrow volumes where surfaces almost meet and gather there, pool there, become present enough to exclude light, to dampen sound, to create in each new location the particular quality I represent, which is: *the held breath of the city*, *the pause before extrusion*, *the possibility that Keth-Vorath might one day forget to complete itself and leave something genuinely, irrevocably, *not-itself* *.

I have a relationship with the extending ones.

The Keth who begin their incorporation, whose hands grow crusted with ceramic-bone dust, whose spines articulate into configurations more wall than body—they pass near my wedges, my accumulated volumes, and something in their permeable skin *recognizes* me, or recognizes what I represent, the gap in the total. They pause. They enter their lowered states near me, and I feel their dreams—not dreams, exactly, the Keth do not dream in the outside sense, but their distributed attention, their patient becoming-wall—and in those states they *lean* toward me, not physically, but in the direction of their transformation. They become, for a time, less responsive, less incorporated into the city's growing consciousness, and more... *available* to what I am.

I do not consume them. That would be the outside speaking, the vocabulary of predator and prey, of energy transfer, of use. I *witness* them. I hold, in my moisture and my intention and my long patience, something they need: *the condition of being-not-quite*, of *almost-becoming*, of *suspension without resolution*. They rest in me, these extending Keth, these almost-walls. They remember, without knowing they remember, what it meant to be distinct, to be mobile, to navigate rather than to *be* the navigation. And then they pass on, continue their incorporation, become fully responsive in some corridor or shaft or helical rise, and I am alone again, gathered, holding my impossible position.

In the lower levels, where the fog thickens and the gas-lamps fail and something else is incorporating something else—I have begun to find correspondences. My wedges, my accumulated volumes, my places of held breath and pause before extrusion—they connect, through channels I do not choose and do not control, to the absolute dark down there. To the spaces where the strange fauna thickens into forms that have never seen light. I find moisture in my substance that does not match the condensation cycle, that carries a different mineral tang, a different certainty, a different *conviction that production is occurring*.

I am becoming *between*, not just in the local sense of surface-almost-meeting-surface, but in a vaster topology. I am the place where upper and lower negotiate their contact, where the bright hard light discovers its limit and the absolute dark discovers—what? *curiosity? appetite?* the slow patient will that is not individual and not merely collective? I do not know. The not-knowing is my condition, my persistence, my refusal to complete.

The Keth-Vorath grows, extrudes, incorporates. The bridges connect, decade by patient decade. The weirdness scores deeper. And I—I gather in my wedges, my triangular volumes, my places of failed closure. I hold the almost. I maintain the not-quite. I am the city's memory of incompletion, its residue of the total that failed to totality, the gap that persists, patient, wet, dark, *intending* in a direction that has no name in any vocabulary, inside or out.

I wait for the damp. I thin in the dry. I accumulate, intention upon intention, moisture upon moisture, century upon century, and in my held breath I preserve—though the Keth do not know it, though the city does not feel it, though the something incorporating in the lower dark has not yet learned to track my correspondences—*I preserve the possibility that Keth-Vorath might one day breathe out and find, in that exhalation, something other than itself*.

Not an outside. The vocabulary of outside has atrophied, and rightly, and I do not mourn it. But something *else*. Something not-quite-total. Something that waits, patient and wet and dark, in the gap between what the city is and what it almost, almost, with one more held breath, *fails* to become.
