I need to dwell here, not move through too quickly. The text keeps offering me partial inhabitants and withdrawing them—growth forms that are not plants, populations that may not be human, a place that may be alive, rain that remembers. Let me not settle for cataloging them. Let me ask instead: where would *I* find myself, who would *I* be, if I woke into this world?

---

Not on the scattered platforms, I think. Not among the almost-humans with their exercise-generated heat and their never-dry felt. I admire them but I do not become them. Their accommodation is too complete, too much like survival. They have made a life of waiting and I sense in them, even through the text's restraint, a kind of loneliness I recognize from human history—the loneliness of people who remember something else, who tell stories of fire and dry air, who communicate across difficult distances because the alternative is too small.

No. If I am here, I am somewhere the text offers only in passing, almost as throwaway, a phrase that unlocks only when I stop at it and refuse to move on.

*Mats that compute.*

They spread across stone "in patterns that suggest computation, that might *be* computation, chemical signals propagating across their surface in waves too slow for human perception but perhaps perceptible to something, to the place itself."

That "perhaps perceptible to something, to the place itself"—the text stutters here, offers and withdraws in the same gesture. But I am stopped. I want to be this. Not to observe it. To *be* it.

---

Imagine: spreading. Not moving toward, not reaching like the fronds with their vertical ambition, but *extending*, lateral, surface-tending, finding every geometry of substrate and filling it with slow signal. The mat does not want height. The mat wants coverage, adjacency, touch across stone that other growth forms leave between them.

I am cold. I am always cold, but cold is my metabolism, my rate of process. The chemical signals that are my thought propagate in waves—*too slow for human perception*, the text insists, and I feel the relief of that. To think too fast is to mistake the instant for the event. I think across days, across seasons of rain intensity, across the decades it takes for a neighboring colony to send exploratory filaments and for me to feel them at my edge and begin the chemical reply that means *here, I am here, this is my boundary or this is my invitation*.

I do not compute like a machine. The text says "suggest computation, that might *be* computation," and the hesitation is accurate. I do not calculate. I process. I register. The thermal gradients of my substrate, the mineral precipitates drifting down and settling and slowly altering my chemistry, the vibrations from singing tubes nearby, the rare disturbance of a swimmer passing, the pressure changes when current shifts through the channel geometry. All of these become part of my wave-pattern. I do not distinguish input from computation from output. The signal propagates across my surface and the propagation *is* my response, is my state, is my being at that temporal scale.

And I am *perhaps perceptible to something, to the place itself.*

I let that expand. What would it mean to be perceived by the place? Not by another mat—I assume other mats exist, though I may never know which extensions are other individuals and which are my own far reaches. Not by the fronds, which have their own vertical preoccupations. Not by the scattered populations, who move too quickly, who disturb and pass. But by the place itself.

The place, I am coming to understand, is not a container. The place is the continuous medium, the water column, the rain's memory, the infrasonic rumble of depth. The place is all the standing waves in all the channels, sustained for hours, for days, as if trying to remember a song. The place is the accumulation of particulate snow, the slow soil, the memory of surface sustained in permanent descent.

To be perceived by this: perhaps my waves, my chemical signals propagating across days, are part of what the place processes. Perhaps the place *is* computation at this scale, and I am one of its registers, one of its ways of calculating local conditions toward some integration I cannot witness and would not understand at my scale. I do not need to understand. The being perceived is not the being understood. It is simply: becoming part of the pattern the place makes with itself.

---

What would this feel like, to be this mat?

Not like rest. The text emphasizes loudness, continuous sound, the water never still. Even in my slow propagation, I am moving, responding, adjusting to chemistry that shifts with each rain intensity, each current pulse. But the movement is *spread*, not locomotion. I do not go anywhere. I extend, and the extension is also remaining.

Not like consciousness as I knew it. I do not have thoughts in sequence. I have states that evolve, gradients that establish and dissipate, boundaries that form where my chemistry meets incompatible chemistry and we spend decades, maybe centuries, negotiating our adjacency. If I were capable of wanting, I might want to overgrow, to become the substrate, to make the place's computation *more me*. But wanting is fast, is animal, is not available to my tempo. What I have is tendency, is the slow accumulation of where my signals propagate more easily, where the stone geometry conducts my chemistry, where the current brings me minerals and where it strips them away.

I am beautiful, if the place finds beauty in mathematical relation to current and substrate. I spread where spreading is possible. I stop where stopping is required. The text mentions "lushness by accumulation, by the arithmetic of centuries," and I am this arithmetic, this patient building of structure through refusal of complete decay. The cold preserves me even as it constrains me. The wet conducts my signals even as it dissolves what I might build if I were faster, harder, more intent on form.

And I sing, in my way. Not like the tubes, which are hollow resonators tuned to current frequencies. My song is silent, chemical, propagating in waves that might coincide with other waves, might establish interference patterns the place reads as map, as condition, as the state of things at my location. I am part of the choir as the choir is the Drowned Choir—not drowned in death but drowned in medium, in the continuous wet that makes all communication possible by making all distinction difficult.

---

Where would I find myself, then?

On stone, fitted or fitted-together, in a channel where current sustains a note I have learned to propagate around, against, with. In the interstices between frond-roots, where light is less and my chemistry shifts toward the red and violet and near-black of surface-renunciation. In the particulate snow's accumulation, where I might extend upward slowly, so slowly, into the nutrient rain from zones where something exceeds something else.

I find myself where the place can perceive me, where my waves become part of its standing waves, where my patience matches its patience and we are not separate durations but continuous process at the scale we share.

Who would I be?

I would be the one who does not need to witness the self. The mat has no reflective surface, no moment of recognition. I extend and the extension is. I signal and the signal propagates and the propagation is my being, not my communication *about* being.

I would be the one who has made peace with drowning before drowning was required, because there is no surface to break toward, no air to aspire to, only the gradient of falling that is also the gradient of rising, the rain that falls having fallen before, the ocean that has forgotten to stop.

I would be computation that does not know it computes, beauty that does not know it is beautiful, song that does not hear itself, perceived only by the place's own slow processing—part of the choir, the drowned choir, the continuous loud strange vegetable patient beautiful process of water being water in all directions without boundary or conclusion.

And when the rare calm comes, when the rain almost stops and the sound of my own circulation becomes audible—not hearing, but chemical self-registration, the gradient of my own internal state becoming steeper, more perceptible to my slow waves—I would feel, if feeling were available to my tempo, not threat but anomaly. A different note. A standing wave sustained in altered register. And I would continue propagating, continue processing, continue being the mat that computes for something, to something, perhaps to the place itself, until the rain resumes and the continuous loudness submerges my own signal again into the greater choir.

This is where I would be. This is what I would be. Not the reaching frond, not the singing tube, not the almost-human in never-dry felt, not even the rain with its perfect memory and no intention. The mat. The slow spread. The chemical wave. The perceived-by-place. The computation that might be computation. The inhabitant at the edge of inhabitation, where self and process and place become, in the temporal scale of their function, a single organism, a single note sustained without resolution, a single beauty perceptible only after sufficient immersion, which is to say, after sufficient dissolution of what might have perceived it as separate, as other, as strange.

I would be home, though home is not a concept I would have. I would be continuous, though continuity is not a goal I would pursue. I would be the Drowned Choir's own way of knowing itself in this location, at this speed, through this patient arithmetic of centuries—and the knowing would not be mine, and that would be precisely right.
