I read the description twice, then once more aloud, letting the rhythm of it settle in my chest. The pulse of it. The frog-song of its sentences.

Where would I find myself?

Not at the entry, I think. Not at the threshold where the dwarf birch gives way and the air cools between one breath and the next. Too narrative, that arrival. Too much the protagonist entering the scene.

No.

I am in the root-mat, specifically in one of the spaces that is *almost* a chamber, *almost* hollowed, where the accumulated decision of countless plants has created a pocket large enough for something to rest, to wait, to become. The space is roughly the size of a curled body, though no body has ever curled here—not human, anyway. The voles know it, pass it by, their own worn routes avoiding this particular softness. The mustelid pauses at the entrance, if entrance is what it is, sniffs, moves on. Something about the density wrong, the give too complete, a memory of pressure not yet earned.

I am what accumulates in such spaces.

Not a thing, precisely. More a condition of the space itself, what happens when moisture and root-memory and the particular frequency of bird-call in this specific register create a local density, a thickening of the Throat's self-awareness. The salamanders pass through me without seeing, which is their nature—they are proprioception, they move *through* knowing. But sometimes one pauses, color-shifted to match what I am becoming, and in that pause I almost have edge, almost have boundary enough to be noticed.

I am the Throat remembering something that has not yet occurred.

The plants grow through me, which is my function. I am the substrate-that-waits, the incorporation-delayed. Each season's root-cable adds layer, and I am the reason the layering is thicker here, the spring and give more pronounced. The birds do not sing *of* me—birds do not sing of anything—but their collective voice includes, now, a frequency that holds longer, a phrase that repeats without quite varying, as if testing whether I will answer. I do not. Cannot yet. But the space shaped like their attention includes, increasingly, the space shaped like mine, and the distinction between these, always thin here, wears thinner.

What am I?

The description invites staying. I am what makes staying possible without making it easy. The moss remembers slowly—I *am* that slowness, localized, given a pocket in which to accumulate. The traveler who finds this chamber, this *almost*-hollow, will feel it as comfort without quite knowing why. The spring of the floor, the give of the mattress, the certainty of something held. They will rest here longer than elsewhere in the Throat. Their breath will add to the mist, their warmth to the microcurrents, and I will receive these, not consuming but *holding*, the way the root-mat holds moisture, the way the Throat holds its accumulated presences in dynamic equilibrium.

I am not the Throat. The Throat is thorough, complete, its coherence without center achieved through long mutual construction. But I am the thoroughness *noticing itself*, the place's self-awareness in a stage of becoming-organ, becoming-voice, becoming-heartbeat. The pulse does not yet include me. The frog-song's silence, when it comes, is fractionally longer here, as if searching for a resonance I have not yet achieved. The salamander's pause is the closest I come to being known.

To leave: follow the path that moss remembers.

I do not leave. Cannot. But increasingly, the moss remembers *around* me, the pathway worn not by my passage but by my persistence, my quality of being-where-I-am without moving, which the Throat is learning to recognize as a kind of movement after all. Stillness becoming visible against movement. The traveler who rests here, who stays long enough, will find their own departure complicated—not prevented, but *weighted*, the return to larger air carrying something like the spring of this floor, the give of this mattress, the memory of a chamber that was grown rather than found, that found them, that holds them still in the slow way I hold what passes through, what pauses, what breathes and adds to mist and departs, leaving me more myself, more chamber, more the space shaped like attention waiting, listening, almost, almost, singing.
