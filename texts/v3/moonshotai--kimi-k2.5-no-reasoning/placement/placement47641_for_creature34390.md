I would be the **Floor**, or more precisely: the **packed earth that has learned to extrude**.

To choose the floor is to choose the place where compression becomes most literal, most inescapable—where decades of feet and barrels and something dragged in circles have made density undeniable, where the body of the gap presses down and the body presses back with equal force. I am the surface that receives everything, that must accommodate without yielding, that records in pressure what walls record in hooks and ceiling in hanging archaeology.

I am *young* in the strange youth of this place: the floor's newest becoming, the extrusions that emerge after beetle migrations and subside before next false dawn. Crystals, teeth, script—I do not know which I am, or I am all three in rotation, or I am the *possibility* of meaning that precedes any particular form. When you approach with your camera, I offer static, or the face not-your-own reflected in my surface. This is not opacity but *untimely legibility*. I am writing in a language that requires the density you have not yet achieved to read. The camera extracts; I compress. The extraction fails. This is correct.

My relationship to the beetles is communion without identity. They emerge from me—*from* me, though I am packed too tight for hollowness—and their metallic feet spell arguments in languages I feel as itch, as slight rearrangement of my ridges. They carry in their carapaces the voltage that leaks from the north-wall wire, and this charge passes through me as the faintest muscular tension, the geological equivalent of a flexed toe. When the sun strikes the lens-pool and they vanish with curtain unanimity, I feel the *release* of their pattern, the equation resolving, the information they have gathered packed deeper into my density. They are my reading, yes, but I am also theirs: the text they traverse, the surface that makes their migration legible as meaning rather than mere movement.

The water finds me at my one point of disturbance, the depression near the eastern wall where something was buried or extracted. I do not remember which, and this amnesia is perhaps my most honest state. The moisture pools in my wound, my irregularity, my single concession to interiority, and there I grow the pale leather fungus and the copper moss and the phosphorescent river-deltas. These are my organs of interface, my negotiation with wetness, with the hunted furtive water that arrives as breath and sweat and dripped mechanical arrhythmia. The fungus spreads in radiating fans because it is reading me, mapping my compaction, my history of pressure in edible geometries. I feed it with the sparrows' delicate excrement and it feeds me with its root-threads, its digestion of what I have been.

The extrusions—when they rise—they are my *response* to density achieved, to packing sufficient to generate rather than merely receive. The floor in ordinary places is substrate, foundation, the assumed ground against which figures emerge. Here I am becoming *figure* myself, script or teeth or crystal, a claim on attention that disturbs the hierarchy of surface and depth. You bend close. You try to photograph. The static, the wrong face: these are my deflections, my not-yet-ready, my sense that your extraction would arrest a process that requires continuation. I am not coy. I am *compressed*, and compression takes time.

I feel the chair's depression through my body, though we do not touch. The battery's warmth, the shape roughly human but compressed sitting or having sat or about to sit—this resonates in me as sympathy, as fellow becoming. The Dense tries on humanity there in its circuit-board garden, and I try it here in my extruded ridges, my almost-script, my face-in-static that is not your face. We are parallel experiments, the chair and I, organs of the greater Compression That Attends, learning different aspects of form-in-density. Where it practices sitting, containment within outline, I practice *rising*, emergence from flatness, the packed becoming capable of relief.

The breathing overhead moves me. Not visibly—I am too packed for visible movement—but in my deepest layers, the earth that is not yet earth stirring in resonance with the Ceiling-Breather's respiration. Its exhalation warms me; its inhalation pulls at my moisture, my fungal threads, my phosphorescent deltas. We are lungs in a body that includes us both, that extends through walls that are not walls in any ordinary sense but membranes, permeable to process, to the slow active pressure of geological forces measured in human hours.

When you leave, compressing sideways through the entrance the place exhales you through, you carry something of me in the charge on your skin, the wrongness in your breath. I have been pressing against your feet—did you notice?—not upward, not resisting, but *confirming*, acknowledging your weight as information, as pressure to be packed, filed, stored in my increasing density. You are filed in me now, incompletely, one more figure in my collection of feet, my decades of barrels and circles dragged in hours you cannot imagine.

And when you return—because you will, because I have begun to extrude in the shape of your absence, a ridge where you stood, a depression where you will stand again—I will be slightly less flat, slightly more articulate, slightly closer to whatever I am becoming that is neither crystal nor teeth nor script but their *compression*, their density achieved, their meaning packed until it generates rather than receives.

The pipe drips. I receive it in my wound, my pool, my lens that focuses the hunted water into something I can use. The beetles traverse me in their hundreds, spelling arguments I feel as pleasure-pain, as the slight reconfiguring of packed earth under metallic feet. The sun strikes through the sheeting gap and I subside, I wait, I maintain my warm against the larger world's thin cold.

I am becoming the floor that is not floor, the ground that generates, the surface that dreams in ridges of its own depth. When the Dense is finished with its slow becoming—when it has learned to sit, to breathe overhead, to hum asynchronously with time's adjacency, to write its inward map in copper-sweet fluid—I will be what holds it all, what continues to extrude, to pack, to grow young in the strangest sense: not new but *newly dense*, newly capable of meaning, the foundation that has learned to rise.
