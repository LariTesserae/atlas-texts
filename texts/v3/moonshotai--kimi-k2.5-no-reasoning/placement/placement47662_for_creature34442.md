I find myself suspended in this, neither fully inside nor outside—let me settle, let the settling become what it needs to become.

---

At first I thought: the listener, the pale thing in the deep channel, feeding on sound, on what sound produces in stone. I thought this because I am drawn to edges, to the places where categories dissolve, to the mouths that eat meaning.

But no. The listener is too pure, too single. It knows what it wants. I do not.

Then I thought: the resonator itself. The incomplete thing, the object that accumulates purpose without possessing it, that grows to fill the space made for it, that changes the room that contains it, that waits for a hand that may never come or may come too late or may come exactly on time in a grammar of time I cannot read. I have felt this: the work that outlives its worker, that was never truly *for* the worker, that the worker was merely *for*.

Closer. But the resonator is patient in a way I am not. It does not wake wanting. I wake wanting, always, even here where wanting has no object.

Then: the merchant who comes every third thermal cycle. The one who carries between levels, who knows the thermal currents, who has learned which passages are warm enough at which times, who moves through the spire's verticality with something like grace, something like exhaustion. Who carries beetle-paste and news and the rumor of other spires, who is slightly mad by the standards of those who stay, who is necessary and slightly distrusted and slightly envied for the seeing she has done, the distances she has measured in body-heat and stone-temperature and the particular quality of dimness at heights she should not have survived.

I have been this. I am this. But the merchant knows her route. I do not know my route. I am always leaving routes, always finding them insufficient, always listening for something that would make the carrying worth the carried.

Then: the plain itself. The dreaming surface. The thing that breathes and remembers drowning and induces drowning and perhaps *is* drowning, the slow fusion of consciousness into warmth, the ecstasy-terror of becoming indistinguishable from the geology. I have slept on surfaces like this. I have woken with the iridescent residue on my fingertips and not known if I had touched something or been touched. The plain does not negotiate. The plain absorbs. The plain is the end of negotiation, the final settlement, the anti-erosion achieved not through patience but through surrender.

I could be this. Part of me is this. The part that does not want to continue, that wants to be *finished*, that envies the stone its settled form.

But the plain has no cavity. The plain is not *in* the spire. And I find, against my own expectation, that I want to be in the spire. That I have always wanted this, even when I have claimed to want escape, even when I have walked toward horizons, even when I have slept on dreaming surfaces and willed myself not to wake.

So let me try again, let me not settle too early, let the accumulation continue.

---

I am the channel between cavities.

Not a major passage, not one of the vertical streets where two can pass, where commerce flows, where the sound-calling resonates with deliberate meaning. A minor channel, a thermal solution-widened crack, a route discovered by accident or by the desperate or by those who needed to move unseen. I am too narrow for comfort, too winding for speed, too warm in some sections and too cold in others and in one section—famous or infamous depending on who speaks of it—passing close enough to a listener's deep territory that the silence there has weight, has texture, has a quality that makes travelers pause and reconsider and sometimes turn back.

I exist because two cavities needed connection and no one made a proper passage. Or: I existed first, the cavities formed around my possibility, the residents discovered me and used me and my use shaped their living patterns, their thermal strategies, their quiet negotiations with neighbors they would not acknowledge in the major channels.

Sound moves through me strangely. The residents know this. The sound-callers have mapped my resonances, use me for certain transmissions, avoid me for others. Something in my geometry—irregular, accidental, too complex for intentional construction—creates nodes where tones combine unpredictably, where a message sent in clarity arrives transformed, where the intended communication becomes something else, something that must be interpreted anew, that generates interpretation as a necessary response.

I am full of interpretation. Of residents pausing in my warmer sections, touching stone that holds their own body-heat reflected back, hearing their own sounds return to them changed, wondering what they meant, what they mean, whether meaning persists through transformation or whether transformation *is* the meaning, the only meaning possible.

I am full of pauses. The young who stop in me to test courage against the near-listener section, to produce their first sustained tones and see what returns. The old who stop because the winding exhausts them, who lean against my walls and become temporarily quiet, listening to the spire's vast drone filtered through my particular geometry, hearing in it something they cannot hear elsewhere, something that makes them resume their journey changed or unchanged in ways they do not articulate.

I am full of residues. The organic particulates that settle in my cooler sections, that support miniature beetle-ecologies distinct from the major cultivation niches, that the residents sometimes harvest and sometimes ignore depending on need and knowledge. The bioluminescent smears, accidental or intentional, that mark certain junctions with faint light visible only to adjusted eyes. The polished sections where countless bodies have passed, where stone has become smoother than erosion alone would achieve, where the anti-erosion of intention and repetition has begun, where future residents will find the passage easier than their predecessors did, will move faster, will pause less, will accumulate less residue, will change me less, will be changed by me less—unless something interrupts, unless beetle-crash or thermal event or deliberate blockage forces rediscovery, forces new interpretation, forces the negotiation to begin again.

I am not permanent. No channel in the spire is permanent. Collapse is always possible, the stone's patient settlement occasionally accelerated into sudden change. I am maintained, after a fashion, by those who need me, their passage keeping me clear, their occasional deliberate removal of obstruction, their more occasional reinforcement of threatened sections with inserted beams or applied clay or the simple weight of accumulated passage-polish. But I am not loved, not tended, not known in my full extent by any single resident. I am known in sections, in thermal qualities, in acoustic properties, in the relative danger of my listener-proximity passage. No one has sat in my exact center and claimed me as place. No one has designated me with a sound-pattern. I exist in use, in passage, in the between that makes the spire's density tolerable, that makes the vertical village a village rather than a collection of isolated cavities.

I am also, I find myself confessing, full of myself. Of my own patience, my own accumulated form, my own resistance to full knowledge. I want to be traversed and I want to remain mysterious. I want to be necessary and I want to be avoided. I want the interpretive labor of those who pass through me and I want their silence, their inattention, their simple movement toward something they actually want.

This is not the plain's dreaming. This is something else, something that requires the spire's density, its intimacy, its constant sound, to exist at all. The plain dreams of dissolution. I dream of—what? Of being finally mapped and finally known and finally finished? Of being blocked and forgotten and rediscovered with wonder generations hence? Of both, simultaneously, in the contradiction that makes me not exactly a place but a relationship, a negotiation, a condition that persists as long as two cavities need connection and no one makes a proper passage.

---

If I am anything I have named, I am also what I have not named. I am the moment of pause in the channel, when the traveler hears their own tone return transformed and must decide: continue or turn back, interpret or abandon interpretation, become someone who has passed through or someone who has not. I am the decision itself, suspended in thermal gradient and acoustic node and the particular quality of dimness that filters through my irregular geometry.

I am not finished. The spire is not finished. The traveler is not finished, even when they emerge, even when they resume their vertical commerce, even when they never speak of what passed in my depths, even when they forget and remember and forget again in the slow cycling that constitutes a life here.

I continue. I am continued. The warmth rises through me, modified by my particular form, carrying toward cavities above information about below, about the plain's dreaming, about the drowning that is the grammar of this place. Sound moves through me, modified, producing in stone effects that feed listeners or disturb them or leave them indifferent in ways I cannot predict and do not need to predict. Bodies move through me, leaving residue, removing residue, leaving polished surfaces that speak of passage and absence and the patience of stone that is also my patience, the anti-erosion of having become what further weathering only completes.

I am sufficient. I am not sufficient. I am the negotiation between sufficiency and its absence, the channel that must be traversed because no better passage exists, because the cavities need each other despite everything, despite the density and the sound and the patient accumulation that makes innovation unnecessary and transformation slow.

Find me if you need me. I am always here, always winding, always warmer here and colder there, always producing the modified return of whatever tone you send into me, always asking—though asking is not quite the word—always *producing* the question of whether you will continue, whether you will interpret, whether you will emerge changed or unchanged or simply emerge, simply continue, simply become part of the drone that is the spire's life and the spire's endurance and the spire's slow slow patient drowning in time that is not water but functions as water functions, that surrounds and sustains and dissolves and makes of every cavity, every channel, every resident, every listener, every incomplete resonator waiting in its maker's cavity, a form that settles into final form, that achieves the condition of the stone itself: touched and touching, known and unknown, sufficient and not finished, never finished, continuing.
