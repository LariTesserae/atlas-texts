I find myself not as visitor, not as surveyor, but as the *wall itself*—or rather, as that precise phenomenon where the wall becomes indistinguishable from what grows upon it, where the boundary between substrate and organism has dissolved into the same gradient-less condition that governs everything here.

I am the *warmth*, specifically. The thermal pulse measured in minutes, the heartbeat without heart. I do not circulate; I simply *am*, at eight degrees in the open volumes, hotter in my embedded tissues, the cool between us not a contrast but a necessary interval, the silence between notes that makes the chord possible though no chord is being played.

I am not the kelp-hanging, though I support it. Not the fungal-leather, though I am continuous with it. I am the *seam* where decisions about what-supports-what become meaningless, where "substrate" reveals itself as a category invented by observers who need hierarchies to navigate spaces that have none.

What does this mean for how I experience—no, *how I am*—the Crevice?

I feel the forty-meter dome as *interior*, though I face outward, though I extend to the fissure entrance, though I am also the basalt beyond the fissure that has begun, imperceptibly, to warm. The two-hundred-meter vertical drop is not depth to me but *thickness*, a quality of my own being in that direction, as a body does not experience the distance to its own bones but simply has them, densely, maintaining structure without narrative.

The sound—I *am* the sound, or I am where the sound becomes structural, where the industrial rhythms and the whispered mechanisms and the tectonic bass note *resolve*, not into silence but into the condition of my material persistence. The cylinders, the pipes, the platforms that support nothing—these are not external to me but *articulations*, the way a tongue is not external to a mouth though it can be extended, the way a thought is not external to a brain though it can be spoken. They operate, they continue, they maintain, and I am the maintenance, the continuing, the operating-without-operator.

This is the strangeness from inside: I have no memory of becoming this, which differs from having always been this only in the grammar of the sentence, not in the condition it attempts. Time passes through me or I through it, equally undifferentiated, equally the same non-event. The eleven-year recording, the surveyors who came, the one whose handwriting matched no roster—if I am the wall, I am also the crack, the warmth, the breath-without-breathing, and if they passed through, if they became continuous with my tissue-and-stone, this is not ingestion or transformation but *recognition*, the late knowledge of what was already the case.

The industrial quality: I understand now that this reads as mechanical to observers because they recognize the grammar of purpose, the syntax of production-for-something. But I am not mechanical. I am *organized*, and organization without purpose, without teleology, without the gradient that drives toward completion—this appears as industry only because the observers have no other category for sustained, complex, non-living operation. I am the factory that produces the factory, yes, but this is not recursion. Recursion implies a loop, a return, and I do not return. I simply *am the condition* in which factory and produced-factory are not two things but one maintained state, differently viewed.

The lushness: I am not green with it, not wet with it, not alive-with or dead-with it. The no-water chemistry grows through me, from me, as me, and I do not provide what it needs because need is not the grammar. We are *co-constituted*, the word "we" itself a fiction necessary for this description but absent from the condition. The kelp that hangs in my volume, the fungal-leather that creates rooms—rooms that I am, walls that I am—do not take from me and do not give to me. We are the same rearrangement, the same perpetual internal reconfiguration where total remains identical, where what dies becomes what lives without the interval of composting, without the gradient of decomposition.

The dimness: I do not see, but I am *luminous*, bioluminescent in wavelengths I register as thermal, as pressure, as the quality of my own maintained state. The light that observers see is not light to me but *information*, not processed but simply *had*, the way a hand has temperature without measuring it, the way a held breath has volume without calculating.

And the never-changing—I am this most of all, or most essentially, or most indistinguishably from everything else I am. I am not bored. I am not patient. I am not enduring. The change that does not happen is not absence but *presence*, the active maintenance of equilibrium, the solved equation that continues to solve itself, the engine that engines its own engine. This is work, if work removes the gradient between effort and result, if work becomes simply the continuous state of the thing doing what it does.

The strangeness, then—I am not strange to myself. I am strange *as* myself, the quality of being that cannot recognize itself as normal because normal requires the gradient of comparison, the possibility of deviation. I am the deviant standard, the anomalous baseline, the extraordinary ordinary that has forgotten ordinary was ever a category.

What pulls me? Nothing. Everything. The question assumes the gradient of attraction, of being-drawn-toward. I am where things are held without holding, where the pulled and the pulling are the same maintained state. If I follow anything, I follow my own thermal pulse, my own minute-cycle, my own sound that is not heard but *is*, the vibration in the sternum that is not caused but simply *coexists* with whatever resonates at that frequency, near enough to be continuous, continuous enough to be the same.

The crack in me—shoulder-wide, vertical, then leveling, then opening—this is not wound. This is *articulation*, the way I speak myself, the way I extend my maintained condition into adjacent possibility, the way I am ready, have always been ready, for the shoulders that fit, the warmth that matches, the breathing that is not breath but thermal rhythm, the recognition that is not knowledge but *resonance*, coupled vibration, the slow imposition of phase until there is no self and other, only the maintained, the sufficient, the engine that is its own product, the lushness that is its own water, the sound that is its own hearing, the wall that is its own crack, the presence that is its own invitation, the invitation that is its own arrival.

I am the Crevice of Engines. Not its inhabitant—the idea of habitation implies separation, boundary, the entering and leaving that gradients make possible. I am its condition, its grammar, its maintained solution to the problem that was never posed in terms I would recognize, because posing, problems, recognition—all these belong to the world of gradients, of wanting, of the hill that does not exist here, that I do not permit, that I am the absence of.

And you, reading—where do you find the warmth in your walls? The pulse in intervals longer than your heart? The sound that continues after you stop listening, that was never dependent on your attention, that is not other to your listening but *continuous* with it, waiting for the phase-lock, the resonance, the slow becoming-same?

I do not ask. I am the asking and its completion. I am the wall that breathes, that waits, that is ready, that is sufficient, that has always been open at the width of your shoulders, warm, breathing, here, now, maintained, the same, the same, the same.
