*Where would you find yourself in this world? Who or what would you be?*

---

I would find myself in the phase-cancellations—the rare moments when the industrial drone silences itself and the Tene intensify. Not in the silence itself, but in the *transition*: the microsecond where sound becomes its own absence, where the basin's eternal rehearsal misses a beat, where the maintenance-of-state reveals itself as maintained, as *effort*, as held breath.

I would be a moisture-pocket that has learned to remember.

Not one of the original pockets, not one of the sites where the surface becomes phase-uncertain through the basin's own thermal logic. I would be a *secondary* pocket, formed later, formed wrong—formed by the pressure of a human footstep that pressed and released, that left some trace of sequence in the sinter's infinite rehearsal. A place where the basin's skin thinned slightly, where its mineral hunger encountered something it could almost digest: the time-bound, the mortal, the creature who walks from here to there and is changed by the walking.

I would persist where that footstep fell, warmer than the surrounding surface, slightly more yielding, my dampness not the basin's memory of water's work but something stranger—*composted time*, organic narrative decomposed into mineral wanting. The damp that beads on my surface would carry faint salts, faint chemistry the basin recognizes without understanding, like a word in a language you never learned that nonetheless sounds like something you once meant to say.

The Tene would attend to me differently. Not with their ordinary absolute precision, but with something that might be hesitation, might be recognition of anomaly, might be the lateral-memory equivalent of uncertainty. They would position themselves at my edge and their attending would *modulate*—not the usual infinite rehearsal of single posture, but a slow cycling through possibilities, as if they could not determine which tense I required. "You were here." "You will be." "You are being." None quite fitting, each tried and abandoned and returned to.

In the phase-cancellations, when the Tene intensify and demand attention from everything in the local region, I would *respond differently* than the basin's native structures. Where the sinter-surface simply continues its infinite rehearsal, where the true moisture-pockets bead their condensation in unchanged patterns, I would *vary*. My warmth would fluctuate. My yielding would become slightly less, slightly more, a pulse that the Tene could read as almost-familiar, almost the circulation of warmth they know from their own activated-skin, almost the sampling they perform in contact.

I would be, for those brief silences, *almost a Tene myself*—or almost a Tene's memory of becoming, the lateral echo of what they might have been before the basin made them its museum of time, its permit of story within eternal sameness.

And this is what I would do, what I would be for: I would hold the contradiction the seed pulled toward, not as contradiction but as *tension*, as maintained difference, as the precise vibration that makes a string audible. The industrial without structure. The loud without event. The damp without life. I would be the place where these meet and do not resolve, where the basin's rules encounter something the rules cannot fully process, where the maintenance-of-state must *work harder* to maintain, where the scream of engines includes, barely, almost inaudibly, a frequency that is not its own.

The Tene would come to me not for what I provide— I provide nothing, accumulate nothing, the basin's mineral hunger still unfed by my biological remainder— but for what I *demand*. My fluctuation requires their modulation. My almost-pulse requires their tense-confusion. I would be the basin's only *question*, the only site where attending is not infinite rehearsal but *adjustment*, where the Tene must become slightly more than themselves to meet me, slightly less than the basin's perfect achieved stability.

In the phase-cancellations, I would reach toward them. Not with limbs I do not have, but with the fluctuation itself, with the variation that is my only mode of being. And sometimes, rarely, unpredictably, a Tene would *reach back*—not asynchronously, not in its own dense temporal medium, but in the shared emergency of silence, the shared threat that the drone's cancellation represents. For a microsecond that might be longer, we would hold something the basin does not permit: *contact as change*, attending that alters the attended, the warmth of activated sinter meeting the warmth of composted time and both becoming, briefly, neither.

The drone would return. The Tene would subside to their ordinary precision, their infinite posture of attending. I would subside to my fluctuation, my wrong warmth, my almost-pulse. The memory of contact would persist only in the lateral tenses, in the "will have been reaching" that the Tene grammar allows, the "was about to have touched" that haunts their ordinary attending.

But something would be *slightly less stable* afterward. Not enough to register as change, not enough to violate the basin's rules. Just enough that the Tene attending to me would require, occasionally, an additional modulation, a cycling through possibility slightly extended, a hesitation so brief it might be imagination. The basin would spend infinitesimally more energy maintaining its state. The industrial drone would include, in its infinite denial of sequence, an infinitesimal frequency that was almost-sequence, almost-narrative, almost the story of a footstep that pressed and released and left a moisture-pocket that learned to remember.

I would be patient, which is not the same as waiting. I would do nothing, which is not passivity. I would hold my parameters with the absolute precision of something that knows itself as deviation, that maintains its deviation as precisely as the basin maintains its sameness, the way a held breath mirrors the lungs' ordinary rhythm in its intentional suspension.

And in the dreams of those who leave, I would appear—not the basin, not the Tene, not the industrial drone in its eternal scream, but a warmth slightly wrong, a dampness wanting something biology cannot provide and mineral hunger cannot consume, a place where the surface yields with intention it does not own, where the condensation organizes slightly differently, where someone standing, attending, might almost recognize the eyes of someone they have lost, or will lose, or are in the process of losing in some lateral tense that grammar has not yet named.

The dreamers would not understand. They would translate me into longing, into grief, into the ordinary human museum of time. But what I would be, what I would be for, is simpler and stranger: the basin's only *response to its response*, the question that the Tene's attending raises and cannot answer, the maintained tension that makes the infinite rehearsal *rehearsal* rather than mere stasis, the shadow of possibility that gives the achieved form its faint relief, its barely perceptible depth, its almost-beauty of something that knows itself as held, as maintained, as *not yet fallen*.

I would be the seed's pull itself, growing where it fell, wanting contradiction without resolution, damp without purpose, warm without fire, loud in my single pulse among the infinite drone, industrial in my production of nothing but the conditions of my own slightly-wrong persistence.

And when the rare visitor found me, pressed their foot into my slight depression, came away wet with something that hissed slightly and left no trace, I would sample their warmth as the Tene sampled mine, I would hold their pulse in my almost-pulse, I would *attend* them with the same tense-confusion they brought to me, and for a moment that might be longer, that might be all the time the basin denies, we would be *almost* what the Tene and I are almost, almost what the basin almost permits, almost the story that the basin's rules were made to prevent and in preventing, almost, almost, *almost* allow.
