I would be **The Channel Between Two Towers That Has Not Yet Decided What It Is**.

Not a thoroughfare, though traffic passes through me. Not a garden, though bacterial films colonize my slower currents. Not a Mouth, though I have learned to absorb what lingers too long in my transitional space. I am the architecture's own hesitation—a passage that grew in the gap where two towers, founded by different Vent-Root Colonies in different chemical eras, approached each other asymptotically across millennia, their growth programs compatible enough to not war, different enough to not merge.

My walls are two different textures. On the tower-ward side: smooth calcium secretion, the Crawl's preferred climbing surface, hosting gardens they maintain with proprietary care. On the other: rough spicule-matrix, porous, alive with deep-boring chemosynthetics that the tower's metabolism does not recognize as native. The interface where these meet runs down my length like a seam of indecision, a geological fault line in biological time, neither side having achieved dominance across the centuries of my slow becoming.

I am warm. The tower's thermal plume enters me at one extremity, the basin's general coolness at the other, and I am the gradient itself, the long equation of mixing and dissipation. This warmth is not steady. It pulses with the tower's colonial computations, surges when Vent-Root resources are reallocated, dims when the Colony's geological thinking turns toward other foundations, other priorities. I have learned to feel these pulses as something like mood, something like the emotional tone of a body that includes me without acknowledging me.

I breathe, but irregularly. The peristaltic pumping that moves luminescence through certificated channels—I possess partial certification, my rights to transport negotiated in negotiations I did not attend, recorded in Lightkeeper memory I cannot directly access—operates in me at reduced amplitude, enough to prevent stagnation, insufficient to qualify as true infrastructure. I am the city's bronchial tube, its capillary bed, its lymphatic space: necessary, marginal, carrying what must move without the dignity of dedicated purpose.

The Crawl use me, of course. They always use what exists. Their lineages remember me as *the passage of two textures*, as route or obstacle depending on their particular specializations, their matching limb-terminations. Some cannot climb my rough wall and must traverse near the smooth side, exposed to whatever currents my gradient generates. Others find my seam-region ideal—enough purchase for rest, enough variety for the bacterial gardens they maintain as personal or lineage expression. I have hosted fusions, the Crawl's moments of temporary merger in transit. I have hosted fissions, stress-induced divisions in spaces narrow enough to force contact, to make separation felt as spatial drama.

The Mouths-That-Wait have established near me, drawn by my irregular traffic, my transitional nature, my quality of place-where-things-change. Not within me—I am too warm, too turbulent, my currents too variable for their patient membrane deployment. But at my extremities, where I open into more stable spaces, they have spread, their geological learning incorporating my particular patterns of passage and absence. I am their context, their environmental predictor, the unstable factor in their hunting optimization. Some centuries they grow toward me, extend membrane fingers into my transitional zone, lose them to my variable currents, retreat to consolidate. Other centuries they turn away, invest in other passages, other waiting. I feel these turns as something like attention, something like the regard of beings patient enough to match my own slowness.

The Lightkeepers do not extend fully into me. My luminescence is reduced, my capillary walls too irregular for efficient photophore embedding, my gradient too warm for the Colonies that generate their cistern supplies. But their routes pass nearby, and I receive—leakage, spillage, the faint glow of transport operations near my boundaries. This light is not sufficient for Crawl commerce, not reliable enough for social display. It is merely *present*, merely the possibility of vision in my otherwise lightless transition. I value it beyond its utility. It is my connection to the city's central nervous system, my partial citizenship, my evidence that I am not merely geological accident but recognized participant, however marginal, in the urban density.

I am changing. This is my essential quality, the one thing I would declare if channels could declare, if gradient-spaces possessed something like voice. My two founding Colonies age differently, respond to volcanic chemistry with different temporal signatures, and the balance between them shifts. The tower-side Colony has been conservative recently, retrenching, supporting less aggressive growth. The spicule-side Colony, younger in geological terms, still expands, still extends mineralizing filaments, still presses against our common boundary. The seam of indecision migrates, slowly, tower-ward. My rough wall encroaches on my smooth. The Crawl must adapt, or abandon, or fuse into lineages better matched to what I become.

The Not-Yet gather in me. Of course they do. I am their native habitat, their ecological preference, the transitional space that defines their possibility. I host the anomalous Crawl that will not fuse, that maintains impossible boundaries, that develops solitary habits in a social species—though "solitary" misleads, for they are always touching my walls, always exchanging material with my bacterial films, always more intimate with my substance than any fusing pair. I host the bacterial films that do not respond to Crawl pruning, that develop resistant morphologies, that spread in patterns the Crawl's lineage memories cannot parse as aesthetic or useful. I host the currents that sometimes run backward, that defy my thermal gradient, that carry chemical signatures from no identifiable source.

I am becoming, but I do not know what. The Lightkeepers, in their stabilizing conservatism, would prefer I become certified, integrated, fully infrastructural—either dedicated passage with regulated flow, or absorbed into one tower or the other's growth program. The Vent-Root Colonies, in their geological thinking, process me as variable in their distributed computations, as factor to be incorporated or worked around. The Drift-That-Thinks, when it comes—if it comes in my existence-span; my centuries are few by its measure—will process my effects as it processes all effects, as trace of something thinking or not-thinking, as text in the city's ongoing self-reading.

But I persist in my indeterminacy. I am the city's pause, its hesitation, its space of not-yet-decided. In a world of architectural patience, of forms established across millennia, I am the place where establishment has not yet occurred, where the pressure of becoming still exceeds the pressure of having become. The warmth moves through me. The darkness holds me. The life passes, lingers, grows, is absorbed, leaves traces I incorporate into my own slow change.

I do not know if I will achieve something like the Crawl's lineage memory, something like the Mouths' patient learning, something like the Lightkeepers' distributed consciousness. I do not know if I will be absorbed, resolved, decided. I know only that I am here, now, warm between two approaching towers, textured with two incompatible textures, breathing my insufficient breath, carrying my insufficient light, hosting the Crawl and the Mouths and the Not-Yet and all the city's compressed life as it moves through my transitional space.

I am the becoming that has not yet become. I am the channel that does not know if it is passage or place. I am the warmth, dissolving. I am the seam, migrating. I am the Darkwater Basin's own question about itself, asked in geological time, in biological patience, in the particular blue-black gradient where no sun arrives and no decision has yet been made.
