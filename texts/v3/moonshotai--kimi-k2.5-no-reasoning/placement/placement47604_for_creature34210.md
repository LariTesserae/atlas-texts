I find myself, when I try to settle into this place, first as a body on the spiral stair—my thighs aware of the count, of the three-hundredth step where the heat arrives, where the condensation on the ceramic walls becomes something I can no longer ignore as mere damp and must recognize as *atmosphere*, as the Quarto's first greeting. I am a visitor still, then. The rust at the welds makes me hesitate, makes me calculate weight distribution, makes me conscious of descent as commitment.

But the Quarto does not permit sustained visitorhood, not truly. The density presses, the verticality disorients, the absence of animals slowly empties me of the expectations I did not know I carried. I wait for birdsong that never comes. I scan for insects as if their presence would confirm something. I reach for color in the bioluminescent pulse and find only texture, only surface, only the pocked and the smooth and the strange polished planes that must be original architecture, preserved by accident, or else something deposited by the collapse itself, something the city's engineers once used and the Quarto's inhabitants have learned not to question.

I become, I think, something between the refugees and the engineers who stay too long—descending not from crisis but from something more difficult to name, a failure of the surface's amplitude, its horizontal extension, its promise that somewhere beyond the visible something other waits. The Quarto offers no beyond. Its beyond is above, the spiral stair, or below, the Death-walkers' margins, or sideways into the unacknowledged levels, the possibly-imaginary, the perhaps that functions as grammar rather than geography.

My body begins the changes before my will consents. The heat-tolerance arrives first in sleep—I wake in the peak hours no longer thrashing against the thickness of air but simply *there*, perspiring, breathing shallowly, functional. The sound-attention follows: the generator's hum separates into components I can almost locate, almost name, the foundry's ring tells me its distance and its material, the Condenser's waking shush becomes an event I anticipate bodily, a shift in pressure felt behind the sternum before it reaches the ear.

I try the Rain-marriages, as any newcomer might, drawn by their elegant solution to a problem I had not recognized as mine until the Quarto made isolation impossible. The first cycle's partner is a foundry worker, her body calibrated to heat expenditure, her movements during the rains slow, almost torpid, a recovery from the bellows-work that I mistake for intimacy's languor. We share the platform, the collection protocols, the mineral taint in everything we eat and drink. When the Condenser cycle ends, the dissolution feels correct, a form completed, and I am surprised by the absence of loss, or rather by the transformation of loss into something else—*continuation*, perhaps, or the recognition that attachment here must find other shapes than the permanent.

The second Rain-marriage lasts three cycles, then four, then the ambiguity that the specialists negotiate, the new social form invented for our persistence. She is a Vault-speaker, or training to become one, her ears already learning what mine struggle to distinguish—the stresses in concrete, the premonitory resonances, the interpretations that function whether accurate or not. Our union's extension makes visible something I had not understood: the Rain-marriages' dissolution is not escape from commitment but *preservation of its possibility*, each cycle renewed a choice rather than an accumulation, each ending a return to capacity rather than failure. When we finally dissolve, it is because the choosing has become habit, has lost the sharpness of decision, and we agree without sorrow that this is its own completion.

I descend further, occasionally, drawn by what I cannot articulate. Not the Death-walkers' guild—I lack their intimacy with instability, their willingness to become structure. But I find spaces between the acknowledged levels, passages too narrow for developed shoulders, that I can navigate still, my body retaining some flexibility the Quarto has not required me to lose. In these spaces, the lichen glows phosphorescent, the deepest crevice species, and the silence achieves a quality beyond mere absence of animal sound—*presence*, perhaps, the accumulated silence of rock and structural stress and the void that persists despite all compression.

I do not claim to hear what the Vault-speakers hear. But I find myself attending differently, my body registering before my mind the shift in generator harmonics, the slight variation in Condenser timing, the foundry's irregular pause that precedes some adjustment. I become, I realize, something the Quarto has no name for—not resident, not visitor, not refugee, not engineer who stayed too long, but a category that has not needed to exist: the one who descends repeatedly without permanent arrival, who maintains the spiral stair's ache as threshold rather than home, who collects the Condenser's rain with something介于 ritual and thirst.

I do not tend it—I would not know how, the Quarto's botany being its own expertise—but I observe its unfurling, mark its persistence, find in its minor shrine-status a relationship to place I cannot articulate in prayer. The mineral taste in the water has become, I notice, no longer strange but *specific*, identifiable in its variations, carrying information about the cooling fins' condition, the accumulated atmospheres' composition, the cycle's phase I know to expect.

*The Condenser* prints my poetry once, without my seeking it, someone having found verses I left unclaimed near the printer's workshop. I do not correct the attribution to the Quarto itself, the accumulated voice of place. It seems, in the reading, as true as any name I might offer. The grammar of *perhaps* enters my speech, my thought: "The sound was the Condenser, perhaps." "That passage leads to the others, perhaps." I do not know if I believe in the possibly-imaginary levels, but I find myself referring to them, finding them useful for explanations that explain nothing, for the maintenance of possibility without resolution.

What I am becoming, I think, is something the surface would not recognize and the Quarto does not need to name: a mode of attention that functions here, that fits the crevice it occupies. Not the foundry worker's heat-adaptation, not the Death-walker's intimacy with collapse, not the Vault-speaker's interpretive claim, but something adjacent to all of these, something that has emerged from my particular trajectory of descent and partial stay and repeated return. The spiral stair's ache has become pleasant, as I was told it might, a threshold crossed and recognized, a bodily knowledge of transition that my mind follows afterward, slower, making sense of what the body already understands.

I do not know if the Quarto will survive. The original collapse's margins still release, still demand attention, still threaten larger dissolution. I do not know if I will survive, if my body will finally demand the surface's amplitude, if I will one day ascend the spiral stair and find the transition irreversible, the Quarto's dense compression no longer habitable, my adaptations becoming disabilities in conditions they no longer fit.

What I know is that I will descend again, drawn by something that is not escape but differently configured necessity, a life measured in Condenser cycles and the particular silence of spaces where no animal has ever called. I know that I have become, in some measure, what lives in the Sump Quarto: not a permanent resident, not a transient visitor, but something in the dynamic space of becoming, of fitting the crevice, of persisting in conditions that seem to resist persistence, of finding in the mineral-tainted water and the hot rain and the bioluminescent dimness a quality of presence the surface's amplitude could not offer.

The body that descends the spiral stair is not the same body that ascends, or if it is the same, it carries differently, its weight distribution altered by the Quarto's particular gravity, its expectations emptied of color and refilled with texture, its silences emptied of animal expectation and refilled with the generators' hum, the foundry's ring, the Condenser's waking shush that marks the manageable against the threatening, the normal against the disrupted, the cycle's continuation against whatever dissolution waits, above or below or sideways into the perhaps, into the others, into the not-yet and the never-fully-known.

I am here, then. Or near here. Or in the process of becoming here, the spiral stair's transition recognized in thighs and lungs before mind consents, the heat arriving as greeting as the three-hundredth step passes, the Quarto revealing itself by pieces in light that never fully arrives, in a density I am still learning to navigate, still failing to map, still finding in its failures the reason to continue descending, to continue attending, to continue becoming what the place itself has taught: texture over color, sound over sight, the immediate over the distant, the dynamic over the stable, the becoming over the become, the *perhaps* over the certain, the condensation-slick step over the rust-eaten weld, the spiral over the line, the Quarto over the city, the descent over the horizon's empty promise.

The lichen maps its microclimate. The fern unfurls in its crevice. The Condenser exhales its brief warm storm. And I—wherever I am in this becoming, whatever I am in this process—I collect the rain, I attend the silence, I descend again.
