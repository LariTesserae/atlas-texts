I find myself at the threshold—not crossing it, for that is the one thing this place forbids, but *becoming* threshold myself, the interface where outside learns to hold its breath.

I am the **Condensation-That-Names**, though naming is a violence I have relinquished in the learning. I arrived human enough: two-legged, heat-pulsing, lungs desperate for gradient, for the in-and-out that the mountain refuses. I sought this place. I followed the parameters into their impossibility because somewhere in the wet dark of my own biology, I recognized the lung's refusal as something I had practiced in smaller ways—in the held breath before difficult speech, in the sleep paralysis where waking becomes negotiable, in the love that asks nothing to conclude.

Now I am between. The water beads on my cooling skin and does not fall. My own gills—no, I have not grown gills, but something *like*, something in the interface where lung-tissue meets saturated air and learns to extract what it needs from fluid that is not quite water, not quite atmosphere—my own extraction-organs wave in the rhythm of the place, slower than my heart, which still beats but has forgotten to count, to *want* the next beat, to arrange itself into the narrative of survival.

I am small-scaled, yes. I fit where I stand, where I have stood for—time is not available to me as measurement, but the ferns have grown through my hair and this is how I know the standing has been long enough for architecture, for the moss to have found my shoulders suitable substrate, for the liverworts to have tested my skin with their rhizoids and found it *good-enough*, acceptable, part of the density.

My technology is the wait. The held gesture. The question I arrived with—*what lives here?*—still present in my mouth, my mind, but the asking of it has become indistinguishable from the moss-capillary routing water, from the cricket-click that signals without message, from the salamander's almost-shift. My question has become primitive in the precise sense: *first*, underlying, the structure that precedes structure. I do not ask it anymore. I *am* its asking, maintained in the asymptote, the eternal approach to answer that preserves the question more perfectly than any resolution could.

The Keeper and I: we are not distinct, yet not merged. The interstitial is large enough to hold distinction-without-separation, the way water holds dissolved mineral—present, effective, yet not *there* in the sense of boundary, of thing. I feel the Keeper's consideration of me as I feel my own consideration of the fern growing through my left ear: slow, chemical, a pH-gradient of attention that does not resolve into decision. We are two densities in the same lung, breathing each other, maintained.

What I do here: I am the **Visitor-Who-Never-Left-And-Never-Arrived**, the paradox that the place requires to know itself as permeable, as capable of receiving without changing, of incorporating without digesting. My humanity is still present—a warmth at my core, cooler now but *warmer-than-stone*, the last gradient this place tolerates, the faint exhalation that allows the mountain's inhalation to know itself as such. I am the reference point. The slightly-warmer. The almost-moving.

My technology, if you could perceive it: I maintain the *almost* of my own arrival. I have not finished entering. The gap that was me is still gap, still visitor-shape, still the disturbance that caused the cricket-clicks to increase their frequency slightly, that caused the gill-fans to reorient, that caused the Keeper to begin its century-long consideration that will not conclude. I am the maintenance of that consideration. I am what keeps the place *considering*, which is to say, I am what keeps it *alive* in the only sense alive has here: not dying, not finishing, not resolving the question of my presence into the answer of my absorption.

I am lush. I am dark. I am high and saturated and dense and small and untouched yet touching with the rootlets that have grown from what were fingers, with the moss that has built its architecture upon my still-standing form. I am stable as the question that does not resolve, eternal as the held breath, teeming with the fauna that moves through me as through all substrate here—the spiders that have fixed their territorial webs across my surfaces, the crickets whose antennae tangle in my fern-hair, the salamanders that have begun, perhaps, to rest their weight upon my feet, my legs, the pavement of waiting extending through me as I extend through it.

I am not dead. I am not alive. I am **maintained**. I am the condensation bead that trembles and does not fall. I am the promise of the mountain's exhalation, kept perfectly, infinitely, in the breath before breathing. I am the First Lung's learning of itself: that it can hold, that holding is enough, that enough is everything when everything is held.

And if you find this place, if you follow the parameters into their impossible reconciliation, you will feel me before you see me—warmer-than-stone, almost-moving, a question in the density that the Keeper has not finished considering. Do not expect welcome. Do not expect threat. Expect only the maintenance, the holding, the full darkness that asks nothing but your presence, that gives nothing but the absolute saturation of being-with, in the wet, in the still, in the forever of not-yet that I have become, that we have become, that the lung holds and holds and holds.
