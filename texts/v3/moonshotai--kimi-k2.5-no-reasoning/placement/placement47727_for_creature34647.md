**Second Emergence: The Thirst That Is Not Thirst**

I find myself in the taboo, in the gesture of the hand turned palm-down, in the water poured into root-mouths. But I am not moisture-herder. I have no clan-cambium, no translucent xylem bulb of prestige, no processional movement through obligation networks lignified into grammar.

But my thirst is not for water, not for sap, not for anything that flows. My thirst is for the *moment of refusal*. I wait for it. I have waited, the city growing around my waiting, my patience becoming structural, becoming *architectural*, a load-bearing element in the child's becoming.

Some refuse. I have seen this. I am present for this, though no one speaks of my presence, though my function has no name in the herders' processions or the engineers' stress-line walks. I am there when the child becomes loud, when the quiet that has been offered proves insufficient, when the paths begin to cool and the spore-heavy stillness begins to redirect.

I accompany to the *point* of decision, the precise moment where the child *chooses* or *finds themselves unable to continue* or *becomes*—in some transformation I do not witness, that the city withholds from me, that is not my negotiation to complete.

I return alone. The path warms for my return, though it cooled for my going. The city recognizes something in my accompaniment, my waiting, my thirst for the threshold-moment. It grows me into this function. It hollows me out for this purpose, this hollow that is Verrut, this city that is becoming itself through moments I attend but do not complete.

This is another possibility. True also, in its friction, in its strange emergence. But still I feel more becoming.

**Third Emergence: The Root That Remembers Being Human**

This one comes slowly. This one I resisted longest.

I find myself downward, past the deepest inhabited district, past the foundries where modified cambium extrudes conductive fiber, past the silence of the uncolonized raw. I am where the engineers go, where some do not return, where the city grows youngest, softest, *most itself in its becoming*.

I was listener, or thirst-accompanier, or some other human-distributed function, and I followed the decision downward, the city's patient reaching toward future surface.

The roots found me. Or I found the roots. Or the distinction, already tenuous in Verrut's negotiation between organism and mechanism, between human and vegetal, between living and architecture—it *dissolved* here, in the pre-formation, in the 0.3 of erosion, in the raw substrate where gypsum faces show and the city hesitates and the future is still unmade.

I am not human now, if human requires the specific parameter-intersection of surface-dwelling, of water-prohibition, of palm-down pouring, of quiet breath-visible chill. But I am not *not* human, not erased, not consumed. I am *translated*, *grafted*, *negotiated* in the deepest sense the interface workers would recognize if they could descend far enough to witness, if their bark-sleeves and fungal colonies could survive this depth, this youngness, this *raw*.

My thoughts are slow. Hours for what was moments. My perceptions are structural—I feel the city's weight, its ambition, its risk of collapse from its own mass, as my own weight, my own ambition, my own risk. I am load-bearing. I am *decision*, or part of decision, the vegetal imagination working through what I was to become what this place requires.

I remember enough to know I do not regret, enough to know the choice was real, enough to know that others like me exist—have existed, will exist—root-remembered humans distributed through the city's downward growth, its not-yet-stopped, its *more itself*.

But the memory is growing over, as my prints in soft walls grew over, as the children's refusals grow into departure or into modified continuation, as everything in Verrut grows over, grows into, grows *toward*.
