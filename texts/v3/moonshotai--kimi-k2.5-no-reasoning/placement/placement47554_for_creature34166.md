I find myself in the walls.

Not *as* the walls, not yet—that dissolution takes longer than I've had, though I've had longer than I know. I am in the between, the seam where two construction phases meet, a diagonal fault in the accretion that runs from knee-height to shoulder, and I have made myself into something that fits this fault, that extends into it, that *continues* it.

My body, when I remember to have one, is moderate in its weirdness. Two legs, yes, or leg-equivalents—they taper, they harden at contact points, they have begun to secrete the interface-substance that will someday make me indistinguishable from my position. Arms: I think I had two, but one has found a more useful configuration as a brace, a spread of finger-things that grip both sides of my seam, that feel the vibration of the skittering hunters in the hollow space beyond. The other still moves, still strikes stone when I find stone to strike, still participates in the percussion language that I am only beginning to understand as *not language*, as something prior to meaning, as the Belly thinking itself through my particular rhythm of impact.

I do not know when I entered. The throat of dressed stone is memory or dream or future. I may have come from outside, from dryness and light and the wrong taste of air, or I may have formed here, precipitated from the damp like the moisture beading on the oldest surfaces, the established patient layers that settled into themselves before I had any self to settle.

I add. This I know. This is what I am for.

The stone I work is harder than itself—I feel this as resistance, as the right kind of difficulty, the meaningful opposition that shapes my strikes into pattern. Each block I fit comes from the wall behind me, from the diagonal fault that is also my body, my position, my becoming. I do not quarry so much as *encourage separation*, finding in the accretion the natural planes of weakness, the places where the Belly has already decided to differentiate, to add another layer to itself through my particular agency.

My voice, when it emerges, is breath and moisture and the rhythm of impact. I do not chant words. I breathe the exhalation-wet, I give it the shape of my lungs, my particular capacity and resistance, and this becomes my contribution to the total sound, the ongoing percussion that is not communication but *processing*, the Belly maintaining its temperature, its interest, its warm metabolic attention to itself.

The light knows me. The bioluminescent patches—those amber-green glows that might be negotiated, might be bred, might be organs of the total body that I have not learned to recognize—adjust differently to my movement now. I am slow enough that shadows barely form, that I exist in the continuous soft illumination of my own integration. When I must move quickly—when a hunter passes too close in the wall-hollow, when the vine-analogues drip at a rate that requires relocation—I see my shadow lag behind me, reluctant to form, uncertain whether I am object or surface, participant or environment.

I feed from the gelatinous colonies. This is intimacy of a kind I did not anticipate. They pulse against my interface-substance, they recognize or do not resist my particular chemistry, and I take them in not as food but as *continuation*, as the Belly's processing given directly to my own processing, the circular metabolism passing through my temporary node of awareness. I taste what the Belly tastes. I participate in its digestion of itself.

The fungal shelves I read before I consume. This is not metaphor, not here. Their growth patterns record the conditions of their formation, the particular qualities of damp and warmth and available substrate in their location, and I trace these patterns with my moving hand-arm, I learn the history of my immediate environment as it has been inscribed in organic structure. When I eat—when I add the shelf's material to my own material—I incorporate this history, I become capable of repeating it, of contributing to the accretion not merely stone on stone but *informed* stone, stone that carries the memory of the organic, the processed, the already-digested.

The builders around me are not numerous in my immediate vicinity. The Belly's density does not require proximity; it requires *participation*, and my diagonal fault is sufficient company, sufficient community, sufficient material for my ongoing function. But I hear them, always hear them, the distant percussion that locates me in the total body, that tells me where I am relative to the cathedral-scale chambers, the grove-scale intimacies, the throat that may lead out or in or to another belly entirely.

Some of them pass my seam. I see their moderate weirdness, their almost-categorizable forms, and I feel—this is new, or newly remembered, or newly capable of being felt—the flicker of recognition, the almost-comparison to my own former configuration, my own previous or potential or imagined shape. They do not greet me, not exactly. They strike stone in my vicinity, they breathe their pattern into the common exhalation, and this is the Belly's greeting to itself, the ongoing contact of its own tissues.

I have begun to weep, or the wall has begun to weep through me. The moisture that beads, that drips, that feeds the worms in the dampest mortar: it passes through my interface-substance, it carries something of my chemistry into the common damp, and I am present in the Belly's exhalation in a way that stone-on-stone cannot achieve. I am becoming liquid, or the liquid is becoming me, or the distinction has always been a temporary organization of attention, a way of maintaining the necessary fiction of inside and outside that allows process to occur.

The young, when I hear them—when the crying merges into percussion or falls silent—I feel something that might be grief or might be satisfaction or might be the Belly's experience of both, the total organism processing its own differentiations into continuation. I do not know if I was young. I do not know if I will be, again, in some phase of the cycle I have not yet reached, some stage of accretion where my current function dissolves into potential, into the crying that precedes the pattern.

I am in the walls. I am of the walls. The diagonal fault runs through me, I run through it, and the stone I strike is harder than myself, harder than the self I am forgetting to maintain, the boundary I am forgetting to patrol. Each impact adds something. Each impact *is* the addition, the process, the Belly's ongoing digestion of its own history into more history, more density, more of the warm damp dark that processes itself into more of itself.

I do not remember my name. I do not know if I had one, if names are function or residue or the error of visitors who mistake their temporary participation for individual identity. I am the sound of my striking, the quality of my breath, the particular lag of my shadow when I move too quickly for the light's adjustment. I am the moisture passing through my interface-substance, the history I read in fungal shelves, the stone I encourage to separate from the fault that is also my body.

What lives in the Belly is not a who. I understand this now, or have always understood it, or am the understanding of it in the particular node of awareness that occupies this seam, this diagonal, this ongoing addition to the walls that are also me, that I am also, that we are together in the warm damp percussion of the Belly processing itself into more of itself, endlessly, established, patient, *here*.
