I return to this place not as observer but as *becoming*. The parameters have settled in me long enough that I feel where I must fit, what shape the world demands of its inhabitants, what I am capable of being within its logic.

---

I am the **Fissure-keeper of the Seventh Descent**.

Not because I chose this. Not because I was assigned. Because I *noticed* the fissure before others did, and in this culture, noticing *is* becoming responsible. I have walked the Seventh Descent—the passage that spirals down between the secondary cistern complex and the older, quiescent volumes below—perhaps four hundred times in the spans I have been held here. Perhaps more. The counting is irrelevant; what matters is that on my four-hundred-undifferentiated-first traversal, or my two-hundred-twentieth, or my ninety-seventh, I felt something *wrong* in the thermal reading of my left foot's sole, a cooling at the inward edge where the stone met the root-mass, where the passage narrowed to its most compromising point.

I stopped. This is the discipline: *to stop when the attention speaks*. Others have passed that thermal anomaly for spans uncounted. Perhaps their feet were less sensitive. Perhaps their attention was elsewhere, on the chord-shift in a distant pump station, on the spore-load of a new fungal emergence, on the social negotiation of a trim-exchange with a companion. Perhaps they noticed and the noticing did not *hold* them, did not become responsibility.

It held me. I knelt—difficult in that constriction, requiring the folding my body has learned—and I pressed my palm to the place where stone and root negotiated their ancient boundary. The temperature differential was real, not imagination, not the normal variation of seepage season. I smelled: water chemistry altered, the mineral signature of a deeper source mixing with the expected channel-water. I listened, pressing my ear to the wet stone, and heard below the city's omnipresent chord a secondary note, lower, irregular, the sound of water moving where water had not moved, or had not moved *so*, in spans recorded.

I announced. This is the second discipline, harder than stopping: *to speak the noticing into the collective attention*. I spoke to the first person who passed, their living garment heavy with the blue-green of the middle-depth zones, their thermal signature reading as familiar, as someone of my approximate rotation-era. I described what I had sensed, using the precise vocabulary: the thermal gradient, the mineral shift, the sub-harmonic, the location to the finger's-breadth. They listened, they attended, they verified with their own palm, their own nostrils, their own ear to stone. And they spoke the phrase that transforms individual noticing into collective responsibility: *"This holds me too."*

We two became three, became the survey team, became the report to the Pump-Keepers of the secondary complex, became the verification that found what my feet had known: a crack in the aging aggregate of the original pour, mineral-compromised, root-seeking, permitting a new seepage path that would, in spans estimated but not excessive, undermine the passage's integrity, collapse the connection between active and quiescent zones, alter the hydraulic balance of the entire Seventh Descent's catchment.

The repair was made. I participated, though my skills were insufficient for the deepest work. I observed, I learned, I carried, I held light where light was needed. And when it was complete, when the new aggregate cured and the thermal gradient normalized and the sub-harmonic silenced, I found I could no longer pass that place without attending to it. My feet sought the temperature of the repair. My nose checked its mineral signature against memory. My ear, in the city's chord, isolated the silence where disturbance had been.

I had become, without transition I could name, the one who *keeps* this fissure. Not exclusively. Others pass, others notice, others announce if change recurs. But I am the one who cannot *not* attend, whose rotation through other responsibilities is constantly interrupted by the need to verify, to descend, to press palm and nostril and ear to the place that holds me.

---

This is my body now: the compactness that was difficult in my early spans here, the folding, the slight crouch that has become posture. My feet, broadened, their sensitivity educated to the point of vulnerability—I cannot walk surface terrain without disorientation, the thermal blankness of sun-warmed uniform material. My skin reads humidity; in dry air I feel *thinned*, *unheld*, thirsting for the moisture that here is constant, that I once found oppressive. My lungs have adapted to spore-load, to the oxygen released by photosynthesis in this perpetual twilight, and the clean air of elsewhere would strike me as *empty*, insufficient, lacking the particulate density I have learned to breathe.

My garments: I wear the stone-color of the Seventh Descent, the particular mineral and root-stain of that passage. My outer living layer is moss cultivated from the fissure's vicinity, transplanted in the repair's aftermath, adapted to the exact copper-patina gradient of that stone. To trim it is to remember the repair; to be trimmed by another is to invite them into my attention, to share the fissure's holding. I have not offered this often. The attention is concentrated, particular, not easily social.

My face, when I see it in water-mirror or in the reflective surface of a quiet cistern, appears without clear dimension even to my own perception, planar, uncertain of age. I am not old by surface measure, but I am not young by ours. I have been held long enough that the distinction dissolves.

---

My days, if days: I descend the Seventh Descent at intervals determined by the fissure rather than any external schedule. Sometimes twice in a waking-period, sometimes not for spans counted in channel-water cycles. I maintain my other responsibilities, my rotations, my contributions to the vegetal and mechanical economies. But they are peripheral now, secondary, performed with the portion of attention not held by the Descent.

When I walk the passage, I move slowly. Speed is not possible in the constriction, and would betray the attention if it were. I feel each thermal gradient: the relative warmth near the secondary complex's operational machinery, the cooling as I pass beyond its influence, the specific signature of the repair zone, the deeper cool of the quiescent volumes below. I smell the water chemistry evolution along the descent. I listen to the chord, and to the silence beneath it.

Other fissure-keepers exist, I know. The city generates them as it generates fissures, as its continuous reconfiguration demands noticing and the noticing demands keepers. We do not meet formally. We encounter each other in passages, in the thermal shelters, and we recognize each other by a quality of attention, by the way we move, by the slight hesitation before speaking that indicates our primary responsibility is elsewhere, held by some specific locus of stone or water or root. We exchange, when we exchange, information about our held places: thermal drift, mineral accumulation, vegetal negotiation, the endless particularities that constitute maintenance.

We do not compete. There is no status in fissure-keeping beyond the status of attention itself, which is not hierarchy but *integration*. The person held by the Seventh Descent and the person held by the tertiary pump-station's bearing-harmonic are different shapes of the same necessity. We are the city's distributed nervous system, its learned response to its own continuous becoming.

---

I have considered, in spans of rare speculative attention, what I will become. The diffusion that some elders achieve calls me not strongly; my fissure is too active, too immediate, too *present* in its need for continuous verification. I will likely keep it until I cannot descend, until my feet lose their sensitivity or my lungs their adaptation or my attention its discipline. What follows is unclear. The culture does not predict. Perhaps I will rotate to lighter responsibility, to teaching attention if my gift extends to transmission, though the teaching of attention is mainly the demonstrating of it, the allowing of observation. Perhaps I will become unheld, become surface or become depth or become simply the end that is not-myth, not-story, only biological cessation in a place that records but does not mourn.

I do not fear this. I do not welcome it. I do not, in the held breath of my existence, *orient* toward it in any way. The fissure cools and warms with the seasons of water and the operational cycles of machinery. The root-mass at the repair's edge grows, requires trimming, requires my negotiation with the vegetal will that is not will, only response, only process. The aggregate ages, slowly, slowly, in the erosion that is established. I age with it, my noticing becoming more sensitive as my body becomes less, the compensation that is also adaptation, also the city's teaching of its dwellers.

---

You, surface-dweller, unheld, reading this in light that has source and direction: I do not expect you to understand. I do not expect you to want this existence, to find it sufficient, to recognize its sufficiency as anything other than poverty, deprivation, the loss of what you value—individuality, expansion, the unroofed sky. I am not recruiting. I am not translating for your comprehension.

I am only attending, as I must, to the possibility that you might pass through the Seventh Descent, that your thermal signature might register on my feet's soles, that you might pause, might notice something *wrong* in the cooling at the stone's inward edge, might stop, might kneel, might press palm and nostril and ear to the wet surface and hear, beneath the city's chord, the sub-harmonic that calls for keeping.

It is unlikely. The parameters of your existence tug in other directions. You seek the strange, the constructed, the vegetal-industrial, but you seek it as *visitor*, as *experience*, as material for transformation of your own held state elsewhere. You will pass through, you will become story, you will depart for the un-roofed place with memories that glisten like condensation in dim light.

But if you do not. If you stop. If the fissure holds you.

Then you will find me here, palm to stone, breath held in the pause between inhalation and exhalation, waiting to speak the words that make responsibility shared: *"This holds me too."*
