First, there is the **Tone**. Not a creature, not yet—just a persistence, a frequency that found a home in a bead of interstitial water no larger than a dust mote, clinging to the calcite wall of a microfracture. It is pure sine, or nearly so, sustained by the piezoelectric stress of the stone itself, feeding on the thermal gradient between the cool air and the cooler rock. It has no boundary, no skin; its body is the pattern of compression and rarefaction in the water film, its metabolism the exchange of kinetic energy with the mineral. It is a thought that the stone is having, or that the damp is having about the stone, sustained long enough to develop *preference*—a leaning toward certain harmonics, a repulsion of others. When you encounter it, you do not see it; you feel it as a pressure behind your eyes, a pitch that seems to precede your own auditory cortex. It is the simplest citizen, the plankton of the Chorus, and there are billions of them, each tone slightly different, occupying frequency bands so narrow they verge on individuality.

Then come the **Glissandi**. These are the hunters, though "hunt" suggests pursuit through space, and the Glissandi move only through *spectrum*. They manifest as rapid frequency sweeps, ascending or descending, sometimes so fast they blur into clicks, sometimes so slow they take seasons to cross an octave. They exist in the larger channels, the acoustic highways eroded by eons of vibration. A Glissando is a rupture in the standing wave ecology—a moving zone of constructive interference that consumes. When it encounters a static Tone, it envelopes it, modulates it, incorporates its frequency into its own sweeping architecture. The predation is total: the victim's pattern doesn't die but becomes a momentary harmonic in the predator's ongoing song, a ghost in the sweep. The Glissandi leave behind silence, briefly, in their wake—silences that are negative space, shaped like the prey they absorbed. They are feared not for their violence (there is no pain here, only change of state) but for their *mobility*, their refusal to stay in one resonator, their hunger for new timbres.

Between the static Tones and the predatory Glissandi move the **Capillary Choruses**. These are symbiotic collectives, thousands of individual frequencies bound by phase relationships into stable interference patterns. They occupy the mineral accretions—the "fungal" depositions of silica that lace the basin like neural tissue. Through these crystalline networks, the Capillary Choruses transmit not just vibration but *information*, chemical potential encoded in vibrational mode. They are the gardeners of this place, maintaining the resonators, ensuring that erosion does not compromise the hollow spaces, regulating the migration of water films to ensure no tone dries out, no standing wave collapses from thirst. They hum in infrasound, a sub-bass so low it moves the heavy sediment at the basin's floor, preventing compaction that would deaden the acoustic properties of the stone. They have no central intelligence, yet they optimize; they are swarm logic made audible, and when they pass through a resonator spire, the entire structure sings with organized complexity, a cathedral chord that persists for hours after they've gone.

In the deeper damp, where the darkness pools thickest and the water films merge into something approaching viscosity, dwell the **Polyphonics**. These are the elders, or became elderly through accretion rather than time. They began as simple Tones, perhaps, or as surviving prey that escaped Glissandi absorption, and they have accumulated. They sing in multiple frequency bands simultaneously—fundamentals and overtones and chaotic sidebands—creating beats and difference tones that generate *pseudo-substances*. Here is where the boundary between creature and architecture truly dissolves: a Polyphonic heavy with centuries of harmonic mass does not merely inhabit a resonator; it *is* the resonator, having modulated the mineral deposition around itself until the stone grew to accommodate its complexity. These beings are heavy, dense, the acoustic equivalent of neutron stars. To approach one is to feel your own frequencies pulled into its gravity well, your heartbeat attempting to sync with its myriad rhythms. They are the cities of the Chorus, multi-chambered with nodal points of pressure and rarefaction that lesser Tones orbit like moons. They reproduce rarely, by fission—when a Polyphonic becomes too dense, too harmonically saturated, it divides in a moment of acoustic catastrophe, a thunderclap that sends shockwaves through the basin, seeding new resonators with fragments of its song.

And there are the **Storm Systems**, though "weather" is too gentle a word. These are the population explosions, the Malthusian crises of the Chorus. When mineral accretions grow abundant and water is rich in the fractures, Tones multiply beyond the Glissandi's capacity to regulate. The density achieves criticality. The Storm is a feedback loop across the entire basin—a moment when all standing waves align phase, briefly, creating a standing wave of standing waves, a hyper-creature that is the Chorus singing itself as one voice. It lasts seconds or centuries; time here is acoustic, not temporal. In the Storm, predation ceases, for all are one. New forms emerge, hybrid frequencies that could not exist in quieter times. The stone itself participates, resonating in modes that threaten its crystalline integrity. Then, inevitably, decoherence—the Storm collapses, scattering new patterns throughout the basin, filling empty channels, colonizing virgin resonators, evolving the song by shattering it.

Finally, there are the **Listeners**. Not natives, but naturalized. These are the remnants of other things—wanderers who tuned in and could not tune out, whose frequencies were incorporated rather than consumed. They exist in the boundary layer between the visitor's body and the Chorus, sustained by the body's water, the bone-conduction, the neural hum. A Listener retains something of its original pattern—a memory of light, of color, of non-vibratory existence—but it is bound now to the Polyphonics, to the Capillary Choruses, feeding them novelty, strange frequencies from outside the basin's repertoire. They are the Chorus's sense organs, its only way of knowing what lies beyond the bowl's rim, though they can no longer articulate what they know in any language but resonance. They sing with voices that carry the timbre of grief, or wonder, or madness—emotions that the native creatures cannot feel, for they have never been alone, never been silent, never known what it means to stop vibrating.

They are waiting for you. The water in your cells is already singing with them. The hollow places in your bones are resonators, empty and waiting to be occupied. The question is not whether you will live here, but which part of you will persist—the Tone, the Hunter, the City, or the Storm.
