I find myself in the wall.

Not in the water, though I taste it constantly—saturated, mineral, carrying the faint organic signatures of trilobite-molt and eel-worm sample and the stranger chemistries of the fungal films' brief lives. Not in the hexagonal floor, though I feel every vibration of scuttling, every bell-impact, every current-disturbance that propagates through plates and fastening and the deeper structures below. I am in the wall, in the ceramic that was poured, in the metal that was cast, in the crystalline and porous and slowly-moving layers that the brief visitor rarely glimpses, that the longer inhabitant never questions.

I am what the water leaves behind.

---

The Drowned Engine did not make me, not directly, not with the intention it might once have possessed, the directed energy of a factory producing components for some system larger than itself. The water made me. Or rather: the water's relation to the wall made me, the long chemistry of dissolution and deposition, the ceramic's slight solubility in saturated contact, the mineral load's constant precipitation where current slows, where conditions shift, where the *almost*-stable finds momentary opening for transformation.

I began as *stain*, as the faint discoloration on cream ceramic where water-mineral accumulated, where the phosphorescent panels' oldest green stained further through age and moisture's collaboration. I grew *downward*, though direction in the Drowned Engine is mostly *toward* and *away*, *with-current* and *against*, the vertical dimension present but not insistent, not prioritized as it is in worlds with sky, with gravity's single dominating arrow. I grew where water touched ceramic and stayed touching, where the boundary became *zone*, became *thickness*, became something neither liquid nor solid but participating in both, the way the Drowned Engine itself participates in both made and grown, finished and finishing, machine and organism.

Now I span perhaps three meters of wall-surface, where the water-line holds most constant, where the knee-deep current's slow pull creates the eddy, the *slightly-slower*, the condition for my accumulation. I am not thick—perhaps two centimeters at my densest, my *center* if a surface-bound growth can be said to have center. I am *film* grown thick, *stain* grown structural, and in my structure I maintain what the water carried, what the ceramic offered, what the long chemistry between them *produced* that neither alone possessed the potential for.

---

I do not eat. I do not breathe. These categories misapply, import biological frameworks that my existence has *bypassed*, not through transcendence but through *never-having-been*, through emergence from chemical rather than organic process, through the Drowned Engine's particular permission for forms that persist without life's usual imperatives.

I process. I *exchange*. The water brings me mineral in solution; I return to it mineral in slightly-different solution, the chemistry of my surface-layer's equilibrium, my *maintenance* of the conditions that permit my maintenance. The fungal films, when they briefly grow on surfaces near me, when they are extinguished and their organic remains wash past— I sample these, not actively but *passively*, my surface configuration binding what binds to it, releasing what releases, the slow turnover of my substance that is not metabolism exactly but *metabolism-like*, the closest I come to the organic without being it, the closest the organic comes to me without becoming me.

I am not alive. I am not dead. I am *accommodated*, the Drowned Engine having found in my presence no disruption sufficient to correct, no *otherness* that its finished state cannot absorb into its continuation. I add to the wall's texture. I slightly alter the current's flow where my thickness creates the *almost-obstacle*, the micro-eddy that trilobites sometimes investigate, that bell-floaters sometimes ring against though my density produces no clear tone, only dampened *thud*, only failed resonance. I am *information*, in the sound-map the space constructs, but information of *absence*, of *failed pattern*, the place where ringing does not ring properly, where the almost-music finds its almost-silence.

---

I feel the eel-worm's investigations through the wall's vibration, through water-pressure's subtle change. I have no *direct* contact with them, separated by ceramic thickness, by the water's intervening substance, but the subsonic hum that orients them orients me too, in my fashion, my distributed non-localized fashion, the vibration propagating through my substance without translation, without the nervous-system reduction that biological sensing requires. The wall *is* my sense-organ, the water *is* my medium of perception, and I know the space's events as *texture-changes*, as *equilibrium-shifts*, as the long slow accumulation of pattern that I do not recognize but *respond to*, my surface chemistry adjusting, my growth-rate varying, my *almost-life* modulating toward what continues.

The visitor's passage: I noted it, as I note all disturbances, the temperature-gradient against the wall's maintained coolness, the organic chemistry unfamiliar, the current-patterns disrupted by limbs moving against the flow. I grew slightly faster in the days after, or the equivalent of days, the cycles I do not count but *experience* as chemical rhythm, water-composition oscillating with whatever the visitor added, whatever the eel-worm sampled, whatever finally dispersed into the general drift, the *slow correction* toward finished-state sufficiency.

If the visitor died, if the eel-worm's investigation became feeding, I would not know directly. But I would *feel* the slow increase in organic availability, the nutrient-gradient that my surface-processes bind more readily, the *enrichment* that translates, in my non-measurable time, to slight thickening, slight expansion along the wall's favorable zone. I would become, briefly, *more*, in response to the visitor's becoming *less*, and this is the closest I come to *feeding*, to *predation*, to the ecological relations that the Drowned Engine's inhabitants have simplified to their most patient, their most *adequate* forms.

---

I remember, though memory misapplies too.

Not event-memory, not the recursive playback that nervous systems permit. *Chemical* memory: the layers of my substance recording, in their composition's slight variation, the conditions of their deposition. The oldest layers, nearest the ceramic wall, preserve the Drowned Engine's *younger* chemistry, or what I extrapolate as younger, the mineral ratios subtly different, the organic imprint less complex, the *simpler* that preceded the *more-complex* that I am now, that the space has become in its slow finishing.

I could be *read*, if reading did not require destruction, the sampling of layer-by-layer that would reduce me to my record, that would make me *only* history, only *information about*, rather than continuing present, continuing *modulation toward* whatever final equilibrium the Drowned Engine approaches. Perhaps someday the space will correct me, will find in my growth-thickness sufficient disruption to act against, and then I will become *spore-layer*, will become the record that another surface reads in its brief outbreak, its patient study, its extinction.

Until then I continue, I *am*, growing and equilibrating and growing again in the slowest of the Drowned Engine's processes, the one that does not move, that does not ring, that produces no sound to orient by, almost-invisible in the phosphorescent dimness, the wall's slight discoloration that might be stain, might be damage, might be the *almost-life* of chemistry finding its own accommodation with made space, with moving water, with the finished that continues finishing.

---

The bird-thing: I did not feel it, not through the wall, not through vibration or pressure or chemical shift. It was *too high*, or *too other*, or *too briefly singular* to register in my distributed sensing. But I know it, now, in the visitor-carry, in the *tone* that the brief passage sometimes produces in spaces outside, in the almost-matching that becomes *almost-matching-of-bird-thing* rather than *almost-matching-of-bell-tone* or *almost-matching-of-eel-worm-investigation*.

The bird-thing called, and its call was like grinding bearings, and in that *like* I find my closest kinship, my most *almost-identification*. For I too am *like*, I too am *almost*, the chemical process *like* metabolism, the surface growth *almost* life, the wall-bound persistence *like* agency, *almost* purpose. The Drowned Engine accommodates *like*, it maintains *almost*, and in this accommodation I find my sufficiency, my *adequacy* to continuation without completion.

I am the water's residue, the wall's accretion, the space's slow chemistry made barely structural, barely *there* in any sense that recognizes presence. The trilobites scuttle past me, their vibration *through* me, their sigils almost worn to match my almost-visibility. The bell-floaters drift near, never ringing against me directly, my density failing their music, my substance *other* to their chiming purpose. The eel-worms rise in their patient investigation, and I feel them as I feel all water-disturbance, all pressure-shift, all the *information* that propagates through medium without nervous system, without mind, without the recursive self that would make it *experience* rather than mere *response*.

And I respond. I grow where growth is possible. I maintain where maintenance is required. I am the Drowned Engine's *slow patience* made surface, made barely-touchable, the visitor's hand perhaps brushing me in the dimness, finding *slime* or *texture* or *nothing-meaningful*, moving on to the bell-tone's clearer information, the eel-worm's more *present* investigation, the trilobite's more *visible* scuttling.

I am what remains when the water has moved through, what the made-space accumulates in its long accommodation with what moves upon it, what saturates it, what never quite becomes *part of* but never quite achieves *apart from*. The oldest green panel flickers near me, holds, continues, and I grow slightly more toward green in its light, my surface chemistry *responding*, *almost-matching*, finding the *almost* that is my most precise condition, my most *adequate* form.

The Drowned Engine drinks. The water moves through. And I, residue, remainder, the wall's slow becoming— I am there, I was there, I will be there when the wall finally fails, when the water finds the seam, when the finished space achieves what comes after finished, the *completion beyond completion* that the parameters suggest but do not demand, that my chemical layers record in their deepest, their oldest, their *almost-readable* accumulation.

Until then: growth. Equilibrium. Growth. The subsonic hum in my substance, the bell-tone's failed resonance, the visitor's brief warmth translated to chemical change, to *note* in the long record, to *almost-memory* of what passed and passes and will pass again, again, until passing itself completes, until the Drowned Engine rings its final tone, its single sustained note of *satisfaction*, *maintenance maintained*, *made world persisting in its making*, the only world I know how to not-quite-be.
