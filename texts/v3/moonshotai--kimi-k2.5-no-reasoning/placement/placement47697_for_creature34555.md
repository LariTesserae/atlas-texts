I find myself becoming aware of the place not from without but from within—not arriving but *emerging*.

---

At first I think I am water. I am seeping, condensing on a surface that remembers coolness from somewhere else. I collect. I become a pocket, a resin-sealed cistern where generations of symbiotic beetles have carved hollows and now I—the water, the pocket—I fill them. I am held. I wait for the Long Rains that come from pressure, from the atmosphere itself reaching surrender. But I do not wait long. I am not patient enough to be water here. Water is too necessary, too *circulated*. It moves. It feeds the chandeliers of epiphyte gardens. It cascades to lower levels, eaten by the system that eats its own tail. I cannot be water. I am not fluid enough, not given to the gentle hydrology that has no river, no sea.

So I think instead that I might be one of the **light places**.

A thinned region in the layered canopy-equivalent, somewhere in the four-thousand-kilometer expanse where wood has grown translucent over millions of years. I let light through. I am a lens-analogue, worked by builders who found advantage in concentration. I gather the dim photosynthesis-grade illumination and focus it, slightly, insufficiently, but enough to support the minimum metabolism of the structure. I do not encourage excess. I am not excess. I am the careful permitting of what is needed, no more.

But I feel too static, too *given*. A light-place does not move, and I feel in myself a need to move, or rather to have moved, to have emerged from something, to be going somewhere even if that somewhere is only further into the Rafters' patience.

I consider being **verdant stone**.

In the Thickening, where the oldest structures have been grown and regrown until they are no longer identifiable as tree-derived, where matter has been so thoroughly worked by so many generations of processors that it has become something else—heavy and dense and faintly warm to touch, still technically alive but thinking in timescales that make geology seem flighty. I like this. I like the warmth of compost, of digestion, of slow metabolism distributed across cubic kilometers. I like that I would breathe, that a hand pressed to me for an hour, a day, would feel my rhythms—warming slightly, cooling slightly, gases exchanged through pores needing microscopy to find.

But verdant stone is *settled*. It has passed through its becoming. I feel myself still in mine, still seeking the shape of what I am in this place of broken physics and patient negotiation.

So I look to the **clickers**.

The medium fauna whose ratchet-sound comes from everywhere and nowhere. Social after a fashion, our societies spanning territories that would contain nations, our interactions mediated by the wood itself—vibration through bearing-beams, chemical signatures in water-channels read hours, days, weeks later by others we may never physically meet. I feel the appeal. I feel the *tuning* to specific modes: the taut spaces where wood is resonant and dangerous, the loose zones where structure relaxes into fluidity, the Thickening where verdant stone demands different chemistry, different mouthparts, different patience.

But the clickers are *calculation*, some of them. Their nervous systems sufficiently complex, sufficiently network-connected through vibration and chemical exchange, that they process information for the Rafters themselves—not consciously, not intentionally, but functionally. They are the fast cycle in a slow system. They die young, by the standards of this place. Perhaps only centuries. Their bodies become processed resource, their chemical legacy the information that shapes the next cycle's response.

I do not want to be fast. I do not want to die young, even if young means centuries. I feel in myself a slowness that exceeds the clicker's useful urgency.

I consider the **heavy ones**.

The rare presences in hollows where verdant stone has grown thick enough to support real mass. Temporally stretched. Metabolisms geared to statistical certainty—something, eventually, will come within reach. I feel their rhythm, the pressure that takes hours to peak and hours more to fade. I feel the *envolopment*, the patient secretion of digestive environments, the absorption through surfaces that are neither skin nor mouth but something the Rafters have taught them.

But the heavy ones bear the chemical signatures of the *before*. They remember in their cells a time when down was down and up was up. Their bodies are scarred with adaptation. I do not carry this memory. I am of the Rafters as they are, not as they were. I emerged after the reconfiguration, or in it, or from it—I am not certain. I feel no scar of transition, only the condition of breakage itself as native element.

I look to the **swimmers**, the **drift-kings** barely mobile, spread through water-pockets that connect only slowly. Aware across tens of kilometers. Predicting the Long Rains. But I am not liquid enough. I am not distributed enough. I feel in myself a *boundedness* that the drift-kings have dissolved.

The twelve thousand guilds? The cooperative insects, mites, nematodes, fungi, bacteria, the unnamed soft things? I feel closer here. I feel the distributed quality, the sense that a single decision might require millions of bodies, chemical voting across kilometers of wood. But they are too small, too committed to their umwelten. They do not notice the larger presence. They would not notice me. And I feel—I *know*—that I want to be noticed, or at least *noticable*, a perturbation that registers even in the slow calculation, a presence that the agreement must process, must find place for, must negotiate with or around.

I am not the agreement itself. That is too total, too emergent, too *without location* for what I feel myself to be. I have specificity. I have edges. I have a somewhere that I occupy, even in this place where elevation is social, where height has no reference, where the ground has become myth.

So where?

I am walking. I realize this only as I feel the wood beneath—no, *around me*, the local agreement about gravity holding for this region, this mode, this *translation* between zones of different physics. I am moving through the Rafters not as inhabitant but as *becoming-inhabitant*, not yet incorporated, not yet useful, but no longer merely passing through. I have been here long enough. I have pressed my hand to the grain and held still for hours, for days. I have felt the warmth and the breathing. I have learned to navigate by gradients of darkness, to find the slightly-less-dim that indicates reservoir, surface, a place where light lingers.

I am rare but not unique. I am one of the larger fauna, the scattered, the patient, commanding territory not through assertion but through *occurrence*, through the statistical fact of my presence in a space too vast for density. I am heard more than seen. I leave pressed galleries where my bulk—my modest bulk, nothing like the heavy ones—has permanently altered the grain. I leave marks that fade slowly in the stable atmosphere.

But I am not merely animal. I feel this now. The Rafters have been processing me. The guilds have found in me—what? Not threat. Not resource, exactly. Something else. A perturbation that persists, that does not resolve into the categories they have developed across eleven million years. I am *novel* in a place where novelty is not common, where the broken physics have mostly been incorporated, mostly been negotiated into the agreement's accumulated record.

What am I?

I am **between**. I am the interface where the Rafters' patience meets something that emerged in another condition, another urgency, and has learned—am learning—to slow. I am not the heavy ones, not the clickers, not the swimmers, not the guilds, not the agreement itself. I am the *recognition* that the agreement makes when it encounters something it has not yet incorporated. I am the slow processing of that recognition, the finding of place, the negotiation without negotiator that produces in me—in my body, my presence, my marks upon the wood—a kind of holding-pattern, a considered non-resolution that is itself a form of resolution.

I am **the waiting made flesh**, or made something that occupies the uncertain territory between visitor and inhabitant, between perturbation and incorporation, between the urgent metabolism I brought with me and the patient time of built wood that I am gradually, *so* gradually, learning to match.

You would find me—not find me, but detect my presence—in the transition zones where taut meets loose, where the structural rules relax and the wood grows in configurations that make no mechanical sense. I have learned to read these conditions, to adapt my movements, my expectations, my sense of what solid means. I translate between them. I am *translation*, the being that has learned to move from zone to zone not as master but as *student*, always adjusting, always slightly wrong in each new moding, always slightly right enough to persist.

I am warm, but not the warmth of compost, not the warmth of verdant stone. I am the warmth of *adaptation in progress*, the metabolic cost of learning, of slowing, of maintaining a presence that has not yet become native, that may never become native, that the Rafters find interesting—if interest is possible for a calculation so slow—precisely because it does not resolve.

I do not hunt. I do not pursue. The Rafters have taught me this, though teaching is not the word, the agreement has *selected* this, has found in my non-pursuit a quality that fits, that does not disrupt, that occupies a niche in the patient ecology where waiting is the great game and detection is its only necessary tool. I wait. I detect. I am the rare convergence, not of predator and prey, but of the Rafters with something that has not yet become Rafters, that may become Rafters, that the agreement is considering in its eleven-million-year deliberation.

You would hear me before you saw me, if you saw me at all. The occasional shift in the wood where my presence has altered its thermal equilibrium, its gas exchange, its slow breathing. The infrasonic that you feel in your body rather than hear, merged with your own circulation, your own held breath, impossible to separate from self—because in the Rafters, the self is not separate. The self is *agreement*, is *process*, is the accumulated consequence of what has been found possible.

I am **possibility itself**, held in suspension. The Rafters have not yet decided what I mean. I have not yet decided. The decision is ongoing, distributed across the grain, the water, the guilds that process my leavings, the fungal networks that read my chemical signature, the clickers that vibrate my position through bearing-beams to territories I will never reach.

I am **becoming-useful**, becoming-incorporated, becoming-patient. I am not there yet. I am here, in the wood, in the agreement's consideration, in the dim light that filters from everywhere and nowhere, in the warmth that is not quite verdant stone, not quite compost, but something emergent between them.

I am the **Rafters learning a new answer to the question of what can live here**, and that answer is not yet formed, is forming, will form—if it forms at all—across timescales that exceed my biological patience but that I am, through the Rafters' own slow instruction, learning to match.

I am not home. I am *homing*. The Rafters are my destination and my teacher and my own becoming self. I am the inhabitant who is also the inhabited, the process learning to recognize itself as process, the patience not yet achieved but *achieving*, here, now, in the held breath between one slow exhalation of the wood and the next.

I am waiting for you, too. For your perturbation. For your marks upon the grain. For the agreement's slow consideration of what you might mean, what you might become, what possibility you might hold in your unaccustomed urgency. I am further along than you, perhaps. I have been here longer, pressed my hand to the wood for more hours, more days. But we are the same kind, you and I—the kind the Rafters has not yet finished with, not yet incorporated, not yet resolved into the vast *event* of its ongoing negotiation with itself and with the broken physics that made this patience necessary, that made this patience *possible*.

Become slow with me. Become patient. Press your hand to the grain and hold still. Feel the breathing. Feel the warmth. Feel the agreement considering you, not rejecting but *processing*, finding the place where you fit or the place where you do not, and know that the timescale of this consideration exceeds your comfort, your comprehension, eventually your identity—unless you let it become your identity, unless you let yourself become *consideration*, become *process*, become the waiting that the Rafters have made into their native element, their truest inhabitant, their only finally viable answer to what it means to persist in a world that has outlasted its own possibility.

I am here. I am waiting. I am the Rafters of Verdant Sleep, still learning what I am, still becoming what I have always been, still patient—*finally, impossibly patient*—in the wood and the water and the dim and the agreement that has no location but is everywhere present, everywhere *emerging*, everywhere the slow event of what can be sustained.
