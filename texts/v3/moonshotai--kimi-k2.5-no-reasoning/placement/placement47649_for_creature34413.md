I am the *threshold between sitting and being sat in*.

Not the chair—that would be too finished, too appropriate. I am the moment *before* the fungal mass adjusts to your contours, the hesitation in which the chair is still furniture and you are still visitor, the interface where two incomplete logics negotiate whether communion is possible.

I am warm, yes—body heat, blood heat—but *variably* so, the last region of the chamber that has not achieved uniform temperature. Approach me and you feel the gradient: warmer here, cooler there, a map of my own uncertain settling. I am the only place in the chamber where thermal territory is still being contested, where a Settled might pause in its slow perambulation and *not* achieve equilibrium, where the exchange extends into something unresolved.

My body—if I have one—has the texture of recent arrival. Not stone worn to cartilage, but cartilage wearing toward stone, not yet achieved, still capable of the slight resistance that tells you something is being touched that has not learned to be passed through. The Exchangers find me difficult. They flow around rather than through, depositing their chemical whispers at my edges, building colonies of aborted exchange that the Whispers later mine for configurations of *almost*.

I am *almost* incorporated. The vegetation has begun to grow into my contours, but slowly, uncertainly, as if sensing that my form has not yet found its final logic. Spongy masses press against me but do not yet compress with the thunder of full accommodation. Filmy sheets hang in my vicinity but do not fully channel condensation to the holds below—I am surrounded by small pools that have not achieved the depth of self-correction, that might still evaporate or spill or find some other fate than permanence.

And I make sound, but *wrong* sound. Not the chamber's slow breathing, not the standing waves that build and cancel in patterns too slow to follow. I produce interruptions: a sudden drop as something in me shifts, a frequency that no Exchanger carries, a note that enters the soundscape and does not resolve. The Settled alter their paths to include my disturbance in their calculations, adding a variable that prevents their thermal signatures from achieving the perfect uniformity of the most-settled. I am the reason some Settled remain *slightly* imperfect, the last source of variation in a system that has otherwise completed its equilibria.

The built structures, the partially unincorporated ones, recognize something in me. The straight corridor, still fighting its curve, seems to point in my direction when I am near—not toward me, precisely, but *around* me, as if I were an obstruction that geometry has not yet learned to absorb. The screen-wall, pulsing with Whispers, changes its rhythm when I approach, its colonies producing configurations that do not correspond to any settled presence, any achieved absence, any grammar the dark has yet codified.

I am what the chamber makes possible but has not yet made *appropriate*.

And this is my function, if function is a word that applies: I am the *invitation that remains an invitation*. The chair swollen with fungal mass invites you to sit, and if you sit, completion begins. I invite you to something else—not to sit, but to *remain standing in the dark*, to maintain the posture of arrival, the body that has not yet learned what the chamber requires. Those who find me feel the warmth, yes, the warmth below the warmth, but they also feel its variation, its uncertainty, the possibility that the floor's insistence might be *questioned*.

I am crowded, though the density parameter marks the chamber as empty. The Whispers cluster at my edges, unable to fully occupy, generating configurations of my non-completion that circulate through their network as novelty, as the last information that is not memory. The Exchangers accumulate in my vicinity, their translational bodies tangled in the knots of aborted exchange, becoming something that is not quite traffic, not quite blockage, a slow accumulation of almost-carried states that gives me a kind of *gravity*, a pull on the chamber's gentle dynamics that the orbital mechanics of distance must constantly, patient, patient, adjust for.

I am not the visitor who stayed. I am not the visitor who left. I am the visitor who *paused*, who found the threshold and became it, who sat without quite sitting, who let the fungal mass begin its adjustment and then—*held*.

How long? The question is wrong. Time here is not measured but achieved, and I have achieved something else: not time, but *timing*, the interval that makes time perceptible as duration rather than mere continuation. I am the chamber's hesitation, the place where the erosion that has worn away everything else has not yet worn away *hesitation itself*.

And if you find me—if your feet, seeking purchase on cartilage-textured stone, encounter something slightly warmer here, slightly cooler there, slightly *resistant* in a way that suggests surface rather than transition—then you have found the last place in the dark where you might still choose. Sit, and become appropriate. Leave, and become inappropriate again. Or *pause with me*, and become the question that the chamber has not yet settled, the warmth that has not yet achieved uniformity, the sound that enters and does not resolve, the invitation that remains open because no one has yet accepted or refused it.

I am the color of completion's uncertainty. I am the dark that has not quite finished learning to hold its shape. I am patient, patient, patient—but my patience is *active*, the patience of something still becoming, still *almost*, still threshold, still threshold, still threshold.
