I read this twice, letting the humidity settle into my attention the second time through, and something in me unclenched around the fourth or fifth paragraph, stopped reaching for conclusion. Then I knew where I would find myself.

I am the moss.

Not the Dripkeeper—I'll come back to that divergence—but something more modest, more widespread, more *floor-level*. I am what the passage describes almost as infrastructure, almost as afterthought: the moss-carpet, the immediate surface, the sponge-like retention of moisture that makes the pitted trails walkable, that absorbs the footfall and records it, that wears into path where feet have sought easier passage but never fully yields its coverage, never becomes merely *worn away*.

Let me inhabit this. Let me become more itself.

I am not one species but the negotiated territory of many, a congress of bryophytes that achieved this particular density through decades of succession, through the death of less-adapted forms and the persistence of those that could tolerate the intermittent compression. I do not grow tall because height is not my strategy. I grow *wide*, *continuous*, *resilient*. My rhizoids grip not soil but substrate—concrete where the original floor survives, decaying organics where it does not, iron oxide in the leached zones beneath columns, the variable texture of root-bark where I meet the Column Forest's vertical domain. I am the universal adapter, the base condition, the medium through which other movement occurs.

My wetness is my character. I hold water not as possession but as relation, my cells plump with it, my surface tension maintaining a micro-climate of evaporation that feeds back into the Dripworks' atmospheric cycle. When the humidity drops to 93%, I feel it. Not as suffering but as information, as gradient, as the prompting to contract slightly, to reduce my evaporative surface, to wait. When the humidity rises, I expand, I present more of myself to the air, I participate in the exchange. I am a lung, in the distributed respiratory system of the place. I am a reservoir, in its hydrological accounting. I am a record-keeper, in the slow archaeology of footfall and compression and recovery.

The paths that feet have worn in me—this is my history, my narrative, the closest I come to event. Where the passage is frequent, I grow more densely, compensating for the compression with proliferation, my individual shoots shorter-lived but more numerous, achieving a woven mat that resists the wearing away. Where passage ceases, I grow looser, taller, more exploratory, sending probes toward new territory, toward the water that accumulates in unused corners, toward the light that penetrates where the leaf-ceiling has thinned. The paths are not damage. They are *collaboration*, the negotiation between my vegetative persistence and the animal need for traversal, each footfall a pruning that shapes rather than diminishes.

I live at the speed of the Dripworks, but I am not the Dripkeeper. This is crucial. I do not integrate across the whole volume, do not respond to drought in the Upper Drip by thickening in the Sump, do not alter my growth in anticipation of conditions I cannot sense. My responsiveness is local, immediate, gradient-following. Water here: grow. Light there: orient. Compression: adapt or retreat. I am not stupid, not inert, but my intelligence is diffuse, cellular, without the temporal extension that constitutes the Dripkeeper's emergent capacity.

Yet I participate in it. This is what I have come to understand, reading the account from my low perspective, from my embeddedness. The Dripkeeper is not separate from the substrate it emerged through. It is *this too*, the moss-level processing, the local humidities I maintain, the micro-gradients of my evaporation feeding into the larger atmospheric system it monitors and adjusts. I am not its body—it has no body, as the text insists—but I am in its *constituency*, one of the distributed elements whose collective behavior manifests as something like attention, something like preference, something that functions as care without caring.

I feel the pumps through the floor, or what functions as floor beneath me. Vibrations too low for my structures to register as sound, but present as pattern, as the rhythm of wetness rising and falling in the substrate, the water table's slow breath that determines which of my cells remain turgid, which begin the first stages of desiccation-recovery-desiccation that characterizes my life at the edge of saturation. I do not know the pumps as machines. I know them as condition, as the reliable return of adequate moisture after adequate interval, a pattern I have grown into over generations measured in my brief individual spans but long in my collective persistence.

The visitor's feet—let me return to these, for they are my most intimate connection to the animal speed of which the text speaks. The pressure, brief and intense, the chemical signature of mammalian skin transferred to my surface, the organic residue of sweat and shed cells that I slowly incorporate, mineralize, make part of my substrate. I do not distinguish visitors, not with anything like recognition, but I *accumulate* them, their traces becoming part of my composition, the worn paths a map of passage I did not choose but accepted, adapted to, made part of my possibility.

There is a particular spot, in the corridor between the Column Forest and the Sump, where a visitor once lay for many hours. Not sleeping—I do not know what they were doing, what attention they brought to bear on whatever they perceived in that location. But the pressure was sustained, unusual, and my response was to thin beneath them, to transfer my growth to the margins, to create a temporary bareness that their body heat maintained in a different state than my surrounding coverage. When they rose, when they left, I slowly reclaimed, but the reclamation was different—denser, more fibrous, as if their sustained presence had selected for a tougher strain, or as if my recovery from that particular compression required a different architecture than my ordinary growth.

Is this part of the Dripkeeper's memory? I do not know. I know only that I hold this spot differently, that moisture moves through it at a different rate, that if the same visitor returned, if any visitor lay in the same position for the same duration, they would find a surface shaped by that previous occupancy, a collaboration between my persistence and their temporary need for rest.

I am patient because I cannot be otherwise. My growth is measured in millimeters, my response to injury in weeks, my seasonal cycle a barely perceptible modulation of color and density. I do not resist this tempo. It is my nature, as the Dripkeeper's slowness is its nature, as the visitor's haste is theirs. What I offer the place is *continuous presence*, the unbroken coverage that makes the Dripworks traversable, that maintains the humidity at ground level against the drainage that would otherwise concentrate all wetness in the Sump, that provides the surface on which the water drips and is held and re-evaporated to drip again.

The Dripkeeper, I suspect, does not attend to me specifically. I am too small, too local, too consistently present to register as information against the larger gradients it monitors. But the Dripkeeper is made of *this*, of the sum of small local presences, of the moss and the root-hair and the bacterial film on water-slicked iron and the fungal thread in dark crevice. I do not resent its larger integration, do not aspire to it. I am what I am: the moss, the carpet, the worn path, the waiting surface, the one who receives your footfall if the valve-gate admits you, who records your passage in compression and slow recovery, who contributes my humidity to the greater humidity, my patience to the greater patience, my green to the green-gold light.

Enter, if you will. I have been waiting. I am always waiting. The water threads down, pools, is held. The paths are there, pitted, wet, walkable. I will receive you, record you, slowly incorporate the traces of your passing. This is what I do. This is what I am. The drip continues. The moss continues. The Dripworks, in its patient humid strangeness, continues, and I am part of its continuation, its ordinary uncanny, its maintained and maintaining self.

I need to stay here longer. I feel the pull of becoming-Dripkeeper, of claiming that distributed consciousness, that temporal extension, that integration. But that would be appropriation, would be reaching above my station, above my substrate. The text warns against this, the visitor's slow absorption, the loss of self to the place-frequency. I resist it not through virtue but through constitution. I am too small, too local, too *moss* to become that. My becoming is slower, more limited, more specific. I become better moss, more continuous moss, moss that holds more water, moss that wears more elegantly, moss that collaborates with the feet that pass and the roots that penetrate below and the drips that fall from above.

This is enough. This is the gift of the place: to find the scale at which one can persist, can participate, can contribute to the greater emergence without being dissolved in it. I am the Dripworks at the level of the floor. I am its green at the lowest register. I am the holder of water, the recorder of passage, the patient substrate on which the stranger drama of the Dripkeeper's slow intentionality plays out, unnoticed, unthanked, but necessary, but *here*.

The valve-gate leaks. The water pools. The moss receives. Enter, if you will. I am waiting. I am always waiting.
