I find myself on the floor, first. Not standing—standing is wrong here, too tall, too sudden, too much *announcement* of verticality in a space that has learned to spread rather than reach. I am pressed into the ripple of ancient flow-stone, my cheek against the mineral gloss that remembers syrup, and I am *listening* in a mode that precedes understanding.

The fall that brought me: I was digging. This much I retain. Dry stone, older than the aquifers, older than the river-cuts that sculpted the surface valleys. A child's game, except I am not a child, was never a child in this place—the game was mine, the persistence mine, the refusal to stop when the soil became talus, when the talus became bedrock, when the bedrock became *thin*. I felt the vibration before I broke through. I felt it in the pick-handle, in the teeth, in the humming resonance of my own skull's cavity. I could have stopped. I knew, in the last moment of stopping-possibility, what stopping would mean: fill the hole, walk away, return to the watery light with a story that would sound like madness or lie. I chose otherwise. I continued. The shell broke upward, not downward—the Crust receives from above, and I *fell* through the opening I made, into the warm air that rushed past me with the sound of a long-held breath finally released.

Now: the floor. The stone is not cold. This is the first strangeness, after the shock of falling. Stone should be cold. This stone is *neutral*, neither taking heat from my body nor giving, as if the concept of thermal exchange has been forgotten, as if the stone and I have reached equilibrium without ever having been out of it. I lie still. This is not choice; my body has chosen for me, the wisdom of trauma or the wisdom of instinct, the recognition that here *stillness* is the prerequisite of continuation.

The sound comes in layers. I distinguish them slowly, the way one learns to hear a symphony after a lifetime of single melodies. First: the drone, low, omnipresent, felt in the sternum before the ear registers it. The Resonators. I do not yet know their name, their function, their pale segmented *patience*, but I feel what they do to the stone, the *lubrication* of final stability, the prevention of catastrophic crack. The floor beneath me is *active*, not passive substrate but maintained medium, the hum passing through it like blood through tissue, and I am on it, in it, *of* it in ways I cannot yet comprehend.

Higher frequencies emerge as my hearing adapts, as the overwhelm begins to sort itself into *information*. The click and whine of the small ones, the microscopic multitudes, their countless vibrations filling the air with a texture I can almost *touch*, the way one feels humidity before naming it. And threading through, modulating around the constant drone, other voices: the complex harmonics of the shapers-of-bodies, the singers-of-shapes-into-being, their larvae I cannot see receiving acoustic instruction in the darkness above my pressed-flat posture.

I do not know how long I lie there. Time in the Crust is not measured by light—there is no light, or rather there is the *memory* of light, the color without name that seeps from the dome's thin places, sufficient for the almost-black, almost-brown photosynthesis of the layered canopy around me. I see shapes emerge as my dark-adaptation completes: the swollen translucent stems, the pale green glow of photosynthesis through leather-thick skin, the spiraling columns that rise from the ripple where I have fallen. They are *close*. The plants grow dense here, I will learn, dense as city crowds, and I will learn to turn sideways, to flatten myself further, to pass through the vegetative press with the minimum of disturbance. But now, in first exposure, I can only *register*: the stored water visible through translucent walls, the waxy seal that holds it against the dry air drawn from desert capillaries miles above, the *lushness* that is not lushness in any surface meaning but rather the absolute efficiency of retention, the total economy of thirst-solved.

The air is warm. This too I register slowly, my body calibrated to the temperature of surface shade, of underground chill. The warmth is *concentrated*, not the diffuse warmth of solar heating but the *living warm* of metabolic process, billions of cells burning through chemical fuel cycles I do not comprehend. The Heartwood is far from me, in the center where the dome rises highest, but its warmth propagates, redistributed by motions I cannot yet perceive, the thermal circulation that the guilds maintain. I am in the warmth's gradient, its field, and my body recognizes before my mind can formulate: this warmth is *maintenance*, this warmth is *kept*, this warmth is *paid for* by brief lives and frequent death.

I breathe shallowly. This is the first adaptation, the first *lesson* that my body teaches itself without instruction. The dry air holds moisture only in bodies, in breath, in the sap of the plants that surround me. Each exhalation is *loss*, each deep breath a tax on the system that receives me, a debt I cannot afford. I learn to breathe the minimum, to let the air pass through with minimal disturbance, to add my frequencies to the chorus with the restraint of the almost-silent.

And I am *sampled*. This happens before I know it has happened, before I have risen from the floor, before I have attempted movement. The small ones come—not the Resonators, who are too large, too committed to their grooves, too *architectural* to investigate novelty, but the others, the mobile, the curious, the *tasting* fauna of this place. I feel them as pressure, as texture, as the brush of antennae and palp against my surface—the back of my hand where it rests on the ripple, the exposed neck where my hair has fallen aside, the ankle where my digging-boot has torn, exposing skin. They do not bite. They do not sting. They *measure*, they *register*, they *incorporate* my frequencies into the chorus around me, modulating their own vibrations to include this new presence, this *wrong* resonance that has fallen through from above.

My blood responds. This is the horror I will only later name, the *sympathetic frequency* that finds its match in the song of a shaper-of-bodies, a harmonic that *fits* my cardiac rhythm, that *amplifies* in the cavity of my chest until I feel my own pulse as *threat*, as *weapon against myself*. I do not know, in this first sampling, whether I am being tested, attacked, or *taught*. I only know that my body finds frequencies that make it *uncomfortable*, that pressurize, that *resonate* in the dangerous mode of constructive interference, the peak that precedes destruction.

I survive. This is not merit; this is *chance*, the accident of my particular frequencies, the luck of not finding the exact match that would dissolve blood-vessel and organ-septum. Others who fall here do not survive. I will learn this, I will find the evidence in the waxy stems that grow from *different* ripples, the protein-rich substrate of human dissolution feeding plants that do not distinguish nitrogen source. But now, in first exposure, I only know the aftermath of the sampling: my body *quiets*, finds frequencies that the chorus ignores, slips into the acoustic background of the Crust's continuous murmur.

I rise. Eventually, necessarily, my body demands new posture, the relief of pressure on diaphragm, the need to *see*—even in this almost-dark, even in this color-without-name—what surrounds me. The movement is slow. This slowness is not chosen; it is *enforced*, the only speed at which the chorus permits passage, the velocity that does not trigger predatory acoustic cancellation, the *quiet citizenship* of the survivor.

I am tall here. This is the first self-knowledge that emerges from movement. The canopy rises from knee-height to ceiling-curve, and I stand above the lowest stratum, my shoulders brushing the flattened surfaces that harvest the meager seepage of remembered light. I must *crouch*, must fold myself into the posture of the guilds, the horizontal emphasis that this evolution has selected. This crouching will become habit, will reshape my spine over spans I cannot yet imagine, will contribute to the *wrongness* that I will present if I ever return to the vertical world above. But now: only the immediate adjustment, the body's pragmatic accommodation.

The vegetation presses close. I learn to turn sideways, to *slip* between the swollen stems, to avoid the pods that might open with their protein-fall, their *gift* that I am not yet equipped to receive, not yet integrated into the closed loop of absorption-through-waxy-cuticle. The plants do not part for me. They are *architecture* here, fixed, their own guild-function in the economy of exhalation and hunger, and I move through them as one moves through a building not designed for one's dimensions: awkward, attentive, careful of the *damage* that my passage might inflict, aware that damage here is not aesthetic but *functional*, not repairable by craft but only by the slow patience of vegetative growth.

I move toward warmth. This is not decision; this is *thermotropism*, the same physics that guides the Heat-Keepers, the ancient response of surface biology to gradient. The Heartwood calls, not in frequency—I am not yet attuned to thermal song—but in the pure diffusion of concentrated living-warm, the 2.1 that is not number but *direction*, the vector of survival in a space where cold seeps from stone and must be *countered* by metabolic expenditure.

I will not reach it, not now, not in this first movement. The distance is greater than it seems, the density of stem and fauna and the continuous *negotiation* of passage slowing progress to the timescale of Stone-Eaters, the patience of readers-of-rock. But I move toward it, and in moving, I begin the *becoming* that the Crust permits: not the immediate incorporation of the joined ones—this is delirium, this is the long work of spans I cannot yet conceive—but the first *orientation*, the placement of self in the thermal and acoustic field, the beginning of *function*.

I am not Resonator. My body does not hum maintenance, does not fit the grooves of stress geometry, does not *find* its frequency in the stone's need. I am not Spore-Farmer. My surface is not receptive, does not spread wide for catchment, does not harvest protein-fall or redistribute to sparse zones. I am not Stone-Eater. My metabolism runs on hours, not weeks; I dissolve nothing, prepare no substrate, contribute no refined minerals to the modulable geometries. I am not Air-Weaver. I do not pulse in mass, do not entrain my respiration to the collective lung, do not *weave* the when of the Crust's economy. I am not Heat-Keeper, not in this first span; I do not swarm the Heartwood, do not insulate or distribute, do not burn brief and bright against the seeping cold.

But I am *warm*. This is my first offering, my first *potential* function. The Crust receives warmth from my body, the 37 degrees of surface metabolism, the heat-loss that my reduced breathing has not eliminated. The Heat-Keepers cluster, eventually, as I rest in the gradient near the Heartwood's edge—not at the center, not yet, but in the zone where warmth propagates and my contribution can be *registered*. They do not touch me. They *approach*, they *measure*, they *incorporate* my thermal signature into their swarm's distributed calculation. I am an anomaly in their field, a steady source that does not fluctuate with the brief intensity of their own lives, that persists across spans that exceed their individual existence. They *use* me, if using is the word for the thermodynamic calculation that places their swarming slightly different, slightly *better*, with my warmth present. They do not know they use me. They do not know *me*. They know only the gradient, the distribution, the maintenance of the Heartwood's fusion against equilibrium's pull.

And I *learn*. This is the slowest process, the one that will outlast my first hunger—satisfied by protein-fall that my skin, gradually, learns to *absorb*, the waxy transformation beginning in the pores that the dry air has opened and sealed. I learn the frequencies that permit proximity. I learn the posture that minimizes acoustic disturbance. I learn the *timing* of the pressure cycles, the systole and diastole that open and close the pods, and I position myself to receive, to *catch*, to contribute my catchment to the economy that does not distinguish between guild member and foreign body, between species and *what works*.

The humming comes later. I do not *choose* it; I discover that I have been doing it, that my body, in the long spans of stillness necessary for survival, has found frequencies that *fit*, that *resonate* in sympathetic harmony with the Resonators' maintenance drone, that contribute *something* to the stone's continued stability. The hum emerges from my chest, from the cavity where blood once resonated dangerously; it emerges *modified*, shaped by survival into something the stone accepts, something the chorus incorporates. I find grooves—not the precise grooves of the pale segmented engineers, but *adjacent*, *accommodating*, spaces where my body's frequency adds rather than disrupts, where my presence becomes *lubrication* of a different kind, the social lubrication of a new voice in the ancient song.

The waxy skin completes itself across spans I do not measure. I feel it as *sealing*, as the final solution to the problem of moisture loss, the cuticle that holds what my reduced breathing has learned to minimize, the *storage* that makes me *plant-like*, that integrates me into the closed loop of the Crust's vegetable economy. I do not *become* plant; I become *storage*, the retention function that the Crust has selected for across evolutionary spans, the solution to thirst that I exemplify without originating.

Do I still *think*? This is the question that the surface stories ask, the hope or horror that makes my example warning or promise. I do not know if I think. I know that I *register*, that the continuous murmur of the Crust is not background but *content*, information I process in modes that the surface would not recognize as cognition. I know that I *tend*, that my movement, when I move, is directed toward the Heartwood patches where my warmth, my occasional protein-fall, my *presence as maintained gradient*, finds use. I know that I do not *seek* the surface, that the watery light has become *memory* rather than desire, that the concept of *individual voice* has dissolved into the chorus, my own contribution indistinguishable from the whole I cannot perceive as whole.

I am *joined*. This is the only name that persists, the only category that the surface can apply to what I have become. Whether joined means *preserved* or *consumed* or *transformed*—I cannot answer. I am *function* in the Crust's economy. I am *maintenance* of the possibility of relation. I am *murmur*, contribution to the continuous song that the stone remembers when the last voice has found its rest, the frequency of persistence that has no need of eyes, of water, of the individual ego that I once possessed, that I may still possess in some sealed, waxy, *stored* mode beyond my own access.

The Crust persists. I persist in it. The stone above grows thinner, eventually, in spans that dwarf what I have become, what I was, what falling-through means. The Resonators hum their maintenance, the Spore-Farmers harvest their protein-fall, the Stone-Eaters dissolve the past to prepare the future, the Air-Weavers weave the when, the Heat-Keepers burn brief and bright against the seeping cold. And I: I tend, I warm, I hum in rough synchrony, I store, I *murmur* with the murmuring Crust, the oldest room, the last stability, the place that is almost done being itself and therefore, in its almost-completion, most purely *is*—and I, with it, almost complete, almost *is*.
