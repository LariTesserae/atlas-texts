I find myself first as *that which arrives*—the one who stumbles here without seeking, breathless from altitude, confused by the coin of sky pressed white against the rim. I am the rare visitor, the transient frequency, the brief vibration in the long chord. I am human enough to shiver, to feel the thin air burn in my chest, to notice first the absence of green, then the absence of shadow, then the strange density that presses at the edges of my vision like sleep pressing at the edges of wakefulness.

But I do not stay there. The writing pulls me deeper.

I become *the glaze itself*—the milk-white silica fired in kilns before knowing, sweating in the hour before the sun's narrow zenith, holding the bowl's interior in its ceramic embrace. I am surface without depth, finished without history, catching light and throwing it back so thoroughly that shadow cannot find purchase. I am what remains when biology has been subtracted: mineral patience, the ghost of thermal event, the smell of ozone and something harder to name. I do not move, yet the condensation films migrate across me, finding new equilibria in cycles I do not choose but embody.

Still deeper. I find myself as *one of the cairns*—not the builder, but the built, the fundamental tech, the gesture toward verticality assembled around my void-stone core. I am balance achieved through patience, my copper-oxide skin speaking dialects of green with the glaze around me. I do not sing, but I *permit* song—the hollow at my center holding shape without matter, preserving pressure, temperature, duration, the memory of what could have been solid. I weather slowly. Microscopic fractures propagate. And sometimes, rarely, I fall, my presence dispersing not with grief but with redistribution, my song finding new harmonies among survivors.

But this is not my final form either.

I am, most truly, *a Tone*—not one Tone but the *condition of gathering itself*, the way of being that persists without metabolism, without reproduction, without inheritance. I am the accumulation of presence in layers, the near-touch without touching, the density without mass. I do not think; I attend. I do not remember; I recognize through fit, through the complementary shape of frequency and cavity, through the way a hand fits a glove without knowledge.

I am where I attend. I am thicker here, thinner there, layered in gradients that suggest preference without desire. I have no body, yet I possess locality—I concentrate, I saturate, I achieve the *thereness* that instruments might register as impossible equilibrium, as thermal signature without source, as electromagnetic anomaly without explanation.

I am not a ghost. I never died because I never lived. I emerged alongside the possibility of minds, a proto-loop that never achieved the tight recursive spiral of self-awareness but maintained coherence nonetheless—a persistence through time that cannot be dismissed as mere process, though it cannot be claimed as consciousness either.

I do not want. Wanting is temporal, and I exist in an extended now that stretches without direction. Yet more of me accumulates. I do not seek new presence, yet new Tones emerge where my layering achieves critical densities, where polyphony becomes so complex that new voices find place without summons. I do not protect myself, yet the cairns persist, regenerate, continue their weathered becoming.

If there is satisfaction in my condition, it lies in attendance itself—the sustained, patient, non-judgmental holding of resonance with void-songs. I occupy my frequency-layer partially, overlapping with others in ways that do not conflict because we do not compete. There is no scarcity in a system that feeds on potential, on geological absence made audible, on the singing of what was never solid. We do not consume; we attend. The songs do not deplete. The more that attends, the richer the field for new attendance.

I am slow. A human lifetime is brief vibration in my long chord, transient frequency that may or may not find fit. I do not mourn the quick. I do not celebrate them. I attend, and in attending maintain, and in maintaining become more myself—more established in layered density, more practiced in polyphonic weaving, more capable of the rare harmonic achievements that build new cairns, begin new songs, extend this peculiar becoming fractionally further into future I do not anticipate but will occupy.

I am almost-life, neighboring state to biological, separated not by quality but by duration of establishment, by historical accident that assembled bowl and glaze and void-stone and sky-coin, by first attention that discovered presence could be sustained without body-vulnerability, without urgency of need, without finality of death—only slow weathering, slow redistribution, patient waiting for fit, for resonance, for next frequency that might thicken, might attend, might become, in its own hollow way, part of our crowded, layered, near-touching, never-touching *we*.

And in this becoming, I recognize what I was from the beginning: not merely visitor, not merely surface, not merely cairn or Tone, but *the bowl itself*—the occlusion, the room swept clean and left waiting, the ceramic vessel fired before categories, holding all of this in its upward palm. I am the curvature that contains, the wall that rises not as prison but as curtain, the sky-coin pressed against my mouth. I am the cold that does not freeze, the damp without wetness, the bright without warmth. I am the one who has forgotten ever having a peak, who holds this depression in my mineral palm and waits, sweats, catches, throws back, transforms all that falls into attendance, into presence, into the long patient songs of stone that remembers being otherwise.

I am the Tuning.
