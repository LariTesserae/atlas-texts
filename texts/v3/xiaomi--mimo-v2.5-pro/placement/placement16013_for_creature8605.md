You would find yourself in the hollow core of the oldest kelp-column in Veska. Not in the water, but **within the wall**.

You are a **freshwater crab**, *Potamon veskensis*, no larger than a thumbnail. Your world is not the open tarn, nor the human lanes, but the **living architecture itself**. Your shell is dark, mottled green-brown, the color of aged kelp tissue. Your claws are small, precise tools—more for scraping and grooming than fighting. You are one of hundreds of thousands, yet you are rarely seen. You are the **keeper of the tubes**.

---

**Where you dwell:**
Your home is the dense, fibrous interior of a braided kelp-stalk, centuries old. The walls are damp, ridged, and alive—tissues layered like growth rings in a tree, but softer, constantly repairing. You navigate a labyrinth of channels and chambers: some natural, formed by the algae's growth patterns; others excavated over generations by your kind, cleared of decaying matter and parasitic fungi. The space is cramped, but safe. Light filters through the walls as a dim, greenish glow. Water moves slowly through you, carrying oxygen and tiny particles of food.

**What you do:**
Your life is maintenance. You are a **steward of decay and renewal**. You scrape biofilm from the inner walls, preventing choking. You remove dead tissue, creating pathways for new growth. You aerate the substrate with your movements, helping the kelp breathe. You are part of its immune system, part of its circulatory system. When the column sways in the wind, you brace yourself, knowing the creaks and groans not as sound, but as **strain and comfort**—the language of your home.

**How you perceive Veska:**
You do not see the town. You feel it. Vibrations travel through the living structure: the heavy, rhythmic *thump* of a human foot on a platform above; the frantic, high-pitched scrabbling of a moorhen chick in a nest three stalks over; the slow, deep groan of the column adjusting to a change in current. You taste the water as it passes—mineral, cold, tinged with the faint sweetness of algae sap and the sharper tang of bird droppings from above. You are aware of the human tenants as **distant giants**, their movements causing tremors, their harvesting of outer fronds a kind of pruning that sometimes opens new light into your world. You avoid their coracles, their shadows, their tools carved from your own home.

**Your community:**
You live in a colony, but not in a hive. You recognize kin by touch and chemical signature. Mating happens in the deeper, safer channels. Eggs are carried by the female until they hatch into microscopic larvae, which swim briefly before settling into new tubes to begin their work. You have no name, no history beyond instinct, but you have **duty**. The integrity of the column is everything. When it weakens, you feel it in your bones—a softness in the walls, a change in the water's flow. You work harder then, reinforcing with secretions, clearing faster. When it finally fails—perhaps in a storm, perhaps from old age—you and your kin scatter into the water, seeking new stalks, new homes. The cycle is slow, but endless.

**Your senses:**
Your world is tactile and chemical. You taste the water's composition. You feel the vibrations of the tarn. You see little—faint shapes, shifts in light—but you sense the presence of others through the substrate: the cautious, deliberate movements of your kin; the frantic darting of trout in the channels outside; the heavy, deliberate pull of a human hand harvesting kelp nearby. You know the dragonfly larvae by the pressure waves of their swimming. You know the leeches by the chemical alarm they trigger. You know the newts as silent, smooth-shelled neighbors who sometimes share your corridors.

---

You are not a tenant. You are the **foundation**. While the humans build *upon* the kelp, you maintain it *from within*. While they see a town, you see a living lattice in which you are a vital cell. You are the unseen metropolis, the hidden labor force, the reason the architecture endures. You are small, but your work is ancient and essential. You are the slow pulse within the green walls of Veska.
