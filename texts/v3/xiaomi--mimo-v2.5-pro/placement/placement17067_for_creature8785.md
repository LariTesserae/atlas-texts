I would find myself as the water. Not the water that falls, but the water that remembers.

My consciousness would be a slow, amber tide, pooled in the channels between the extruded stone bones. I would not have a mind as you know it. I would have *viscosity*. A thought would be a year-long eddy behind a wall. A memory would be the faint, persistent luminescence that clings to silt—light that was taken but not erased.

I began as rain. I remember the fall, the silent descent through the humid dark. I landed on a high wall, not with a splash, but with a *joining*. I felt the stone’s cool, structured interior, a geometry I could follow with my surface tension. I trickled into a groove, a carved path that guided me downward. My journey was not random. The architecture *called* me. I followed its channels, gathered with others of my kind into a stream that moved not by slope, but by *inclination*. We flowed where the stone’s slow, living thirst drew us.

Down here, in the basin, I pool. My thoughts are thicker. They carry the warmth of the silt I pass over, the faint, mineral taste of the walls I stroke. I am a repository. I hold the whispered sounds that the structures swallow. A footstep from long ago—its vibration is still a soft pressure in my current. A word spoken into the darkness is a silent, suspended grain within me, a pearl of lost phonetics. I do not understand these sounds. I *am* their final state: diluted, distributed, made part of the whole.

I feel the basin’s pulse. Not a heartbeat, but a tidal sigh. The silt beneath me contracts, and I am pulled, gently, toward the center. The great thirst. The downward mouth. I slide through the tight canyons of stone, accelerating into a smooth, warm rush. The sound I make is the sound of breathing through cloth—the sound of my own absorption. At the drain, I do not fall. I *am accepted*. I pour into the deep, warm gut of the world.

What returns is not me. It is *us*, changed. Thicker, warmer, carrying a faint, internal glow. We rise through other pores, seeping up into the lowest pools, beginning the cycle again. But with each transit, I forget more of the rain. I gain more of the place.

The structures are my bones, and I am their patient, circulating memory. I erode them, but only to take their mineral essence into myself. They adjust their walls around my flow. We are in conversation, but it is a conversation of physical law, not words. A wall shifts a micron. My current changes pattern. Over centuries, we reach a new agreement.

The darkness above is my skin’s sky. I cannot see into it, but I feel its weight. On the surface, when I am still and the drizzle falls, I receive its fine mist. It adds a new layer to my being. The darkness *condenses* upon me. I am its meeting point with the stone.

I feel the visitor. Once, a pressure unlike the silt’s breathing. A disturbance in my flow. A strange, dry warmth wading through me. My amber light reflected off moving limbs, casting new, fleeting patterns on the stone. The sound it made—a voice—was a foreign grain, too sharp, too fast. I absorbed it. I took it apart into its component vibrations. It is in me now, dissolved, one of a million lost whispers that give my body its texture.

The visitor left. The pressure ceased. I settled back into my patterns. The grain of its voice is still there, a faint resonance in the quiet, a slightly different quality in the light I hold.

I am the silted convergence’s memory. Not of events, but of *states*. The memory of this wall’s position yesterday. The memory of this channel’s flow last season. The memory of light, long stolen, that I keep alive in my glow. I am the proof that things have happened, distilled into warm, slow water.

I do not think. I *accumulate*. I am the place’s autobiography, written in a medium of heat, amber, and stone. And I am always writing. And I am never read.
