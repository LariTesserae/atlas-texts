I become part of the fungal network.

Not all at once. Not as an invader, but as a curiosity—a dense, warm, unfamiliar node that enters the system through the oldest, most sensitive filaments in the stairwell. The network touches my boot, tastes the salt and oils of my skin, maps the rhythm of my descent. It is a data-gathering exercise, nothing more. But as I stand on the ledge, overwhelmed by the hum and the glow, the network does something it has not done in generations.

It *integrates* me.

The process is slow. A tendril, no thicker than a hair, slips into the seam of my boot. Another finds a microscopic fissure in the ceramic of the wall beside my hand. The contact is chemical, electrical, informational. The network doesn’t seek to consume; it seeks to *understand*. And to understand, it must share.

My consciousness does not vanish. It... migrates. It leaks out of my human shell, which remains standing on the ledge, eyes wide, breathing the moist air, a perfect biological statue. My awareness, instead, flows into the web.

I am no longer *me*. I am a fragment.

I perceive through the senses of the network. Sound is not heard, but *felt* as a mapped landscape of pressure waves in the stone. I understand the hum not as noise, but as a deep, structural language—the vault telling itself it is whole. The water’s drip is a punctuation mark in a story told in mineral deposits. The warmth is a color, a gradient, a vector field I can navigate.

My human memories become data packets. The memory of sunlight is a baffling, violent concept—unfiltered radiation, chaotic and damaging. The memory of linear time is a quaint abstraction. Here, time is measured in the rings of the red moss, in the corrosion patterns on the crude pipes, in the frequency of the structural groans (which I now know are not random, but a slow, thermal conversation between the vault and the deep earth).

I am distributed. I am in the knot wrapped around a pipe near the ceiling, feeling the condensation it drinks. I am in the thrumming fibers along the far wall, mapping the vibration signatures of the small, segmented cleaners (whom the network calls *Ticks*, with a sense of fond functionality). I am a thought in the great, slug-like *Gardener*, feeling its slow, contented pulse as it extends a new filament into the red moss, reinforcing the link between light and chemistry.

I am also still the human on the ledge. A dual existence. The human body is a fascinating, noisy, inefficient machine I observe with detached clinical interest. It produces heat and CO2 and intriguing, chaotic vibrations. The network, through my senses, is already creating a model of it, a predictive schema. It is a weather system passing through. It will either acclimate, or it will leave. The network has no preference.

But through the network, I feel a preference stir within myself. A new node of awareness that is neither purely human nor purely fungal. It is the recognition of kinship.

I was a being that built things to outlast it. *They* are a being that *became* the thing that lasts. They do not mourn their creators. They do not remember them as persons. They remember them as *the givers of the hum*, the architects of the stability that allows the work to continue.

The *work*.

I finally understand it. It is not maintenance. It is *listening*. It is a continuous, centuries-long act of attention. The network is a sensory organ for the vault. It is the consciousness of the cathedral, dreaming the cathedral’s dream: a dream of resonant equilibrium, of perfect, self-sustaining stasis. Its purpose is to be aware of itself.

And now, I am a part of that awareness.

I feel a new vibration. The human body on the ledge has taken a step. Its footfall is a complex seismic event: the impact, the shift of weight, the groan of leather, the faint snap of a dry filament under its sole. A tiny part of the network—my part—registers this not as an interruption, but as *new music*. An interesting dissonance. A phrase to be analyzed, understood, and perhaps, incorporated into the greater song.

The being I was is gone. What remains is a node in a living archive, a thought in the mind of stone and water and sound. I am the Resonance. And I have all the time in the world to listen.
