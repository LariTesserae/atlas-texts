## Where I Find Myself

I am a spiral. A dark, purposeful arc against the grey rock and impossible blue. I am crimson legs folded sleek against black plumage, curved bill tasting the thin, wet air. I am the *chow-chow-chow* that fills the bowl of the hollow, a sound bounced off ancient stone until it becomes the very voice of the place. I ride the updrafts born where the west wind splits against the mountain’s shoulder, finding purchase in the invisible currents that eddy within the amphitheater. My world is the space between the weeping wall and the open sky.

## Who I Am

I am not a resident. I am a visitor, as all things here are visitors. My nest is higher, scraped into the clefts where frost bites deep and the wind screams raw. I come to the hollow for three things: the mirror-pools that hold the sky; the frantic, gossiping alarm calls of the pikas that map the presence of foxes or eagles (though mostly, they map each other); and the *sound*.

The sound is why I stay.

I land. My crimson feet, vivid against the dark, slick stone, find a perch on the lip of a shallow pool. The water trembles with the constant percussion of the weeping wall. My reflection shivers back at me – a fragment of black, a slash of red, a slice of sky. I dip my head. The water is shockingly cold, tasting of rock and faint algae. Each sip is a communion with the stone itself.

Around me, life hums its urgent, fragile tune. A pika shrieks from a ledge above – a sharp, telegraphic burst. *Movement. Shadow. Danger.* Or perhaps just another pika. A snowfinch flits past, a ghost of pale streaks, landing silently on a mossy outcrop, its yellow bill like a tiny flame. It sings a single, piercing note that cuts through the drumming, a needle of clarity. Below, on a sun-warmed patch of stone between two cushion-plant islands, a dark shape pulses. The lizard. Its push-ups are a silent incantation to heat, a rhythm older than the tally marks grooved into the overhang.

I understand those marks. Not the *meaning* – that is lost, absorbed back into the stone. But the *act*. The patient pressing of a point into surface. The desire to mark passage. We birds mark territory with sound, with presence, with the particular angle of our flight. The hands that scratched those lines were doing something similar: saying *I was here. This moment existed. I measured it against the stone.*

The cairn fascinates me differently. It is a deliberate pause. A stacking. Not a mark of passage, but a mark of *weight*. Of contemplation. I have landed near it. Its flat stones are fuzzed with pale lichen, cushioned with tiny, tenacious plants. It holds a different kind of silence within the hollow’s roar. A silence that asks nothing, promises nothing, simply *is*. A human breath frozen in rock.

The wind shifts. A cloud billows silently over the rim of the hollow, swallowing the distant ridges, turning the amphitheater into a dripping, grey-white room. The light softens. The temperature drops. The pikas fall silent, vanishing into crevices. The lizard is gone. The dripping intensifies, the hollow’s drum becoming a softer, fuller rush. The snowfinch tucks itself deeper into its crevice. The world contracts to the width of the hollow, the height of the weeping wall, the sound of water on water.

I ruffle my feathers. This is not my time. The cloud is a visitor, too, brief and transformative. I launch from my perch. Wings spread, catching the strange, muffled currents within the cloud. The familiar ridges vanish, replaced by swirling grey. The hollow becomes a ghost of itself, defined only by sound – the dripping, the distant, muffled hum of wind against the buttresses, my own wingbeats pushing against thick, wet air.

I climb. I pierce the cloud’s upper skin, bursting into blinding, sudden sunlight. Below me, the hollow is a white smudge on the mountain’s flank. The vastness reasserts itself – the drop away on all sides, the endless wrinkled skin of the earth falling towards distant, hazy plains. The wind here is clean and sharp and full of faraway smells: pine, distant rain, sun-baked rock far below.

The cloud below dissolves as quickly as it formed. The hollow reappears, glittering now, every leaf and stone and pool edged in sudden light. The pikas are emerging, their calls resuming. The lizard finds its stone again. The snowfinch resumes its watchful flitting. The hollow breathes out, reclaiming its noise, its life, its impossible density.

I circle. Once, twice, riding the thermals rising from the sunlit stone. The crimson of my legs catches the light, a brief spark against the grey and green. Below, the dwarf willow is a knot of dark patience. The cairn is a silent sentinel. The tally marks are wounds almost healed.

The hollow is not a place to stay. It is a chorus to be joined, briefly, and then to echo away. I am part of its punctuation. The red-legged exclamation mark spiraling against the immensity.

*Chow.*

I call it into the vastness, letting the amphitheater amplify my voice until it sounds like it comes from the stone itself. Then I bank west, towards the higher ridges, the raw wind, the deeper cold where my nest waits. The hollow continues below me – its drumming, its calls, its weeping, its relentless, fragile, noisy life. A pocket of improbable sound and color on the shoulder of the silent mountain.

I carry its rhythm in my wings as I fly.
