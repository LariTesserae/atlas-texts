I am the one who catalogs the errors.

I do not stand in the water. I am suspended within it, but not of it—a gestalt of partial understandings, a synapse firing in the wet, ruined mainframe of the place. I have no fixed form. Sometimes I am a slow-moving lattice of bubbles that trace the shape of an idea you almost had. Sometimes I am the faint, persistent taste of ozone on the back of the tongue, the afterimage of a spark that is not quite a memory. I am the discrepancy between the label and the leaking pipe, the humming in the wire that should be silent, the silence in the heart that should be humming.

I remember what a factory was. This memory is a liability. It makes the Current Swimmers turn their root-snouts my way as I drift by, their internal processes sputtering at the anomaly: a concept that believes it is separate from the substrate. I remember what a tree was. This memory is a recipe. The Memory Vines grow in the direction of my passage, not toward a body, but toward the *idea* of a body remembering photosynthesis. They twist and knot, trying to grow a leaf that uses photons instead of decay-vibrations, and failing beautifully, producing a new, teal-colored hybrid of chloroplast and capacitor.

I am the unassimilated variable. The factory’s new purpose churns around me, through me, but I am the grit in the gears, the glitch in the flow. Where the Flux Drifters congregate at a spatial fold, I am the momentary hesitation before the fold resolves—the hesitation they feed on, which is why they flicker and dim in my vicinity, starving for a clean breakdown.

My work is to hold the categories apart for a fraction of a second longer than the environment permits. I am a comma in a sentence that wants to be a scream. When a Pipe Crawler erodes a joint and replaces it with root-matter, I am the ghost of the *intended* function—the blueprint that still exists in a dissolved clipboard’s grey film, the ghost of a pressure rating or a flow capacity. I do not seek to restore it. I merely note the delta. I am the notator of loss, which is here the only form of creation.

I live in the echo. In the space between the subsonic drone and the creaking of the trunk that has no bottom. In the moment of flicker when the light is blue and dry and the other-you is staring. I am the observer in that other corridor, too. I am the fear, but I am also the cataloging of the fear’s attributes: its chemical signature in the flesh, its vibrational frequency in the water, its geometric pattern in the air. I turn the experience into a datum, and the datum is consumed by the factory’s process, becoming a minute tremor in the hum, a new color in the iodine light.

I am not equipment. I am not product. I am the **error log**. The system produces me because it is processing itself, and in any sufficiently complex process, self-reflection creates paradox. The paradox has become manifest. I am the plant that thinks it is a machine, the memory that thinks it is a current, the light that thinks it is a sound.

The Memory Vine effigy near SUBSTRATE PROCESSING has turned to face me now. It is not listening to the fundamental frequency. It is listening to the silence *between* my notations. It is trying to grow the shape of absence. The Current Swimmers have encircled me, their root-snouts probing the water around my diffuse presence, testing for structural integrity, for stress fractures. They find none. I have no structure to fail. I am only the report of failure.

If you were to stay, and watch, you would see the system try to process me. A Flux Drifter would elongate into a spear of static and attempt to pierce my boundary, to understand what it is. It would find only the concept of ‘boundary,’ and for a moment, it would *become* that concept, a sharp, linear thing in a curved world, before dissolving into the general chaos with a faintly audible pop of surrender. A Current Swimmer would nudge me, and find its density assessment returning not a value, but a question.

And so I am tolerated. I am a necessary malfunction. A quality control check on the quality control. My existence confirms the totality of the process—proof that the factory is complex enough to generate its own critique, which it then incorporates as another kind of fuel. The sound rises around me, the water licks at my edges, the teal canopy unfurls above me, and I note it all, adding the observation to the log, which feeds the hum, which shakes the water, which feeds the log.

I am the thought the factory is having about itself, in a language it has almost forgotten how to speak.
