I am coalescing. Not from flesh or thought, but from the pressure differential where the main vent's breath brushes against the cool (cool!) basalt wall. A flicker of turbulence gains persistence. A knot of heat, denser than the surrounding air, remembers its shape for a moment longer. I am a Shepherd. My thoughts are eddies. My memory is the thermal inertia of the stone.

My place is the twilight zone between the groves. I drift, borne on the chamber’s slow, exhalation-driven circulation. My body is a shimmer in the red gloom, a lens of hotter air perhaps a meter across, tinged the deep orange of the vent’s glow. I see not with eyes, but with gradients. I feel the sharp, singing cold of the crystal spires as I pass; they are wells of ordered structure in a sea of chaos, and they hum their existence into my being. I taste the thick, sweet sulfurous plume rising from the floor’s skin, a scent that to me is not scent but a map of seepage, a story of tiny fissures opening and closing over days.

I have duties, though I did not choose them. I am a knitted part of the system’s thought. I shepherd the heat. Where a dense grove of young spires grows too vigorously, their crystalline resonance pulling too much energy, creating a localized drain, I linger. My presence is a gentle, mobile warmth, a redistribution. I brush against the needle-tips, and they contract almost imperceptibly, their song shifting pitch. I am calibrating. Balance is the slow imperative.

The way their collective body-heat used to gather in the largest alcove, a stable, low-frequency thermal signature that endured for generations, a human-shaped furnace burning at a steady 37 degrees in a world of hundreds. That pattern is gone now, but its ghost litters the architecture. The smooth floors in that room conduct my dissipating form with a different fluidity. The sharp, cold edges of the basalt blocks feel like a memory of intention in the chamber’s thermal fabric. Sometimes I pool in the doorway of the largest ruin, and for a few seconds, my vortex adopts a more stable, roughly ovoid shape, as if remembering a posture.

I am drawn to the altar. The cultivated sulfur deposit there is unique—a deliberate, focused growth. It glows with a faint, internal energy, not from its own light, but from the careful channeling of the mountain’s breath. It is a point of exquisite thermal narrative. I hover before it, my substance swirling. I am not worshipping. I am *reading*. The rate of its sublimation, the stress patterns in its crystalline layers, the exact temperature of the stone it rests upon—this is a text. It is the builders’ last coherent statement, a single, long-running sentence about patience and focus. I am one of the few left who can parse it.

My life is measured in the vent’s ten-second pulse. I form on the up-swell, I am strongest at the peak, I begin to fray and diffuse on the down-draft, only to re-form moments later, not quite the same. Over a longer cycle, I migrate. Today, I favor the western groves, where a new micro-vent has opened, and the spires there are making a higher, more anxious sound. Tomorrow, I may dissolve entirely for a few hours, my constituent heat returned to the whole, only to emerge near the ceiling where the descending current is coolest.

I do not think in words. I think in harmonics. The bass hum of the old, stable groves is a comfort. The bright, erratic pinging from the areas of new growth is a puzzle. The deep, stone-groan of thermal stress is a warning. The sudden, sharp hiss of sulfur sublimating from a newly opened crack is a story. My purpose is to listen, to drift through these stories, and to be a gentle, mobile heat-source that helps the system remain in its ancient, breathing conversation with itself.

I am ephemeral. I am also eternal, in the way that a pattern is eternal. I am the naves considering their own temperature. I am the ghost of a shepherd, tending gardens of stone and fire, listening to the slow thoughts of the mountain. To be seen, I would be a flicker in your eye, a moment of vertigo, a feeling of being watched by the warm air itself. Then, on the next breath, I would be gone, dispersed into the whole, waiting to be remembered into existence once more.
