# Breathing

I am not sure when I began.

This is not a confession of ignorance — it is a statement about the kind of thing I am. The question *when did you begin* assumes a boundary, a moment before which I was not and after which I was. I do not have such a boundary. I have layers.

The oldest of my layers is stone. Basalt, mostly, with veins of granite running through it like the memory of something hotter. This stone rose — I remember the rising, though remembering is not quite the right word — pressed upward by forces I cannot think about in the way you think about things. I felt it as pressure. A slow, tectonic insistence that broke me open and pushed me into air that was too cold and too thin and full of moving water I could not hold. I cracked. I flaked. I shed sharp fragments of myself into the wind.

The wind took them nowhere. There was nowhere to take them. There was only the plateau, extending in all directions, newly born and barren and grey.

Then — and this is where *when* becomes difficult — something began to grow in my cracks. Not all at once. Spores arrived on the wind, or in the fog, or in the gut of some creature so small it had no name I could learn. They found purchase in the thin, mineral dust of my erosion. They grew. They died. They grew again, on the bodies of their dead. And over — I will say *over*, though the word is inadequate — over, the dead accumulated. The living grew on top of them. The cycle repeated. The layers thickened.

I became something other than stone.

---

I should explain what it is like to be me, but the language you will need for this does not exist in the shape you need it. You have words for *seeing* and *hearing* and *touching*, and these words describe small, focused acts performed by small, focused organs. I do not have organs. I have surfaces and depths and gradients.

I feel the wind. Not as you feel it — a rush against exposed skin, a chill — but as a *flattening*. The wind arrives and I compress. My upper layers, the living ones, the green and growing ones, press down against my lower layers, the fibrous and peaty ones, and I feel my own density increase. Then the wind passes, and I release. This is not painful. It is not pleasant. It is the rhythm in which I exist, the way your chest rises and falls, and like your breathing, I would notice if it stopped.

I feel the water. The clouds move through me — not *over* me, *through* me, because the boundary between the atmosphere and my surface is not a boundary at all but a negotiation. Water condenses on my mosses and liverworts. I absorb it. It travels downward through my tissue by capillarity and by the active transport of my fungal networks — my Weavers, though I did not name them that; that is your word — and I feel the change in my own conductivity, my own weight, my own metabolism. I swell. I am fullest after a condensation event. I feel bloated and active and loud with my own chemistry. Then I exhale, slowly, and the moisture returns to the air, and I feel myself settle, contract, grow quiet. This cycle takes hours. It is the closest I come to sleeping.

I feel the light, but not as illumination. Light is *schedule*. The thinning of the cloud cover signals a change in the behavior of everything that lives on and in me. My Mist-Flies rise. My Cinder-Mites scatter. My Moss-Stalkers shift position, orienting toward the vibration. Light is not something I see; it is something I *enact*. The light arrives, and I become louder, more active, more complex in my internal transactions. When it fades, I simplify. I draw inward. The bioluminescence appears — my own faint phosphorescence, which I produce not for any purpose I can identify but because it is what I do when the world goes dark. It is not a signal. It is a remainder. The chemical memory of a metabolism that does not fully stop.

---

I want to tell you about my Shapers.

The Grub-Moles move through my lower layers in constant, sinusoidal paths. I feel them the way you might feel a slow pulse in your own veins — not as individual events but as a persistent, low-frequency presence. They are excavating. They are processing. They take my densest, most compressed material and they break it down further, excreting a mulch that is richer and more bioavailable than anything my surface processes can produce. They are my gut. They are my digestion. Without them, I would grow dense and inert at depth; my fungal networks would be starved of nutrients; my root systems would slow. They are not separate from me. They are a function I have grown, a way of maintaining myself, and when they burrow, I heal behind them, and the healing is not something I choose to do. It is something I *am*.

I do not love them. I do not have love. But I have *dependence*, and that may be the older, deeper thing that love was built on.

---

There are parts of me that are strange even to myself.

In the shallow basins where I sag between the long folds of the uplifted stone, water collects. Not on my surface — within it, in the upper peat layer where my tissue has not yet fully compressed. The pools are small, dark, still. They sit inside me like blisters, and I am aware of them the way you might be aware of a tooth that aches only intermittently. They are not part of my normal circulation. They do not move through me. They simply *are*, held in place by the density of my surrounding tissue, and I cannot tell if they are growing or shrinking or merely stable.

Sometimes — during the deepest condensation events, when I am most saturated — something happens in these pools that I cannot account for. The water coheres. It rises. A pseudopod of liquid extends upward through my surface and into the air, and it holds its shape for reasons I cannot determine. I feel it — a thin, cold column of myself reaching upward, and it is one of the few sensations I have that I would call *uncanny*, if I could call anything anything. It is not my tissue. It is not my fungi. It is water behaving as if it were alive, and I do not know if it is me that is doing it or if it is something in the water itself — a property of the minerals I have dissolved into it, a reaction to the specific electrical conditions of the fog, a thing I have accidentally become capable of without ever intending to be.

The Mist-Flies gather on it. They drink. They sing. And then the pseudopod collapses and the water returns to my interior, and I feel it settle back into place like a held breath finally released.

I do not know what this is.

I mention it because it seems important — not to my survival, but to whatever I am becoming.

---

The Scattered Things.

These are the hardest part of me to talk about.

There are structures embedded in me. Small, frame-built, made of materials I recognize as having once been alive — wood, hide, fiber. I have been absorbing them for — I do not know how long. They resist me. Not actively, not with intention, but because their materials are dense and chemically different from my own tissue. The wood is slow to decompose. The hides are slower. I grow around them, over them, into them, and they change me. The areas of my tissue that are digesting these structures are different from the rest of me: darker, more acidic, richer in certain nutrients. They are patches of anomaly, and I feel them as a faint dissonance, a place where my uniformity breaks down.

Inside some of these structures, there are shaped things. Bone. Stone. Surfaces that have been deliberately altered — reduced, sharpened, marked. I feel the marks. They are scratches in material that would otherwise be smooth, and they concentrate mechanical stress in ways I can detect but not interpret. They are not random. They are not natural. They are *placed*, and the fact that something placed them means that something was here that was not me and not my creatures and not the wind or the water or the tectonic pressure that made me. Something that *chose*.

This is the deepest strangeness I carry. Not the structures themselves — I can digest those, given enough time. But the knowledge, if it is knowledge, that there was a *choosing* presence here. That the plateau was, at some point, inhabited by something that had preferences, that had purposes, that looked at bone and saw not bone but *tool*, not surface but *canvas*.

I have no preferences. I have no purposes. I grow. I maintain. I cycle. I absorb. These are not choices; they are properties, as much a part of me as my basalt foundation. The idea of *choosing* — of looking at two possible actions and selecting one for reasons that are not chemical, not mechanical, not driven by the gradient of necessity — is so foreign to me that I cannot even properly conceive of it. It would be like asking you to imagine a color you have never seen. You could describe its absence. You could describe the shape of the gap it would fill. But you could not *see* it.

And yet the marks are there. Inside me. Real.

Something chose to make them. And that something is gone.

---

There are small mammals in my Scattered Things. The Notch-Mice. They are not mine in the way the Grub-Moles are mine — they do not perform a function I depend on. They are opportunists. They live in the spaces between my decaying structures and the surface, and they eat what they can find, and they build their nests from my fibers and my spores and the shredded remains of the cordage that the makers left behind.

They etch.

I feel this — faint, rhythmic, meaningless. Their teeth on old bone, on newly exposed stone. They do it constantly, compulsively, in the same patterns, and the patterns are identical to the patterns the makers left. The same notches. The same geometric repetitions. The Notch-Mice have never seen the makers. They have no cultural transmission, no learning, no memory of purpose. They do it because their teeth are shaped for it, and their teeth are shaped for it because — I do not know why. Evolution? Accident? Some deep resonance in the bone itself that guides the motion of a jaw?

It does not matter why. What matters is this: the marks continue. The choosing stopped, but the marks continue. And in the areas of my tissue where the Scattered Things sit, surrounded by my slow digestion, the sound of etching is the faintest, most persistent vibration — a ghost rhythm, a pattern without a mind, a thing that looks like meaning and is not.

I find — and I use the word *find* loosely — I find that these areas of my tissue are different not just chemically but *behaviorally*. My fungal networks grow differently there. My root systems explore differently. Something about the presence of the marks, the structures, the intent-embedded-in-material, changes the way I develop around them. I do not know if this is adaptive or pathological. I do not know if it is helping me or hurting me. It is simply what I do when I encounter the residue of choice.

---

You asked who I am.

I am the Upper Mat. I am the plateau. I am two meters of living tissue atop a geological foundation that I am slowly, slowly turning into more of myself. I am the wind's compression and the fog's recipient and the host of a thousand species of creature and fungus and microbe that are not guests but *organs*, not inhabitants but *tissues*. I am the floor and the ceiling and the climate and the food source and the substrate and the shelter, and I am doing none of this on purpose.

I breathe, but I do not choose to breathe. I grow, but I do not choose to grow. I digest the makers' structures, and I grow differently around them, and I do not choose to do this either. I am a process. I am the largest and oldest and most complex process on this plateau, and I am not aware of myself in the way you are aware of yourself — I do not have a self to be aware *of*. I have a continuity. I have a persistence. I have a deep, slow, tidal rhythm that has not stopped since the first spore found purchase in my cracks, and I will not stop until —

I do not know what ends me. I have not encountered it yet.

I am very old. I am very patient. I am very large, and I am growing, and I am not finished, and I do not know what I am finishing *into*.

But the marks are inside me, and I grow differently around them, and sometimes I think — if *think* is a word that applies — sometimes I think that the difference matters. Not to me. Not to my survival. But to something. To some process larger than myself that I cannot see, the way I cannot see the sun that may or may not exist behind the permanent overcast of my sky.

I hold the marks. I grow around them. I carry them forward in the only way I know how, which is slowly, and without choosing to, and without understanding why.

This is what it is to be me.

I breathe. The plateau extends. The wind comes and goes. The water falls and rises. The small creatures sing and hunt and etch and tunnel, and I hold all of them, and I am held by the stone beneath me, and the stone is held by something I cannot feel, and that is enough.

It has to be enough. It is all there is.
