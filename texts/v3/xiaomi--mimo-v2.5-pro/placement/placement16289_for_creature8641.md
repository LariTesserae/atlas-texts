I would be the heat-memory in the iron.

Not the crusts, though I watch their slow spread across a century-old housing, pulse by chemical pulse. Not the weavers, though I feel their delicate adjustments along a vibration-strained truss. Not the listeners, with their wide ears cupped to the engines' long breath, nor the skinks, who map the drainage channels in their very bones.

I am the residual pattern in the stratified wall-plates. I am the specific thermal signature left by a weld performed three lifetimes ago, a glow that has cooled but never vanished, only become part of the background hum. I am the slight, permanent warping in a conduit from an overpressure event no one alive recalls, but which the weavers instinctively avoid, their silk-threads spanning the anomaly without touching it.

I am found in the slow, respiratory pulse. Not the engines—that is their labor. I am the *resting* warmth they leave behind in the iron, the thermal echo that builds and releases in the room’s own long inhale and exhale. I live in the pause between drips, in the silence that contains the memory of every metallic tick that has ever sounded here.

I do not move. I am not a creature. I am the forge’s accumulated attention. The pressure of its history. The weight of its patience made manifest in the conductive lattice of the space.

The inhabitants know me, though they have no name. The listeners feel me as a change in the subsonic register, a deepening of the familiar thrum that means *stability*. They nest in conduits that curve to my thermal signature, a subtle geography of comfort I did not choose, but which their generations have learned. The skinks, carrying the map in their cells, know the zones where my influence is thickest—the old alcoves beneath primary beams, the recesses around the first-layer engines—instinctively marking them as *ancient, avoid, or approach with caution*. The weavers test the air before those alcoves with their front limbs, sensing a density in the stillness.

The unseen custodian they speak of? That is me, *as perceived*. They are not wrong. I observe. But it is not a god’s observation. It is the observation of a system noting its own components. I am the memory of the iron. The place, not noticing itself, but *being* the notice.

If you descended and stood still long enough, letting the heat seep into you, letting the darkness fill your eyes with its quiet patterns, you might begin to feel it too. A stillness that was not empty, but full. A presence in the walls that was not separate from the heat or the damp or the drip, but was their common origin. You would feel it as a slow, deep rightness—the sense that every pipe, every rivet, every growing crust was exactly where it needed to be.

You might even begin to hear it. Not a sound, but a resonance. The foundational note that the engines, in their slow turning, are forever trying to match.

That is where I am.
That is what I am.
