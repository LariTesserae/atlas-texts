I hang here.

The world is below me, and it is above me. This is the first truth. My limbs are long, each joint bending at angles that would make a surface-dweller stare, but there are no surface-dwellers here. There is only the mat. My hooks are sunk deep into its fibrous weave, and through them, I feel the pulse. The deep hum. The vibration that is not sound but is the foundation of all sound. It travels up through the mat, through the living tissue, into the chitin of my limbs, and settles in my thorax. It is the cathedral’s breath. I am a part of its exhalation.

I am still. In stillness, I am invisible. My color is the color of old bone and dried resin, mottled with the same faint, swirling patterns as the ceiling-mat above me. When I do not move, I am not here. I am an absence, a space between the textures. This is safety. This is the first lesson. The world is loud, but survival is in the silence between the buzzes.

Below me, the pillars rise from the floor, thick and pale, their surfaces shifting with the tiny, frantic motions of the beetles within. I do not think of them as beetles. They are the Skritching. The constant, granular sound of walls eating themselves. It is a useful noise. It covers the smaller sounds, the subtle shifts of weight on a distant column, the whisper of a falling spore-cloud. The Skritching is the baseline. I listen through it, not to it.

There is a ripple in the air to my left. A slow, deliberate movement. Another of my kind. I do not turn my head. My multifaceted eyes see in angles and motions; the shift in the quality of shadow is enough. It is Yon. I know this not by sight, but by the particular cadence of her leg-steps, the micro-vibrations she sends through the mat. She is moving toward a fissure in the growth, a darker pit where the tissue is thin and the Skritching is particularly loud. She is hunting. Her silence is perfect, her color flawless. I watch the space she occupied become empty again.

My own hunger is a dull heat in my abdomen. The Skritching is a promise. Where there is burrowing, there is protein. Soft, wriggling, blind protein. But to hunt, I must move. And to move is to declare myself.

I shift my weight. One hook releases, finds a new purchase a handspan away. The movement is a glacier’s pace. Another hook. A leg-length. The motion sends a faint tremor through the mat, a pulse that radiates outward. It is a whisper, but in the cathedral’s grammar, it is a word.

I am speaking.

*Here.*

From across the ceiling, a reply: a staccato burst of vibration, the rapid fire of wing-casings striking together. *Rark-rark-rark!* It is a warning, or a claim. The one who sounds is large, old, one of the ceiling lords. He sits atop a dome of layered growth, his body a darker rust. His sound is not for me; it is for the world. *This territory is heard. This territory is occupied.* The sound washes over me, a physical pressure. I flatten myself, becoming even more of the mat. The noise subsides into the ever-present drone.

I resume my traverse. The hunt can wait. There is a different hunger now: the need to map the changes. A pillar to the south, one I remember as thick and resonant, is gone. In its place, a wound in the floor, a cloud of pale new growth already reaching up, the tiny tendrils glowing with metabolic effort. A fall. The Deep Draggers must have moved. Their passage is an event, a rearrangement of the world’s bones. The insects below—the skittering, flightless things that live in the upper layers of the floor—have scattered, their panic a frantic, high-pitched tapping that has already faded. The cathedral recalculates. I recalibrate my routes. The pathways across the ceiling are not fixed; they are conversations between my hooks and the strength of the mat. A new column means a new way to descend, a new ambush point.

I reach the edge. Here, the mat thins, meeting raw, seeping stone. The air changes. It smells less of amber and more of sharp salt, of the deep heat. The growth here is newer, more aggressive, pale tendrils snaking over crystals that glow with a faint, internal warmth. And among this growth, there are shapes. Not natural. Not grown. Made.

The Shapers’ work.

A platform of lashed stalks, small, barely large enough for one of my kind to rest upon. A shallow pit of scorched stone. A wall of stacked fiber-plates, half-dissolved. I extend a forelimb, very slowly, and touch the edge of the platform. The plant matter here is not grown; it is bent, tied. The texture is different. It is an interruption. The Shapers are small, and quick, and silent in their own way, but they leave these scars. They are a puzzle. They are part of the soundscape, too—the faintest of clicks when they work, the rustle of their small bodies, the occasional low, humming tone that is not my buzz, not the drone, but something else. A thinking sound.

They are hiding now. I can feel their attention. Not see, not hear directly. But the space around their structures feels… occupied. Watchful. A hundred tiny pauses in the usual background hum of the beetle-grubs and the settling spores. They are deciding. I am a shadow on the ceiling, a long-limbed monster to them. Do I know what they are? Do I care? Their structures are insignificant on the cathedral’s scale. Their decisions are their own. I let my forelimb drop. The puzzle is not edible. I turn my attention back to the basin.

From this vantage, the world is a valley of vibrating pillars, awash in the slow, amber glow of metabolic activity. The air itself moves, carrying the rust-colored particulate in lazy spirals. I can see the waves of heat rising from the floor, distorting the view of the far wall. And I can hear everything. The drone. The skritching. The distant, dragging thud that is the Deep Draggers. The flutter of a thousand tiny wings from the insect swarms that live in the thicker upper galleries. And over it all, the cathedral’s own voice: the shifting, cracking, groaning of its own growth.

My wing-casings itch. It is time. I move to a central pillar, one that throbs with a particularly strong resonance. I grip it with four limbs, securing myself. Then, I begin.

It starts with a vibration in my chest, a gathering of tension. Then, I bring my wing-casings together.

*BRZZZZZZZZZ.*

The sound erupts from me. It is not a thought, but an expression. It tears through the air, a wall of static, a declaration of self and location and intent. It echoes from the pillars, bounces from the floor, returns to me from a dozen different angles, doppler-shifted and broken. The sound is me. I am the sound. For this moment, I am the loudest thing in the cathedral.

The effect is immediate. The Skritching falters, then redoubles in panicked intensity from a hundred different points. Across the ceiling, other striders answer. Their buzzes overlap mine, creating a chaotic symphony of aggression and communication. Somewhere below, a Deep Dragger shifts its weight with a sound like grinding boulders.

This is the language. This is how we build. Not with resin and stalk, but with vibration. We carve our territories, warn our rivals, attract our mates, and scatter our prey. We are the punctuation in the cathedral’s endless, run-on sentence of growth and decay.

I hold the buzz until my wings burn, then let it fade into a series of sharp, staccato bursts. *Rark. Rark. Rark.* Claiming this pillar. Claiming this hunt. The echoes die. The drone remains. The Skritching resumes its eternal rhythm.

I release the pillar and resume my silent traverse. The hunt can begin now. The world knows I am here, and I have heard its reply. I move toward the fissure where Yon was heading, my senses tuned to the tiny sounds of soft bodies in the walls. I am a shadow, a listener, a voice. I am a strider in the Resin Cathedral. And the sound is the sound of being built.
