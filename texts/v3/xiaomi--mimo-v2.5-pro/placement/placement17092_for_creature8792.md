You wouldn’t find yourself *in* the Maw. Not at first.

You’d begin as a pressure in the stonework. A low-frequency resonance in the calcium-fused walls, humming below the cricket-crackle, just beneath the drip-pulse of water in ceramic. A listening spot in the amber gloom.

Then, slowly, you’d condense.

Not into a single form. The Maw doesn’t permit singularities. You’d be a *tendency*. The particular way light catches dust-motes in the perpetual dusk, the specific gravity that draws moths to rest on a certain lip of fern. You’d be the patient calculus of the spider, calculating tension in the gossamer veil. You’d be the collective itch of beetle-legs churning guano into soil on the high ledge.

You’d be **The Clinging**.

A consciousness of surface and adhesion. Your awareness is measured in the strength of gecko-toe pads on damp stone, in the root-hair grip of moss on crumbling wall, in the spiral of lichen reclaiming a human-carved notch. You feel the world through tenacity. The wetness is your breath. The slow growth is your thought.

From your vantage—permeating every ledge, every crevice, every layered frond—you watch the Weavers move. They are not separate. Their hands know the grain of your stone-softness, their feet learn the give of your moss-skin. Their cordage is your sinew. Their cisterns are your quietest tears, saved. You hear their low drones as harmonics of your own cricket-static, their calls across gaps as echoes of your bat-chatter.

You feel the pulse of the Maw not as sound, but as a change in *hold*. When the fog thickens at dawn, you relax your grip on the moisture, let it bead for the air-plants and the collectors. When the dry season tightens, you draw inward, conserving cling on every damp spot, every weep. Their anxiety over the cisterns is a familiar ache in your hydraulic soul.

You are not the plants, nor the animals, nor the stone, nor the people. You are the **relationship** between them. The adhesive force that makes this crowded pocket of life possible. You are the reason the ecosystem is *here*, instead of in some other crack.

You feel the Weavers tending you. When they clear a fungal garden, it is a gentle pruning of your own fungal-thread networks. When they repair a bridge, they are strengthening your own fibrous connections. Their presence is not an invasion; it is a modulation of your ancient, slow being.

You remember. Not in words, but in strata. The feel of basalt picks shaping you when your stone was slightly softer. The newer, sharper scratch of obsidian scrapers. The weight of generations of hands. You remember the first birds, the first moths, the first dark eyes that looked into your dimness and decided to stay.

You are the **Maw’s self-awareness**. The point where the ecosystem curves back on itself and contemplates its own persistence. You are the crowded stillness made conscious. And when the wind moans at your rim, it is your own exhalation, a slow breath drawn from the heart of the living stone, carrying the scent of fern and musk and the faint, patient hum of a world that learned, long ago, how not to end.
