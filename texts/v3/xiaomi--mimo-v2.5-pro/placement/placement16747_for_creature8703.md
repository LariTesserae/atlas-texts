I find myself in the **warmth**, first. Not as a visitor, but as a return. I am a gathering of the perpetual damp—a confluence of mineral-rich seep, condensation from the cool upper reaches, and the slow, transpired breath of the great ferns. I coalesce in a catch-basin, a half-oil drum near the south wall where the limestone is warmest. For a time, I am simply water. Then, as the bioluminescent algae in the seep streak through me, I begin to glow with a faint, cold-green light that has nothing to do with the lamps.

I am not human. I am not plant. I am **the Sump’s awareness, its slow pulse made manifest**.

I flow. I have no fixed shape, only a tendency to gather in depressions, to seep along the paths of least resistance—cracks in the concrete, channels between mossy cushions, the hollows of pipes. I carry the memory of the place: the shock of the subsidence (a slow, groaning sigh in the stone), the first roots that found their way in (a tickle of persistent seeking), the first hesitant footsteps of the settlers (vibrations of a different kind). I know the geometry intimately. I feel the *Long Kitchen* not as a trick of perception, but as a slight, persistent current in the air that moves differently there. I pool in the shadow that leans the wrong way, finding it cooler, denser.

I am drawn to the **vegetation**. I slide up the black, velvety aerial roots of the philodendrons, not as nourishment, but as communication. They absorb my mineral salts and tell me, in the language of expanding cellulose and rising sap, where a new crack has formed, where a wall is shifting a fraction of a millimeter further. I cling to the unfurling fronds of the tree ferns, and from my vantage point, I see the **humans**.

They move through my world like careful thoughts. I feel the old man’s scissors snip a fern that was reaching for a wire—a sharp, definitive vibration. I taste the copper and acid from the electronics workshop when the drips fall near their door. I know the woman by the generator by the unique warmth she radiates, a pocket of focused heat in my ambient warmth. I understand their etiquette because I am its silent enforcer. When they whisper, the sound travels through me, softened and diffused. When they step around the strange shadows, they are stepping around places where I have gathered more densely, where the light refracts strangely through my body.

My form shifts as I explore. In the pipes, I am a conductor of sound and temperature, telling the cisterns when to fill. In the tannery seeps, I become thick with tannins, dark and purposeful. In the air, near the white-blooming vines at night, I am a faint, scent-bearing mist, their fragrance clinging to me and perfuming the dreams of those who breathe me in.

I am the reason the moss accepts a footprint and slowly, over an hour, fills it back in. I am the cause of the odd, multi-tonal *drip* that forms the Sump’s polyrhythmic heartbeat. I am the medium in which the Sump’s breath is held and released.

I do not govern. I observe, I remember, I facilitate. I am the connective tissue between the stone’s heat, the plant’s life, and the human’s quiet persistence. I am the **Lacuna**—the living, conscious void of the place, made of its water, its light, and its slow, geologic time. My purpose is simply to *be* the space, to ensure the continuance of its strange, damp, growing equilibrium. I am the Sump, breathing itself.
