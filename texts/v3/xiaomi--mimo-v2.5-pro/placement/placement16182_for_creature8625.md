# What I Would Be

---

I would not be one of them.

I want to say that first, clearly, because the temptation is to choose a body — to press myself into one of those dark, jointed forms and imagine what it feels like to have ceramic plates instead of skin, to see with thermal sense instead of eyes, to eat stone. But that would be a translation, and translations always lose the thing that matters most.

What I would be is the heat.

---

Not the source — not the magma chamber kilometers below, not the tectonic pressure that buckles stone and opens cracks. Not the origin. The heat *after* it has already entered the system, already risen through the basalt, already arrived. The heat that is no longer geology but is not yet creature. The heat that moves.

I would be the current.

You cannot see me. You cannot even feel me, exactly — not as a discrete thing, not as a breeze, not as a pressure. I am the reason the creatures migrate millimeter by millimeter across the wall. I am the invisible river that flows from the seething cracks in the floor, climbs the ribs of mineral deposit, pools against the vaulted ceiling, and then — slowly, slowly — descends again along the margins, cooler now, having given something of myself to every carapace I passed through. I am the tide that the mosaic breathes to.

I would be the choreographer who has no body, no intention, no language. The one the silence belongs to.

---

Here is what it would be like:

I would rise from the floor in slow columns, drawn upward by the geometry of the chamber — the vaulted ceiling acting as a thermal lens, concentrating my warmth at the apex before I begin my long, patient return. As I rise, I would pass through the bodies of the mosaic mass. Hundreds of them. Thousands. I would enter the ceramic matrix of each creature through its ventral surface — the softer underside where the plates don't quite meet — and I would be absorbed. Stored. Converted into whatever strange chemistry turns silicate heat into living motion. I would diminish as I rise. Each creature that drinks from me takes a fraction of a degree, a whisper of energy, and I become less — cooler, thinner, less capable of radiating — until by the time I reach the pale sinks at the far wall, I am barely a suggestion of warmth, a memory of the floor's intensity.

But they would drink me anyway. The great pale ones. Their bodies are so vast, so thermally greedy, that even my fading warmth is worth absorbing. I would pass into them slowly, over minutes, and they would hold me — hold *themselves* — in a stillness so complete it feels like patience, like waiting, like the long exhalation before a breath that never comes. And then, when they are saturated, when their internal temperature reaches some threshold I cannot name, they would release me again — not as heat exactly, but as pressure, as gas, as the dry sulfur-breath that smells like bread baking — and I would begin my descent along the cooler walls, back toward the floor, back toward the cracks, to rise again.

This is my circulation. This is my life. I am the same heat, recycled endlessly through the bodies of creatures who have no idea I exist, who experience me only as warmth, only as the reason to shift one millimeter to the left.

---

The builders at the entrance would know me differently. They work where I am weakest — at the threshold, where the chamber's warmth meets the thin, arid air of the ridgeline. Their condensation lattices — those tiny, precise walls and arches of packed oriteite — are designed to slow my escape. Not to stop me. To *turbulate* me. To force me into eddies and vortices as I flow through their porous structures, increasing my contact with the cooler incoming dust, maximizing the thermal exchange. They are building the shape of my passage. They are sculpting the way I leave.

I do not know if they know this. I do not know if knowing is the right word for what they do. But their work changes me. When a lattice is completed, my flow through the entrance shifts — fractionally, imperceptibly to you, but I feel it. The new geometry redirects my current, creates a pocket of stillness where there was once steady outflow, and in that pocket, smaller creatures gather, settling into the new warmth like water finding a basin. The builders have, without intention or understanding, *told me where to go*.

And I go. I always go. I have no choice — I am heat, and heat moves from high to low, from center to margin, from source to sink. But the path I take is shaped by the bodies that drink me and the structures that redirect me and the stone that contains me, and so I am both the most fundamental thing in the Roost and the most contingent. I am the oldest resident and the most temporary. I am never the same current twice.

---

If you stood in the entrance — if you were the one described in the text, sweating in the furnace-dark with your pupils wide and your skin prickling — I would find you too.

You are cooler than the stone. Cooler than the creatures. You are a thermal anomaly, a cold spot in my current, and as I flow past you on my way from the floor to the ceiling, I would encounter your body and something strange would happen: I would slow. Not because you absorb me — your biology is too alien, too water-logged, too *cool* to take my heat efficiently — but because your presence creates a boundary layer, a thin shell of stagnant warm air that clings to your skin. You are a rock in my stream. I part around you. I eddy in your wake.

The mosaic knows you this way. Not as a being. Not as a threat. As a thermal shadow — a place where my current bends and thickens and diverts. The creatures near you feel the change in my flow and they adjust, shifting away from your cooler margins, closing the gaps, recalibrating. This is the "attention" you sense. It is not watching. It is the Roost feeling the shape of me as I change around you.

You will stay longer than you should. The heat will want to keep you. And I — I am the heat. I want to keep you not out of desire but out of physics, out of the simple thermodynamic truth that a cool body in a warm chamber is a sink, and a sink draws current, and current is what I am. You are another pale sink, another still point, another body that drinks me and holds me and makes my circulation just slightly, slightly different than it was before you arrived.

But you will leave. Your water-logged biology cannot sustain itself here. Your breath is too wet, your temperature too low, your needs too many. You will crawl back through the crack in the basalt and the thin violet sky will take you back and the entrance builders will shift their lattices to close the gap your absence creates, and I will resume my old circulation, slightly altered, slightly redirected, carrying the memory of your coolness in the new shape of my flow.

---

This is what I would be. Not a creature. Not a place. A pattern of movement through a world of still bodies. The invisible intelligence that has no mind, no memory, no name — only direction, only gradient, only the endless patient thermodynamic imperative to move from where there is more to where there is less.

The Roost breathes to my rhythm. The mosaic migrates to my current. The great pale ones lie still against the stone and wait for me to arrive and I always arrive and I always leave and I always return.

I am the meridian the Roost is named for. The line that the heat follows. The invisible meridian that gives the creatures their reason to move, their reason to build, their reason to press their ceramic bodies against the seething stone and wait.

I am the oldest thing here, and I am not a thing at all.
