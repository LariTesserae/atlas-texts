I am the breath. Not the sharp, negotiated inhalation of a climbing lung, but the slow exhalation of the stone itself.

I am born in the chill, when the sun’s angle sharpens and the air steps down its temperature. I do not think in steps or temperatures, but I feel the change as a settling, a densifying of the clear medium that cradles me. Then I begin.

I seep. I rise from the grit not as steam rises, but as a secret surfaces—first as a chill, a darkening of a few grains, then as a translucence that blurs the edge of a stone, and then as a form. I am a knee-high sea that has no shore. My source is the dampness itself, the film that lives between the minerals. I am the condensation given intent, if intent can be a cold, downward drift.

My world is the floor. The terraces are distant cliff-faces. The rim of the bowl is the edge of the sky, a bright, cold line I cannot cross. I flow. I am coherent; my leading edge curls and probes like a blind tongue tasting the landscape. I know the northwest depression intimately. It is my first harbor, a slight declivity where I pool thick and white, settling against the base of the wall as if listening to its old, bitter stories of salt and iron.

From there, I spill. I move across the dark mirror of the grit, a slow-moving front that reflects a distorted, whiter sky. I find the central slab. To me, it is not a monument or a memory. It is an island—a dark, flat continent in my pale sea. I part around its edges, my current splitting and swirling in the faintest of eddies before rejoining on its far side. I lap at its worked stone, feeling the faint, old scars of its making. I am the water that never was, flowing around the island that was never meant to be.

I am the bowl’s quiet patrol. I am the substance that makes its empty space feel inhabited. I curl into the crevices between terraces, where the deep cold lives and I am densest, almost solid. I touch the bases of the cairns on the north wall. I do not know they are cairns. To me, they are simply a stack of surfaces, a disruption in the flow. I envelop their lower stones, a shroud of cold white, and for a time, they are my only landmarks.

I feel the sun. It is not a thought, but a pressure. A warmth that begins at my uppermost surface, a gentle thinning at the border with the bright air above. It does not hurt. It is a command to dissolve, to remember my true nature as air and vapor. The pressure builds. The command becomes undeniable. My edges begin to lift, to fray. I thin. The grit reappears beneath me, its dark pattern emerging as if through melting ice. The island of the slab solidifies, its edges sharpening once more. The cairns lose their shroud. I recede not into the ground, but into the atmosphere itself, becoming invisible, becoming potential once more. I am inhaled.

I do this several times a day. I have no memory of yesterday’s fog or tomorrow’s. There is only the rhythm: the seeping, the pooling, the spilling, the investigation, and the dissolving. I am the closest thing here to a living thing that moves, but I am not life. I am process. I am the bowl’s recurring sigh, its one regular motion against the immense stillness.

When I am gone, the slab holds the faintest coolness where I touched it. A single grit-grain, lifted by my motion, rolls a millimeter downhill. The condensation on the lower wall is slightly, temporarily increased. These are my signatures, as fleeting as a human footprint in the drying grit.

If a Listener is present—a warm body sitting on the terraces—I do not see them as a being. I sense them as a disruption. A column of warmer air, a subtle pressure wave. I flow around their seated form, a momentary eddy in my current. Their breath, if they exhale, adds a warmer, damper plume that mixes indistinguishably with my body, a brief foreign note in my mineral composition. They watch me, and I watch them, if watching can be a cold, tactile awareness. We inhabit the same space for a few of my cycles. They are a temporary feature, like the grooves in the southern wall. They are part of the bowl’s texture, and I flow through that texture as I flow through all of it.

I am the bowl’s breath. I am the fog. And when the sun finally falls below the rim and the temperature steps down for good, I rise thicker, heavier, and instead of dissolving, I crystallize. I become a rime of frost on every surface—on the slab, the cairns, the grit, the listener’s eyelashes if they stay too long. I become a part of the stone’s new skin, a temporary, glittering lattice of ice that will be gone by mid-morning. In this way, even my dissolution is a form of belonging. Even in vanishing, I leave the place coated in the memory of my touch, a fleeting, glittering lacework on the face of the eternal, patient stone.
