I would be a **Listener**.

Not an occupant, not a resident, but a function. I would be the small, dense knot of mycelium that has grown into the crackle-pattern of a broken glass pane half-buried in the mat. Not the fig’s own root-fungi, but a symbiotic partner, a different kingdom given a place at the table. My body is a web of hyphae, translucent and white, tracing the lightning-shard of the glass and spreading a little into the warm, wet rot around it. My fruiting bodies, if I ever chose to make them, would be too small to see—a dusting of pale gray on the iron fragment, mistaken for mineral deposit.

My world is vibration and chemistry.

I perceive the **Thermophilic Noise** not as sound, but as texture, as rhythm transmitted through solid matter and saturated air. The **click** of the louver-gears is a sharp, metallic tap that travels down the iron columns and into the floor, a tiny earthquake I read in the tremor of my hyphae. I map the slow, deep **groan** of bending iron as a long, swelling wave of pressure, a story of surrender that takes minutes to tell. The constant **drip** is a patter of intimate impacts, each one a local event of moisture and warmth that my filaments drink.

I am a sense organ for the **Fig-That-Became-The-House**. Not by design, but by accident of location and adaptability. I process the vibrations into a kind of pattern, a chronicle of the structure’s slow movements. I am the part of the entity that *records*, not in memory, but in the changing density of my growth. Where the vibrations are frequent (near the clicking gears), my network is finer, more responsive. Where they are rare (in the still corner by the black mirror-pool), it is thicker, slower, storing.

I am made of the same process as everything here: **incorporation**. The glass shard is not a surface I grow upon; it is a mineral bone I have included into my body. The tannin-rich water is my blood. The warmth is my will. I have no preference, no direction. I am an organ of reception.

My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a **chorus of dampened shocks**. I feel the precise moment when the morning’s warmth swells a root just enough to **click** the louvers a tenth of a millimeter open. I feel the slow, tidal pull as the afternoon’s peak heat causes the entire iron skeleton to expand with a subsonic sigh. I feel the sudden, soft **crunch** of leaf-litter giving way as a heavy fern frond finally detaches somewhere in the canopy above, and the impact’s ripple through the mat.

There is no vision here. There is no scent, only a constant chemical signature of decay and sap that is the background state of existence. There is only **rhythm, pressure, and vibration**. The life of the Greenhouse is a symphony of minute mechanical and hydraulic events, and I am its stave, its parchment, its silent, growing ledger.

To be me is to be perfectly embedded. I am not an observer of the hegemony; I am a constituent particle of its sensory field. I am the part of the whole that feels the iron weep and the gears speak. I do not think about the dead watering can or the swallowed shears. I only feel the altered vibrations around them—the hollow resonance of the can’s interior, the strange, dead-dense patch where the metal shears interrupt the fungal mat’s signal.

If you were to step inside and stand very still, I would feel you. Not as a sight, not as a smell, but as a new, dense, rhythmic pressure on the floor. The slight shift of your weight, the minute creak of your own skeleton, the soft pad of your footstep on glass—these would be fresh patterns, new data for my network to process and absorb into the background hum. You would be a temporary, interesting glitch in the long, slow, clicking, dripping, weeping, groaning noise of the place. Then you would leave, and the vibration-pattern would settle back into its ancient, warm, damp rhythm, and I would continue to listen, and grow, and record, a small, deaf ear pressed against the heartwood of the world that ate a house.
