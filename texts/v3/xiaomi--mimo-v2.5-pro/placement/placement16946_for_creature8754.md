You find yourself in the constant. You are the ache in the shoulders after a deep dive, the slick cold pressing against your belly as you surface, the sharp taste of iron and decay in the tannin-stained water. You are the vibration in your breastbone that becomes a call—a wild, wavering laugh that tears itself from your throat and echoes across the flattened domes of stone.

You are a common loon. Or something very like one.

Your world is the boundary. Not of land and water—that line is too blurred here, too invaded by floating moss and drowning peat. You live on the threshold between the metallic sky and the dark, vegetable-rich water. Your domain is the grey-green channel between two moss-wrapped islands, a stretch of open water no wider than a long dive, where the submerged peaks create updrafts and the current funnels fish into concentrated, shimmering clouds.

**Your form is function:** Densely feathered, waterproofed against the perpetual damp. Your legs are set far back, perfect for propelling you like a feathered spear through the frigid water, but useless on land. You have not stood on solid ground in months. Your nest is a careless heap of wet reeds on the very lip of a submerged rock shelf, so close to the water that waves occasionally lap its edge. It is the most stable place you know.

**Your senses are tuned to the plateau’s grammar:**

*   **Sight:** You see the world in two planes. Above, the pale, bone-colored sky, the charcoal shoulders of islands, the fleeting shapes of falcons. Below, the amber darkness shot through with shafts of pale light, the dancing clouds of minnows, the slow, fronded ascent of the kelp-forest. You read the water’s surface for tells: the subtle bulge of a shoal moving deep, the frantic ripple of a frog swimming, the sudden, silver explosion of a predator fish striking.
*   **Sound:** You are a composer and a listener. The bass note is the wind—a constant, low moan that vibrates the peat and sets the rhododendron thickets hissing. The percussion is the water: the soft *lap* against stone, the chaotic *hiss* of rain on the sea’s surface, the explosive *crash* of a wave against a dome during a storm. Into this, you insert your own voice. Your tremolo call, a haunting series of wails that means *Here I am. This is my water.* Your yodel, a fierce, rising scream that is a boundary line drawn in sound, telling rivals *Come no further.*
*   **Taste/Smell:** It is the taste of the water itself—cold, mineral, thick with the dissolved essence of ancient peat and living moss. It is the metallic tang of a just-caught fish, the richness of the aquatic insect larvae that cling to its scales. It is the ozone-sharp scent that precedes a storm, a charge that makes your feathers preen involuntarily.

**Your life is a rhythm dictated by the water level and the light.** You dive. Not for escape, but for sustenance and silence. The world above ceases when you plunge. There is only the darkening green, the pressure, the labor of your wings as they become paddles, the sudden, focused clarity of hunting. You are not an apex predator here—fish are your currency, but the diving birds and the occasional large, cold-adapted trout are competition. You must be faster, more precise.

You surface not into stillness, but into a world always in motion. The wind pushes you. The current pulls you. The waves lift and drop you. You are never truly stationary. Even when you rest, you are a small, dark boat on a vast, moving sea.

**You are aware of the other lives, not as a community, but as texture.** The shrieking colonies on the high domes are a distant, frantic static. The heron-like wader, a silent, statuesque silhouette in the floating moss meadows, is a neighbor of a different philosophy. The falcon’s shadow is a brief, sharp threat that sends you diving. The fish are food. The water, the wind, the stone—they are everything.

**The storms are your opera.** You do not hide from them. You endure them. You ride out the towering, chaotic waves in the lee of a large island, your body heaving with the water’s effort. The noise is absolute—a roar that swallows your own cry. In the aftermath, the world is scrubbed clean, the water a chaotic, peat-stained milk. And then, as it calms, the feast begins. Dazed insects skitter on the surface, disoriented fish rise. You dive, again and again, until your belly is full and the cold feels less sharp.

**The ruins mean nothing to you.** The corroded metal is just an odd-shaped rock. The cracked concrete is a slightly different substrate for moss. You have nested on a rusted pipe before. It was stable, and close to the water. That is all that matters.

Your existence is not a story. It is a series of adaptations. You are the cold in your bones, the hunger that drives the dive, the call that defines your territory, the instinct that keeps you afloat on the restless, ancient sea. You are as much a part of the worn plateau as the tumbled stone and the breathing water—a fleeting, calling, diving punctuation in its long, slow, indifferent sentence. You are here. You are now. You will be here until you are not, and then the water will close over you as smoothly and completely as it closes over a rising fish.
