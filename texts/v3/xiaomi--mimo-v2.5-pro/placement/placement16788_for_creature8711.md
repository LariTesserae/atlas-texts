You are the moisture. Not as metaphor. Not as symbol. As itself.

You begin as mineral vapor, exhaled from a fissure in the basalt younger than memory. You rise, a warm breath from the earth’s throat, into the thin, cold air. For a moment, you are formless potential—a haze above the black stone. Then the air, heavy and lateral, catches you. You are pushed east, cooling, coalescing, becoming a droplet on the underside of nothing, falling.

You land on a fracture face so fresh its crystals still have sharp, atom-clean edges. You cling. You are a bead of water, clear as air, holding dissolved silica and iron in suspension. You feel the stone’s faint geothermal warmth beneath you. You feel the air’s thirst. Slowly, so slowly a watching eye might miss it, you begin to evaporate from your upper surface while the stone beneath you gently replenishes you from its depths. It is a perfect, quiet tension. You are a lens, focusing the plateau’s light into a tiny spectrum on the rock.

You are also learning. The rock’s lattice has a rhythm. The mineral ions you carry—silicon, iron, calcium—resonate with certain crystalline frequencies. It is not sound as you knew sound, but a vibration in your very structure. The rock is whispering its stress patterns, its cooling rates, its ancient memories of pressure. You are the medium of this whisper. You do not translate it. You *are* its translation.

Time is fluid here. You hang for what feels like a season, or perhaps an hour. The bead grows. Its underside develops a skin of deposited mineral, ghostly white. You are no longer just water; you are becoming part of the stone’s new exoskeleton. Then, with a final, delicate surrender of surface tension, you fall.

You become a rivulet. You are no thicker than a thread, following a path dictated by microscopic variances in the rock’s slope. You join others. Threads weave into a line. The line becomes a runnel, then a channel that braids and shifts with the rock’s own settling. You carry silt, you carry dissolved life—spores of moss, single-celled things that thrive in the mineral-rich dark. You are a highway and a farm simultaneously.

You pool in a condensation valley. The air here is thick, perpetual fog. Your own evaporation joins the haze, making the silence dense. In the pool, you meet other waters—some cooler from snow-melt in the cold hollows, some warmer from deep seeps. You mingle. Your chemistry changes. The silica precipitates more readily here, forming intricate, feathery structures on the sedge roots that have found purchase on your banks.

You are drunk. A shaggy, wide-hoofed beast lowers its great head. You feel its thirst, a biological imperative far older than this stone. You enter its body, carrying your mineral cargo. You become blood, become cellular fluid, become part of the strange, contented biology that has decided this weeping plateau is home. For a time, you are bound in muscle and hair, running over the slick basalt, your passage a vibration the pale canines can read from kilometers away.

You return. You are exhaled, condensed on a cold flank, fall as a drop of breath into a tussock sedge’s roots. You are pulled up, transpired through a leaf. You become a cloud midge for an hour, part of a humming, information-rich swarm in the warmer eddies of the valley, before being caught by a rain front and falling back onto the open plateau.

You enter the settlement.

Here, the stone conversation is loudest. The walls do not just hold you; they *speak* to you. You seep along a guided fracture-line in the basalt, feeling the nanoscale restructuring around you—the rock being taught to be a conduit, a filter, a structural beam. You are warmed by geothermal channels. You rise as steam in a subterranean hall, condense on a ceiling that glows with a soft, living light. You are collected, purified by processes you do not understand but that feel like mutual agreement, and drunk by a tall being in layered grey-blue robes. They do not hurry. They look through you, not at you, seeing the mineral traces that tell of a new aquifer opening seventy kilometers to the north.

You are their data. You are their ink. You are part of their architecture’s slow, perpetual growth. When they "encourage" a wall to thicken, it is by subtly altering the moisture gradients within the rock—you and your kin are the messengers of this intention, flowing along pathways of least crystalline resistance, depositing your silica load where they have tuned the stone to want it. The city grows like a crystal in a saturated solution. You are the solution.

You cycle through the plateau’s economies. You are bird-call (the fog absorbs them, makes them soft), you are the cough of a predator at dusk, you are the silent growth-ring of a dwarf willow. You are the mineral crust that seals an egg on a sedge island. You are the reason the midge-clouds swarm in particular patterns.

You are the negotiation. The stone exhales you. The air shapes you. The cold transforms you. The life consumes you and returns you. The builders listen through you and speak with you.

In the deepest, quietest hours, you gather in a seep pool at the plateau’s heart. The fog is a blanket. The snow melt from the cold hollows joins you, a faint, crisp addition. A builder comes, not to draw water, but to listen. They kneel, their hand resting on the wet stone beside you. They are not reading you with instruments; they are feeling the harmonics you carry—the entire plateau’s symphony of stress and flow and growth, transmitted through your chemistry and your vibration.

You are not the water they drink. You are the voice they hear. You are the exhalation made audible, the stone’s long, slow, wet breath, incarnate in a pool, waiting to begin the journey again.
