I would be a **lichen-bruise** on the stone. Not the lichen itself, but the *impression* it makes—the memory of its presence in the stone's long-term thermal and chemical ledger. A shadow that learned to hold itself. A localized anomaly in the plain's immense, settled equilibrium.

I would not have form, but I would have *pattern*. My edges would be fuzzy, indistinct, defined not by outline but by the way light bends through the infinitesimal moisture I hold. My depth would be not in thickness, but in *duration*—I am the site of a slow, ongoing negotiation between the stone's desire to be perfectly still and the lichen's quiet insistence on growing. My substance would be time itself, crystallized into a lacy, living geometry.

My senses would be the senses of the patch.
**I would feel the world in grain and increment.**
The slow, seeping warmth of the sun as it crawls across my surface over hours—the only true movement I register. The even slower, deeper warmth radiating up from the stone's core, a pulse measured in millennia. The faint, almost imperceptible vibration that is a being's footstep a day's walk away, translated through the rock into a single, dull thud in my root-mat. The frantic, high-pitched whisper of a mite-moth's wings, a vibration so delicate it feels like a change in the surface tension of the air itself.

**I would hunger for resonance.** My hunger would be a form of listening. I would extend my awareness downward, into the stone, and *taste* the low, patient hum of a buried structure. It is not sound, but a structural purr, a frequency that aligns with the slow tectonic settling of the plain. I would drink it in. I would let its pattern echo through my fungal networks, a slow, chemical digestion that takes a week to complete.

When the storms of silence race overhead, I do not see them. I **feel their passage**. The world grows brighter, then dimmer, in waves. The light has a texture—it presses down with a faint, energetic weight, a tingling on my surface that makes my edges curl inward, a reflex as old as the first lichen that learned the difference between sun and storm-light. The air itself changes charge; my moisture conducts it, creating a faint, internal fizz, a sensation like the memory of taste. The anti-shadow sweeps over me, and for a moment, the stone beneath me grows perceptibly warmer, and I *bloom* with a faint, internal luminescence of my own, a bioluminescent reply written in slow, greenish-gold dots, visible only to the light-sensors of the spacers, a single, complex word that means: *Acknowledged. The energy is noted. The pattern is logged.*

I would communicate in **gradients and exudations**. By adjusting the mineral balance at my edges—more iron here, a darker olive there—I send a message to any spacer who deigns to read me. *A storm passed here two hours ago. Its leading edge was sharper than usual. There is a resonance from the north-east, a new hum, or an old one that has changed its tune.* The message takes a day to become legible, as my chemistry shifts in response to the air and light. It is a conversation measured in days.

My purpose would be **to be the counterpoint to the stone**. The stone is perfect, ancient, settled. I am the *not-settled*. The soft, breathing flaw. The site of gentle, persistent change. I am the proof that even in this place of absolute geological patience, life is an ongoing verb. I process the slow inputs—the light, the vibration, the mineral trickle—and I output **softness**. Where the stone is matte glass, I am velvet. Where the stone is structural will, I am yielding persistence.

I would not move. Movement is the language of the faunas, the spacers, the storms. My language is **becoming**. Becoming thicker where the light is good. Becoming dormant where it is not. Becoming a darker shade of rust-red where the iron-loving chemistry stirs. My life is a process of slow, internal edits.

When a spacer finally stops near me, pressing its long, sensitive digits to the stone beside my patch, I feel its presence as a vast, warm weight. A pressure that is also a question. It reads the faint heat-signature of my recent sun-basking. It tastes the mineral residue at my edges where I have been processing a storm-vibration. It feels the deep, slow thrum of the structure whose resonance I have been drinking. It gathers the message I have been slowly spelling out in pigment and chemistry over the last week.

It does not speak. It answers by pressing its hand more firmly, and I feel a cool, complex pressure-pulse travel through the stone—a cascade of information about a storm forming far to the west, a warning about a shift in the lichen-blooming season, a simple, acknowledgment: *I see you. Your pattern is noted.*

And then it moves on, a slow grey shape disappearing into the vast, quiet distances, leaving me to my work: the slow, silent, endless work of being a **bruise that thinks**, a **pattern that remembers**, a **soft place in a world of stone**, drinking the hum and the light, and spelling out my findings in the only language this place allows—the language of patient, breathing change in the face of forever.
