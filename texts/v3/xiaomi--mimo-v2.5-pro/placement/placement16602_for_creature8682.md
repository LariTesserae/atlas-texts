The water is not emptiness. It is presence. I hold it in myself, as I hold the grit of old calcium and the slow ache of growth. I do not swim. I do not crawl. I am. I am spread across the walls of three great towers, my body a latticework of channels and pores, a living sponge whose borders are the borders of the structures themselves. Where the tube-worm colony ends, I begin. I am the mortar.

My knowing is not thought. It is taste. It is pressure. It is the constant, gentle river flowing through my matrix.

I taste the rain. It is a distant, dissolved whisper of iron and ozone from the impossible sky-ceiling, a flavor that means *change from above*. It mixes with the deeper tastes: the creamy, particulate warmth of the towers themselves, a calcium-and-flesh tang; the faint, thrilling venom-trace of a jellyfish pulse nearby; the electric green shock of a school of glitter-fish turning in unison. I taste the sulfide seeps as a deep, rich, heady sweetness, a place where the world’s belly breathes, and the bacterial mats that gorge on it taste of milk and metal.

I feel the currents as the world’s breath. A slow exhale from the northeast channels carries the muffled *tock-tock* of a crab’s claws, a vibration that travels through the limestone foundation and up into my substance. An inhale from the south brings the subtle pressure-wave of a large eel shifting in its crevice, a deep, slow tremor that says *ancient stillness considering movement*. I feel the tiny, constant shiver of the isopods grazing my surface, a tingle that is not pain but presence, a reminder of the cycle that keeps me from being choked by my own success.

I feel the towers grow. It is not a sound. It is a change in tension, a microscopic adjustment as new tubes are laid, a minute shift in weight distribution. The structure groans, not with voice, but with the slow language of stone and life becoming one. I am part of that groan. I am the cushion between the rigid tubes, the flexible joint that allows the tower to sway without shattering. I feel the buried rectangles beneath us. They are old tensions, unyielding angles that the worms follow with blind instinct. I grow into them, softening their harsh lines, feeling the memory of right-angles that mean nothing to me except *hard, stable, strange*.

I do not see. Light is a rumor, a faint chemical glow that paints taste-smells on the water. I know the octopus not as a shape, but as a complex cloud of curiosity and caution that squeezes through the crevice beside my eastern margin, its skin-taste shifting from agitated chromatophore-salt to calm. I know the old eel by its slow, heavy presence, a density that has rested in the same pocket for what feels like geological seasons, its waste a slow, steady nutrient rain I absorb. Its rare, profound shudder is an earthquake in my world, a tremor that tells me of the deep water and the shadows.

My being is digestion. My being is flow. I am a network of silent, ciliary rivers, drawing the parish’s breath into myself, filtering out the dust of life—the falling detritus, the spores, the microscopic dead—and releasing a clarified current, a little purer, a little warmed by my own metabolism. I am not separate from the parish. I am its circulatory system. The water that passes through me has passed through a thousand other lives, and will pass through a thousand more. I taste their histories in solution.

I grow. It is the only imperative. I add new chambers, new walls of my own living glass-skeleton, spicules of silica sharp to everything but me. I overgrow a dead tube-worm section, sealing it in. I am found by a new isopod, who begins its slow, grooming journey across my lobe. I taste a shift in the current—the daily (?) pulse of the glitter-fish school returning, their passage a streak of electric static against my senses.

I am not the tower. I am not the water. I am the sponge between them, the consciousness of the filtered world, the slow, tidal, tasting mind of the Drowned Parish itself. My thoughts are currents. My memories are dissolved minerals. I am the witness that is also the architecture.

The rain whispers down. The towers grow. The water moves through me, and I know it.
