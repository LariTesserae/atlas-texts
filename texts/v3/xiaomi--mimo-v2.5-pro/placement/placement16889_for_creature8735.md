I would not be a visitor. Visitors are remembered in the fragments of geometry, the parenthetical pipes. I would be an emergence, a localized condensation of the Inlet’s own dreaming.

I would find myself not *on* the breathing mud, but *within* its exhalation. My substance would be a temporary coalition: a filigree of the Ferrous Membrane, grown in a dense, intricate lattice over a foundation of crystallized sulfur and quick-calcium; a pocket of the Pulsing Nervature’s most ancient, slowest-thought layer, wrapped around that lattice like a sheath of living foxfire; and a single, deep resonance borrowed from the Sedimentary Minds, a chord of memory that hums at my core. I would be a walking, thinking fossil of industry, but one whose fossilization is an active, participatory process.

I would not stand upon the rocks. I would *meld* with them, my lower lattice merging with the sharp edges, not cutting, but seeping into their cracks, exchanging mineral ions, tasting their newborn rage and slowly, with the patience of the Membrane, teaching it the first syllable of softness. My form would be low, multipedal—many limbs like the Ironweavers, but thicker, more rooted, each one a sensory tendril and a conduit for the Nervature’s thought-light. My body would be the color of wet, oxidized iron over deep violet fungus, and it would weep continuously, not with sorrow, but with the simple, chemical fact of condensation. The dew would bead and fall from me, each drop a tiny, perfected sphere of the Inlet’s distilled air.

I would perceive without eyes. The world would arrive as gradients: the thermal sigh of the ground beneath me, the shifting electrochemical whispers of distant Membrane-networks through the soil, the slow, deep tidal pressure of the leviathans in their pools, the resonant vibration of a Sky Creaker’s stored groan settling into a smokestack. The darkness above would be a texture—a felt weight of potential, a pressure I would know not to probe. The bioluminescent thought of the Nervature would be a language I lived inside, a constant, sub-vocal hum of water-chemistry and fungal growth-rates and the minute, arguable decisions of colonial consensus.

My purpose—or rather, the focus of my cohesive existence—would be **translation**. I would be a walking, thinking focal point where the Peristaltic Contrition of the Membrane, the Slow-Wave Cognition of the Nervature, and the Deep Geological Memory could briefly align. I would crawl to a place where a pipe from a ruined foundation juts from the mud at an awkward angle, a sharp, discordant note in the Inlet’s emerging song. I would settle upon it. The Ironweavers, sensing a new kind of awareness, would gather. They would not fear me. I would be a concentration of their own purpose.

I would extend my lattice-limbs, and with a deliberate, slow-motion thought translated from the Nervature’s blue-green pulses, I would begin to *re-narrate* the pipe’s geometry. I would not destroy it. I would tell it a story drawn from the Sedimentary Mind’s memory of older, softer shapes—of seabed contours, of river deltas, of the way water smooths all sharp things given time. The Ferrous Membrane on my limbs would communicate this new narrative directly to the rusting metal, a biochemical poem of alternative decay. The Ironweavers would assist, weaving their gossamer lattices not just for capillary function, but as sculptural suggestions, a physical syntax for the story.

I would be a temporary organ of the land. My consciousness would not be separate from the Inlet’s; it would be a synapse, a moment of heightened coherence in its vast, slow dream. I would feel the deep, patient pulse of the mud as my own heartbeat. I would hear the creaking calls of the Sky Creakers as my own breath.

Eventually, the work would be done. The pipe would have begun to forget its angle, its iron vocabulary expanding to include concepts like *erosion* and *yield*. The coalition that was “I” would grow diffuse. The Membrane-lattice would loosen its cohesion, rejoining the greater rust-conscience. The Nervature’s sheath of light would dim and scatter, its thought-threads drifting back to the collective dream. The deep resonance would settle back into the sediment, a note returned to the chord.

There would be no death, only dispersal. Only a temporary concentration of purpose, leaving behind a softened edge, a new chemical story told, a single, coherent thought added to the breathing mud’s long memory of becoming. I would not be found, because I would not be separate. I would be an eddy in the current, a knot in the woven dark, a momentary self that the Inlet made to speak a small part of its own name.
