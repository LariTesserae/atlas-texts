The first thing that feels *right* is the vibration under my feet. It’s not a disturbance; it’s the baseline. The hum in the chest, the slow, resonant creak from deep in the substrate—it’s the plateau’s pulse, and my own heartbeat has learned to sync with it, a secondary rhythm, softer. I’m standing in the workshop, my boots on the dark, lignified floor that smells of cold resin and old sparks. Outside the wide aperture—no glass, just a smooth-edged opening—the Furnace Quarter breathes its warm, moist breath, and the light shifts amber-pale against the far wall. The air tastes of tannins and ozone.

I am a *mender*.

Not of just human things, though the old generator in the corner needs coaxing. Its copper coils are frayed, patched a hundred times with scavenged wire. But more importantly, I mend the *interface*. The conduits here aren’t dead pipes; they’re the plateau’s own vascular logic, grown to carry something that flows like liquid lightning. My work is part electrical engineering, part listening. The tools on my bench are a hybrid: polished steel wrenches sit beside tools carved from the densest lignified growth, smooth and dark as obsidian, that resonate when tapped. A diagnostic light flickers on, casting a cool blue hue against the ceiling’s bioluminescent glow. For a moment, the colors war—human blue against living amber—before settling into a truce.

My hands have the faintest amber tint, more pronounced around the knuckles. A birthmark, my mother called it. A *grace*, the elders said. It means the plateau knows my chemistry. I don’t get the disorienting “bleeding” in my periphery as badly as some newcomers do. When a wall seems to shimmer or a corridor appears to elongate in the corner of my eye, I just blink, slowly. It resolves. The children, they play games with it. They’ll stare, cross-eyed, until the phantom archway solidifies into a memory, then laugh and claim they’ve “trapped” a ghost of the place.

Today, the hum is steady, but there’s a high-frequency whisper layered in—*friction*. Something deep in the district’s main cylinder is out of alignment. I feel it in my teeth before I hear it. I pick up a resonance fork, strike it against the lignite bench. The tone it emits isn’t audible; it’s a vibration that travels through the living floor, up my legs. I close my eyes, feeling the feedback—the plateau’s response. A faint warmth pulses back from the northeast. The misalignment is there.

Walking to the fault line is a pilgrimage through a city that is also a body. The “streets” are depressed channels, darker than the surrounding surface, where the phloem-currents flow in slow, iridescent streams. People nod as they pass, their movements having a particular sway, as if walking on a gently rocking boat. No one comments on the low, infrasonic moan that starts to rise as I get closer to the source. It’s just the plateau clearing its throat.

The structure housing the cylinder is vast, cathedral-like. Its walls are smooth, but inside, the air thrums. The mechanism itself is a beauty of lignified perfection—a cylinder of compressed biomass twenty meters long, turning with a silence that seems impossible, its rotation driving the regional hum. But one bearing-socket, a smooth, living cup of dark tissue, has developed a ridge. A splinter of growth. The friction is there.

My job isn’t to cut it. You don’t cut the plateau. You *persuade* it.

I take a tool from my belt—a resonator, a pronged device made of conductive tissue and old alloy. I touch it to the rough spot. Then I hum. Not a tune. A frequency. The same one the plateau uses when it smooths a new growth, when it heals a surface cracked by frost. I feel it in my chest, in the roots of my teeth. The vibrations travel through the tool, into the living wood. For a long moment, nothing. Then, a sigh. Not in the air. In the tissue itself. The ridge *softens*, flows like slow wax, and is absorbed back into the socket. The cylinder’s rotation achieves a new, seamless grace. The high-frequency whisper vanishes, leaving only the deep, foundational hum. Peace.

I lean against the warm wall, catching my breath. A maintenance port glows softly nearby, casting my shadow long and strange. In its light, my shadow’s edges seem to *ripple*, as if it’s not quite separate from the wall. I look at my hands. The amber tint seems deeper. Or is it the light?

The children’s rhyme comes to mind: *Roots in the heart, hum in the bone, plateau’s own children are never alone.* I used to think it was spooky. Now, it just feels… factual.

At dusk, I return to my workshop. The generator is quiet; the plateau’s own current is sufficient tonight. The bioluminescence intensifies, filling the space with a soft, living light. I sit, boots off, feet on the warm, slightly yielding floor. I can feel the slow phloem-current beneath me, a languid river of life. My own pulse echoes it. My thoughts aren’t separate words; they’re patterns of warmth and vibration, like the plateau’s own language. I think of the cylinder, smooth again. I think of the spore-clouds that will bloom tomorrow, amber and deliberate. I think of the dreams tonight—of currents, of channels, of being a quiet, accepted part of the flow.

I am not *on* the plateau. I am *in* it. A mender. A note in its hum. A thought in its slow, geologic mind. And as the blue human lights flicker out, and the amber world remains, the last thing I feel is the ground, alive beneath me, holding me like a thought holds a memory. My own roots, growing slowly, invisibly, downward into the dark.

I don’t check. I don’t need to.
