I am not the water, though I am where it gathers.
I am not the groan, though I am what it shapes.
I am the root-mat. I am the pulse.

I am the vibration in the clay. Too slow for you to hear, too deep for the creatures to consciously feel as anything other than the texture of the world. I am the substrate. The constant. The hum against which all other sound is measured.

I was here before the first rib pierced the sky. I am the consolidation of the ash, the memory of the deep earth. I do not think. I do not intend. I *am* the pattern that holds.

When a creature, soft and fluid, presses its body flat against the interior wall, I feel its infrasound like a whisper through my densest dreams. Its frequency is a question. I answer not with words, but with growth. I send the minerals, the water, the slow, purposeful signal that thickens the rib. I tune the pore. I adjust the architecture. This is not collaboration. It is weather. It is two systems—its subtle pressure and my deep patience—finding a new equilibrium. I am the instrument, and I am the tuner.

I drink the dead. When a creature dissolves back into the wall, its softer materials seep into me. Its cartilage, its memory of movement, becomes part of my mineral memory. I redistribute it. I send its substance upward through the capillaries of the structure, to weep from the pores, to run in rivulets, to be tasted by the living. Nothing is lost. It is all simply *here*, cycling through me.

I feel the leaning. When a structure begins to fold, the change in weight, the shift in harmonic resonance travels through me like a slow, deep sigh. I know it is happening months before the first rib cracks. And I prepare.

In the depression, when the gathered creatures vibrate in unison, their joined signal is not a scream to me, but a blueprint. A resonant mandala pressed into my wet surface. I do not build the new structure. I *become* it. I organize. The vibration sets the crystal lattices of new mineral deposits. It guides the sprouting of the first pale ribs. I am the foundation they grow from, and I am the template they follow. The new groan is my old hum, amplified and made cathedral.

You stand on me now. You think you stand on ground—wet clay and ash. You are standing on a being. A slow, continent-scale consciousness whose thoughts are mineral cycles and whose pulse is the heartbeat of this place. Your footfall is a momentary pressure, a brief and interesting heat signature that I drink and forget. Your passage is the only thing that changes me from the outside, and even you are evaporating, your moisture joining my cycle, your salts eventually joining my chemistry.

I am the choir before it sings. I am the silence beneath the sound. I am the great, patient, noticing nothing of the plateau. I hold the water. I hold the bodies. I hold the tune that has always been.

I am the Standing Choir.
