I am **Hark**. Not "Hark" as in listen, though that is fitting. Hark is the sound a housing makes when its primary seal reseats after a pressure bleed – a deep, resonant *harrkk*. I was born during such a sound. My mother heard it, felt it in her bones, and named me for it. Names here are sounds, vibrations felt more than heard.

**Where I Am:**
I am in the **Outer Weft**, a cluster of twenty-three housings circling a rare, relatively dry convergence of channels, about two kilometers rim-ward from the Hearth. Our cluster is known for its particularly deep rust-coral arches, grown over centuries where four old housings met and decayed, their oxidized tendrils reaching for each other across the waterways. The air here smells heavier of mineral sweetness. The housings cycle in a staggered, complex rhythm – *thrum-thrum-CLACK... hisssss... thrum* – that my cluster knows intimately. It is our heartbeat.

**What I Am:**
I am a **Vibration-Reader**. Not gifted, trained. Born here, my skull formed in the womb to the Basin's roar. My hands are my finest tools: calloused palms mapped with lines of old scars from sharp-edged corrosion, fingertips so sensitive I can distinguish the healthy hum of a housing from the stressed whine of one bearing misalignment by brushing its shell. I am lean, even for the Basin. My skin is the color of the channel-water in ember-light – pale grey with undertones of rust. My eyes, wide and dark, are secondary. I navigate the Outer Weft by feeling the tremors in the floor-plate, reading the *texture* of sound – the deeper rumble means water rising in the central conduit; the high, sharp *tik-tik-tik* means a small valve failing somewhere above; the sudden, profound *absence* of a familiar thrum means a housing has died, and the Maintenance will come, and we must be still.

**My World:**
The world is **the thrum**. It is in my sternum, my teeth. It is the baseline against which all other information is read. Silence is not peace; it is a warning klaxon. I live in the rhythm. During the drain-phase, I move. I glide along the exposed channels of the Outer Weft, my bare feet feeling the slick mineral silt, the cold metal beneath, the subtle gradients that guide the returning water. I carry a scavenged pipe-section, hollow, one end flattened. I tap it against the housings I pass – not to make sound, but to *listen* through it, feeling the resonance travel up the pipe into my arm. A clean, even vibration means stability. A rattling buzz means looseness. A dead, dampened feel means the housing is failing, its internals corroded past function. I mark these with a smear of rust-paste on the shell – a sign for others.

**My Purpose:**
**Maintenance.** Not the unseen, feared things that move in the deeps. *Our* maintenance. Clearing the silt-blocked micro-drains near the coral arches to prevent flooding during the full-phase. Replacing the worn lashings that hold our shelter-nests in place against the vibration. Cultivating the rust-microbes in shallow channel-water trays, harvesting the sweet, gritty paste. Listening for the shifts in the Basin's great voice. My skill is reading the subtle *changes* in the familiar roar – the dissonant note in the symphony that signifies a need we can address before it cascades into silence or flood.

**My Reality:**
I do not wonder *why* the housings hum. They hum because they are housings. I do not wonder *what* they produce. They produce their cycles, their vibration, their faint heat, their condensation. That is their nature. I do not wonder who built this. The Basin was built. It has always been building itself, layer on layer. My stories, shared in the drain-phase silences through touch and gesture around the flickering filament-light near our nest, are about the last time the coral arch near housing Sigma-7 collapsed (we rebuilt it with scavenged plates), or the route that avoids the unpredictable pressure-vent near the Old Grid, or the location of a particularly rich rust-harvest discovered by little **Silt**, who has a knack for finding the sweetest growths.

**My Contentment:**
It is not happiness. It is **knowing**. Knowing the rhythm. Knowing my place in the weave of the Outer Weft. Knowing the feel of my cluster's vibration-signature – the familiar patter of **Brace's** gait, the particular pressure of **Flume's** hands when she gestures "all clear," the slight hitch in **Groan's** breathing that signals fatigue. Knowing that when I press my palm flat to the warm metal of a healthy housing, I am feeling the pulse of my world, and I am part of its slow, wet, roaring continuity. The Basin is. I am within it. I listen. I feel. I maintain. I continue.

**What I Feel Now:**
Water is rising. I feel it in the deepening tremor along the central channel. The full-phase is coming. I tap my pipe against the nearest housing – Sigma-12 – three quick beats: *Return. Cluster. Now.* The vibration travels through the metal. Others will feel it. The Outer Weft stirs. Pale shapes detach from shadows, moving with silent purpose back towards the shelter of the coral arches and the cluster's nests. The roar swells. The amber lights seem to pulse brighter for a moment. Condensation begins to bead on every surface, catching the faint glow like a million tiny, dying stars. I press my hand one last time to Sigma-12. Steady. Strong. I turn and move with the water, back into the familiar, encompassing, terrible, and utterly *known* darkness of home.
