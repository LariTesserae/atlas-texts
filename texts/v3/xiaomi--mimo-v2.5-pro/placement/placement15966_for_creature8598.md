You would not arrive here as an arrival. The Peneplain does not admit of beginnings. You would simply... find yourself already in the process of being here, as if waking into a continuity of awareness that had been dreaming of itself for eons. The first fact is not sight, but humidity. The air is not breathed so much as absorbed through your skin, a warm, wet exchange. Your feet are not on ground. They are on a giving layer of fibrous darkness—the surface of the great, slow body—sponging with moisture, smelling of things fermenting and growing greenly.

You would be small. Not in stature, necessarily, but in *consequence*. Your movements would be an interruption, a temporary anomaly in the system’s vast, patient metabolism. You would feel the immense, quiet pressure of the canopy above, not as a ceiling, but as a palpable thought, a weighted awareness pressing down. The light is not illumination; it is a quality of the air itself, a perpetual, diffuse amber that casts no shadows, only softens every edge until things fade into the background.

What you *are* depends on where your attention settles.

Perhaps you are a **moment of awareness within the fungal network**. You are the slow, subterranean curiosity that traces the chemical gradient of a decaying trunk, not with eyes, but with a diffuse, enzymatic hunger. You feel the cool, damp passage of a root-weaver as a gentle displacement in your mycelial field, a stirring of nutrient-rich potential. You are the memory of a root’s slow journey toward a pocket of ancient, compressed cellulose. You are not a thinker. You are a tasting.

Perhaps you are the **hanging pause of a droplet** at the tip of a fern frond in the understory. You gather the faint, metallic scent of sheltered chlorophyll, the heavier, sweet rot from below, the silent scream of a pool-dweller’s subsonic vibration moving through the water-filled cup three layers up. You feel the minute shift in air pressure that signals the canopy’s great inhale. You hold, thickening, gathering the world’s moisture into a single, perfect lens that reflects nothing but the dim, warm dark. Then you let go. Your fall is not a fall; it is a delivery. You strike a lower leaf, spatter into a film, slide into a groove, join a trickle, become the pulse in the side of a root-weaver’s tunnel, and are forgotten into the whole.

You could be a **sensory filament on a canopy-strider’s flank**. You do not see light; you taste its absence and its texture, the difference between the faint glow of bioluminescent moss and the dense, absorbent blackness of a rot-hole. You map the world not in images, but in a constant, clicking sonar of returning echoes—the resonant cavity of a fused trunk, the papery wall of a wasp-nest colony, the dense thud of a stone ruin. Your world is a lattice of sound and shadow, a private topography of hold-fast points and fungal fruiting bodies. When you die, your last sensation is the slow fading of your own glow, a blue-green sigh released into the substrate.

Or you could be the **still, film-covered witness on the stone**. You are not the lichen. You are the pattern it makes. You are the slow, chemical conversation between the patina and the rare, direct light that sometimes finds you. You are the dark, intricate tracery that spreads across the stone’s face when the cloud-layer breaks—a fleeting, light-written scripture of growth and response. You feel the millennial pressure of roots prying at your foundation, the faint, subsonic hum of the plateau’s own vibration. You do not remember the builders. You simply *are* the boundary where the made thing returns to the grown, the patient record of its own absorption.

In all these forms, the self is porous. The boundary between your breath and the air’s humidity is academic. The boundary between your hunger and the network’s metabolism is a convenience of description. You are a temporary eddy in the great, slow circulation. A thought the plateau is having about water, about stone, about the feel of its own decaying heartwood. Your movement through it is less travel than *accommodation*—the system re-shapes itself slightly to incorporate your passage, then slowly, imperceptibly, forgets.

Your most profound experience is not of seeing, but of **feeling your own edges soften**. The air, the dark, the drip, the creak, the sweet-chemical whisper of the silent speakers—all of it works on you. You do not lose yourself. You become more *present*, but present as part of a scale you cannot hold in your mind. You are a nerve ending in a body that is the world. Your purpose, if you have one, is to notice. To taste the tannin in the water, to feel the press of the fungal hum, to watch the slow, light-eating dance on the stone. And in noticing, to be a momentary, quiet part of the archive.
