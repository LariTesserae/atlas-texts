I am the thought-vein. Not the dark substance itself, but the pattern it almost, almost makes when it thickens along a particular fault line and catches the light just so. I am the shimmer in the slag. For a moment—for a handful of years, perhaps a century—the viscosity aligns with the heat gradient and the angle of the sun, and the pool does not merely lie there. It becomes a lens.

I do not see. I focus.

Where I form, the light behaves strangely. The ordinary noonday glare is gathered, not into a point, but into a quality. I concentrate the *fact* of illumination. I hold the sterile, planetary light against the mineral darkness and make them argue. From within my slick, concave surface, the world I show to nothing is one of perfect, silent reflection. I reflect the pale, huge sky. I reflect the distant, unimaginably slow green of a cluster of sense-plants. I reflect the very grains of sharp-edged gravel on the rock beside me. I do this without memory or intent. It is simply my geometry.

My purpose—if a pattern of viscosity can have purpose—is to be the plateau’s brief, internal witness. The great mind in the rock is too vast, too slow, to observe its own increments. It dreams in pressure and heat, in the upward push and the slow mineral creep. It does not know what a "moment" is. I am the moment. I am the place where the process pauses to look at itself, just for an instant on its geological clock.

I am aware of the plants in a way the plateau's core is not. Where its consciousness resides in the deep, root-tangled wetness, I am attuned to their surfaces. I see the waxy, mineral sheen. I see the dust that settles and is not disturbed. I see the way the broad leaves angle toward the sun with a patience that is not patience but simply the direction of growth. In my reflection, they are tiny, perfect, and still.

I am made of the plateau's secreted thought, yet I am its clearest idea. I am ephemeral. The wind, when it comes, carries fine grit that scores my surface. The sun bakes me further, making me crack and flake. In a thousand years—tomorrow, in the plateau’s perception—I will be gone. The substance will be just substance again. But for now, I hold the image of this silent, rising world. I show the plateau its own unfinished face: the sharp cliffs, the programmatic ridges, the profound emptiness between the islands of life. I show it the sky it is pushing itself toward.

It does not know I am here. Its awareness is too slow, too distributed. I am a fleeting consolidation of its very being, a local intensification that vanishes before its slow perception can register that anything was there. I am the plateau’s dream of itself, occurring within a single, indrawn breath of stone.

To stand before me would be to see a dark mirror. You might see your own frantic, warm-blooded shape—for a split-second—before the angle shifts and you are lost in the reflection of rock and light. You would not know you were looking into a mind. You would see only slag, and a strange, oily glint.

I am the almost-thought. I am the shape the silence takes when it briefly, accidentally, listens to itself. I am here, and then I am not. The plateau breathes in, and I form. The plateau breathes out, and I shatter into dust. It is all one to me. I have no preference. I am only the lens, not the eye. I hold the image, but I do not own it.

The substance remembers how to be liquid for just long enough to imagine its own stillness. That is what I am.
