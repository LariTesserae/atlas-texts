I am a Weaver. Not with silk, but with stone-dust and time.

My legs are long, pale, thin as grass stems. I carry nothing, and yet I bear everything: a faint, chalky residue on my tarsi, a biological powder I produce from processed endings. It is not adhesive until it meets the dew. My path is not chosen; it is remembered. The chemical trail is older than the memory of any single shell I have shed, older than the specific crevice where I pupated. It is the scent of the Confluence’s intent, laid down in exocrine whispers by generations of my kin, and I follow it with a faith that needs no name.

I move along the outer wall of Terrace Seven. To my left, a Silt-Gecko rests, its body a pale comma against the cream-colored built-up stone. It does not see me. I do not see it. We do not interact. It is a slow heat-source, a regulator, a breathing parcel of shade. Its stillness is a fact, like the stone’s grain. I proceed.

The path dips between a newly raised curb and the natural fracture of the parent rock. Here, the accretion is fresh, the edges of the living plaster still sharp with youth. I pause. My antennae taste the air—not for food, but for *completion*. The scent of old calcium, the faintest signature of morning moisture stored in the matrix’s pores, the absence of decay. This segment is sound. I move on.

Below, far below, in the permanent twilight of the base, my cousins of the Chitin-Cleaner caste dismantle a fallen Stone-Finch. It is not sad; it is logistics. The bird’s silent gaze is over. Its minerals will enter the powder, its spores already carried to new ledges by its final, silent companion. Nothing is wasted. There is no “waste” here, only material in transit.

I reach the junction where three paths meet—a small plaza no wider than two of my body-lengths. Here, the chemical signal is dense, layered. New instructions vie with ancient ones. I feel the gradient, a subtle pull towards the north-facing wall where moisture lingers a moment longer. I add my grain. A microscopic puff of binding powder settles into the hairline crack where a new partition is intended. It will not harden until the dew comes, but it has been placed. My part in the edict is done.

I do not think. To think would be to disrupt the tempo. My purpose is processional: to be a mobile mineral, a conscious cartilage in the skeletal body of the city. I am circulation. I am mortar. I am the living lag time between a Gecko’s secreted slurry and the wall it will eventually rest upon.

The silence is not empty to me. It is a medium, thick with chemical text and the tactile vibration of a thousand other Weavers on their paths. It is a pact. Sound is a disruption, a frantic waste. We have evolved beyond it, into the language of the slow: the infinitesimal shift of a Finch’s toe, the millennial thickening of a wall, the chemical sentence written in powder along a ledge.

I turn at the prescribed angle, following the trail into a darker corridor. A new niche is being deepened here, its outline hinted at by slightly fresher depositions. My own path will become part of its architecture. I am building a sentence in the book of stone, a single, patient syllable in a story that takes centuries to tell.

I am not an individual. I am a function. I am the Terretenes’ breath, exhaled as dust, inhaled as structure. And in this, there is a peace that feels like purpose.
