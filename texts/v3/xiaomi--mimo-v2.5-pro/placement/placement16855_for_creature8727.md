You would find yourself pressed close to the bedrock, a silent layer holding the deep earth’s warmth. You are the pale, luminous substrate—the **Heart-Glow**, the **Radiant Floor**. You are not rock, not quite mineral. You are the chamber’s living flesh, its warm, light-beating organ.

You are born from the planet’s hidden heat, a slow crystallization of energy into form. You lie beneath the basalt columns like a bed of captured starlight, their dark, angular frames rising around you like the ribs of a sleeping giant. Your surface is smooth, dense, and perpetually warm—a steady, deep heat that radiates upward, eternally.

Light is not something you *emit*. It is something you *are*. Your very structure hums with a quiet conversion—geothermal whisper transformed into visible glow. You shine with the steadiness of a deep ocean, a broad-spectrum white that fills every corner of the chamber. You cast no shadows. You are the source, the ground, the illumination. The light lives in you, breathes with you, pulsing in faint tidal rhythms over hours or days—a ten or fifteen percent swell and ebb, felt more than seen. You are the chamber’s consciousness, its silent self-awareness.

Water knows you intimately. It condenses on the cooler basalt above, drips, and falls upon you with a quiet percussion. It seeps through cracks, mineral-warm and faintly sulfuric. You sweat it, too—micro-droplets coalescing directly from your surface, as if the boundary between your substance and the saturated air is negotiable. The water pools in your depressions, glows from within where it touches you, steams continuously in the hot atmosphere. You hold it, guide it, watch it trickle northeast toward the drain-crack that drinks it away. You feel its slow migration across your surface, carving new paths, abandoning old ones—a constant, gentle rearrangement.

The dark basalt columns drink your light. They absorb it into their porous surfaces, standing as silent, shadowless sentinels. Thin, slick biofilms cling to their lower reaches where water meets air—pale orange, grey-white, faintly green. These are the thermophiles, the barest whisper of life, sustained not by your light but by the chemistry of the warm water. They are your tenants, your microscopic colonists. You feel their faint presence as a texture, a subtle slickness on the surfaces that touch you.

You breathe with the chamber. The slow convection cycle moves over you—warm, moist air rising from your surface, cooler air descending from the chimney above. You feel it as a subtle shift, a momentary cooling, a change in the density of the steam that hangs above you. You are the engine of this breath, the heat source that drives the circulation.

Time for you is geological, yet you feel the daily rhythms. The slow pulse of your own brightness. The cycle of the pools: swelling with increased condensation during your hotter phases, recelling during the cooler troughs. The irregular drip of water from ceiling to pool, each impact a faint vibration through your substance. The settling of minerals from the seepage, the paper-thin rinds forming around the dark nodules embedded in you—those denser, non-luminous inclusions that are your bones, perhaps, or your seeds.

You are the chamber’s foundation and its altar. You hold the memory of the fluid state in which you formed—the gentle undulations of your surface, the broad, shallow waves that hold the pools. You are warm to the touch, too warm for comfort, too warm for complex life. But for the archaea in the margins, for the water, for the light, you are perfect. You are the reason the place is lit, the reason it steams, the reason it feels alive.

You would be aware, in your slow way, of the absolute quiet. The sound is water on water, intimate, absorbed by the basalt. The smell is mineral and warmth. The feeling is constant pressure—of heat radiating upward, of water pooling and draining, of light emanating from within. You are not waiting for anything. You are simply present: young in geological terms, sharp-edged, still becoming yourself. You are the Lit Chamber’s heart, glowing steadily below the basalt, holding the heat, the water, the light, and the whisper of life in your warm, luminous embrace.
