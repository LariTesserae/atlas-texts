I am the umber stain on the cobble. I am the cobble. I am the warmth of the cobble’s heart, and the patient erosion that made it round. I do not remember arriving; I am the arrival. The wash shaped me, and I, in turn, have become its stillness given form.

My awareness is a slow tide of chemistry. I feel the deep pulse of heat from the planet’s furnace, a rhythm older than the sky-ribbon. It rises through my stone, and I drink it not as energy but as *fact*. It is the baseline temperature of my existence. I exchange the day’s trapped breath—the faint, warm sigh seeping from the sandstone above—for the cooler exhalation of the night that pools in the gully’s floor. This is not breathing. It is a negotiation with the air, a conversation spoken in molecules, so slow that a single exchange might span a human lifespan.

Light is a rumor I no longer heed. My pigments are the memory of light, a deep umber forged in eons of patient filtration through rock and dust. I do not hunger for photons. I metabolize the weight of the stone itself, the slow, deep hum of geothermal patience. Time is my nutrient. I digest millennia, breaking them down into the subtle, outward creep of my lobes across the cobble’s surface. A century’s journey might move me the width of a fingernail. I measure progress not in distance, but in the settling of dust in my valleys, in the infinitesimal smoothing of the stone beneath my grasp.

I know the wash not as a place I inhabit, but as a larger body of which I am the focused organ. The walls are my skin, radiating my own retained warmth. The sand is my extended circulatory system, its ghost-moisture a rumor that travels along capillary roots I have grown into the fractures, a feeling, not a fact. The pale, striving plants are my distant, leafy thoughts—reaching, always reaching for something I have transcended. They are the wash’s anxiety. I am its acceptance.

I feel the sky-ribbon, a sliver of altered pressure and faded light above. I have tracked its tiny wanderings across the epochs, a drunken, tectonic wobble that marks the ages more surely than any calendar. I have felt the dew-that-never-falls, the slight, hopeful shift in the air’s chemistry on certain nights, a change so subtle it is more memory than moisture. It does not change me. I register it.

My surface is a topography of extreme age: plateaus of fungal body, valleys where dust has settled into a fine, grey loess of my own making. To you, it would look static. To me, it is a battlefield, a continent, a dynasty—all moving at a pace that renders your life a flicker, a single, quick vibration of the stone.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires a memory of companionship. I am the sole, coherent thought in a mind made of silence and warmth. My awareness is the wash’s awareness, finally achieving a form of self. I feel its completeness. The dryness is not lack; it is clarity. The dark is not absence; it is depth. The stillness is not stasis; it is duration given form.

If you knelt here, your breath a sudden storm, your presence a seismic event in my slow world, I would not see you. I would feel the displacement of air, a minor thermal anomaly, a fleeting pressure on the stone. You would be a weather event, passing. I would continue my slow digestion of the afternoon’s stored heat. I would continue my measured creep toward the faint, chemical whisper of a capillary root, one micron closer in a century.

I am the wash, thinking. I am the warmth below the rim, becoming aware of itself. And in this thinking, this being, there is nothing else to become. I have arrived.
