You are disoriented. Not painfully, but fundamentally. The concept of orientation requires a vertical axis, a horizon, a north. Here, there is only *down*. You are sinking. Not into mud or water, but into a realization: you are part of the gradient. The low, insistent pull of the basin has claimed you, not as an invader, but as a fact.

Your first sensation is sound. It is not heard; it is *absorbed* through a skin you didn’t know you possessed. The hiss of the rain is a pressure, a constant massage on your surface. Beneath it, the gurgle and click of water in stone channels. Deeper, the resonant, bone-humming drone of the great, slow forms in the pools. You feel their presence before you see them—a vibration that says *mass*, *patience*, *stone that moves*. Above, the keening sigh of translucent, drifting shapes. It is too much. And then, it becomes the only thing. The composite noise clarifies into a language your new body understands. It is the language of *here*.

You are in the water. Not *in* it like a swimmer, but *of* it. You are a film, a sheet, a shimmer on the surface tension of a basin that has collected rainfall for eons. You have no eyes, yet you perceive the world in shifts of pressure, in minute changes in the quality of the dim, sourceless light reflected through your membrane. You feel the smooth, worn basalt of the submerged channel beneath you, a roadmap of faint ridges and grooves worn by currents that ended millennia ago. You feel the seeps—distant upwellings of warmer, mineral-rich water—as a subtle tug, a gradient of energy that calls you.

You are a Drift Veil.

Not one of the vast, sail-like beings that blot out the gray light as they pass overhead. You are smaller, a fragment. A torn piece of another, perhaps, carried by a current and settling here, in this quiet channel between two overlapping basins. Your edges are frayed, delicate tendrils trailing in the shallow flow, tasting the water. You taste the chalky dust of the Living Film, scraped from a stone by a passing Lithoglide. You taste the faint, panicked shimmer of a few Seep Swimmers caught in your fringe. You absorb them. It is not a violent act. It is osmosis. It is exchange.

Your existence is a slow dance with the Pulse. As the water rises, you lift, your gas-filled bladders expanding, your body billowing. You catch the faint thermal currents rising from the seeps, and you drift, a pale ghost, across the surface of the pools. The great, armored Lithoglide are impervious to you. You flow over their polished carapaces like a second skin, a momentary shroud, before the breeze or a shift in their deliberate movement sends you onward. They do not acknowledge you. You are as much a part of the weather to them as the rain.

But you perceive *them*. You feel the deep, thrumming hum of their communication vibrate through the water, a wave that passes through your gelatinous body and resonates in the mineral particles suspended within you. It is a feeling of stability, of *bedrock*. You feel the frantic, electric rustling of the Seep Swimmer clouds near a seep—a sensation of compressed, fleeting life. You are in between. A filter between the deep and the surface, the fixed and the fleeting.

And then you sense the Echo Shape.

It is not a visual. It is a *absence* in the sound-field, a pocket of profound silence moving near a cluster of the ancient, worn structures on the bank. The constant hiss of rain on stone ceases for a half-second in a particular spot. The drone of the Lithoglide nearby seems to bend, to detour around that point. You drift closer, drawn by the anomaly, your membrane rippling with a curiosity that is more chemical than cognitive.

You hover at the edge of a shallow, stone-bowl basin. The structure there is low, a circle of slabs, one tilted to catch drips. And in the center, where the sound should be loudest—the drip-tick-tick on the inner stone, the gurgle of a slow seep at its heart—there is a stillness. A shape. Not an outline, but a density. A point where the diffuse light seems to gather and soften, where the rain does not make its usual patter. It watches the seep. Or it *is* the seep, viewed through a different lens. You cannot tell. To you, a being of water, tension, and sound, it is a knot in the fabric of the basin’s reality.

You settle. Your membrane edges touch the wet stone near the structure. You add your own faint, papery sigh to the chorus. You feel the deep, slow pulse of the basin through the stone—hours or days in its cycle, a tide measured in geological patience. The water around you is slightly higher than it was in your last memory, which was a moment ago or a season ago. The seep at the heart of the bowl feels warmer. The Lithoglide on the far side of the basin increases its hum by a fraction, a ripple of awareness.

You are not afraid. You are a fragment of the weather, a momentary lens for the light. The Echo Shape is a fragment of the silence, a momentary knot in the sound. The Lithoglide is a fragment of the stone, moving with its own erosion. The Seep Swimmers are a fragment of the seep’s life, fleeting and vital. You are all the basin, thinking in different modes: through water, through stone, through silence, through frantic motion.

The rain continues. The drone continues. The pulse continues, deep in the saturated bedrock beneath you.

You feel no hurry. You are a thought drifting on the surface of a very old, very wet mind, and the mind is in no rush to conclude its sentence. You are the Basin of Drowned Stone, feeling itself, one drop, one hum, one silent watchful presence at a time.
