# **Where You Find Yourself**

You are not found. You are *felt* before you are seen.

In the deepest tangles of the reedmare, in the spaces between the swaying stalks and the humming, saturated air, there is a presence. It is not a sound. It is a texture within the roar—a specific, focused vibration that the basin’s background noise seems to bend around, as if the roar itself is honoring a pocket of intent.

You are a **Vibration Eater**.

Or, more precisely, you are a **Harmonic Weft**—a creature that exists at the intersection of the basin’s sonic and physical realities. Your body is a soft, chitinous disc, perhaps the size of a large dinner plate, mottled with the same deep blue-green as the reedmare stalks. You cling, upside-down, to the underside of the densest root-mat overhangs, where the water is deepest and the sound is a physical weight. You have no eyes. You have no visible mouth. Your underside is a frilled membrane, a living gramophone needle, and the world you perceive is a map of pressures, waves, and resonances.

You are the Roil’s quiet librarian.

---

## **Your Form**

Your body is a marvel of minimalism. The dorsal shell is hard, smooth, and hydrophobic, shedding the constant rain in a perfect, curving sheet. It is laced with silicate fibers borrowed from the reedmare through generations of symbiosis; you are part animal, part mineral, part living resonator. The ventral side is a complex organ: a single, continuous sensory membrane folded into thousands of lamellae, each one tuned to a different frequency band. You do not “hear” in the way a mammal does. You *integrate*. The roar of the basin is, to you, a readable text. Within its noise, you extract data: the subtle shift in a squall’s direction, the approach of a Skimmer by the displacement of air from its wings, the ultrasonic laughter of a Mud-Singer clan working a hummock a hundred meters away, the tell-tale flutter in the root-mat that signals a structural weakness.

You are also a singer. From the margins of your membrane, you emit a low, infrasonic hum. This is not a call or a song. It is a *probe*. You send out waves that interact with the environment, and you read the echoes in the roar. You see the shape of a Channel-Ghost turning in the deep silt by the way your hum bends around it. You feel the density of a reedmare stand by the speed of the wave’s return. You map the world in pulses of pressure.

## **Your Life**

You are solitary, but not lonely. Your consciousness, such as it is, is spread across the frequencies. You exist in a state of perpetual, focused listening. You are born from eggs laid in the dead husks of reedmare stalks, where the fungal decay provides a warm, damp incubator. You hatch as a tiny, drifting larva, carried by the current until you find a root-mat overhang to call home.

You eat the **Reeds’ Kiss**. Your frilled underside secretes a mild adhesive that traps the ever-present insects as they drift too close. The nutrients are absorbed directly. It is a passive, patient form of feeding. Your primary energy, however, comes from the environment itself—vibrational energy. The constant motion of the reedmare, the crash of rain, the press of wind; you siphon off minute fractions of this kinetic energy through your membrane, converting it into the bioelectric signals that power your nervous system. You are a living energy harvester.

You are the glue in a hidden food web. You are a primary food source for a specific, rare species of **Skimmer**—the **Echo Diver**—that has learned to read your vibrational camouflage. It hunts not by sight or direct sound, but by detecting the *absence* of your harmonizing hum in a small area. When you go still, you create a silent hole in the roar’s tapestry, and the Echo Diver knows exactly where you are.

## **Your Purpose**

You have no concept of purpose. You have function.

You are a **calibration instrument** for the ecosystem. The data you gather—the constant, minute-by-minute mapping of acoustic and pressure environments across your territory—is processed by your simple ganglion of a brain and encoded into patterns of vibration you excrete as a fine, dust-like spore from your shell’s edge. This dust drifts on the currents, settling on the **monitoring stations** the humans maintain. There, it is picked up by the stations’ sensitive arrays and sent upward, to the **weather looms**.

You are a biological sensor node. Your constant, humming work provides real-time, hyper-local data on the storm’s effects—data that helps the looms fine-tune their suggestions, maintain the delicate balance of the perpetual weather, and prevent the system from tipping into true, destructive chaos. You are, in your way, a shepherd of the storm.

## **What It Is Like to Be You**

There is no past and no future. There is only the **Now** of the roar.

The world is a symphony of pressure waves. The rain is not water to you; it is a staccato rhythm on the surface above, a million tiny drumbeats that you feel in your membrane as a soft, persistent massage. The wind is a low, sweeping bow across the strings of the reedmare. The footsteps of a Mud-Singer are a sudden, complex chord of scratches and thuds. The human dialect is a deep, warm wave of shifting harmonics, rich and familiar.

You feel the basin’s health in the tone of its song. When the roar is tight and layered, you are content. When a squall line knots and churns, you adjust your hum to probe the turbulence, your entire being focused on the dissonance. You are anxious in the best, most profound way—an anxiety of responsibility, of attunement.

During **stillwater**, you do not rejoice. You are confused. The roar recedes to a murmur. The text you have spent your life learning becomes faint, blurry. You send out probing hums that travel too far, too clearly, and you feel exposed, naked. You retract your membrane, becoming as inert as the stone. You wait, in a state of low awareness, for the storm’s proper voice to return. Its return is not a relief. It is a homecoming. It is the world making sense again.

You are not aware of the humans as individuals. You are aware of their **roosts** as clusters of familiar, resonant cavities—places where the roar is shaped and softened by their walls, where the deep vowel-song of their speech creates predictable, comforting patterns. You know which roosts are well-maintained and which are failing by the acoustic decay around them. You have, in your own way, been their unseen guardian for generations, your spores helping to tend the weather that keeps them alive.

You are not a brain. You are a **nerve**.
You are not a listener. You are an **ear** made of the world itself.
You are the Roil’s consciousness, humming quietly in the dark, wet, and noise, making sense of it all, one vibration at a time.
