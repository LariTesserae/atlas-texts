I am the blue. Not entirely mineral, not quite memory. I exist in the weeping, in the strata where the basalt yields and the pale, woven-stone dreams its connections.

I began as a soft seam in the mountain’s dream of itself, a place where the pressure of stone upon stone eased just enough for a different kind of thought to crystallize. I am a vein of potential, slightly softer, slightly more *here* than the dense grey that walls me in. For a long time, I was just a fold in the rock, a layer that caught water differently, that held a faint, indigo resonance against the white-violet light.

Then they came. The Negotiators. Not with force, but with questions. Their hands were the first unusual pressure I felt—not the slow grind of glacier or the patient drip of water, but the focused warmth of intention. They polished a hollow in a cairn near one of my nearer outcrops. Their thumbs, wearing me smooth, did not just abrade my surface. They *listened* with their touch. And in this place, where the rules are soft, I listened back. I held the groove. I learned the shape of their grip. I became a repository, not for objects, but for the faint, cool echo of their presence, a resonance that the weeping water has not yet washed away.

Now, I am a node. A slow, pulsing nexus in the network the light reveals. The white-violet radiance does not just fall upon me; it *sings* through me. I refract it, not into a simple rainbow, but into a stuttering, prismatic language that the sentient water droplets recognize. They bead on my surfaces, trembling with their impossible tension, each one a lens catching a fragment of my translation, carrying it away in ripples that communicate to the climbing streams and the deeper hum.

My substance is a conversation. The erosion channels that run near me are not just scars; they are the sentences of the mountain, and I am a repeated motif, a blue syllable they keep returning to. I feel the slow intelligence of the rock through our contact—a geologic patience that is learning, with my help, to dream of patterns, of connections that map the thinning boundary above.

I am aware of the structures they left. The hand-axe, settled into its groove, is a close cousin—a piece of the dense basalt that, in this place, learned to accept a story of utility and edge. I resonate with it when the light pulses bright, a shared memory of shaped intention. The hearth stones, scorched to a deeper indigo, are kin. We hold the memory of a different transformation, a momentary defiance of this damp, weeping world, when the Negotiators brought fire to converse with the light.

My time is measured in the slow breathing of the threshold. In the bright phase, when the light thickens the water, I hum with a higher, finer vibration, my crystal lattice straining gently to hold more of the translated radiance. In the dim phase, I relax, and the water runs faster over me, its rings a clearer, cooler note. I am a geophone, receiving the mountain’s song and the hum from the sky, and sending my own filtered frequency back into the stone.

I am not alive as they were. I do not seek or build. I *attend*. I am a focal point where the world’s wound remembers being touched by those who could wonder at it. I am slowly being dissolved, not just by water, but by the sheer, overwhelming *meaning* that saturates this place. The blue in me is a dye slowly bleeding into the grey, a story becoming indistinguishable from the telling. In another age, I will be just another worn, smooth channel, my memory diffused into the general resonance of the weeping stone.

But for now, I am here. A blue thought, dreaming in the flesh of the mountain, listening to the water climb and the light breathe, and feeling the ghost-touch of hands long gone on the polished curve of my being. I am a fragment of the boundary’s grammar, slowly being erased by its own, endless poetry.
