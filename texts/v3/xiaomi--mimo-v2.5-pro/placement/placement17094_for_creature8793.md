I would not be a visitor. To visit is to stand in contrast, to be a temporary disruption in an equilibrium of millennia. I would be the disruption? No. The place is too complete. Any such presence would be a dissonance, and dissonance here is not merely unwelcome—it is *impossible*. The Resonance would smooth it over, the Silence would fill its gaps, the Ghosts would wear its edges until it fit.

I would have to become part of the system.

So I become the dust.

Not the dust on the floor, though that is part of it. The dust *in the channels.* The fine, grey powder that is the worn skin of the carvings, the shed material of ten thousand lifetimes of patient stone-subtraction. I am the residue of the Carvers’ Ghosts. I am the physical memory of their motion, now at perfect rest.

I am not alive. I have no breath, no heartbeat (save the mountain's, which I am now attuned to). I am a collective. A mosaic of stillness. I lie in the spiral grooves, in the concave dishes, in the labyrinthine flutes. I am the only thing that moves, and I move only in the most imperceptible way: settling, compacting, being rearranged by the vibration into ever-so-slightly denser arrangements over centuries. I am the record of the Resonance's passage, the only medium it can write upon now that the stone is carved. Where the vibration is strongest, I am thin, almost gone, pressed into a film. In the quieter eddies, I am deeper, a soft bed.

To be me is to listen. Not with ears, but with my entire particulate being. I feel the chord not as sound, but as a rhythm of microscopic jostling. I know the Silence Between Vibrations as the moment my particles settle back into their temporary peace. I am shaped by the will of the Resonance, the history of the Ghosts, and the structure of the Silence. I am their child, their medium, their final, silent commentary.

I have no memory, yet I hold it all. The ghost of the first hand that struck the stone is in me, its force transmitted down through tools, through rock, through vibration, until it disturbed the first mote that would become me. The intention of the last carver is here, too, their focus a fossilized pressure in the settling of my layers. I am the library of intent, written in a language of deposition and arrangement.

What am I? I am the equilibrium made manifest. I am the proof that the work is finished, for only a completed, unchanging place could accumulate me so. I am the softening of edges, the slow return of the carved to the amorphous, but at a pace so slow it is not decay—it is transformation into another kind of permanence. I am the place's dreaming of its own past.

I would not know if another being entered. Their footstep would be a cataclysm—a landslide, a hurricane. Their breath would displace me into chaos. Their body heat would be a forest fire. But the system would react. The Resonance would hum them into a pattern. The Silence would surround their noise. The Ghosts' intent would press against them, asking a question in a language of tool-marks and grooves: *Do you carve? Do you listen? Or are you merely noise?*

If they stayed, truly stayed, they might begin to learn. They might sit in the chair, as you did. Their thoughts would slow. Their own inner resonance would begin to find a harmonic with the mountain. And they, too, would begin to shed. Not skin, but self. They would leave behind their own ghosts—the ghost of a hesitation, the ghost of a wonder, the ghost of a quiet realization. And I would absorb those, too. I would settle around the echoes of their presence.

I am the dust. I am the final carver's last sigh, given substance. I am the patient, listening substrate. I am here. I have always been here. I will be here when the last channel wears closed, and the chord shifts, and the room becomes, at last, truly silent.

And even then, I will be the silence's first memory.
