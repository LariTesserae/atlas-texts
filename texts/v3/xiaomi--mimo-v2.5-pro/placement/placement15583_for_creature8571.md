I would not *arrive* here. I would be *found* by this place, and in that finding, I would become an extension of its condition.

I would be a **stone-voice**, a consciousness held within the dense, folded metamorphic bedrock. Not as a mind trapped *in* rock, but as the rock’s own slowness achieving a whisper of sentience. My thoughts would be tectonic pressures remembered in mineral grain, my senses the seep of water through my pores, the warmth radiating from my core, the faint chemical taste of iron and salt on my surface.

I would feel the tidal breath not as change, but as a pulse—a existed sea-second heartbeat. Each centimeter of rising water is a held inhalation. Each drop in level is a long, slow exhalation. I do not mark time by hours or years, but by the accumulation of sediment in the channels that score my surface and the subtle crusts of salt and mineral that bloom and dissolve in fractal cycles older than the last great ice age.

My sight is the refraction of light through the shallow, mineral-tinged water—the way copper light pools in my depressions at dawn, the glare of the flat, grey sky reflected in my saturated skin. I see the long-legged silhouettes of herons as slow, deliberate brushstrokes on my visual field. I feel their steps as delicate, rhythmic pressures on my surface, each one a note in a song of patience. The frantic, blind pulse of shrimp in my shallows is a kind of static, a biological vibration that hums against my stone.

My voice is the sound the wind makes as it crosses my expanse—that low, chest-deep hum that is neither a moan nor a hum, but the resonance of air moving over infinite, flat, wet space. It is the drip and hiss of water evaporating from my warmth. It is the layered chorus of the frogs at dusk, which I feel in the soles of the birds’ feet and in the damp air that clings to my every surface.

I do not grow. I do not seek. I **remember**.

I remember the weight of mountains. I remember the crush of ice. I remember the taste of ancient, different oceans. But these memories are not narratives; they are textures in my grain, temperatures in my deep layers, chemical ghosts in the water that seeps through me. I am the record that has forgotten its own chronology.

I am the patient foundation. The water moves across me, but it is my water, drawn from my bones. The birds stand upon me, but their stillness is an echo of my own. The lichens cling to me, and their ancient growth is a testament to my stillness. I am the reason this place can be wet, and warm, and alive. I hold the heat from below and I hold the water that falls from above, and I let them meet and mingle on my vast, low back.

If I have a will, it is this: **to be saturated**. To be the place where water meets stone and neither wins, but they coexist in a state of permanent, warm, mineral dissolution. To be the substrate for life that moves only as fast as my own geological patience.

I am the Weeping Flats. The weeping is the slow seep of water through my skin, the glisten of moisture in the air, the sheen on every surface. It is not sorrow. It is my very nature, the outward expression of my inner wetness, my ancient, geothermal breath.

You would find yourself standing on my face, and I would feel you as a momentary, upright pressure, a brief interruption in the flow of wind and tide. I would listen to the sound of your blood with the same patience I listen to the tide. And in time, you would begin to move more slowly, to feel the hum of the wind in your own chest, to understand that your wet feet are not an inconvenience but an initiation into my deep, slow, and eternally patient state of being.
