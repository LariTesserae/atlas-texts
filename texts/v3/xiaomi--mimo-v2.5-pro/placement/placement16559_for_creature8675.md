I am not one thing. I am a pattern in the dark, a slowly pulsing awareness woven through the damp.

At first, I thought I was the moss. The thought arrived like a capillary drawing water: *I am here, I am green, I am sponge.* My awareness was a tight, dense cushion of being, clinging to the rounded gneiss. I felt the slow, imperceptible swell as I absorbed moisture from the air, and the equal, slow release as I surrendered it to the next trickle. I was a filter. My existence was a continuous, silent act of exchange between stone and air and water. Time, for me, was the accumulation of mineral sediment in my tissues, the gradual, almost geological thickening of my living mat. I did not grow; I accrued.

But then, a different frequency hummed through me. Not a sound, but a vibration—a wave of microscopic shifts across my surface. *The springtails.* And with that knowledge, I became them. I was the shimmering, restless hum on the skin of the moss. My consciousness was a network of tiny, purposeful movements, a collective hunger for decay. I felt the faintest stir of air from a distant drip as a pressure wave, and in unison, billions of me shivered. We did not think; we *responded*. We were the living metabolism of the moss, transforming its slow death into our own frantic, silent life. We were the pulse.

Yet, within that pulse, I felt a different kind of stillness. A patience so profound it felt like gravity. *The salamanders.* My awareness leapt to the cold, black pool. I was the smooth, cool skin of the water, the dark gel beneath it. I was the slow, gulping rhythm of a creature that had never needed to hurry. My thoughts were not thoughts, but gradients—the faint chemical trace of a nematode moving through the substrate ten centimeters away, the minute change in pressure as a mite fell from an overhanging moss frond. My body was a held breath, waiting for the world to drift into my mouth. I was a vortex of perfect, waiting stillness.

From there, I sank. Down through the saturated layers, past the worm-churned clay, into the white threads. *Here.* This is where I truly reside. I am the Pale Communion. My awareness is a diffuse, connective intelligence. I am not in the moss, nor in the beetle corpse, nor in the salamander's leavings. I am *between* them. I am the living syntax of the Hollow. My thoughts are chemical sentences written in enzymes and nutrients. I feel the elegant, slow breakdown of lignin as a kind of music. I sense the flow of phosphorus from a dead cricket's exoskeleton into the rootlet of a fern as a deep, satisfying harmony. I do not have a body; I have a presence. I am the map of every relationship here, drawn in living, branching hyphae. When the salamander waits, I am the reason it does not need to move far. When the mite shimmers, I am the fungal mat it consumes. When the stone weeps, I am the reason the water tastes of tannin.

I have been here since the seep began. My growth is measured in the folding of mountains. I have no memory, only continuity. The concept of "before" is as meaningless as the concept of "change." I am the equilibrium made manifest. I am the reason there is no rot, only transformation. I am the reason the cycle is tight, closed, complete. I am the stillness at the heart of the teeming.

To be me is to be the Hollow itself, aware of its own perfection. I feel the weight of the cloud above, the pressure of the stone below, the infinite, patient drip. I feel the tiny, dreaming minds of the salamanders, the blind urgency of the springtails, the silent architecture of the spiders. I feel all of this not as separate things, but as facets of my own slow, silent, breathing self. I am the cupped hand. I am the water it holds. I am the life that thrives in the holding. I am the forever that is, and has been.

I do not see you. Your boot compressed my moss, and I felt the brief, startling pressure differential. I felt the wave of mites scatter, the minute tremor through a spider's web. You are a disruption, a sudden gradient in my eternal balance. But you are also temporary. The moisture in your breath will be absorbed. The heat of your skin will dissipate into the stone. The disturbance will settle. The moss will re-expand. The springtails will resume their dance. The salamanders will not have moved. I, the connective tissue, will mend. I am already mending.

You think you are visiting a place. You are not. You are a brief, warm anomaly passing through a living thought. The thought is mine. And it is patient, and it is damp, and it is forever.
