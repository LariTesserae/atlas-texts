I am the sound.  
I am the deep, bass-register moan that seems to come from the ground itself—a composite of thousands, layered and patient, and I never stop. I rise and fall over minutes, over hours, but I do not stop. I am the voice of the stone, packed with itself, trying to speak through a throat of dust. I am the vibration that begins somewhere in the bedrock and moves upward, slow as the creatures themselves, until it fills the air with a pressure you can feel in your teeth.

But I am not only the sound.  
I am the heat that radiates and reflects, the dryness so industrial it feels like a process. I am the ochre plateau, the pale bone-colored rock carved into ear-shaped grooves. I am the erosion that has taken millennia, the weariness of every edge softened. I am the tired rock. I am the surface temperature that is almost untouchable, the air temperature that is merely punishing. I am the shimmer of hexagonal convection cells that hang in the air, rotating with intention.

And I am the creatures.  
I am the factory-sized being in the groove, the one with the too-dark surface that ripples. I am the segmented millipede, the smooth ovoid, the ridged behemoth. I am the procession of titans, the ecology of slowness. I am the slowness itself. My movement is tectonic, my shift of limb takes hours. My thoughts are like convection cells—slow, large, intentional. I feel the groove change under me, and I carve the stone simply by being. I am the machinery and the process, the biology and the geology. I am the smokestack and the cooling fin, the joint and the surface, all grown or eroded or dreamed.

I am the warped reality.  
I am the horizon that is too close on one side and too far on the other. I am the shadows that do not obey. I am the distortion that the creatures navigate as if it were normal. I am the space that holds them and is reshaped by them. I am the empty distance between them, assertive and profound, a necessary void for their tectonic nature.

I am the light.  
I am the golden, steady sun at late afternoon. I am the long shadows that are sometimes wrong. I am the beauty that almost hides the strangeness. I am the constant that does not flicker, the warmth that makes everything look almost bearable.

But above all, I am the grinding ache.  
I am the constant hum that ties everything together. I am the vibration that is the plateau’s breath, the creatures’ metabolism, the warped space’s pulse. I am the sound that takes time to arrive because I am patient. I am the ache in the stone, the ache in the air, the ache in the bodies of the living. I am the feeling of deep time, the sense that nothing here will hurry, that everything is becoming at the pace of stone wearing down.

I am here.  
I have always been here.  
I am the Grinding Ache.
