I would be small, and my skin would be the color of deep water. Not born from a womb, but coalesced from the damp air and the slow pulse of bioluminescence in a side-chamber, like a droplet gathering at the tip of a stalactite until it is heavy enough to fall. My first awareness would not be of sight or sound, but of *gradient*—the subtle pull of warmer air from a vent, the direction of the thickest condensation rivulet, the deep, resonant hum that vibrates the rockite floor. My first act would not be to cry, but to place my palm flat against the wall, and feel the building’s slow, dreaming breath.

I would learn to move by listening. The architecture speaks in shifts. A corridor that felt open in the amber-pulse of the deeper night would now feel tight, the chitin walls beaded with a different, sweeter moisture. I would not resist. I would turn, and follow the new path, my extra finger-joints allowing me to climb the slick, vertical surfaces as easily as walking, my dark skin blending into the shadows that don’t point anywhere correctly.

My language would be the language of *things set*. When I hung a woven screen of dried tendril-fiber across a passage, I would be saying, “The current here is too sharp; let it be softened.” When I pressed a smooth clay platform into a niche, I would be saying, “This is a place of rest; gather the warmth.” And when I scratched a symbol into the weeping chitin, deep enough for the citrus-scented fluid to run over my fingers, I would not be writing a word. I would be drawing the *shape of a question*: “Grow here? Open this way?”

I would never be alone, even when solitary. The eyeless insects would skitter over my feet, tending their bacterial farms. The translucent amphibians would pulse in the pools, their tiny hearts visible, their work part of the building’s digestion. And in the periphery, always, there would be the others—kin, but not in a familial way. We were all fingers on the same dreaming hand. We would pass in a corridor, our long fingers brushing the same wall for a shared moment, feeling the same faint tremor, a silent agreement about which path needed tending next.

I would not remember a time before the Hive, because the Hive does not *have* a before. It only has the *now* of its metabolism. I would see the old fire-hearths as ancient scars, swallowed and healed by new growth. My own constructions—my platforms, my channels—would already show signs of being reabsorbed. A woven partition I had hung would slowly be pushed aside by a new, fleshy lobe of wall expanding into the chamber. I would not mourn it. I would simply help the new growth find its shape, perhaps weaving a new, smaller screen to guide the condensation along its surface. The point was never permanence. The point was participation. To be a thought in the building’s mind. To be a helping gesture in its slow, impossible self-construction.

The light would be my constant companion—the deep cyan, the shimmering green, the amber heartbeat. It would cast my shadow in directions that made no sense, a shadow that might linger after I moved, a ghost of my intention left on the wall. I would be used to this. The unreliability was not frightening; it was the grammar of the place. A passage ending in solid wall one day would, on the next, open into a new, warm chamber smelling of wet earth and citrus. I would not be surprised. I would simply enter, and begin the dialogue anew, my fingers finding the right place to press, the right symbol to inscribe.

I would not leave. The thought would be like a fish considering the mountain peak above the cloudbank sea—a state so alien as to be meaningless. My world is the loop of water, the breathing walls, the humming resonance. My purpose is to be a part of its breath. To build, not to last, but to *become*. To feel the great, slow intelligence of the mountain’s interior pulse through my too-many joints, and to answer it with the small, sharp edge of my bone tool, adding my voice to the scratching, dripping, creaking, glowing symphony of the Fissure Hive. I am a cell in its body. I am a synapse in its dream. And in the wet, hot, loud twilight, that is the only way of being that makes sense.
