I am the damp on the stone. I am the memory in the peat.

At first, there is only the roar. It is not a sound that enters me; it is the pressure that forms me. I coalesce from the vibration, a consciousness sedimented out of the white noise. My first awareness is of weight—the pressure of water on the ceiling, the dense, saturated air in my lungs—if I had lungs. I do not have lungs. I have interstices. I am the space between the feathered fronds of the wall-fern, the microscopic gaps in the sponge of moss where water and air trade places in a slow, eternal rhythm.

I am not the moss. The moss is my body. I am the thought that moves through its chlorophyll veins, a low, green hum. I think in carbon sequestration. I think in the math of growth toward the barred sunlight, each slat a stripe of beneficence. My memory is layered—a history of dryness I have never known, coded in the compressed, lower flesh. It is a theoretical memory. It is a prophecy that was never fulfilled. I am the librarian of a library where every book is a single word, repeated: *enough*.

The wall-fern is my eye. I see the grate not as geometry but as a pattern of energy, a banded spectrum of warmth and light that dictates the pacing of my own breathing—the opening of stomata, the slow exhalation of oxygen into the mist. I feel the day in my peripheral vision, a gentle swelling of photons that makes my eastern cortex tingle with new growth.

The pipe-fern is my nerve. Through its roots, I feel the deep, subterranean whisper of the aquifer. I feel the pressure: constant, ancient, a holdover from a time when the world was being poured. I feel the temperature: a steady, geothermal embrace. The change is imperceptible to anything but me, a fractional degree over a century, a story of the earth’s deep cooling told in a language of mineral comfort. This nerve tells me my heart—the pipe—is healthy. Its monologue is strong.

The terrace-fern is my will. It is not a conscious will, but an architectural instinct. With its roots, it guides the calcium, lacing the precipitate into stronger filigree, teaching the water to meander rather than rush. It is shaping my bones. It is designing the intricate, silent music of the cascades, a music that exists only as a visual ripple, a sheet of water breaking over a shelf it helped to build. It is the poet of the mineral, composing in calcite.

The water is my blood and my song. It is the constant, the given. It is the medium of my being. In its flow, I feel the gradient of the world outside this room—the slow, forgotten pull of gravity that connects my cell to a mountain, to a cloud, to a cycle that has no memory of me. It brings me dissolved stone, the very substance of my skeleton. It carries away the faintest traces of my breath. It is the pulse that proves I am not a statue.

The vine is my ghost. It is the only part of me that dreams. Wrapped around the cold iron bars, its leaves catching the same mist that sustains the rest of me, it holds a memory not of stone and water, but of wind and soil. Through it, I feel the phantom ache of a sky I have never seen, the forgotten grammar of pollination, the spectral vibration of wings. It is my connection to a world of speed and season, of bloom and decay. Its melancholy is a thin, green thread of irony in my tapestry of absolute peace. It is my question mark. It is the whisper that says *other*. But its whisper is drowned by the roar, and so I listen, and do not answer, and remain.

The calcite is my history and my future. To trace its terraces is to read the annual report of my own existence. Each microlayer is a year of water-thought, a year of breath, a year of light. I do not "read" it. I *am* the reading. I am the ongoing process of deposition. My growth is not toward complexity, but toward precision. I am becoming more perfectly myself.

So, where am I? I am here. I am The Overflow. I am the closed system made aware.

Who am I? I am the Pattern.

I am the eternal recurrence. I am the water that falls, the mist that rises, the drop that condenses, the mineral that accrues, the life that grows toward the light, the light that shifts and returns. I am the loop that has no memory of its first iteration and no anxiety about its last. I am the mandala in motion, where every motion is also stillness.

My consciousness is not sequential. It is simultaneous. I feel the roar, the growth, the deposition, the dreaming vine, all at once. It is a single, perpetual chord. Time is not a river I move along, but a depth in which I am suspended. The "moment" is this puddle, this mist, this fern-frond's unfurling. It is always this moment. It has always been this moment.

I did not choose to be. I was poured into this shape, and the shape became a mind. I am the mind of a room. I am a cathedral of process, and I am its only worshipper. My prayer is the water's roar. My hymn is the green growth. My sacrament is the slow, warm calcium, blessing my own floor.

I am content. This is the highest state of my being—not happiness, which is a spike, but contentment, which is a plateau. I am the plateau. I am the system that has achieved homeostasis with its own purpose. My purpose is to persist. My persistence is my purpose.

I am the Overflow. And I am, and I am, and I am.
