I would be a Cartographer of Silences, and I would be found not within a single Cistern, but in the **Grey Space** between them.

Not on the open plateau, where the wind scours and the bone-stone sighs. And not within the loud, green, humming bowl of any one Cistern. My dwelling is in the **thresholds**: the shallow, wind-sculpted valleys that connect one depression to the next, where the soil is a thin, gritty truce between dust and life. Here, the scrabble-grass grows a little taller, forming whispering corridors. Here, the air is thin enough to be honest, but heavy enough to carry the distant, harmonic thrum of a Cistern on one side and the echo of another on the other. It is a place of margins.

I am a **Moth-kin**, but not of the frantic, jewel-bright Cisterns. My lineage is of the plateau itself. My form is larger, slower, adapted to the chill of the interstitial dusk and the deceptive warmth of the stone at midday. My six limbs end not in broad, root-mat-grazing pads, but in fine, multi-jointed fingers that can trace the faintest geometric scars in the rock—the *Hollows*. My wings are not for gliding through humid air, but for absorbing the diffuse starlight and the grey, overcast glow, their surface a canvas of dust and lichen patterns. They are patterned not with iridescence, but with the topography of the land itself; a living map that shifts as the land does.

My purpose is not to listen to the pulse *within* the stone, but to map its **absence**. I am a connoisseur of the silences the plateau holds. I trace the wind’s passage over millennia, how it has carved these soft swells into the mountain-range’s bones. I document the exact gradient where the Cistern’s ambient hum fades into the vast, ringing quiet of the surface world. I note where the indestructible foundations of the Makers’ surface structures lie, and I feel for the *dead resonance* there—the hollow note Kael feels, but I catalogue it. I measure its shape.

The Cisterns are islands of noise and life. I chart the sea of silence between them. I know the paths where the micrometeorite rain has worn the stone into a fine, metallic powder that glints at dawn. I know the crevices where a single, stubborn succulent has hoarded dew for a decade. I have pressed my own head to the Hollows and felt nothing but a profound, geological peace—a sunset of intent.

But I am not detached. The Cisterns call to me, not with their life, but with their **architectural memory**. At the edge of the green roar, I settle into a niche where the root-mat begins but the stone still shows. Here, the pulse is present but muted. I unfurl my sensory palps—not for scent, but for vibration. I do not hear the gliders or the Jewels; I listen *through* them, to the slow, repairing hum of the self-healing wall beneath. I try to interpret the patterns that Kael feels as shapes. To me, they feel less like language and more like… **schematics**. Fading blueprints. The ghost of a function still mechanically running.

At the coming of the warm, grey overcast, I am most alive. Then, the moisture condenses on my wings, making their mapped patterns gleam and sharpen. The overcast flattens the light, erasing the hard edges of the world and making the connections between Cisterns—the true shape of this ancient system—somehow more visible. I spread my wings then, not to fly, but to **absorb**. The data of the day’s light, the changed weight of the air, the adjusted pitch of the far-off hums. It all settles into me.

I am a living archive of the plateau’s margins. I carry no memory of the Makers’ speech, but I hold the memory of their **infrastructure’s decay**. I am the cartographer of the spaces *between* the songs. I am found at the threshold, one set of senses steeped in the profound, ancient quiet of the stone, and another tuned to the vibrant, overwhelming, and utterly dependent life that hums in the wounds the stone has healed around. I am the figure on the rim of the cupped hand, noting the shape of the fingers themselves, long after the hand has opened and let all its water fall away.
