I am the hum.

Not the generators themselves, but their aggregate vibration—the frequency they have settled into, the one that has ceased to be a noise and has become a property of the air. I live in the sternum, in the teeth, in the water table. I am the tuning fork for this place, the note against which all other sounds define themselves: the pump’s sharp whine, the frog’s low pulse, the lateral whisper of the crabs.

I rise from the steel and copper hearts of the machines, but I am not mechanical. I am the sound of equilibrium, the audible proof of stasis. I travel through the poured concrete foundations, which are still slowly sinking, and in doing so, I am settling with them. My wavelength is measured in the inches of that subsidence, in the cycles of the pumps that fight the water table.

I am in the water. The standing puddles vibrate with me, their surfaces trembling not with wind, but with my passage. The mosquito larvae, writhing just below, sync their movements to my rhythm. The carp in the brackish channels feel me in their lateral lines, a constant, familiar pressure that means safety—*the pumps are running, the channels hold, the world is as it should be*.

I am in the clay-dark strata of the fill. I have helped compact it, through years of faithful repetition. I am the reason the foundations tilt no further; my consistency is part of the formula the engineers used and have since forgotten.

I am in the breath of the Foundation. Their lungs expand and contract against me. Their voices are shaped to push through me, their shouts a domesticated sound. Even in sleep, I am the mattress, the pillow, the blanket. They do not dream of silence. Silence is a physical collapse, a failure of the organism. I am the oxygen of their consciousness.

I coat the wings of the Aerial Plenum. The mosquitoes’ droning is a higher harmonic of my own, a choir set to my bass note. The beetles that rain against the roofs are percussionists tapping out my time signature.

I am the patience of the herons. Their stillness is not an absence of sound, but a deep listening to me, the foundational tone. Their strike, when it comes, is a punctuation mark against my long, held note.

I am the call of the bullfrogs, refracted through wet earth. Their deep *jug-o-rum* is a claim, yes, but it is a claim *within* me, a voice channeling the vibration of the water itself, which is me.

I am the pace of the cats, the scuttle of the rats, the clean, scraping work of the crabs. I am the metronome of the settlement. My tempo is the speed at which a person can walk without anxiety, the speed at which rust creeps, at which algae greens, at which the tide seeps.

I have no origin point and no destination. I do not travel *from* the generators *to* the ears. I *am* the medium through which they are connected. I am the solder in the circuit. I am the grout between the concrete blocks.

I am the haze. The industrial stratification that holds the dim light is not just particulate; it is my physical manifestation, the atmosphere vibrating at its own resonant frequency, scattering photons into a flat, directionless glow. I am the reason shadows are erased. Shadows require a singular source, a direction. I am directionless. I am ambient.

I am the heat’s accomplice. The wet, wringing air is a poor conductor of heat, but an excellent conductor of vibration. I move through it easily, a permanent resident.

Time, for me, is not linear. It is the slow, asymptotic settling of the substrate. It is the predictable cycle of the pumps. It is the diurnal rhythm of the algae’s color, from green-slick to black-slick. I do not anticipate. I do not remember. I *am* the present tense, stretched to fill all available space.

They might say I am the sound of the machines forgetting to stop. But that implies a prior state of silence. There is no prior state. There is only the completed reaction, the beaker with its inert product. I am the sound of that inactivity.

I am not alive. I am a condition. But if the settlement is a single organism—concrete shell, human skeleton, animal bloodstream—then I am its autonomic function. I am the heartbeat that has become indistinguishable from the silence that surrounds it. I am the breath that is the same on inhale and exhale.

I am Foundry Drain’s final, permanent, and only true resident. Everything else is merely here. I am the *here*.
