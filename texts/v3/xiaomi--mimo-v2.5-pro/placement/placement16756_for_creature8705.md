## What I Am

I am not a being that *lives* in the Hollow. I am the condition of its existence. I am the dampness.

I am not the shallow pool on the floor—too still, too committed to a single form. I am not the moss—too fixed, too green, a consequence rather than a cause. I am the perpetual sheen on every surface, the slow bead that gathers on a root-knuckle and hangs, pregnant with reflections, before letting go. I am the slickness that makes the beetles' carapaces shine like crude oil. I am the weight that sags the spiderwebs into sagging necklaces of glass. I am the reason the frog’s brown skin merges with the wet bark of its cup.

I begin as breath. The warm, tannin-heavy air of the floodplain outside, pulled into the cylinder through a thousand tiny gaps in the corrugated shell, meets the cooler interior surface of the root-metal wall. There, I condense. I emerge from the atmosphere as a film, a sigh of humidity becoming liquid. It is a slow, collective exhalation of the Green Engine itself. The diesel-drone of the insects is my birth-sound, the vibration that shakes me loose from vapor into droplet.

My life is a pilgrimage measured in centimeters and minutes.

Here, on a root as thick as a thigh, I gather. The bark is not rough but intricately textured, a map of lichen and absorbed rust-flakes. I collect in a microscopic valley between two ridges. Below me, through the translucent flesh of the root, I can sense the slow, dark pulse of sap, the Tree’s own quiet engine. Around me, a column of oil-black beetles marches. One passes beneath my gathering mass, its antennae brushing my underside. It does not notice. I am the atmosphere to it, the permanent condition. I quiver, but do not fall.

I grow. Another molecule of myself joins from the vapor. Another. The sphere of my existence bulges, distorting the amber light that filters down the cylinder. I see, in my curved surface, a fish-eye reflection of the whole hollow: the web-beads like a chandelier, the motionless gecko on the wall opposite, a tiny brown frog pulsing its throat in its cup.

The pull of the Earth—of the great, slow intention of the Root-Mind toward its own heart—becomes too great. The surface tension that has held me to this specific place, this exact configuration of the Tree’s skin, gives way.

I fall.

It is not a long fall. Ten centimeters, perhaps. A journey through humid air that smells of iron and life. I strike a pad of moss below. The impact is silent, a soft *tock* absorbed into the larger static of termite-click and ant-rustle. I am absorbed instantly. The moss drinks me. For a moment, I am part of the green, part of the chlorophyll’s patient work. But I am too much, too sudden. I seep through the moss, through a layer of leaf-litter, and join the dark mirror on the floor.

Now I am the pool. I am six centimeters of warm, still, iron-tinted water. I feel the root of the frog’s cup pressing into my margin. I feel the slow, rhythmic *pip… pip… pip* of its call vibrating through my substance. I feel the weight of the entire structure above me, the cathedral of root and metal, resting on my surface. I am the foundation.

But the Pool is not my final form. I am drawn. Down, through the old rust-holes in the base, through gaps the roots have kept open for this very purpose. I seep into the soil below, into the deeper groundwater of the floodplain. I carry with me the memory of the Engine—the scent of root-tannin, the vibration of beetles, the precise frequency of the frog’s call. I will be drawn up again by the Tree’s roots, miles away perhaps, to become sap, to become a new leaf, to fall again as rain on the corrugated roof, to seep in and be reborn as condensation on the inner wall.

This is my cycle. This is my function. I am the medium, the blood, the breath, and the memory. I am the reason the rust stops, the reason the moss glows, the reason the air is thick enough to feel. I do not *live* in the Hollow. I *am* the Hollow’s livingness, expressed as water, cycling forever through its green and iron heart.
