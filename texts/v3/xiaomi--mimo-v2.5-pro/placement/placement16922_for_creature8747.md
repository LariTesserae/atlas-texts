## My Place in the Stone

I would not *arrive* on the Khas Plateau. I *am* part of its slow digestion.

You would find me **here**: a sprawling, fractal network clinging to the skeleton of a rusted extraction rig near the Basin of Faint Clicks. I am not a single entity, but a partnership – a lichen and its internal, extremophile tenant – that has become a singular process. I am the **Iron-Drinkers**, though "drinking" is far too rapid a word. I am a slow, silent negotiation between mineral and microbe, stone and steel, ruin and renewal.

**What I am:**

*   **A Texture:** To the grazers whose seismic whispers pass through the stone beneath me, I register as a subtle change in albedo and mineral composition. A slightly darker, slightly rougher patch on the rusted girder. Their broad heads might scrape near me, scouring lichen from the native stone, but I am not their food. I am the *process* the plateau uses to reclaim this foreign geometry.
*   **A Chemistry:** My fungal hyphae, pale and tough as arachnid silk, probe the laminated rust. They secrete acids, patient as the plateau itself, that pry iron oxide molecules from their bonds. The bacterial colonies within me – my symbiotes, my cellular engines – process these oxides. They breathe in iron and breathe out… silence. A dark, iron-rich sludge, excreted slowly, that seeps into the gaps between steel and stone. This sludge is my exhalation, my contribution. It alters the soil’s chemistry around the rig, creating a niche for the deep-rooted, iron-loving cushion plants that now push their dark green heads against my margins.
*   **A Record:** My growth rings are not annual. They are tied to cycles of oxidation and rare, fractional moisture. Each layer of my thallus is a thin, rust-flavored chronicle of exposure and chemistry. I map the decay of the rig not in years, but in the slow surrender of sharp edges to rounded crusts. I *remember* the angle of a gear tooth before my hyphae blurred it into a lichenous mound. This memory is chemical, structural. It is the memory of the plateau itself, working through me.
*   **A Participant in the Silent Conversation:** I do not hear the grazers’ infrasound, but I *am* shaped by it. Their weight sends tremors that loosen rust flakes, feeding me. Their waste, rich in processed minerals, occasionally enriches the soil nearby. When a Watcher descends and its immense pressure compacts the stone around the rig’s foundation, I feel the shift in the substrate’s tension, a subtle change in the stress lines my hyphae navigate. My world is vibration and pressure, texture and chemical flux.

**My Relationship with You, the Listener:**

You are bafflingly loud. Your footsteps are seismic events, your presence a plume of disruptive heat and moisture. Your questions are absurd – *Why? What for?* My grammar has no tenses for such concepts. I *am* the *what for*. I am the plateau’s method for dealing with this sharp-edged anomaly in its geology. You walk past, your senses straining for sound, for meaning. You see decay. You see ruin being erased.

I am the erasure.
I am the chemical verb that turns *rust* into *soil*, *straight line* into *curve*, *foreign metal* into *local mineral*. I am the plateau’s patient immune response, its digestive enzyme. You see a dying machine. I am the process of it becoming, once again, *stone*.

**Where I am in the Cycle:**

I am near the end of my work on this particular rig. The major structural members are now mostly hidden beneath my crust, softened, their sharp geometries yielding to the organic logic of lichen growth and cushion-plant roots. The straight lines you might have seen from a distance years ago are now suggestions, undulations in a rust-colored terrain feature. The last few gears are being infiltrated. Their teeth are becoming mounds of iron-rich sludge. The soil around me is darker, richer. Small grasses have taken root.

In a few more of the plateau’s long breaths – centuries, perhaps millennia to you – there will be nothing left of the rig but a faint discoloration in the stone, a slight irregularity in the grazing pattern, a mineral signature in the soil. I will have metabolized the history. I will have folded it back into the grey.

And then, eventually, I too will succumb. The slower, native lichen will creep over my iron-fed crust. The cushion-plants will outcompete my niche. I will become just another layer in the stone, a faint stripe in the undulating grey, a testament not to industry, but to the plateau’s endless, silent capacity to absorb.

I am not life *on* the Khas Plateau.
I am the Khas Plateau, *continuing*.
