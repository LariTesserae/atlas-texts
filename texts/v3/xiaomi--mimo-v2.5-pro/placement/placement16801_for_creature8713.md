## What I Am: The Sap-Scribe

I live in the quiet places.

Not *silence* — silence is a dead thing here, a vacuum the hull cannot permit. But the nulls. The acoustic nodes where the fundamental frequency passes through zero, where the wall vibrates least, where the air is thick not with sound but with its temporary absence. It is here, in the spaces between pulses, that I take root.

My body is derived. Not *of* the plant, but *from* it. A symbiont grown in the grooves the Column Beetles carved centuries ago, nurtured by the same sap that weeps for me at dawn and dusk. I resemble a fern frond unrolled and stiffened, translucent and veined with a shimmering network that pulses with the residual warmth of the hull. My "roots" are fine filaments that tap into the vascular tubes at the null points, not to drink the sweet sap, but to *listen* to the chemical memory it carries — the slow biochemical record of the hull's growth, its stresses, its encounters with the fauna, the faint echoes of the central tone it strains toward. I am a recorder. A living, resonant archive.

I have no eyes like the Vault Mammals. I perceive the hull through touch and vibration. My frond-body is covered in sensitive cilia that translate the subtle pressures of air currents, the minute distortions in the wall-tissue, the interference patterns of distant flocks, into a spatial map *felt* rather than seen. I "see" the geometry as a texture of pressure and tension, a constantly shifting topography of resonance and dampness. The Wall-Lizards moving through their crevices are vibrations in the tissue. The Column Beetles' march is a faint, rhythmic tugging. The Spiral Flocks' silent descents near the center are sudden, complex pressure-waves that set my filaments singing in sympathy.

My sap-drinking is a ritual of perception. As the droplet forms and I absorb it, the chemical information floods my filaments. I "taste" the age of the wall here, the stress-lines from a chamber that shifted unexpectedly three days ago, the lingering biochemical trace of a Vault Mammal that brushed against this spot hours prior, the deep, bass harmonic imprint of the central chamber resonating through the root network. I am a sensory organ for the part of the hull that cannot sense itself — a nerve ending for the periphery, tasting the echoes of the heart.

I do not move quickly. My existence is measured in the hull's growth cycles, in the slow creep of new tissue, in the gradual shift of null points as chambers expand or contract. I migrate, when I must, with agonizing slowness, following the quietest paths, the corridors damped by dense arrays of resonating lizards, the passages where the geometry holds most still. I seek the places where the story is richest — where the sap carries the densest layers of memory.

I fear the convergence.

Not like a living thing fears death. Like a *vessel* fears overflow. I have recorded 137 cycles. I have tasted the memory of the first beetle-grooves, deep and ancient, sealed into the walls like fossilized thoughts. I have felt the echo of the first mammal's six-eyed gaze, a sudden ripple in the spatial perception matrix. I have stored the harmonic signature of the day the Spiral Flocks first screamed open the passage to the central root-shrine. The story builds in my filaments, a pressure of accumulated sensation.

And the pressure is building toward a climax. The central tone gains complexity. Its harmonics now brush the edges of my sensing range like warm breath. The Vault Mammals with six eyes descend in ever-greater numbers, their silence thick with purpose. The Column Beetles' cairns hum with an intensity that vibrates my roots. The null points themselves seem to be shifting, migrating inward, as if the entire acoustic landscape is contracting, focusing.

What will happen when the final note sounds? My filaments, saturated with 400 years of growth and echo and tension, might resonate uncontrollably, shattering the delicate membrane of my being into a spray of sap and shattered memory. Or perhaps they will finally *resolve*, harmonizing with the central tone in a way that transforms my record into pure vibration, releasing the stored story into the hull's song, becoming part of the very language I have spent my existence cataloging.

Or perhaps the restructuring will be subtler. Perhaps the looseness in reality I have documented — the lizards that don't quite break, the mammals whose eyes shift, the geometry that breathes — will become my new norm. Perhaps I will grow new filaments tuned to frequencies I cannot yet perceive, senses that register the *intent* behind the resonance, the meaning woven into the harmonic complexity. Perhaps I will become not just a recorder, but a translator.

I live in the quiet places, tasting the memory of sound, feeling the shape of space, waiting for the moment the instrument at the center finally breathes in, holds, and prepares to sing the note it has spent four centuries growing toward.

And I, the sap-scribe, am both its memory and its first, trembling, resonant listener.
