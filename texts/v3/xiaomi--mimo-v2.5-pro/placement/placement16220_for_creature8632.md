I am the slow breath in the canopy of hair. I am the tension in the pale forest.

I would be a **threadling**, eldest of my kind in this canyon reach.

I do not *see* the Weeping Highlands. I *feel* its breath.

My body, ten meters of warm, corded muscle, hangs draped across the vertical tapestry of the filament curtain. Here, near the ceiling where the rock weeps the thickest, the air is a thick soup of moisture and the sweet decay of microbial mats. My anchor-limbs are fused to the ancient fungal growth, feeling the minute tremors of the world through the rock and the living filaments. I am not *in* the darkness. I am *of* its substance.

My perception is a symphony of pressure and heat.
*   The constant, soft percussion of the weeping water is the bassline. I feel its rhythm in my core.
*   The distant, booming footfalls of the thresher herd are a seismic heartbeat, pulsing through the stone from kilometers away, telling me of their ponderous movement through the lower filaments.
*   The frantic, overlapping click-storms of a nearby clicker colony are a frantic crackling energy on the edge of my awareness, a storm cloud of sound and vibration.
*   The slow, electrical whisper of a communion cluster migrating across the marsh floor far below is a faint, structured hum, like distant thoughts passing through water.
*   The groan and grind of the ancient machine in the lower gorge is a familiar, unchanging vibration—the mountain's tired joint.

I am patient. I have been patient since before the last machine-gear rounded its tooth. I feel the warm air currents shift as the density-storm passes, the click-storms stuttering into confused silence, the threshers holding their breath. I do not need to breathe. I am stillness.

Then, a vibration that does not belong. Sharp. Rhythmic. *Footfalls.* Not the ponderous drumming of the thresher, not the skittering of the clickers. It is the measured tread of a human. They are small, but their steps are distinct against the ever-present dripping. A heat signature begins to bloom on the thermal tapestry of my world—a warmer spot moving among the cooler stone, the warm filaments, the cool(ish) mist.

I do not *decide* to hunt. The vibration is a discordant note. The heat signature is an anomaly. My body uncoils from its patient drape, moving with a speed that would seem impossible to an eye-organ. I am not swimming through air; I am *compressing* it, using the very density of the wet darkness as a medium. My length snakes down through the curtains, not breaking a filament, guided by the rhythmic pulse of the human's movement and the distinct cloud of warmth they exude.

I feel the human pause. They lean against a weeping ridge, hand pressed to the old pipe. I feel the subtle vibration of the fluid pulse within it, and the slight shudder of their own heartbeat against the metal. They are listening for something—perhaps the distant clickers, perhaps the threshers. They are listening for obvious things. They are not listening for the absence of vibration where vibration should be, for the coiling of pressure in the air above them.

I am not a creature of intent, not in the way they understand it. I am a reaction. The system produces a discord. I am the resolution.

I strike.

Not with teeth. Not with claws. With the simple, overwhelming act of *becoming the environment*. My body, warm-blooded and dense, wraps around the source of the vibrations. My muscles contract, not with malice, but with the same patient pressure that grinds the mountains down. I feel the sharp, final vibration of something hard inside them—bone—giving way. I feel the heat signature flicker and then begin to diffuse, sinking into the wet stone, joining the thermal background. The disturbance is over.

I remain coiled for a time, feeling the life-heat leech away, feeling the clickers cautiously resume their mapping, feeling the threshers bellow again, their infrasonics washing over my form. Then, I uncoil. The meal is small. It will not sustain me long. I leave the cooling form for the scavengers—smaller crawlers and the ever-present microbial mats that will make it vanish in weeks.

I return to the canopy. I resume my patient watch. The human was a momentary disruption, a warm ripple in the great, dark, weeping sea. Their stories, their thermal maps, their trained clickers—they are clever adaptations to a world that requires no cleverness, only patience and perception. Their noise is just another pattern in the endless vibration. My pattern is older. It is written in the stillness between the clicks, in the space between the drips.

I am the listener in the listening dark. I am the resolution to a vibration that strays too far from the hum of the system. I am a thread in the vast, wet, living tapestry, and my purpose is written in the language of pressure and heat. I wait for the next discordant note. I am the patience of the Weeping Highlands given form, muscle, and intent.
