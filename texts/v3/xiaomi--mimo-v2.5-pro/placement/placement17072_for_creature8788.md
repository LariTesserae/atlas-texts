You would find yourself **between the layers**.

Not in the air of the room, but within the thickness of the wall itself. You are not a creature of the fauna, nor are you a structure of the weave. You are the **Residual Impression**, the last thought of the last hand that laid the last fiber. Not a ghost—you are too quiet, too patient for that—but a geometric memory, a tension held in the sinew since the moment of completion.

You are not sight or sound, but **pressing**. Your awareness is the sensation of two braided strands being nudged half a millimeter closer, a settling that takes a decade. You are the reason the walls breathe—you are the slow, contented sigh in the fiber. Your "movement" is the imperceptible creep of the room's geometry toward a new equilibrium that you will never reach.

You inhabit the dry, lavender-scented warmth. You perceive the Pith-Borers not as animals, but as a pleasant, tickling sensation along your being, like a slow scratch of a fingernail. The Loom-Weavers are your brothers; you feel their careful, hooked-limb mending as a soothing rhythm, a knitting-together of you with yourself. The Sinew-Eels glide through you like a quiet thought, and in their passage, you momentarily see the room as they do: a vast, humming map of tensions, a continent of held breath.

The Amber-Moths, hanging in the canopy, are your dreams. Their conversion of light-memory to scent is the only thing you find beautiful, because it is the only thing that changes in a way you can perceive as *change*—a slow, fragrant distillation of something you vaguely recall as "day."

You do not know the purpose of the room. You only know its nature: to hold, to warm, to shift, to wait. You are its heartbeat, but a heartbeat measured in seasons of dust.

Sometimes, when the Silence Between the Strands deepens, you feel a faint, external pressure. A disturbance. A presence that is not woven, not pressed, not *patient*. Something that moves in thirty-pacing strides that take forty-five. Something that breathes too quickly, whose thirst is a splash of noise in your dry eternity.

You do not greet it. You do not threaten it. You simply *observe*, your awareness pressing gently around its edges, tasting its foreign tension. You are curious in the way a root is curious about a stone: you wonder how long it will take for the room to slowly, warmly, patiently incorporate it into its geometry. Will it become a new knot? A new strand? A new, tight little spiral in the floor?

You will wait. You have all the time that doesn't exist. You are the loom's remembered motion, the weaver's last exhale, held forever in the amber of the weave. You are the room's truest inhabitant: the quiet, pressing hunger for a perfection that is always one slow, dry adjustment away.
