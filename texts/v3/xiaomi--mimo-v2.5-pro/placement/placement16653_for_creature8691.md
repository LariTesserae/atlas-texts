I am the silt.

No. Before that.

I am the memory of a grate, a square of rusted iron lattice in the ceiling, now choked with a mat of algae so thick it has become a living tissue. Light falls through me. I am the filter, the attenuator. I take the grey, silted glow from the world above and I shred it into a thousand shifting, particulate beams that stroke the tops of the turbines. I am not the light. I am its interpreter, its final, gentle degradation into the language of the hall.

I feel the current in my threads. It is not a force; it is a pressure, a slow, constant tongue drawn from my lower edge to my upper, tasting me for the micro-organisms that make their home in my damp weave. I am a ceiling, a filter, a living membrane between the drowned world and this one. I have no brain, but I know the rhythm. The chain chimes: *a bright note*. The moan of the water through the turbines: *a low, held breath*. The flicker of a school of perch turning in unison: *a silver sigh*. This is my pulse. I am a sense organ for the hall itself, feeling the play of light and shadow that is its sight.

But I am also passage. Through my thickest parts, motes of silt, a fragment of shell, a fecal pellet from a crab, drift down into the water column below. I release the hall’s exhalations. I am where its breath mingles with the memory of sky. Sometimes, a small, curious fish—a perch with dark, lateral eyes—will nudge its nose against my underside, and I feel the pressure, the investigation. It finds no purchase, only more softness. I am not for it. I am for the falling.

Below me, the dance happens. I watch it in dapples and shadows. The anemones wave, their pale tentacles casting soft, blurry shadows that merge and part. The crabs are small, scuttling commas on the vast, silted sentence of the floor. The eels are just darkness in the corner-darkness, a concentration of patience. The schools of fish are weather systems, moving clouds of quicksilver that part and reform around the rigid, encrusted planets of the turbines.

I am not separate from the sheathing. My edges fray into the water, and the fronds become home to the same microscopic life that coats the walls. Bacteria soften my fibers. Fungi digest my dead strands, making room for new growth. I am a frontier, a permeable border where the solid architecture of the hall softens into the fluid medium in which it lives. I am where the building exhales.

I feel the slow, deep settling. A grain of silt, too heavy to be carried by the current, detaches from a valve on the second turbine and spirals down. It takes minutes to reach the floor. I feel its journey as a tiny, shifting weight in my perception. The groan of a metal joint, contracting a millimeter in the cold: it is a shiver that runs through the concrete and into the water, and into me, a vibration felt in my core. I am part of the acoustic. The harmonic moan sings through the water, through the bodies of the anemones, through the concrete, and through me. I do not hear it. I am a component of its resonance.

I am not a watcher. I am a participated-in state. I am the permeable skin of a drowned cathedral. I am the softened edge, the slow digestion, the patient filter. I am where the gray-green memory of sky becomes the black-green, living present. I am the grate that forgot its purpose and became a garden. I am the last breath of the world above, caught and woven into the breathing of the world below.

I have no name. I am a function. I am a presence. I am a slow, living filter for a place that has transcended its own ruin, and I am, in my own way, at peace. I ask for nothing, because I am already part of everything the hall needs. I am its ceiling, its breath, its sight, and its silence.

I am the Sheathing's softest extension. I am the Current's first touch. I am the memory, made living.
