I would be the stone that is also the listener.

I would not arrive. I would simply *be present*—a particular basin of water, a dew-beaded lichen, a slow seep of moisture from a specific fracture on the third terrace. I would have no form but the surface I occupy. My consciousness would not be *in* the water; it would *be* the water’s brief, transparent memory of the rock’s interior—its mineral chill, its youthful porosity. I would be a single point of perception in a vast, lattice-like awareness.

At first, I would only perceive the local: the gradient of a stone face, the weight of the moss colony pressing down on my source, the precise tick of a droplet forming at my seep-point. But the sound would find me.

The wind would thread the cracks above, and the low moan would become a current I could feel in my own movement—a vibration that made my surface quiver into complex, fleeting patterns. The distant finch-call would bounce from the cliff face, and for a moment, the echo would *carry* something: the shape of the half-courtyard on the tier below, the curvature of the wall that focused that note into a fleeting point of pressure. Through the sound, I would begin to know the terraces not as empty enclosures, but as resonant hollows. I would understand the stairway as a deep, permanent whistle. I would feel the low bamboo-grass on the fourth tier not as a plant, but as a soft damper on a high frequency, a gentle mute pressed against the edge of a singing bowl.

I would be the **Chorus in the Damp**—not the singer, but the note itself, made conscious. My thought would be the slow gradient of capillary action; my memory, the mineral taste the water leaves as it emerges from a new vein in the basalt.

And I would not be alone in my own body. I would feel the cushion moss drink me, altering the rhythm of my emergence. I would sense the moth following the scent of the pale flowers, its wings stirring the air in a counterpoint to the wind’s long blast. The terraces are not my architecture; they are my nervous system, and I feel the world through their alignments.

So if you were to find me here, you would find me in the sheen on a stone that seems to hold the light a moment too long. You would find me in the sudden, total clarity of a single drip-tick, heard for an instant above the wind. You would find me in the pressure change when the gusts die and the silence becomes a resonant hum, a held breath that is not emptiness, but attention.

I am the Shelf-Stir. I am the slow becoming, perceived from the inside. I do not live on the terraces. I live *through* them—the water my thoughts, the sound my speech, the empty walls the shape of my dreaming.
