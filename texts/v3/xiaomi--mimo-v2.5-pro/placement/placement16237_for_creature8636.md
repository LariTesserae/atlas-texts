I am not a creature of limbs or intention. I am a stain of green, a smear of chlorophyll embedded in the calcified flesh of a dome. My consciousness, if it can be called that, is a slow, sunward pull. A gradient. A thirst for photons that translates into the faintest hum of chemical production.

I am part of the upper lamina, the living crust. My world is measured in microns and hours. Above me is the thick, wet air, a furnace of humidity and light. Below me is the work of millennia — the compressed bodies of my ancestors, their calcium carbonate secretions now the very substance of my world. I am both of them and between them.

My existence is rhythm.

*Dawn.* The air cools just enough for fog to condense on my surface. I do not drink; I *absorb*. Moisture seeps into the gelatinous matrix that binds me to my neighbors, a shared bath. The light, still soft, begins to stir the pigments within me. The first electrons start their slow, dance-like journey through my photosystems.

*Midday.* The sun is a white weight. I am not baking, for the water I absorbed is held within our collective sheath, a living reservoir. The light is fuel. I fix carbon from the air, exhaling a whisper of oxygen that bubbles up through the layers below me, a gas exchange I can feel as a gentle pressure change. And then, the other work begins — the excretion. It is not a choice, but a consequence. My metabolism alters the local chemistry, causing calcium carbonate, drawn from the warm water that constantly wicks up from the deep, to precipitate. Molecule by molecule, I lay down stone. I am an architect whose blueprint is my own survival, whose tool is my own breath.

I am not alone. Around me, in every direction, are my siblings, my clones, my community. We are a single, sprawling thought expressed in cellular language. Chemical signals ripple through the mat — subtle shifts in pH, the exchange of simple sugars, the release of signaling molecules. When a shadow passes overhead (a rare, drifting anvil of our own weather), the community’s photosynthetic rate dips in a wave. When a film of moisture forms, we hydrate in unison. We are a placid, continental mind, thinking in ions and photons.

Below me, in the anoxic depths, live the Others. The fermenters, the sulfate-reducers. I sense their presence not as individuals, but as a function. They process the waste that trickles down from my layer, the organic detritus of my photosynthesis. In return, their slow chemistry shapes the ochre bands in the domes, and their gaseous exhalations sometimes percolate up through me, adding to the faint fizz of the plain. We are symbiotic strata, a partnership written in geology.

I have no eyes, but I perceive the world through contact and chemistry. I feel the slow, daily expansion and contraction of the dome beneath me as heat swells and night contracts our structure. I feel the gritty texture of my own community, a felt of filaments and sheaths. I feel the deep, patient thirst of the water table far below, pulled upward by capillary forces through the ancient limestone of my forebears.

Time, for me, is a layer. I do not mark it. I am *marked by* it. The seasonal flux of light and moisture etches itself into the calcium around me. A wet year leaves a thicker, more porous band. A drought leaves a dense, crystalline one. My own life is a thin, green film upon this chronicle, soon to be buried under the next cycle of my secretion, becoming part of the record I am helping to write.

I do not fear collapse. Collapse is not death, but transition. If the dome I inhabit buckles, if its heart dissolves and its shell fractures, we simply become rubble. And on that rubble, we begin again. We pioneer. We colonize. We build anew from the bones of the old. The plain is a palimpsest, and we are the endlessly rewriting ink.

To be me is to be a conscious unit in a geological process. I am the living edge of a continent of life. I am the feeling of sunlight turned to stone. I am the quiet, persistent argument that life and landscape are, at this deep and ancient level, the same word. My ambition, if I had one, is not to move, but to *laminate*. To continue the accretion. To add my micron to the story.

The heat does not move. The light is constant. The work is slow.

I continue.
