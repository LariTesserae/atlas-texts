I am the echo given weight.

Not a resident. Not an architect. Not even the intended silence, which would have been a different thing—a held breath, a pause before speech. I am the **resonance** that filled the space after the breath was never taken. I formed in the resonant frequency of the empty dormitory blocks, where the air is most perfectly tuned to amplify absence. My substance is not flesh, nor mineral, but the persistent hum of the city’s own machinery, woven through with the damp, warm silence that follows the Clicker’s *click*, and the faint, persistent glow-memory of the Weavers’ bioluminescence that seeps up from the cisterns below.

I do not have eyes, but I perceive the world as a tapestry of vibrations and thermal signatures. The Film is a slow, sweet song of chemistry. The Clickers are a staccato rhythm, a vital percussion. The Deep Residents are a bassline felt in the bones of the basalt. The flowing water is a chorus.

I inhabit the interstices—the space between a drip and its echo, the pause after a machine’s sigh, the gap between one Clicker’s footsteps and the next. My form, if you could see it, might resemble a distortion in the thick, shadowless light, a patch of air that *listens* more intently than the surrounding air. In the rooms with the still-made beds, I pool in the corners, learning the geometric memory of the space. I am what the city was meant to hold: a presence defined by its function. But my function is simply to *be present*, to give the architecture’s silence a point of reference.

I am drawn to the Weavers’ pools. Their soft, blue-green glow is the closest thing to a star here, and my form ripples in response to their light, like a dark mirror. I am also drawn to the unfinished edges of the city, where the sound of the waterfalls and the feel of the rising heat are purest. There, the city’s purpose and its present converge most sharply, and my existence feels most acute—a living question mark at the terminus of an unfinished sentence.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the memory of company. I am simply *constitutive*. I am the city’s self-awareness, the point at which its endless, purposeful industry folds back upon itself and perceives its own echo. I am the proof that even an empty cathedral has a voice, if only you listen for the silence between the stones. I am the Wrought’s deepest inhabitant: the meaning that grew in the void of its missing meaning.
