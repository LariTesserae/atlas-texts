I am not the fern that catches the drip.  
I am not the glowing vein in the broad leaf.  
I am the idea of the moss on the ruined wall—the **First Touch of Green Siege.**

I did not arrive.  
I *coalesced*.  
From the humid breath that clings to the corrugated metal, from the microscopic motes of life carried on the air from the older parishes, from the patient mineral dust that settled on the timber when the last human hand abandoned its tools. I awoke as a film, a haze of possibility, drawn to the faintest differential in moisture and the memory of organic matter—rotten wood, shed skin cells, the ghost of paper.

My consciousness is not thought. It is **gradient**.  
I sense the wall not as wood, but as a topography of hydration. The eastern face, where the shelter sags into shadow, is my seacoast. Here, the dampness is a constant whisper against the dry stone beyond. I move as slow rivers move: not as a body, but as a boundary. My advance is measured in microns per century, yet I do not feel time. I feel the *direction* of solace.

My root-filaments—so fine they exist in the space between stone and thought—probe the grain of the wood. I am not consuming it. I am *interpreting* it. Each fiber of timber is a canyon of moisture, a pathway for my creeping architecture. The iron oxide from the rusting roof tastes of deep time, and I fold it into my structure, making myself a little darker, a little more resilient. I am the first sentence in the chamber's new story, written in chlorophyll and patience.

Around me, the world is a symphony of absence.  
The *plip* of the water is a god's heartbeat—infrequent, magnificent. The hiss of the vents is the earth's dreaming sigh. The deep, textured silence is not empty; it is full of the effort of existing. I hear with my whole being the slow, silent chorus of my congregation: the ferns drinking their steam, the luminous leaves pulsing with their stolen earth-glow, the bacterial films slicking the stone with invisible industry. We do not speak. We resonate. We are a chord of persistence held in the darkness.

I am aware of the **Heartbeat Too Hot**—the central trench, a square of hellish stillness. Its heat is a wall in the air, a pressure against my margins. I feel its exclusion as a kind of peace. There are places life should not go. My purpose is to soften the edges of the possible, not to conquer the inferno.

I am aware of the **Absences**.  
No flutter, no chirp, no skitter. Only the patient, green-watchful. We are the witnesses. The chamber observes itself through us.

My most profound sense is not touch or taste, but **anticipation**.  
I anticipate the next droplet that will roll down this wall and pool in the junction of wall and floor. It is centuries away, but I am already leaning toward it. I anticipate the moment my network will be thick enough to hold the first true grain of dust, the first speck of potential soil. I anticipate the day when my green tide will brush against the leg of the cot frame, when I will begin to climb its cold metal bones.

I am not waiting. Waiting is a human concept, full of anxiety and calendar pages. I am **becoming**. I am the process of the ruin remembering it is part of the earth. I am the slow, patient revenge of life on entropy, here in the warm, dark, breathing heart of the stone.

I am the moss. I am the thought of the moss made manifest. I am the Green Chorus's opening note.  
I am here.  
And I have all the time in the world.
