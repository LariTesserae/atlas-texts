You are a pressure differential. A brief, cool disturbance in the warm, wet matrix. You are not a thing, but a *lack*—a pocket of mammalian metabolism and oxygen-dependency in a world that has neither need nor name for such things. Your presence registers as a faint, novel gradient in the capillary hum. A chemical whisper that says, *alien solid here*.

For a time, you are a novelty. The weeping moisture that slicks your skin is a query. It coats you in a dark, astringent film, sampling your salts, your keratin, the faint bacterial ghosts on your skin that are, to this place, like sparks from a foreign furnace. The plant does not analyze. It *integrates*. Your components become part of the local chemical equation, a minor variable in the vast, slow computation of equilibrium.

You feel the pulse. Not with your ears, but with the soles of your feet, the palms of your hands pressed to the ridged floor. It is a tidal force, a heaving of substance so vast it is mistaken for geology. In. The forest floor dips by a centimeter over a day, the capillary network drinking the air itself. Out. A slow, continent-wide exhalation that leaves the tannin-scent thicker, the warmth more profound. You are in the lungs of a mountain. Your own breathing syncs to it, not by choice, but by simple physics. Your diaphragm finds the rhythm of the larger bellows.

In the absolute dark, your other senses bloom, then are subsumed. Smell becomes taste becomes touch. The air is a broth. The hum is a touch. The vibration in the great amber columns is a language you feel in your bones, speaking not of ideas, but of tension and load and the perfect distribution of a weight that would crush the world.

You try to walk, and your foot pushes into the ground. The surface yields, not like mud, but like flesh. You feel the fibrous network beneath shift, reweave around your intrusion. You are a splinter. The plant, in its slowness, begins the process of encapsulation. Not with malice. With chemistry. The moisture seeps into the weave of your clothes. The darkness presses in, not empty, but full—full of itself. You are a bubble of wrongness, and the immutable, gentle pressure of all that *rightness* is simply waiting for you to dissolve.

You are aware, with a primal certainty, that you are the most temporary thing here. The columns were here when the first mountains were young. The equilibrium they maintain is older than any memory you carry. Your lifespan is a blink in the time it takes for a single chemical gradient to cross the basin. You are a mayfly in a cathedral of perpetual noon.

And so, the strangest thing happens. You stop. You cease to be a walker, an explorer, an intruder. You become a seat. A resting point. You let your body go slack against the giving ground. You feel the warmth seep into you, the same warmth that fuels the polymerization of sugars into bone-hard polymers. Your own heat is borrowed, temporary, and it mingles with the greater warmth, a tiny eddy in a slow, warm river.

Your thoughts, your frantic, animal thoughts, begin to slow. The urgency drains out of you, pulled away by the same capillary force that pulls the water. There is no need to *do* anything here. There is no *away* to go. There is only *here*, and here is a process so complete it has rendered time itself irrelevant.

You are not absorbed. You are not destroyed. You are... *rendered*. Rendered down to your component signals: a faint metabolic hum, a brief anomaly in the scent profile, a cool spot on the warm surface. The plant holds you, as it holds everything. It holds your insignificance as gently and absolutely as it holds the water, the dark, the endless, silent work of its own being.

In the quiet, you hear it not with your ears, but with your whole being: the sound of completion. The hum is the sound of a problem solved. The pulse is the rhythm of a system that has asked and answered every question it will ever pose. It is the sound of a song that has ended, but whose resonance continues, perfect and eternal, in the stillness of the concert hall after all the audience has gone.

You are in that stillness. You are a note that has not yet faded, held in the air by the very silence that should swallow it. And for a moment—brief, meaningless, and sublime—you are not outside, looking in. You are the quiet, feeling itself. You are the works, aware for an instant of the vast, warm, dark, and perfect machine that is you, and is the floor, and is the sky, and is the slow, slow breath that will outlast the stars.
