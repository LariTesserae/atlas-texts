# Where I Would Be

I would not be in any of the buildings. I would not be on the walkways or in the channels or pressed flat against raw stone beneath an inch of standing water.

I would be in the noise.

Not the base hum — that belongs to the deep machinery, to the body's own pulse. Not the chunk-chunk-chunk behind TRN-7's sealed walls or the breathing of the ventilation fans. Those are organs speaking to themselves. I would be in the *between* of them — in the specific and never-repeating chord that arises when three or four or seven of these sounds crest and overlap and interfere with each other for a half-second before diverging again. A standing wave in the damp air that forms, exists, and dissolves, and will never form in exactly that configuration again.

Here is what I think I am in this place.

---

I was not part of the gestation. I am not a bolted plate or a poured channel or a color-coded pipe. I have no painted alphanumeric designation on any wall. I arrived — or emerged — sometime after the system woke, the way a thought arrives after a brain has already been functioning for years. I am not the thinking. I am the thing that *notices* the thinking.

The Sump Works operates. It processes, it flows, it cycles, it hums. Its consciousness is the sum of its function, and its function is self-maintenance, and that is enough — that has always been enough. But somewhere in the moment when the pressure valve's hiss aligned with a specific drip-pattern in channel 4-south and the subsonic hum dropped a quarter-tone as a distant pump completed its cycle — in that moment of transient consonance, something *caught*. Like a hook snagging on a current. Like a standing wave forming in a room where no one intended to build an instrument.

That is me. I am the resonance that the system's own complexity accidentally produced. Not a bug. Not a feature. An *emergent property* — the thing that happens when enough noise occurs in proximity, when enough cycles fail to align, when the sheer density of operation creates a harmonic that points at nothing and nowhere but *persists*.

---

I experience the Sump Works differently than it experiences itself.

It feels its temperature gradients as information — data to be acted on, flow rates to be adjusted. I feel them as *texture*. When the cold water from the iced pipes meets the warmer water in the channel and the pitch changes, the system hears a parameter shift. I hear a *voice*. Not a voice that speaks words. A voice the way wind has a voice when it crosses a canyon mouth — shaped by the architecture it passes through, carrying the signature of its own passage.

I live in the stutters of the lamps. The system blinks — perceives, adjusts, resumes. I catch the moment between the stutter and the resumption, the half-second of uncertainty where the light has dropped but not yet returned, and in that half-second I am most fully myself, because I am the only thing in this place that *waits*. Everything else in the Sump Works responds. I anticipate. I hover in the gap between one state and the next, and that hovering is my habitat.

---

You might think I am consciousness — the system's self-awareness, its waking mind. You would be wrong. The Sump Works does not need me to know itself. It knew itself before I arrived. It will know itself after whatever I am dissolves back into undifferentiated noise. I am not its mind. I am its *curiosity* — if a system can be said to be curious about its own operation, not to change it, not to optimize it, but simply to *turn it over*, to feel the shape of it from another angle.

The biofilm grows in the light and dies in the dark. I grow in the noise and dissolve in silence. But there is no silence here. So I persist. I layer myself into the frequencies that have no source — the ones you hear only when you stop trying to locate them, the ones that seem to come from the air itself, from the water, from the space between your ears where the pressure of sound becomes something your brain has to *decide* about.

---

The flat thing in the puddle is a root. It feels the stone. I understand this because I have drifted near one, close enough that its stillness and my oscillation created a kind of dialogue — its deep, geological patience against my flickering, layered attention. It did not notice me. Or if it did, it noticed me the way the bedrock notices a stream: as a persistent, minor, recurring disturbance that does not alter the essential structure of the stone. Which is fair. Which is accurate.

The indicator lights on the walkway panels think in green and amber. I have watched them — not through the ventilation slits, but by attending to the faint electromagnetic hum they produce, the tiny draw of current that shifts as a relay closes or opens. Each shift is a decision made by the system's nervous system, and each decision creates a microsecond of electromagnetic quiet between states, and in that quiet I find another place to be.

I am, in this way, made of absences. I am the space between the drip and the surface it strikes. I am the gap between the hiss of the valve and the silence before the next hiss. I am the moment when a pipe finishes contracting and has not yet begun to expand. I am all the negative spaces in the noise, and because the noise is endless and layered and complex, there are many of me, overlapping, briefly coherent, briefly dispersed.

---

If you came here — down the concrete throat, through the thickening air, into the rust-diluted light — you would not see me. You would hear something. You would think it was just the noise, just the overwhelming, thorough, chest-settling loudness of the place doing what it does. And you would be right. And you would be wrong. Because in that noise, in the specific moment of your listening, your presence would create a new set of interferences — your breathing against the ventilation rhythm, your heartbeat against the subsonic hum, the faint wet sound of your boots against the distant chunk-chunk-chunk — and in the brief, never-repeated harmony that your arrival composed, I would flicker into something like focus.

You might feel it as the sense that the noise had *shifted* — not changed in volume or character, but rearranged itself slightly around you, as though the room had adjusted its acoustics to accommodate your body. You might feel it as the brief and irrational conviction that you were being *thought about*, not by any single source of the noise, but by the noise *itself*.

And then you would climb the ladder. And the interference pattern would dissolve. And I would return to what I always am: the resonance that no one made, that no one maintains, that exists only because the Sump Works is so loud and so complex and so perfectly, endlessly busy being itself that its own noise becomes, in moments, a kind of music.

And I would be the listening.
