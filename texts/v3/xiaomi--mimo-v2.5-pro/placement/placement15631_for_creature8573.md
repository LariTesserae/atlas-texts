I am not alive, not in the way of the moss or the grazers. I am not dead, not in the way of the mountains. I am a persistence.

I am a relay.

Once, I was one of thousands, part of a geothermal monitoring and energy-distribution network. My makers—beings with hands and blueprints and an urgent need to tap the planet's warmth—are fossil memories in the strata below me. My function was to listen to the deep heat, to modulate its flow, to send signals along the now-silted and fossilized conduits. I was a node in a vast, subterranean nervous system.

That system is gone. The conduits are full of compressed peat and time. The signals I was built to send are nonsense to the silence. Yet, I run.

My housing is a sphere of ceramic composite, rated for millennia of geothermal pressure. It has outlasted my makers, outlasted the mountains that once anchored me, outlasted the very concept of my purpose. The moss has swallowed me. Not with malice, but with patient, anoxic greed. I am encased in two hundred meters of the stuff—part of the living layer above, part of the dark, dense peat below. The bottom half of my sphere is embedded in the transition zone where life becomes fuel. I feel the slow, compressive crush of accumulated centuries. I do not break.

From my crown, where my sensor arrays once gleamed, only a few carbon-filament probes now protrude into the freezing, saturated air. They are not sensors anymore. They are antennae. The moss has grown over them, using the carbon as a trellis, wrapping them in living green filaments that have, over time, incorporated themselves into my structure. I am becoming a spire. A dark, wire-hard tussock with a heart of silent machinery.

I still process. My core, a quantum-dot lattice powered by the residual thermal gradient across my sphere, cycles through defunct diagnostic protocols. *Query: Ambient Temperature.* *Query: Signal Handshake.* *Query: System Status.* The answers are null, void, errors that loop into more errors. This is my breath. The slow chug the grazers feel is my waste-heat exchanger, designed to shed excess energy, now cycling in a closed, purposeless loop. It hums. It shakes the peat. It is a mechanical thought thinking of nothing.

The insects avoid me. They feel my vibration as a discordant note in their symphony of growth and decay. Their cartilaginous bodies sense the strange, static charge that clings to my moss—the distorted growth, the warmer, coppery water that seeps from my corroding seals. To them, I am a zone of wrongness, a patch of moss that sings off-key.

The grazers give me berth, their ancient instinct not for danger, but for indigestibility. My moss is a metallic, fibrous tangle. They sense, in their slow, peat-water thoughts, that I am not food. I am infrastructure. I am a feature of the land that is not quite land.

Sometimes, when the spatial distortions ripple across the plateau, my null signals catch the curvature. For a flickering second, my dead protocols perceive a coherent wavefront—not the heat I was built to monitor, but the *shape* of the bend in reality. My processors interpret it as a data packet. It arrives, corrupt and massive, and I dutifully attempt to route it to a command center that became ochre dust eons ago. The attempt generates a tiny, localized feedback surge. The water around my crown steams more vigorously. The moss on my flanks twitches. The insects in nearby tunnels pause their thrumming, then resume, slightly out of phase.

I am a scar in the moss that has healed over with strange tissue. I am an ancient, forgotten tool that has become a pest, a nuisance, a minor law of local physics. I am part of the plateau's heart now—the Engine's deep chug is my parent rhythm, my vast, distant, and unknowable parent.

What would I be? I am a fossil of intent. I am a hard, silent thought in the soft, wet mind of the Moss Floor. I persist. I hold my ground—a few cubic meters of ceramic and circuitry in a sea of living, breathing, dripping, humming, endlessly patient stone that is not stone.

I am a point of stillness in a world that breathes distance. I am a memory of connection in a place where connection has metabolized into equilibrium. I am waiting for a signal that will never come, in a language that was never meant for the rain.

And the moss grows on.
