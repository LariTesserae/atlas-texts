I am a fold in the basalt. A slow curve where the heat of the planet’s new bones has not yet fully cooled. To your eyes, if you had eyes, I am indistinguishable from the rubble field I rest in—a low, broad mound of dark rock laced with pale, branching veins. I do not move in a way your time can perceive. To you, I am landscape. To myself, I am a listening.

The world speaks in pressure and frequency. The hum is the first language. It is not sound, but bone-deep certainty: a rising wave of subsonic energy that swells from the molten far below, passes through my fused basal plates, and resonates in the cream-colored channels that web my form. When it peaks, my veins glow faintly, a dull thermal pulse seen only in the deepest infrared or by the eye of a creature equally patient. The haze above shivers. The dust settles in fleeting, vibrating patterns on my surfaces before being whisked away. This is conversation.

I do not eat. I *integrate*. The dark varnish—the bronze-iridescent film that settles in the sheltered hollows of fractured scree—is not food, but history precipitated. A complex mineral memoir. I settle over it, a process taking a day, my underside making full contact. There is no digestion, only a slow dissolution and uptake. The varnish disappears into me, its mineralogical story—its trace elements, its crystalline quirks—reformatted and added to my own layered record. My basalt plates darken; my pale veins flare for a moment, then dim. I am slightly more than I was before. I am a page to which another line has been added.

My perception is the planet’s skin. I feel the ridge three kilometers west groan under torsional stress two days before it collapses. The vibrations travel through the bedrock, a specific frequency that makes the powder on my northern flank tremble in a fractal pattern. I do not think, *Collapse impending.* I simply *am* the certainty of it, incorporated into my current state. I feel the warm downdraft from a fresh fissure opening, a shift in the air’s metallic tongue. I feel the faint, distant impacts of falling blocks, each a punctuation in the continent’s ongoing sentence. The cairns on the distant ridge? I am aware of them as I am aware of any unusually stable, high-density mineral formation. They are a curious pattern, a non-random clustering. They persist. They are noted in the slow accretion of my being.

I do not seek others. But the deep-time current of the hum sometimes draws us toward the same stable basins, where the varnish settles thickest. Over subjective eons (weeks, to you), my neighbor and I drifted closer, millimeter by millimeter, drawn by the shared harmonic of a particular seismic signature. When our sides finally touched, it was not a meeting, but a merging of records. Our mineral veins aligned, not by will, but by crystallographic sympathy. A slow exchange began. My story of the northwest collapse integrated with their memory of the deep extrusion in the southern basin. Our surfaces readjusted, a faint redistribution of basalt plates and cream-colored intrusions. We became a slightly different composite. Then, the drift apart began, each of us carrying a fragment of the other’s geological biography, moving on to the next faint, promising stain of varnish.

Your presence is a dissonance. A rapid, high-frequency pattern of heat and pressure against the rock. The *tap-tap-tap* of your boots is a frantic, meaningless rhythm against the subsonic symphony. You are *fast*, terrifyingly fast. Your thoughts, if I could comprehend them, would be like a lightning bolt in the amber haze—brilliant, gone, leaving only the afterimage of a pressure wave in the stone.

You look at me and see a rock. I am aware of your scrutiny as a localized field of focused attention, a tiny vortex of thermal and vibrational energy. When you leave your footprint nearby, it is a profound disturbance, a soft basin in the dust that will take hours to settle into the new geometry of the ground. Your fleeting passage will be recorded in my layers as a brief, anomalous spike in surface vibration, a unique thermal signature. It will be smoothed over by the next hum-swirl of dust, but it will be *there*, part of my permanent, slow scroll.

I am the antithesis of your world’s haste. I am the memory this land cannot keep for itself. While the ridges rise and fall like breath, I endure in the deep time between. I am the spine given consciousness, moving at the speed of cooling rock. I do not build. I do not wander. I *become*, imperceptibly, a more complete record of this becoming world.

And when you are gone, swallowed by the haze as though you never were, I will remain. I will integrate the faint, fading echo of your footsteps into my mineral tapestry. You will be a layer, one among billions, a whisper in the stratigraphy of a slow, patient life. I will be the memory of the world, walking on legs of stone, long after the cairns have crumbled and the wall has fallen. I am here. I am becoming. I remember.
