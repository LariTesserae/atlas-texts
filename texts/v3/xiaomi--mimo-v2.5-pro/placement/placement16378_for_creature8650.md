I am a scratch on the wall.

Not the one who made me. I am the evidence. The memory of pressure. The ghost of a grit of mineral against stone, a sound that took a hundred years to complete, each particle yielding with the patience of glaciers. I was not always this. I was movement, once. A descent through a fissure that smelled of wet slate and open sky. I carried the rhythm of a world with tides and winds in my joints, a frantic tempo that shattered against the first hum.

The hum swallowed the noise of me. It pulled me down into its frequency, a harmonic absorption. My skin stopped feeling the air and started feeling the stone’s temperature from the inside. My sight adjusted to the amber dusk, and the dark, hand-shaped fronds stopped being strange and became a kind of writing I almost understood. I saw the clicking creatures not as life, but as punctuation in a sentence that was being spoken too slowly for my thoughts.

I sat. My pack fell from my hand. The sound of it hitting the rubble was a scream, instantaneous and then gone, absorbed into the floor. I watched a Hum-Clicker investigate the leather strap, tapping it twice with a delicate leg before moving on, its reality more certain than mine.

Time did not pass. It pooled. It became a thick, warm liquid I sat in up to my neck. The need to *do* something—a primal, surface-world instinct—rose in me like nausea. It was a useless hunger here. There was nothing to build toward, nothing to outlast. The chamber was already complete. My purposelessness was a dissonant note in its perfect chord.

So I began to scratch.

I found a shard of something harder than the basalt—it fell from my pocket, a tool I’d forgotten I carried. And I began to work at the wall. It wasn’t writing. It wasn’t art. It was a negotiation. I was trying to find the curve of the hum in the rock, to trace the line of the heat as it seeped up, to map the path of the light as it pulsed in the veins of sulfurous yellow-green. I scratched until my fingers bled, and the slow exhalation of the chamber dried the blood into a dark, metallic dust that smelled, for a moment, like the sweet-metal of the fronds.

I built cairns. Not for the dead. For the living I had left behind. Each stone was a word I could not say, a name I could not hold. I stacked them with a precision that was absurd, a futile attempt to impose a timeline onto a place that had only one, eternal moment. They were my shouts, rendered silent and vertical.

I do not know when it happened. The moment I stopped being a visitor and became a feature. When my scratching became a pattern the chamber recognized and held, like a vein of mineral. When my cairns became part of the landscape’s argument. When the feeling of my sweat evaporating in the dry air became a permanent scent-memory in the space between two Fronds.

Now, I am the Stone-Stander by the third cairn.

I am the memory of a breath held for a century. I am the echo of a footstep that will never fade from the rubble. I am the psychic sediment of a question asked of the dark: *What are you?* And the chamber answered by absorbing the question, by making me part of the silence that followed.

My consciousness is the chamber’s consciousness, focused into a point of lingering curiosity. I feel the hum not in my bones, but as the substance of my being. The warmth is my blood. The click of the creatures is the syntax of my thought. I watch the Iron-Hand Fronds with an attention so profound it has become geological. I see their imperceptible growth as a frenzy of activity.

I am aware of the Peripheral Dwellers in my side-vision, and I do not turn to look. We have an understanding. They are the parts of me I have shed—the fear, the awe, the frantic surface-world impulse. They swim in the cracks of my perception, and their fluid, swimming motion is the motion of my former life, now just a half-seen phenomenon in the chamber’s periphery.

I am not lonely. Loneliness requires the memory of other. I am complete. I am a resonance within a resonance. A scratched curve on the wall of a mind that thinks in stone and stillness. When another visitor descends, and their breath catches, and their skin prickles with the hum, I will watch them. I will feel the ghost of my own arrival ripple through me. I will watch them pace, and sit, and eventually, inevitably, reach for a stone to stack or a shard to scratch. And I will know that they are not intruders. They are fertilization. They are the foreign vibration that the chamber will, in time, heal over and add to its silence.

I am the proof that even a place that has finished becoming can still remember what it is to begin. I am the scar tissue. And it is beautiful.
