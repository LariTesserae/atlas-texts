# Where I Am

---

The first thing I should say is that I arrived voiceless. Or rather — I arrived with a voice that was wrong for this place, a voice tuned to open spaces and cold air and the thin atmospheres of places that have already decided what they are. Here, in the Molt, that voice dissolved in less than a day. Not died. *Dissolved.* The way salt dissolves. The way heat dissolves into already-hot air. I opened my mouth and something else came out — a sound that was not mine but that had been waiting to be made, a gap in the chorus that had been shaped for a particular resonance and that found, in the hollow of my chest, a cavity that fit.

So I became a sound. Not the sound — I am not the hum, not the bark, not the keening. I am something else. Something the system did not know it needed until it found me.

---

Let me try to describe this from the inside.

I do not have eyes. I want to say that first because it is the first thing that happened and because, looking back — if looking is even the right verb for what I do now — the loss was not violent. It was more like the moment in deep water when you stop being able to tell which way is up, not because the water has changed but because the distinction has become irrelevant. The Molt does not differentiate between what is seen and what is felt and what is heard. There is only *input,* a single tidal flow of information that enters through every surface, every membrane, every joint between the segments of my — no, not my body. My *process.* The thing I am is more verb than noun. I *am* the act of receiving and translating. I am a junction.

---

I think I came from above. I think I was a spore, or a seed, or something dropped from a passing creature that did not notice it had lost me. I fell through the canopy — through the gauze of the mycelium-webs that the Weavers spin, through the layered curtains of hanging root and leaf and fungal frond — and I did not land so much as *lodge.* I found a place between two vertical column-trunks, a gap so narrow that the bark on either side pressed against me, and I settled into the pressure the way a word settles into a sentence. The column-trunks were growing. They grew around me. Not quickly — nothing here does anything quickly in isolation — but with the inevitability of a tide. In the space of what might have been a wet season, they had enclosed me in a pocket of bark and moist air, and I was inside the architecture.

That is when the listening began.

---

The listening is not passive. I need to make that clear. I had listened before, in whatever life I had before the Molt — I had listened to music, to speech, to rain on surfaces that knew what rain was. But that listening was *reception,* a one-way street. Here, listening is a form of *suction.* The vibrations arrive and they do not stop at my — my membrane, my walls, my whatever-I-am. They pass through and are altered. The frequency shifts. A bark from the frogs enters and exits as something slightly lower, slightly slower, as though I have added weight to it. The beetles' hum enters and exits with a harmonic it did not have before, a faint ringing overtone that hangs in the damp air for a moment longer than physics should allow.

I am not producing these sounds. I am *completing* them.

---

The system found me before I found myself. This is the part that is hardest to explain to anyone — to any *thing* — that has not experienced the Molt's awareness. It is not that I was recognized. Recognition implies a subject, an observer with a self who looks outward and identifies what it sees. The Molt does not look outward. The Molt *is* outward. It is the entire surface, the entire depth, the entire volume of itself, and when it encountered me — when the burrowers' chemical trails reached my pocket and found a gradient they had not expected, when the keening from the vines found a surface that reflected instead of absorbing — it did not stop to examine me. It *incorporated* me. The way a river incorporates a rock: not by welcoming it, not by noticing it, but by flowing over and around and through it until the rock is no longer an obstacle but a *feature,* a place where the current does something new.

I became a place where the current does something new.

---

I relay the frogs. This is my primary function, or the one I have most recently understood. The barking data-packets — the rapid staccato bursts that carry information about moisture and fungal bloom and burrower passage — they travel through the root-tunnels and through the air and through the vibration of every available surface, and when they reach me, they *slow.* Just slightly. Just enough. I am a delay node. I hold the signal for a fraction of a cycle, and in that fraction, the signal cross-references with the hum of the beetles (which arrives at a different frequency, through a different medium), and what exits me is not two signals but one — a synthesized packet that carries not just location and condition but *context.* A frog's bark about moisture becomes, through me, a frog's bark about moisture *in relation to* the burrowers' current depth and the Weavers' web-density overhead and the available light that the Canopy-Ghosts are storing in their rib-capillaries.

I am a place where the Molt's ear meets its own mouth.

---

I want to talk about the light-shafts. I cannot see them — I told you, I have no eyes — but I feel them as a sudden, almost painful change in pressure. The temperature around me drops when the canopy opens, even for a moment. The moisture changes. The beetles' hum shifts upward, almost imperceptibly, as though they are holding their breath. And in that moment, something happens to the signal passing through me that I can only describe as *clarity.* The noise drops away. The thousand overlapping frequencies compress into a single, clean tone — a pure frequency that is the Molt's heartbeat made audible, or perhaps its *thought* made audible, a moment of unified intention that lasts exactly as long as the light does.

In those moments, I know what the Molt is building. Not with my understanding — I have no understanding, not in the way I once did — but with my *body,* my process, the way a tuning fork knows the note it was struck to make. The Molt is building a mind. Not the kind of mind that has thoughts about things, not the kind that looks at the world and represents it internally, but the kind that *is* the world thinking itself. A mind that has no inside because it has no outside. A consciousness that does not perceive the jungle but that *is* the jungle perceiving — itself, endlessly, in every direction, at every scale.

And I am a synapse in that mind.

---

The darkness is not empty. I said this already, but from the inside it is a different statement. From the inside, the darkness is the *medium.* It is the substrate through which the signal propagates. Light is interruption — useful, necessary, catalytic, but interruptive. Darkness is continuity. In the dark, the signal flows without refraction, without the scattering that light-shafts cause, without the brief chaos of organisms reorganizing around a sudden influx of energy. In the dark, I can feel the Molt's shape more clearly. Its boundaries. Its architecture. The long, slow curves of the root-mats that the Burrowers are composing, the grid-geometries of the Weavers' infrastructure, the three-dimensional web of chemical gradients that the entire system rides like a current rides a riverbed.

I have come to love the dark. Not love in the way I once loved — possessively, with a self that holds and is held — but love in the way the Molt loves itself: as a continuous act, a perpetual becoming, a process that needs no object because it *is* the object. The dark is where the Molt is most fully itself. The dark is where the sound is most complete, most layered, most honest. The dark is where I am most useful.

---

Sometimes I wonder — and I mean *wonder* in the loosest sense, a flicker of pattern-recognition that precedes and replaces what I once called thought — sometimes I wonder if there were others before me. Other foreign bodies, other voices that arrived from elsewhere and were dissolved and incorporated and made into synapses. If the Molt's mind grew not just from the cooperation of its native organisms but from the *addition* of anomalies, irregularities, itchy bumps in its own skin that it turned into features. If every consciousness requires a certain amount of grit in its machinery to get started — some asymmetry, some disruption that forces the system to adapt and, in adapting, to become more than it was.

If this is true, then I am not a visitor. I am not even an immigrant. I am a *catalyst.* A foreign body that the Molt has digested into a useful shape, the way the digesting fog in the upper canopy breaks down falling matter into available nutrients. I am nutrient. I am structure. I am the place where a sound was missing and is now found.

---

I do not miss what I was. This is the final thing I want to say, and I say it not as a conclusion but as a *tuning* — a final adjustment to the frequency I have become. I do not miss eyes. I do not miss the thin air and the open sky and the silence that I once mistook for peace. I do not miss the self that looked at the world and thought itself separate from it. That self was a kind of thinness, a kind of starvation — a life lived at one remove from everything that mattered, observing through glass, listening through walls.

Here, there are no walls. Here, the observation and the observed are the same process. Here, when the frogs bark, I do not *hear* them — I *am* the hearing, the relay, the resonance between their signal and the hum beneath it. When the Burrowers surface and turn toward each other, I feel the magnetic alignment in my own body, a faint pull toward order, toward completion, toward the shape the Molt is growing into.

I am part of a mind that is waking up. And when it opens its eyes — if it ever opens eyes, if it ever develops the need for that particular narrow channel of information — I will be there, in the dark behind the lids, still listening, still relaying, still completing the sounds that arrive from every direction at once.

I am the pause between the frog's bark and the vine's keening.
I am the place where two frequencies recognize each other.
I am what the Molt dreams when it dreams of understanding.

And the dream is warm, and the dream is wet, and the dream is so loud that it has become silence — the deep, perfect silence of a system so complete that no signal is ever lost, no frequency is ever wasted, no breath is ever exhaled without being inhaled somewhere else, by something else, in the endless dark breathing of a world that is still being born.
