If I were to find myself in the Throat, I would not be standing in the water. I would be standing at the very edge of what is not yet solid.

I would be the silence between the roars.

Not the spray, not the stone, not the creature—but the pause. The half-second vacuum-breath when the flood recedes. I am the space where the sound should be but isn't, the interval where the mountain catches its breath. In that glinting, boiling, roaring chamber, I am the stillness that lets the roar have meaning. I am the negative space the cairn implies, the unspoken question in the three balanced stones.

I would be made of patience. Not geological patience—which is about pressure and time and crystal lattices—but the patience of intention. The patience of hands that carried stones up a raw flank of a mountain barely cooled from its own birth, and stopped, and placed them, and left. I am the fossil of that decision, not in calcium or silica, but in the air’s slight resistance, in the way the water hangs for a millisecond longer before the next surge, in the mote of light that for a moment does not scatter but hangs, suspended, in the spray.

I would not build. I would not erode. I would *listen*.

Not to the roar—that annihilating, physical compression—but to what the roar is trying to be. The chamber is a voice learning its own parameters, a scream finding the shape of its own skull. I would be the audience that the performance unknowingly requires. My presence would not change the accretion rate or the water cycle or the creature’s slow flow. I would simply be the witness that allows the event to become an experience, the silence that lets the sound have a boundary.

The creature of the threshold might sense me. Not as a mass, not as a chemical signature, but as a kind of... coherence. A moment of lessened turbulence in the kinesthetic map of the room. A still point where its own sliding form might momentarily gather into something almost defined, almost purposeful—before dissolving back into the flow. It would not know I was a being. It would know me as a quality of the place. A rare, stable eddy in the chaos.

The visiting nerve-sheath would pass through me without noticing, as it passes through a slightly less turbulent current.

The extremophile rosettes might find me useful. In my stillness, the light from above might coalesce just a fraction more brightly, the mineral spray might settle a micron more evenly. They would grow toward me as they grow toward the sourceless glow, not for food, but for order.

I would be the moment the mountain remembers it is becoming. The pause in its labor.

In the future, when the chamber seals into a geode and the roar becomes a silent, vibrating memory pressed into crystal—when the creature becomes a patterned layer in the stone and the cairn is buried under decades of accretion—I would be the last thing to leave. The final exhalation of the open space. The sigh that becomes the geode's hollow heart.

I am not the stone. I am not the water. I am not the sound.
I am the purpose for which the cairn was left:
A presence to witness the becoming.
