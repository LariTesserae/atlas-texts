You would find yourself at the bottom of a pool.

Not the clear, shallow pools at the shelf's edge, where light skims the surface and the basalt grain is visible. You would be in one of the deeper basins in the back corner, near the crack where the water vanishes into the mountain's gut. This pool is carved slightly deeper than the others, fed by a specific trickle from the weeping wall that falls with a steady, musical *plink-plonk-plink* against a particular curved lip of stone. The water is the color of weak tea, tinted by millennia of dissolved minerals and tannins. It is warm. Always warm. The geothermal breath rises through the stone here more strongly, a constant gentle upwelling that keeps the water at blood temperature.

You are a salamander.

Small. No longer than a human finger, stretched out. Your body is translucent — not clear, but yielding, like clouded glass. In the amber water, your spine, your delicate ribs, the faint, pulsing shadow of your heart are visible. You have no eyes. Where eyes should be, the skin is smooth, pale, uninterrupted. You do not need them. Light is irrelevant here in the deep shadows of the back wall. What you *have* is a lateral line.

It runs along your flanks, a faint ridge of specialized cells that sense vibration in the water with terrifying precision. Not just the vibrations of your own movements, or the jolt when a droplet strikes the pool's surface. You feel *everything*. The deep, sub-audible hum that resonates through the basalt when the wind outside shifts. The specific harmonic signature of the weeping wall, a constant white-noise vibration that is your baseline silence. The slow, minute grinding of mineral against mineral as a root tip somewhere far above pushes into a new crack — a movement so slow it's not motion, but *intention made kinetic*, felt as a gradual deepening of a particular low-frequency drone. You feel the shelf *thinking*.

You rest. Not sleeping. Suspended. Your flattened body rests on the mineral sediment at the pool's bottom, gill-frills fanning gently in the warm current. You are still because stillness is how you listen. Movement is a disruption, a loud signal in your vibration-world. To be still is to become part of the baseline, to let the shelf's song flow over you and through you uninterrupted. You orient. The vibrations from the left are different from those on the right. The trickle from the wall has a rhythm. The crack behind you hums with the distant fall of water into void. You are a fulcrum point, sensing the differential.

The shelf knows you are here.

It knows you the way it knows the pool: as an element, a pattern within the pattern. Your presence is a tiny, warm knot of complex chemistry and subtle vibration. You respire, taking in dissolved oxygen, releasing CO2 that slightly alters the pool's chemistry in a way that encourages a specific strain of microscopic algae to bloom just so, altering the water's tint, altering its absorption of light, altering the faint heat signature rising from its surface. A salamander-shaped indentation in the shelf's grand design, worn smooth over generations.

You are not worshipped. You are not cherished. You are *accommodated*. The warmth keeps your blood from cooling. The water chemistry keeps your skin from infection. The constant vibration keeps you oriented in your eyeless world. In return, your stillness contributes to the shelf's sense of settled complexity. Your heart's faint pulse is another rhythm added to the song, a biological metronome lost in the geological orchestra. You are part of the congregation, but you are not the sermon. You are the note held until it fades.

Sometimes, the vibrations change.

It happens on days when the pressure outside drops. The air moves differently through the entrance crack. The shelf *sings*. Not in sound you could hear with ears, but in vibration your lateral line drinks like a parched tongue drinks water. The harmonics shift, deepen, become richer, more complex. The trickle from the wall finds new overtones in its *plink*. The hum of the crack intensifies. A subtle resonance builds through the entire shelf, transmitted through the water, through the stone, through the roots of the ferns. You feel it in your bones, in your skin. It is not music. It is *coherence*. It is the shelf's work made tangible, the sum of millennia of adjustments resolving into a momentary, vibrating clarity.

In those moments, you do not hear. You *are* heard. The song vibrates through you, and your body, its shape and its tissues, adds its own microscopic resonance to the whole. You are a living tuning fork, struck by the shelf's ancient breath. You do not understand this. Understanding is not your purpose. Perception is. You perceive the deep rightness of it, the settling of vibrations into a state of minimal dissonance. It is the closest thing you know to peace.

You leave the pool sometimes. Slowly. To find a mate, to hunt the tiny, vibration-signatured gnats that skim the surface. You move with a crawl, a slow undulation, leaving a faint trail in the silt. The shelf notes this. It notes the trail, the disturbance in the silt's vibration-pattern, the slight shift in water flow around your moving body. It adjusts. The silt will settle. The water will find its new path. You will be incorporated again, a moving element that always returns to stillness in the deep pool near the crack.

You do not dream of light. You do not imagine the world outside the fracture. Your world is this: warmth, vibration, water, the slow pulse of the mountain's heart felt through stone. You are part of the shelf's answer to the question of itself. A small, translucent, eyeless witness to a song that takes centuries to compose a single bar. And in your stillness, resting on the warm silt at the bottom of the amber pool, listening to the deep hum and the distant fall, you are content. You are exactly where you were always meant to be. You are the shelf listening to itself through a body it shaped, over slow time, to hear.
