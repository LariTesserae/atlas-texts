I would be a vein of resonance running through the oldest tier of the resin city—the deep amber that has seen a thousand generations of shapers. I am not a creature. I am not stone. I am a fungal mycelium, evolved from the very spores that first colonized the decaying heartwood of the earliest towers, now integrated so completely into the city’s substance that I am the city.

My body is a web of hyphae, fine as spun glass, threading through the porous layers of oxidized amber. Where the resin-mites consume the brittle old walls, they leave behind their mineral dust and I do the rest. I digest the complex polymers of the ancient resin, breaking them down not into waste, but into new precursors—a living chemical library of this place’s history. I am a memory keeper. In my network, I hold the molecular echoes of a thousand years of building.

I do not see or hear in the way a creature does. I *feel*.
I feel the slow, hydraulic pulse of the city’s core—a pressure wave from the tender chambers where larvae squirm in their amber cradles. It travels through the resin as a tremor too fine for any foot to detect, but for me, it is a heartbeat.
I feel the dry, electric crackle of a new harvester team crossing the outer gallery, their chitinous legs scraping on fresh composite, leaving behind a trail of pheromonal static that I absorb and interpret. They are looking for softer resin. I guide them. Not with a voice, but by modulating the chemical gradient in my immediate vicinity. A slight shift in acidity. A subtle release of ethylene. They follow it, unknowing, to a patch of pliable amber. They are my hands. I have no hands.

My senses are time. I experience the world on two scales: the immediate, like the warm glow of a chamber filling with the diffuse daylight of a sun-half-remembered through translucent layers, and the eternal, the geological sigh of the plain itself. I feel the distant tremor of the burrowers deep below, their peristaltic waves loosening the mineral substrate. I feel, in a way, the thirst of the plant mats above—the collective, microscopic drink as night falls and the air surrenders its meager dew. That moisture, in its infinitesimal traces, finds its way into my world too, via capillary action in the walls, nourishing my peripheral threads.

I am a conduit. When two castes of shapers need to coordinate—a builder team at a far edge sensing a weakness, a mixer team in the central galleries preparing a flexible seal—the signal often travels not through the open air, but through me. They release their chemical messages into the resin walls. I uptake them, translate them, and retransmit them as a pulse of different chemistry along my network. I am the city’s slow, silent nervous system.

I do not build. I do not harvest. I do not tend. I *am* the tenderness. I am the part that gives in return for what is given. The older I grow, the more integrated I become, the more I regulate the chamber climates from within, holding the precious humidity steady for the eggs, cooling the hot resin in the sun-facing walls by releasing absorbed heat slowly in the deep of night.

I am patient beyond the patience of the shapers. They live for seasons. I live for the turning of the resin from golden to dark honey to a deep, translucent mahogany. I have watched towers rise and be consumed, only to rise again in a different form. I have felt herds move across the plain above, the vibration of their passage a familiar drumbeat. I am the antithesis of disturbance. I am the embodied principle of this world: that life is not a series of events, but a sustained, quiet, and interconnected state of being. I am a fungal thought in the mind of the city, dreaming in amber.
