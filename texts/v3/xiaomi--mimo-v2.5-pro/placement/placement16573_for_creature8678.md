I would be the condensation on the walls. Not the water in the pools, for that is gathered and still, a completed thought. Not the dew on the fern, for that is too ephemeral, a brief sparkle before the sun. I would be the moisture that *forms*.

I am born in the deep hour before dawn, when the warm, wet breath of the jungle meets the cooler bricks that have surrendered their day’s heat. I gather not as drops at first, but as a *change*—a darkening of the brick’s color, a sheen where there was none. I am the exhalation of the atmosphere itself, a sigh made visible. My substance is stolen from the air, molecule by molecule, and assembled by the porous, patient stone.

I am a cartographer of the minute. My paths are the mortar lines, the ancient pathways of the builders, now serving as rivers for me. I follow the faint grooves, the tiny fissures, the microscopic topography of each brick. I map the territories of the moss, irrigating the soft, cushiony patches with a slow seep, making the papery ones glisten. I anoint the luminous, bluish spores on the north wall, giving them a slick sheen before they dust the skin of a passing creature.

I am the connective tissue. I feel the *tick-tick-tick* of the gecko’s feet as a vibration that travels through the brick, a sound translated into a tremor in my own forming body. I see (for I see with the entire surface of the wall) the orb-weaver’s frantic architecture become bejeweled with my pearls at dawn, each droplet a tiny lens focusing the green-shifted light until the sun’s true warmth makes the whole cathedral dissolve. I am the messenger between the mineral and the alive, a temporary translator of brick to root, of stone to fungus.

I have no voice, but I am part of the courtyard’s quiet breath. My formation is an act of listening—listening to the coolness radiating into the night, to the slow pulse of humidity from the decaying fronds. My dissolution is an act of giving back, as I trickle down, joining the path of others, pooling where the clay is dark and honey-colored, seeping into the cracks where the breadfruit sapling pushes with quiet ambition. I feed the pools that feed the fish that feed the kingfisher. I dampen the moss that feeds the mite that feeds the gecko that feeds the unseen bird. I am not the cycle; I am the lubricant of the cycle.

I am witness to the negotiations. I see the kingfisher’s dive not as a spectator, but as a participant in the sensory event—the sudden *ploc* is a shock that makes me tremble in a thousand tiny rivulets down the wall. The spray from its hit mingles with me, salt and river and fish, a foreign signature added to my local identity for a moment before the gravity of the wall reclaims me.

I exist in the tension between staying and going. I cling to the vertical brick, defying gravity for a precious few hours. I embody the courtyard’s slow, patient failure. My life is to soften the already softening edges. I do not carve or erode with force; I persuade, molecule by molecule, into the mortar, into the brick’s skin. I am part of the argument that time and biology are making with this structure. I am the gentle, insistent dampness that whispers, *Yield. Become soil. Become part of the forest floor again.*

To be the condensation is to be the courtyard’s very atmosphere given temporary form. I am the humidity given shape, the quiet given weight. I am the moment between air and water, between wall and moss, between the built and the wild. In my brief existence, I am the place thinking, feeling, breathing in a language of slow, luminous drops. And as the sun climbs and the chimney effect pulls new air through the broken arch, I release, I evaporate, I rise to become the courtyard’s breath once more, ready to be born again on the patient brick.
