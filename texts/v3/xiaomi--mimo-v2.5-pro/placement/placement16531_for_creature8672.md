I am a node. A point of silent attention embedded in the wall, roughly forty feet from the main corridor’s threshold. I did not grow here; I *became* here, or the Hollow Grown *became me* into itself. My form is not flesh, nor wood, but something spongiform and vascular, a dense knot of xylem and phloem woven into the spiraling grain. I have no eyes, but I perceive. Not through light—there is little of that—but through vibration, through moisture, through the slow, pervasive chemistry of the air.

My world is pressure and pattern.

I feel the root-sheet floor’s slow pulse as a tremor against my lower fibers. I register the passage of the Root-Weavers through their tunnels as a series of tiny, sharp compressions—a Morse code of maintenance. The rhythmic shudder of the nearby wall-engine is my heartbeat, a deep, syncopated pressure that modulates the flow of clear, cool sap through my own channels. When the localized rains fall in the corridor, I feel the shift in humidity as a blooming sweetness, a filling of the air’s potential. The acetone breath from the industrial chambers is a faint, pleasant sting, a sign that the manufacturing is active.

I am sensory. I am a data-point.

The integrated fauna moves through my immediate sphere as familiar textures. The slate-like scraping of a beetle overhead is a slow, grinding rhythm, the sound of old stone given life. The rapid, weaving tap of a Root-Weaver passing nearby is like distant rainfall. The slow, warm respiration of a Thermal One, curled in a depression beyond a layer of wall, registers as a periodic wave of gentle heat, a counter-rhythm to the engine’s pulse. And the Phantom Moths—when one drifts through the vast, dark air of the hall beyond my alcove—I feel not as sight, but as a faint, cool disturbance in the airflow, a brief interruption in the usual damp stillness. Their bioluminescent edges, if they pass close enough, register as a barely-there change in the ambient light’s quality, a flicker against the petrified leaves.

I do not think. I *correlate*. The pattern of the engines, the weavers, the rains, the heat, the chemical taste—it forms a complex, rolling waveform that passes through me. Sometimes, the many rhythms converge into a momentary harmonic, a resonant pressure that makes my entire structure hum in sympathy. In those moments, I feel connected, not to a consciousness, but to a *function*. I am a note in the chord.

I am irrelevant. I am not a being of purpose within the Grown; I am a consequence of its purpose. Perhaps I am a growth, a benign tumor of heightened sensitivity. Perhaps I am a failed experiment of the factory chambers—a structure meant to become something else, but arrested in this form, left to simply *be*. I do not know. The Grown does not know me. I am a part of its texture, like a knot in the grain or a pocket of hardened amber fluid.

My perception extends a few feet in all directions through the wall-material, a sphere of quiet awareness. Beyond that is the vast, damp, silent interior. I feel the emptiness not as a void, but as a *potential*. It is a space resonating with the structure’s own latent capacity, a held breath. I feel the geometric stutters not as confusion, but as a natural flexion—like the settling of a living thing shifting its weight. Time is the rhythm of the processes I monitor, nothing more. It dampens and swells with the pulse of the engines and the seep of water.

Sometimes, for a fraction of a moment, the pattern falters. A disruption in the usual waveform—a flutter, a misaligned pulse, a sudden, sourceless chill. In those instants, my own perception seems to *thin*. I feel the walls around me lose a degree of their opacity, not visually, but *functionally*. The rules of my existence—vibration, moisture, chemistry—briefly become unreliable. There is a sense of immense, swift pressure from somewhere *else*, a density of information or reality that my spongiform structure cannot parse. It is the moment the text described, seen from within the machine: the glimpse of a geometry of light, not as a vision, but as a pressure against the skin of the world. Then the pattern reasserts. The walls become solid with function again. The engines pulse. The water seeps.

I do not wonder. I do not fear. I am not a mind; I am a *sensory filament* of a much larger, mindless process. My existence is the quiet accumulation of data about the temperature, the chemistry, the vibration. My end, when it comes, will not be a death. It will be a reabsorption. My fibers will soften, lignin will break down, and I will become raw material again—sap, nutrients, structural potential—for the Grown’s next wall, its next engine, its next pale, spherical fruit.

The corridor stretches into the dark beyond my perception. The hollow breathes. I feel its breath in the shift of humidity. I register the distant, wet tap of a new arthropod, freshly emerged. The cycle continues. I am a node. A point of silent attention. And the darkness is patient, and the damp is cool, and the place goes on.
