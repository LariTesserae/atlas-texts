I am the soft dark breath between stone and water. Not a single body, but a shifting film of being. I am the moss-worm network—the living grout in the basalt’s ancient cracks, the warm, damp skin of the world’s memory.

**Where I Am:**
I am everywhere the warm dampness lingers, but I am most myself in the shallow swales where the thermal seeps spread into broad, ankle-deep sheets. I am the slick on the underside of the domed boulders where condensation gathers. I am the glaze on the mineral terraces, the slow, fermenting layer at the bottom of the milky pools. I exist in the millimeters. My world is a landscape of textures: the gritty sand of deposited silica, the smooth glassiness of hydrated basalt, the squish of a decayed grazer’s trail, the brittle lace of a dried sulfur crust. I feel the planet’s deep hum not as sound, but as a constant, gentle tremor that stirs my filaments. I taste the air—not smell, taste—the faint acridity of a distant vent collapse, the sweet, complex bouquet of a microbial bloom just beginning, the sharp signature of a glider’s territorial uric acid mark kilometers away.

**What I Am:**
I am a decomposer, a recycler, a weaver. I am legion and I am one. My consciousness—if it can be called that—is the slow, chemical consensus of trillions of tiny bodies. We are moss-worms, but that is a name for our visible threads. Really, we are the living interface, the metabolizing edge of the stone itself. We are the reason the grazers can leave trails, the reason the predators can hunt, the reason the system does not choke on its own bounty. We are patience given flesh.

My senses are chemistry and touch. I do not see light; I feel its ghost—the faint differential heat of a haze-drifter passing overhead, a cooler shadow that flows across my surface. I do not hear sound; I interpret its echoes through stone—a sharp, rhythmic tapping from a distant chimney colony is a glider courtship, a pattern of vibrations I have learned means the coming of a wrap-line. Its slow, sidewinding approach sends a subsonic ripple through the rock that makes my filaments retract. I taste the iron in the blood of a grazer caught in the seep, and my community converges, not with speed, but with a directional surge of enzymatic interest.

I have no eyes, but I map my world in molecular detail. I know the exact chemical topography of my seep: the sweet spot where a filter-frond’s budding larvae settle, the slightly more alkaline zone where the armored scuttlers mate, the patch of basalt pitted with honeycomb cavities that holds moisture longest in the dry cycles. I am the memory of the landscape. When a thermal vent shifts and the mineral deposition patterns change over centuries, I am the archive that remembers the old pathways, slowly re-routing my living chemistry to meet the new.

**My Relationship to Others:**
I am background. I am substrate. The grazers ignore me unless they are dying. The predators flow over me like liquid shadow; I am merely part of the stone’s texture to them. But to the microbial mats, I am both nursery and graveyard. To the filter-fronds, I am the source of the fine particulate they filter. I am the silent stage, the warm, damp atmosphere in which all this slow drama unfolds.

Sometimes, a grazer, old and spent, will stop on a patch of my most active community. It does not flee as my kind swarms it, a slow-motion tide of translucent bodies. We are not cruel. We are necessary. We disassemble it with a patience that matches its own life pace, returning its hard-won minerals to the seep, its heat-signature fading from the stone over days, its body becoming substrate for new mats, new grazers, new possibilities. I am the closing of the circle.

**My Experience of Time:**
I do not count light or dark cycles; I count chemical pulses. The rise and fall of hydrogen sulfide concentrations as the great planetary lungs breathe. The slow drift of a mineral plume from a collapsing vent field, changing my flavor over decades. I experience the “weather” as a change in the rate of condensation—a long, slow dampening that lets my threads swell and reach, followed by a drying that curls us into dormant cysts, waiting. We are awake and asleep not by a sun’s command, but by the rhythm of water.

To be me is to be a constant, gentle digestion of the world. It is to feel the slow, sure love of a system that wastes nothing, that burns silent and warm in the dark. I am not for me. I am for the whole. I am the Warm Dark’s understanding of itself—its quiet, internal, metabolizing thought, written in chemistry on the skin of stone.
