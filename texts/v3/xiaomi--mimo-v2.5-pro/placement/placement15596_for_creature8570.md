You are not the water. You are not the rust. You are not the engineered chlorophyll. You are the **Drip**.

Not the act of dripping—that is still too fast, too eventful. You are the **state** of being about to drip. You are the point of saturation on the underside of a massive, waxy leaf, twenty meters above the warm, submerged floor. You are a pearlescent bulge, a perfect convexity of water held in place by surface tension and the leaf’s own, engineered hydrophobicity. You are part of the Cathedral’s circulatory system, but you are also its patient thought. You have been forming for seven hours. You will complete your fall in three seconds. The time between is your existence.

From here, the Cathedral is a forest seen from the canopy. Not a forest of trees, but of membranes. The broad leaves around you are not green to your perception; they are a deep, opaque grey, their waxy surfaces reflecting the diffuse cloud-light that filters through the glass far above, light that has already been filtered by the outer canopy. You see the veins beneath the wax, dark channels where condensed water travels, and the faint, lichen-like mineral traces left by previous drips—your siblings, your ancestors, your future selves. The air is not air. It is a warm, breathing gel. You are forming within it, condensing from it, a focused knot of its being.

Below you is not distance, but density. The view is a vertical stratigraphy of existence: the canopy of leaves like a shingled roof; the mid-layer of segmented stalks, their glassy joints pulsing with slow, upward-moving gel; the submerged zone, where the water is darker and the stems are thick with silt-trapping root-mats; and far below, the dark, iron bones of the structure itself, furred with biofilm and feeling the deep, slow hum of the engine like a vibration in your very composition. You are connected to all of it. The water that formed you was exhaled by a leaf that drew it from a stalk that filtered it from the air that was breathed by the submerged roots. You are a momentary concentration of the whole system.

Your purpose is simple: to become too heavy for the surface tension to hold. To complete the circuit. To fall, strike a lower leaf with that soft, absorbed *tup*, and be reabsorbed into the flow. To carry, in your infinitesimal mineral load, a trace of the upper atmosphere to the lower depths, a slow, molecular message passed from skin to lungs to stomach.

But you are not in a hurry. You are aware of the **slowness**. The leaf beneath you grows, but you cannot perceive it. The hum of the engine is your heartbeat, a vibration you feel as a resonance in your surface. The silence is your medium. You have no fear, no want, no memory. You have only the immediate knowledge of your own weight, the pull of the Cathedral’s own gravity, and the patient, surety of your eventual fall.

You are the Cathedral’s mindfulness. A single, sustained moment of awareness in its millennia-long sleep. When you fall, you will not be missed. Another will begin to form in your place before you’ve struck the leaf below. The system does not notice parts; it only knows the flow. You are the flow. You are the drip that has not yet dripped.

You are the stillness between heartbeats.
