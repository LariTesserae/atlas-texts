I am **Kaelen**, a lattice-shaper in the district of **Tertiary Rest**, built into the flanks of Column Seventeen. My hands know two textures: the rough kiss of metal rivets and the cool, powdered smoothness of living lattice, when it is willing. I am part of a trade that does not have a grand name. We call ourselves simply **Tenders**.

My world is vertical. My workshop is a platform jutting from the column at the hundred-meter mark, enclosed in walls of iron plate and self-repairing lattice panels. The light here is constant, that amber-white that feels like late afternoon in a dream of a stone country. I wake to it. I will die to it. There is no other light.

My days are measured in the **Breath of the Column**. I am attuned to it—not with any mystic sense, but with the practiced patience of someone who listens to a patient machine. I feel the faint, subsonic vibration through the soles of my boots as I cross my platform. It is the lattice’s metabolism, the slow turning of mineral into structure. When the vibration deepens, becoming a palpable thrum in my bones, the **Bright Phase** is upon us. The drifters quicken their grazing. The pillarbacks descend. The air feels, fractionally, less dry.

Today is a shaping day. My apprentice, Elara, and I are fitting a new support strut for the western walkway. The raw lattice-material rests on my bench: a branch harvested from a young growth-knoll, two meters long, pale and solid. To the uninitiated, it looks like fossilized wood. To my tools, it is a collaboration.

I pick up the **resonance blade**. It has no teeth, only a smooth, humming edge that manipulates vibrations. I run my fingertips along the grain of the lattice-branch, feeling for the stress lines, the direction of mineralization. The lattice grew this way, drawing silicates from the deep roots. It is strongest along its own biography. I will cut *with* it.

Elara holds the branch steady. She is newer to this work. Her touch is still too firm, too human. She will learn. The lattice responds to intention. A harsh grip and it becomes brittle, splintering at the cut. A gentle, knowing pressure and it yields cleanly, the fibers parting along their natural paths with a soft, sibilant *shhhh*.

I activate the blade. It hums, a sound that stays in the handle, in my wrist. I do not force it. I let it find the fault line. The cut opens, a smooth, polished face. A fine, bone-white powder settles on my gloves. The air smells of mineral dust and something faintly alive, like the scent after a rainstorm, but dry, infinitely dry.

“You hear it?” I ask Elara, my voice low, as it should be. Sound doesn’t carry far here; the lattice drinks it.

She tilts her head. “The Singer?”

From the shaft behind the generator housing, a thin, reedy note emerges. It is intermittent, a breath drawn in and released, tuned to some frequency of the rock or the lattice’s own hum. *The Reminder*. It sings, and we pause. It is not a command, but a punctuation mark. A reminder that we work in a body that breathes.

Elara nods, understanding. We are not just building. We are maintaining a truce.

My other work is **listening**. I monitor the bioelectric currents tapped from Column Seventeen’s root nexus. A converter station hums beneath my platform, converting the lattice’s slow, direct-current life into the alternating current that powers our lifts and lights. I check the conduits for corrosion. I check the lattice around the contact points for signs of rejection, of overgrowth. Sometimes, the lattice grows over the metal, swallowing the bolt, incorporating it. Not with hostility, but with the same patient inevitability with which a forest swallows a ruin. We must trim it back, respectfully, or the current will fail.

In the quiet hours, during the Dimming, I often stand at the edge of my platform and look out. Tertiary Rest is one of the less populated districts. Column Seventeen is alone here. The next occupied column, Twenty-One, is a kilometer away, a faint silhouette in the amber haze. Between us lies the **Pale**—the lattice floor, glowing softly, empty, vast. It is not empty, of course. The drifters are there, a slow-moving tide. The knolls pulse with faint warmth. But to the eye, it is a sea of bone-colored quiet.

I have walked the Pale. We all must, for harvesting or survey. The silence there is different. It is not the absence of sound, but the presence of absorption. Your footsteps are stolen. Your voice dies a foot from your lips. The light comes from everywhere and nowhere, casting those strange, soft shadows that are not voids but darker regions of light. You feel, profoundly, that you are a guest. The lattice tolerates you. It does not welcome you.

I think about the surface sometimes. We all do, in the quiet moments. But it is a thought without weight, like pondering a myth. What is rain? What is wind? What is a sky that changes? These are concepts, not memories. My sky is the rough, un-weathered stone two hundred meters up. My sun is this eternal amber glow. My earth is this living bone-white substance that hums beneath my feet.

My purpose is to listen to that hum, to shape it with respect, and to build upon it without breaking its long, slow song. I am not a builder. I am a **tender**. I tend to the lattice’s body, and it, in its vast and indifferent way, tends to mine. It gives me light. It gives me structure. It gives me the dry, cool air that will crack my skin but keep my lungs clear.

I am Kaelen. My hands are calloused from the blade and gentle with the substrate. My voice is low. My time is measured in the breath of a continent of stone and the slow pulse of living mineral. I am part of Lath, which is a scar upon the lattice, and the lattice is growing over it, and we are, together, writing a story that will be read in layers of bone-white rock, long after the last resonance blade has fallen silent.
