# Mending

The rope goes through my hands.

I know the weight of it. I have known it — how long? There is a callus on my left index finger, third joint, where the cord crosses. It was not there before. Or it was always there. I do not know which, and the not-knowing is itself new, or is itself old, and I am doing the thing again, the thing the others do not do, which is to say I am distinguishing between *new* and *old* and the distinction is a crease in the air that does not need to be there.

The net is whole. It was whole when I began. I mend it.

This is what I do. This is what I have always done, which is to say: this is what I did on the first day I came here, which was the last day I came here, which is today. I came up the switchback stairs — I remember this, or I perform remembering, and I have not yet learned which — and I counted the steps. There were a hundred and forty-six. I have never counted them again. I do not need to. The number is in me the way the moisture is in the stone: settled, permanent, a relic of a moment when counting still meant something.

---

I will tell you — and I am telling no one, because I have stopped speaking, because speaking requires the belief that words travel, and I am not sure they do — I will tell you that I was not always here.

I think this. I believe it the way you believe in a color you once saw in a dream: fully, completely, with a certainty that has no evidence and needs none. There was a road. There was a descent. There was a body that moved through space at a pace that demanded hunger, sleep, the replacement of worn boots. There were — I want to say *others*, but the word is too large. There was a place I left. There was a reason I left it. I cannot tell you the reason. Not because it is hidden but because the reason, if I held it up to the light, would be the same as every reason: *because I was not yet here, and then I was, and the space between those two states was a door I walked through, and the door closed behind me, and I did not turn to see if it was still there.*

I do not think it is still there.

---

The work of my hands is this: I sit on the fourth terrace, on a low stone bench worn — no, not worn. The word keeps trying to arrive and I keep sending it back. The bench is not worn. The bench is. I sit on it, and in my lap there is a net, and in my hands there is cord, and the cord goes through the net in the pattern of mending, and the mending is perfect, and the net is whole, and I begin again.

Why do I do this when the others do this differently?

I have asked myself. The self that asks is not the self that answers. The self that answers does not use language, because language is — I will say this carefully — language is *temporal*. It has sequence. First this word, then that word. First subject, then verb, then object, and in that ordering there is the faintest suggestion of *movement*, of one thing leading to the next, and in Stillrise nothing leads to the next because there is no next. There is only *this*. This rope. This hand. This knot.

But I think in language still. I have not stopped. Where the others have let the muscle of narration atrophy, I have — I have *exercised* it. Quietly. In the privacy of my own skull, where no one can hear the words forming and unforming, I narrate. I describe. I explain to myself what is happening even as it happens, and the explaining creates a hairline crack in the present, and through the crack, the past leaks in, and through the past, the future — the ghost of the future, the *idea* that something might follow something else — settles on the surface of my mind like the condensation settles on the basalt.

Not quite dripping. Not quite drying.

I am the imperfection in the crystal.

---

Here is what I think the others would say about me if they used words the way I use words:

*He does not yet know how to stop.*

They would not mean it as judgment. Judgment requires the gap between what is and what should be, and there is no gap here — except in me, the small gap, the crease, the place where the stone does not quite fit its neighbor and so there is a channel, and through the channel, something flows. Not water. Something thinner. Attention, maybe. The kind of attention that is not presence but *watching* — the difference between being the mountain and standing on the mountain and looking out.

I sit on the fourth terrace and I mend the net, and I watch myself mend the net, and the watching is what makes me different. The woman beside me mends a stone wall. She does not watch herself mend it. She *is* the mending. Her hands and the stones and the motion between them are one thing, one event, one moment that has no interior. My moment has an interior. It has a small dark room inside it, and in the room there is a voice, and the voice is mine, and it is saying:

*You are mending the net. The net does not need mending. You are doing this because —*

Because.

The word hangs. It will not complete itself. In Stillrise, *because* has no object, no consequence, no answer waiting on the other side. Causation is a story we tell in the places where time still moves, and here time does not move, and so the *because* hangs in the air of my mind like a hawk that has stopped circling and simply hovers, motionless, over a landscape that does not change.

---

But something is different today.

I will not say *changing*. I will not. But I am noticing — and the noticing is itself the thing I am noticing, the recursion, the mirror facing the mirror — that the air is thinner. That the damp, which is always here, which has always been here, which is the first and last thing about Stillrise, the damp is — less? No. The damp is the same. But the quality of the air around the damp has shifted by some increment so small that no instrument could detect it and yet my body knows, the way the goats on the upper ledges know, the way the hawks that are not circling today know, the way the lichen knows, the way the stone knows.

The clouds are moving faster.

I have watched the clouds through — through how many mornings? Through all of them. Through the only one, the always-one, the morning that is every morning and no morning. And the clouds have always moved at a particular pace: low, fast, purposeful, passing through the upper terraces and leaving glistening behind. Today they are faster. Not much. Not enough to alarm. But faster, and in the faster there is the faintest suggestion of something I once knew — something about *urgency*, about *movement*, about a world where things are not balanced but tipped, tilted, falling forward through time toward a destination that does not exist.

I do not know what it means. I do not know if it means.

---

The others have not noticed. Or they have noticed and it has not registered as different, because different requires the comparison between this moment and the last, and they do not make that comparison, and so the clouds move faster and they are still the clouds, and the air is thinner and it is still the air, and nothing has changed because nothing can change because they are not watching for change.

I am watching.

This is my flaw. This is my gift. This is the channel cut through the stone of me where the water — where the *time* — still flows, slow and dark and invisible, wearing its groove deeper with every repetition, every knot, every word I form in the quiet of my skull.

I am mending the net. My hands know the pattern. The cord slides through, tightens, holds. The net is whole. The net has always been whole. And yet I mend it, because the mending is not for the net, the mending is for me — it is the only thing I know how to do that is the same as what they do, the only moment when my hands and the task and the present collapse into one thing, one motion, one held breath.

For the length of a knot — a single knot, tied and pulled tight — I am here. Fully here. The voice in my skull goes quiet. The narration stops. The gap between watching and being closes, and I am not the imperfection in the crystal but the crystal itself, and the light passes through me without refracting, and there is no before or after or because, there is only the cord and the pull and the hold.

And then the knot is tied, and I reach for the next loop, and the voice returns:

*You are mending the net. The net does not need mending. You are doing this because —*

Because I cannot stop. Because the stopping would be the arriving, and the arriving would be the end of the only thing that makes me still me, which is the reaching — the reaching for the next knot, the next word, the next thought, the next distinction between this and that, here and there, now and then. If I stopped reaching, I would sink into the equilibrium like a stone into water, and the ripples would close over me, and there would be no trace, no channel, no imperfection, no voice.

I would be them.

And I am afraid — and the fear is itself the proof that I am not yet them, because they do not have fear, because fear requires the possibility of loss, and they have nothing to lose because they have already arrived at the place where loss and gain mean the same thing, which is nothing — I am afraid that the arriving would not be freedom but absence. That the stillness I admire in them, the luminous total presence, would in me be only silence. That the voice would not go quiet but go *out*, like a candle in a room with no windows, and the darkness that followed would not be peace but nothing, and I would not know the difference because I would not be there to know.

So I mend the net. I tie the knots. I speak in my skull. I watch the clouds move faster and I do not say *change* but I think it, and the thinking is the crack, and the crack is the channel, and the channel is the only thing between me and the stillness that would swallow me whole.

---

I wonder sometimes — and the wondering is the most human thing about me — whether they see the crack. Whether the woman on the fourth terrace, placing her stones with that total absorption, ever looks up and sees me sitting here with my net and my cord and my too-busy eyes and thinks: *there. That one. He has not arrived yet.*

I think she does. I think she looks at me with those calm dark eyes and sees the channel in me, the place where the time still flows, and she sees it the way she sees the condensation in the stone channels: as something that is, as something that belongs, as something that will either pool or evaporate but is not, in either case, her concern.

She returns to her stones.

I return to my knots.

And the clouds move faster above us, and the air thins by an increment that only I can feel, and somewhere on the mountain — on the sharp young rock that should be eroding and is not, in the lichen fields that should be spreading and are not, in the space between the terraces where the wind has shifted and the goats have gone still and the hawks have stopped circling and are watching, all of them watching, with the same patience that is not patience but *being* —

Something is beginning.

Or something is ending.

Or something has always been there and I am only now seeing it because I am the only one who still looks.

I tie the knot. I pull it tight. The net holds.

I begin again.
