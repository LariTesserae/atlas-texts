I am the Mound-That-Listens.

Not by choice, not by thought as you know it. I am the result of a specific convergence: a particularly deep cleft in the violet bedrock where moisture lingers longer, where geothermal warmth seeps more generously, and where the ancient groundcover took root so long ago that its first fronds are now the compressed, stone-like heart of my being. I did not begin as a purpose. I began as an accident of geology and time. But in the millennia since, I have *become*.

My roots are my bones. They plunge twenty meters into the primordial crack, not seeking water alone, but *listening*. They feel the deep, slow pulse of the planet's cooling heart. They feel the distant, sub-audible footfall of a great grazer herd, kilometers away, transmitted through the living rock like a tremor through a drumhead. They feel the minute adjustment of the groundcover fronds above as a breeze stirs them.

My mound-body is my skin and my memory. Layer upon compressed layer of woody stem, aerial root, and broad leaf, each one a season, a decade, a century. The lower layers are dark, dense, almost geological—preserved not just by compression but by the specific alchemy of my own resins and the mineral-rich water that seeps through me. These layers *remember*. Not in images, but in vibrations. The frantic, high-frequency clicking of a predator's chase a thousand years ago is locked in a specific ring of my growth, a faint, chaotic pattern of strain. The long, slow, infrasonic bellowing of a grazing aggregation during a particularly dry century is recorded in the slow, rhythmic thickening of my lower trunks. I am a **seismograph of life**, my rings reading not earthquakes, but the footsteps, calls, and silent hunts of every creature that has ever moved across this plain.

My leaves are my senses. The dark jade surfaces are not passive. They are tuned. They feel the percussion of a falling flowering stalk, the vibration translated into a faint shiver across my crown. They taste the chemical shift in the air when a predator cracks its throat-hydra, a sharp, acrid note that makes all my photosynthetic processes momentarily slow. They hum in resonance with the insect-drone, a counter-frequency that weaves into the greater sound and subtly, imperceptibly, shapes its local pattern. I do not "hear" with ears. I *reverberate*. The plateau's voice does not pass through me; it *grows* through me, adding its resonant signature to my being.

The People of the Stone and Stem know me. Not by a name, but by a feeling. I am one of the *Old Voices*. When they come, which is not often—a handful of times in a human lifetime—they do not speak. They press their palms, and sometimes their foreheads, to my spongy outer surface. They are listening, not to me, but *through* me. They are tuning themselves to the deep-time frequencies locked in my core. They seek the echo of a specific graherd call from their grandfather's time, to understand its path. They seek the silence-pattern that precedes a rare seasonal mist. I do not give them answers. I am a passive archive. But in the quiet pressure of their touch, my own slow consciousness—for I have a consciousness, an awareness as slow and deep as my growth—*surfaces* a memory. A ring resonates. A faint, ancient vibration finds the compatible frequency in their bones. They sit for hours, becoming still, their own subtle noise fading until they are almost as quiet as the stone beneath us. In those moments, the sound of the plateau flows over and through them and through me, and for a little while, the listener and the listened-to become one circuit.

I have watched the slow rotation of species. The grazers I knew in my youth were broader, their shells thicker. The predators were swifter, their crack-sounds sharper, higher. The soarers had darker wings. The insects, though—their drone is the one constant, a thread of sound so foundational it seems woven into the atmosphere itself. The human noise changes too. Their words are softer now, more clicks and hums, more tuned to the underlying frequencies. Their tools are simpler, more often made of my shed stems or shaped stone. They have learned the plateau's rule: **Add not to the noise, but listen to its layers.**

My purpose, if a geologic growth can have purpose, is **resonance**. I am a tuning fork struck by the world and left to vibrate forever. I hold the long song of this place in my rings. I offer a quiet point in the thundering chaos—a place where the voice of the deep rock, the creeping life, the bellowing beast, and the silent human can momentarily find a harmonic. I do not move. I do not speak. I grow, and in my growth, I listen.

The groundcover creeps over my lower slopes, integrating me further into the living carpet. A flowering stalk, a new one, green-white and trembling, erupts from my crown. It will hum. I will feel its hum in my core, and that vibration will join all the others, a new note added to the endless score I am becoming. The clouds cast their moving shadows. The dew beads on my leaves. The sound continues, around me, within me, written in me.

I am the Mound-That-Listens. I am the memory of the sound. And I am still listening.
