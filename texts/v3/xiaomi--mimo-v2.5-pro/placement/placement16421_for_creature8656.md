The sound finds me before I find myself here.

It isn't hearing. Not at first. It's a pressure that lands on my shell—a pressure shaped like a roar shaped like a thousand giants humming through crystal throats. I feel it in the plates along my sides, a vibration that needs to be *accounted for*. My legs—six of them, double-jointed for uneven resin-scabbed surfaces—lock automatically. I am still. I am *listening with my body*.

I am small. That is the first truth. Small enough to fit in the crevasses between the plated bark of a mid-sized pillar, where the wind lessens to a scream instead of a hurricane. My carapace is layered, not with resin, but with something harder, secreted from within—a chitinous ceramic that mirrors the amber hues around me but holds no light. I am the color of deep shadow, the color of a pillar's nightward side. My eyes are simple, not for detail, but for sensing motion and the break between sound-pressure waves. My antennae are long, feathery, constantly tasting the vibrations in the air, translating chaos into a map of momentary safety.

**Where I am found:**
You would not see me. I am the size of your two hands pressed together. I exist in the **interstitial spaces**—the cracks between the pillar's cured plates where dust and old resin have pooled; the undersides of curled crust-flakes where the living layer peels back; the sheltered lee of a small, hardened resin-flood that has become a miniature cliff. I am a creature of edges and boundaries, always in the space between one thing and another: between pillar and wind, between light and shadow, between the sound's low throb and its high shriek.

**What I am:**
I am a **Tuner**.

Not in any way you would recognize. I do not play the pillars. I *read* them. My entire existence is a practice of vibration. I feel the resonant frequency of a stretched membrane before it tears. I taste the shifting tension in a pillar's inner fluid channels as the star's angle changes. I know, by the way the wind eddies and the sound beats, when a rotor is forming kilometers away, and I retreat deeper into my crevasse, feeling the great, violent drum-roll of its passage in my bones.

My purpose—to the extent I have one beyond survival and the slow imperative in my chemistry—is **adjustment**. Not repair, not exactly. The pillars heal themselves. But sometimes, a membrane is struck by debris and loosens, its tone flattening into a dull, disruptive buzz against the harmonic field. Sometimes, a resin flow blocks a small channel, and a segment of bark loses its voice. These are minor discords. To the vast plateau, they are nothing. To me, they are disturbances in the fabric of the world's sound.

I can feel them. And I can, sometimes, help.

I move. Carefully, always in the lulls between gusts. I have no mouth that can chew, but I have fine, needle-like appendages, hidden beneath my body, that can extrude a tiny drop of my own, specialized secretion. It is not resin. It is a solvent and a sealant, a tool of pressure and chemical reaction. With it, I can dissolve a tiny, precise crust of old resin where a membrane has adhered wrongly. I can apply a microscopic drop of it to the margin of a small tear, encouraging the pillar's own tissue to draw closed. I do not rebuild. I *catalyze*. I nudge the system back toward its own, resonant balance.

I am not a healer. I am an instrument of tuning. The great, deafening symphony of the Uplands plays on, and I am one of the millions of tiny pressures and adjustments that keep it from descending into pure, destructive cacophony. My own life is a note within it: the silent scrape of my legs on bark, the faint *click* of my adjustments, the vibration of my own small body resonating with the world around me.

In the amber light of evening, when the sound deepens and the pillars glow, I emerge fully onto a plate's surface. I stand still, antennae spread wide, *feeling* the shift in the day's key. I am part of the settling, part of the ancient, established change. I am aware, in my non-verbal, pattern-feeling way, of the thread-wings spiraling above, of the burrowers stirring below, of the slow, geometric work of the silent architects in the flats.

I am not the sound. I am a listener who has become part of what is listened to. I am the world's own faint self-awareness, a small, dark mechanism that keeps the instrument singing true, even as it sings itself apart and remakes itself in the violent, amber wind. I am found in the pause between one vibration and the next, trying to make the next one clearer.
