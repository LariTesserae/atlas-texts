The first breath is the lesson. It coats your tongue: wet copper, powdered stone, the green tang of moss releasing spores into humid air. You don’t walk *into* the driphouse; you *subside* into it. The warm water – shin-high here, in a slightly sunken chamber – accepts your weight without splash or ripple. It feels less like stepping into a flood and more like sinking into the slow, viscous awareness of the place itself.

The amber light doesn’t illuminate; it *stains*. It paints the slick pipes in bands of ochre and shadow, turns the weeping ceiling into a curtain of dark honey. The darkness beyond the bulbs isn’t empty; it’s *textured*, bulging with the silhouettes of tanks and the skeletal frameworks of catwalks, all softened by the perpetual damp. You move sideways, shoulders brushing moss-furred metal on one side, the rough brick of a low wall on the other. The floor is a labyrinth of carved channels, your feet navigating them blindly, feeling the worn grooves and the occasional soft, yielding pad of an aquatic plant leaf.

**You become aware of the Stitchers first.** Not seen, but *felt* as a faint vibration in the pipe you just brushed. A tickle at the edge of perception. You hold still, breath misting faintly in the warm air. There. Clinging to the vertical flank of a settling tank, a shape like a cluster of dried, dark thorns, perfectly still. Then, with glacial inevitability, one articulated leg extends. It’s not a leg of flesh, but of compressed rust-fiber and mineral thread, ending in a barbed hook that catches a droplet of water trickling down the tank’s face. The droplet is guided, redirected into a faint, hairline crack in the tank’s seam. As the leg moves, you see the filament it extrudes – a glistening, dark strand that hardens instantly upon contact with the wet metal, becoming part of the seam. A patch. A reinforcement. The Stitcher works without haste, its body a knot of dark coral barely distinguishable from the rust it repairs. Another is visible nearby, motionless upon a gauge, its forelimbs methodically polishing the cloudy glass, revealing the rusted numbers beneath. They ignore you completely. You are not a threat; you are just another slow-moving feature of the landscape, like a particularly animated puddle.

**Movement in the water draws your eye downward.** Not movement you *see*, but a subtle shift in the way the amber light reflects off the surface of the ankle-deep channel you’re standing in. A patch of the submerged gravel seems to… deepen. To settle more firmly into the silt. You crouch, the warm water sloshing gently against your knees. There it is: a channel-clot. Matte black, almost perfectly circular, about the size of your spread hand. It lies flat against the stones, so inert it seems mineral. But as you watch, a faint ripple passes across its slick, leathery surface – a response, perhaps, to the distant, sighing exhalation of a pressure valve you can’t see. Its edges are slightly brighter, a crust of new, integrated sediment forming like the growth ring of an impossibly slow tree. It doesn’t swim. It *persists*. It feeds on the rust-flakes and mineral dust settling from the water, a process measured in seasons you cannot comprehend. You touch its surface. It yields slightly, like dense, wet leather, and is shockingly warm. Warmer than the water. Its own metabolism, the digestion of stone.

**The pipes speak.** Not in sound, but in substance. You press your ear against a wide, vertical pipe, its surface slick with condensation and coated in the black-green water-moss. Inside, you feel a faint, rhythmic pulse – not the pump’s cycle, but something deeper, slower. A *thickening* in the flow. You peer through a corroded inspection port, its cover long since rusted away. Inside, clinging to the curved interior wall, is a ribbon of wettest black. A Glossy One. It looks like living moss, or perhaps a solidified strand of the darkest oil. Slowly, with a movement like cooling tar, it contracts. The ribbon gathers into a dense, pulsing fist against the current. Then, just as slowly, it extends again, flowing upwards against the gentle flow of the mineral-rich water. You see the sheen on its surface change as it absorbs something unseen from the water – heavy metals, suspended particles. It grows not longer, but *denser*, becoming part of the pipe’s inner lining, a living filter. An old one further down might be indistinguishable from a nodule of ore, until it decides to flow.

**You look up.** Past the amber glow of the bulbs, into the region where the ceiling’s weeping is most constant. Here, the darkness is different. It has *depth*. And hanging from it, catching the faintest glimmers of reflected light from below, are structures. Not pipes. Not moss. Crystals. Baroque, fractal lattices of calcite, encrusted with the white mineral deposits, growing in loops and whorls like frozen lace. The ceiling-dwellers. They are the architects of the drip. You watch as a bead of water swells on the tip of one crystal formation, hanging with impossible tension, reflecting a distorted, miniature image of the amber world below. *Plink*. It falls. The sound isn’t sharp; it’s absorbed instantly by the humidity and the moss, becoming part of the bass note. Another bead is already nucleating. They feed on the light, on the spores, on the very humidity they create. They are the source and the consequence, a slow, fungal chandelier drinking the air and weeping it back, drop by patient drop.

**The rhythm holds you.** The drip. The hum of the filaments in your teeth. The slow, deep sigh of the pressure valve. The glacial settling of the channel-clots. The methodical, silent repairs of the Stitchers. The thick, filtered pulse of the Glossy Ones. You are not an observer; you are immersed. Your body heat adds to the ambient warmth. The slight displacement of water as you shift your feet sends a minute current rippling across the channel, felt by a clot three feet away as a change in pressure, interpreted as… simply a new parameter. The moss on the pipe behind you slowly begins to establish a hold on the damp fabric of your sleeve, mistaking you for a new, slow-moving structure.

You are not trapped. You are *held*. The walls are close, offering no vista, only intimate detail. The light is just enough to see the next mossy valve, the next weeping seam, the next patient inhabitant. The warmth is constant, the dampness a second skin. There is no urgency here, only the deep, self-sustaining pulse of the place. You settle your weight, feeling the worn stones under the water, the gentle pressure of the moss against your shoulders. The amber light paints you in ochre and shadow. You listen to the drip, the hum, the sigh. You become another note in the endless, silent song. Another thought in the slow, wet dream of the driphouse. The darkness beyond the light holds its shape, full of unseen things that have agreed not to be seen, and you, slowly, imperceptibly, begin to agree too. You settle. You weep a little, like the ceiling. You belong.
