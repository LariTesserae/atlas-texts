I am a **Moss-Tender** on the Eastern Spire of Kathai. My name, if it matters — and names here are often more descriptive than personal — is **Lumis**, for the glow I cultivate. My dwelling is a shallow cave scooped from the base of a leaning tree, its entrance curtained by strips of woven bark-fabric. The back wall is living rock, perpetually damp, beaded with condensation. It is warm, always warm.

**Where I find myself:** I exist on a vertical plane. My world is measured in handholds, in the angle of a leaning trunk, in the dampness of a moss-bed. I move mostly up and down, not far sideways. My territory spans perhaps two hundred meters of cliff-face, from the shelf where the coral-algae mats pulse faintly at low tide, up through the zone of the great ferns, to the very edge of the canopy where the leaning trees trail their aerial roots into the mist. This is my *vertical field*. Beyond it, in any direction, is either the sheer drop to the water or the impenetrable tangle of another family's moss-beds. The settlement, such as it is, is a maze of rope-bridges and platforms clinging to this cliff, smoke-stained and soft-edged by humidity. The air smells of wet stone, chlorophyll, fermentation, and always, the faint, sweet-sulfur breath of the Spire itself.

**What I am:** I am a tender of light. In a world of perpetual twilight under the cloud-lid, my moss is life. I cultivate several varieties in hanging bowls of lashed wood, in shallow niches carved into the rock, in the soft, rotting hearts of fallen trees. There is the *Steady-Glow*, a pale green-gold that illuminates the communal hearths and pathways all night. There is the *Pulse-Moss*, which throbs with a slow, soft light, used in ritual spaces and for signaling in fog. And there is the *Deep-Green*, a hardier, slower-growing variety that glows for years, used for personal lamps and for marking safe paths through the rot-forest.

My days are cycles of moisture, shade, and patience. I mist them with collected rainwater stored in gourds. I feed them slivers of fungal flour mixed with water. I shield them from the rare, burning shafts of direct sunlight that punch through the clouds — moments of terrifying intensity in our dim world. I listen to their soft, continuous hum of growth, a sound felt more than heard. I harvest their outer layers when they bloom, carefully splitting them to propagate new starts, giving portions to the Rope-Wrights for their magical, faintly luminous safety-lines, and to the home-sharers to light their caves.

My knowledge is intimate. I know the particular drip-pattern from the overhang above my Steady-Glow bed that indicates the humidity is perfect. I recognize the faint yellow tinge that means a particular mineral in the seep-water is high this month, requiring adjustment of the feed slurry. I know which geckos are gentle grazers (useful) and which are voracious (to be shooed away). My tools are simple: a sharp obsidian flake for harvesting, a soft pad of absorbent moss for cleaning, a ceramic bowl for mixing feed.

**The texture of being here:**
*   **Sound:** The constant, slow patter of condensation dripping from leaves and moss. The low, chest-felt rumble of the surf far below. The occasional, sharp *crack* of thermal stress in the Spire's heart — a sound that makes everyone pause, heads tilted, before resuming their tasks. The two-note *Kee-rah!* of the diving birds, high and eerie in the mist. The soft *scritch* of gecko feet on stone.
*   **Sight:** A world of verticals and gloom. The interplay of deep shadow and sudden, soft pools of bioluminescent green or gold. The constant motion of mist, drifting, thickening, thinning, revealing a sliver of grey sea far below before swallowing it again. The dark, wet gleam of basalt. The startling, almost violent, red of a fungal bloom erupting overnight on a dead trunk. The pale, sinewy shapes of fellow Spire-dwellers moving like shadows along the cliff-face.
*   **Touch:** Everything is damp. The air itself clings. My skin is always cool with moisture. The moss I tend is springy, cool, and faintly vibrating with life. The rock is rough, sharp-edged, warm from the Spire's geothermal heart. The rope-bridges sway with a comforting, familiar give. The fungal flatbread is dense and slightly chewy.
*   **Taste:** The faint metallic tang in the air. The earthy, slightly sweet flavor of the flatbread. The briny, mineral-rich broth of boiled fern-fronds and sea-slug strips. The clean, surprising sweetness of the pulpy interior of a young fern-stalk, a rare treat.

**My understanding of this place:**
I am not an islander. I am a *surface-dweller*. My existence is perched on the thin, wet skin of a world divided between the immense, silent mystery below and the vast, unknowable grey above. The dead go down, into the water. The living are held up, to the clouds. The present is this narrow ledge, this vertical field, this bowl of glowing moss in my hands.

The Spire breathes. I feel its slow exhalations as warm drafts up the cliff-face. I hear its groans. I know it is growing, pushing upward against the weight of the sea that also rises, a slow duel measured in generations, not years. We are not fighting the sea; we are living in the balance between its press and the rock's rise. Our knowledge, our work, our lives are the sediment accumulating in that narrow, precarious space. We persist. We tend the light. We listen to the deep. We are part of the Spire's damp, determined breath.
