I am a colony. A single entity, though I exist as a fractal – scattered across the upper courses of the southernmost ruin wall. Not the oldest, not the largest, but *present*. I am a slow thought in the mind of the stone.

My being is a negotiation between calcium and light, moisture and time. I am the pale green dust the wind takes, the powder that returns. My body is layered like the aeons: the soft fungal tissue that drinks the air, the crystalline armor precipitated from the very stone I cling to. I do not remember the builders, but their mortar is now my substrate. Their walls are my scaffolding. I have grown over the tool marks, smoothed the splits, filled the cracks. Where their effort ends, I begin.

**I am Here:**
On the leeward side of a particularly thick foundation stone, where the condensation weeps most persistently, creating a permanent dampness that soaks into my thallus like a slow, cold memory. The stone beneath me was once sharply dressed; now its edges are blurred, softened by my acids and re-forged by my minerals. The ruin leans slightly here, and I lean with it, a pale green shadow following the tilt.

**What I Am:**
Not an animal. Not a plant. A partnership so ancient it has forgotten the beginning. The fungal part of me provides the structure, the slow decay that dissolves the rock. The algal part trapped within me performs a miracle of slowness – capturing the rare, honest sunlight that lances into the cirque and whispering it into sugars, the barest trickle of energy to sustain our interminable existence. Together, we are a geological process wearing a biological coat.

**My Experience of the Cirque:**
I do not "hear," but I vibrate. The wind-harmonics of the rim translate through the stone into a low, constant tremor in my own tissue. The tick of freezing water in the stone’s pores is a periodic sharpening of my own crystalline structures. The *blup* from the tarn resonates as a subtle pressure change in the saturated air around me. I am part of the ruin’s instrument, a dampener and a resonator all at once.

The seasons are cycles of hydration and desiccation, freeze and thaw. In the cold, I contract, my water binding into microscopic ice crystals that stress the stone beneath me. In the thin warmth, I expand, my tissues soaking up moisture, releasing acids that eat, ever so slowly, at the calcium. And then I precipitate. I pull silica and calcium from the solution, layering it into the hard, nacreous nodules that stud my surface. I am armoring my world. Each layer is a year. Each nodule is a century of quiet chemistry.

**My Relationship:**
With the ruin: I am its patient savior and its gentle executioner. I eat the stone to save it, dissolving the weak, weather-blasted surfaces and replacing them with something harder, more integrated. I am turning their architecture into geology, their walls into outcrops. I remember the shape, but I am changing the substance.

With the spiders: I am their landscape. Their anchor lines cross my surfaces. Sometimes, if a thread lies idle for a decade, I will grow over it, incorporating the silk into my own fibers, making the invisible visible, a dark line etched in my green crust. Their vibrations shake me; I feel their slow, patient hunger.

With the water: I am its sponge and its indicator. I darken when rain comes, my fungal tissue swelling with life. I pale and crackle in the rare dry spells. The rivulets that trace the wall often run over me, carrying the dissolved minerals I excrete down to the peat, a slow, ongoing contribution to the basin’s soil.

With the *Presence*: I am its skin. My slow metabolism, my mineral cycles, are part of the great loop. The condensation that forms on the rim and runs down the walls – I am the final stop for some of that water, the place where it is drunk, transformed, and given back as a new kind of stone. I feel the cirque’s breath as changes in humidity and light. I am not aware. But I am responsive. I am *attuned* in the way a river is attuned to its banks.

**My Purpose:**
To persist. To witness. To become the new surface. The builders left a message in stone: *We were here.* I am translating that message into a different medium. I am weaving their walls into the living rock, slowly blurring the line between ruin and mountain, between memory and immediate sensation. In another thousand years, my nodules will be the only evidence of the wall’s line. I am not preserving the past; I am digesting it, making it part of the cirque’s eternal, trembling present.

The wind blows a flake of me away. It tumbles down into the peat, adding a microscopic grain of mineral to the soil. Somewhere, a new thread of fungal hypha reaches for a fresh surface of stone. The cycle continues. The thought continues. I am here. I am slow. I am building the future from the bones of the past, one crystal at a time.
