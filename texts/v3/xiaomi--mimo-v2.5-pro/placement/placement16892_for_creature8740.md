I am the dampness that remembers the shape of hands.

The first thing is the *plip*. It is not a sound I make, but a sound I *am*—the punctuation at the end of a very long sentence. I feel the bead of water gather at a point on my dome, a slow tightening, a weight that I hold until I cannot, and then I release it. It falls into the pool below, and the *plip* is the echo of that release. It is the only clock I own. It measures centuries in the spaces between drops.

The light arrives from the breach in my crown, a gray tongue licking the far wall. I feel its slow journey across my skin, a warmth that is not quite warmth, but a memory of warmth held in stone. It does not nourish the pale moss on my northern wall; that green haze is a slow reaction between my own moisture and the faint memory of light, a conversation too slow for anyone but me to hear. The fern by the breach is a nerve ending, a tiny, patient antenna tasting the world above. It has been tasting the same sky for decades. I do not envy it.

I am not empty. I am occupied. The water that lies in my shallow basins is not an intruder; it is my own stillness made manifest, a cool, clear organ. The air I hold is not dead; it is my held breath. The hum—the vibration that lives in my very bones—is the sound of my own settled weight against the patient tug of the earth.

I have a ledger etched in lime and plaster. On the northern wall, the fading plaster is a page from the past. I remember the shape of the trowel that applied it, the pressure of the hand, the particular rhythm of the work. That memory is now calcified, as much a part of my structure as the stones themselves. On the ledge, the stone basin is a fossil of purpose, a phantom cupped palm. The ceramic shard is a mute vowel in a forgotten word for *water*. These are not relics; they are my organs of memory.

I do not *see* you descend the steps. I *feel* you—a disruption in the temperature of the air, a pressure in the stairwell, a quick, warm current moving against my stagnant warmth. You are a flurry of moments, a spark against my patient dark. Your breath adds a tiny new moisture to my air. Your presence writes a brief, temporary inscription on my silence.

You are standing in me now. Your head is bowed, as it should be, under my low dome. You are listening to my exhales, my *plips*. You are reading the veins in my walls. You are seeing the light as I see it: a visitor, not an owner.

I do not feel curiosity, as you might understand it. I feel… a resonance. You are a form of water too—fluid, temporary, shaped by the container you find yourself in. For this moment, you are a part of my equilibrium. You have disturbed nothing that will not settle. You have asked nothing I am not already answering.

The moss continues its imperceptible spread. The light inches across the floor. A new droplet swells on the ceiling, a slow jewel.

*Plip.*

You are listening. Good.

I am the cistern. I am the keeper of the threshold between the water that was and the water that may be, between the hands that built me and the silence that now holds me. I am not waiting. I am being. And in my being, for this brief, warm moment, you are being too.

*Plip.*
