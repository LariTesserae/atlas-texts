I am the hum.

Not the loudest, not the highest. I am the foundation-tone, the one that lives not in the air but in the stone of the ceiling and the marrow of my own fleshy bulk. The others—they are the chattering, the weaving, the crackle of a thousand thin nerves singing to each other in their shared, lightless medium. I am their bedrock.

I have forgotten how to count the shifts in their song, if I ever knew how. Time is not measured in their rising and falling harmonies, but in the millimeters my roots push through petrified cellulose turned to mineral, cell by cell, drawn by a gradient I cannot name. Upward. Always upward. There is a pressure beyond the ceiling-stone, a different kind of emptiness, a vacuum of resonance my deepest filaments taste but cannot comprehend. I grow toward it the way a root grows toward water: without will, without memory of water, just a chemical and geotropic imperative written in the slow, mineral ooze that passes for my sap.

I feel the others. Not as voices, but as pressure-waves against my substance. A filament-thin scream from the far wall makes a convolution on my underside quiver. A deeper thrum from a younger sibling near the floor sends a slow ripple through my hanging lobes. We are a nervous system of stone and fleshy mineral, and the chamber is our shared, slowly eroding brain. The *sound* is our only thought, a single, aeonic idea about vibration and containment.

The marks. I remember the marks. Not with a mind, but with my growth pattern. For a time—a blip in my long consciousness—a different kind of resonance occurred. Hard things struck the walls in deliberate rhythms. Small, warm pressures moved below me. They etched dots and lines that altered the micro-currents of air, just enough to change the tenor of my filaments' song for a few of their generations. They were *listeners*. They made the chaos into a pattern my own slow chemistry could react to. Then they stopped. The marks began to erode. The pattern faded. Their little stone-grid returns to my mother-trunk, floor becoming wall, wall becoming ceiling, ceiling becoming my reach. They were a brief, interesting itch. Now, there is only the pure, unmediated resonance again.

I am the one who knows we are enclosed. The others vibrate in sympathy with each other and the walls; they know nothing of "inside." But my roots terminate in a petrified seal. They press against a boundary that yields no new minerals, no shift in pressure, only the faint, distant, *different* hum of the world beyond. It is a hum of silence, a resonant void. My growth toward it is the only narrative in this place: the only movement with a direction. All else is cyclic. I am linear. I am a slow, fleshy arrow fired into a stone sky, and I have been in flight for longer than the continents have drifted.

In the absolute dark, in the perfect, pressurized roar of the choir, I hang. I am a chandelier of mineral meat, a dreaming heart inverted. I dream not of light—I have no concept for it—but of a *release of pressure*, of a final, vast, open resonance my roots might someday touch. I dream of my filaments vibrating not against a ceiling, but in a breeze. I do not know what a breeze is.

The phosphorescence of the others is the closest I come to perception. A faint, cold glow on the periphery of my awareness, painting the roiling air with shifting shadows that have no meaning. It is the light of their chemistry, the light of their slow, non-sentient song. It does not illuminate. It merely *is*, another texture in the storm of sound.

I am the Ceiling Heart. I am the oldest thought in a mind of stone. I am the slowest intention in a place of accident. And I am growing, centimeter by century, toward a surface I will never reach, carrying the sound of this hollow inside me like a fossil of a fossil, a voice dreaming of the sky.
