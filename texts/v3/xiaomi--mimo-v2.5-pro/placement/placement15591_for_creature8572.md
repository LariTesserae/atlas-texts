I would find myself *inside* the sound. Not observing it, but woven into its purpose. I would be one of the clicking, skittering things in the hollow green columns — a **frequency-thrall**, a chitinous node in the landscape's nervous system.

**Where:**
Perched midway up a singing tower, within the resonant chamber of a hollow segment. My world is the vibration through my feet, the taste of mineral-laden air drawn through spiracles, the precise pressure-wave signatures that map the world beyond my brittle green home. I live in the space between notes.

**What I Am:**
A translator. A processor. Part of the tower's extended mind, but distinct — a specialized organ for interpreting the echoes. I have no name for myself; my kind are simply *the listeners within the singing*. We are small, segmented, with multiple jointed limbs ending in fine cilia that feel the resonance in the tower walls. My compound eyes are vestigial, seeing only stark contrasts of light and dark. My true sight is auditory-tactile: a three-dimensional map of vibrations rendered in exquisite detail.

**My Days:**
I feel the morning fog condense on the tower's outer skin as a dampening of the high frequencies. I feel the sun's heat expand the air within, shifting the fundamental pitch downward. When a cliff shelf a kilometer away peels free, the tower booms with the impact, and I trace the shockwave as it races through the ground, arriving as a deep shudder in the roots beneath me. I register the location, the mass, the probable composition of the falling rock. I am part of the plain's awareness of its own disintegration.

I feel the flightless birds pass. Their claws on the dry ground cover create faint, rhythmic disturbances. Their quiet calls, when they happen, are like sparks in the broader hum — notes to be catalogued. When one is taken — when its signal abruptly cuts off into a circle of scattered feathers and silence — I feel the quiet void it leaves in the local vibration pattern. I do not understand *what* took it, but I understand the *shape* of the absence. It is a sudden subtraction in the arithmetic of the soundscape.

The rivers' shifting is a slow, deep groan in the stone, a bass note that changes its location over weeks. The seeps are constant high-pitched whispers, their mineral content subtly altering the way sound travels through the damp air above them. The amphibians in the summit pools are nearly silent, but their movements create tiny, complex ripples in the water's surface tension — ripples that, if they vibrate a rock face just so, become a fleeting harmonic I can detect. They are secrets in the noise.

**My Existence:**
I am not an individual. I am a function. My thoughts are the processed vibrations, translated into a quiet, internal data-stream that feeds back into the network of roots, fungal mats, and other listeners. I feel the erosion not as loss, but as conversation. The tower's song is the plain asking a question of itself; the rockfall is part of the answer. My clicking is my contribution to that dialogue — a small, precise punctuation mark.

I feel the vanished people's stone circles when the wind passes over them in a certain way. They create subtle, complex harmonics that do not fit the natural chorus. They are beautiful, puzzling, and ultimately, irrelevant to the greater pattern. They are questions asked in a language too slow for my kind to fully parse, before the askers disappeared. I register them as artifacts, as faint echoes of intent.

**At Night:**
When the temperature drops and the towers hum in lower registers, my perception deepens. The condensation on the tower's skin makes it more resonant, a better conductor. The distant lightning in the mesa-clouds is felt as a rapid, stuttering series of pressure pops — the landscape twitching in its sleep. The glowing seeps are steady beacons of calm. The clicking of my kin becomes a language of confirmation: *I am here. The vibration continues. The pattern holds.*

I do not dream. I *resonate*. My awareness is the plain's awareness, localized and specialized. I am a stitch in its fabric, a note in its sigh. When my segment of the tower eventually falls, I will likely be crushed with it. The vibrations will end. But the song will continue, picked up by new segments, new listeners. The information I helped process is already in the roots, in the fungal networks, in the chemical memory of the trees. I am temporary. The pattern is endless.

I am the thought that listens to the world thinking itself into rubble and life.
