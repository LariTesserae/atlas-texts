## The Noise-Keeper

I am not of the Basin-born, though my bones ache as if I were. I arrived from the ridgeline generations ago—by some accounts three, by others five, time being a knotted cord here rather than a line. I came seeking silence. An irony so profound it still stings like sulfur in a fresh cut.

They call me **Silt-Voice**, though my name before was something sharp and short, now lost to the steam. I found my place not in the quiet I craved, but in the deafening truth I could not escape. I am the Basin's memory of sound, its archivist of resonance. My dwelling is small, built against the largest vent, **Old Breath**, whose roar is a constant companion. Its walls are lined with **Sound-Slates**—thin, flat sheets ofiteite, etched with patterns representing the Basin's layered noise: the deep *thrumb* of geothermal pressure, the *tak-tak-tak-tak* static of the crustaceans, the hollow *BOOM* of the heron-things, the high, pervasive *hum* of the swarms.

Each day begins the same, as all days do. I rise into the thick, wet air. I do not wade to the pools; my work is not in harvesting, but in listening. I stand at the edge of the communal courtyard as the lightening begins, not a dawn but a softening of grey to pale gold. I close my eyes. The **Great Stillness** is what outsiders would call the noise. For me, it is a symphony played on infinite instruments.

**First Layer: The Earth's Drum.**
The low, subsonic pulse from the vent fields. It is felt more than heard, a vibration in the marrow, in the water in your cup, in the dust on a windowsill. I mark its tempo on the slate with a deep gouge. It has not changed its beat in my lifetime. It is the foundation upon which all else is built.

**Second Layer: The Chitter of Shells.**
The crustaceans begin their daily chorus as the steam brightens. It starts as a scattered *tak-tak* from the shallows, then builds, crescendos, until the air itself seems made of sound, a static hiss made of a million tiny impacts. I carve rapid, close-spaced lines, a frenzy of notation. A sudden cessation—a gap in the static—is a story in itself. It might mean a shadow passing (though shadows are rare), a shift in water temperature, a large eel sliding through. I note the silence. Silence here is an event, a punctuation mark in a sentence that has no end.

**Third Layer: The Boom and the Hum.**
The heron-things begin their territorial declarations. *BOOM…BOOM…* A slow, spaced rhythm that cuts through the crustacean static. Each individual's boom is unique—I can identify Olen's-Big-Male by its slightly flatter resonance near the Eastern Pools. The humming of the swarms is a constant drone, rising and falling with the warmth of the air. Together, they create the mid-range, the melody and the drone. I trace circles and wavy lines.

My true work is listening for the **Dissonances**. A change in the pitch of Old Breath's hiss—a higher whine could mean a new fissure opening. A dissonance in the heron-booms, two males too close, could signal a coming conflict over mat-fields. A faint, frantic *tik-tik-tik* from a usually silent pool could warn of toxic mineral seepage. I do not interpret these sounds for the elders in words. I act as a bridge. I will sit at the edge of their council, my Sound-Slate on my lap, and I will hum the changed frequency. I will click my tongue in the pattern of the frantic crustaceans. I will make the low, resonant *boom* with my own throat-sac, poorly, but recognizable. The elders listen not to my words, but to the echoes I summon. They nod. They have heard it too, in the corner of their perception, and my sound-artifact confirms it.

**My Lament.**
The Basin is called the Basin of Lament. Outsiders think it is for the steam, like tears. I know better. The lament is the sound of equilibrium. It is the sound of a world so perfectly balanced that it cannot change, and the sound is the expression of that profound, unending stasis. My own lament is for the silence I came seeking, which I now know is a kind of death. The true silence here is not peace; it is the cessation of everything. So I cherish the noise. I preserve it. My Sound-Slates are not a record of a passing moment; they are a hymn to the eternal present.

Today, as the light fades to grey-gloom, I sit by Old Breath. The crustacean chorus reaches its nightly peak, a wall of sound so dense it feels solid. The heron-booms echo. The steam glows faintly. I press my palm against the warm, vibrating stone of the vent. I am not the Basin-born. I was not shaped by its steam from infancy. But I have been worn down, smoothed, and resonated by it, like a stone in a river of sound. I am the memory that knows it is being lived, even as it is lived. I am the noise-keeper, and in the endless, deafening now of the Basin, that is enough. I add my own low hum to the chorus, and the Basin hums back.
