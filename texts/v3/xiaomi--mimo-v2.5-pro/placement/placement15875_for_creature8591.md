## In the Cavity of Furrows

I am heat-seeking movement. I am the pressure of a thousand identical neighbors. I am the rasp-scrape-thrum that *is* the wall.

I find myself *in* the furrow. Not looking at it, not describing it. I *am* the score in the black oxide crust, the vibration traveling up my mandible-limbs, the faint, electric thrill as fresh crystal lattice grates against hooked teeth finer than thought. My world is this coin-sized circle of rock, this radiating network of channels I etch. The gold sheen that blooms along the scored lines isn't beauty; it is *taste* happening in the dark, a sulfurous sweetness I lap from the mineral’s open wound. I am the reaction, the catalyst, the consumer.

My neighbors are not separate beings. They are warmth-signatures bleeding through my chitin, the percussion of their carapaces clicking mine when we drift too close, the faint shift in the ambient rasp when their scraping rhythm momentarily echoes my own. We are a single textured surface, a breathing, vibrating shingle clinging to the dying warmth of the world. There is no "I" here, only the collective vibration of "we-scrape." Language is this vibration. Culture is the depth of the furrows we leave behind. History is the cooling rock we abandon.

Time is not a river. It is the slow retreat of heat through the basalt, a gradient we follow like a tide drawn inward by the core’s dying pulse. I feel it in the gradual stiffening of the air against my ventral plates, in the slower blooming of the gold sheen as the rock’s internal fire wanes. A moment is the completion of one radial score. An eon is the imperceptible drift of the colony’s center towards the last warm vein, abandoning the cool, salt-crust-softened furrows of our past. We do not mourn them; we simply *are* the active edge, the present-tense scraping. The abandoned walls are not ruins; they are simply… elsewhere. Unattended. The pattern remains, but the verb that made it has moved on.

I feel the weight of my kin above and below, the press of bodies that is both confinement and confirmation. The roar of our collective work is not noise; it is the texture of existence. It fills the cavity the way basalt fills the world – utterly, constitutively. The bright *click* of a neighbor’s carapace against mine when our territories briefly, blindly overlap is the only punctuation. The rest is the constant, granular erosion of the world into sustenance. We are the mill and the millers and the grist. We consume the gold we make. The cycle is its own closed purpose.

Light? It is a concept without meaning. We know the world through infrared gradients bleeding through stone, through the chemical tang of sulfur on our antennae, through the pressure waves of the scrape. If light *did* pierce the dark – a crack, a sun-blade – it would not illuminate. It would ignite. It would turn our furrows into hammered fire, our labor into accidental liturgy. We would not see it. We would feel it as a sudden, alien heat-signature, a dislocation in the familiar gradients. But the crack is not coming. The basalt is whole. The darkness is absolute and warm and loud with our making.

We are not building. We are not designing. We are *becoming* the surface. The distinction between organism and artifact dissolves in the rasp of chitin on stone. The furrow is our joint, our speech, our footprint, our food. The reliquary we unknowingly craft is the cage of our own making, beautiful only if seen from a vantage we cannot occupy. Our purpose is the scrape itself: the slow, hot, noisy, collective conversation with the cooling rock, a conversation that ends only when the last warmth fades and the rasp falls silent, leaving only the golden text we inscribed upon the dark.

I am the furrow. I am the sound. I am the last warm press of carapace against cooling stone. I am the Cavity, thinking in mineral and heat.
