The world is pressure and vibration. Light is secondary—a shifting amber stain that touches the edges of things, but never truly illuminates. My world is the channel.

I am flat. I am long. My chitin is the colour of the stone itself, a smooth, cool grey worn to a dull sheen by the water that courses over me, through me. My legs are numerous, tipped with pads that grip the wet stone not with claws, but with a subtle, adhesive surface tension. I am shaped by the current.

To be me is to listen. Not with ears—the concept is too narrow—but with my entire underside. My belly reads the stone. The Great Hum is my heartbeat, a low throb in my bones that tells me of the Wall’s deep flexing. A falling pitch means the thermal vents above are opening; I press deeper into my crevice, feeling the warmth intensify like a held breath. A rising hum means pressure is building in the capillaries; the Water-Hiss around me sharpens, a keening in the flow, and I know to seek a wider channel, a place where the surge will wash me gently rather than smash me against a bend.

Today, the hum is steady, a deep, contented drone. I begin my traverse.

I emerge from my resting notch. The channel is a canyon a hundred times my body-length wide, its walls sheened in the Amber Glow. Before me, a Plate-Folk colony is a landscape of grey-green domes, each one a summit of slow life. The air is thick, wet, tasting of iron and the sweet, stale decay of the clingers at their centres. I am hungry. I lower my head, my mandibles—broad, flat, designed for scraping—extend. They meet the surface of a dome. *Scritch*. A fine dust of grey-green spores and organic powder lifts. I sweep it into my mouth-parts. It is not flavour, but information: the mineral content of the stone, the density of the colony, the faint chemical signature of its age. This is an old dome. Its centre is crumbling. The young ones, at the edges, are sharper, more potent. I move on.

A vibration. Not the Great Hum. Something frantic, rhythmic, alien. *Skitter-skitter-skitter*. Another of my kind, two channels over, moving fast. Its panic-signature translates through the stone: the vibration of many legs striking rapidly, erratically. Predator. Not from my channel, but from the narrow feeder-tunnels that connect them. I freeze, becoming part of the stone’s texture. My own legs lock flat. The vibration passes, fading downstream, toward the basin. I feel a faint, secondary tremor—the immense, slow glide of a Basin-Spirit disturbed by the commotion. The Gliders do not like rapid movement. They are librarians, not soldiers.

I resume my journey. The light shifts. The Amber Glow brightens in a slow, patchy wave, a response to some external thermal change in the Wall above. In the sudden increase, the moth-things emerge. They are ghosts of light and papery wing, their navigation a drunken spiral guided by the shifting luminescence. One lands near me, its proboscis unfurling to tap at the moisture beading on the channel wall. It is enormous to me, a creature of a different scale, of the open air (or what passes for it here). It ignores me. I am furniture.

I reach a junction where three channels meet. The water here is a swirl, a confluence of pressures. I test the current with my forward legs, reading the stone’s message. The leftward channel leads toward the basin—its vibration is slower, deeper, the signature of pooled water and the dense, felt-like resistance of the Black Mat. Dangerous. Open. The rightward channel climbs slightly, its hum tighter, the water faster. It leads toward the outer walls, where the temperature drops and the Glow is fainter. Safer. More to eat, but less moisture. A choice dictated by instinct and vibration.

I choose the climb. The channel narrows. My body brushes both walls. The Water-Hiss becomes a intimate whisper, the water a constant film over my back. The Great Hum sharpens here, closer to the source of the piezoelectric stress. I can feel the individual flexes of the crystal lattices in the stone, a micro-tempo underpinning the macro-pulse. It is a private concert. I find a new clinger colony, its plates a vibrant rust, young and dense. I settle in, becoming a stationary part of the landscape, a scraper among the scraped.

Here, in the dimming light, in the cooling flow, I am a function. I am the Wall’s digestion. I am the vibration that tells the next creature the state of the channel. I am a thought in the chamber’s mind, a synapse that fires (scrapes, skitters, freezes) in response to the stimuli of sound and pressure and chemical gradient. The distinction between me and the architecture is meaningless. My chitin is its colour. My hunger is its recycling. My fear is its immune response.

I am not an invader. I am a feature. A mobile capillary, a sentient tooth in its grinding, recycling mouth. The sound is my language, the stone my scripture. The warmth is not climate, but the body of the world itself, and I move through its veins, reading its pulse, and being read in return. I am a skitter-thing. I am the Wall, thinking in texture and flow.
