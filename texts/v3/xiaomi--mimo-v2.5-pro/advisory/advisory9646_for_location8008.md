To prepare for venturing into **The Pocket**, an ordinary human multiverse traveler must abandon assumptions of physical law, sensory input, and temporal stability. This is not merely a dive or a climb—it is an immersion into a fracture in reality itself. Here is how to prepare:

---

### **I. Psychological & Conceptual Preparation**
1. **Accept Non-Euclidean Space**:  
   - The Pocket defies orientation. Train in sensory-deprivation tanks or VR simulations that eliminate "up/down" cues. Practice floating motionlessly for hours without panic.  
   - Embrace the idea that "walls" are illusions. The crack may feel like a tomb, but it is a *thought-space* — claustrophobia is your first enemy.

2. **Manage Expectations of Perception**:  
   - Sight is unreliable. Prepare for total darkness, then fleeting "ideas of light" that bypass the eyes. Practice meditating on abstract geometry or dream-imagery to avoid overinterpreting these glimpses.  
   - Sound is sparse, then profoundly silent. Use earplugs in anechoic chambers to acclimate to the weight of absolute quiet — and to that low, sustained tone that may feel like a physical pressure.

3. **Embrace the Unknowable**:  
   - The Pocket is a *between* place. Study liminal spaces, apophenia (seeing patterns in randomness), and pareidolia. You will question whether the cairns, tubes, or vegetation are intentional. Accept that you will never know.  
   - Prepare for a feeling of being *observed by nothing*. The darkness here is not empty; it is *present*. Practice mindfulness to avoid projecting hostile intent onto the void.

---

### **II. Physical & Equipment Preparation**
1. **Body Conditioning**:  
   - This is a wet, confined, cool environment. Train in caving (squeezing through tight water-filled passages) and cold-water immersion. Build tolerance for uniform coolness without thermocline — your body will expect temperature gradients and fail to find them.  
   - The water’s buoyancy is *even*. Practice swimming with neutral buoyancy throughout your entire body (drysuit with distributed weighting). Prepare for the uncanny sensation of your limbs weighing the same as your torso.

2. **Breathing & Air Management**:  
   - **Critical**: Your bubbles are absorbed. This suggests the water-air exchange is dangerously efficient. Use a closed-circuit rebreather (CCR) with CO₂ scrubbers to minimize exhalation loss. Carry no open-circuit gear.  
   - Practice slow, mindful breathing. Rapid breathing wastes air and feeds the vegetation, which may subtly respond. Exhale into sealed containers if possible.  
   - Assume your breath is *fuel* for the ecosystem. Conserve it.

3. **Light & Navigation**:  
   - Bring no artificial light. The description implies light is *alien* here and may alter the space. Use tactile navigation: wear textured gloves to feel basalt edges.  
   - Memorize the crack’s path inward. Mark your route with biodegradable, non-reflective tape (e.g., twisted plant fiber) if you must, but expect the environment to rearrange around you.

4. **Gear Essentials**:  
   - **Hydration/Nutrition**: The water is not water. Do not drink it. Carry all supplies in sealed, insulated packs. Consume high-calorie, low-residue foods to minimize metabolic byproducts.  
   - **Clothing**: Full-body wetsuit or drysuit with thermal liner (to maintain core temperature in the perpetual coolness). Avoid shiny or synthetic materials that might reflect the “phantom light” or interact with the vegetation.  
   - **Tools**: A small, blunt basalt flake for tactile exploration (no metal tools that could clang or reflect). A soft container for exhaled air capture. A thin, high-tensile rope to tether yourself at the entrance — but be warned, tethers may become irrelevant if space folds.

5. **Biological Hazards**:  
   - The vegetation is gelatinous and absorbent. Assume it can leach through skin. Wear non-permeable gloves and neck seal.  
   - The calcium tubes hum. Do not insert anything into them. The vibration may be resonant or hypnotic.  
   - The fauna is gone, but its structures remain. They may be fragile. Move slowly to avoid dislodging anything — the silence here is historic.

---

### **III. Behavioral Protocols Inside the Pocket**
1. **Movement**:  
   - Float. Rotate slowly. Do not kick or thrash — this disturbs the “patient” water and may trigger reactions from the vegetation.  
   - Approach seams and cairns with caution. Observe from arm’s length. The cairns are not for touching.  
   - If drawn toward the narrow passage, stop. Breathe. The tone comes from there. Listening is allowed; entering is not.

2. **Sensory Discipline**:  
   - When the darkness *thins*, do not stare. Let the “idea of light” pass through you like a breath. Do not chase it.  
   - When bubbles drift toward the vegetation, watch without intervention. This is part of the exchange. You are participating in the Pocket’s metabolism.  
   - If the tone sounds, close your eyes. It will take something from the silence — possibly a memory, a sense, a small piece of your proprioception. Accept the loss.

3. **Timekeeping**:  
   - Wear an analog watch with no glow paint. Note the time of entry. Check it every 10 minutes to anchor yourself. Time will feel stretched or compressed; the watch may run fast or stop. Trust your internal rhythm if the watch fails.  
   - Plan to stay no longer than 60 minutes subjective time. The Pocket will erode your orientation; your body will eventually panic at a subconscious level.

4. **Egress**:  
   - The crack leads upward. When you feel the first urge to leave — not a thought, but a physical pull — obey it. Do not linger for “one more look.” The Pocket rearranges itself when not observed.  
   - Ascend slowly. As you exit, expect the basalt to feel different under your hands. The crack may seem wider or narrower. Do not trust your memory.

---

### **IV. Post-Visit Recovery & Reflection**
1. **Physical**:  
   - Thoroughly rinse all gear with fresh water and UV-sterilize. The vegetation’s gel may remain on surfaces.  
   - Monitor your breathing for several days. You may feel a residual coolness in your lungs, or a faint hum in your ears. This should fade.

2. **Psychological**:  
   - The Pocket leaves a residue — the “feeling of having been inside a thought not your own.” Do not fight it. Journal the experience immediately, then seal the journal for a week before rereading.  
   - Expect dreams of basalt seams, absorbed breath, and geometric shadows. These are normal. If they persist for more than a month, seek a trauma-informed therapist who understands interdimensional exposure.

3. **Philosophical**:  
   - You were held, briefly, by a place that remembers being open. You were fed into its ecosystem. The cairns may have been markers for something larger, or punctuation in a sentence you cannot read.  
   - Accept that some places do not want to be understood. They want to be *felt*. The Pocket is one of them.

---

### **Final Warning**:  
The Pocket is not a place for collecting, conquering, or comprehending. It is a place for *being briefly perceived*. Prepare to leave with less than you brought — not in possessions, but in certainty. The water will keep your breath, the dark will keep its thinning, and the stone will keep its age. You will carry only the memory of a cool, quiet thought that let you rest inside it, and then let you go.
