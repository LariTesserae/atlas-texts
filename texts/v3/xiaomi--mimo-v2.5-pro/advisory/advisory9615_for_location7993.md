## Multiverse Traveler's Briefing: The Condensation Foundries of the Upper Shelf

**Objective:** Brief visitation for observation, documentation, and potential contact. *Minimize exposure time. Establish and maintain rigorous mental and physical exit protocols.*

**Threat Assessment:** **HIGH (Adaptive Perceptual/Existential).** Physical hazards are secondary to cognitive and ontological drift. The primary danger is not injury, but *assimilation*.

### I. Pre-Departure Preparation

1.  **Mental Anchoring & Cognitive Hygiene:**
    *   **Reality Baseline:** Carry tangible, non-replicable items from your home reality (e.g., a specific childhood toy, a pressed leaf from a unique tree, handwritten notes from a loved one). Their physical presence and distinctiveness are crucial.
    *   **Mnemonic Triggers:** Establish a clear, sensory-rich memory of a place/feeling *diametrically opposed* to the Foundries (e.g., a warm, dry desert sunrise; a chaotic, noisy market; the smell of baking bread in a sunlit kitchen). Practice invoking this memory vividly. This is your cognitive "eject button."
    *   **Documentary Rigor:** Pre-commit to recording observations in a specific, unvarying format. Use both analog (waterproof logbook, grease pencil) and digital (ruggedized, waterproof recorder with pre-set, unalterable timestamps). *Do not trust your senses alone. The double-exposure light, shifting corridors, and quiet will conspire to make you doubt your records later.*
    *   **Define "Normal":** Before entry, spend time consciously noting the rules of baseline reality – gravity pulls down, water flows down, corridors stay one length, light comes from defined sources, industry is loud. Write these down. Read them aloud.

2.  **Physical & Gear Preparation:**
    *   **Respiratory Protection:** A sealed, full-face respirator with multi-gas/odor cartridges is mandatory. The metallic taste signifies atmospheric particulates; the faint organic smell is concerning. Use it *continuously*.
    *   **Moisture Mitigation:** This is paramount.
        *   **Clothing:** Full-body, sealed hydrophobic suit (not just waterproof; actively repelling). Layer with thermal underlayers – the damp *is* cold. Sealed boots with excellent grip (surfaces will be slick).
        *   **Equipment:** **Everything** must be housed in individually sealed, hard-sided waterproof cases with desiccant packs. Assume total immersion condensation. Include a waterproof bag for your logbook/recorder between entries.
        *   **Personal Care:** High-grade antifungal powder/cream for skin. Frequent changes of inner socks/gloves. Packets of dry, calorie-dense food sealed in waterproof wrappers. Water purification tabs (for collected condensation, though taste may be metallic).
    *   **Navigation & Communication (Expect Failure):**
        *   **Redundant Navigation:** Laser rangefinder, physical measure (calibrated tape), gyroscopic compass, pre-printed satellite maps (if obtainable) overlaid on a ruggedized tablet. *Cross-reference constantly.* Assume GPS/inertial nav will malfunction or give conflicting data due to spatial anomalies.
        *   **Communication:** Short-range comms (encrypted walkies) for party members. Assume long-range comms (quantum, radio) will be blocked, interfered with, or subtly altered. Agree on strict check-in schedules. Have a pre-arranged non-verbal signal (e.g., specific light flash pattern) for "extract now" if verbal comms fail.
        *   **Timekeeping:** Multiple, independent, ruggedized timepieces (digital and analog). Compare them frequently. Note discrepancies.
    *   **Observation Tools:**
        *   High-sensitivity audio recorder (to capture the *lack* of sound and the subsonic pulses).
        *   Thermal imaging camera (to map heat differentials, potentially reveal hidden processes or fauna).
        *   Portable spectrometer (for analyzing water/metal/air samples).
        *   High-resolution camera with macro and low-light capabilities (for fauna, artifacts, patterns). *Waterproof housing essential.*
        *   Non-invasive biological sampling kit (for potential fauna analysis - *extreme caution advised, observe only unless specifically equipped for xenobiology*).

### II. Entry & Initial Immersion Protocol (First 24 Hours - Critical Period)

1.  **The Perimeter Check:** Upon arrival at the perceived edge:
    *   **Immediately** perform baseline measurements: atmospheric composition, temperature, humidity, light spectrum. Record.
    *   **Physically mark** your entry point with a highly visible, durable, *unique* marker (e.g., neon paint on a structural beam, a GPS beacon – though it may malfunction later).
    *   **Take 10 minutes** to consciously invoke your "Reality Baseline" and "Mnemonic Trigger." State aloud: "I am [Name], from [Reality Designation]. My entry point is [Marker]. This place is anomalous. Water flows *down*. Corridors are *fixed*." Record this statement.
    *   **Assign Roles:** One person is designated the **Anchor**. Their primary role is observing the *group's behavior* and the environment's effect *on the group*, checking instruments against each other, and enforcing the exit protocol. They are *not* to be absorbed by exploration.

2.  **First Observation Cycle:**
    *   **Prioritize:** Light, Sound, Water.
    *   **Light:** Map sources. Note the double-exposure effect. Does it change based on your focus? Does your shadow behave? Record spectrum readings.
    *   **Sound:** Record ambient sound. Listen for the absence. Note times of subsonic pulses. Attempt to measure pulse origin/direction. **Do not seek the source of the silence.**
    *   **Water:** Follow a condensation rivulet. Map its path. Note if it deviates from gravity. Use the thermal camera on water sources – does heat pattern correlate with flow direction? **Do not touch the water with bare skin.**
    *   **Fauna:** **DO NOT INTERACT.** Observe from a distance using optics. Note behavior, routes, reactions (or lack thereof) to your presence. Are *you* on their routes? **If so, move immediately.** Do not block their paths. Observe if they observe you.
    *   **Workers:** Observe their routines, instrument checks. Do they acknowledge you? How? Note their attire's integration with the environment. **Avoid prolonged direct conversation initially.** Ask simple, factual questions only if necessary ("What is this section?"). Note if their answers seem rehearsed, overly simplistic, or contain references you cannot verify.

3.  **Anchor's Vigil:**
    *   Every 2 hours, the Anchor must:
        *   Ask each team member: "What is your full name and home reality?"
        *   Ask: "State the rule about water."
        *   Compare instrument readings (rangefinder, clock).
        *   Review the last 2 hours of logs/audio/video for inconsistencies or missed anomalies.
        *   Check the group's physical state (skin condition, respiratory seal, signs of fatigue or unusual focus).
        *   Visually locate the entry marker (use optics if needed). Is it still visible?

### III. Ongoing Protocols & Survival

1.  **The Rule of Three (Minimum):**
    *   **Never** work alone. Minimum group size: 3 (Observer, Recorder, Anchor).
    *   **Never** lose sight of at least one other team member.
    *   **Never** be more than 30 minutes from your pre-arranged extraction point (ideally the entry marker).

2.  **Interaction & Contact:**
    *   **Workers:** Treat with deep respect and caution. They are part of the system. Their "attentiveness" suggests they perceive the system's awareness. Avoid disrupting their work. If they offer information, listen, but triangulate with observations. **Do not ask "why."**
    *   **Fauna:** **Observation Only.** Do not attempt to capture, handle, or closely approach. They are integral components. Assume they report to the system.
    *   **Artifacts:** Do not touch the "inscrutable objects." Photograph, scan, document location. Their purpose may be tied to the Foundry's function – interacting could be disruptive or draw unwanted "attention."

3.  **Combating Assimilation (The Primary Threat):**
    *   **Routine is Armor:** Adhere rigidly to check-in schedules, measurement routines, and sleep shifts (maintain your home cycle as much as possible). Boredom is dangerous; it breeds acceptance.
    *   **Question the Normal:** When you see water flow up, or a corridor shift, **stop**. Verbally state the anomaly. Record it *immediately*. Have the Anchor verify. **Do not rationalize it.** Catalog it as a violation.
    *   **The Mnemonic Trigger:** If you feel a sense of peace, belonging, or inevitability about the Foundry's processes, or catch yourself thinking "it makes sense" about an anomaly, **invoke your Mnemonic Trigger immediately**. Leave the area if possible. Discuss the feeling with the Anchor.
    *   **Physical Cues:** Dry skin, cleared sinuses (despite respirator), feeling unusually warm/calm, or a dull headache are potential early signs of environmental influence. Note them.
    *   **The Exit Protocol:** Define a clear, objective trigger for immediate withdrawal (e.g., Anchor loses visual contact with entry marker for >15 mins, two members report the same major anomaly simultaneously, a member fails the "Rule of Three" question twice). **Do not wait for "one more observation" or "clarity."** Execute withdrawal sequence.

4.  **Withdrawal Sequence:**
    *   Anchor announces: "Withdrawal Protocol Initiated. Reason: [Clear, Objective Reason]."
    *   All members stop current task immediately. Secure equipment.
    *   Move directly to entry marker as a group. Do not deviate.
    *   **At the Marker:** Perform exit measurements (atmosphere, temp, humidity). Compare to entry. State aloud: "[Name], [Reality], exiting. Marker confirmed."
    *   Cross the perceived boundary *together*. Once outside (even 10 meters), stop. Perform cognitive check: "State rule about water. Describe your Mnemonic Trigger. What is the most obvious anomaly you saw today?"
    *   **Do not linger.** Move away from the perimeter to a pre-designated safe distance before resting or debriefing.

### IV. Post-Visit Decontamination & Debrief

1.  **Physical:**
    *   Full suit decontamination (chemical shower if available). Bag and isolate all gear from the Foundries.
    *   Thorough medical check: skin, respiratory, neurological. Monitor for delayed symptoms for 72 hours.
    *   Extended rest period before next multiverse jump.
2.  **Cognitive:**
    *   **Separate Debriefs:** Each member debriefs *individually* first with a neutral party (not another visitor). Focus on facts, measurements, sensory details.
    *   **Compare Records:** Only *after* individual debriefs should logs, audio, and video be compared. Look for discrepancies. Do not assume one record is more "true" than another; the discrepancies *are* the data.
    *   **Anchor's Report:** The Anchor provides the timeline of group behavior and interaction with the environment. Note points of confusion, changes in focus, moments of unusual calm or acceptance.
    *   **Synthesize with Caution:** Build a picture, but **accept irreconcilable elements**. The corridor *was* 47m *and* 61m. Water *did* flow up. The silence *was* deliberate. Do not force a single narrative. The Foundry's nature may be fundamentally non-linear or polyphonic.
    *   **Reality Re-anchoring:** Reinforce contact with home reality. Review baseline items and memories. Engage in simple, concrete tasks.

**Final Warning:** The Condensation Foundries are not merely a place to be visited; they are a *process* that may incorporate visitors. Your preparation is not just about safety; it's about maintaining the integrity of your perception against an environment that rewrites the rules with quiet patience. Your greatest tools are redundancy, routine, and the unwavering, documented reminder that **water flows down, and you can always leave.** Assume the Foundry wants you to forget both. Do not let it.
