They do not have names. They do not need them. They are functions in a wet, humming stasis.

**The Fur of the Walls.**  
These are the ones you see first, or rather, *sense* first—a shifting, breathing texture on the gunmetal. They are the *Lepidoptera reciprocis*, though they are not moths. Their wings are translucent membranes, veined with a cold blue light that pulses faintly, independently, a constellation of patient, living glimmers. Their bodies are segmented sacs, pale and swollen with the condensation they sup. Thousands cling per square inch, a living fur that drinks from the air itself. They do not mate. They do not die. Their purpose is to consume the excess, to be a stabilizing layer, a bio-regulator. They are aware only of the film of water beneath their proboscis and the need to remain still. Their existence is a constant, gentle consumption. They are the first filter, the membrane between the engineered atmosphere and the weeping walls.

**The Grinders of the Skin.**  
Beneath them, in the slow, dark lanes between the moth-clusters, move the *Crustacea iterum*. Armored, many-legged, each the size of a lentil or a pinhead, they scrape at the living biofilm that coats every surface—a designed algal varnish, a food source that regenerates at the exact rate it is consumed. The crustaceans move in loops, tracing the same eternal circuit on the same panel, their passage a glacial migration that begins and ends in the same square centimeter. They are the maintenance crew. Their grinding mouthparts keep the biofilm from overgrowing, from disrupting the delicate equilibrium of condensation and temperature. They know only the texture of the film and the imperative to move along their prescribed path. They are not alive in the way of birth and death; they are alive in the way of a gear that turns.

**The Sustained Chord.**  
And then there are the *Chironomidae constantia*, the midges. You do not see them easily. They are a haze in the middle air, a shimmer of near-invisible wings beating in perfect, unsynchronized unison. Each is a vibrating filament, engineered to resonate at 340 hertz. Together, they are not a swarm but an instrument—a choir of identical, soulless throats producing the drone that is the chamber's heartbeat. They feed on microscopic particles suspended in the wet air, their bodies too simple for thought, their wings too precise for variation. They are the sound that has replaced silence. They are the tone that holds the ceiling up. They are not making music; they are *being* it. Their existence is a vibration, a perpetual output of acoustic energy that fills every cubic millimeter and hums in the teeth of the metal. They are the reason you cannot hear the water fall. They are the sound of the Engine's thought.

Together, they are not an ecosystem. They are an *organism*. The moths are its skin and lungs, drinking and breathing the wet. The crustaceans are its slow-moving cilia, grooming its surface. The midges are its voice, its vibrating inner ear. The water is its blood, cycling endlessly through its metal veins. The cold is its temperament, holding all action in a perfect, liquid stillness.

They do not interact. They do not conflict. They occupy their strata with the precision of oil and water. A crustacean may pass under a cluster of moths; it is not noticed. A midge may drift through the slow-falling droplets; it is not impeded. They share the space the way organs share a body—ignorant of the whole, dedicated to their function.

They are aware only of their own imperative: *to continue*. To feed on the film, to grind the film, to vibrate at the prescribed frequency. They are not alive in a way that involves curiosity or fear. They are alive in a way that involves being exactly where and what they were built to be. They are the proof that the chamber is perfect. They are the components that make the perfection run.

If they had a language, it would be the hum. If they had a scripture, it would be the specifications printed on the builder's plans. If they had a heaven, it would be this—this eternal, wet, unchanging present.

They are the Drowned Engine's inhabitants. And they are very, very busy doing nothing at all.
