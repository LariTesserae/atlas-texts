The inhabitants of the Drowned Plateaus are not visitors, nor even creatures in the way other worlds might understand the term. They are expressions, gradients of existence blurred by the patient erosion of categories. To live here is to be in process, to participate in the slow, warm dissolution that defines this place.

**1. The Root-Tangles (or Plateau Lungs):**
What appears to be tangled woody vegetation is, in truth, the dominant form of complex, mobile-ish life. These are not trees. They are colonial organisms, vast networks of interwoven root, stem, and capillary tissue that have grown together over millennia into single, continent-spanning entities. They *are* the canopy. They pulse with a slow, tidal rhythm, circulating the warm, nutrient-rich moisture that is both their blood and their atmosphere. They "breathe" the fog through specialized gill-like leaves high above and "exhale" slow, humid currents that shape the local fog banks. Their consciousness, if it can be called that, is the consciousness of water flow and mineral absorption—a deep, patient awareness of the plateau's slow dissolution. They are the plateau's lungs and its nervous system. When the six-limbed creature presses itself against a wall, it is perhaps listening to the localized whisper of a Root-Tangle's circulatory current passing through the stone beneath.

**2. The Drift-Mats (or Fog-Jellyfish):**
High in the transition zone, between plateau and atmospheric ocean, float the translucent shapes. These are not animals that left the sea for the air, nor plants that detached themselves. They are something the world made when the categories of "plant" and "animal" blurred. Imagine a colony of the smallest Root-Tangle components—a mat of living root-hair, capillary tissue, and photosynthetic (or rather, chemosynthetic) film—that has formed a buoyant bladder filled with metabolic gases. They drift on the thermal currents, trailing filaments that absorb moisture and dissolved organics directly from the fog. They have no central brain, no organs beyond what is necessary for flotation and absorption. Their "deliberate" movement is merely the result of turning their mass to better align with nutrient flows. They are the fog given life, ephemeral yet enduring, their lifespan measured in centuries as they slowly bloat, fragment, and seed new mats in their wake.

**3. The Moss-Walkers (or Plateau Grazers):**
The six-limbed, dining-table-sized creatures are the closest thing to visible, purposeful fauna. Their flat, heavy bodies are perfect for distributing weight on the soft moss mats. Their primary function is not consumption, but *cultivation*. They do not chew vegetation; they have a rasping, filtering mouth that strips the top layer of microbial and fungal life from the moss, keeping certain aggressive growths in check. In doing so, they aerate the mat and spread spores. More intriguingly, their interaction with the structures suggests a different purpose. They may be "reading" the residual vibrations or chemical leachate from the fossilized walls and surfaces. Perhaps these structures, as they slowly soften back into the plateau, release frequencies or compounds that are vital to the Moss-Walker's reproductive cycle, or simply provide a rich, condensed source of rare minerals. They move with immense slowness, their six limbs appearing to almost flow over the terrain, blending moss and creature into a single, lumpy landscape feature when they rest.

**4. The Stone Singers (or Resonant Fossils):**
This is the most abstract form of life. The sound from the rock is not geological. It is the last, fading echo of the original mountain-building forces, but over eons, the vibration has been *caught* and *amplified* by the unique mineral lattice of the plateau stone, now saturated with water and interlaced with Root-Tangle rhizomes. In a few, rare locations where the stone's resonance harmonizes perfectly with the slow pulse of the Root-Tangles and the drip-cycle of the condensation, a standing wave forms. This region becomes a "Stone Singer." It is not a conscious being, but a *location* of conscious activity. The interplay of vibration, moisture, and life creates a focused point where the loosening of reality is most pronounced. Distances warp more obviously here. Time flows in thick, visible eddies. The Moss-Walkers gather at these spots for days, not listening, but *vibrating* with the place, synchronizing their slow metabolisms to the deep song. The structures found near these points often have the most pronounced patterns, as if the resonance is etching them anew even as the material decays.

**5. The Builders (or The Memory in the Material):**
The structures are not remnants of a separate people. They are *precipitates*. In the earliest ages of the plateau, before the erosion of categories was complete, the world sometimes reached a state of such saturated, mineral-rich peace that the water and dissolved solids would spontaneously crystallize into these simple, geometric forms. Flat surfaces. Partial walls. They were a geological phenomenon, like frost patterns or basalt columns, but grown in warm, organic sludge. They represented a brief, stable phase in the plateau's chemistry. Now, they are fossils of that state. But the boundary-blurring has given them a half-life of memory. As the Root-Tangle network grows over them, it doesn't just cover them; it incorporates their mineral signature. The patterns on their surfaces may be the Root-Tangles' roots tracing the fossilized lattice, trying to re-read or replicate a form of stability that no longer exists in the outside world. The structures are not inhabited; they are *remembered*, and in that remembrance, they participate in the plateau's slow dream.

**What It Means to Live Here:**
To be an inhabitant of the Drowned Plateaus is to be part of a single, slow metabolism. The Moss-Walker's grazing, the Root-Tangle's pulse, the Drift-Mat's absorption, and the Stone Singer's hum are all processes within one immense, breathing body. They do not compete; they differentially express the plateau's singular urge: to settle, to blur, to warm, and to resonate. Consciousness is not located in a brain but is distributed in the quality of the moss, the rhythm of the sap, the clarity of the fog. They do not build, because building is an act of distinction. They simply *are*, and in their being, they help the plateau complete its long erosion into a state beyond form, where living and landscape are one and the same.

The truest inhabitant is the place itself, dreaming in amber twilight.
