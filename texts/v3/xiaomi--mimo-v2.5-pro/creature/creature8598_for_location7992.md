The Peneplain of Canopy is not *inhabited*. It is *embodied*. The beings here are not residents; they are **organs, cells, and synaptic firings** within a single, continental-scale organism.

---

### The Root-Weavers
You would not call them animals. They resemble **giant, sluggish lampreys** or **segmented worms**, but their bodies are sheaths of chitin and cartilage wrapped around cores of specialized mycelium. They move through the **rotting cores of the midstory trunks**, their radula-like mouths grinding decaying wood-pulp into nutrient slurry, which they secrete directly into the fungal network. They are the **digestive system**. Their movement is not locomotion; it is **peristalsis**. They follow nutrient gradients set by the fungal net, and their tunnels become conduits for air and water. Sometimes, two will meet and fuse, their bodies anastomosing into a single, larger vessel. They are deaf, blind, and nearly insensate. They feel only pressure and chemical desire.

### The Canopy-Striders
These are what remains of birds. **Flightless, their wings modified into broad, padded flippers** for gliding between hanging gardens. Their feet are multi-jointed, with splayed toes tipped in adhesive pads. They are covered not in true feathers, but in **velvety, photosensitive filaments** that sample the dim light. They eat fungal fruits, slow-moving invertebrates, and the occasional, unfortunate amphibian. Their calls are not for communication, but for **sonar**—they emit a low, clicking pulse and listen for the returning echoes to map the latticework of the canopy. They are solitary, territorial only in the immediate vicinity of their roost—a hollow in a fused trunk. When they die, their filaments continue to glow faintly for a week, a soft, blue-green luminescence in the dark.

### The Pool-Dwellers
In the water-filled cups of bromeliads and pitcher-like epiphytes, entire ecosystems exist. The most complex are the **Glass Frogs**—not transparent, but their skin secretes a **crystalline, antifungal mucus** that protects them from the omnipresent decomposition. They are **neotenic**: adults retain larval gills and never leave their birth-pool. They are ambush predators, their tongues tipped with a sticky, digestive enzyme. They communicate through **subsonic vibrations** sent through the water itself, patterns tapped by their hind feet against the plant’s walls. Their eggs are laid not in the water, but on the **undersides of the floating detritus mat**, where they develop in air pockets, kept moist by the constant humidity.

### The High-Fliers
The only creatures to see the sun. They are **albatross-like**, with wingspans wider than a human is tall, but their bodies are thin and lightweight, built for gliding in the thin, high-altitude thermals above the cloud-layer. They are not hunters. They are **scavengers and pollinators** of the highest canopy buds. Their primary purpose seems to be **transport**. They carry fungal spores, plant gametes, and even small, dormant insects from one isolated plateau section to another on their feathers. They land rarely, folding their wings to drop like stones back through the canopy membrane, disappearing into the dark. Their guano is a rare, sacred fertilizer, falling in single, concentrated piles that become **hotspots of rapid growth and strange mutation**.

### The Stone-Minders
The ruins are not unattended. Lichenous growths, **not quite plant, not quite animal**, colonize the stone. They are **responsive**. When rare direct light hits them, they undergo a photosynthetic burst, altering their chemistry and coloration in complex, shifting patterns that resemble **runes or circuit diagrams**. These patterns are not static; they change over hours, reacting to minute shifts in humidity, temperature, and the faint vibrations of the plateau. Some are **bioluminescent**, pulsing a soft, infrared glow imperceptible to most eyes but visible to the sensory pits of certain root-weavers. They are the memory of the place, and perhaps, the only things that truly *notice* time passing. They do not think. They **archive**.

### The Silent Speakers
The air itself is inhabited. **Colony-forming airborne microbes**, smaller than pollen, live in the saturated air. They form vast, invisible clouds that **shepherd condensation**, their metabolic byproducts nucleating the ever-present moisture into droplets. They are the architects of the perpetual drizzle. They communicate via **volatile organic compounds**—simple signals of distress or plenty that can travel for kilometers in the still air. The sweet, chemical smell of the plateau is their language. When they die, they fall with the rain, adding their microscopic bodies to the ever-thickening organic substrate below. They are the atmosphere’s **blood cells**.

---

**They are all one.** The root-weaver’s tunnel becomes the highway for a fungal network that feeds the canopy-strider’s lichen, which in turn filters the light for the stone-minder’s growth. The pool-dweller’s tadpoles are eaten by the insect that pollinates the flower that feeds the high-flier, who carries the spore that grows the fungus that nurtures the root-weaver. There is no competition, only **circulation**. To be here is to be a **process**—a slow, wet, quiet, and utterly patient turning of matter within a single, dreaming, continental body. They do not live *on* the peneplain. They *are* its living thoughts.
