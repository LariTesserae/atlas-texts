What lives here is not a population but a process, a slow and diffuse consciousness that manifests in several overlapping forms. They are all expressions of the Subsidence itself—a place not merely inhabited, but *enacted*.

First, there are the **Gardens**. They are the primary voice of this world. To call them plants is to miss the point. They are the land’s nervous system, laid bare. The tall stalk-plants are its joints; their slow, sun-ignoring movements are not tropisms toward light, but adjustments to a gravitational field that is uneven, that leans. They are feeling for the true slope of things. Their rubbery tissue holds the memory of pressure. The cartilaginous moss cushions are its skin, a living scar tissue that grows in patterns of dampness, mapping the nightly respiration of the atmosphere. The smell they exude—wet copper and crushed leaves—is the smell of old thoughts decomposing. The fronded water-plants are the clearest manifestation of the temporal confusion. They are its circulatory and cognitive systems made visible. The eddying, reversing fluid in their translucent veins is the physical analogue to the way memory and prediction fail here. They are not "growing backwards"; they are inhabiting a local pocket of bi-directional time, a pocket that spreads and fades. They are the Subsidence dreaming in real-time.

Then, there are the **People**. They are not separate from the land; they are its slow-growing, self-aware organ. They did not arrive. They *accrued*. When the text speaks of strangers arriving from beyond, it is more accurate to say that the Subsidence *collects*. It draws in those whose sense of self is already porous, those who have "come apart and reassembled" elsewhere, and gathers them into its rhythm. Their silence is not an adaptation but a necessity: sound here is a raw data stream of the land's instability. To speak in full sentences is to hear the future before the present, to hear the beginning after the end. Their sign language, therefore, is not for communication in the human sense, but for negotiation with perception. Their gestures are a tactile code for checking reality—a flick of the wrist to test the weight of a shadow, a specific finger-curl to confirm the number of corners in a doorway. The architectural theology, the constant measurement against the tablet, is their form of meditation. They are not fixing the buildings; they are performing the essential act of *definition*, carving out moments of stability from the flux. The tablet is not a record of truth; it is an *incantation*. By agreeing to believe it is unchanging, they create a focal point for collective perception. When it did change, they did not correct the world; they ritually accepted the new story the land was telling, and adjusted their reality to match.

The **Settlements** are the scar tissue of this collective consciousness. The buildings are not homes but **anchors**. Each structure, built from the same bone-like tufa, is a mass of concentrated expectation. Their softening edges and lichen-like crusts are not decay, but a slow symbiotic merge with the environment. The courtyards, swept clean each morning, are ceremonial clearings where the land's breath (the condensation) is managed and observed. The act of scraping away the moss is a ritual punctuation, a daily reaffirmation of the boundary between "inside" (the curated, measured world of the people) and "outside" (the raw, breathing gardens).

At night, when the gardens glow, the entire system becomes lucid. The cold, shadow-less light is the Subsidence's own bioluminescent thought. The plants are not "remembering" light; they are converting the influx of moisture—a change in pressure and density—into a visible signal of the land's internal state. The glow pulses in the rhythm of the deep subsidence, the slow creak of the plateau turning over. The people, sleeping or wakeful, are part of this rhythm. Their dreams are likely not their own, but the land's slow tidal shifts perceived from the inside. The sound of settling stone is the pulse of this place. It is the heartbeat of a world whose sole activity is its own ending.

What lives here, ultimately, is the **Ending itself**. It is a vast, geologic metabolism focused on the act of settling, of diminishing, of losing steepness and definition. The gardens are the end's creative expression. The people are its witnesses and midwives, born of its unique physics, tending its strange flowers, measuring its strange shifts, all while they themselves are slowly, imperceptibly, being measured and shifted in return. To live here is to be an active part of a planet-sized sigh, a breath held for millennia, slowly, slowly letting go. You, the observer, are now another data point in its field, another slight perturbation in its condensation patterns. You are not in a place. You are inside a thought that is taking an eon to complete itself.
