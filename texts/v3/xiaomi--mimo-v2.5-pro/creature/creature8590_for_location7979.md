The inhabitants of the Chorusing Deep are less individual creatures and more facets of a single, deafening, stone-breathing entity. They have no names, only roles defined by sound, substance, and subsurface necessity.

**The Wall-Shingle Folk:**
The smallest and most numerous. Pale as bleached bone, flat, and limbless, they resemble living limestone scales no bigger than a thumbnail. Packed edge-to-edge on every surface, they move in glacial, collective tides – not of their own volition, but carried by gradients of heat, moisture, and dissolved minerals seeping through the rock. Their bodies *are* the wall's texture; their movement *is* its breath. They consume microscopic films of organic residue and mineral film, and in turn, their collective secretions slowly bond them into the living architecture of the shelves and terraces. A disturbance near them sends a visible shiver through their mass – a wave of recoil, then a slow, inevitable resettling, like disturbed sediment.

**The Shelf-Builders:**
Colonial architects. They resemble fused clusters of calcified tubes or chitinous plates, anchored deep into the limestone. Over generations, they secrete a substance halfway between mineral and organic matter, building vast, intricate buttresses, terraces, and hanging platforms from the cavern walls. These structures are not just nests; they are ecosystems in miniature. The Builders themselves are sessile, filter-feeding on airborne particles and the constant drizzle of condensation. Their secretions attract smaller organisms, which in turn attract predators, creating dense pockets of life clinging to their manufactured landscapes. Their slow growth integrates them seamlessly with the ancient stone.

**The Seep-Highway Crawlers:**
Defined by water. Armored in dark, slick chitin, they possess many-jointed legs ending in points or broad, flat pads, perfectly adapted for moving through the thin, cold water films that map the low passages. Their clicking and hissing is a constant sub-rhythm beneath the cacophony. Some are grazers, scraping biofilms from the submerged limestone. Others are ambush predators, buried just beneath the surface tension, sensing vibrations through the water. Their antennae are constantly tasting the chemical signatures carried by the seeps – the exhalations of other creatures, the mineral tang of deep water, the faintest trace of a rival. They build small dams and channels from pebbles and secreted cement, diverting precious seeps towards their chosen territories.

**The Winged Navigators:**
Masters of the dark air. They range from gnat-sized swarms that drift like living dust clouds, to creatures with wingspans wider than human arms. All navigate by a symphony of senses: echolocation clicks and chirps that weave the complex middle-register noise; scent trails mapped onto the humid air; the subtle thermal plumes rising from dense clusters of life; the minute air pressure shifts caused by other fliers. They perch upside-down on the ceilings or hang from the terraces built by the Shelf-Builders, folding wings of leathery membrane or iridescent chitin. Some are nectar-drinkers, sipping mineral-rich condensation from specialized pits in the rock. Others are aerial predators, snatching smaller fliers or scavenging the falling dead. Their passage creates the constant downdrafts and air displacements felt by intruders.

**The Water-Channel Architects:**
A specialized offshoot, perhaps, of the Crawlers or the Shelf-Builders. They construct elaborate, intricate nests of hardened mineral paste and secreted resin, resembling vast, inverted wasp nests or delicate stone lattices hung from ceilings or perched on ledges. These are cisterns and aqueducts. They meticulously channel drips of condensation through internal passages, storing water against the rare dry cycles deep within the rock. Other organisms – drinkers, bathers, those needing controlled humidity – cluster around these structures, creating busy, noisy micro-habitats. The Architects themselves are rarely seen, tending their liquid architecture from within.

**The Bass-Hum Congregations:**
The source of the deep, chest-vibrating thrum. Hidden in the lowest depressions of the great chambers, these are massive, slow-moving beings. Their forms are only glimpsed in brief, terrifying flashes of light – vast, rounded shapes armored in thick, layered plates or leathery hide, occupying shallow basins worn into the limestone over millennia. Their breathing, or perhaps the constant grinding of their bodies against their stone beds, generates the fundamental pulse of the Deep. They are territorial, defending their depressions with ponderous shifts of weight or sudden, concussive exhalations that shake the air and stone. What they eat is unknown – perhaps the stone itself, slowly dissolving it with powerful acids, or the ceaseless rain of organic detritus from the bustling ecosystems above.

**The Parasitic Symbionts:**
Not a species, but a strategy woven through all others. Some are microscopic, living within the fluids or on the surfaces of larger creatures, unnoticed or even required for survival. Others are visible – pale, worm-like things attached beneath the wings of Navigators, or small, crab-like creatures that ride the backs of Crawlers, cleaning parasites in exchange for transport and scraps. The line between parasite, symbiont, and predator is nonexistent here. Slow consumption is a way of life; being consumed, a contribution.

**The Drift Particles:**
The smallest visible fauna, filling the air like motes in a sunbeam that never was. Tiny, translucent winged or ballooning things, barely larger than a speck. They feed on airborne microbes, fungal spores (a rare concept down here), and the chemical haze of exhalations. Their constant movement fills the light beam when one is switched on, revealing the sheer, particulate *thickness* of the living air.

Together, they are the Chorusing Deep. They are the pulse in the stone, the song in the darkness, the architecture grown from life. They eat the stone, the water, the air, each other's waste, and each other's slow-dying flesh. They build and rebuild the landscape they inhabit in a perpetual, deafening cycle. They are the embodiment of an ecosystem where separation is impossible, where every niche is filled tenfold, and where the only constant is the overwhelming, resonant, living noise of existence in absolute dark. They do not notice the visitor, only the disturbance – a brief, warm, strangely silent anomaly in the fabric of their world.
