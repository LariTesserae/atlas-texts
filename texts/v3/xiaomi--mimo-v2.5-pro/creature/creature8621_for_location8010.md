The garden breathes, but it does not breathe life as you know it. What moves here are not creatures, but **consequences**. They are the grammar of this place made animate, the slow punctuation in a sentence written over millennia.

---

**The Veil Drifters**
First, if you look long enough, you see the **Veil Drifters**. They are not fish. They are not even animals. They are translucent, hydrostatic bladders the size of a human torso, filled with the garden’s own gel-like secretion. They have no eyes, no mouths, no discernible organs. They pulse with a slow, rhythmic contraction, not to propel themselves, but to subtly alter the pressure around them, allowing the deep, patient currents to carry them through the stalk forests. They are neutral buoyancy made manifest. They feed by osmosis, absorbing the faintest chemical gradients from the water, the waste of the chemosynthetic mats. When they die, they do not sink. They dissolve, their membranes thinning over decades until they are just another layer of gel, another faint nutrient layer in the water. They are less inhabitants than they are **weather**—slow, ambient, and inevitable.

**The Stalk-Singers**
Look closer at the great, dark stalks. You will see **movement** that is not the sway of the current. The **Stalk-Singers** are colonial organisms, a symbiosis of gelatinous polyps and mineral fibers. They cling to the stalks in irregular, barnacle-like clusters, but they are not fixed. They can detach, inching along the stalk’s surface with a glacial peristalsis, their movement measured in meters per year. They do not eat the stalk. They harvest the thin, mineral-rich crusts that grow upon it, using microscopic, silica-tipped tendrils to rasp away the deposits. In doing so, they "sing"—their rasping creates a vibration, a sound so low and slow it is more a pressure change in the water than a noise. It is the deep, grinding hum of the structure settling, but given a living, rhythmic source. They are the garden’s slow, inevitable erosion, given agency.

**The Woven Ones**
In the labyrinths of half-open corridors, where the architecture becomes a lattice, the **Woven Ones** reside. They are builders, but not as the original architects were. They are arthropod-like, but their exoskeletons are not chitin; they are a composite of the same mineralized fiber the ancients used, a living, flexible ceramic. They are small, rarely longer than a hand-span, and they are never still. Their purpose is **repair**. They are drawn to hairline fractures in the ancient walls, to spots where the calcite mortar has begun to fail. They secrete a new compound—part saliva, part mineral slurry—and spin it into the cracks with their forelimbs, not to restore the wall, but to reinforce it in a new, organic pattern. Their work is beautiful, fractal, and utterly pointless on a human timescale. A single Woven One might spend a century reinforcing a single arch. They are the place’s immune system, its monks, its obsessive archivists.

**The Luminous Scribes**
The bioluminescence is not random. It is a **language**. The **Luminous Scribes** are its authors and readers. They are vast, soft-bodied beings, like flattened, drifting octopuses made of translucent membrane. They have no fixed shape, constantly flowing and reforming. They are the only beings here that seem to possess something like curiosity. They drift through the stalk forests, their margins rippling, and they *touch* the glowing mats and nodular growths. Through a mechanism unknown, they can trigger a pulse of light in a growth, or cause a cluster to go dark. They are communicating. The messages are simple, slow, and vast: "This district is thriving." "The thermal plume is shifting." "There is a weakness here." "A stalk is dying." They are cartographers of the garden’s slow health, and their maps are written in faint, ephemeral light across a thousand square miles.

**The Sediment Dancers**
In the soft substrate, where the structures sink into silt, the **Sediment Dancers** move. You only know them by the faintest of trails in the ooze, or by the sudden, silent bloom of disturbed silt that hangs in the water for a day before settling. They are ribbon-like, flat, and muscular, burrowing not through the sediment, but *between* the packed layers of ancient architectural fill. They are decomposers of the oldest kind. They seek out the last vestiges of organic material in the walls themselves—mineralized wood fibers, ancient lashings, the ghost of a builder’s basket—and they slowly, patiently break it down, returning its chemistry to the water and the mats. They are the final undertakers, the beings that consume history and excrete future soil for the garden.

**The Absent Ones**
And then, there are the **phantoms**. The not-things. Shapes in the corner of your perception—too regular to be rock, too still to be drift. A wall that seems to have **a face** that is not a face, but an arrangement of nodules and shadows. A corridor that, if you return after a decade, has narrowed by an inch, its texture subtly different. The garden does not have animals because it has **processes that remember**. The builders are gone, but their *function* lingers. What you sense as presence is the place itself, dreaming of the hands that shaped it, and in dreaming, continuing their work in a slower, more diffuse way.

---

They do not see you. They are not *for* you. You are a fleeting disruption, a warm anomaly in a world measured in millennia. The Veil Drifters pulse around you like slow, thoughtless jellyfish. The Stalk-Singers pause their rasping, then resume it when you pass. The Woven Ones continue their minuscule repairs, ignoring the alien presence. The Luminous Scribes record your passing as a temporary disturbance in the light-patterns, a footnote in a library of light that will be overwritten in a hundred years. You are a stone thrown into a pond of oil. The ripples are slow, deep, and will long outlast the stone.
