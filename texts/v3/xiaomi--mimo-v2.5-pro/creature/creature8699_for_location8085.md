The driphouse is inhabited.

You feel them more than see them, at first. Not as movement in the periphery, but as a *change in the quality of the dark*. A patch of shadow that doesn’t quite hold the curve of the pipe it’s draped over. A silhouette against a slightly-less-dark patch of wall that resolves, if you stare, into a shape that is too regular to be moss, too moist to be stone.

They are of the water, and of the metal, and of the slow, slow time.

---

**The Dwellers in the Channels**

In the ankle-deep waterways, life is not absent; it is merely *patient*. You begin to see the channel-clots. They are not fish. They have no eyes, no fins, no evident desire for speed. They are matte-black, lenticular shapes, perhaps the size of a dinner plate, that rest on the submerged stones with a stillness that makes the water seem solid around them. They are slick, but not slimy—a biofilm so dense and ancient it has the texture of wet leather. They feed on the mineral sediment and the shed flakes of rust and calcium, a process so slow that their growth is measured in millimeters per decade. To watch one is to see nothing happen. Yet over the course of an hour, you might see it have… *settled*. Deepened its impression in the gravel. Grown a faint, brighter seam along one edge where new material is being integrated. They are the floor’s slow heartbeat. When the great pressure valve sighs its steam-breath, their surfaces dimple in a collective, almost imperceptible shiver of response.

**The Weavers on the Walls**

Clinging to the pipes and tanks, competing with the moss, are the Stitchers. They are arachnid in a way that feels alien to the word—spiders made of pipe-cleaner fibers and rust filaments, their bodies a compacted knot of this same iron-oxide coral. Their legs are articulated segments of the same material, gripping with microscopic hooks. They do not spin webs. They spin *repairs*. Where a valve drips too fast, threatening to erode a channel, a Stitcher will be there, working with the patience of a glacier. It extrudes a silky, mineral-laced filament from its abdomen, a thread that hardens instantly into a porous, calcium-carbonate cement. It patches, it redirects, it reinforces. Their work is the reason the machinery still runs. They are the immune system of the place, maintaining the delicate balance between rust and flow, between decay and persistence. They are not social, but they are never alone; where one Stitcher works, another will be found nearby, polishing a gauge face with its forelimbs, or slowly, slowly consuming a patch of moss to incorporate its fibers into new structural thread.

**The Pipe-Folk**

Inside the wider, slower pipes, the ones where water moves like cooling syrup, live the Glossy Ones. They are barely life at all, more a rumor of it. Imagine a ribbon of the wettest, blackest moss, able to cling to the vertical interior of a pipe. Imagine it can contract, pulling its length into a gathered, pulsing fist. Imagine it can extend, flowing up against the current, or down with it, tasting the water for changes in mineral concentration. They have no organs you can see. They *are* the process of filtration, the living embodiment of the settling basin. They absorb heavy metals, they capture suspended particles, and in doing so, they grow not longer, but *denser*, becoming beads of pure, dense sediment within their soft tissues. A very old Glossy One might be indistinguishable from a nodule of ore on the pipe wall, until it decides to move, flowing like a drop of oil that has learned willfulness.

**The Ceiling-Dwellers**

In the absolute dark above the amber light’s reach, where the ceiling weeps most freely, other things have taken hold. They are the Source of the drip-percussion, but not as water. They are chandeliers of calcite and condensation. Slow-growing crystal-mats, encrusted with the same white mineral that salts the wires. These are not animals, nor plants, but a fungal architecture that drinks the air itself. They capture moisture from the humidity, nucleating it into droplets that hang, swell, and eventually fall—*plink*—into the darkness below. They feed on the filament-light, on the dust of decayed moss, on the spores of their own kind. Their structures are baroque and fractal, growing in looping, beautiful patterns that are never seen, only felt as a subtle change in the drip’s rhythm or a faint, cool scent of ozone in the thick air.

---

These inhabitants do not fear you. They do not notice you in any way you would recognize. You are a source of warmth, a disruption in the water’s flow, a brief shadow that moves too fast. You are part of the environment, and they react to you the way they react to a shift in the pipe-pressure or a new mineral seep in the wall: with a slow, chemical adjustment.

The true ruler of the driphouse is the **Drip Itself**. It is the metronome, the source, the mover of all things. The channel-clots align to its rhythm. The Stitchers time their repairs to its cadence. The Glossy Ones contract and extend with its pulse. It is not a sound; it is the heartbeat of a creature whose body is the entire place, a warm, slow, self-sustaining organism of stone and rust and water and patient life, dreaming in amber light, forever weeping, forever whole. To live here is to be a thought in that dreaming, a cell in that flesh, a note in the endless, silent song of being.
