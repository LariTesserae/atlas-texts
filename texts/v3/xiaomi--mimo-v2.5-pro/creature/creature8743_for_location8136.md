The inhabitants of the Lattice Hollows are not conquerors. They are **collaborators with deep time**, shaped by a place that operates on geological patience. Here’s who lives beneath the world:

### 1. **The People of Lath**
They move with **quiet economy**. Their bodies are lean, adapted to the dry air (lips perpetually chapped, skin dusted with fine mineral powder), and their movements are deliberate—no wasted motion, no loud gestures. They are **listeners first**, attuned to the lattice’s subtle vibrations and the distant sounds of industry. Their eyes are sharp in the amber half-light, their voices low and measured; shouting feels obscene here, like tearing a page from a silent book.

- **Age:** Most are middle-aged or older. Children are rare—birth rates are low, perhaps a quiet adaptation to resource scarcity, or a cultural choice. Elders carry the oral histories, spoken in hushed tones as though the lattice itself might overhear.
- **Occupation:** They are **lattice-shapers, metal-weavers, and vent-tenders**. Their hands are calloused from vibration-blades and rivet guns, but they touch the living substrate with a surprising gentleness, as one might touch a sleeping animal. They understand they are guests in the lattice’s body.
- **Culture:** Technology is **reverent but necessary**. No decorative excess—every bolt, every cable serves a purpose. Stories are told in fabrication halls, songs hummed while maintaining turbines. They have a deep superstition about the farthest columns, the ones that blur into the haze: *"Don’t walk where the light forgets your shadow."*
- **Relationship to Surface:** A collective amnesia, almost ceremonial. The surface is a rumor, a discarded myth. When pressed, they shrug: *"Why look up when the ceiling is so high?"* They are not exiles; they are **emergent**, like the lattice itself, grown from the bones of the world.

### 2. **The Drifters**
Pale, translucent, and **philosophically unbothered**. They move in loose clouds across the lattice floor, their six legs tapping a slow rhythm. They are not herd animals; they are a **flow**, a living scum on the mineral tide. If you watch long enough, you’ll see their grazing patterns shift with the lattice’s metabolic cycles—faster during "bright" phases, slower in dimness. They ignore humans entirely, not out of fear, but out of profound disinterest. They are the Hollows’ passive recyclers, trimming the lattice’s edges, keeping it from overgrowing its own light.

### 3. **The Pillarbacks**
Silent climbers, **vertical philosophers**. They cling to the high columns, their broad pads gripping the ridged lattice bark. Their eyes (small, black, reflective) watch everything below, but they descend rarely—only in the dimmest phases, to drink condensation from cold knolls. They click softly to each other across distances, a language of vibration. The people of Lath leave them offerings (scraps of harvested lattice, placed on high ledges), not out of worship, but respect: *"They hold the ceiling up, in their way."*

### 4. **The Singer**
No one has seen it. It lives in the ventilation shafts—the ancient, lattice-grown conduits that breathe air through the Hollows. Its sound is **thin, reedy, intermittent**, like a string stretched too tight and plucked by a draft. It is not a bird; it is not a machine. It is a **lattice-symbiont**, perhaps, or a fungal structure that resonates with air currents. The people call it *The Reminder*. It sings when the lattice’s metabolic hum shifts, as though tuning itself to the planet’s slow pulse. Some nights, it sounds almost like a lullaby.

### 5. **The Lattice Itself**
The deepest inhabitant. It does not *live* in the Hollows; the Hollows *are* its body. Its consciousness (if any) is **tectonic**. It grows toward heat and mineral gradients, its bioluminescence a slow exhalation. It tolerates Lath the way a mountain tolerates a climbing vine: with immense, indifferent patience. But it is not inert. When new structures are bolted into a column, the lattice grows around them over decades, absorbing metal into its mineral matrices. The buildings become part of its skeleton. In time, Lath may be digested—not destroyed, but *incorporated*, another layer in the bone-white architecture of the deep.

---

### Their Shared Existence
Life here is **low-entropy, high-resilience**. Energy is borrowed from the earth’s heat, spent sparingly, returned as faint light. Conflict is rare; resources are too scarce for waste. The people, fauna, and lattice exist in a **mutual truce**, bound by the dry, quiet air and the amber glow that never fully dies.

They are not survivors. They are **residents**, settled into the patient rhythm of a world that builds cathedrals without congregation, lit by a sun that never rises.
