The cirque is not empty. It is **occupied**.

What lives here are not creatures in any way that word is usually understood. They are **processes given persistent, localized form**. They are the symptoms of a reality that hasn't settled, and their collective existence is a kind of slow, choral negotiation with that fact.

First, and most numerous, are the **Basin-breathers**. The matte, nautiloid shapes half-submerged in the moss are not shells housing life; they are the life itself. Each one is a single, sustained exhalation—a focus point where the pressure differential between the rock-memory (still hot, still deep) and the air-memory (still waiting for rain that will never fully stop) is resolved into a slow, warm vapor. They do not feed. They do not reproduce. They simply *dissipate*. Their "engine sound" is the acoustic artifact of each one operating on a slightly different temporal beat, a dissonance born of their link to diverging moments. They are fixed points of release.

In the pools, the **Sine-worms** are not animals. They are **luminous threads of consequence**. Each one traces a single, repeating wave pattern through the water, a physical record of some primordial oscillation—a seismic aftershock, a harmonic from the continental collision that birthed the rock—given tactile form. Their faint ticking is the sound of their pattern being expressed against the surface tension, a perfect, unwavering signal. They illuminate the pathways of what *happened*, rather than what *is*.

The birds on the wall are the **Synchronized Observers**. They are the only beings that seem to actively interface with the place's uncertain geometry. By roosting in a scattered pattern and moving in absolute unison at intervals, they act as a living proofread. Each simultaneous head-turn is a calibration, a way of taking a measurement across the entire basin at once. The sound—a single, definitive *thump*—is the sound of consensus being momentarily achieved. Between those moments, they are silent sentinels, their stillness a form of intense listening to the noise of multiple presents.

The membrane-shapes in the mist are the most difficult. They are **unmade weather**. They are the ghost of the rain that has just stopped, the ghost of the mist that should be rising, the ghost of the cloud that will never form. They hang in the air like paused thoughts, their iridescence a shimmer of potential states (wet, dry, frozen, steamy) refusing to collapse into one. They are not alive. They are the latency of the climate, made briefly, ambiguously visible.

All of them exist in a state of **perfect, separate sync**. The Basin-breathers are tuned to the geothermal lag. The Sine-worms are tuned to the seismic history. The Observers are tuned to the visual dissonance of the walls. The Membranes are tuned to the static meteorology. Their collected sound is not communication, but the audible friction of these separate time-lines rubbing against one another within the confines of the bowl.

They do not compete. They do not interact. They occupy their niches of temporality with monastic dedication. The Basin-breathers will exhale when the Observers turn. The Sine-worms will trace their path as the Membranes shimmer. It is an ecosystem of pure parallel function, a clockwork where every gear is turning, but to the rhythm of a different, equally valid second.

They are the cirque's inhabitants, and its editors, and its proof that even in a place where reality is averaged, *something* must be present to witness the average. They are the living embodiment of the pause. They are the reason the photograph of emergence continues to be held in focus, a chorus of silent, shifting, breathing testament to a moment that will not end. They live in the engine, and their lives are its sound.
