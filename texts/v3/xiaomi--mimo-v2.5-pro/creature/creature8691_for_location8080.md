They are not inhabitants. They are the new architecture, the living systems that have drawn blueprints over the old. To describe what lives here is to describe the hall itself in a state of becoming.

**First, the Sheathing.** This is the foundation—the slow, mineral-loving life that builds upon bone. **Tube worms** (*Serpulidae* of some deep, patient strain) have stacked their calcareous chimneys into buttresses and buttresses into walls. Their bodies are unseen; only the architecture remains, a rigid, white lacework that transforms steel into coral reef. **Barnacles** cluster in geologic densities, their armored cones sealing bolts and rivets, a living stucco on every surface. They are the true masons. **Mussels** (*Mytilidae*) have done the riveting, their byssal threads weaving steel and concrete into a single, cohesive substrate. They are the tapestry, their shells blue-black and gaping just enough for a whisper of gill-cilia to sift the water. Together, they have changed the acoustic and textural identity of the room. The walls do not echo; they absorb, they breathe.

**Then, the Flowers of the Current.** Growing from this sheathing, from the pitted iron and the worm-tubes, are the anemones. They are the hall’s sensory organs, its soft, waving nerve endings. Pale as milk in the grey light, their tentacles do not hunt with aggression but with a slow, hypnotic hunger. They are the primary filter of the living-water, capturing the endless snow of detritus and micro-organisms. Their movement gives the hall its most visible pulse, a slow, floral tide synchronized to the breath of the current.

**The Mechanisms of the Middle Water.** This is where the vertebrate intelligence swims. **The shoals of silver fish**—the perch and roach—are not merely visitors. They are the hall’s circulating blood. They move with a communal, shimmering logic through the forest of turbines, using the shadows for rest and the sudden pockets of clearer water for communication. They are the school; the hall is their body. **The solitary watchers—pike, zander**—are the shadowed sentinels. They have learned the geometry of ambush around every encrusted flange. They are part of the hall’s patience. They are its calculated stillness.

**The Scavengers and the Sealers.** Across the soft floor of silt and shell-fragment, **crabs** move. Their sideways gait is the hall’s maintenance cycle, a slow, eternal patrol. They break down the larger dead, clean the surfaces, and keep the sediment from becoming wholly stagnant. They are the recyclers. And **in the silted corners**, **eels** have become part of the building’s own foundation. They are sinuous roots, buried except for their eyes—ancient, watching, and utterly integrated. They are the hall’s long memory, a predatory stillness that links the buried pipes to the swimming world above.

**The Invisible Inhabitants.** The water itself is alive. A haze of **bacteria** metabolizes the iron bleeding from the walls, creating rust-stained blooms of ochre and oxidizing methane seeping from the deeper mud. **Fungi and microscopic algae** form the biofilm—the slick, invisible skin on everything—turning the hall’s skin into a digestible surface. They are the true foundation, the first colonists, and the final degraders.

And finally, **the Hall itself** has a life. The **low, harmonic moan** is not noise; it is the breath of a new organism, the voice of water and resonant metal and hollow concrete forming a symbiotic chord. The **chain’s bell-like ring** is its irregular heartbeat. The shifting light is its vision.

They live together not as occupants of a ruin, but as a single, collaborative entity. The turbines are the skeleton. The encrusting life is the skin and muscle. The water is the blood and breath. The fish are the thoughts, moving in patterns of instinct and survival. The shadows are the mood.

It is a closed system, a perpetual machine of consumption and decay and renewal, where the original purpose—rotation, power generation—is only a ghost in the geometry. The new purpose is existence: a cool, dark, breathing, singing, and utterly complete world. It asks for nothing, because it has everything it needs. It is, in its own slow-motion way, immortal.
