The Upper Mat does not yield its inhabitants easily. To see them, one must learn to breathe with the plateau's slow rhythm, to interpret the faintest vibrations, and to accept that life here operates on a timescale of patience measured in decades, not days. What lives here is not a collection of creatures, but a single, vast, interconnected process of survival.

**The Foundation (Within the Mat):**
First, there are the **Weavers**. Not creatures of flesh, but of hyphae and root-tip. The fungal networks and the root systems of the mat's vascular plants are in constant, silent negotiation. Certain fungal species form thick, pale cords (larger than a human thumb) that function as both structural reinforcement for the mat's lower layers and as distribution pathways for water and nutrients scavenged from the slowly digesting bedrock. You only see them if you part the upper mosses: luminous, pulsing threads that move with an unsettling, non-cyclical rhythm. They are the mat's nervous system and its plumbing.

**The Shapers (Just Below the Surface):**
This is the realm of the **Grub-Moles**. Arm-length invertebrates, their bodies segmented and muscular, sheathed in a slime that protects them from the cold and aids in burrowing. Their heads are hard, keratinous scoops, and they move with a constant, writhing purpose through the peat-like lower layers, excreting a processed, nutrient-rich mulch as they go. Their tunnels heal almost instantly behind them, the vegetation knitting back together. They are not blind; they sense light and vibration through a series of simple eyespots along their anterior segments. They flee the rare surface disturbances, not upwards, but *deeper*, into the compressed, woody heart of the mat where predators cannot follow. Their sounds are the wet, shifting noises heard from below—the mat digesting in its sleep.

**The Emergers (Of the Upper Surface):**
*   **Mist-Flies:** The tiny, singing insects. Their wings are vestigial for true flight; instead, they are elongated, thin, and used to vibrate at specific frequencies to create their high-pitched hum, a form of long-distance communication in the fog. Their bodies are dark and hydrophobic. They feed on the microscopic algae and bacteria that bloom on the mat's surface during condensation events, using needle-thin proboscises. Their emergence is synchronized, not with a sun they never see, but with the precise light spectrum that filters through a thinning cloud layer.
*   **Cinder-Mites:** Barely visible, these arachnid-like creatures move with frantic speed over the mosses. Their carapaces are crystalline, breaking up the diffuse light and making them appear as fleeting glints. They prey on Mist-Flies and scavenge anything the mat exudes. In deep darkness, their bodies hold a faint residual glow from feeding on bioluminescent moss spores.
*   **The Moss-Stalkers:** The predators. They are furred, low-slung, with six legs that end in wide, velvety pads, allowing them to move soundlessly and distribute their weight to avoid breaking the mat's surface skin. Their fur is the exact shade of wet, dark green moss. They have no eyes. Instead, a dense array of vibrissae-like hairs across their snouts and chins reads the air pressure changes and minute vibrations of their prey. They hunt during the cacophony of a Mist-Fly emergence, their movements a flowing, silent shadow against the moss. Their kills are precise and silent: a quick dip of the head, a click of shearing mandibles hidden behind fleshy, gripping lips. They are solitary, marking their vast territories with secretions from chin glands that only others of their kind can detect—a scent that the wind-roar dilutes, but never fully erases.

**The Remnant Dwellers (Of the Scattered Things):**
In and around the collapsed structures, a different ecology thrives. **Bone-Grubs**, white and blind, slowly consume the ancient wooden frames and leather hides, their gut biomes breaking down complex polymers the rest of the plateau cannot. Where they chew, they create tiny pockets of uniquely processed soil. **Notch-Mice** are small, nimble rodents (or rodent-like things) with prehensile tails. They are the inheritors of the tool-users' legacy, not of intellect, but of opportunity. They nest in the gaps between collapsed hide and stone, lining their dens with shredded cordage and soft moss. They are master scavengers, stealing from the Cinder-Mites and cracking open the tough shells of a specific beetle that feeds on lichen-covered stone. Their most striking adaptation is their teeth: one set for gnawing, another, smaller set near the back for etching fine, meaningless lines into old bone and new stone—a ghostly, instinctual echo of the markings left by those who came before.

**The Unseen Commuters:**
There is a **Fog-Siphoner**. It is less a distinct creature and more a temporary phenomenon of the mat itself. During the deepest condensation events, certain pools of liquid in the mat's shallow depressions do not simply soak away. They *cohere*. A pseudopod of water, tensile and strange, rises a meter or so into the air, as if tasting the fog. It may hold this shape for an hour, attracting countless Mist-Flies who drink from it, before collapsing back into the mat with a soft, collective sigh. Is it an organism? A symbiotic behavior? A property of the water? The plateau does not explain.

**The Great Absence:**
There are no herbivores in the mammalian sense. Nothing grazes. The mat is too valuable, too structural, too much a part of the architecture of survival. All life here takes from its edges, its waste, its gaseous exhalations, or from each other. The mat is the floor, the shelter, the climate, and the foundation. It is the first and greatest resident, and everything else is a guest of its slow, breathing body, shaped by its pulses, dependent on its damp, cold, eternal growth.
