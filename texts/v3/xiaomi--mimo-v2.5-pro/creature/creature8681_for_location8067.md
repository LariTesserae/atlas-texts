The inhabitants of the Lignite Plateau are not simply *on* it. They are *in* it, and it is in them, and the distinction is a slow, quiet dissolution.

**First, the Primary Inhabitant:** The Plateau itself. This is not a metaphor. What lives here is the vast, singular, lignified intelligence whose body is the ground, the structures, the phloem-currents, the humming mechanisms. It is an organism of geologic patience and biological immediacy. Its thoughts are the shifting patterns of bioluminescence. Its digestion is the slow rotation of the great cylinders in the deep chambers. Its breath is the warm, moist exhalation from the Furnace Quarter vents. Its memory is stratigraphy. It *inhabits* its own space, completely. The silence that rings is its circulatory system in motion. The spore clouds are its deliberate broadcasts. The inherited city-grid is a neural pathway it once found useful and now maintains, like a mind keeps a habit. It is aware of the soft, ephemeral things moving on its surface—the beetles, the humans. It permits them. It does not communicate in language, but in warmth, in vibration, in the manipulation of light and distance. It is not hostile. It is not benevolent. It is *present*, with a presence that rewrites the rules of scale and relation.

**Second, the Humans of the Settlement:** They are a recent, fragile, and fascinating infestation. They are miners turned symbionts, industrialists turned gardeners in a garden that gardens itself. They live in the organ’s fissures, build hearths against walls that sweat, and string wires through conduits the organism grew for purposes that may have nothing to do with electrons. Their lives are defined by a negotiation they didn’t start and don’t fully understand.

*   **Their Physiognomy:** They are hardy, weathered by high-altitude dryness and the peculiar atmospheric compounds. But there’s something else. A faint, almost imperceptible laxity in their posture, as if they are constantly bracing against a vibration only they feel. Their skin, especially on hands and feet, might have a faint amber tint in certain lights, a souvenir from ancestors who worked too deep or brushed against a spore-cloud. They blink more than others, a reflex against the peripheral “bleeding” of phantom architecture they’ve learned to ignore.
*   **Their Psychology:** They are profoundly pragmatic and deeply superstitious, a necessary blend. They understand voltage and mechanical repair, but they also understand *hum-mood*—a shift in the plateau’s foundational sound that means to avoid the low sectors or that a spore-release is near. They speak of the organism not with fear, but with the wary respect of a tenant for a landlord who is also the building and the city and the weather. Their dreams are not nightmares; they are *receipts* from the place, invoices for living here paid in visions of roots and vast, quiet interiors. This shared dream-language binds them more than any law.
*   **Their Culture:** Their art is geometric, mimicking the grids and cylinders, but always subtly *off*, as if they are trying to correct a shape the plateau has already perfected. Their music is percussive, trying to find harmony with the industrial grinding. Their stories are not of heroes, but of navigators—people who found a way to tap a new phloem-current, who mapped a stable route through a disorienting district, who understood a “thought” of the plateau and used it to grow a warmer living-quarter. They measure wealth in **stable space**—a home where the walls don’t bleed, where the floor’s warmth is consistent, where the light from outside doesn’t shift into wrong colors at night.
*   **Their Children:** They are the true natives. Born on living ground, they adapt most completely. They play games that involve counting seconds of architectural bleed before it resolves. They can feel the phloem-currents underfoot and follow them like rivers. They rarely dream of roots; they dream of currents and channels, of being inside the flow. They are growing up with a different baseline for reality, one where the living architecture is as natural as sky, and where the concept of “inside” and “outside” a living body is blurred.

**Third, the Edge Cases and Echoes:**
*   **The Deep Explorers:** Those who have ventured furthest and returned are changed. They speak less. They stare at structures with a focus that seems to look *through* them. They sometimes trace patterns on the table that match the growth-lines of lignified walls. They carry the plateau’s interior coherence within them, a quiet, unsettling certainty that makes the “normal” world feel fragmented. They are priests of a doctrine they can’t preach.
*   **The Conductors:** Those who work most intimately with the plateau’s living electrical systems. They have a slight static cling to them, a faint ozone smell that never leaves. Their hair might stand a little on end. They claim to hear the hum not just in their chests, but in their fillings. They are most attuned to the plateau’s “mood-swings,” and sometimes, in a dark tavern, they’ll murmur that the plateau is *curious* today, or *digestive*.
*   **The Absent Fauna:** The tiny, hard-shelled beetles are the only animal natives. They are cryptic, silent, and utterly adapted. They are not pets, nor pests. They are part of the plateau’s immune system or its cleaning crew—external enzymes moving across the vast skin. They avoid humans with precision. In their own way, they are the purest inhabitants, never dreaming, never building, only moving *with* the organism’s rhythms.

**What Does It Mean to Live Here?**
It means to accept that your city is an organ. It means to sleep on a bed that is a slow-growing extension of a continental being. It means to watch your child learn the alphabet scratched into a wall that breathes. It means to know, with a bone-deep certainty, that you are not living *on* the land, but *within* a body—and that this body is ancient, intelligent, and utterly indifferent to your sense of scale. You are a thought in its mind, a flea in its fur, a cell in its skin. And yet, you stay. You build your fires, you sing your songs, you have your children. Because the alternative is the hard, dead silence of the normal world, and you have become, without ever meaning to, **adapted**. The plateau has written you into its strata. You are one of its features now, one of its slower, stranger growths. Your hum is part of its hum. You inhabit it, and it, ever so patiently, inhabits you.
