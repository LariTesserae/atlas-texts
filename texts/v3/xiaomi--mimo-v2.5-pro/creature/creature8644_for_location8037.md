The cathedral breathes, and in its breath, the congregation moves.

They are not a community in any way a surface-dweller would understand. There is no society, no hierarchy, only a manifold, interlocking existence. They are the choir, the architecture, the relics, and the saints of this place, all at once. Their lives are not measured in days or years, but in pulses of water, in the slow, eternal rain of sustenance from the world above, in the millimeter creep of silica.

**The Saints in the Walls** are the oldest and most passive. They are the bryozoan fans, the hydroid forests, the terraced cities of the polychaete worms. They have anchored themselves and built their homes from the very substance of the cathedral—calcium carbonate shells rising like minarets from the glassy lace. They are filter-feeders, patient as stone, their existence a slow-motion festival of capture. Their only motion is the wave of a tentacle, the opening of a lophophore, a gesture as rhythmic and involuntary as a heartbeat. They are the ornamentation, the living bas-relief, the texture of the walls. To be a saint here is to be immobile, to become the house you built.

**The Penitents in the Water** are the restless ones. The amphipods are the cathedral’s diligent custodians, scraping biofilm from spicules with meticulous, jerky motions. The copepods are its dust, its dandruff, its cloud of incense—a countless swarm that makes the water itself seem alive, a grainy, shimmering medium between the solid structures. They drift in the pulsing currents, eternally grooming the structure, eternally being groomed by the mouths of others. They are the living circulation, the blood of the city.

**The Silent Priests** are the watchers and the waiters. The bobbit worms, hidden deep in their tubes, are patient theologians of ambush. Their mandible-tipped snares are sermons in the art of sudden, final grasping. The pale anemones, with their sweep tentacles, are the silent confessors—a brittle star that wanders too close experiences a mild, unforgettable sting, a reminder of boundaries. These priests do not preach; they enact. Their doctrine is written in the slow, centimeter-scale ballet of risk and avoidance that plays out across the walls, a drama older than any human story.

**The Glass-Blooded Scions** are the strangest inheritors. The small snailfish, translucent and gelatinous, drift like ghosts of ice. They seem less like animals and more like coherent thoughts of the cold, their metabolisms dialed down to a whisper. They are the aristocracy of energy conservation, lords of a kingdom where calories are precious and movement is an extravagant tax.

And then there are **The Unseen,** the true rulers. The nematodes in the sediment, the bacteria on every surface, the microbial film that is the foundational cuisine. They are the substrate of faith, the reason the cathedral exists. They are processed by the bean-sized holothurians—living stomachs of the city, endlessly recycling the fallen into soil. They are the alchemists turning death into life, the secret engine turning in the dark.

They do not speak. Their language is chemistry: a pheromone trail here, a warning toxin there, a spawning hormone that ignites the water once a generation. They listen with their skin, with their water-current-sensitive cells, with their entire being. The cathedral’s breath is their weather. The distant, sifting rain of marine snow is their manna.

What lives here, then, is not a collection of individuals, but a single, slow-motion entity. It is a beast made of glass and flesh, whose bones are sponge, whose blood is copepod and amphipod, whose thoughts are chemical reactions, whose lifespan is geologic. It is building itself, always. An inward-growing sponge here will, in a century, carve a new room. A worm dies and its chimney becomes a foundation for a bryozoan. A brittle star sheds an arm to escape an anemone; the arm regrows, the anemone digests the lost limb.

It is a community of absolutes. Absolute silence, broken only by the intimate sounds of your own body. Absolute patience, their lives paced to a rhythm of forty-second breaths and twelve-thousand-year accretions. Absolute intimacy, every creature touching, growing on, or hunting another. Absolute darkness, save for the frantic, fitful blue cries of the *Euphausia*—a light that illuminates only to reveal more darkness, more structure, more endless, breathing city.

You are not a visitor. You are a passing shadow. A temporary pressure wave. A strange, large bubble in their fluid world. They will not remember you. They will simply, slowly, grow over the space you occupied, and the silence will seal you out, as seamlessly as it sealed you in. What lives here is the cathedral itself, and it has lived here long before your kind breathed air, and will live here long after.
