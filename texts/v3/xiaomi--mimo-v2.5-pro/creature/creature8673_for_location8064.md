The Saturated Cirque breathes.

Not with lungs, but with moss. Its life is measured in centimeters gained over centuries, in water tracing paths through a living sponge a hundred generations deep. This place does not host life so much as **is** life, condensed and slow and utterly, profoundly green.

At its foundation, the **Foundation-Moss** (Sphagnum, Polytrichum, and a dozen others whose names are lost to this altitude) is not a carpet but a civilization. It is a meter-thick, waterlogged metropolis of stems and leaves, living at its bright green crown and dying into compressed, brown peat at its heart. It does not grow *on* the rock; it has consumed it, building its own topography. Its citizens are invisible: countless mites, springtails, and nematodes in a realm of capillary water, tending to decay and renewal in a slow-motion frenzy your eyes cannot perceive. It is the bedrock, the aquifer, the silent, respiring ground.

Clinging to and growing from this foundation, the **Veil-Weavers** (hepatics, leafy liverworts like *Porella* and *Scapania*) are the atmosphere made tangible. They hang in translucent, emerald curtains, each frond a masterpiece of chlorophyll and intricate geometry. They are the architects of the shadows, creating the sense of enclosure, of corridor and chamber. Their growth is a creeping, perpetual unraveling. They do not rustle; they ooze, extending their edges into the damp air, splitting and branching, colonizing their own dead predecessors. They are the walls, the ceilings, the green stained-glass windows of this cathedral.

Reigning above them in the steepest, most windswept zones, the **Lichen-Spires** (crustose and fruticose lichens like *Stereocaulon* and *Cladonia*) are the slow-time royalty. Pale grey, almost white, they grow in branching, coral-like structures or scurfy crusts that are less alive and more *occurring*—a partnership between fungus and alga so ancient it is a process unto itself. They are the barren, skeletal trees of this forest, reaching for a sun they will never feel, their growth so slow that a human lifetime would see no change. They are the sentinels, marking time in millimeters per century.

Punctuating this monochrome are the **Blood-Jewels** (sphagnum mosses of the sect. *Acutifolia*, like *S. capillifolium* and *S. subnitens*). In the lower, wetter basins where the water table is highest, they explode in patches of startling crimson, gold, and ochre. They are the only warm notes in the cirque's composition, vibrant and alive. And among them, the **Glistening Hunters** (*Drosera*, the sundews) raise their sticky-leaved stems. They are the cirque's sole acknowledged predators, slow and magnificent, their tentacles glistening with mucilage to trap the tiny midges that drift through the heavy air—a feast measured in weeks.

And the fauna? They are ghosts, whispers. **The Unseen Host** (micro-invertebrates, nematode worms, rotifers) inhabits the water film between moss cells, a universe of microscopic life. **The Flickering Shade** (a possible salamander or newt, likely *Salamandrella* or a similar cold-adapted species) moves through the deepest peat only when you are not looking, a single, pale vertebrate dream in the vegetative silence. The **Dancers in the Mist** (chironomid midges) are almost the only winged things, their tiny forms a cloud of life that feeds the sundews and, in their larval stage, the mites below.

They are all here. But the overwhelming truth of the Saturated Cirque is that the plants are not the environment *for* these creatures. The plants are the primary inhabitants, the conscious-feeling entities of this place. The animals are tenants, fleeting and marginal, living in the cracks and hollows of a world defined by vegetative will. The cirque's life is the **Slow Convulsion of Growth**—the constant, imperceptible heave of moss expanding, the silent, drenching fall of condensation, the century-long thought of a lichen. It is an ancient, saturated mind, dreaming in green and peat and patient, patient water. It does not need to move. It is already everywhere it will ever be.
