The inhabitants of the Roar Alcove are not visitors. They are participants in its slow, resonant existence.

**The Stone Listeners.**
First are the moths. Not ordinary moths, but something quieter, older in their adaptation. By day, they are not so much resting as *merged* with the ceiling stone. Their wings are the exact color of wet basalt, patterned with lichen-like splotches of grey and blackish-green. They do not flee when the light enters; they do not flutter. They are patient as the rock they cling to. To call them moths is a convenience. They are more like the alcove’s nerve endings, tuned to the vibration. When the wind shifts and the drone deepens, a faint, almost imperceptible shudder might pass through them, a tremor of acknowledgement. They are the sound made form, waiting.

**The Pool Keepers.**
In the brackish seepage, the amphipod is less a creature and more a timepiece. Translucent, its internal organs visible like a ghostly blueprint, it navigates the shivering water with an economy of motion that is profound. It is not hurried; it is not still. It *pulses*, each flex of its tiny legs a measured beat against the alcove’s deep rhythm. It consumes the microscopic life that the vibration loosens from the stone—the only food source in the perfect darkness. It is the alcove’s metabolism, its solitary, minuscule heart. Its existence is a testament to what can thrive in resonance, in patience, in a world of constant sound and scarce light.

**The Rooted Memories.**
The moss and liverworts and filmy ferns are not just vegetation; they are the alcove’s long-term memory. They grow towards the vibration as other plants grow towards the sun. Their damp, black-green is the color of remembered tides. In their dense, soft cushions, they trap echoes. The fern fronds, in their slow unfurling, trace the pathways of the condensation and the way the sound curves around the walls. They are the alcove’s skin, sensitive to the moisture the drone pulls from the air. Touch a patch of moss and you feel not just wetness, but the stone’s own cold, held and softened over centuries.

**The Stain of Attentive Ones.**
The shelf and the smoke stain are not merely marks. They are a presence. Someone, many someones, came here. They were not casual. They carved the ledge with tools of other stone, a labor measured in days and seasons of their short lives, to create a place to sit in the dark and be filled by the sound. They breathed the damp air, exhaled smoke that has now become a dark ghost on the rock. They are not ghosts who linger with purpose; they are an imprint of a *practice*. Their presence is the residue of focused listening. They came to be unmade by the sound, to let it scour away the small noise of their own lives. The empty shelf is a throne for absence, an invitation to the same dissolution.

**The Voice Itself.**
And finally, there is the resident that *is* the alcove: the Tone. It is not an event; it is a state of being. It is the geological patience of the basalt made audible, the pressure of the sea and wind translated into a hum that has outlasted generations of the attentive ones. It is a slow, constant breath taken by the earth. The amphipod moves in its trough. The moths vibrate in sympathy. The moss drinks its moisture. The empty shelf holds the space for listening. All of them—life and trace and stone—are kept in this resonance, not as a cacophony, but as a harmony. They are the components of an instrument that has been playing itself for millennia, and will continue long after the headland is gone. They do not live *in* the alcove. They live *of* it.
