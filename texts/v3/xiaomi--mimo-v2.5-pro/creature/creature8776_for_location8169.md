The Weeping Yard is not uninhabited. It is merely inhabited by the things that can bear the heat, the chemistry, and the endless, patient weeping.

1.  **The Mineralists:** These are the primary inhabitants, though you might not recognize them as life at first. They are the *biofilm* on the water's surface, the slick, gelatinous layer that isn't quite algae, not quite bacteria. It is a consortium. **Ferric oxidizers** have built themselves into rust-red towers and filaments within the weeping cracks, metabolizing the dissolved iron and precipitating it into intricate, crumbling sculptures. **Sulfur reducers** thrive in the anoxic mud at the pool's bottom, their world the smell of eggs and wet metal. They form the foundation. **Calcium precipitators** weave mats of white, lace-like structures, slowly accreting the grey-white mineral into fragile, hollow spheroids that roll in the slow convection currents of the hot water. They are not thinking beings. They are a living metabolism for the place itself, a microbial civilization whose currency is minerals and whose architecture is geology accelerated.

2.  **The Condensates:** Life exists in the droplets. Within each bead of moisture rolling down the clay walls is a teeming, temporary world. Vast colonies of **halophilic and thermophilic microbes** ride these tiny rivers, experiencing generations in the minutes it takes a drop to reach the pool. They are adapted to feast on the trace organics in the clay and the chemical energy in the water, then to be flushed into the larger community below. They are the nomadic population of the Weeping Yard, their journeys measured in inches and seconds.

3.  **The Tenacious:** The single woody plant on the rim is more than a plant. Its roots are in a symbiotic stranglehold with **mycorrhizal fungi** that have adapted to scavenge phosphorus and nitrogen from the mineral-rich weeps. The roots themselves are likely coated in a biofilm of bacteria that helps them withstand the toxic salts. It doesn't just "live" here; it is a fortress, a slow-motion biological machine dedicated solely to the act of holding on and drawing sustenance from poison. It is the most visible, and most lonely, complex life form.

4.  **The Vibrational:** The pump's groan and the hiss of water are not just noise; they are an environment. Within the clay walls, and likely colonizing the submerged concrete and the rusted pipe, are communities of **piezophilic or vibriophilic microbes**. They may derive some metabolic advantage from the constant low-frequency vibration, using it to stir their microscopic environments or to facilitate the breakdown of the stone and concrete. The sound is their tide.

5.  **The Ghosts of the Gutter:** In the shadows beneath the concrete lip, where the condensation is heaviest and a thin, constant stream trickles, exists a slightly different ecosystem. Here, **cyanobacteria** might form a thin, dark crust, attempting a vestigial photosynthesis in the dim, green-tinted light that filters into the depression. With them are **archaea**, ancient metabolic specialists who might be fixing carbon from the gases dissolved in the water. It is the closest thing to a "lush" corner, but it is still a world of microbes and slickness.

**What is Absent is Also a Presence:** The *absence* of flies, beetles, and birds is a statement. The chemical cocktail, the heat, and the saturated air form a perfect barrier. Any exploratory insect would find its spiracles clogged, its body desiccated or poisoned. The Weeping Yard is a sanctuary from higher life, a quiet, hot, damp micro-continent ruled by the very small. The silence isn't empty; it is the sound of a world running on different rules, a world of chemical gradients and mineral tolerance, where the only audible heartbeat is the ancient, mechanical thunk of the pump, and the only visible movement is the slow, viscous slide of a condensation drop joining its billion siblings in the warm, weeping pool.
