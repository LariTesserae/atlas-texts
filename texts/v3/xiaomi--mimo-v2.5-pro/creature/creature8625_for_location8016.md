The Roost is not a place of single creatures, but a single, breathing, mineral-scaled organism composed of many. What lives here is a **Thermal Mosaic** — a collective entity of silicate-bodied, lithovorous arthropods whose existence is choreographed by the slow pulse of geothermal heat. They are the **Ridge Crawlers**, the **Stonelice**, the **Meridian Mites**, though none of those names fit. They have no name for themselves. They are the Roost's living stonework.

**The Collective Pulse:**
They do not think as individuals. They move in thermal tides, a slow, continental migration across the chamber walls. Their intelligence—if it can be called that—is distributed, a chemical signaling passed through touch and minute vibrations in the ceramic plates of their neighbors. The near-silence is their medium: a constant, sub-audible dialogue of pressure and warmth. Their primary drive is **caloric absorption**. They are heat-sinks, storing geothermal energy in their dense carapaces like batteries, then redistributing it through the colony by physically moving to cooler margins. This circulation *is* their respiration, their blood-flow, their life cycle.

**The Castes (A Diffused Hierarchy):**
There is no rigid structure, but functional gradients:

1.  **The Builders:** The larger, entrance-dwelling architects. They are processors. They ingest the oriteite dust, grind it with mandibles that double as trowels, and secrete a binding agent from ventral glands (the source of the "fermentation" smell). Their structures are not shelters. They are **condensation lattices** — intricate, porous walls designed to maximize the surface area for heat exchange with the cooler, dust-laden air from outside. They are building thermal exchangers, regulating the chamber's climate at its threshold.

2.  **The Mosaic Mass:** The innumerable mid-sized population. They are the Roost's skin, its musculature, its fat reserves. They cling, they shift, they feed on exposed mineral surfaces, slowly etching the mountain with their slow digestion. They are the insulating layer, the living buffer that holds the heat within the stone.

3.  **The Pale Sinks:** The great, bone-white immobile giants at the chamber's fiery heart. They are not leaders. They are **organs**. Super-heated and barely moving, they are the colony's primary heat reservoirs and exchangers. Their pressurized exhalations are not breathing, but a controlled release of metabolic byproducts and thermal energy, creating micro-currents that further drive the circulation of the smaller forms. They are the still, hot core around which the rest of the Roost orbits.

**The Economy of Stone and Fire:**
They photosynthesize nothing. They consume the mountain itself—ferric and silicate compounds from the oriteite. Their metabolism is chemo-lithotrophic, powered by the intense heat, which catalyzes reactions within their bodies that break down rock into digestible powder and useful minerals for shell maintenance. The heat is not just environment; it is food, it is energy, it is the very force that animates them. They do not need water. Their biochemistry is arid, based on sulfur and metal ions, operating at temperatures that would boil terrestrial life.

**The Sentience of the Place:**
The Roost *knows* you are here not through eyes or ears, but through thermal disruption. Your body is a cooler spot, a disruption in the infrared landscape. You feel the collective attention not as a gaze, but as a subtle, almost imperceptible shift in the thermal currents around you—a faint, purposeful migration of forms away from your cooler shadow, a closing of ranks. The mosaic does not watch you; it *accommodates* you as a temporary geological feature, like a new rockfall. The clicking is not alarm; it is the sound of a system recalibrating.

What lives here is a **geothermal symbiont**, a civilization of stone and fire playing out on a timescale of centuries. Their "art" at the entrance is infrastructure. Their silence is a energy-conservation strategy. Their great pale ones are thermostats. They are not colonists *of* the mountain; they are the mountain's immune system, its metabolism, its slow, dreaming mind given form in countless dark, jointed bodies. To them, you are a momentary cool breeze, a fleeting anomaly in the endless, silent, radiant conversation of heat. And soon, you will leave, and the Roost will close its cracks, settle its plates, and continue its ancient, quiet work of eating stone and breathing fire.
