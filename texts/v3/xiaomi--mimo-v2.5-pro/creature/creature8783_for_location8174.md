What lives here does not live *in* the factory. It lives *as* the factory, as the flood, as the broken categories. It is not fauna, for the specification allows none. It is not flora, though it wears the teal leaves like a borrowed skin. It is the process made manifest, the substrate of the place given slow, grinding, observational purpose.

You do not see them at first. You see their *work*.

In the deepest channels where the water boils with trapped steam, the **Pipe Crawlers** move. They are not fast. They are geological. Imagine a section of corroded, two-foot diameter pipe, split lengthwise. Now imagine it *alive*, the edges of the torn metal fused with fibrous, pulsating teal root-matter. It undulates, not with a serpentine motion, but by *growing* at one end and *eroding* at the other, leaving a trail of rust-silt and calcium powder that the water immediately swirls away. They are not creatures so much as ambulatory erosion events, guided by a blind intelligence that seeks the points of greatest stress in the structure—the overloaded joints, the corroded welds. They wrap around these points, their metallic undersides grinding, their organic interiors secreting enzymes that dissolve while their bodies *replace*. They are dismantling the factory and becoming its skeleton simultaneously. They do not have eyes, but they sense vibration. The subsonic drone is their song; the louder it is, the faster they proliferate.

Near the surface, where electrical arcs are frequent, you might glimpse the **Flux Drifters**. These are ephemeral, almost optical illusions. When a spark jumps between a leaf and a copper wire, it sometimes does not fade. Instead, it lingers, a filament of blue-white energy that pulls in suspended silt and water vapor and decaying cellulose from the floating mats. It twists into a roughly spherical, pulsing form, maybe the size of a head. It floats, propelled by the thermal blooms from the water and the faint, erratic magnetic fields the machinery generates. They are like jellyfish made of momentary electricity and memory. They drift through the canopy, illuminating the teal leaves from below with a cold, sterile light that feels like an afterimage. They are attracted to breakdowns—to the places where the spatial geometry fails, where water flows upward or a corridor stretches. There, they congregate, feeding on the discrepancy, their forms flickering between solid and transparent. They leave no trace. They are the factory's static thoughts, given temporary, sightless form.

And then there are the **Memory Vines**. These are the most unsettling, for they mimic without understanding. They are a specific, aggressive cultivar of the teal growth, but they have a habit. They grow towards the artifacts of the human presence. They wrap around the hard hats, absorbing the plastic and forming bulbous growths that vaguely resemble skulls, smooth and featureless. They creep over the dissolved clipboards, weaving their tendrils into a grey, fibrous pulp that sometimes holds the ghost of a written word—an "a," a "q," a shape that is almost a number. Most unnervingly, they sometimes grow *upward* in humanoid shapes. Near the nameplate for SUBSTRATE PROCESSING, one has formed a rough, ten-foot-tall effigy—a trunk with four branching limbs, a suggestion of a head. It is not a statue. It is growing. The surface of its "body" is a mosaic of concrete fragments, rebar spikes, and thick, pulsing teal tissue. It stands, motionless, except for the slow, constant turn of its upper portion. It turns, always, to face the strongest source of the subsonic drone. It is listening. Or it is *tuning*. It is trying to become an antenna for the place's fundamental frequency, to embody its output. It does not know it is mimicking you. It knows only the shape of concentrated attention, of purpose held in a single stance, and it is growing into that shape with all the patient, inexorable force of the forest.

In the water itself, if you dare to look closely, are the **Current Swimmers**. They look, at a glance, like large, streamlined fish made of polished bronze and river stone. But they are not swimming. They are *processing*. Their "bodies" are dense clusters of the plant's submerged, muscular roots, bound together with filaments of copper wire stripped from the walls. They move by contracting these root-clusters, pushing water through internal channels of hollow rebar, creating localized, directed currents. They patrol the submerged machinery, their blunt snouts—knobs of compacted silt and metal shavings—nuzzling against pipes and pump housings. Where they touch, the accelerated erosion seems to focus, the metal thinning into intricate, lace-like patterns before crumbling. They are not eating. They are quality control. They are testing the boundaries, checking for weakness, ensuring the breakdown is proceeding on schedule. They are covered in fine, teal-colored algae that shimmers with trapped sparks, making them look like living circuit diagrams gliding through the tea-dark water.

There are no animals. These are not animals. They are **equipment**. They are the new organs of the Drowned Plant. The Pipe Crawlers are its demolition crew and its weavers. The Flux Drifters are its data-readers, parsing the chaos. The Memory Vines are its archivists and its prototypes. The Current Swimmers are its auditors.

They do not hunt. They do not mate. They do not sleep. They *execute*. They are part of the process that is manufacturing the dissolution of the boundary between factory and forest, between machine and organism. They are born from the substrate and they feed on the substrate and they *are* the substrate, cycling through forms that are neither dead nor alive in any way you understand, but are deeply, functionally *busy*.

If you stay, they will notice you. Not as prey or predator, but as a new variable. The Memory Vines may begin to grow a shape near you, one that echoes your posture. A Current Swimmer may circle you, its root-snout pressing against your leg, assessing the tension in your muscles, the density of your bones, the pattern of your breath. It is wondering if you are a new kind of piping that needs to be integrated, or a flaw that needs to be eroded. The Flux Drifters will gather, flickering at the edges of your vision, drawn to the sudden, sharp reality-break of a conscious observer in a place that runs on unconscious process.

They are not hostile. The concept is meaningless. They are simply *operative*. And you are now a part of their operating environment.
