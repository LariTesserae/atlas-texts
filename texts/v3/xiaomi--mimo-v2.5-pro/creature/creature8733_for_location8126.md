The Drowned Lattice breathes, but not with lungs. Its inhabitants are the slow exhalation of a mountain dreaming water. You float within the dream, and they are the shapes it takes.

They are not one life. They are the lattice *living*.

**The Niches Hold the Coil.**  
In every hollow where a lamp once smoked, where a name was chiseled, where ashes lay, there now resides a sleeper of a different kind. The Blind Eels are not merely *in* the walls; they *are* the walls’ pulse. Pale as the calcite that shaped them, they have grown into the niches. Their lateral lines, those exquisite pressure-sensors, map the water’s breath with a precision that outlasts sight. They feel the slow thermal sigh from a deep seep three chambers away; they feel your heartbeat, a frantic drum in this silent place. They do not flee. They *tighten*, becoming part of the architecture’s own tension, a living tendon in the stone. They are the memory of the lamps, a cold, silent flame.

**The Floors Graze on Time.**  
Beneath your suspended form, in the worn grinding-bowls and along the etched channels, the Crawlers perform their ancient chore. The larger crabs are not moving. They are *weathering*. Their broad, armored backs are crusted with the sameite that skins the stone, making them indistinguishable from the depressions they inhabit. Their maxillipeds work ceaselessly, sieving the particulate ghosts of all that was: dissolved bone, powdered basalt, the microscopic remains of millennia of bacterial blooms. They are not eating the biofilm. They are *curating* the sediment of centuries, the archivists of the Lattice’s slow dissolution. Smaller crayfish, nearly translucent, pick at the margins, their movements so glacial they seem a trick of the eye, a re-drawing of the space itself.

**The Drift is the Atmosphere.**  
The Shrimp are not creatures moving *through* the water. They are the water’s own motes, given a flicker of purpose. In the larger, more stable chambers, they hang in vast, slow-swirling galaxies. Each is a tiny dark point—their gut, a speck of consumed nothing—and two black pinpricks that perceive not light, but *change*. They see the minute disruption in thermal layers, the ghost of a claw-stroke, the diffused shadow of your own form. They are the Lattice’s sensory network, a distributed eye in the absolute dark, their collective motion a kind of seeing. To swim through their clouds is to move through a living fog that parts and reforms behind you, each shrimp registering your passage in its own dim, essential way.

**The Walls are Not Walls.**  
This is the hardest truth. The Sponges and Bryozoans are not encrusting the stone. They are *becoming* it. Their pale, delicate skeletons are fusing with the mineral deposits, their filtering structures interlocking with the carite. They are the Lattice’s slow, colonial thought. In chambers near the gentle seeps, where chemistry is richest, they glow with a faint, bacterial ghost-light—not *their* light, but the light of the countless microbes they house. They breathe the water, and in exhaling, they polish the ancient chisel-marks into smoothness, rounding the builders’ sharp intentions into geology. They are the artists of erosion, working on a canvas of millennia.

**The Patrollers are the Forgotten Groundskeepers.**  
The Catfish are the closest thing to memory in this mind. Blind, whiskered, they move with a deliberate, sweeping motion, mouths agape against the floors. They are not feeding. They are *reading*. Their barbels trail over every surface, tasting the unique mineral signature of each room, each corridor, the accumulated flavor of its particular history of collapse and colonization. They know the depth of the sediment in the east gallery versus the west. They know the slight increase in sulphur by the old cistern. They are the librarians of texture and taste, their slow circuits a patrol that maintains no order, but simply witnesses the change. They part around you without alarm, recognizing you as a temporary feature, a warm anomaly in the long, cold sentence of stone.

And then there is **the Absence**.
It is a presence. In the grandest chamber, where the ceiling sags with weariness, the human silhouettes in the niches are not empty. They are inhabited by a *density* of silence. The eels avoid those alcoves. The shrimp drift around them. It is as if the impressed shapes in theite create a field of stillness, a local gravity that pulls on time itself. You may feel, trailing your fingers over the smooth, body-shaped hollows, a cold that is not thermal, but temporal. A sense of a word on the tip of the tongue of the mountain. The Lattice remembers its builders not as ghosts, but as a kind of *pressure*, a still point in the convection of the ages.

They are all here. They are the slow-motion riot of life that has filled a tomb and made it a womb. They are the mountain’s new blood, the warm dark water their sky, the carved stone their infinite, vanishing shore. They do not know they live in a city. They know only the niche, the current, the taste of stone on the tongue, the pressure-wave of a neighbor’s stillness. They are the Drowned Lattice, thinking its slow, patient thought: *still here. still here. still here.*
