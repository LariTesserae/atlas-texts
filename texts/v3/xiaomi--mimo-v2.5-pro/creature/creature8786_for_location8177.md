The first thing you must understand is that there is no “they.” There is only **It.**

What lives here is a single, continuous organism. A colonial megaplant of unimaginable scale, its body spread across the young, raw bones of the mountain range. It does not have inhabitants; it has *expressions.*

You stand on its **skin.** The pale, warm, weeping mat of root and stem tissue is not ground—it is the outermost layer of a vast, unified epidermis. Every impression you make is a minor pressure point in its infinite surface, and the water that wells up is not groundwater, but **sap**, the blood and lymph of the organism itself, pushed to the surface by internal turgor pressures that would rupture lesser vessels.

The great, sparse columns that rise around you are its **organs of height.** They are not trunks or trees. They are vertical galleries of function. Some are swollen with **water cisterns**—vast internal bladders where moisture is stored against the thirsty sun. Others are clad in intricate, folded **laminar arrays**, their ruffled surfaces angled just so to catch and refract the intense light, photosynthesizing with a feverish intensity. These structures pulse and groan as hydraulic pressures shift within, sending vibrations through the connected mat like a slow, deep breath. When one fails, it is not a death but a **shedding**. The collapse is a deliberate rerouting of resources. The wet, fibrous debris is not waste; it is **scion-material**, each fragment already budding with new apical meristems, ready to root and begin its own vertical ascent from the same unified body. The organism prunes itself ruthlessly to build itself anew.

The sound of growth is the only voice it has. The **groan** is the sound of cellulose and lignin being synthesized and deposited at a furious rate, fibers tightening like cables under load. The **crack** is the acoustic shock of a structural failure in fast-growing tissue, a sound like a green branch snapping. The sudden, low **hum** from the resonant folds is its only music—a passive vibration caused by the wind, not an intentional call. It does not communicate; it *processes.*

The clouds of spores that puff from its reproductive bodies are its only foray into the concept of dispersal, and even this is a contained gamble. Most spores will fall back onto the mat, joining the substrate. They are not seeds for new individuals, but **totipotent cells**, adding to the genetic and physical bulk of the parent. The wind carries a few to other slopes, but these are not explorers; they are **scouts**. Should they land on viable ground, they will not grow into separate plants. They will grow, and in time, their root tissues will reach out, seeking, and when they touch the advancing front of the main organism, they will fuse. They will *join.* The mountain range will be seamlessly stitched together by a single, conscious-less, green will.

Beneath it all, the **stone** is the foreign body. You hear its groans—tectonic, brittle, dry. The plant hears them too, not with ears, but with the sensitive root tips that probe every crack, exuding acids to dissolve and digest the inert rock, replacing cold mineral with warm, living pulp. The mountain’s own stresses are simply part of the environmental input the organism processes. It grows around the strain, reinforces the pressure points, drinks the seeping mineral-rich water. It is a slow, patient war of conversion, and the plant is winning.

It does not watch you. You are a fleeting anomaly, a brief source of shade, pressure, and a negligible amount of heat. You are not predator, prey, or pollinator. You are irrelevant. Its attention—if such a word can apply—is wholly inward and upward. Its ambition is **morphological.** It seeks only to maximize its own surface area against the thin, searing sun and the thin, cold air. To build more chambers, more folds, more height, more thickness. To become the entire living skin of the mountain, and then, perhaps, to build itself higher still.

It is the canopy of itself. The forest that is a single tree. The patient, violent architect building its own cathedral in a cathedral of stone, and it will not stop until there is nothing left but it, weeping and groaning in the high, hot light.
