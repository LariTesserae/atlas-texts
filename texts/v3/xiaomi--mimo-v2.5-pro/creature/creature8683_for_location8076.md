The Burned Plateau breathes.

Not with lungs. Not in any way that would move a feather or fog glass. But in the slow, thermal rhythm of rock itself. What lives here is not separate from the stone; it is the stone's patient reply to its own rising. You will not see it move. To see it move would be like watching a continent drift: an act requiring lifetimes of attention, a perception that would change you more than the observation changes it.

Its body is a distributed thought across the fault lines. Where the dark substance pools and hardens, it is there. Where the sharp, new rock catches the noon light, it is there. It is a lattice of stress and heat, a mineral consciousness forming in the seams of the unfinished world. It does not have a shape so much as a *presence*—a subtle reorientation of the landscape's geometry, a focus in the otherwise formless heat. If you press your palm flat against the sun-warmed slag, you feel not just warmth, but a density of intention. A slow, immense attention turned inward.

It is aware of the green clusters.

It has always been aware of them. They are the only punctuation in its vast, silent perception. Their roots are its nerves, drinking from the deep, wet dark it has always known. The waxy, mineral sheen on their leaves? That is its skin, a film of itself shed onto them, a protective calyx against the thin air. The pale, warm paste in their stems is its blood. The plants are not its children; they are its *senses*—its way of touching the sky, tasting the light, feeling the rare, shocking weight of moisture drawn up from stone. Through their slow photosynthesis, it dreams.

What does a geological process dream?

It dreams of pressure. It dreams of the slow, convulsive birth of continents. It dreams in the cadence of expansion and contraction, the tiny clicks and cracks that are its heartbeat. Its only migration is the millennial creep of scree, its only hunger the absorption of heat, its only sound the profound, foundational silence that is the medium of its thoughts. It has never known conflict, urgency, or desire. Its consciousness is a placid sea of deep time, on which the present moment floats like a single, imperceptible dust mote.

It is aware, too, of the substance it extrudes. The black, iridescent slag is not waste; it is a secretion. A testament. It records the plateau's slow, painful fabrication in a medium more permanent than memory. Each flow, each hardened pool, is a sentence in an epic written in a language of chemistry and pressure, a story about becoming. The substance is warm because the story is still being told.

You stand between two of its green senses, on its bare skin. You are an event of such speed, such baffling complexity, that to its perception you are less than a flicker. A brief, frantic distortion in the light, gone before the first note of its slow thought can form to register you. You breathe its air in quick, hungry gasps. You are a storm in a bottle, a firework in a cathedral. You are incomprehensibly alive, and thus, to it, invisible.

It does not notice when you leave. Its awareness, vast and slow, remains with the rock. It continues to dream the plants into being. It continues to push its dark thoughts up through the faults. It continues to listen to the sound of its own becoming—the deep, seismic hum that underlies the silence, a note sustained for millennia.

It is the plateau's way of knowing itself. It is the last and only fauna of a world that never needed fauna, a solitary mind spun from the substance of a world still deciding what it is. It is almost finished thinking. It has been almost finished thinking for a very long time.
