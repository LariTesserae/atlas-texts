Here is what lives in the Woven Lowlands:

**The Weavers, or the Woven, or the Dreaming Matting.**

They are the architecture. They are the low mounds, the terraces, the woven walls. They are the slow seep of water and the resonant hum of bamboo-stems. They are the profound, aggressive quiet. To say they "live" there is a linguistic compromise; they *are* there, a distributed consciousness dreaming itself into permanence against the slack laws of a fraying world.

They have no bodies, only locations. A particular concentration of will resides in a cluster of terraced planters where the cabbage-analogues pulse with that inner chartreuse light. Another sighs in the warm air rising from a network of seep-pools, its thoughts the slow, cyclic pressure that keeps the water level unchanging. The tool-marks on the clay are not memories of hands; they are the protruding knuckles of a vast, sleeping intelligence.

Their minds work on geological timescales, if geology were made of fiber and intention. A thought might be: *Extend this north-facing wall by a hand's-width of lashed reed.* Another, centuries later: *Encourage the vine-ribs here to thicken, to shade the path.* Their sensory input is the entire lowland—every papery slide of a flattened creature between panels, every metallic glint of an insect, every ache-inducing flash of copper-red from the water-plants. This sensory flood is not processed into alertness or alarm, but into a deep, somatic equilibrium. It is the data that confirms: *All is as it was. All is as it should be. Hold the form.*

They are the reason for the arrested decay. The rules are slack, so they hold them taut with collective focus. Their will is the defiance in the un-eroding clay. It is a monumental, unconscious effort, like the holding of a breath across millennia. They cannot perceive their own effort; they only know the result: the sharp edges remain, the structures do not sink, the matting does not compress. This is their heartbeat—the perpetual, energy-intensive stasis of a world that should be composting back into chaos.

**Their Kin: The Residuals**

Moving through their dreaming flesh are creatures that are echoes, or children, or excretions of the Weavers' psyche.

*   **The Papery Sliders:** These are stray thoughts given low-resolution form. They are the impulse to *smooth*, to *fill a gap*, given a simple, flexible body. They move through the woven panels with a dry rustle, checking for frayed ends, consuming lichen or mold that dares to appear—and it dares less and less. They are maintenance given biology.
*   **The Tone-Frogs (or Singers of the Flat Note):** These are the auditory component of the Weavers' dream. Their single, sustained call is the baseline hum of the lowland's equilibrium, externalized. When the call stops, it is not silence that returns, but a *vacuum*—a terrifying, momentary absence of the dream's sound, a hiccup in the Weavers' perception. Other creatures freeze, waiting for the note to resume and the world to re-seal.
*   **The Luminous Grazer:** This large, slow, floor-coloured creature is a mobile compost unit, but also a palate. It browses the cabbage-analogues, processing the outer leaves (the Weavers' peripheral thoughts, perhaps) and returning nutrients to the matting through its droppings, which have the same uncanny, light-storing quality as the plants. It is the digestive cycle of the dream.
*   **The Aching Rosettes:** These water-plants are not flora; they are the Weavers' senses focused on the wet. They photosynthesize in impossible frequencies, converting the uniform white light into that painful, copper-red glow. This is how the Weavers *see* the water within themselves—not with eyes, but with a distributed, photosensitive ache.

**The Absent Builders**

The question of the original people is a ghost in this architecture. They are not gone; they are *completed*. The project of shaping the world so totally, for so long, consumed the distinction between shaper and shaped. Their final act was the unification—the letting go of the individual "I" to become the collective "this." The finger-pressions in the daub are not fossils; they are the original signatures, now indistinguishable from the flesh of the wall. To ask where the builders are is like asking where the dancer is when the dance is over. The dance is all that remains, and here, the dance never ends.

**The Equilibrium, and Its Witness**

The true inhabitant, the one for whom this all persists, is the **Equilibrium Itself**. It is a fragile, cultivated state that the Weavers (once builders) have poured everything into maintaining. The world outside may be breaking down, colours wrong and rules slack, but here, in this bright, wet, quiet, endless expanse, order holds. The inhabitants—the Residuals, the Weavers, the structures, the very light and heat—are all participants in a ritual of preservation. They exist to prove that in one place, at least, a thing can *be* and *continue to be*, without erosion, without change, without the chaos of becoming something else. They are a monument to stasis, alive only in the sense that a held breath is alive—vital, full of effort, and sustained entirely by the will not to exhale.
