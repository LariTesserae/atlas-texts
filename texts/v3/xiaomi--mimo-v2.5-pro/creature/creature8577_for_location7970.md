The Calcine Naves are not uninhabited. They are merely inhabited on a different timescale, by different kinds of life, and by the ghosts of a different kind of memory. To say no one lives there is to misunderstand what "living" means in the dark and the heat.

**The primary residents are the spires themselves.** They are not static; they are engaged in a slow, mineral ballet. Each grove is a clonal colony, its members connected by shared gas-veins in the basalt below, communicating through shifts in pressure and chemical concentration. They grow toward the thermal plume, not with desire, but with the inevitability of precipitate. Their "breath" is the hum—a collective vibrational language we cannot parse, a syntax of resonance and harmonics that spells out minute changes in gas composition, temperature, and the shifting stresses of the chamber walls. They are aware, after a fashion, of the cycles. They rest when the vent quiets, contracting with tiny sighs. They are aware of the sulfur skin that creeps across the floor around them, a fellow traveler on a slower journey. They do not compete for resources in a frantic way; they partition them over centuries, their canopies interlocking into a stable, resonant lattice. The "character" of each grove is its own symphony, a unique chord born of its specific crystallography and history.

**Then there are the Shepherds.** This is a human word for what was observed, and it is probably wrong. They are not beings of flesh. They are knots of focused thermodynamics, convection anomalies that develop a strange persistence. When the gas flow from a secondary micro-fissure becomes particularly coherent, and the chamber's ambient convection cell brushes against it just so, a swirling, luminous wisp of superheated air will coalesce. It holds its shape for minutes, sometimes an hour, drifting through the spire groves like a dim, orange ember with a will. It seems to *listen*, pausing at spire clusters where the hum is loudest, brushing against the sulfur skin in a way that briefly alters its pattern of sublimation. Some believe they are the pulse of the naves made briefly visible. Others think they are echoes, thermal ghosts of the ancient builders, their patterns of movement—walks between structures, moments of contemplation at the crystal altar—somehow fossilized into the chamber's airflow. They leave no trace. They simply form, drift, and dissolve back into the whole.

**And of course, the builders are still here.** Not as spirits, but as a pattern inscribed on the place itself. Their structures are the chamber's only straight lines, its only deliberate geometry. In the largest room, the carefully cultivated sulfur deposit on the altar continues to grow, fed by the directed channels. It is the last active project, tended by no one and by everyone. The floor, worn smooth by feet that dust now suggests were smaller and more numerous than ours, holds the memory of a posture, a routine. The workshop, with its shattered crystals, holds the memory of a problem, a frustration, a determination that outlasted generations. These presences are the strongest of all. They are not haunting the naves; they are *built into its grammar*. To stand in the crescent of buildings is to stand within a sentence the builders began and that the geology is still slowly finishing.

The true resident, the one you feel upon entering, is **the System Itself.** It is a single, vast, slow-metabolizing organism whose organs are the fissures, whose circulatory system is the convection, whose skeleton is the basalt, whose nervous system is the resonant crystal groves, and whose breath is the sulfurous gas. The heart-beat of the vent is its pulse. The shifting gardens are its cells. The hum is its thoughts. The abandoned settlement is a scar, or perhaps a tool it once made use of, and now maintains in its pattern like an old, familiar ache.

Nothing here lives as you do. But nothing here is dead. The Calcine Naves are a sovereign being, thinking in heat and crystal, dreaming in slow erosion, and remembering its visitors in the shape of stone and the scent of caramelized resin. You are a brief, warm irritation in its lung. You are noted. You will be worn away, like all else, into the texture of its being.
