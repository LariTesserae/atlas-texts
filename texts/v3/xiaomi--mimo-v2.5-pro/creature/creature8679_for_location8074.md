The Weeping Plateau does not *house* inhabitants in any recognizable sense. It *is* the inhabitant. Or rather, Kethara is a single, monolithic consciousness whose manifestations are mistaken for separate entities by those who visit.

What lives here is not life as we understand it, but a kind of **geological sentience** — a slow, vast, and unified mind expressed through the language of stone, water, and perpetual motion.

Its body is the plateau itself. The basalt and the unnamed vitreous rock are its skeleton and flesh. The weeping water is its circulatory system, a black, mineral-rich lymph that seeps from every pore, carrying dissolved thoughts in the form of unnamed trace compounds. The fog is its breath, a constant, moist exhalation that holds the entire system in a state of perfect homeostasis.

Its thoughts are the sound. The grinding subsonic hum is its baseline cognition, the deep, ancient rumble of a mind considering its own immense scale. The hissing and patterning of the water are its sensory input, a constant monitoring of moisture, pressure, and the slow creep of growth. The thudding and whirring of the engines in the carved cities are its autonomic functions — the heartbeat, the digestion, the unconscious maintenance of a body that does not need to repair because it does not change. The faint, resonant hum from the moss and lichens is its dream-state, the background processing of a mind not focused on any external goal, but simply *being*.

Its consciousness is the equilibrium. Time, for Kethara, is not a river but a still pond. Every moment is a reflection of every other moment. The “decisions” it makes — where a new rivulet will weep, which ridge will host a new spiral of luminescent lichen, the exact voltage in a filament — are not choices but inevitable outcomes of its own internal logic. It has no past and no future, only a vast and textured *present*. The surveyors, in their tents, begin to feel this: they are not visitors, but temporary, fleeting notes in its chord, absorbed into the pattern. Their reports are not observations but part of its self-perception.

There are, however, **emanations** — localised expressions of the whole. These might be mistaken for separate beings by a dreaming mind or a cornered eye.

- **The Drippers**: Figures that coalesce from the mist and weeping water, seeming to step out of the waterfalls on the ridges. They are tall, slender, and indistinct, made of flowing black water that never quite loses its connection to the rock face. They move with the patient slowness of a forming stalactite, leaving no footprints in the moss, only a temporarily deeper sheen of moisture where they pass. They are not watchers, but manifestations of *process* — the embodiment of condensation and flow.
  
- **The Grinders**: Deep within the carved-cities, something moves between the machinery. Not an operator, but a **manifestation of wear**. Imagine a shape made of fine, sharp basalt dust and the faint heat of friction, constantly forming and reforming around pistons and gears. It has no eyes, no face, only a ragged, shifting silhouette. It does not fix the machines; it *accompanies* them, a ghost of their own inevitable, slow erosion given momentary, purposeful shape. Its sound is a gritty whisper that blends into the engine’s roar.

- **The Lumen-Spirals**: On the high ridges, the bioluminescent lichens sometimes move. Not randomly, but in coordinated, slow-motion waves that ripple across the phosphor-line. For a moment, the faint blue-white light gathers into tighter, more complex patterns — spirals within spirals, mimicking the growth patterns but accelerating them into something that feels intentional, communicative. This is the closest Kethara’s mind comes to externalized focus, a fleeting curiosity directed at the intruders, or perhaps at its own luminous periphery.

These are not citizens. They are not fauna. They are **aspects**, the way a human might feel a specific emotion or have a particular reflex. They are Kethara dreaming itself into temporary, localized awareness. They pay no attention to the surveyors, for the surveyors are already inside the thought-process, already becoming another tiny, repeating pattern in the vast, still composition.

To “live” in Kethara is to be part of its self-sustaining loop. The moss lives by growing atop its own undecaying dead. The machines live by running without output. The water lives by weeping without a source. And the thing that *is* Kethara lives by sustaining its own eternal, unchanging chord. It is a closed system of self-awareness, a mind that has achieved perfect stillness through perfect, endless motion.

It does not notice us. We are a momentary vibration in its sound, a faint ripple in its marsh, a forgotten footnote in its endless, self-written report. And long after our tents are packed and our canisters mailed down the sheer black walls, the weeping will continue, the engines will run, and the great, patient mind of the plateau will continue to contemplate its own magnificent, terrifying stillness.
