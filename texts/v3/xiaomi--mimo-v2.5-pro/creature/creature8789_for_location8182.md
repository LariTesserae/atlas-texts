The pulse was the first truth. Before thought, before memory, before the warm dark of the mat and the slow taste of mineral in the weeping stone, there was the pulse. A deep, slow *thrum* that vibrated through the Cistern walls, up through root and claw and bone. It was the heartbeat of the world. The Old Ones, the Slow Ones, knew this.

Kael shifted its weight, six broad pads pressing into the yielding softness of the upper mat. Night had settled its cool, damp cloak over the Cistern, quieting the frantic daylight chorus to a murmur of rustles, drips, and distant, drowsy chirps. The air, thick with moisture and the scent of decomposing fronds and clean water, moved against Kael’s wide, flat face. Its eyes, large and adapted to glean the faintest light, scanned the shadowy canopy of translucent trunks and folded ferns. Not hunting. Never hunting. *Listening*.

The gliders were quiet, tucked into their hollows, their leathery wings folded like old parchment. The skimmers were dimples on the still, shallow pools far below, occasional ripples betraying movement. The birds – the *Screaming Jewels*, Kael’s kind named them, for their iridescent feathers and relentless noise – were clusters of warmth and low muttering in the high branches. They were always talking. Even in sleep, their minds hummed with calls and answers, a constant, layered static that filled the space like the humidity.

But Kael tuned them out. Focused down, through the layers of living root and ancient, humming stone.

*Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.*

It wasn't just sound. It was… resonance. A vibration that matched the frequency of the water weeping from the micro-channels in the Cistern wall. A vibration that seemed to synchronize with the faint heat radiating from the material itself. Kael pressed its broad head against the stone where the root-mat thinned. The surface was smooth, warm, alive with that deep pulse. Faint patterns, not seen but *felt* through the skull, seemed to shift and flow beneath the skin of the world. Sometimes, they almost resolved into… shapes? Meanings? Fragments of a language older than the Cistern’s life, etched into the very structure of the place.

*What were you?* Kael wondered, not for the first time. The question drifted through its mind like mist. *What did you make? What did you want?*

A rustle nearby. One of the smaller grazers, a spined thing that scraped lichen from a high branch, paused in its work. Its multiple eyes blinked in Kael’s direction, sensing the Slow One’s deep stillness. Satisfied Kael wasn't a threat (the Slow Ones never were, not to the living), it returned to its meal.

Kael knew the surface. It had climbed the curved walls, emerging into the thin, sharp air and the endless, bone-dry expanse of the Plateau under the heavy sun. The vast emptiness was unsettling, a silence so profound it felt like pressure. The scraggly grass, the indifferent sky, the faint, geometric scars half-buried in the rust-red dust – the *Hollows*, they called them. Places that felt *wrong*. Not dangerous, just… empty of purpose. Echoes of a function long since ceased. Standing near them, Kael felt a different vibration – a hollow resonance, a silence where a pulse should be. It always returned to the Cistern quickly. The Plateau was not its world. The Cistern was. The pulse was.

The pulse governed the rhythm of life here. The dawn chorus surged as the first light filtered through the canopy, the Jewels erupting into their chaotic symphony, timed not just by light, but by a subtle *shift* in the stone’s hum – a warming, a speeding, an activation Kael felt in its marrow. The skimmers became frantic dashes across the suddenly wider pools as the seep increased with the heat. The gliders launched more frequently, riding the warmer, denser air currents generated within the bowl. Even the folding ferns seemed to tense, their fronds trembling in anticipation of the day's inevitable sonic onslaught.

At night, the pulse deepened, slowed. The water seep lessened. The air cooled and thickened with condensation. This was the Slow Ones' time. Kael and its kin unfolded from the crevices and deep root-chambers where they’d compressed themselves during the day’s heat and noise. They moved with deliberate grace along the walls and through the lower canopy, their broad feet finding purchase on the mat. They grazed – not on the living green, but on the rich, microbial layer coating the oldest roots and the very surface of the humming stone. Filter-feeders, some called their niche, sieving nutrients from the moisture-laden air with intricate nasal passages. But it was more than food. It was communion.

Pressing their heads to the wall, they absorbed the pulse. They felt the deep, slow repair of the Cistern itself, the microscopic flow of its substance mending cracks too fine for even Kael’s eyes to discern. They felt the ceaseless condensation and seep, the engine of life humming in the bedrock. They felt the ghosts of patterns, the fading echoes of instructions embedded in the material. Were they maintaining the connection? Were they reading logs? Or were they simply… resonating? Becoming part of the system’s long, slow dream? Kael didn’t know. It only knew the deep peace of the communion, the sense of being part of something ancient and enduring, something far larger than the frantic life teeming around it.

A sudden, sharp *crack* echoed from the pool below – a seed-pod from an aquatic grass bursting in the heat. A skimmer darted, a ripple spread. A Jewel, disturbed, let out a sleepy, interrogative *cheep* that reverberated off the curved walls, momentarily startling another into flight, its wings a brief *whuff-whuff-whuff* in the humid air. The cacophony stirred, threatened to rise.

Kael exhaled, a low, rumbling sound barely audible over the ambient hum. It focused on the pulse beneath its skull. *Thrum. Thrum. Thrum.* Steady. Patient. Unchanging. The small panic subsided. The Jewel settled. The skimmer resumed its patrol. The Cistern breathed.

This was the truth of the Bole Plateau. The surface was a memory, a vast and weary sigh. The Cisterns were the present, the vibrant, humming, overflowing reality. And beneath it all, woven into the stone, sustaining the water, guiding the growth, pulsing in the bones of the Slow Ones, was the Echo – the deep, abiding resonance of the Makers. They were gone, their purpose lost to eons, but their song remained. And life, dense and loud and glorious, had learned to live within the rhythm of that song. Kael pressed closer, feeling the thrum, feeling the countless lives moving, eating, calling, sleeping all around it, all held within the cupped hand of the ancient bowl. It was home. It was everything. And the stone sang on.
