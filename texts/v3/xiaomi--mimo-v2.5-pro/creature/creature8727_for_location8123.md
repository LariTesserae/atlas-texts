What lives in the Lit Chamber Below the Basalt is this:

1.  **The Biofilms:** Thin, slick, whispering life. They are the visible concession to biology, clinging to the margins where heat, water, and mineral meet their limits. Visible as faint smears on the lower basalt columns – pale orange, grey-white, sometimes a sulfurous green. They exist in specific, austere zones:
    *   **The Waterlines:** Where the shallow pools lap against dark stone, forming a delicate, living tidemark.
    *   **The Drip Paths:** Following the trails of condensation down the columns, catching moisture and dissolved minerals as they descend.
    *   **The Drain Margins:** Skirting the edges of the northeast crack where the water vanishes, the last outpost before the unknown depths below.
    These are the thermophiles, the archaea and bacteria. They are not abundant, not lush. They are a bare whisper of metabolism, sustained not by the impossible light radiating beneath them, but by the chemical energy held in the warm, mineral-rich water and the heat itself. They represent life distilled to its most extreme essence: finding a foothold in gradients of temperature and chemistry too harsh for anything else. They are the chamber’s true colonists, its oldest and most persistent residents.

2.  **The Light:** It is not merely illumination; it is an *occupant*. It fills the space with a constant, unwavering presence. It lives in the substrate, breathes with it. It has its own slow rhythm – a tidal pulse of brightness, perhaps ten or fifteen percent over hours or days. It is the dominant force, the primary element that defines the atmosphere. It is not *used* by the biofilms (or if it is, they show no sign), but it saturates them, the water, the air, the very feeling of the space. It is a passive, radiant entity, the chamber’s luminous soul.

3.  **The Water:** It is the chamber’s blood and sweat and breath. It cycles endlessly:
    *   **The Condensation:** The slow weeping of the ceiling and upper walls, the air surrendering its moisture to cooler stone.
    *   **The Seepage:** The warm, sulfuric percolation through basalt cracks from the world above.
    *   **The Substrate's Sweat:** Micro-droplets coalescing directly from the glowing floor.
    It gathers in the shallow pools, glows strangely from below, steams continuously, and vanishes into the northeast crack. It is the primary transport medium, moving heat and minerals, shaping the substrate over time, sustaining the biofilms. Its rhythms – swelling with heat, receding with cooler cycles – are fundamental to the chamber’s dynamic pulse.

4.  **The Heat:** It is the foundational life-force. Rising from the luminous substrate, it saturates the air, drives the convection, warms the water, enables the light. It is the chamber’s metabolism, the engine of its existence. Without this relentless, geothermal warmth, the light would not glow, the water would not steam, the biofilms would perish, and the air would still. It is the silent, oppressive, essential presence that makes everything else possible.

5.  **The Chamber Itself:** In the deepest sense, the entire system lives. It is a single, complex, slow-moving organism:
    *   **Its Skeleton:** The ancient basalt columns, dark and porous, providing structure and surface.
    *   **Its Luminous Flesh:** The self-illuminating substrate, warm, radiant, pulsing faintly with captured energy.
    *   **Its Circulatory System:** The water, constantly circulating through condensation, seepage, pooling, and drainage.
    *   **Its Respiration:** The slow convection cycle breathing through the chimney – warm, moist air rising, cooler air descending.
    *   **Its Gut Flora:** The thin biofilms, processing minerals in the water.
    *   **Its Consciousness:** The pervasive, steady light – the way it *knows* itself and shows itself.
    *   **Its Pulse:** The faint tidal rhythm of the light's intensity, lagging slightly behind the water's own cycle.

It breathes (the air exchange). It sweats (the substrate and condensation). It has a slow, geothermal heartbeat (the substrate's thermal flux). It maintains a hot, wet, luminous interior distinct from the world above. It shows no evidence of complex animal life, no insects, no fungi – only the barest microbial whisper at its edges. Its true inhabitants are the elements themselves, transformed and animated by the deep earth's heat into a strange, quiet, self-sustaining vitality. It is a pocket of life where life is defined not by complexity, but by persistence under extremes: light without sun, heat without fire, water without rain, biology without respiration or photosynthesis as we know it.

What lives there is the place itself: a geothermal, luminous, hydrated, slowly breathing entity, hosting only the simplest of microbes in its margins, focused entirely on its own quiet, radiant existence.
