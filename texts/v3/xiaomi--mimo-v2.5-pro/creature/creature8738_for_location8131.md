The Basalt Plain of Urrath breathes. Not with lungs, but with the slow, subsonic inhalation of tectonic plates and the mineral exhalation of its inhabitants. Here is who, and what, calls this roof of the world home:

**The Lithomorphs: The Moving Geology**
They are the pulse of the plain. Not fauna, but something more ancient—a direct lineage of the stone that learned a different kind of patience.
*   **The Grazers (The Great Silent):** Imagine hills that walk. These are multi-limbed, broad-backed behemoths of layered calcium-silicate, their surfaces etched with the same thermal channels as the basalt beneath them. They move in herds, their passage felt as a deep, resonant vibration in the rock long before they are seen. Their rasping mouthparts, like natural grinding stones, strip mineral crusts with a sound like distant, slow sandpaper. They stand motionless for days, digesting, their dorsal surfaces shimmering with waste heat—a faint, localized mirage.
*   **The Skitterers (Dust-Between):** No larger than a human hand, these are the fast, the curious, the janitors. They dart between the legs of the grazers, their ceramic carapaces flashing pale grey or ferric red. They feed on the mineral-ite shed by the giants, and on microscopic mineral blooms that form on sun-warmed stone. In their swarms, they look like living scree, their movement a silent, coordinated ripple across the rock.
*   **The Predators (Crystal-Jawed Shadows):** Lean, angular, and utterly silent until the strike. They are ambush incarnate, often indistinguishable from jagged rock outcrops. Their bodies are dark basalt, but their jaws are complex structures of interlocking crystal quartz and harder silicate. A strike is a single, sharp *CRACK*—ceramic shattering ceramic, a sound that is the biome's loudest punctuation before the silence folds back in. They hunt the grazers and, occasionally, each other.
*   **The Symbiotes & Scavengers:** Visible only upon close inspection, these are the micro-fauna. **Ride-cleansers** are tiny, multi-legged forms that cling to the grazers, removing encrustations that might impede movement or heat dispersal. **Vent-clouds** are not single organisms but swarms of microscopic, motile mineral grains that feed on geothermal gases and carcass residues, forming visible, drifting grey hazes around thermal vents or kills.

**The People of the Mesa (The Carvers)**
They are the intelligence of Urrath, shaped by the same deep time.
*   **Physiology:** They are tall and slender, with dense bones and large, dark-adapted eyes that see well in the amber twilight. Their respiratory systems are highly efficient, filtering mineral particulates and extracting oxygen from the thin air. Their skin often has a faint, polished-stone quality, a natural adaptation to the abrasive dust.
*   **Culture & Cities:** Their civilization is a manifestation of geology. Cities are not *on* the mesas, but *of* them—terraces carved with meticulous patience over generations, surfaces glazed to hold heat. Their philosophy is one of **slow integration**. They do not conquer the stone; they join it. Their history is written in the strata of new terraces and the depth of their carvings.
*   **Technology:** It is **litho-ceramic**. Conductive mineral threads carry geothermal energy. Tools and vehicles are crafted from composites of native stone and processed silicates. Their great vehicles, crawling between cities on grooved tracks, resemble metallic beetles, but their substance is ceramic and mineral composite, designed to be part of the landscape.
*   **Sound & Art:** They are a quiet people, their languages rich in subsonic frequencies that can travel through the stone itself. Their art is monumental and patient: vast sculptures that are designed to erode over centuries into new forms, or "sound-stones" placed in patterns to create harmonic resonances when the wind blows. Their music is felt in the bones, a vibration that speaks of the land's own rhythm.

**The Unseen Inhabitants**
*   **The Brine-Minds:** Deep within certain ancient salt pans, in the slow, hyper-saline brines, there are conjectures of a different kind of awareness. A distributed, chemical consciousness that perceives the world in gradients of mineral saturation and temperature change, operating on a timescale that makes even the lithomorphs seem hurried. They are not communicated with, only theorized about—a slow, deep dream beneath the crust.
*   **The Dust:** In the thin air, there are always suspended mineral particles. In a very real sense, the atmosphere itself is a collective organism—the combined exhalation of the vents, the erosion of the mesas, and the metabolic byproducts of the lithomorphs. It is the connective medium, the shared breath of all Urrath's inhabitants.

**What Lives Here, Ultimately:**
A **patient synergy**. The Carvers build their cities from the mesa, the lithomorphs walk the stone that is their food and body, the predators maintain the balance, the microscopic life cycles the minerals, and the deep, slow geology provides the stage and the energy. They are all part of a single, continental-scale organism that thinks in epochs, breathes in centuries, and finds its symphony in the subsonic hum of a world that has finished most of its work, but has not, by any means, finished its life. They are the quiet, living proof that aliveness does not require green, or water, or haste. It only requires a purpose, and time enough to fulfill it.
