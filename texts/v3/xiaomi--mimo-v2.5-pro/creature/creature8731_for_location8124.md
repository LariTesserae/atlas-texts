What lives in The Carving? **The Builders.**

Not as inhabitants, but as its *purpose*, its animating principle. They are the reason for the damp, the sound, the endless grooves and chambers. They are the flesh and sinew of the architecture itself.

**Form (Glimpsed):**
You never see them whole. Movement is crouched, knees pressed to damp floors or wedged into alcoves. Limbs are thick, powerful, ending in hands that dominate the field of vision – broad palms, blunt fingers with knuckles like river stones, nails worn to nubs or deliberately chipped to useful edges. Skin, where visible, is the color and texture of the half-set wall material: pale grey, slightly yielding, laced with faint veins of the bioluminescent mineral, perhaps deliberately embedded. Faces remain turned away, bowed in concentration over tool or surface; you catch a heavy brow ridge, the thick column of a neck, nothing more. They move with a dense, grounded deliberation, as if gravity clings to them more strongly. They leave no personal trace beyond the work itself – no discarded tools, no waste material, no scent unique to an individual. They are friction, pressure, focused erosion and construction made flesh.

**Presence (Felt):**
Their presence is the *tik-tik-tik*, the scrape, the low hum. It’s the source of the overwhelming sound that permeates every surface. It’s the warm dampness radiating from recently worked walls. It’s the air pushed through the wall-cavities by their breath, carrying distorted vocalizations – short, sharp bursts of sound that feel less like language and more like the sonic equivalent of a tool-strike: precise, functional, communicative only in the barest sense of timing, location, or warning. There is no idle chatter. Every sound has purpose: signal, rhythm, coordination. Their conversations are conducted *through* the stone, vibrations passing between them via the medium they shape. You feel their attention only in the negative space of your own irrelevance. They do not ignore you; you simply do not register on their plane of existence, which is entirely consumed by the act of making and refining the space.

**Relationship to the Space:**
They are not building a home; they are performing the act of building as their fundamental state of being. The Carving is not a project with an end point; it is the ongoing manifestation of their essence. They don't *inhabit* chambers; they *are* the process occurring within them. The distinction between Builder and Building blurs. A wall softened by recent work might be the result of a Builder’s presence moments ago, its residual warmth and dampness a lingering echo of their body heat and labor. When a passage is reshaped, it is because a Builder decided its form was insufficient, the need for alteration as instinctive as breathing. Their tools are extensions of their hands, the wedge and scraper merely harder, more efficient edges for the same fundamental action: shaping the substance of their world, which is also the substance of themselves.

**Other Life:**
The small, pale, eyeless things scuttling in the ceiling grooves are not Builders. They are vermin, margin-dwellers, insignificant specks of unmade life thriving in the damp margins and forgotten channels. They are tolerated, ignored, incidental as dust motes in a sunbeam. They hold no meaning for the Builders, who are concerned only with the deliberate, the crafted, the *made*. The Builders are the only true "life" here in the sense of intentional agency. Everything else, including the moisture, the light, the very air, is a consequence of their work, a byproduct of the structure they endlessly forge.

**The Essence:**
To encounter a Builder is not to meet a person. It is to witness a force of nature contained in a vaguely humanoid frame – the geological patience of erosion combined with the frenetic energy of creation. They are the physical manifestation of the Carving's motto: *Emerging / Under Construction*. They exist in a state of perpetual becoming, and their existence *is* the becoming of the space. They are the damp, the sound, the warmth, the vibration. They are the reason the walls insist on your attention. They are the architects of the Braille-grid underfoot. They live not *in* the place, but *as* its relentless, uncaring, deafening heartbeat. They are the Carving, and the Carving is them, and neither will ever be finished.
