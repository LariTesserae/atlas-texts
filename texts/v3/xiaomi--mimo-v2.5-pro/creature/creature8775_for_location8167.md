Based on the profound description of the Resin Cathedral, here is a picture of what **lives** within its living walls, resonant throat, and shifting floors:

1.  **The Cathedral Itself (The Primary Inhabitant):**
    *   **The Living Architecture:** The dominant "life form" is the vast, interconnected network of chemotrophic plant-analogue matter. It is not merely *in* the space; it **is** the space. The pillars, ceiling-mat, floor, and walls are one sprawling, metabolizing organism. Its "breath" is the warm, sulfurous air; its "voice" is the deep thrumming drone; its growth and decay define the cathedral's ever-shifting architecture. It consumes minerals and heat, exhales processed gases and particulate, and builds itself relentlessly. It is the foundation, the habitat, and the ultimate inhabitant.

2.  **The Excavators (The Sound of the Walls):**
    *   **Pillar Beetles:** These are the ubiquitous, unseen workhorses. The size of a human hand, their entire existence is tunneled within the dense pith of the cathedral's structural columns. Their life cycle revolves around burrowing, consuming the inner plant matter, and digesting the mineral-impregnated fibers. Their collective, relentless *skritch-skritch-skritch* is the baseline white noise of the cathedral, the sound of its constant, granular transformation. They are the primary recyclers, breaking down old growth and creating voids that other life eventually fills.

3.  **The Striders (The Canopy Storm):**
    *   **Ceiling Mantids:** These are the cathedral's most visible and aurally overwhelming fauna. Long-limbed, perfectly camouflaged against the ochre and bone-white ceiling, they move with an eerie, deliberate grace. Their limbs are partially fused with the ceiling-mat, allowing them unparalleled purchase. Their defining feature is their sound: the rapid, chainsaw-like buzz of their wing-casings. This isn't flight; it's communication, warning, and territorial display – a language of vibration against the omnipresent drone. They are likely predators (perhaps of the beetles or other insects) and prey (to the large, unseen things below). Their constant buzzing is the cathedral's urgent, percussive heartbeat.

4.  **The Deep Galleries' Occupants (The Heavy Drag):**
    *   **Unnamed Lumberers:** These are the titans hinted at by heavy, dragging sounds, the cracks of collapsing columns, and the panicked surges of insect noise. They move in the voids deep within the floor-plane and between growth layers. They are large, strong, and likely herbivorous or detritivorous, physically reshaping the cathedral's interior as they move. Their passage is an event – a localized catastrophe triggering a cascade of collapse, insect panic, and rapid regrowth. They are the agents of the cathedral's violent creative equilibrium, their existence measured in the aftershocks of their movement.

5.  **The Spore-Drifters (The Atmosphere Itself):**
    *   **Microscopic and Particulate Life:** The air is thick with life in suspension. Fungal spores, bacterial colonies, pollen analogues, and shredded fibers constitute a constant rain of organic matter. This isn't just debris; it's a dynamic medium. Some microbes likely thrive in the warm, sulfurous updrafts. The spores seed new growth wherever they settle. This airborne life is the connective tissue of the ecosystem, the fallout of the cathedral's metabolic exhaust.

6.  **The Shapers (The Hidden Makers):**
    *   **The Builders of the Margins:** This is the most enigmatic layer. Small, intelligent beings – likely arthropod or small vertebrate analogues – inhabit the edges and less-occupied galleries. They are cautious, stealthy, and adapted to the soundscapes.
    *   **Appearance:** Small (perhaps hand-sized or smaller), with bodies likely colored in mottled ochre, rust, or translucent yellows to blend with the plant matter. Excellent hearing, adapted to parse critical sounds (strider buzz, beetle skritches, deep gallery movements) amidst the overwhelming drone. May have specialized limbs for manipulating resinous secretions and plant fibers.
    *   **Behavior:** They utilize the cathedral's materials – lashing plant-stalks into platforms and alcoves, using heated stone to shape resin-sap, building simple walls from fiber-plates. Their fire-pits suggest controlled use of the cathedral's warmth or perhaps even friction-ignition. They are listeners, observers, hiding within the very soundscape that defines their world. Their existence is marginal, exploiting niches the larger inhabitants don't dominate, possibly scavenging or farming smaller organisms. Their decision about the intruder is paramount: Are they threat, prey, tool, or irrelevant noise? Their structures are whispers of intentional order within the cathedral's overwhelming, self-organized chaos.

**In Essence:**

The Resin Cathedral is a world **defined by its inhabitants**:
*   **The Architecture itself** is the largest inhabitant, growing, breathing, and resonating.
*   **The Beetles** are its internal transformation engines.
*   **The Striders** are its loud, vibrant canopy predators/performers.
*   **The Deep Galleries' Beasts** are its agents of large-scale change.
*   **The Airborne Life** is its pervasive connective medium.
*   **The Shapers** are its elusive, intelligent edge-dwellers, crafting simple structures amidst the overwhelming organic machinery.

Life here is not *on* the cathedral; it **is** the cathedral, expressed in countless forms, all bound by the chemical energy from below, the sound that permeates everything, and the constant cycle of collapse and regrowth. The human intruder is a fleeting anomaly in a system that speaks in mineral ions, vibration, and the slow, relentless growth of bone-white pith.
