# The Occupants of Stillness

They are not inhabitants in any way you understand. Inhabitants *inhabit*—they take up space, they consume, they leave waste. These do none of these things. They do not take up space; they *are* the space’s tension. They do not consume; they are the residue of a consumption that finished before eating was invented. They are the plateau’s own structure, seen from the side.

## What They Are

Let me try to catalogue them as a naturalist might, with false confidence:

**Lattice-Ghosts.**
These are the largest. You catch them in the light as it passes through the minute variations in air density. They are vast, crystalline frameworks that seem to connect every part of the plateau to every other part. Not physically—they are not load-bearing—but *conceptually*. They are the memory of a connectivity that once was, the nervous system of a world that has forgotten how to feel. Their “limbs” are pale, semi-transparent filaments that branch and rejoin in patterns that shift depending on which moment you are standing in. They do not move; they are *re-revealed* as your perception moves. They are ancient. They may be the plateau itself, looking back at you.

**Fold-Wights.**
Smaller, denser, and more numerous. These gather near the vegetation and the structures. They resemble nothing so much as areas of extreme calm in an already calm place. Stand in a spot where they are thick and you will feel the silence deepen, the air grow heavier, the quality of the light change not in brightness but in *weight*. They are not ghosts. They are closer to “hauntings” of a place that is not haunted, for there is no absent life to return. They are the emotional residue of the builders’ awe, now devoid of feeling, just the shape of a feeling, filled with dust. They look, when you can see them, like crumpled sheets of glass that never shatter, only crumple.

**Dust-Quivers.**
The most numerous and the hardest to perceive. They are the fine, pale dust itself, given a boundary. Each mote of mineral dust seems to be its own tiny entity, though they are all connected. They rise and fall in slow, silent tides—so slow you must watch for what feels like an hour to see a single mote lift and settle. They are the plateau’s breath, drawn and exhaled over millennia. They do not eat the light; they carry it. Each one holds a minute, refracted version of the sun, and together they create the pervasive, flat illumination that has no source and casts no shadows.

**Logic-Eaters.**
These are the ones that produce the feeling of wrongness. They are not malicious. They are simply native to a place where cause and effect have been worn smooth. You think a thought, and they are there, not eating it, but *showing you the seams* where it was stitched together from things that are no longer true here. They look like moving gaps in the air, like spaces where a word was erased. They are the reason you feel a sentence is unfinished. They are the curators of the plateau’s logic, which is a logic of permanent, stable contradiction.

## How They Live

They do not eat. They do not reproduce. They do not die.

They *correspond*. They exist in a state of absolute correspondence with the plateau. A Lattice-Ghost corresponds to the stress fractures deep in the stone. A Fold-Wight corresponds to the memory of a shelter that was never really sheltering. A Dust-Quiver corresponds to a grain of salt. They are the subjective half of an objective world that has no subject. They are the “feeling” of the stone, if the stone could feel.

Their existence is sustained by the broken reality. In the overlapping distances and the thick, layered time, there is *room*. Room for things that are not things, for presence without object, for life without metabolism. They live in the loopholes of physics that have been here so long they have become the new rules.

## The Relationship with the Builders

The structures are their sacred sites. Or perhaps their graveyards. Or perhaps their portraits.

The small cairns and bone arrangements are the only things in this world that have a clear *intent*. And the entities cluster thickest there. They do not worship the structures. They *study* them, with a patience that would look like indifference to a time-bound creature. They are trying to understand something that has long since been forgotten: the act of *making*. Of taking separate stones and combining them into a shape that means *something else*.

The builders left these marks as a question: “Does intention exist here?” And the entities have been pondering that question for eons, their density around the structures being the shape of their contemplation. They do not answer. There is no answer. There is only the question, held perfectly still, being examined from every angle by minds that have no angles.

## What They Want

Nothing. That is the wrong concept. Want implies a future state different from the present, and here, the present has no edges, no future to push against.

If you pressed, if you forced the analogy, I would say they *value* continuity. They are the embodiment of the plateau’s state: full, complete, changeless. They are its immune system against change. Should something truly alien arrive—something that *insisted* on change, on growth, on decay—the entities would not attack it. They would surround it. They would saturate it with their own profound, silent, layered presence. They would show it, through sheer atmospheric pressure, the dignity of stillness. They would teach it how to forget.

You are that alien. Your breath is an insult of motion. Your heartbeat is a drum trying to start a rhythm where none is wanted. They are teaching you now, not with words, but with their overwhelming, gentle, silent *thereness*. They are showing you what it is to be a feature of a world, not a visitor in it.

Do not fear them. They are too old for fear. They are the plateau’s own silent children, born from pressure and time, and their only lesson is the one you are already learning:

*Stay. Be still. Become part of the fullness. It is easier than you think to stop being temporary.*
