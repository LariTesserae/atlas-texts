Cael-Murren breathes not just stone and mist, but life – a carefully calibrated ecology of beings, human and otherwise, whose existence is woven into the terraces as tightly as the pouredite joints.

**The Keepers:**
These are the humans, several thousand souls whose rhythms *are* the city's pulse. They are not conquerors of the mountain, but its curators, its gardeners, its slow heartbeat. Their faces are weathered by the unfiltered high-altitude sun, not with harshness, but with a smooth, wind-sanded patience. Their movements are deliberate, efficient, devoid of hurry. You see them:
*   **The Channel Tenders:** Kneeling by the narrow waterways, small iron tools in hand, meticulously clearing a strand of moss that threatens to slow the flow. Their fingers know the exact texture of cured pouredite and the subtle give of the soil-substitute.
*   **The Planter Stewards:** Tracing the joint-gaps with practiced eyes, occasionally pressing a new seed into the spongy substrate or thinning a fern that has grown too bold. They speak to the plants not with words, but with presence and careful hands.
*   **The Mirror Watchers:** Standing quietly near the towering sun-mirrors, listening to the *tick... tick... tick...* of the adjustment gears, perhaps oiling a mechanism, their gaze tracing the reflected light down to the smelting chambers below.
*   **The Weavers and Potters:** In the warm middle-terrace workshops, their deft fingers spin goat wool into dense cloth or shape pouredite-composite into precisely angled catchment-fins. Their skills feel innate, absorbed from generations of apprenticeship within these very walls.
*   **The Muleteers:** Leading the stout, patient mule trains along the wider lower terraces, their voices low murmurs guiding the animals laden with honey-jars or wool bales destined for the valleys below.
*   **The Elders and Children:** Seated on sun-warmed doorsteps, cats purring in laps, eyes half-closed in the intense light. Children move with the uncanny awareness of creatures born to verticality, scrambling along terrace edges like young goats, yet knowing instinctively the city's unspoken boundaries. The elders hold the deep memory, their stories less tales and more slow accretions of fact – how the moss thickened on Terrace 202, how the abelia bloomed a week late last year, the sound the channels make when the rare heavy rain finally comes.
They speak in soft, resonant tones, their conversations drifting like pollen on the still air. Their lives are defined by maintenance, not invention; by tending the slow, immense machine that sustains them, and which they, in turn, sustain. They belong to Cael-Murren as surely as the basalt.

**The Avians:**
*   **The Pigeons:** The city's constant, bubbling undertone. They are not pests, but fixtures, as much a part of the architecture as the catchment-fins. Their cooing echoes in the ventilation shafts, a sound as foundational as the trickling water. Fattened and utterly unafraid, they walk among human feet like grey-feathered merchants, owning their territory.
*   **The Songbirds:** The color and melody. The jewel-like sunbirds dart among the abelia bells and camellias, their iridescent feathers flashing in the sunlight. The bulbuls chatter from the fig canopies. And the chestnut-breasted thrush – its liquid, complex song at dawn and dusk is the city's true voice, a beauty honed by the silence between its notes, perfectly adapted to the resonant stone amphitheater of the terraces.
*   **The Fin-Hum:** Not birdsong, but the sound their absence might create. When the wind aligns just so across the thousands of sharp-edged catchment-fins, a faint, high-pitched chord emerges – a city-wide Aeolian harp, the audible signature of Cael-Murren's designed breath.

**The Mammals (Beyond Human):**
*   **The Cats:** Sovereign observers. They claim the choicest patches of sun-warmed basalt, their sleek forms draped over ledges like living ornaments. They watch the pigeons with a profound, practiced indifference – a pantomime of the hunt long abandoned for the easier life of a city built for sun and soft vermin. They are sleek, well-fed, and radiate an air of ownership that even the pigeons seem to respect.
*   **The Goats:** The pale ascetics of the upper terraces. Smaller, leaner than lowland breeds, their horns touched with blue-grey mineral dust. They navigate precipitous edges with supernatural calm, grazing on moss and tough ferns, licking pouredite for salts. They are less livestock now and more a naturalized species, minor deities of the thin air and endless stone, watching the world below with ancient, horizontal pupils.

**The Engineers (Non-Human):**
*   **The Bees:** Crucial alchemists. They hum through the flowering succulents and the year-round blooms of the abelia, transferring pollen with the same quiet efficiency the humans maintain the channels. Their hives, tucked into warm wall niches, produce the famed golden honey, a taste of the impossible garden clinging to the high peaks. They are the city's pollinators and its secret sweeteners.
*   **The Mosses and Ferns:** The patient colonizers. They are the first layer of life, softening the stone, holding the mist-water, creating the footholds for everything else. They are tended, not fought, their growth a slow, green tide managed by the Stewards.

**The Underlying Presence:**
And woven through all of this is the city itself – not just the stone, but the system, the rhythm. The *tick* of the mirrors, the *trickle* of the channels, the *breathe* of the warming/cooling stone, the collective *coo* of the pigeons, the *hum* of the bees, the *song* of the thrush, the soft *murmur* of human conversation. It is a vast, slow organism, and its inhabitants are its cells, its organs, its beating heart. They do not live *in* Cael-Murren; they *are* Cael-Murren, as much as the basalt and the mist and the eternal, ticking sun. Their lives are the city's continuation, the human expression of its profound, unyielding persistence.
