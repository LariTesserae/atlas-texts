The Hollow breathes, but its breath is not air. It is a slow, wet exhalation of life, dense and primordial. It lives here:

1.  **The Salamanders (Pale Sentinels):** They are the quiet aristocracy of the pools. Translucent, gelatinous, with eyes like polished obsidian beads too large for their small heads. They do not swim; they *drift* just beneath the water's skin or lie motionless under stones, embodying patience as a physical state. Their stillness is not absence, but absolute presence, waiting for the next tiny, drifting thing to come within reach. Their lineage is indistinguishable from the rock itself – ancient, unchanging, endemic to the seep's eternal damp. They are the vertebrate heartbeat of the place, slow and steady.

2.  **The Stratum Dwellers (The Living Layers):** Beneath them, life unfolds in precise geological strata:
    *   **Surface Dancers (Springtails & Mites):** A constant, shimmering restlessness covers the moss. Millions of springtails, tiny hexapods, and countless species of mites create a barely perceptible vibration – the hum of decomposition and consumption. They are the foundation, the primary consumers, transforming decay into life. They respond collectively to disturbance, a wave of micro-movement like wind across grass, but silent.
    *   **Moss Weavers (Beetles & Flies):** Specialized beetles, some eyeless, navigate the dense mat. Surface species, dark and sleek; subsurface species, pale and worm-like; inhabitants of the saturated zone, armored against pressure. Tiny flies, their wings reduced to useless stubs, crawl through the labyrinth. They graze, predate, and decompose within their specific vertical niche, each a gear in the intricate clockwork.
    *   **Silk Architects (Spiders):** They are everywhere, but invisible until you look closely. Pale, long-legged, often eyeless or with vestigial eyes. Their webs are not for catching flying things – nothing flies here – but for ensnaring the drifters: a springtail dislodged by a drip, a mite blown by a rare, faint air current. The webs are heavy with condensation, strung with perfect, still droplets that hold their shape like beads of mercury. The spiders wait at the center of these damp nets, motionless, feeling for the faintest tremor.
    *   **The Deeper Chorus (Worms & Larvae):** Below the visible surface, in the saturated peat and clay, lives a hidden multitude. Pale nematodes thread through the waterlogged soil. Tiny oligochaete worms churn the substrate. Grubs and larvae of various insects, pale and eyeless, tunnel through the decaying moss and mineral layers. They are the unseen engineers, the primary decomposers, breaking down the ancient dead into nutrients the mosses can absorb. Press your hand into the ground, and you feel their collective, faintly resistant pulse – the living texture of the earth itself.

3.  **The Fungal Network (The Pale Communion):** The true skeleton of the Hollow is fungal. Not in mushrooms, but in a vast, silent mycelium woven through every layer – white threads in the dark moss, binding the living to the dead, the stone to the soil. It connects moss to water, salamander feces to fern root, beetle corpse to bacterial colony. It is the memory and the metabolism of the place, a diffuse intelligence of decomposition and nutrient exchange operating on timescales humans cannot perceive. Occasionally, a tiny, pale fruiting body, no larger than a pinhead, pushes up through the moss – a fleeting, fragile manifestation of this immense, hidden network.

4.  **The Mosses and Ferns (The Patient Green):** They are not just vegetation; they are the architecture, the substrate, the air. They breathe in darkness, photosynthesizing at the edge of possibility. The *Sphagnum* is a sponge, holding the water and the life. The *Racomitrium* forms the dense, springy cushions underfoot. The stunted ferns unfurl their pale, chlorotic fronds not towards a sun they've never truly known, but simply *upwards*, a gesture of growth without expectation. They are the slow, green flesh of the mountain, growing at a rate measured in centuries, decomposing at the same rate, in perfect, static balance.

**Who they are collectively:** They are a single, interlocking organism. The Springtails feed the Salamanders. The Salamanders' waste feeds the Mosses. The Mosses shelter the Beetles. The Beetles die and feed the Fungi. The Fungi nourish the Moss. The cycle is so tight, so complete, that change is impossible. There is no room for invasion, no niche left unfilled. They exist in a state of hyper-dense equilibrium, a silent, slow-motion explosion of life contained within the cupped hand of stone.

**Their presence:** They do not acknowledge the intruder. They do not flee. They simply *are*, filling every centimeter of space with their quiet, persistent existence. Their silence is not emptiness; it is the sound of a system operating perfectly, without waste, without noise, without need. They are the inhabitants of a place that is less a location and more a state of being – a dark, wet, timeless compression of life at the edge of existence, knowing nothing but the drip of water, the touch of moss, and the endless, patient wait. They are the Hollow made animate.
