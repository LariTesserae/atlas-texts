## Inhabitants of the Drowned Parish

**The Foundation: Tube-Worm Colonies**
The primary residents are sessile, silent architects: the tube-worms. Calcium carbonate tubes, fused over generations, form the towering, pocked structures that resemble ruined cathedrals. Each tube houses a single worm, its crimson gills extending into the water to filter organic detritus. They are the bedrock, the streets, the walls. Their growth is slow, geological, measured in decades per inch. They do not think; they build, blindly, their discarded and overgrown casings becoming the very landscape others navigate. Their faint red bioluminescence is a passive glow, a mark of their presence, not a communication.

**The Gardeners and the Patrol: Crustaceans**
Life moves around the towers. Armored isopods, dark and flat like river stones, crawl methodically over every surface. They are grazers, scraping the encrusting layers of bryozoans, limpets, and bacterial mats with specialized mouthparts. They prevent any single organism from monopolizing space, maintaining a rough, competitive equilibrium. Their movement is slow, deliberate, a constant pruning. Larger crabs, their carapaces broad and mottled, patrol the floor channels. They are territorial, claiming segments of the "streets" between towers. The sharp *tock-tock* of their claws on limestone is a rare punctuation in the waterlogged quiet, a warning or a claim. They scavenge and hunt smaller invertebrates, their sideways intelligence evident in their pauses and calculated lunges.

**The Drifters and the Glitter: Jellyfish and Fish**
Schools of small, translucent fish – eyeless, sensing the world through lateral lines that shimmer with stored light – pulse through the channels in coordinated bursts. They are the pulse of the place, the quicksilver movement. Bioluminescent clusters within their bodies give them a ghostly, internal glow, a hazy static that marks their passage. They are prey, constantly reacting to pressure waves and the faint electric fields of predators. Jellyfish, their bells a pale, translucent blue-white, drift at a more stately pace. Their trailing tentacles, shimmering with bioluminescent lures and warning flashes, create gossamer curtains in the currents. They are the lanterns, their light unreliable and beautiful, language and warning in the profound dark. Together, the fish and jellyfish provide the scattered, ethereal sparkle that defines the parish's light.

**The Watchers and the Shadows: Cephalopods and Eels**
In the spaces between the towers, squeezing into crevices, live the octopuses. Small, warm-adapted, their skin shifts with complex, pale patterns – camouflage and communication in one. They are curious, intelligent, exploring the nooks and crannies, hunting crustaceans and fish with swift precision. They are the closest to individuals here, each with its favored den, its hunting patterns. Yet they are wary, for the shadows hold the ancient ones: the eels. Pale as the limestone itself, vast and patient, these eels inhabit the same crevice for decades, their bodies conforming to the rock. They grow immense, their wide jaws capable of taking an unwary octopus or a large crab. They are ambush predators, a stillness that becomes sudden, overwhelming motion. Their occasional, deep shift against a tower sends a resonant boom through the structure, a tremor felt more than heard. They are the parish's apex predators, living geological time.

**The Interstitial Weavers: The Hidden Multitude**
Life saturates every void. Within the sponge-filled walls and sealed cavities, polychaete worms reign in staggering diversity: some with fern-like gills filtering particles, some with backward-hinging jaws, some spinning intricate mucus nets. Brittle stars, impossibly long and thin, extend their arms like ghostly fingers from cracks, delicately tasting the water. Anemones, their tentacles a forest of stinging threads, cling to every surface, waiting. Bacterial mats, fed by sulfide seeps, blanket the softest sediment, a living carpet grazed by specialized worms. Sponges, the mortar between the tube-worm bricks, actively pump water, their bodies vast filtration systems. There is no empty space. Every cubic inch teems, a constant, silent hum of feeding, growing, reproducing, and dying – the metabolic engine of the drowned world.

**The Integration: Human Ghosts**
Deep within the sediment, beneath the living structures, lie the true foundations: rectangles of stone and concrete, the stubborn geometry of the human parish. The tube-worms unknowingly anchor to these buried walls. Occasionally, a strong current reveals a glimpse – a sharp right angle, a flat surface – a symmetry alien to the organic architecture. These remnants are merely geology now, substrate for new life. The creatures that swim over them are utterly indifferent to their origin. The human world is not remembered here; it is simply the bedrock upon which this vibrant, alien civilization builds and rebuilds, forever, in the warm, dark, whispering water. The rain falls. The water holds. The towers grow. The parish lives on, remade.
