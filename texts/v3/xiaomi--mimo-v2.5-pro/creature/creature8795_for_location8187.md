Based on your vivid description, the inhabitants of The Vents of Alto Pluma are not animals, but a **tenacious, layered community of plants, locked in a symbiotic struggle with the violent geology that birthed them.** They are the sole, silent witnesses to the earth's ongoing work, colonizing chaos with an insatiable green fervor.

Here is who and what lives there:

1.  **The Moss Hordes:** They are the first wave, the vanguard of life. Saturated, nearly black-green, and spongy, they coat every stable surface – shattered rock, dormant vent crusts, toppled slabs. They are water-hoarders and foundation-builders, transforming barren stone into a living substrate. They drip constantly, a slow, patient erosion of the mineral world above and a reservoir for the world to come.

2.  **The Fern Jungles:** Rising thickly from the moss-mat, these are the basin's dominant canopy. Multiple species compete aggressively:
    *   **The Hair-Leaf Ferns:** Fronds fine as threads, forming dense, mist-catching curtains.
    *   **The Fleshy Giants:** Broad, almost tropical fronds unfurling constantly in the humid air, reaching shoulder height and beyond. They grow so densely they form impenetrable walls, transferring the basin's perpetual dampness to anything that brushes against them. They are the basin's lungs and its loudest silent statement – life rampant.

3.  **The Stunted Trees and Leaners:** Twisted, moss-cloaked shrubs and small trees emerge from the fern thicket. They are stunted by altitude and competition, their bark peeling in papery curls from relentless moisture. They lean perpetually towards the steam plumes, seeking warmth and light filtered through the fog. Their branches are platforms, hosting:

4.  **The Epiphytic Hitchhikers:** Small, waxy, pale orchids perch precariously on the higher limbs of the leaning trees. Their roots dangle freely into the mist, drawing moisture and nutrients directly from the thick air. They are fragile jewels amidst the general roughness, symbols of life exploiting every possible niche.

5.  **The Lichen Rosettes:** Joining the mosses on older surfaces, pale grey-green lichens fan out in intricate rosettes. They are the patient chemists, slowly breaking down the mineral crusts into soil.

**How They Live:**

*   **In Saturation:** Every surface is colonized. Life fills every gap, every crevice. The density is overwhelming – layer upon layer of growth, an interlocked system hiding the true ground beneath.
*   **In Exclusion:** Only the hottest, most toxic vent mouths remain bare – circles of mineral crust (yellow sulfur, white silica, orange iron) and boiling, colored pools. These are the windows to the volatile earth below, the places where even this tenacious flora cannot tread. The plants press up against these zones, testing their limits.
*   **In Constant Motion:** They are not static. Fronds unfurl constantly. Roots delve deeper. Growth races to colonize new mineral deposits before the next geological shift buries or fractures the substrate. They are pioneers on a battlefield.
*   **In Silence (Deafening Silence):** They exist without animal companionship or competition. The air is too toxic for fauna. Their life is a silent, sessile struggle against the elements and each other, punctuated only by the geological symphony – hissing, barking, pulsing, groaning vents and shifting rock. They absorb the sulfur taste, the mineral tang, the crushing humidity.

**What Defines Them:**

They are **adaptors and opportunists.** They have seized the warmth and moisture gifted by the volatile earth beneath and transformed a high-altitude volcanic crater into a choked, dripping, dark-green hothouse. They are not peaceful; they are pioneers locked in a race against geological instability. They are not diverse in kingdom, only in form and strategy, all converging on the same life-sustaining formula: *warmth + water + mineral-rich, unstable ground = relentless growth.*

They are the **green response to the earth's unfinished fury.** They are the living proof that even in a place still actively being born from fire and steam, life, in its vegetative, silent, saturated form, will find a way to fill the room. They *are* the basin, as much as the steam and the rock. They live not *in* Alto Pluma, but *as* it – a breathing, growing, damp, dark, and utterly singular manifestation of life exploiting the extreme edge of possibility. They are the answer to the question "Who lives here?" – and their answer is a choked, verdant, dripping silence.
