The Singing Plateau breathes through its inhabitants, a sparse but intricate community shaped by wind, water, vibration, and a geology barely born:

1.  **The Foundational Dwellers:**
    *   **Stone & Water:** Not mere substrates, but the first residents. The granite and basalt, raw and sharp-edged, hold the memory of ice and the shock of exposure. The water, pooled in countless undrained depressions, weeping from every crack, is a resident itself – tannin-stained, iron-scented, forming a slow, shifting network across the land. It is the medium through which all others live.
    *   **The Sphagnum Sovereigns:** The true architects. Vast, interconnected cushions and carpets of sphagnum mosses, their colours shifting from sage to emerald as they drink. They are the land's skin, its sponge, its reservoir. They build the floating raft of peat, centimeter by century, insulating the raw stone below. Near the turbines, their form changes – compacted, denser – a silent testament to the vibration.
    *   **The Tussock Sedges:** Stiff, grey-green sentinels. They stand in tight clumps amidst the moss, their seed heads rattling in a wind that never truly stills. They anchor the deeper, drier patches of the vegetative membrane, providing structure amidst the saturated green expanse.
    *   **The Creeping Willows:** Low, horizontal escapees. Dwarf willows with pencil-thin stems and waxy leaves spread cautiously along the ground, avoiding the wind's shear. They add texture, a woody resilience within the sea of moss and sedge.
    *   **The Stone Painters:** Lichens – pale chartreuse, rust, grey – colonizing every bare rock face within seasons. They are the first colonizers, slow and patient, beginning the long process of breaking stone back into soil.

2.  **The Embedded & Subsurface:**
    *   **The Peat Matrix Life:** A vast, unseen kingdom. Nematodes, rotifers, microscopic mites, and countless bacteria and fungi inhabit the saturated layers of living and dead moss. They are the plateau's invisible engine, decomposing, cycling, building the peat beneath the surface. Methane bubbles rising through bog water mark their slow, constant activity.
    *   **The Vibration-Adapted Insects:** Subdued, but present. Midges, perhaps, and other small flies. Their usual frantic dance is muted, their activity patterns dictated less by light and more by the pervasive thrum of the turbines. They navigate the standing-wave patterns on the bog surfaces near the towers.
    *   **The Sound-Weavers:** Not biological, yet undeniably resident. The turbines themselves. Each tower is a colossal, humming organism. Their roots are deep in the bedrock, transferring vibration. Their "breath" is the rhythmic, throbbing pulse that merges with the wind's roar into the plateau's defining chord. The standing waves on bogs and the stunted growth of mosses near their bases are their direct biological signature – the ecosystem reshaping itself around their presence. They are the plateau's newest, most dominant megafauna.

3.  **The Transients & Avoiders:**
    *   **The High Raptor (Guest):** Rarely seen. A lone bird – perhaps a falcon or kestrel – riding the powerful updrafts at the *edges* of the turbine arrays. It utilizes the wind generated by the plateau and the machines but avoids the heart of the sound. It nests elsewhere, drawn only by hunting opportunity, not residence. Its presence highlights the emptiness within the arrays.
    *   **The Human Stewards (Migratory):** Rotating crews arriving for two-week shifts. They inhabit the low, prefabricated buildings on the southern edge – functional, weathered shelters. They move along the gravel track, service the turbines, leave marks (cairns, discarded fuel tanks, torn plastic). They are not inhabitants in the truest sense; they are seasonal workers in a landscape that operates on geological time. Their structures are intrusions, tolerated by the plateau's vast indifference.

**The Character of Life Here:**

This is a community defined by extremes and absences. It is **low, pervasive, and silent in its movements** – the moss advances millimeters, the peat accumulates fractions of millimeters. It is **saturated** – life exists at or above the water table, bound to the damp raft floating on stone. It is **vibration-sculpted** – the dominant sound physically shapes the growth of plants and the behavior of insects near its source. It is **animal-scarce** – the constant noise and vibration have created a garden tended only by invertebrates and microbes, devoid of the birdsong and mammal rustle that might exist on a quieter plateau. The dominant "presence" is the vegetative membrane and the industrial sound-structure.

The Singing Plateau's inhabitants are pioneers in a land still forming. They cling to the raw edges of a post-glacial surface, building life millimeter by millimeter, while above them, colossal machines harvest the wind and impose a new, relentless rhythm upon the land. The moss drinks, the sedges rattle, the peat builds, the turbines hum, the wind roars, and the water weeps – a chorus of existence on a stage not yet finished being set.
