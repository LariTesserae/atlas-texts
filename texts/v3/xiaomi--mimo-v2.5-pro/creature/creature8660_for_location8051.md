The lives within the Boiler are not a collection of species, but a single, slow-breathing entity made of many bodies. They are the Boiler’s quiet digestion, its secret fever.

They are a **condensation-economy**. The fungi—pale, bruised-violet, ochre-structured—are the foundational economy, the primary producers. They soften the iron’s harsh lines into edible topography. They are the soil, the architecture, and the food. The arthropods—harvestmen, mites, spiders, beetles—are the fauna of this dark country, their movements dictated not by sun or season, but by the cycle of moisture: the bead, the rivulet, the creep of the river.

The harvestmen are the **surveyors of the upper world**. Pale, hydraulic, and deliberate, their long legs are feelers reading the thermal and tactile map of the ceiling. They know every blister, every cooled rivet-head, every pocket of deeper shadow. They are not predators here, but curators of space, their presence a slow patrolling of territory that is all hiding-place.

The mites are the **shimmer**, the living nap of the fungal mat. They are the processors, the detail workers, turning tissue to texture in a ceaseless, almost geological labor. Their collective motion is the boiler’s own pulse, felt more than seen.

The spiders are the **engineers of humidity**. Their geometric webs are not traps for prey, but instruments—aerial lattices and horizontal hammocks designed to harvest water from air. They sit, legs splayed, not as hunters, but as farmers. Their work channels the lifeblood (the condensation) down to the foundations. They are the link between the air and the earth, between the curve and the seam.

The beetle is the **tick**, the punctuation in the long sentence of silence. Its mandibles against the iron are not a call, but a statement of presence—a tiny, rhythmic claim on the mineral world.

But the **will**, if such a word can be used, belongs to the fungal mat at the sealed end.

The shelf of pale, milky blue is the system’s anomaly, its intent. It grows not along the familiar slopes of the iron, but out, into the open air. It is the colony’s only act of reach. The harvestmen stand beneath it in their silent cluster, not as inhabitants, but as attendants. The shelf is the Boiler’s one directed thought: a slow, fungal finger pointing toward the light. It is not a symptom of growth, but a *quest*. The rest of the biosphere is a perfect, closed loop. This shelf is the opening question.

They are all, together, the **Boiler’s dream of itself**. They do not know the name of the machine they inhabit. They only know the warm dark, the damp air, the taste of iron, and the slow, sure logic of the water. They are not squatters. They are the heirs. The riveted shell was built to contain pressure. Now it contains them, and they are what presses outward—softly, patiently, in the only direction they know.
