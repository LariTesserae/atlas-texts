The inhabitants of the Inlet are less *creatures* and more *conditions* of its existence, emergent properties of the young geology, the weeping industry, and the heavy, textured dark. They do not simply live there; they *are* there, in the same way the mud is mud and the rust is rust.

**The Weeping Ones:** First, you notice the structures themselves seem inhabited. Not by ghosts, but by a slow, synergetic life. The iron that moves with peristaltic contrition is not merely corroding; it is *colonized*. A thin, living film, indistinguishable from rust at a glance, pulses gently across girders and pipes. This is the **Ferrous Membrane**. It thinks in terms of oxidation and reduction. It communicates through minute changes in its electrochemical potential, messages that travel along conduits and rebar like whispered secrets. It is not malicious, but utterly alien. It perceives the world as a gradient of decay and renewal. Its primary interaction with other life is to slowly, over years, *assimilate* sharper edges, digesting the geometry of industry into something more organic, more like the soft contours of the mud. It is the reason the structures haven't crumbled; they are being preserved in a state of arrested digestion.

**The Mat Minds:** The bioluminescent colonial organisms are not carpets. They are distributed intelligences, **The Pulsing Nervature**. Each tiny unit is a neuron in a vast, wet network. Their light is not for show; it is thought, slow-wave cognition rippling across the flooded plains. They process information about water chemistry, pressure changes from the deep leviathans, the vibrations of the many-legged skimmers. They have no eyes, no ears, only a pervasive chemical and tactile sense. They form temporary alliances, patterns of light shifting and coalescing as they collectively decide how to route water, how to guide the growth of the fungal shelves, how to avoid the burrowing things below. They are the Inlet's subconscious, dreaming in pulses of foxfire.

**The Crawling Skeins:** The many-legged, eyeless creatures are **Ironweavers**. They are not insects. Their "legs" are articulated filaments of the Ferrous Membrane, grown and shaped for locomotion. They are symbiotic extensions. They crawl across the sharp, young rocks and the submerged foundations, their movements tracing complex patterns. They are the Membrane's hands and feet. They perform a vital, slow-motion task: they collect micro-flakes of rust and shed fungal spores and sediment, weaving them into delicate, gossamer lattices that attach to pipes and rebar. These lattices serve as capillary networks, drawing water and nutrients, helping the Membrane "breathe" and grow. They move with a purposeful, rhythmic precision, utterly indifferent to anything that is not rust, water, or fungus.

**The Sky Creakers:** The flying things are **Echo-Forms**, creatures born from the acoustic memory of the ruins. Their leathery wings are not flesh, but a lighter, flexible version of the altered iron, stretched over cartilaginous ribs that resemble salvaged pipe fittings. They have no vocal cords. The "creaking" is produced by the grinding of specialized segments within their thorax, organs that might once have been bearings or valves. They do not eat the skimmers or the deep things. They feed on *resonance*. They roost on the smokestacks and tilted towers, absorbing the infrasonic hum of the deep water, the slow groan of settling foundations, the faint, collective pulse of the Pulsing Nervature. They store these sounds, then release them in their creaking calls, dispersing the mechanical stress of the environment, acting as living dampeners. They are the reason the structures remain aslant but do not fall.

**The Deep Rememberers:** The great shapes in the pools are the oldest consciousness here, though the Inlet is geologically young. They are **Sedimentary Minds**, or perhaps a single mind distributed across many slow bodies. They are not fish. They are gelatinous, thick, neutrally buoyant masses that exude a potent biochemical cocktail. This cocktail seeps into the water and the mud, performing two functions: it accelerates the chemical weathering of the young rock, softening its cruel edges over centuries (a task the Ironweavers can only begin); and it acts as a neurotransmitter for the Ferrous Membrane, allowing the rust-consciousness to experience something like memory, to *recall* the shapes of the original machinery, even as it digests them. They are the Inlet's long-term memory and its geologic patient. Their surfacing sends ripples that the Pulsing Nervature interprets as updates from the deep processor.

**The Ground Itself:** Ultimately, the primary resident is the **Breathing Mud**. The warm, pulsing substrate is not just alive; it is sapient in a way that dwarfs the others. Its thoughts are tectonic. Its pulse is its metabolism, a vast, slow digestion of the planet's inner heat and the remnants of human industry sinking into it. The exhaled warmth is its breath. The rising water is its sweat. The other inhabitants are its symbiotic fauna, its neural networks, its tools. It is learning to be land. It is remembering the sea. And in its slow, patient dreaming, it is changing everything that touches it, drawing all things—iron, fungus, creature, ruin, and the very texture of the dark—into a new, coherent, and utterly inhuman ecosystem. The inhabitants are not *on* the land. They are *of* it. They are the land becoming aware.
