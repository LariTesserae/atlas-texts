What lives in the Greenhouse Below are not creatures of tooth or wing, but patient architects of silence and chemistry. They are the **Slow Congregations**.

Imagine them not as individuals, but as **extensions of the stone itself**, awakening in the warmth and the wet. They exist in parishes, as the description whispers – small, determined communities bound by necessity.

1.  **The Ferns of the Drips:** Gathered where the ceiling weeps warm tears, they are **Thermal Filchers**. Their fronds are near-black, not from lack of life, but from dense adaptation. They are lush, humid thickets, each frond angled to catch the precious *plip* of condensation. They seem to drink the steam rising from the water they collect. Their stillness is profound, yet they possess a subtle tension – a readiness to unfurl another millimeter over years toward a new seep point. They smell of damp earth and minerals, a scent that clings to the air like a memory.

2.  **The Veined Leapers (Broad-Leaved Ones):** Found along the mineral-crusted thermal channels, these are **Luminous Feeders**. Their pale, translucent leaves are membranes, designed to absorb not light, but dissolved nutrients and the faint, pervasive heat-glow of the stone. The "borrowed luminosity" in their veins is key – it’s a slow pulse, a visual sigh, perhaps tied to internal chemical rhythms or the very slow movement of warm water within their tissues. They don't photosynthesize; they *mineral-synthesize*. Their broad surfaces are slick, not just with moisture, but with invisible microbial partners breaking down the earth's breath into sustenance. They are the most visible sign of the chamber's strange vitality, glowing faintly like embers banked for millennia.

3.  **The Green Chorus (Mosses and Films):** These are the **True Inheritors**, the vanguard. The mosses creep with deceptive speed (geologically speaking), claiming the eastern wall of the ruined shelter with emerald certainty. They are the ultimate adapters, thriving on the sheer humidity and mineral dust. Elsewhere, slicks of **Bacterial Shrouds** coat the wetter stones – not plants, but their essential symbionts. They form the foundational layer, breaking down the earth's exhalations (sulfur, iron, copper) into forms the plants can use. They are the invisible engine of the entire system, giving the stone its unsettling, living sheen. In the margins, where heat and dryness repel even moss, extremophile **Microbial Lichens** – fused partnerships of fungus and bacterium – paint the rock in slow, crusty patterns of gray and rust.

4.  **The Absent Ones (The Watchful Collective):** More than any single plant, the dominant life form is the **Collective Presence**. It's the sense of the parishes *knowing* their boundaries, respecting the fiery trench at the center. It's the slow, radial creep towards the abandoned human shelter – a patient, green siege. It's the feeling of being *observed*, not by eyes, but by countless microscopic and macroscopic processes all oriented towards survival. They don't hunt or flee; they *persist* and *expand* with geological patience. They communicate, perhaps, through subtle chemical signals in the humid air or through the shared network of warmth rising from the stone. Their growth is soundless, yet their existence fills the silence with an immense, slow *intent*.

They are the **Geothermal Gardeners**. They cultivate nothing but themselves and the bacterial worlds beneath their roots. They reshape the chamber imperceptibly, their roots probing new cracks, their fallen fronds (over centuries) adding the first hints of soil to the bare stone. They are locked in a slow-motion dance with the heat and the water, retreating from the inferno of the central trench, advancing into the cooling ruins of human ambition.

They are life distilled to its most elemental: chemistry, water, heat, and an infinite, silent patience. They are the Greenhouse Below, and it is alive because of them, breathing in the slow rhythm of the earth, exhaling the scent of damp stone and ancient green. They are the watchers in the warm dark, and they have all the time in the world.
