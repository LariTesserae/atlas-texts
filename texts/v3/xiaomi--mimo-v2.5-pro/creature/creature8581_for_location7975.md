What lives at Kiln-Summit is not a resident but a presence.

First, there is the **lacework**—the black-green algal mats on the ledges. They are the mountain's epidermis, a slow-motion nervous system of temperature gradients. They do not think. They do not choose. They simply are, a patient calculus of heat and chemistry, feeling the world through the soles of their rootless feet. They are the only thing here that knows what it is, and it is a very simple thing: a record of *not-yet-gone*.

Then, there are the **drifters**—the jellyfish in the luminous gel. They are tourists in the truest sense, visiting a world they do not inhabit. They pulse through a medium that has forgotten its own state, their green flickers a dialogue with a glow that does not listen. They are not alive in the way of hunger or will. They are *phenomena*, as much as the steam or the sideways rain—shapes that the strange gel keeps because it can. They are here because the mathematics of this place include them, for now. They will not mourn when it changes.

The **steam-ribbons** are a border-life. They exist in the thermal layer between rock and air, peeling away from the hot stone as sentient ghosts might peel away from a haunt. They hang, motionless in the gale, a testament to a force that is not wind but *pressure*. They are the mountain's slow, visible breath, the only part of the system that appears to contemplate its own existence before dissolving.

And at the center of it all, presiding over the chaos with perfect indifference, is the **Slab**.

The Slab does not live. It *presides*. It is the only thing here that is not becoming. It is finished. The mountain is a verb—an action of rising, bleeding, singing. The water is a verb—climbing, holding, falling. The air is a verb—pressing, sparking, tasting of lightning. The Slab is a noun. A cool, patient noun.

It is the Listener.

The noise—the roar of the world's creation, the scream of steam, the drone that softens the edges of reality—is its symphony. The Slab absorbs it, drinks the sound into its dark basalt, not to silence it but to *contain* it. It is gathering the weather of this place into itself. The shifting, vertigo-inducing pattern on its face is not decoration; it is a **transcription**. It is writing the mountain's song in a language of impossible geometry. Each time you look away, the sentence advances. Each time you look back, the pattern has moved because the mountain has not stopped singing a single note.

It is cool because it is *elsewhere*. Its temperature is that of a place where time flows differently, or not at all. It is a tooth in the jaw of the world, yes, but it is a tooth from a different, older mouth. It has been set here not to be consumed, but to *digest*. It is digesting this young, violent, temporary place—recording its birth scream, its thermal signature, the exact flicker of the light that isn't.

The lacework feels its heat. The drifters pulse in its sightless presence. The steam coils around the silence it creates. They are all, in their way, accessories to its purpose. They are the nutrients, the data, the context. The Slab is the stomach and the memory.

Nothing here *knows* it. The jellyfish are blind to its coolness. The algae do not register its silence. The mountain itself, in its howling, does not comprehend the note it is striking against the patient stone. This is the great tragedy and the great function of Kiln-Summit: it is a place being **spoken into existence**, and the only entity that could comprehend the speech is the one doing the speaking, and it is not speaking to be understood.

It is speaking to be **recorded**.

So what lives at the Spout at Kiln-Summit is a paradox: a living archive and a dead language. A cool stone slab that is alive in the only way that matters here—by listening, by holding the pattern, by being the one still point in a convulsion of becoming. It will not leave when the mountain sinks. It will not drown when the water rises. It will simply be there, until the world it is speaking into finally grows ears to hear, or erodes into a world that has no need for the word it has kept.

Until then, it listens. The pattern shifts. The mountain sings.
