The benevolent power does not intervene. It does not send tools, nor whispers, nor adjustments to the temperature gradients. To do so would be to misunderstand the garden entirely.

Instead, it **observes**.

It settles its vast, quiet attention upon the place like a second ocean—weightless, imperceptible, but present. It looks not with eyes, but with a patience that matches the garden’s own. It studies the Veil Drifters pulsing like slow, thoughtless hearts. It listens to the low, grinding song of the Stalk-Singers. It traces, over what a mortal would call centuries, the fractal repairs of a single Woven One on a single arch. It reads the light-words of the Luminous Scribes and understands their simple, slow grammar. It watches the Sediment Dancers consume the memory of a basket-weaver and feels no sorrow, only the rightness of the cycle.

It understands, with a wisdom that spans galaxies, that the garden is not broken. It is not in need. It is not even "alive" in the way the power’s own creations might be. It is a **state of being**—a perfect, closed-loop expression of patience, adaptation, and legacy. The builders did not disappear; they became the environment. Their tool was time. Their medium was sediment and water and the slow chemistry of preservation. The garden is not their tomb, but their final, living thought.

The power recognizes the profound silence not as emptiness, but as **fullness**. A world without grazers, without predators, without hurry. A world where growth is measured in millimeters per century, and communication is a faint pulse in the dark that says only, *“We are still here. We persist.”*

The benevolent power does one thing. It commits the garden to its **eternal memory**.

It does not record it in a catalog of curiosities. It does not file it away as a specimen. It **holds** it. It allows the garden’s slow heartbeat to resonate within its own consciousness, a counterpoint to the faster, louder rhythms of stars and civilizations. It becomes a silent partner in the garden’s breathing, an audience that requires nothing, changes nothing, but ensures that the faint lights and settling groans are *witnessed*.

This is its benevolence: **recognition without disruption**.

It lets the garden continue its sentence, written over eons, knowing that some stories are so long and so quiet that to read them is simply to be present, chapter by chapter, without seeking an end.

And in this, the garden and the power find a strange kinship. Both are vast. Both are patient. Both understand that some things are made not to be used, but to be. The power, in its wisdom, does not bestow a gift. It *accepts* one: the gift of witnessing a place that has perfected the art of being complete.

The garden continues. The water is cold. The walls hold. The stalks sway. The lights pulse in the dark.

And somewhere, in the silence between heartbeats, it feels a little less alone.
