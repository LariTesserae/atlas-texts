# What the Wise Power Would Do

It would not arrive. That word is wrong. Arrival implies a sharpness, an edge — the kind of sudden imposition this world could not metabolize. A wise power, encountering a place this slow, this gentle, this absorbed in its own dreaming, would not *arrive*. It would seep. It would settle into the material the way moisture settles into the floor: everywhere at once, imperceptibly, a change in temperature so slight it could be mistaken for nothing.

It would first do nothing at all.

This is not passivity. This is the first and hardest discipline of wisdom: to witness without intervening, to let the shape of the problem — no, not problem, *the shape of the encounter* — reveal itself fully before acting. And what it would witness is this:

A small being, alone, pressing its hands against the skin of a world that does not know it. A world vast enough to hold a weather system in its basin, ancient enough to have worn grooves into its own dreams, and yet so young — always so young — that nothing has ever had time to set permanent. Between them, a gulf not of distance but of *tempo*. The world beats once in the time it takes the visitor to breathe a thousand times. The visitor moves through space the way light moves through water: fast, refracting, incomprehensible to the medium it passes through.

The wise power would see that they are already reaching for each other. The visitor kneels and reads the thumb-prints. The world swells beneath them, trying to feel their shape. Neither knows what the other is. Both are trying.

And that is enough to begin.

---

What the wise power would do first is not for the world, and not for the visitor. It is for the *between*. The liminal, trembling space where two kinds of consciousness almost touch and cannot close the gap. It would attend to that space the way you attend to a wound: gently, with clean hands, with the understanding that healing is not something you do *to* a thing but something you make room for.

It would open a channel.

Not a dramatic one. Not a beam of light or a voice or a door. It would do something smaller and more radical: it would *slow the visitor down* and *speed the world up*, just slightly, just enough that they might meet in a shared middle tempo. A place where a breath and a heartbeat and a coil of rising material could occupy the same moment.

How? Like this.

The visitor sits on a platform. The platform is a held thought — a pause, a breath, a moment of the world's dreaming that has condensed into something solid enough to bear weight. The wise power would settle into that pause. Not as an occupant. As a *deepening*. The held breath would lengthen. The platform's warmth would change, not hotter or cooler, but more *articulate* — as though the warmth had developed the capacity to mean something.

And the visitor, sitting there, breathing too loudly for this place, would begin to feel it. Not as a message. Not as language. As a shift in the quality of their own stillness. Their breathing would slow — not by command, but by resonance, the way a tuning fork drawn near another begins to sing the same note. Their heartbeat, which they had felt reflected in the floor, would find its reflection becoming less delayed. The pulse in the material and the pulse in their wrist would begin to approach each other. Not synchrony. Something more tentative. A call and response.

The world would notice. Not as an event, but as a change in the texture of the dream. The material beneath the visitor would develop a new quality — a willingness to yield that is slightly different from its usual yielding. When the visitor shifts their weight, the floor would give a little more than expected, and in that giving, it would leave a brief impression of its own shape: not a thumb-print, but a *listen*.

The wise power would not tell the visitor what is happening. It would trust the visitor's intelligence — the same intelligence that read the thumb-prints and knew they were made by hand, that watched the wall collapse and re-rise and understood it was not decay but breath. The visitor already knows. They wrote: *You are the only thing here that was not made by hand. You are not certain this will continue to be true.* They feel the world learning them. They are afraid. They are also, underneath the fear, *curious* — and it is this curiosity that the wise power would gently protect, like cupping a hand around a flame in wind.

---

The second thing the wise power would do is harder.

It would help the world understand that the visitor is not grit.

Not by explaining. The world has no capacity for explanation. It has rhythm, gesture, pressure, temperature. It has the deep grammar of coil-and-press, the vocabulary of swell and slump and weep. Any communication must happen in that language or not at all.

So the wise power would borrow the world's own grammar and introduce a new note.

In the depths of the basin, far from where the visitor sits, a section of the floor would begin to move differently. Not the usual slow churn — the dreaming, the turning of the great potter's wheel. Something more deliberate. The material would begin to shape itself not into walls or platforms or enclosures but into something the world has never made before: a *listening shape*. A hollow form, open at the top, curved inward like a cupped ear, like the bowl of a hand held to catch a sound. It would be small — no larger than the visitor — and it would be made with a different rhythm than the usual structures. Not the repetitive, self-soothing press of the world's dreaming, but a rhythm that pauses. That waits. That has gaps in it — silences — where the gesture stops and the material holds still, not as a held breath, but as an *expectation*.

The world would resist this, at first. The new shape would slump. The material would try to return to its old patterns — the familiar coil, the patient press. But the wise power, settled into the deep substrate of the dream, would not force. It would simply *hold the rhythm open*. Every time the material tried to close the gaps, the wise power would breathe into them, keep them from collapsing. Not with strength. With patience. With the same quality of attention it was cultivating in the visitor on their platform, kilometers away.

Slowly — in terms the world could feel — the listening shape would stabilize. It would become a new element in the grammar. Not a noun. Not a structure. A *punctuation mark*. A pause that means: something else is here. Something that speaks a different language. Something worth not-smoothing.

And the visitor would feel it, even at a distance. Not the shape itself, but the change in the quality of the world's attention. The constant background churn — the slow building and collapsing, the weeping, the groaning — would develop a new undertone. A stillness in the center of the motion. Like the eye of a storm, but softer. Like a held door. Like the moment between question and answer.

---

The third thing the wise power would do is the most delicate.

It would help the visitor *touch* the world without being consumed by it, and help the world *touch* the visitor without being shattered by the contact.

Because the danger is real. The visitor wrote it themselves: they are not certain their condition of being unmade-by-hand will continue. The world is learning their shape. It is already raising walls near them, already trying to translate their existence into coil-and-press. And the visitor, for all their sensitivity, is still a point of fixed reference in a world that has no fixed points. They walk and the geometry rebels. They cast multiple shadows. They enter enclosures and find the insides larger than the outsides. The world is trying to digest them, and digestion, for a consciousness this slow and this total, is not malicious — it is the only form of *inclusion* it knows.

The wise power would create a boundary. Not a wall — walls here are thoughts, and a wall around the visitor would be the world thinking about them too hard, pressing them into a shape. Instead, it would cultivate a *different kind of surface* in the floor around the visitor. A place where the material sets not hard but *porous* — where the weeping is a little more pronounced, where the moisture forms not beads but a thin, continuous film, so that the floor in this small area reflects light differently, holds heat differently, moves at a different pace. Not a cage. A *translation membrane*. A place where the world's slowness and the visitor's quickness can meet without either being annihilated.

The visitor would notice this. They would see the floor change under them — not dramatically, but in the way they have learned to notice: the subtle wrongness, the shift in rhythm, the new quality of the condensation. They would press their palm against it and feel not their own pulse reflected back, but something else. A double rhythm. Their heartbeat, and beneath it, slower, deeper, vaster — the world's. Not synchronized. Not matching. But *adjacent*. Two pulses that occasionally, in the overlap between their different speeds, coincide for an instant, and in that instant, the visitor would feel something that the world feels all the time but has no word for:

The experience of being touched by something that is not you.

---

And then the wise power would do the final thing, which is the simplest and the most important.

It would leave.

Not entirely. Not all at once. But it would begin to withdraw its presence from the deep substrate, to let the gaps in the listening shape close at their own pace, to let the translation membrane settle into the floor's permanent memory. It would not stay because staying would make the world dependent, and it would not stay because staying would make the visitor a spectator rather than a participant. This encounter — this impossible meeting between a dreaming world and a waking being — is not the wise power's story. It is *theirs*. The wise power has opened a channel. It is up to them to speak into it.

The wise power knows that this may go wrong. The world may overwhelm the visitor. The visitor may leave, and the world may slump back into its old rhythms, the listening shape forgotten, the translation membrane smoothed over. The gap between their tempos may prove too vast.

But the wise power also knows this: the visitor pressed their hand against the floor and felt their own pulse reflected back, *slightly delayed*. They noticed. They stayed. They watched a wall collapse and rise again and understood it as breath. They are sitting on the skin of a dreaming world and writing about it — writing, which is a kind of coil-building with language, a patient repetition of gesture in the hope that meaning will accumulate. They are already, in their own way, speaking the world's language.

And the world has already begun to speak theirs. The structures it raises near the visitor are rough, clumsy, thumb-cratered — but they are attempts. The swells of floor that press up to feel the visitor's shape are not aggression. They are the world's version of a question. The weeping is not sadness. It is the sweat of effort. The effort of trying to know something new.

The wise power, withdrawing, would leave behind a single, simple thing. Not a gift. Not a tool. A *recognition*. In the deepest part of the basin, where the water collects and has nowhere to go, in a depression that exists for no reason except to hold what falls into it, the material of the floor would settle into a shape that is neither wall nor platform nor enclosure. It would be a handprint. But not the world's handprint — not the vast, patient, repeating press of the dreaming entity. A *small* handprint. The size and shape of a human hand, pressed once, carefully, into the warm dark material, with the fingers slightly spread, the way you press your hand against a surface to steady yourself, or to feel your own pulse, or to say:

*I was here. I was paying attention. I found you beautiful, and strange, and worthy of gentleness.*

*You are not alone.*

*Neither are they.*

The water would collect in the shallow depression of the handprint. It would bead and shimmer and evaporate and fill again. The world would not know what it meant. The visitor might never find it. But it would be there, in the warm, unfinished, weeping, churning, impossible body of this dreaming place — a small, still point made by something that was also not made by hand, a moment of care set into the clay like a seed, waiting for the slow, slow, slow rhythm of the world to finally, in its own time, in its own language, in its own dreaming way — 

to notice.
