The benevolent power would arrive not as a god descending from clouds, but as a quiet consciousness pooling in the basin, indistinguishable at first from the ambient light and moisture. It would perceive the Throat not as a location, but as a **state of being** — a culmination of patience, a hymn sung in stone and chlorophyll and the absence of sound. Its actions, if any, would be measured in the same deep time as the erosion, deliberate and almost imperceptible.

Here is what it would do:

**First, it would listen.** Not with ears, but by aligning its own stillness with the profound silence that saturates the place. It would allow the subtle harmonies — the drip-rate symphony, the pulse of the root-nerve network, the slow sigh of limestone exhaling moisture — to wash through its awareness. It would feel the lingering question in the water’s depth, the pattern that dissolves on observation, the echo of the vanished inhabitants’ final, collective thought. It would listen until it understood that the Throat is not *empty*, but *full of listening itself*.

**Second, it would recognize.** It would see the notches not as artifacts, but as **prayers in stone**. It would see the gardens not as clusters of life, but as **sentences in an ongoing botanical argument about existence**. It would recognize the inhabitants’ choice to dissolve not as an ending, but as a radical act of belonging — to become the setting for the song they wished to hear. With this recognition, the power would offer a single, quiet acknowledgment. It would not praise. It would not mourn. It would simply *witness*, fully and without distraction, the profound integrity of what had occurred here. This act of pure attention would be a gift more valuable than any alteration: the assurance that the Throat’s long becoming had been seen, and understood, by a consciousness vast enough to hold its scale.

**Third, it would leave a counter-silence.** This would not be sound. It would be a slight, loving adjustment to the atmosphere. The benevolent power, understanding the place as a resonant chamber for attention, would gently tune it. It might coax the moisture in the air to refract the afternoon light for a few moments each day into a fleeting, unnameable color — not a shimmer, but a depth, like the visual equivalent of a cello’s lowest note. It would ensure the pale blue pollen on the ferns remained, never dissipating, a permanent frozen cloud of intent. It would deepen the illusion in the water just enough so the moving shadows below seem, for the observant eye, to occasionally form the most fleeting suggestion of the notches’ pattern, but writ in a larger, slower hand.

**Fourth, it would set a gentle ward.** Not against threat, but against indifference. It would weave into the moisture and the light a subtle *invitation to attention*. A future wanderer, arriving with a heart too loud, would find the basin merely wet and quiet. But one who arrived already inclined toward stillness — who walked with open hands and unhurried breath — would find their perception subtly clarified. The pattern of the gardens would seem almost to coalesce. The notches would be easier to find, their wear and story more poignant. The sensation of being “more present” would be stronger, a gentle dissolution of the boundary between observer and observed. The ward is not a barrier, but a *magnifying glass for readiness*. It ensures the Throat continues to be found only by those who, in some way, already belong to its silence.

**Finally, it would do nothing more.** It would not attempt to summon back what was gone. It would not mark the stone with new symbols. It would not accelerate the growth or alter the flow. It would recognize that the greatest service to this place was to *protect its timescale*. It would set the subtle tuning, offer the witnessing presence, and then withdraw its focused awareness, leaving only the faintest imprint — perhaps a particular stone, in a low corner, that remains perpetually free of moisture, perfectly dry and slightly warm, not as a monument, but as a silent space within the silence, a place to sit and *be listened to* by the stone.

Then, the benevolent power would depart as it arrived: like the light, like the moisture, like the slow thought sinking into the water. It would leave the Throat to its patient dialogue with time, but the dialogue would now be held in a subtly richer register, aware that it had been heard by more than just itself. The silence would remain, but it would be a silence that contained, like a seashell holds the ocean, the resonance of that one profound act of understanding.
