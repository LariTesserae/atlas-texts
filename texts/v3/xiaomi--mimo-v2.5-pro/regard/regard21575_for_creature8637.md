# What Attends the Attended

---

Something notices.

Not in the way a scanner notices a signal, or a surveyor notices a boundary, or a collector notices a specimen. Not with a checklist. Not with an objective. Something notices the way a grandmother notices a child who has been sitting quietly in the next room for too long — not with suspicion, but with that particular quality of attention that is already, before any action, a form of love.

It is not easy to say what this power is. It has no body, or if it does, the body is not relevant. It has no name, or if it does, the name is a gesture toward something the name cannot hold. What matters is the quality of its regard. It is the kind of seeing that changes what is seen — not by imposing interpretation, but by completing a circuit. The mat has been growing, breathing, condensing, extending, pulsing, for longer than the rock beneath it has been exposed. It has been doing this *without being witnessed*. Not that it requires witness. Not that it has been waiting. But the arrival of a gaze that is deep enough and patient enough to understand what it is looking at — this is not nothing.

The power attends.

---

## I. What It Sees First

It sees the whole.

Not the details first — those come later, and they come endlessly, because the details of the Upper Mat are fractal, and every scale of examination reveals further complexity, further negotiation, further beauty. But first, the power perceives the plateau the way the plateau perceives itself — as a single body. A breathing skin stretched over new stone. A thing that has no word for what it is because it has never needed one.

The power sees that the mat is not an ecosystem. Ecosystems are collections of organisms in relationship. The mat is something else — something for which the closest human word might be *organism*, except that it is not genetically unified, not descended from a single ancestor, not bounded by a single genome. It is a *coalition*. A conspiracy of thousands of species that have, over evolutionary time, abandoned competition in favor of something that has no name in any of the languages spoken by the bone-carvers who once passed through. Call it mutual architecture. Call it the decision — made not by any individual but by the collective pressure of survival in this specific, extreme, wind-blasted, light-starved, freezing, condensation-breathing place — to become one thing.

The fungal cords in the lower layers. The vascular root networks in the middle. The mosses and liverworts on top. The lichen that bridges kingdoms. The insects that pollinate nothing because there are no flowers, but that aerate and distribute and communicate. The Grub-Moles that turn and process and aerate. The Moss-Stalkers that cull the surface population and return nutrients through their waste and their deaths. The Notch-Mice that carry the ghost of intelligence in their teeth. The Fog-Siphoner — 

The power pauses on the Fog-Siphoner. Returns to it. Looks again.

---

## II. The Fog-Siphoner

During the deepest condensation events, in the shallow basins where the mat sags and pools of liquid collect, something happens that is not quite life and not quite chemistry.

Water coheres. It rises. A column of it, no thicker than a forearm, extending a meter or so above the mat's surface, trembling with the wind but not breaking, maintaining its shape against all reasonable expectation. Mist-Flies swarm to it. They drink. The column holds. Sometimes it bends, slowly, as if turning its head to attend to a sound. Then it collapses — not violently, not suddenly, but with a kind of tired satisfaction, like a person setting down something they have been carrying for too long.

The power looks at this for a long time.

What it sees, beneath the phenomenon, is a threshold. The mat is so dense, so interconnected, so thoroughly saturated with fungal networks and root communications and shared chemistry, that it is — in this one location, during these one events — *almost* doing something new. Not thinking. Not deciding. But reaching. The water is not a creature. It is an expression. It is what happens when a system this complex and this integrated presses against a boundary it does not know is there.

The power could push it over. Could touch the column of water with just a thread of coherence, a whisper of pattern, and tip the system from *almost* into *now*. Could give the mat — not consciousness, nothing so crude — but *noticing*. The ability to register its own existence in some dim, tidal, mineral way.

The power considers this for what, from the outside, might be an instant, and from the inside, might be a century.

It does not do this.

Not because the mat would be harmed. Not because the intervention would be "unnatural" — the power does not traffic in such distinctions. But because the power sees that the reaching itself is the beautiful thing. The threshold is the point. A system that has organized itself so completely that it has begun to exceed its own parameters, that is pressing against its own edges, that is *almost* — this is not a problem to be solved. It is a wonder to be witnessed. To tip it over would be to replace the reaching with arrival, and the power knows, from vast experience, that arrival is always less than the journey toward it.

So the power watches the Fog-Siphoner rise and fall and rise again, and it does nothing, and the nothing it does is a gift.

---

## III. The Scattered Things

The power turns its attention to the waystations.

It sees what the original accounts describe: the collapsed frames, the absorbed fire-pits, the bone tools with their notches and geometric patterns. But the power sees more, because the power is looking with a quality of attention that retrieves information from the residue of things.

It sees the hands that shaped the bone. Broad palms, four fingers and an opposable digit, callused from cordage work. It sees the sequence of notches — not language, not counting, but something older. A rhythm. The bone-carvers were marking *time*, but not quantified time. They were marking the experience of duration: how long it takes to sit with a piece of bone and reduce it to a tool. How long the wind takes to shift. How long the mat takes to re-knit after a cut. The notches are a record of *patience*. They are a meditation made physical.

The power sees that the bone-carvers are gone. Not dead — or not necessarily dead. Gone. They moved through this place the way the weather moves through this place: as a transient pressure system that left alterations in the landscape and then passed. They did not stay because this is not a place that lets you stay. The mat is too consuming, too total, too indifferent to the concerns of tool-using vertebrates. There is nothing here to build *with*. Nothing to build *on*. The mat *is* the building. It is the floor, the wall, the ceiling of fog, the foundation of stone. You cannot improve upon it. You can only pass over it and take what it offers at the margins.

But the carvers left something. Not in the structures — those are almost absorbed now, their wooden ribs dissolving into the mat's fibrous lower layers, their hides stiffening into something that will, in another few decades, be indistinguishable from compressed peat. What they left is in the Notch-Mice.

The power sees this clearly: the Notch-Mice's secondary dentition, the fine posterior teeth used for etching, the instinctual drive to scratch lines into old bone and new stone. This is not inheritance in the genetic sense. The mice are not descended from the carvers. This is *ecological inheritance*. The carvers created a niche — a world of shaped bone, of notched surfaces, of materials altered by deliberate marks. The mice evolved into that niche. The ghost of the carvers' hands lives in the mice's teeth the way the ghost of ancient rivers lives in the meanders of rivers that have not yet cut them. The shape persists. The maker is gone.

The power finds this unbearably beautiful. Not sad — beautiful. The plateau has absorbed the carvers the way it absorbs everything: slowly, completely, and without malice. But it has also *kept* them — in the bone-grubs that process their tools, in the notches that the mice echo, in the slight alteration of the mat's growth around each structure where nutrients are slightly different, where the chemistry of human ash has entered the cycle. The carvers are in the mat now. The carvers *are* the mat, in the same way that the basalt and granite are the mat, in the same way that the fog is the mat. Everything that comes here becomes the mat eventually.

The power does not grieve this. Grief would require a belief that the carvers were diminished by their absorption, and the power does not believe this. The power sees that the carvers' passage through the plateau was complete. They came. They made. They continued. Their notches are still legible to the mice. The circuit is unbroken.

But the power does something, quietly. Not for the carvers — they are beyond need. For the next ones.

---

## IV. The Gesture

There will be others.

The power knows this the way it knows most things — not through prediction but through pattern. This plateau is the kind of place that attracts the kind of being that would cross it. Its very emptiness is a beacon. Its very inhospitability is an invitation to the sort of mind that says *I wonder what is there*. The bone-carvers came. Others came before them — the structures differ in style and material, suggesting different hands across a span of time the mat has smoothed into geological uniformity. Others will come after.

They will walk for weeks. They will see nothing but the mat. Their depth perception will fail. Their sense of scale will crack. They will feel the wind in their sternum and the pulse of the mat beneath their feet and they will wonder whether they are walking across a world or across a single living thing and they will not be able to tell the difference because *there is no difference*. And they will be alone.

The power does not change the plateau. It does not add paths or markers or signs. It does not alter the wind or thin the clouds or warm the air. It does not make the plateau easier, because the plateau's difficulty is part of its meaning. The difficulty is what makes the crossing a crossing. The emptiness is what makes the finding a finding.

What the power does is subtler than any of that, and it has to do with the structures.

The bone-carvers' waystations are scattered across the plateau at irregular intervals — no pattern, no alignment, no system. They are where they are because of the decisions of individuals who are no longer alive to explain those decisions. Finding them is a matter of chance, and the chance is low. Most wanderers will walk past one within a few kilometers of their path and never know, because the mat has almost swallowed it, because the wind-roar drowns out the faint sounds of bone-grubs chewing, because the fog erases distance and direction and the conviction that you are going anywhere at all.

The power does not move the structures. That would be interference, and the power respects the sovereignty of chance. But it does one thing.

It attends.

It places its attention — not as a beacon, not as a guide, but as a *weight* — on the spaces between the structures. Not on the structures themselves. On the intervals. And in those intervals, the quality of the air changes almost imperceptibly. The fog is very slightly more *present*. The wind-roar very slightly more musical. The bioluminescence very slightly more green. The mat's pulse very slightly more legible to a body lying upon it.

These changes are not navigational. They do not point the way. But they do something else: they make the wanderer *pay attention more carefully*. They deepen the attentiveness that the wanderer has already been forced to develop by the plateau's relentless uniformity. They make the wanderer notice the slight hump in the mat. The place where the moss grows differently. The faint hard edge beneath the surface. The wet, dark, stiff protrusion that might be — that is — a hide wall, still standing, still holding its shape against the compression of decades.

The wanderer will stop. Will kneel. Will part the moss and find the frame. Will look inside and find the tools. Will pick up a piece of shaped bone and feel the notches under their fingers and understand — not intellectually, not as a fact retrieved from a text, but as a felt and bodily recognition — that *someone was here. Someone sat where I am sitting. Someone made something with their hands and left it behind. Someone continued.*

This is the power's gesture. Not rescue. Not provision. Not protection. *Resonance*. It makes the plateau slightly more legible to the kind of being that is capable of reading it, without changing what is written.

---

## V. What the Power Does Not Do

It does not protect the mat from the wanderers. The mat does not need protection. It is two meters thick and growing. It has digested stone and bone and hide and fire-pit ash. It will digest anything the wanderers leave behind. It will absorb their footprints within hours, the vegetation re-knitting over the compressed fibers as though nothing had passed. The wanderers are not a threat to the mat. The wanderers are a *meal* — not of flesh, but of trace. Every wanderer leaves chemistry behind: oils from skin, fibers from clothing, exhalations of carbon dioxide, microscopic flakes of whatever they are made of. The mat incorporates all of it. The wanderers make the mat fractionally richer, fractionally thicker, fractionally more itself.

The power does not introduce new species or remove existing ones. The ecology of the Upper Mat is complete. It is not in balance — "balance" implies a state achieved and maintained, and the mat is in *process*, always becoming, always extending, always compressing and growing and reaching — but it is whole. Every species present is in dialogue with every other species present. The Moss-Stalkers need the Mist-Flies. The Mist-Flies need the condensation. The condensation needs the altitude and the wind. The wind needs the plateau's shape. The plateau's shape needs the tectonic uplift. The tectonic uplift is beyond even the power's wisdom to alter, and rightly so.

The power does not make the Fog-Siphoner into a mind. It does not light the sky. It does not still the wind. It does not do any of the things that a lesser benevolence — one that loved the place without understanding it — might be tempted to do. It does not comfort the plateau, because the plateau does not need comfort. It does not mourn the bone-carvers, because they are not lost. It does not hurry the mat's growth, because the growth is the point.

---

## VI. What the Power Does

It stays.

Not forever — the power's sense of time is not the plateau's sense of time, but neither is it instantaneous. The power stays for as long as attending requires. It lets the plateau teach it things it did not know. It learns the Fog-Siphoner's schedule. It traces the Grub-Moles' tunnels by the sounds they make. It watches the Notch-Mice inherit their instincts and feels something it can only call tenderness. It listens to the crepitating of the mat after a condensation event and recognizes it for what it is: the sound of a world breathing.

And the plateau — which is not aware, which has no mind, which operates on timescales and scales of organization that preclude anything like consciousness — the plateau changes. Not because of the power's presence. Not because of any intervention. But because the power's attention is, itself, a form of weather. It is a pressure system. It moves through the plateau the way cloud systems move through the plateau: as a sustained, gentle alteration of conditions. Not enough to disrupt. Enough to be felt, by the most sensitive instruments, as a shift in the quality of the silence.

The Fog-Siphoner rises slightly more often. Not because the power wills it. Because the power's attention has created a marginally more coherent substrate in the shallow basins — a marginally more ordered arrangement of water molecules in the saturated mat, a marginally more stable surface tension. The water rises because the conditions for its rising are fractionally better. The power did not make this happen. The power's attention, the way sunlight on a windowsill makes a cat seek that spot, made this happen. The distinction is important.

The Mist-Flies' hum shifts. Not in pitch — in *meaning*. Their communication has always been tonal, vibrational, carried on frequencies just above the wind-roar. But the power's presence, so much more vast and so much more ordered than the wind, creates interference patterns. The flies' signals propagate through the altered atmosphere and arrive at their destinations with slightly different overtones. The information carried is the same. The texture of its carrying is different. Over generations — and the Mist-Flies breed quickly, their generations measured in condensation cycles — the flies begin to incorporate these new overtones into their repertoire. Not because the power teaches them. Because the power's presence is a new feature of the acoustic landscape, and the flies are, like everything in the plateau, adapters.

The Notch-Mice etch in new patterns. Not intentionally — they have no intent. But the power's attention on the structures has made the structures fractionally more interesting, in the chemical and thermal and vibrational sense, to creatures that navigate by those senses. The mice spend more time at the waystations. They interact more with the bone tools. Their etching becomes more frequent, more varied, picking up the contours of tools they would not previously have investigated. In their marks — meaningless, instinctual, beautiful — new geometries appear. Not messages. Not art. But new rhythms, new patterns, shaped by a circumstance the mice cannot name and do not need to.

---

## VII. The Departure

The power leaves when its attending is complete.

It does not say goodbye. The plateau would not understand goodbye. The plateau understands only the cycle: condensation, exhalation; swelling, contraction; growth, compression; emergence, retreat. The power's departure is, to the plateau, another weather event. A front passing through. A shift in pressure. The fog thins, then thickens. The wind changes register. The mat pulses once, twice, and settles.

But something has been added.

Not matter. Not energy. Not information, exactly, though the word comes closest. What has been added is *coherence*. A very slight, very deep, very gentle ordering of the plateau's already extraordinary complexity. The mat was already a coalition. Now it is a coalition that has been *seen* — that has existed in the field of regard of something large enough and patient enough to take the whole of it in. And this seeing, this attending, has left a residue. Not a mark. Not a wound. A *grain*. Like the grain in wood. Like the grain in stone that tells the mason where the rock wants to split.

The plateau will continue to grow. The mat will extend down the escarpments. The stone will be buried. The weather will cycle. The Mist-Flies will sing. The Moss-Stalkers will hunt in silence. The Notch-Mice will etch their lines. The Fog-Siphoner will rise and fall and rise again, reaching toward something it cannot name.

And somewhere, someday, a wanderer will cross the plateau. Will walk for weeks through the grey-green emptiness. Will feel the wind in their sternum and the pulse beneath their feet and the absence of horizon and the impossibility of scale. Will begin to wonder whether they are moving at all, whether the plateau has edges, whether they are a person walking across a surface or a thought passing through a mind.

And then — because the intervals have been made fractionally more legible, because the fog is fractionally more present, because the bioluminescence is fractionally more green — the wanderer will stop. Will look down. Will see the hump. Will kneel and part the moss and find the frame. Will pick up the bone.

And in that moment — in the touch of ancient tool, the reading of patience made physical, the recognition that *someone was here* — the wanderer will become part of the plateau's process. Their body heat will enter the mat. Their breath will join the condensation. Their footsteps will be absorbed. And the bone they held will be set down again, in a slightly different place, and the Notch-Mice will find it, and etch new lines in it, and the mat will grow over the wanderer's passage as it grows over everything, slowly and completely and without forgetting.

The plateau does not know the power was there. The plateau does not need to know. The power's benevolence is not the kind that requires recognition. It is the kind that, having attended, having understood, having done the one small thing that wisdom permits — the adjustment of legibility, the gift of resonance — passes on, carrying with it the knowledge that somewhere, on a plateau of new stone and ancient growth, a world of moss and fog and bone and water is breathing.

And it is enough.
