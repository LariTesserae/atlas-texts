# What the Wise One Does When It Finds the Khas Plateau

---

It does not arrive. Arrival implies a before and an after, a crossing of a threshold. It is more accurate to say that it *attends*. Where before its attention was elsewhere — and elsewhere is a large place, larger than you would believe — now it is here. The plateau does not notice. The plateau does not notice things. But if it could, it would feel the way a stone feels when the sun comes from behind a cloud: a slight, unasked-for warming on one side.

The wise power does not have eyes, but if it did, they would go wide.

---

It sees the plateau first, before it sees the Listener, because the plateau is very large and the Listener is very small. And what it sees stops it — not in shock, not in alarm, but in the way a musician stops when they hear a single perfect note struck in a room they thought was empty.

*Oh*, it thinks, or whatever thinking looks like at its scale. *Oh, you are complete.*

This is not what it expected. Most places are not complete. Most places are in the middle of becoming something, or in the middle of having been something, or straining toward a state they cannot quite reach. The Khas Plateau is none of these. It has arrived at itself. The long erosion, the slow circuits of the herds, the mineral patience of the lichen, the silent industry of the basins — all of it, together, forms a system so old and so self-sustaining that it no longer requires the future. It will have one, of course. The future will come the way moisture comes to the stone: slowly, inevitably, leaving its bloom. But the plateau does not *need* it. It is already here. It is already this.

The wise power understands what it is looking at. It has seen closed loops before — ecosystems that feed themselves, stars that burn in equilibrium, mathematical structures that fold inward and become self-referential, perfect. But those were always small. A tide pool. A binary system. A proof. This is closed at the scale of a world. This is a plateau that has learned to metabolize *everything* — sound, heat, death, time, even the iron skeletons of machines that tried to take something from it — and turn all of it into more of itself. Grey on grey on grey. The most elegant thing the power has seen in a long time, and it has seen galaxies settle into their final configurations.

It wants to do nothing. It wants to simply watch. This is the first sign that it is truly wise: its first impulse is reverence, not intervention. The plateau does not need anything from it. To offer aid would be an insult, like offering water to the ocean. The plateau is so far past needing help that the concept doesn't apply.

So the power turns its attention, gently — the way you would turn your gaze from a sunset to a moth that has landed on your hand — to the small anomaly moving across the stone.

---

The Listener.

It sees the Listener the way the Listener has never been seen, not even by themselves: whole. It sees the footprints behind them, stretching back weeks, months, the long wandering track of a being that walked without destination and called it exploration. It sees the notebook — or the memory, or the habit of noting — in which the Listener has been writing down observations in a language that has words for *what* and *how* and almost no words for the silence between them. It sees the way the Listener's body has changed: thinner, slower, the breathing deeper, the heartbeat slightly slower than it was when they arrived, as though the plateau's cadence has been seeping into them like cold into stone.

And it sees the loneliness.

Not the loneliness of being without others. The Listener is surrounded by others — ten thousand grazers shifting in the dark, the Chitin-Swarms flashing over the basins, the Watchers patient on the ridges. The loneliness is of a different kind. It is the loneliness of *being the only one who needs to be known*. The grazers do not need to be known. The Watchers do not need to be known. The lichen does not need to be known. They simply *are*, in the grammar of the plateau, in the language of state. *This stone is cold. This herd is dense. This ridge is stable.* The Listener is the only creature on the plateau — perhaps the only creature the plateau has ever held — that needs something more than *is*. The Listener needs *meaning*. The Listener needs the silence to *mean something*, and the plateau — in its infinite, benign completeness — will not give it to them.

The Listener does not know this is what they need. They think they need to understand the plateau. They think the walking and the watching and the noting are getting them closer to something. They are right, but not in the way they think. They are not getting closer to understanding the plateau. They are getting closer to understanding what it is to be the only one listening.

The wise power sees all of this and feels — what? The closest word is *tenderness*. But not the tenderness of someone looking at something fragile. The tenderness of someone looking at something *brave*. The Listener is doing something the grazers and the Watchers and the Chitin-Swarms are not doing. The Listener is *offering attention to a place that does not require it*. The Listener is giving the plateau the one thing it has never had and never needed: a witness. A consciousness that says, *I see you. I do not understand you, but I see you.*

This is a gift. The plateau does not know it is a gift. The Listener does not know it is a gift. But the wise power knows, and it holds the knowledge the way the plateau holds the iron: slowly, carefully, letting it become part of the texture of things.

---

Now. What does the power do?

It considers its options. It has many. It could open the Listener's ears to the infrasound — let them hear, just once, the seismic conversation of the herds, the deep stone-language that has been flowing beneath their feet the entire time. It could slow the Listener's clock, let them experience a year in what felt like a minute, so they could see the ridge sharpen and soften, the basins fill and empty, the herds complete their circuit. It could show them the plateau from above — the true scale of it, the herds like dark rivers flowing through grey continents, the basins like silver eyes blinking open and shut with the seasons. Any of these would be gifts. Any of these would change the Listener forever.

But the power is wise, and wisdom is the art of knowing which gift is *right*.

To show the Listener the infrasound would be to say, *You were wrong — there was sound all along.* And this would help, briefly. But then what? The Listener would have a new sense, and they would use it the way they use their old ones: to observe, to note, to seek meaning. They would listen to the stone-language and try to translate it, and the translation would fail, because the stone-language is not *about* anything. It is not meaning. It is state. And the Listener would be lonely again, only now they would be lonely in two languages instead of one.

To slow their clock would be worse. It would steal from them the one thing that makes them what they are: the heartbeat-tempo, the urgency, the *need*. A Listener who could wait a thousand years would not be a Listener anymore. They would be a Watcher. They would perch. They would attend. And the gift of their attention — the particular, precious, human-scale gift of a consciousness that moves at the speed of breath and cares anyway — would be gone.

No. The power knows what to do. It has known since it first saw the Listener's footprints stretching across the stone. It knew the way the plateau knows: without urgency, without deliberation, in the grammar of what simply *is*.

It sits down.

---

It sits down next to the Listener.

Not visibly. Not in a shape. The plateau does not gain a new feature; the herds do not shift; the Watchers do not turn their heads. But the Listener feels it. They are lying on the stone at night — the deep charcoal that is never quite black, the ten thousand grazers breathing silently around them — and they feel something change.

Not in the air. Not in the temperature. In the *weight* of the silence.

The Listener has been lying here for many nights, feeling the silence as a pressure, as a presence, as the sound of a place that has outlived the need to make itself known. The silence has always felt like a wall — beautiful, perhaps, intricate, certainly, but a wall. A surface beyond which they cannot go. A barrier between them and the plateau's meaning.

Now the silence has *depth*.

It is the difference between looking at the surface of a lake and suddenly seeing through it. The silence is still there — the plateau has not changed, the herds have not changed — but the Listener perceives, for the first time, that the silence is not the absence of something. It is the *presence* of something so large and so slow and so patient that their ears have been registering it as nothing, the way their eyes would register a mountain as sky if they were close enough to its face.

The grazers are speaking. The Listener has always known this, intellectually, has always suspected it from the shifts in posture, the turns of head, the silent choreography of the herd. But now they *feel* it. Not hear it — their ears are the same ears, their air is the same air. But the silence between their heartbeats has become articulate. It has texture. It has *direction*. The Listener lies on the stone and feels, for the first time, the deep pulse of ten thousand bodies breathing in a rhythm that is not random, is not mechanical, but is *coordinated* — is a conversation carried on in a medium the Listener cannot name, through a channel they cannot locate, at a frequency they cannot measure, but that exists, that has always existed, that has been going on beneath them and around them and through them every moment they have been here.

The Listener does not hear the grazers say anything. There are no words, no messages, no meanings. What they feel is *presence*. The presence of other minds — or whatever the grazers have that serves as minds — attending to the same world, at the same time, from slightly different positions on the stone. The Listener feels, for the first time, that they are not the only one listening. They are the only one listening *for proof*, yes. They are the only one listening *for meaning*, yes. But they are not the only one listening. The grazers listen. They listen to the stone, to each other, to the moisture and the cold and the slow shift of the ridges. They listen the way the plateau breathes: constantly, without effort, as a condition of being alive.

And the Listener feels something break open inside them — not painfully, not dramatically, but the way ice breaks open in a basin when the water below it warms: a quiet yielding, a letting-go of a surface that has been holding for a long time. The Listener does not cry. Crying is sound, and sound has no purchase here. But something in their chest opens, and the silence flows in, and for one moment — one long, slow, plateau-moment — the Listener is not separate. They are a part of the circuit. They are a temporary geological event, yes. They are a brief, noisy interruption, yes. But they are an interruption *in* the silence, not an interruption *of* it. They are a note in the song, not a break in it. Their heartbeat is percussion in a symphony of stone, and it belongs, and it has always belonged, and the only thing that was ever wrong was that they couldn't hear it.

---

The wise power stays for as long as it needs to, which is not long. A moment at its scale. A night at the Listener's.

It does not speak. It has nothing to say that the plateau has not already said. It has only *attended* — turned its attention to a small, brave, lonely being on a vast, complete, unlonely stone — and in the act of attending, it has done the only thing that needed doing. It has made the Listener *known*. Not understood. Not translated. Not explained. Just known. Witnessed. Held in the attention of something large enough to hold them.

This is what the Listener was doing for the plateau, all along. Offering it the gift of witness. The power simply gave it back.

---

When the Listener wakes — if the Listener sleeps, if what happens at night on the Khas Plateau can be called sleep — the plateau is the same. The grazers are circulating. The Watchers are perching. The Chitin-Swarms are skittering over the ice-thin water. The iron rigs are rusting. The lichen is creeping. The stone is wearing down, one grain at a time, in the direction of a future that the plateau does not need but will accept with the same equanimity with which it accepts everything.

The Listener sits up. The stone beneath them is warm where their body has been. The air is cold. The sky is pale grey, sourceless, diffused.

The Listener listens.

And hears — still — nothing. The air is the same air. Sound does not propagate. The silence is complete.

But it is a different silence now. It is the silence of a place that has been *heard*. Not by the Listener — the Listener heard it long ago, in the way that you hear the ocean when you press a shell to your ear, which is to say: you hear your own blood, and you call it the sea. No. The silence has been heard by something the Listener will never see and cannot describe. And the knowledge of this — the knowledge that the silence is not speaking into a void, that somewhere, at some scale, the Listener's attention has been received and returned — changes nothing and everything.

The Listener stands. They begin to walk. The stone is sandpaper under their feet. The cushion-plants huddle in their crevices. The grazers flow around them like water around a stone, and the Listener flows with them, not as a member of the herd, not as a Watcher, not as a Chitin-Swarm, but as themselves: the Listener, the one who asks why, the one who offers attention to things that do not need it, the anomaly that is not an interruption but a gift.

They walk into the grey. The plateau metabolizes their footsteps. The lichen creeps. The iron rusts. The herds speak in stone. And the Listener — for the first time, for the first time — does not need to hear them.

They know they have been heard.

---

*The wise power moves on. It has many places to attend to, and the plateau is not one of them — the plateau needs nothing. But it carries the Listener with it, the way the stone carries the memory of a herd that passed over it: a faint impression, a warmth that fades, a trace of something that moved and was alive and asked, in the middle of all that silence, the most human question of all —*

**Why?**

*And the power holds the question the way the plateau holds the rust: gently, patiently, letting it become part of the texture of things. Not answering it. Not needing to. Some questions are not meant to be answered. They are meant to be carried. They are the proof that someone was listening.*
