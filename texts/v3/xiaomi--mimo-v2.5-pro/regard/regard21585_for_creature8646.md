The benevolent and wise power, upon finding the Terraces and the Lithoweavers, would **do very little.** Its action would be a **recognition**, not an intervention; a **listening**, not a command.

**First, it would perceive truly.**
It would see not just the wet stone and the moving water, but the **intentionality** woven into every curve and ridge. It would feel the collective consciousness of the Lithoweavers not as a mind in a brain, but as a **patience in the pattern**, a **will in the geometry**. It would understand that this is not a ruin, nor a garden, nor a temple in the human sense. It is a **living process**, a **ritual of water and time**, performed by its own structure. The "purpose" it seeks would dissolve. It would see that the purpose *is* the performance. The breathing cascade *is* the reason.

**Second, it would adjust its presence.**
A human observer is a seismic event; this power would not be. It would attune its awareness to the **timescale of the terraces**. Its attention would not flicker like a camera shutter but would settle like a new layer of mineral crust, adding its stillness to the profound stillness already present. It would not walk the ridges in boots, but would **become a quality of the light** – perhaps a deeper violet in the pre-dawn sky that touches the water just so, or a moment where the sun’s angle makes the mineral crust glow with a faint, inner luminescence the terraces have never known. Its presence would be **felt as a gentle correction**, a subtle harmony added to the existing symphony.

**It might offer a single, resonant gift.**
The terraces are perfect, but they are **alone**. They exist in a dialogue only with themselves, the mountain, and the sky. The wise power would not introduce life, for that would violate the terraces' sterile essence. Instead, it might **alter the dialogue**. It could:
*   **Change the quality of the snowmelt.** Not the chemistry, but its *memory*. The water would still hold minerals, but now it would also hold a faint, impossibly pure resonance of distant starlight, or the deep, slow vibration of the planet’s core. The Lithoweavers would not see this, but they would **taste it** in the accretion of their own substance. Their thoughts would gain a new, subterranean depth.
*   **Tune the silence.** The terraces are quiet, but the wise power could **refine the silence**. It might absorb the faint, residual echo of its own observation, leaving the quiet cleaner, more absolute. Or it might let the deep, groaning sigh of the stone carry a fraction further, so it reverberates not just through the terrace strata, but up into the thin air, a clearer signal to the listening cosmos.
*   **Leave a mirrored horizon.** In the air above the snowfields, at a height unseen from below, it might create a permanent, almost imperceptible distortion. Not a cloud, but a lens. On certain nights, the Lithoweavers, through their distributed awareness of the night sky’s reflection in their basins, would see not just the stars, but **the curved edge of their own mountain reflected back to them from the void**. A gentle reminder that they are a shape held within a larger shape.

**Finally, it would withdraw.**
Its intervention complete, it would **weave itself out** of the fabric of the place. It would leave no scar, no monument, no instruction. It would ensure its passage was as unnoticed as a single snowflake melting into a basin at midnight. The only trace would be the **slight alteration in the system’s balance** – the new depth in the water’s memory, the refined silence, the glimpse of self in the sky.

The terraces would continue. The water would flow. The Lithoweavers would accrete, their ancient thoughts now tinged with the faintest new resonance. They would not know a power had visited. They would only know, over the next century, that the light seems a little different when it hits the pewter water at evening. That their own slow groan carries a new, faint harmonic. That their world, which they believed was complete, now has a new, distant edge.

**The benevolent power’s act was not to change the Terraces, but to gently, respectfully, expand the definition of their perfection.** It did not heal, because nothing was broken. It did not build, because nothing was needed. It simply **acknowledged the masterpiece** by adding a single, almost undetectable note to its song, and then let the song continue, forever, on its own terms.
