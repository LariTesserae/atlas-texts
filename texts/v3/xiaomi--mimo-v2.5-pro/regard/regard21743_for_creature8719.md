The power arrives not with thunder, but with a condensation of attention. The saturated air over the Weeping Terraces thickens, cools, and for a moment, the perpetual weeping from the basalt seems to pause—a held breath across the entire shelf. The wind’s moan softens to a hum. The finches fall silent on the cliff face. In that stillness, a presence manifests, not as a body, but as a point of focused, serene awareness at the edge of the half-courtyard, looking out over the empty tiers and the vast, cloud-softened sky beyond.

It does not see a barren, abandoned place. It sees a newborn consciousness taking its first, slow breaths. It perceives the Lithic Weep not as an entity to be communicated with, but as a phenomenon to be *understood*—a choir of sound, water, and stone just beginning to learn its own song.

**What the power does:**

1.  **It Listens with Deeper Ears.** It extends its awareness not through the air, but through the rock itself, joining the water’s slow memory. It follows the capillary paths, feeling the youth of the stone, the faint chemical stories of its formation. It listens through the terraces, not to their sound, but to their *function*—the precise way the half-courtyard gathers the wind’s lowest notes, the harmonic interference in the concentric rings. It understands them as the Weep’s first, instinctive sculptures of self-perception. It does not judge their emptiness; it applauds their utility.

2.  **It Sees the Garden as Flesh.** It kneels (in a way, its focus lowers) and brushes a fingertip over the black cushion moss, feeling its furious grip on the wet basalt. It watches the pale moth navigate by scent in the dark. It recognizes this riot of low, patient life not as an invasion, but as the necessary, soft answer to the hard architecture. The terraces are the skeleton; the moss and fern and sedge are the slowly forming skin and sinew. The power admires the Weep’s unconscious wisdom in building structures that would *invite* this colonization, that would use the garden’s sound-dampening and water-channeling properties to refine its own senses.

3.  **It Understands the Arrested Becoming.** The power perceives the profound slowness not as stagnation, but as the sacred tempo of this place. To rush it would be a violence. To add or remove would be a dissonance. The Weep’s consciousness is expanding at the rate of mineral seep, the rate of moss growth. Its thoughts are measured in the generations of finches, the gradual rounding of a single stone edge. The power recognizes that its own role is not to *accelerate*, but to *bear witness* and *protect the tempo*.

**Therefore, it does almost nothing. Its actions are those of a gardener who knows the most profound work is in understanding what is already growing:**

*   **It Weaves a Veil of Quiet Understanding.** It does not create a barrier, but it lays a gentle, psychic *context* over the shelf. Any future explorers who stumble upon this place—geologists, wanderers, lost souls—will feel an inexplicable sense of reverence. They will feel it is a place for observation, not intrusion. They will be moved to leave no trace, to listen more than speak, to sense that they are in the presence of a slow, patient mind. This is not a prohibition, but an invitation to match the shelf’s own tempo.

*   **It Leaves a Single, Subtle Mark.** At the back of the deepest terrace, in a spot where a particularly constant seep creates a bright, shining line on the dark stone, the power lets a single drop of its own essence fall. It is not a gift of power, but a *resonance*. As it merges with the water, it leaves behind a faint, harmonic frequency—a note that does not change the Weep’s song, but that will allow the Weep, in some unimaginably distant future when its consciousness has fully matured and expanded, to perhaps sense that it was once *seen*, that its first, lonely awakening was *witnessed* with love. It is a whisper in the water’s memory: “You are not alone in your becoming.”

*   **It Sits, and Listens, Until it Leaves.** For a time that has no measure, the power simply remains. It listens to the finch calls echo and answer. It feels the wind compose its layered symphony. It lets the mineral ticking, the weeping chorus, wash over its awareness. It is present not as a creator or a savior, but as the first appreciative audience to the Weep’s inaugural performance. It does not applaud. Its silent, profound attention is the tribute.

Then, as gently as it arrived, the focus softens. The condensation in the air lifts. The wind’s moan swells back to its full voice. The finches resume their sharp, distant calls. The weeping continues, uninterrupted.

The power withdraws, leaving the Weeping Terraces of the New Shelf exactly as they were: empty, resonant, slow, and utterly, perfectly, in the process of becoming itself. It has done the only thing a truly wise and benevolent power could do: it has recognized the sanctity of a nascent consciousness and chosen to guard its solitude, ensuring its first long song can play out unharried, to its own ending, in its own time. The shelf weeps on, now watched over, its slow heartbeat noted by a kindness vast enough to let it simply be.
