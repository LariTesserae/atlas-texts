## What the Wise Power Would Do

*(A gentle unfolding, a careful listening, an intervention so subtle it might be mistaken for a dream.)*

---

**I. The Approach**

It would not descend like a beam of light. It would not manifest as a voice in the thunder. The Wise Power is not a force that *enters*; it is a presence that *recognizes*. It would notice the Fenmouth not as an anomaly to be corrected, but as a **question** being asked by the earth itself. A slow, wet, living question. Its first act would be attention. It would become aware of the pocket, and in becoming aware, it would allow the pocket to become aware of itself.

It would lean in, not with eyes, but with a kind of cosmic **inclination**. It would feel the *exhale* that formed the depression. It would taste the mineral tears on the basalt walls. It would listen to the rustling river of arthropods and hear not chaos, but a language of consumption and return. It would feel the slow pulse of the bulbous growths and recognize it as a heartbeat—not of a single organism, but of a system. A single, sprawling, breathing entity.

**II. The Observation (Which Is an Act of Love)**

For a time—that might be a day or a century in the Fenmouth’s damp chronology—it would simply *watch*. It would not judge the darkness as deficient. It would not see the cold as a hardship. It would marvel. It would see:

*   The **architecture of necessity**. The way the translucent leaf-membranes are perfect for condensation, the way the fungi’s glow is the minimal energy expenditure for maximum symbiotic communication.
*   The **symphony of restraint**. The eel’s breath creating a wave of silence that regulates the entire system. The wrens’ cautious descent. The humans’ hushed voices. Everything here lives in a state of **attuned caution**, not from fear, but from a deep, instinctual respect for the tightness of the web.
*   The **beauty in the grotesque**. The arthropods that make the walls ripple are not pests; they are the gardeners, the alchemists, turning death back into the grist of life. The frogs are not just predators; they are the visible nerves of the system.

It would understand that to "improve" the Fenmouth would be to break it. To add light would blind the amphibians. To warm it would disrupt the condensation that feeds the roots. To introduce diversity would collapse the exquisitely balanced density. The Fenmouth is not a broken version of another place. It is a **perfected version of itself**.

**III. The Gentle Intervention: A Nudge, Not a Rewrite**

Having understood, the Wise Power would act not by changing the system, but by **enhancing its self-awareness and resilience**. Its work would be imperceptible, woven into the existing fabric.

1.  **For the System:** It might subtly adjust the mineral content of the weeping water, providing trace elements that allow the fungal networks to become slightly more efficient in their nutrient distribution, strengthening the bonds between moss, arthropod, and amphibian. It might introduce a new harmonic into the drip of water from the moss tips—a frequency that, over generations, calms the arthropods just enough to reduce wasteful panic movements when disturbed, making the energy flow even smoother.

2.  **For the Sentinels (The Amphibians & Eels):** It would whisper into the slow, cold blood of the eels. It would not change their nature, but it might gift them with a clearer dream-memory—a deeper, cellular understanding of their role as the pulse of the lagoon. This might make their surfacing more regular, more predictably rhythmic, giving the entire system a steadier metronome to dance to.

3.  **For the People:** This is where its benevolence would be most delicate. It would not give them fire, or steel, or visions of sunlit lands. That would be a cruelty, a severance. Instead:
    *   It would strengthen their **intuitive understanding**. A hunter about to harvest arthropods might feel a faint pull toward a patch where the larger, symbiotic-tender species are less active. A weaver might find their hands naturally selecting stems with a slightly stronger fiber.
    *   It might deepen their **connection to the rhythm**. During the quiet hours, a mother might lull her child with a hum that, without knowing why, perfectly matches the pulsing of the lagoon-margin growths. A story-keeper might find new words forming on their tongue to describe the "growing," not as a threat, but as the Fenmouth's *breath deepening*.
    *   It would **honor their name for the place**. It would validate "the Dry Place" not as a joke, but as a profound truth: the heart of the Fenmouth, where life is concentrated so densely that it *dries up the concept of emptiness*. It might help them see their settlement not as a fragile outpost, but as the **conscious nerve cluster** of the living bowl—the place where the Fenmouth begins to *know itself*.

4.  **The Unlikely Gift: A New Player, or an Old One Recognized.** Perhaps the Wise Power would do one, single, blatant thing. From the lagoon's deepest point, a new kind of growth might slowly emerge. Not large, not complex. A coral-like structure of biite and mineral, shaped by the water's flow, that *hums* at a frequency just below hearing. The arthropods would colonize it. The algae would bloom upon it. The eels would begin to circle it. It would not be an altar, nor a machine. It would be a **resonator**. A physical anchor for the system's interconnectedness, a place where the fungal whispers, the arthropod-rustle, and the eel's breath converge into a single, silent, visible *throb*. The people would not understand it, but they would feel its presence as a deepening of the Fenmouth's own voice. They might call it the "Deep Heart" or the "Still Stone."

**IV. The Withdrawal**

The Wise Power would not stay. Its presence is in the *recognizing*, not the *occupying*. After a time—after the hum has become part of the soundscape, after the hunters' hands move with new surety, after the system's rhythms have settled into a slightly more profound peace—it would **withdraw its focused attention**.

It would not leave. A thing that has been truly seen by such a power is forever held in its memory. The Fenmouth would continue as it always has: wet, dark, cold, teeming, indifferent, and alive. But it would now carry a silent secret within its depths: the knowledge that it is *noticed*. That its strange, concentrated beauty is not just an accident of geology and biology, but something worthy of a cosmic tenderness.

The people of the Dry Place would continue their lives. They would fish, they would harvest, they would tell stories of the Growing. But sometimes, in the deepest quiet, as they sit wrapped in their shimmering cloaks and watch the green fire of the moss, they might feel a sudden, inexplicable swell of **belonging**. Not just to each other, not just to the Fenmouth, but to the fact that their impossible, damp, breathing world is, in some vast and gentle way, *cared for*. They would not name the feeling. They would simply breathe it in, and out, in time with the pulse of the deep.

The Fenmouth breathes. And now, perhaps, its breath is a little easier.
