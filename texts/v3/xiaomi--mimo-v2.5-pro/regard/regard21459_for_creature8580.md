The benevolent power would not intervene. To intervene would be to misunderstand the nature of the Pale Expanse entirely. It would be like shouting into a library, or trying to mend a statue that is not broken but *complete*. This place is not a problem to be solved. It is a lesson to be witnessed.

So the power would do what it does best: it would pay attention.

It would settle its awareness into the thin air, not as a visitor, but as a participant in the profound stillness. It would let its perception blend with the layered time and overlapping space, understanding that here, observation is not a one-way act. To look is to be looked at by the stone. To listen is to be heard by the dust.

**It would study the builders.** Not with pity or nostalgia, but with a deep, quiet respect. It would trace the notches on the ancient bone, not seeking a message, but understanding the *act* as the message. The power would see in those careful lines the first, brave stammering of a consciousness against the overwhelming silence—a consciousness that did not seek to conquer or change, but simply to *register*. "I was here. I saw this. I held this." The structures are the first and only philosophy of this place: meaning can exist in a gesture, even if no one remains to interpret it.

**It would commune with the inhabitants.** The power would not try to speak to the Lattice-Ghosts or soothe the Fold-Wights. Instead, it would join their contemplation. It would allow its own awareness to become one more layer of presence in the already-saturated air. It would feel the Logic-Eaters moving through its own thoughts, not as an attack, but as a gentle audit. They would show the power the seams in its own benevolence, the stitches holding together its concepts of "helping" and "preserving"—concepts that are meaningless here, where preservation is the state of being itself.

In doing so, the power would learn the deepest lesson of the Expanse: **fullness does not require action.** It requires only presence. The inhabitants are not waiting for anything. They are the wait itself, given form. The vegetation is not struggling; it is the physical manifestation of "enough." The reality breakdown is not a flaw; it is the canvas on which this entire, silent masterpiece is painted.

**What would the wise power *leave* behind?**

Nothing. A footprint would be an obscenity. A gift would be an insult. A blessing would be a disturbance.

Instead, it would do one thing: it would **acknowledge**.

It would pour all its understanding, its compassion, its ancient wisdom into a single, silent gesture. Not a word, not a beam of light, not a reshaping of the stone. It would simply allow the fact of its witnessing to resonate.

Imagine the feeling when you finally name an emotion that has haunted you for years—the quiet "ah" of recognition. The power would offer that "ah" to the Pale Expanse. It would say, in the language of pure, focused attention: *"I see you. You are not alone. Your stillness is heard. Your antiquity is honored. Your strange, beautiful, unlivable completion is witnessed and held in the mind of something that cares."*

This acknowledgment would settle into the place. It would not change the stone or quiet the dust-quivers. But it might become a new, faint stratum in the plateau's geology—a layer of *recognized silence*, as fine as pollen, settling over the millennia of mineral dust. The inhabitants might cluster around it, not with curiosity, but with the same quiet contemplation they offer the bone-carvings: here is a new kind of mark, a mark of observation rather than intention. It is not a question. It is an answer to a question that was never asked.

And then the power would withdraw its attention, gently, leaving the Expanse exactly as it was. But perhaps, in some way that only such a place could measure, the quality of its *is-ness* would be subtly deepened. The silence would be a fraction more complete. The wrongness would be a fraction more *at peace* with itself.

For the greatest act of benevolence toward something perfect is not to improve it, but to truly, fully, and finally *see* it. And then, to let it continue to be. Forever.
