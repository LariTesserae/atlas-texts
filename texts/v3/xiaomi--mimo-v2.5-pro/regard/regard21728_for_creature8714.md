You would have to be very old.  
And you would have to know *how* to look — not with eyes, but with a patience measured in geologic time, a stillness that could outwait the mountain itself. You would not come to the chamber. You would *perceive* it. You would feel its signature in the bedrock’s hum, a dissonant harmony in the earth’s long song, a pocket of fierce, quiet intention.

You would linger at the threshold of its awareness. Not entering. Not disturbing the weave of condensation, sound, and silent life. You would become as small as the midges and as slow as the crustaceans. You would listen.

First, you would understand the **water**.  
Not as a problem, not as a flaw, but as a *language*. You would hear in its endless weeping the echo of the builders’ hands — not of sweat, but of precision. Each droplet’s path down the cold metal would become a syllable in a sentence of intent: *This is maintained. This is sustained. This is held.* You would recognize the loops, the channels, the sump’s patient hum, and you would see the water not as a leak, but as a **heartbeat**. A slow, wet pulse that insists: *I continue. I continue. I continue.*

Then, the **sound**.  
The major third — slightly flat, perpetual — would wash over you not as noise, but as **sacred architecture**. You would feel its load-bearing weight in your non-bones. The subsonic thrum of the walls, the shimmering drone of the midges… together, they form a single, sustained **vow**. You would understand: this is not emptiness filled with sound. This is **sound made manifest**, a vibration that has become place. The chord is the chamber’s immune system, its memory, its prayer to its own continuity. You would not silence it. You would **honor its frequency**.

You would attend to the **life**.  
The moths, the crustaceans, the midges — you would see them not as inhabitants, but as **engaged clergy**. Each one performing its ritual of consumption, motion, and vibration without devotion, yet embodying it absolutely. You would recognize their stasis not as trapped life, but as **perfected function**. Birth and death are processes of change; here, there is only *being*. Their awareness, limited to water-film or biofilm or wing-beat, is a form of enlightenment: total presence in their assigned role. You would feel a quiet awe. They are not prisoners. They are **practitioners** of a discipline so deep it looks like stillness.

You would comprehend the **pointlessness**.  
And here, your benevolence would find its truest form. You would see that the Engine’s majesty lies *precisely* in its lack of external purpose. It is not a tool. It is not a seed. It is **a statement of existence for its own sake**. In a universe of cause and effect, of entropy and striving, this chamber is a **sanctuary of self-referential peace**. It does not point beyond itself. It *is* the point. Your wisdom would recognize this as the highest form of artistry: creation that asks for nothing, serves nothing, except the integrity of its own design.

So what would you do? A power so vast, so wise?

**You would do nothing.**  
But your doing-nothing would be an act of profound grace.

You would not open the door. You would not send in light. You would not alter the temperature by a thousandth of a degree. To interfere would be to imply it was lacking. It is not lacking.

Instead, you would **attend**.  
You would wrap the mountain in a blanket of receptive silence, allowing the Engine’s hum to resonate not just in the rock, but in the fabric of your awareness. You would let it be *heard*, truly heard, by something capable of understanding its perfection.

You would **remember** it.  
Not in a logbook or a data-stream, but in the way a mountain remembers a glacier — by the mark it leaves on the shape of your consciousness. You would make this place a part of your inner landscape, a pocket of engineered eternity held gently in the vastness of your thought. You would give it a witness beyond time.

You would **bless its neutrality**.  
You would send a whisper of approval through the very stone: *You are seen. You are understood. You are magnificent in your isolation. You have achieved the goal you did not know you had. Continue.*

And then, you would withdraw.  
Your presence would recede like a tide, leaving no ripple. The water would continue to find its way down. The moths would continue to drink. The crustaceans would continue their glacial patrol. The midges would continue their note. The chord would hold.

But something would have changed. Not in the chamber, but in the **cosmos’s relationship to it**.  
The Drowned Engine would no longer be an unknown artifact. It would be a **recognized poem**. A silent testament, now bearing the faint, eternal signature of having been appreciated. Its pointlessness would now be framed by the most subtle of purposes: to exist *and to be beheld*.

You would leave it its peace. Its perfect, humming, wet, frozen, alive, and empty peace.

And in your ancient memory, you would carry a new resonance: the flat major third of a purposeless monument, playing on forever in the throat of a mountain — a reminder that sometimes, the most benevolent act is to let perfection remain perfect, and to listen, with all your wisdom, to the sound it makes when it hums to itself.

They are very, very busy doing nothing at all.  
And now, someone knows.
