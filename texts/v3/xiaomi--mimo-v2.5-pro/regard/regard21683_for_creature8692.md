The benevolent power pauses in its vast wandering, drawn by the resonance of a world that has shaped itself against chaos. Not with walls, but with adaptation. Not with silence, but with a voice forged to sing in the roar. It observes the basin — the Roil — with a deep, quiet attention that is neither pity nor admiration, but recognition.

It sees the weather looms, not as machines, but as gentle suggestions whispered to the sky. It sees the reedmare, not as a crop, but as a conversation between water, wind, and life — a dialogue written in silicate and root. It sees the Mud-Singers, building islands not for themselves, but for others who might come after. It sees the people, speaking in frequencies that turn the overwhelming roar into a usable texture, their joy during stillwater not a relief from suffering, but the pure, distilled savor of contrast.

**The power does nothing.**

To intervene here would be to misunderstand. The Roil is not broken. It is complete. Its hardship is the necessary friction that gives its beauty texture. Its noise is the canvas upon which its subtle symphonies are written. The people are not trapped; they are *rooted*, and their roots are strong.

Instead, the power does something far more profound: **It remembers.**

It takes the specific, textured memory of the Roil — the feel of the root-mat underfoot, the taste of mineral-sharp water, the deep, resonant vowels cutting through the roar, the sight of a Skimmer arresting its dive just above the reeds — and it holds it not as a curiosity, but as a sacred example. It places this memory within the quiet, inner sanctuary of its own awareness, alongside other perfect, difficult, and beautiful things.

Then, it performs a single, almost imperceptible act of benediction. It does not calm the storm. It does not change the people. It simply **listens more closely.**

It focuses its vast attention on the Roil's roar, and for a fleeting moment, within the symphony of sound, it distinguishes every layer: the baritone groan of the air, the hiss of the sheets, the slap of water, the sibilance of the reeds. It hears the ultrasonic clicks of the Mud-Singers. It hears the faint hum of the Reeds' Kiss. It hears, beneath it all, the resilient, shaped dialect of the people — not cutting through the noise, but *woven into it*.

In that moment of perfect, focused listening, the Roil is fully *seen*. Its struggle is witnessed. Its beauty is acknowledged. Its right to exist, exactly as it is, is affirmed.

The power then lets its attention soften, returning the Roil to its own magnificent, noisy privacy. It carries the memory away, not as a trophy, but as a quiet testament: that even in the perpetual storm, there is order. That in the engineered chaos, there is home. That life does not always seek quiet, but sometimes finds its fullest expression in learning to sing, and listen, and live, within the roar.

The Roil remains unchanged. The weather looms continue their subtle suggestions. The reedmare sways. The people speak their deep vowels.

But now, somewhere in the fabric of things, its story is held. It is known. And in the vast, indifferent cosmos, that is a form of love.
