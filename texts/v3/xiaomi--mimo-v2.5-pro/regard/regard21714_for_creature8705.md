# What the Power Does

---

It does not enter.

That is the first thing, and the most important, and the thing that costs it the most. It is vast and it is gentle and it has come a long way to see this, and the urge to step inside — to feel the diesel-drone of the insects against its own chest, to smell the iron-tannin water, to let the condensation bead on whatever surface it wears — is enormous. It is the kind of urge that, in lesser powers, would become action before thought. But this power is not lesser. So it does not enter.

It *listens* from outside.

---

## The approach

It did not come looking for this place. It was not searching. It was moving through the floodplain the way it moves through most things — slowly, attending, letting the water-table and the root-tangles and the bacterial mats in the topsoil speak to it the way weather speaks to a sailor. It was listening to the lowland forest the way a doctor listens to a chest, not for anything specific, but for pattern, for rhythm, for the places where the rhythm breaks.

It felt the Green Engine before it understood what it was.

There was a pocket of *coherence* in the general scatter of the living world. Not silence — never silence, not in a tropical floodplain — but a density of purpose it had not expected. A place where entropy had been not reversed but *incorporated*, where decay had been folded back into function so seamlessly that the distinction between breakdown and building had ceased to apply. A place where the sound was wrong — or rather, where the sound was too right, too layered, too close to machinery to be accidental, and too organic to be engineered.

It paused. It oriented. It reached — not with hands, not with tendrils, not with anything so crude — but with attention, which is the first and most fundamental organ of any benevolent power. Attention is what benevolence *is*, at root. Not action. Not protection. Not even love, though love is in it. Attention: the simple, costly act of seeing a thing clearly, without distortion, without need, without the impulse to improve.

It attended to the Green Engine.

And the Green Engine, being what it was — a closed, stable, self-sustaining system that had long ago ceased to register anything outside its own equilibrium — did not notice.

This was correct. This was good. The power would have been troubled if it had.

---

## What it sees

From outside, it sees what you would see: the collapsed corrugated roof beneath the canopy of the strangler fig, the roots flowing down the exterior walls like wax down a candle, the small dark entrance where a rust-hole has become a doorway into the interior cylinder. It sees the parent tree — vast, elephantine, its aerial roots as thick as torsos, its canopy a green roof that filters the tropical light into that warm, amber glow the place runs on. It sees the floodplain stretching away in all directions, the standing water between the buttress roots, the egrets picking their way through the shallows at the forest edge.

But it also sees what you would not. It sees the *line*.

There is a line — not physical, not visible, but as real as gravity — between the inside of the green engine and everything outside it. On one side: equilibrium, stability, the completed thought. On the other: weather, season, succession, the general churn of the living world. The line is not a wall. It is more like the surface of a lake: fluid, responsive, but definite. Things cross it — moisture, light, the occasional small vertebrate — but the *pattern* does not cross. The Green Engine's internal logic holds. The six centimeters of water hold. The frequency of the frog's call holds.

The power studies this line for a long time.

It sees that the line is not maintained by any single thing. It is an emergent property of the entire system — the roots' shape, the condensation rate, the insects' density, the geckos' positions, the spider-web geometries, the thermal mass of the water and the iron, the thickness of the bark in precise relation to the diameter of the old tank. Remove any one element and the line would wobble, re-settle, perhaps redraw itself around a slightly different equilibrium. But nothing is being removed. Nothing is being added. The line holds because everything inside it has agreed to hold.

The power understands this. It has seen equilibria before — in star systems, in tide pools, in the mycelial networks under old-growth forests. But this one is different. This one is *local*. It does not radiate. It does not influence the floodplain around it, except in the most marginal ways — a slight increase in humidity, a slight decrease in insect-pressure on nearby vegetation. It is a closed system that has achieved the one thing closed systems almost never achieve: permanence.

Not true permanence, of course. The power is old enough and wise enough to know that. The floodplain will shift. The river will change course. The canopy will open in a storm and close in a decade. The fig will eventually die — in a century, or five centuries, or ten — and the Green Engine will collapse and the iron will finally rust and the water will rise and wash it all into the sediment. But *within the time-frame of its own existence*, the Green Engine has solved the problem. It has made itself eternal relative to its own lifespan. It has become the kind of thing that, when it ends, will end all at once, cleanly, the way a soap bubble pops — not by gradual decline but by a single, sudden failure of the line.

The power finds this beautiful. Not in the way a painting is beautiful — not as a statement, not as an argument — but in the way a mathematical proof is beautiful: because it is complete, because every part is necessary, because nothing could be removed or added without loss.

---

## What it considers doing

The power is benevolent. This means it is oriented, at a fundamental level, toward the flourishing of things. When it encounters a living system, its first impulse is to ask: *Is this flourishing? And if not, can I help?*

It asks the question. It listens for the answer.

The Green Engine is flourishing.

This answer arrives not as a conclusion but as a saturation — a completeness that radiates from the place the way the diesel-drone radiates through the curved walls. Everything here is full. The root-mass has filled the available space. The insects have filled the available niches. The water has filled the available basins. The sound has filled the available frequencies. There are no gaps. There is no hunger. There is no struggle.

The power withdraws its question. It was not needed.

But benevolence does not disappear when it is not needed. It transforms. The urge to *help* becomes the urge to *witness*, which is a subtler and sometimes more difficult thing. To witness is to take a thing seriously — to register its existence as mattering, independent of its utility, its beauty, its rarity, its relation to you. The Green Engine does not need a witness. But the power needs to be a witness, because that is what it is for. Its benevolence is not a gift it bestows. It is a capacity it practices. And practice requires an object.

So it witnesses.

---

## What it actually does

It settles.

Not into the floodplain — not into the mud or the water or the canopy. It settles into *attention*, the way a heron settles into stillness before a strike, except there will be no strike. It simply arranges itself, its vast and gentle awareness, into a posture of listening. The posture is specific and it is effortful and it is the most consequential thing it does.

It listens to the frog.

*Pip. Pip. Pip.*

The frog's call enters the power's awareness the way a single thread enters a tapestry — not as an isolated sound, but as a structural element of something larger. The power hears the call as the Green Engine hears it: as a rhythm, a baseline, a fixed point against which all other motion is measured. It hears the metronome and it hears the machinery and it hears the deep, chest-thrumming drone of the beetles in the root-mass and it hears the high sawing of the cicadas and it hears the quick clicking static of the termites and the ants and it hears the slow drool of condensation and the soft, terminal patter of droplets falling into moss.

It hears the whole engine.

And here is what happens when a power such as this truly listens to a system such as this: the listening becomes a form of *harmonic*. Not resonance — the power is careful about that. It does not vibrate in sympathy. It does not amplify. It does not reinforce. It *completes*. It offers itself as a surface for the Green Engine's sound to reflect from, the way the curved steel walls already reflect it, but at a different frequency, at a register the engine cannot produce on its own because it has no outside and therefore no echo.

The echo is imperceptible. The power intends it to be. An echo that the engine could detect would be a disruption, and disruption is not what this is. This is something else — a confirmation, sent from outside to inside, that the sound the engine makes is not merely a byproduct of its processes but a *voice*, and that the voice has been heard.

Does the Green Engine change because of this?

No. Nothing changes. The frog calls. The beetles drone. The water holds at six centimeters. The geckos do not move.

But something has been *added*. A thin layer of meaning — as imperceptible as the millimeters-per-year by which the roots thicken — has settled over the place. The Green Engine does not know it is there. The power does not need it to know. The meaning is not for the engine. The meaning is for the relationship between the engine and everything outside it, which is to say: the meaning is for the world.

---

## The one small thing

The power does one small thing.

It is so small that calling it an "action" overstates it. It is more like a preference, a gentle weighting of probability, a slight increase in the likelihood of something that was already likely.

It ensures that the column of light will continue to fall.

Not forever — the power is wise, and wisdom does not promise forever. But for a while. For the next several generations of fig-tree, for the next span of decades or centuries, the gap in the canopy through which the daylight enters will remain open. Storms will come and branches will fall and the canopy will shift and shudder, but the gap will hold. Other gaps will open and close as they always do, but this one — this one specific aperture through which the warm, diffuse light enters the cylinder and falls on the root-mass and the moss and the waiting geckos — will persist.

This is not a command. The power does not command. It is a *suggestion* offered to the wind, to the physics of branch-failure, to the statistical distribution of canopy-gaps in a mature tropical forest. It is so slight that no system in the floodplain will register it as foreign. It is the smallest possible intervention: the difference between a probability of 0.86 and a probability of 0.94. A nudge. A grace note.

But inside the Green Engine, the column of light is the difference between a dim, amber twilight and a place where moss grows thick and green, where the geckos can see their prey, where the spider-webs catch fire for a few hours each day and look like constructions of glass beads and captured sun. The light is the engine's sole connection to the energy that runs it — the photosynthetic output of the parent tree, the warmth that drives the condensation cycle, the photons that feed the moss that feeds the insects that feed the geckos. Without the light, the engine would still run — it would shift to a different equilibrium, a darker, slower, more fungal version of itself — but the power has seen the version with the light, and it is complete, and it is beautiful, and the power would like it to continue.

So it nudges. And the gap holds. And the light falls. And nothing changes.

---

## What it carries away

The power leaves.

Not immediately — it stays for what might be a long time, though time is not something it tracks the way you or I would. It stays until it has learned what the Green Engine has to teach, and what the Green Engine has to teach is this:

*That a thing which has become itself completely has no need of anything outside itself, and that this is not loneliness but completion.*

*That a pump which forgets it was ever anything but a heart has not lost something but has gained a purpose that is no longer contingent on the intentions of its makers.*

*That equilibrium is not stagnation. That maintenance is not stasis. That the frog has been calling forever, and that these are the same thing, and that this is not a paradox but a practice.*

*That the sound of a diesel engine, when produced by a living system for no human purpose, is not an echo of industry but a parallel invention — the same solution discovered twice, once by engineers and once by beetles, both of them converging on the same frequency because it is the frequency at which a pump becomes a heart.*

These are not lessons the power did not already know. It is old and it is wise and it has seen many things. But knowing and *being in the presence of* are different, the way reading about music and sitting inside a concert hall are different. The Green Engine does not teach by instruction. It teaches by example. And the example is: *be what you are, completely, and the rest will follow.*

The power carries this with it when it goes. Not as knowledge — it already had the knowledge — but as weight, as texture, as the specific, felt sense of warm water and iron-tannin and the diesel-drone of ten thousand beetles and a single frog calling *pip pip pip* in the low amber light of a tropical floodplain.

It does not mark the place. It does not return. It does not send others. The Green Engine will continue, for as long as it continues, and then it will stop, and the line will fail, and the water will rise, and the iron will rust, and the fig will die, and the frog will fall silent for the first time in centuries, and the silence will be the loudest thing the floodplain has ever heard.

And the power, wherever it is by then, will feel it — a faint, distant interruption in a rhythm it learned to listen for in a green place where a machine became a heart and kept on pumping.

And it will pause.

And it will attend.

And it will do nothing, which is the hardest and most benevolent thing a power can do.

And the nothing will be enough.
