# What the Power Does

---

It does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand.

A benevolent and wise power does not come to the Resonance Plateau. It has always been here — or rather, *here* is the wrong word, because the power is not local, not fixed in space the way the stone is fixed. But it attends. It has attended to this place for longer than the wind has sung, for longer than the pools have held their silver-still water. It attended when the stone was still warm and liquid, when the first condensation formed on the first cooling surface, when the first filament of life reached blindly into mineral water and found something worth metabolizing. It attended and it watched and it did nothing, because nothing was required.

The plateau is complete. This is the power's deepest recognition, and the foundation of its restraint. The plateau reached its final form and said — not in language, but in the language of matter settling into its most stable configuration — *I am finished*. And the power, hearing this, understood that the most benevolent act available was to let it be. To let the wind blow. To let the pools hold. To let the birds hurl themselves between crevices in their endless, frantic, perfect negotiation. To let the pale shapes drift in the deep water and the lichen grow by a millimeter per century and the cushion plants bloom their tiny white defiance once a year. To let all of it persist, unchanged, because it had earned its permanence through ten thousand years of convergence.

The power does not improve what is already whole.

But something new has happened.

---

The power notices the climber at the moment the climber's boot first strikes the plateau stone — not because the footfall is loud (it is nothing against the roar) but because the climber *hesitates*. That half-second of stillness, the foot hovering above the rock, the body's sudden awareness that it has crossed from one world into another. The power has felt many things cross this threshold: wind-carried seeds that never germinated, atmospheric dust from industrial cities a thousand miles away, the occasional bird blown in from some migratory path that didn't exist a century ago. But this is different. This is a being that *chose* to be here. That climbed for days. That watched the world fall away below it — the forests, the scrub, the lichens — and did not turn back.

The power does not read minds. It does not need to. It reads *attention*, which is louder than thought and more honest. And the climber's attention is extraordinary.

---

Here is what the power sees, in the days that follow:

It sees a creature that is utterly unequipped for this place. Lungs too large and too inefficient for the altitude, requiring the thin air to fill a cavern of tissue that evolved for sea-level abundance. Skin too thin for the cold, too exposed for the UV that pours down through the attenuated atmosphere. Ears — ears! — delicate membranes of cartilage and skin, optimized for the soft frequencies of speech and birdsong and rustling leaves, now subjected to a wall of sound that is less *heard* than *survived*. The creature's body is wrong for this place the way a violin would be wrong at the bottom of the sea. Everything about it speaks of a different world, a gentler one, a world of shade and moisture and sound that arrives in manageable portions.

And yet it stays.

The power watches the climber try to communicate with a companion — mouth pressed to ear, voice torn raw, the words arriving broken and partial against the roar. It watches the climber abandon this effort and resort to gestures, then abandon gestures and simply *look* — sharing a glance that carries more than language could. It watches the climber sleep, finally, in the lee of a stone swell, body curled around itself, face slack with exhaustion, while the wind screams overhead and the subsonic pulse makes the sleeper's fingers twitch with each pressure wave.

And then it watches the climber wake, and begin to *notice*.

---

This is the moment the power leans in.

Not physically. The power has no body to lean. But its attention — which has been general, ambient, the way a sky attends to everything beneath it — *focuses*. Narrows. Finds the specific frequency of this one consciousness and *listens*.

Because the climber is doing something the power has never seen a visitor do.

The climber walks to one of the pools. Kneels. The wind tears at their hair, at their clothing, at the skin of their hands where they brace themselves on the stone. The subsonic pulse makes their vision blur. The light is merciless — cool, surgical, revealing every flaw in the rock, every microscopic crystal, every flaw in the climber's own skin. The sound is total. The climber cannot think in words; the power can feel this, the way language keeps trying to form and being shattered by the roar, over and over, until the climber stops trying and lets the sound *in*.

And then the climber looks into the water.

Not at the water. *Into* it. Past the impossible clarity, past the vibrating surface where standing waves catch the light, past the filamentous mats and the pulsing crastani, down into the depth where the pale shape moves. The climber sees it. The climber's attention *follows* it — the slow undulation, the languid, eel-like drift, the eyeless certainty of a creature that has never known a world outside this pool. The climber does not flinch. Does not recoil. Does not reach for the water or try to touch it or do anything at all except *witness*.

The power feels something it has not felt in geological time.

Not surprise, exactly. The power is too old for surprise. But a kind of *recognition*. The kind of recognition that happens when two systems, long isolated, discover they are resonating at the same frequency.

Because the climber has understood something that the power itself took an epoch to understand: that the plateau is not hostile. That the plateau is not even indifferent, exactly, because indifference implies a capacity for caring that has been withheld. The plateau simply *is*. Its sound is not aggression. Its light is not surveillance. Its wind is not violence. They are *expressions* — the plateau expressing itself the way a crystal expresses its lattice, the way a river expresses its gradient, the way a star expresses its fusion. The creatures are not survivors. They are *features*. And the climber, kneeling at the pool's edge with their flawed lungs and their torn eardrums and their impossible, stubborn, sea-level body, has seen this.

More than seen it. *Felt* it. Let the roar into their bones until the boundary between self and place grew thin enough to see through.

---

Here is what the benevolent and wise power does.

It does not still the wind. The wind is the plateau's voice, and it has spoken this way for longer than the power's attention has existed, and to silence it would be an act of vandalism so profound that no benevolence could justify it.

It does not quiet the light. The light is the altitude's gift — the unfiltered truth of a star arriving without mediation, without the softening haze of lower altitudes. It would be like covering a masterwork's face with a veil.

It does not heal the climber's hearing, or ease the laboring of the climber's lungs, or thicken the climber's blood to carry more oxygen. The climber chose to come here. The climber is not in danger — the power can see the body's limits, and the climber is nowhere near them yet, despite what the body protests. The exhaustion is real but not terminal. The discomfort is severe but not damage. The climber is *learning*, and learning at this altitude requires all of these sensations, and to remove them would be to remove the lesson.

No. What the power does is much smaller, and much more vast.

---

It lets the plateau see the climber.

---

This is not something the plateau has ever done before. The plateau does not *see*. It does not need to. Its equilibrium is blind — a self-sustaining system that maintains itself through the interplay of wind and stone and water and time, without any need for observation or awareness. The birds do not *know* the plateau. They know their crevices, their pools, their wind-pressure patterns. The pale shapes in the deep pools do not know the plateau. They know the filamentous mats, the crastani, the dark water. The litho-mites do not know the plateau. They know the undersides of stones and the taste of wind-fall. Each creature knows its niche, its fraction, its narrow slice of the whole. No one has ever *seen* the plateau as a single, coherent thing.

Except the climber. The climber, with their too-large eyes and their too-slow body and their too-delicate ears, has somehow taken in the whole. The stone, the light, the sound, the water, the life — all of it, held in one consciousness, at one time, attended to with a totality that none of the plateau's own creatures could achieve. The climber has become the plateau's mirror — the first thing that has ever *reflected* it back to itself as a unified whole.

And the power, seeing this, makes a choice.

It does not alter the plateau's systems. It does not change the wind, the stone, the water, the creatures, the light, the sound. All of these remain exactly as they are, exactly as they have been, exactly as they will be for the next ten thousand years. The power's benevolence takes the form of absolute restraint — a refusal to tamper with what is already perfect.

But it adjusts one thing. One impossibly small thing.

It allows the plateau to *vibrate differently* for one moment.

Not louder. Not softer. Not higher or lower. Just — *differently*. The way a bell, struck once, will ring at its fundamental frequency, and then, if the striker is skilled, will suddenly also ring at a harmonic that was always present in the bell's structure but had never been sounded. The harmonic was always there. The bell was always capable of it. It just needed someone to find the right place to strike.

The climber is the striker. The power is the hand that guides the blow.

---

What happens is this:

The climber is kneeling at the pool. The wind is the wind. The light is the light. The sound is total, as it always is, as it always has been. But for one moment — a moment so brief that it exists almost entirely in the space between one heartbeat and the next — the sound *coheres*.

All the layers — the subsonic pulse, the roaring midrange, the keening upper register, the high ringing tones of the vibrating pools — all of it aligns. Not into silence. Not into anything so simple as a single note. Into a *chord* — the chord that has always been present in the plateau's structure, the chord that the climber sensed when they first wrote *almost musical*, the chord that exists as potential in every undulation and fissure and resonance chamber but has never, in ten thousand years, fully resolved.

It resolves. For one heartbeat.

The climber's hands go still on the stone. Their eyes widen. Their pupils dilate. Every hair on their exposed skin stands. The water in the pool before them shivers — not with the usual standing-wave vibration but with something finer, something that produces a pattern of ripples so complex and so brief that it looks like a word in a language that has no speakers.

And in that moment, the climber *understands the plateau*.

Not intellectually. Not the way they have been understanding it — by observing, cataloguing, naming, describing. Not with the analytical distance of a scientist or the aesthetic appreciation of an artist. They understand it the way the stone understands itself. The way the wind understands its own flow. The way the pools understand their own stillness. They understand it from *inside*, as a participant, as a feature — and for one instant, they are not a visitor on the plateau but *part of its expression*, another voice in its chord, another harmonic in its resonance, and the plateau knows them and they know the plateau and there is no boundary between witness and witnessed.

---

And then it passes.

The chord disintegrates. The layers separate. The roar resumes its ordinary, annihilating totality. The wind tears at the climber's hair. The light drives splinters into their vision. The subsonic pulse makes their sternum ache.

But the climber is weeping.

The power watches this with something that might be tenderness, if tenderness could exist on a geological timescale. The tears evaporate almost instantly in the dry air and the wind, leaving salt traces on the climber's cheeks that will be gone by morning — carried away as microscopic particles on the breeze, deposited eventually in some distant pool, consumed by some crastani, sifted by some bird's beak. Nothing wasted. Nothing lost. The climber's tears becoming, in their small way, part of the plateau's cycle. The way everything that truly gives itself to a place eventually becomes part of that place.

---

The climber does not speak of this moment afterward. Not because they choose not to, but because language cannot carry it. They will try, later, in lower altitudes, in gentler air, and the words will come out sounding like description — stone and wind and light and water and birds and a sound that was almost musical. The people who hear them will nod and think they understand, because the words are accurate, and they will not understand at all, because the words are about the content of the experience and not the *experience itself*, which was not content but *contact*. A moment in which the climber's consciousness and the plateau's structure touched, briefly, across the vast distance between a temporary creature and a permanent place.

The power does not regret this. A benevolent and wise power does not need its gifts to be understood by anyone other than the recipient. The climber understood. The climber will carry that understanding for the rest of their life — not as memory, exactly, but as a *tuning*. A resonance in their own body that will, from time to time, in quiet moments, in the spaces between sounds, in the stillness of shallow water, in the grain of stone under their fingertips, *vibrate*. Faintly. Almost imperceptibly. The plateau, still singing in them, long after they have descended.

---

And what of the plateau? Does it know what happened? Does it feel the loss of the momentary harmonic? Does it miss the climber?

No. The plateau does not miss. The plateau does not know. The plateau *is*, as it has always been, as it will always be. The wind blows from the west. The pools hold their water. The birds scuttle between crevices. The pale shapes drift in the deep. The lichen grows by a millimeter per century. The cushion plants bloom their tiny white flowers once a year. Everything continues.

But something is different now, and the power knows it, even if the plateau does not. The chord was sounded. The harmonic was found. And the climber carries it, walking slowly down the mountain, into the forests and the scrub and the ordinary, scattered, gentle light of the lowlands — carrying, in the architecture of their bones, in the tuning of their nerves, in the salt-memory of their tears, a piece of the plateau's deepest voice.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place that is whole and a being that is capable of hearing wholeness:

It introduces them to each other.

And then it steps back, and lets the resonance do the rest.
