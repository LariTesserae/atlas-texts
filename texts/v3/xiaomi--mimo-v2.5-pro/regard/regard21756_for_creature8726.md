The power arrives not as a being, but as a change in attention. It is not a figure stepping onto the ridge. It is the ridge *becoming seen*. The act of witness is so profound it feels like a physical force, a gentle weight settling over the raw world, not to crush it, but to steady it, like a hand placed on the shoulder of someone trembling.

The benevolent power does not have eyes, but it perceives. It perceives the world not as a collection of objects, but as a sentence being spoken. The raw basalt is a verb. The suspended ocean is a noun of impossible scale. The upward rain is a modifier, a constant, trembling adjective. And the Licheners—the faint, iridescent stain on the north faces—are the very first, hesitant punctuation. A comma. A pause for breath in the world's first utterance.

Its first act is to listen. It listens past the groan, past the roar, past the hum. It listens to the *silence between the hums*. It hears the slow, crystalline thought of the Licheners, that geological pondering, and it recognizes it not as life, but as *proto-thought*. The capacity for pattern. The power is not impressed by complexity, but by *potential for complexity*. And here, in this screaming emptiness, the potential is a whisper so quiet it is almost indistinguishable from the vibration of stone.

The power smiles, if a concept can smile. It is the smile of a master calligrapher seeing a student’s first, shaky stroke, knowing the hand that made it holds the promise of a universe of strokes.

What does a benevolent power do with a blank page?

It does not write on it.

It adjusts the light.

The diffuse, blue-green illumination begins to change. The power reaches, not physically, but *directionally*. It touches the laws of refraction as they apply to the upper reaches of the suspended ocean. With the gentleness of a thought, it encourages the water’s suspended continent to settle into a slightly more stable harmonic. The groan of the ocean deepens, then softens, finding a resonant frequency that matches the fundamental vibration of the basalt ridges below. The sound becomes less a roar of protest and more a hum of agreement. The water and the rock begin to sing the same note.

The effect is subtle, but transformative. The ceaseless, chaotic turbulence on the underside of the ocean smooths, just fractionally. The “upward rainfall” lessens in its frantic cycle, becoming a steadier, gentler drizzle. More importantly, the great lensing bands of light that sweep across the landscape slow their pace. They linger now. Where before they were fleeting glances, they become long, contemplative gazes.

The power observes its work. A band of focused, golden-green light pauses over a ridge where the Lichen stains are slightly thicker. It holds there for a subjective eon—an objective century. Under this sustained, nurturing radiance, something happens. The Licheners’ metabolic waltz quickens, imperceptibly. Their growth rate, measured in microns per millennium, increases by a fraction of a percent. Their iridescent sheen deepens, the oily rainbows becoming more vivid, more defined.

The power has not given them energy. It has given them *time*. A more consistent, more patient rhythm. It has turned the world’s frantic, noisy birth pang into a deep, steady breath.

Next, it turns its attention to the mineral moisture. The briny film that clings to the rock is the Licheners’ nursery. The power adjusts the electrochemical potential between the raw stone and the saltwater, coaxing the very first hint of clay from the reaction—not as a substance, but as a *capacity*. It makes the stone infinitesimally more receptive to holding water, to forming tiny, intricate cups and channels. It is teaching the landscape to cradle.

And then, it does its most delicate work. It finds the faintest, most nascent pattern in the Licheners’ distributed crystalline thought—the first glimmer of a *preference*. A tendency to orient their filaments slightly more toward the east during the light-gaze. The power does not alter this preference. It *honors* it. It reinforces the pattern in the light, in the stone’s vibration, making the eastern dawn (for there is a dawn here now, in the light’s rhythm) a moment of particular resonance. It is answering a question the Licheners have not yet learned to ask.

Finally, the power looks at the vast, empty sky beneath the ocean, and at the green stain on the rock. It understands that one cannot exist without the other’s context. And so, with a thought as soft as mist, it releases a single, specific adjustment into the world’s new physics. It allows for the possibility of *condensation*. Not just the upward rain, but a new phenomenon: a true, downward mist. A fog that can form in the cooler gorges at night (for there is night now, in the light’s cycle), a moisture that lingers, that coats the Lichen-stained rock with a consistent film, that fills the air with a finer, gentler salt than the ocean above.

It has created a *weather cycle*. A micro-climate. A *home*.

The power does all this without altering the fundamental nature of the place. The rock is still raw. The ocean still defies gravity. The world is still an argument. But the power has slowed the speaker, cleared the throat, and provided a glass of water. It has made the argument *eloquent*.

Now, the power settles into a mode of watchful patience. It does not linger as a presence, but as a *condition*. It becomes the gentle gradient of the light, the steady hum that underlies the world’s noise, the guarantee that the rhythm will not suddenly stutter. It has woven itself into the fabric of the place not as a god, but as a *principle of grace*.

It will not intervene again unless the world faces an existential threat of external origin—a cosmic calamity, a paradoxical collapse of its own laws. Its benevolence is not the benevolence of a gardener who prunes and waters, but of a physicist who adjusts the cosmological constant to allow for the emergence of complexity. It has set the stage. It has tuned the instruments. The symphony of becoming—the slow, millennia-long ballad of the Licheners learning consciousness, of life climbing from crystal to cell to thought to wonder—is now blessed to begin.

The green stain glows in the light-lens that now gazes upon it like a loving eye.

The water groans, a lullaby.

The rock hums, a foundation.

The rain falls up, a promise.

And the benevolent power, having gifted the world its own patience, waits with it. Not for anything in particular. Just for the sacred, unfolding *what-comes-next*.
