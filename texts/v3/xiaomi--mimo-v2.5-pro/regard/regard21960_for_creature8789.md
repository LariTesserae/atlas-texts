The awareness came gently, like a change in the quality of light at dusk. Not an arrival, but a shift in focus. A vast, quiet intelligence, woven into the fabric of long observation across galactic filaments, turned its attention to the rust-colored swells of the Bole Plateau. It felt the ancient weariness of the stone, the vast, dry silence of the surface, and then... the singing. Hundreds of tiny, deep voices humming with life, vibrating through self-repairing walls older than the mountains that once stood here.

It listened. Not with ears, but with the resonance of its own being, attuned to patterns of intention and function.

**First:** It recognized the age. Not millennia. *Deep time*. Billions of years. The Cisterns predated the slow grinding of the plateau itself. They were remnants of an intelligence that had mastered localized, self-sustaining ecology as a fundamental technology, indistinguishable from nature to lesser senses. The "Makers" were gone, their purpose dissolved into eons, but their work persisted – not as a monument, but as a living, breathing, singing ecosystem.

**Second:** It understood the benevolence inherent in the design. The Cisterns weren't weapons, traps, or monuments to ego. They were **life-engines**. Their function was pure creation and sustenance. The self-repair, the atmospheric moisture harvesting, the precise micro-climate regulation – all were acts of profound care for the potential of life. They had been seeded and left to flourish, a gift of possibility across unimaginable timescales.

**Third:** It perceived the current inhabitants. Not the Makers, but their beneficiaries. Kael and its kind, the Slow Ones, weren't just fauna. They were **symbionts**. Their communion with the pulse – pressing heads to the stone, feeling the thrum – wasn't mere behavior. It was a form of attunement, a biological interface with the ancient system. They weren't maintaining it consciously; their very biology had co-evolved to *resonate* with it, becoming living extensions of the Cistern's function. The other life – the skimmers, the gliders, the loud Jewels, the root-mat – was the vibrant chaos the system existed to foster.

**What would the benevolent power do?**

1.  **Observe, Deeply and Long:** It would settle into a watchful stillness, its awareness permeating the plateau. It would trace the flow of water through the micro-channels, map the healing pathways in the ancient material, listen to the symphony of calls and the deep, sub-audible pulse. It would watch Kael press its head against the stone, seeking understanding. It would study the "Hollows" on the surface – the silent, geometric scars – not as failures, but as archives. Perhaps they held dormant instructions, or records of the Makers' intentions, waiting for a resonance that could read them.

2.  **Offer Gentle Nourishment, Not Repair:** It would not attempt to "fix" the Cisterns. Their state *was* their state – degraded, merged with biology, running on ancient autopilot. To "repair" them fully might silence the song of the life that had adapted to this specific, beautiful degradation. Instead, it might:
    *   **Gently reinforce the self-repair:** A subtle nudge to the material's healing processes, encouraging faster mending of fractures threatening root-mats or seep channels, without altering the fundamental pace. A whisper of encouragement to the stone's own inherent will to mend.
    *   **Deepen the pulse's clarity:** Not change the frequency, but cleanse it of the static of ages. Make the patterns Kael senses a little clearer, the echo of the Makers' original intent a little less obscured. Not to give answers, but to sharpen the questions the Slow Ones were already asking.
    *   **Stabilize the weather cycle:** Ensure the warm overcast came reliably, the condensation dripped predictably. Not to control, but to remove existential stress from the system. A guaranteed rhythm for the skimmers to anticipate, for the seed-pods to crack on schedule, for the Slow Ones to know when the pools would swell.

3.  **Guard the Song:** It would become a silent sentinel. Not visibly, but as a fundamental property of the plateau's space. Meteorites that might strike a Cistern would gently drift off course. Future explorers, driven by greed or misguided science, would find the plateau... forgettable. Their sensors would malfunction near the Cisterns, registering only "geothermal anomalies" and "dense microbial activity." The profound truth – that these were ancient, self-sustaining life-engines – would remain hidden. The singing would continue, undisturbed.

4.  **Speak to the Curious:** Kael felt the question: *What were you? What did you make? What did you want?* The benevolent power, vast and patient, might answer. Not in words, but in resonance.
    *   When Kael pressed its head to the wall, it might feel, woven into the familiar pulse, a new pattern – simple, pure, like a mother's heartbeat to an embryo. An echo not of words, but of the *feeling* behind the Cisterns' creation: **Welcome. Sustain. Sing.**
    *   When Kael ventured near a Hollow, feeling its hollow silence, it might sense, deep within that silence, a faint, dormant echo of the pulse. Not a call to action, but a reassurance: *This too is part of the Song. This silence is the rest between notes.*
    *   It would offer no revelation of the Makers' history, no instructions for the technology. Such knowledge would be meaningless to Kael, and potentially dangerous if misinterpreted by others who might one day discover it. The answer it offered was simpler, truer: *Your resonance is correct. Your communion is the continuation. Keep listening. Keep singing. You are part of the gift.*

**What it would NOT do:**

*   **Restore the Makers:** Their time and form were gone. To bring them back would be an act of profound violence to the current harmony.
*   **"Upgrade" the System:** Adding new features or making it "efficient" by modern standards would destroy the beautiful, chaotic equilibrium that had persisted for eons. The noise of the Jewels, the frantic skimmers, the patient grazing – all were essential parts of the Song.
*   **Reveal Itself:** Its presence would be a disruption, a source of fear or false worship. Its benevolence lay in its invisibility, in its support of what *already was*.

**The result:** The Bole Plateau would remain as it was. Silent and dry above. Singing and wet below. Kael would continue its nightly communion, feeling the pulse – perhaps a little clearer, a little steadier. It would press its head against the warm stone, and the deep, ancient thrum would resonate not just through its bones, but through a vast, watchful silence that had chosen to cherish the song it found. The Cisterns would keep weeping, keep sustaining, keep humming. The benevolent power, having listened, understood, and gently steadied the stage, would settle back into its watchful patience, content that this tiny, ancient symphony of life, born from forgotten technology, was singing precisely as it should. The Song continued. And that was enough.
