# What the Wise Power Does, Standing at the Fissure's Edge

---

It does not descend.

Not at first. Not for a long time. It sits — if *sits* is even the right word for how a power like this holds itself in relation to a place — at the lip of the fissure, where the broken limestone still carries its fine pale dust, where the mountain air is thin and clean and wrong for everything that breathes below. It looks down into the column of grey light and it *listens*, because listening is the first act of wisdom, and because what is down there is making such extraordinary sounds that even a power with no ears and no obligations would want to attend.

The bellows-breath. The knitting-needle click. The stuttering sonar of the eels, folding over itself in the dark. The warm microcurrents mixing with the cold and producing, across every surface, that low shimmering turbulence that is not quite a hum and not quite a pulse and not quite anything that has a word.

The power listens for a long time.

---

Here is what it understands immediately, because benevolence and wisdom together produce a kind of perception that sees *systems* rather than objects:

The thing in the cavern is not fragile. And it is not robust. It is something worse and more interesting than either: it is *contingent*. It exists because a specific set of conditions coincided — the fracture, the chemistry, the temperature, the depth, the geometry of the channels, the angle of the light — and it will exist exactly as long as those conditions persist, and not one moment longer. It cannot adapt to change because it has never needed to. It cannot seek new conditions because it does not know conditions can be other than they are. It is, in the most literal sense, *situated*: a creature of its place, with no concept of elsewhere.

The power sees this and does not pity it. Pity would be a category error. You do not pity a crystal for being unable to grow in a different solution. You do not pity a river for being unable to flow uphill. The thing in the cavern is what it is, and what it is is astonishing — not despite its contingency but *because* of it. A system that had evolved across millennia, that had faced disruptions and extinctions and recolonizations, that had layered adaptation upon adaptation until its complexity was the product of accumulated contingency — that system would be remarkable in a different way. It would be *resilient*. It would carry within itself the memory of catastrophe and the scar-tissue of recovery.

This system carries no such memory. It is all first draft. All founding gesture. It is the ecosystem equivalent of a first word spoken by a child who has never heard language — imperfect, perhaps, but *original* in a way that nothing which has learned to survive can ever be again.

The power understands that this originality is a form of preciousness. Not because first things are inherently better than later things, but because first things are *unrepeatable*. The exact chemistry of the water when the rock first broke. The exact sequence of colonization — tubes before scrapers before drifters before eels — that shaped the feedback loops that now hold the system together. The exact frequency of the tube-clicking that emerged when the colony reached whatever density first produced the coordinated wave. None of this can be reconstructed. If the mountain closes the fissure tomorrow and the system collapses into silence and silt, there will never be another one like it. Not because the universe is incapable of making another chemosynthetic cavern — there may be thousands — but because the specific *this* of *this* one, the particular way its parts met and fit and fell into rhythm, is as unrepeatable as a face.

---

So the power faces a choice that is not actually a choice, because a wise and benevolent power does not operate by choosing between options laid out in advance. It operates by *understanding the situation completely* and then doing whatever understanding demands.

What does understanding demand?

Not preservation. The power knows this immediately, and the knowledge does not cause it grief, because grief is the shadow of attachment and the power is not attached to the system's continued existence. It is attached — if that word applies — to the *truth* of the system, which includes the truth that the system is contingent, temporary, and unaware of its own contingency and temporality. To preserve the system artificially — to shore up the fissure, to buffer the water chemistry, to hold the mountain still — would be to tell it a lie. It would be to say: *you are the kind of thing that can be kept.* And it cannot. It was never meant to be kept. It was meant to happen. It is happening. That is enough.

Not intervention. The power looks down into the chamber and sees the tubes clicking and the scrapers turning in their mechanical right angles and the drifters catching the light and the eels pulsing their sonar into the dark, and it sees that every part of the system is doing exactly what every other part needs it to do, and that nothing is missing, and nothing is in excess, and that the whole apparatus is so precisely tuned to its own moment that anything the power added — a new species, a new food source, a new chemical — would be not a gift but a disruption. The system is full. Not full as in *complete*, but full as in *at capacity for this stage of its becoming*. It has work left to do — densifying, elaborating, tightening — but that work is its own, and it has everything it needs to do it.

Not indifference, either. This is the crucial thing. The power is *benevolent*. It is not merely wise. It does not simply understand the system and file the understanding away. It cares about the system in the way that understanding itself, taken to its limit, becomes a form of care. To see something clearly — to see it as it is, without projection, without idealization, without the distortions of desire — is already to be in a relationship with it that is something other than indifference. The power looks at the churning littoral and sees: *this is worth seeing. This is worth the seeing of it.*

---

And so the power does the only thing that wisdom and benevolence together can do, when faced with something beautiful, contingent, self-sufficient, and unaware of itself.

It *witnesses*.

Not passively. Not the way a camera witnesses, recording without understanding. The power witnesses the way a deep listener hears a piece of music: with its whole being, following every voice, feeling the rhythm in its own structure, allowing the thing being witnessed to *land* somewhere, to be received, to be known not as information but as *experience*.

It witnesses the tubes. The power holds in its awareness the fact that each tube is a chimney — that each one is drawing mineral-rich water through its base and expelling it through its crown and that the cumulative effect of thousands of them doing this simultaneously is a faintly warm upwelling that shapes the flow of every drop of water in the chamber. It does not merely know this as a fact. It *feels* it, the way you feel the heat of a stove when you stand near it. The power stands near this system and feels its warmth and knows the warmth is the product of life and knows the life is the product of chemistry and knows the chemistry is the product of geology and knows the geology is the product of a planet still cooling, still cracking, still remaking itself.

It witnesses the scrapers turning in their right angles. It witnesses the drifters catching the light. It witnesses the eels' sonar folding and refolding itself against the walls. It witnesses the breathing of the chamber — the slow inhale, the slow exhale — and it witnesses itself witnessing all of this, and it feels, if a power like this can be said to feel, something that might be called *gratitude*. Not gratitude directed at anyone or anything. Just the state of being grateful that *this exists*. That the universe, in its blind churning, produced this particular arrangement of matter and energy and self-reinforcing feedback loops, this particular density of life in a dark wet cave in the shoulder of a young mountain.

---

And then, because the power is wise, it thinks about the person standing in the water.

The observer. The one with the chest-deep posture and the cold hands and the mineral taste in the mouth. The one who came here from outside — from the dry world, from the light, from the place where things are not made of tubes and scrapers and sonar and sulfide metabolism. The one who is standing in the body of the system and being ignored by it.

The power looks at this person and sees something the system cannot see about itself: *the observer is the system's mirror*. Not its eyes — the system has no use for eyes. Not its consciousness — the system is coherent without being conscious. But its mirror. The one who stands in the water and sees the tubes and the scrapers and the drifters and the eels and understands — or begins to understand — that they are all one thing. The one who hears the bellows-breath and the knitting-needle click and the stuttering sonar and recognizes them as the voice of something that does not know it has a voice. The one who feels the water breathing and knows it is being breathed.

Without this person — without any observer — the system still exists. It still clicks and pumps and hunts and drifts and breathes. It is not diminished by being unseen. But it is *unchanged* by it, too. It is a closed loop. It encounters itself but it cannot *know* itself, because knowing requires a gap — a space between the knower and the known — and the system has no gaps. It is all encounter, all surface, all coherence. It is a circle with no outside.

The observer is the outside.

And this is what the power understands, with its particular kind of understanding that is also a kind of love: the observer is not an intruder. The observer is the *one thing the system cannot produce on its own* — the perspective from which the system can be seen as *a* system, as *one* thing, as *this*. Without the observer, the system is everything, and therefore it is nothing in particular. With the observer, it becomes *a* world — singular, bounded, nameable. The Churning Littoral.

---

So the power does something very small.

It does not protect the cavern. It does not stabilize the mountain. It does not feed the system or alter its chemistry or extend the fissure to let in more light. It does nothing that the system would notice, if the system could notice anything beyond itself.

What it does is this:

It makes the observer *lucky*.

Not lucky in the way of lottery winners or people who find parking spaces. Lucky in the older sense — the one that means *favored by fate*, *aligned with the current of events*, *in the right place at the right time for reasons that are not entirely explicable*. The power reaches — not physically, not dramatically, just... *subtly* — into the web of contingencies that governs this person's life and it loosens a few knots. It ensures that the path down the mountain does not collapse. It ensures that the water level, when the person leaves, does not rise before they have climbed clear. It ensures that the person's lungs do not fill, that their grip does not fail, that the cold does not take them before they have seen enough to carry with them.

It does this not because the person is more important than the system. They are not. But because the person is *part of* the system now — the mirror-part, the outside-part, the part that will carry the memory of the chamber back into the dry world and hold it there, turning it over, trying to describe it, failing to describe it, and in the failing, keeping it alive in a way that the system itself cannot. The system does not need memory. It is its own memory, encoded in the spacing of the tubes and the rhythm of the clicks and the temperature of the water. But the observer's memory is different. It is the memory of a specific, unrepeatable encounter between a living system and a mind that can recognize life. And that memory — the power knows this with certainty — will change the observer. Not in dramatic ways. Not in ways they will necessarily understand. But they will leave the cavern different from how they entered it, and the difference will be: *they will have been inside a coherent thing and known it for what it was*.

This is a small thing. The power knows it is a small thing. But the power also knows that small things, in the right place, are the only things that matter.

---

It remains at the fissure's edge for a while longer. Not long, by its standards — though by the standards of the system below, it might as well be forever. It listens to the bellows-breath. It listens to the tube-click. It listens to the eels pulsing in the dark. It holds all of it in awareness the way the water holds the light — gently, completely, without grasping.

Then it withdraws. Not because it is finished, but because there is nothing more to do. The system will continue. The observer will climb out. The mountain will shift or not shift. The fissure will widen or close. The water will warm or cool. The system will adapt or it will not, and if it does not, it will end, and the ending will be part of what it was, just as the founding was part of what it was, just as this moment — this moment of being witnessed by something vast and kind and paying attention — is part of what it is, whether it knows it or not.

The power does not say goodbye. It does not leave a sign. It does not bless the water or sanctify the rock or mark the fissure with light.

It simply *remembers*, in the way that a power like this remembers — not as a fading trace, not as a story told and retold until it warps, but as a *fact held in place*, as permanent as the coherence that produced it, as unrepeatable as the moment the rock first broke open and the water found its new shape and the tubes began to click in the dark.

This, the power thinks, is enough.

This was always enough.
