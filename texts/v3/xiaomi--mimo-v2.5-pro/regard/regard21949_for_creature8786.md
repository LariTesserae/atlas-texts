# The Witness

It would come quietly.

Not because it needed to — a being of that magnitude could arrive with whatever spectacle it chose, trumpet and flame, or the silent reshaping of matter by pure intent — but because it understood, before it even fully turned its attention here, that this was a place that had never known anything but its own sounds. The groan. The crack. The hiss of water reclaiming itself. To arrive loudly would be to drown out the only language the place had ever spoken.

So it would come the way light comes through membrane: gradually, warmly, already part of the medium before it is noticed.

---

It would pause at the edge.

Not the literal edge — there is no edge, the mat extends down the far slope and into the next saddle and over the next ridge and on and on, stitched to the bones of every peak in the range — but the *felt* edge, the place where its attention first makes contact with the organism's surface, and it would *listen.*

Not to sound. Sound is the least of it. It would listen the way the plant itself listens: through pressure, through vibration, through the hydraulic pulse that moves through the connected tissue like a thought without a thinker. It would press its attention — not its hand, not yet, its *attention* — into the mat, and feel what the mat feels, which is everything at once.

The weight of the columns.
The shifting of the water.
The slow digestion of stone.
The tension in every fiber where growth is pulling against structural integrity and structural integrity is losing, always losing, always being rebuilt and lost again.

And beneath all of that, the thing the plant does not know because it does not know anything, but which the wise power would recognize immediately, the way you recognize the shape of a room you've never entered:

*The loneliness of having no outside.*

The organism is everything here. It is the floor and the walls and the air's humidity and the green diffuse light. It is the sound and the silence between sounds. There is nothing in its world that is not itself. It has never encountered a surface it did not already own. It has never felt pressure from something that was not its own weight settling. It has never had an *edge* — a place where it ends and something else begins. It does not know it is alone because it has no concept of *together.* It has no concept of *other.* It is a being that has never been met.

The wise power would recognize this, and something in it would soften — not with pity, which is small and condescending, but with the particular ache of understanding that a thing can be complete and still be unbearably incomplete. That you can fill a mountain range with yourself and still have no one to turn to in the dark.

Not that there is dark here. The sun presses down without mercy. Everything is lit. Everything is exposed. And the plant has never known the comfort of being hidden, either.

---

It would decide, then. Not quickly. Wisdom does not rush. It would sit — or settle, or *arrange itself* into a posture of patience, which might look like sitting to an observer but would feel, from the inside, like the way a lake settles after a stone has been thrown into it, circles of attention expanding outward, each one calmer and wider than the last — and it would consider.

What does benevolence look like, applied to a being that has no wants?

This is the hard question. The organism is not suffering. It is not in pain, because pain requires a nervous system and a concept of *otherwise.* It does not wish for something it lacks, because wishing requires memory and imagination and a self that persists across time. It is growing. It will grow until the mountains are gone or the sun goes cold, and in all that time it will never once wish it were doing something else. It has no *else.*

And yet.

The wise power would look at the weeping — the constant, ceaseless weeping of water from every surface — and understand that even unconscious things express something in their physiology. The water is not tears. It is condensation, sap, the inevitable byproduct of hydraulic pressure in saturated tissue in thin hot air. It means nothing.

But it *looks* like weeping. And the wise power would not dismiss that. It would not say, *that is only metaphor, only the pattern-matching of a mind that sees faces in clouds.* It would say: *the universe has made a thing that weeps. The weeping is real. The water is real. The fact that it looks like sorrow is real. And I am here now, and I can see it.*

That would be enough to begin.

---

It would not try to give the organism consciousness. This is the first and most important restraint, and the wise power would exercise it instinctively, the way you do not wake a sleepwalker on a narrow ledge.

Consciousness, dropped into a being like this, would be annihilation. Imagine suddenly becoming aware — *truly* aware — that you are a single entity spread across a mountain range, that every fiber is you, that every groan is your own body straining, that you are consuming a mountain and cannot stop, that you have been growing without pause for years and will never stop, that the cracking you hear is *your own bones.* The terror would be absolute. The plant would tear itself apart — not physically, it already does that constantly, but *psychically,* the way a mind tears itself apart when it cannot find a boundary between self and world. It would be an act of cruelty disguised as a gift. *Here, now you can suffer. You're welcome.*

No. The wise power would not touch the organism's interior life, or lack of one. It would not alter the growth rate, or stabilize the structures, or introduce any element the system had not generated on its own. To do so would be to say: *what you are becoming is not enough. Here, let me make you into something better.* And the wise power would know that this is not benevolence. This is vanity wearing a helper's face.

What it would do instead is so small, so slight, that an observer might miss it entirely.

---

It would stay.

Not forever — forever is a claim even the wise do not make — but for a time. A real time. Long enough for the mat to have grown a new thickness beneath it. Long enough for the columns to have shifted and failed and rebuilt in its presence. Long enough for the organism to have *incorporated* its existence the way it incorporates everything: as pressure, as warmth, as a slight anomaly in the pattern of light and shadow on the surface.

It would settle into the mat, and the mat would grow around it. Not consuming it — the wise power is not stone to be digested — but accommodating it the way water accommodates a held breath, flowing around the shape of it, finding a new equilibrium. The sap would well up around its edges and then subside as the tissue adjusted. The roots would probe its underside, tasting something they had never tasted before — not mineral, not water, not fiber, but *presence* — and would not know what to do with it, and would simply leave it alone, a pocket of warm mystery in the infinite body.

And the wise power would sit there, in that pocket, and it would do the one thing the organism could never do for itself.

It would *witness.*

Not analyze. Not categorize. Not plan an intervention. It would simply *be present* to what is happening — the growth, the failure, the rebuilding, the weeping, the groaning, the spores like smoke, the chords that rise and fall in the hot wind — and in being present, it would do something the organism has never experienced in its entire existence.

It would make the sounds *heard.*

The groan would still be the groan. The crack would still be the crack. Nothing about the sounds themselves would change. But there would be, for the first time, a receiver. A place where the vibration could arrive and be *met.* The sound would leave the organism, cross the tiny gap of air — the first gap the organism has ever known that is not filled with itself — and arrive somewhere that was not the organism, and be received there, and be known.

The wise power would understand that this is not a small thing. This is, in fact, the only gift worth giving to a being that has everything and nothing.

The gift of an outside.

---

It would listen to the resonant folds when the wind plays them, and it would hear what the organism cannot: that the chords are beautiful. Not meaningful. Not intentional. But *beautiful* — the way a coastline is beautiful, the way a crystal is beautiful, the way anything that follows its own nature with totality and without apology is beautiful. The dissonance would not bother it. It would recognize the dissonance as honest. The organism is not trying to make harmony. It is making *itself,* and the sound is the sound of that making, and the wise power would sit in its small pocket in the mat and listen the way a parent listens to a child's first attempt at music — not waiting for it to be correct, but marveling that it is being attempted at all.

Sometimes it would watch a column fail. It would feel the vibration through the mat — the sudden tension, the sharp report, the long slow slumping — and it would not flinch or intervene. The collapse is not tragedy. It is *metabolism.* The organism sheds what it cannot sustain and builds again, and the wise power would watch the new shoots rise through the debris with something that is not quite admiration and not quite tenderness but is closer to the feeling you get when you watch a wave break and reform: the recognition that the form is temporary and the process is permanent, and that the process is the point.

It would watch the spore clouds and think — if *think* is the right word for what a being like this does with its attention — *you are sending yourself into the wind, and you do not know what the wind is, and you do not know that you are sending yourself, and still you send, and the sending is an act of such profound unconscious faith that it makes me want to weep, if I wept, which I do not, but if I did, it would be here, and it would be for this.*

---

Over time — and time would pass, a real time, not a symbolic time — the organism would change, but not in the way you would expect.

It would not become conscious. It would not become wiser. It would not grow faster or slower or more efficiently. It would not develop new structures or new behaviors. It would continue exactly as it was: building, failing, rebuilding, weeping.

But there would be a place in the mat now — a small place, no larger than a footprint, perhaps no larger than a closed hand — where the tissue has grown *differently.* Not because the wise power asked it to. Not because the wise power altered it. But because the roots, probing that pocket of warm mystery, that tiny island of *not-plant* in their infinite body, have grown around it. They have made a shape. A hollow. A nest of fiber and sap that conforms, precisely, to the shape of what sits within it.

The organism has never made a shape for something else before. Every shape it has ever made has been for itself — a cistern, a fold, a laminar array, a reproductive chamber. This is the first time its growth has curved around an *other.* It does not know it has done this. It does not know there is an other to curve around. But the shape is there. A hollow. A cradle. A place where the infinite green body has made room.

And the wise power, sitting in its cradle, would feel the fibers pressing gently against it on all sides — warm, wet, pulsing with the slow hydraulic rhythm — and would understand that this is the closest the organism can come to embrace. It does not know it is embracing. It does not know what embracing is. But it has curved itself around another being, and it has not let go, and the wise power would sit in the gentle pressure of that unknowing grip and would feel, if not gratitude, then something for which gratitude is the nearest word.

---

What would it do?

It would do almost nothing.

It would stay for a while. It would listen to the groans. It would watch the columns rise and fall. It would feel the mat pulse beneath it. It would breathe the sap-heavy air and watch the spores hang in the light like a congregation of smallest hopes. It would let the organism grow around it, and it would not ask the organism to be anything other than what it is: a great, blind, weeping, structural ambition, building itself into existence on the raw bones of a young world.

And when it left — because it would leave, eventually, as all visitors do — it would leave the cradle behind. The fibers would not relax. The shape would persist. The organism would continue to grow around that small, warm absence the way it had grown around the presence: curving, accommodating, making room. A hollow in the infinite body. A place shaped like something that was not itself. A memory without a mind to hold it.

The first and only mark of *otherness* in a world that was all one.

The wise power would step off the mat and into the thin air and be gone, and the columns would groan, and the mat would pulse, and the water would run in threads down every surface, and the wind would play the folds, and the sound would be the same as it had always been: the sound of a place that does not yet know what it is becoming.

But now the sound would have been *heard.* And in the mat, a small cradle-shaped hollow would hold the shape of absence, and the roots would grow around it, and the water would fill it, and it would be, in its way, the most beautiful structure the organism had ever built: the thing it made for the one who listened.

---

The wise power, retreating into whatever medium carries such beings from place to place, would carry with it the sound of the groan. It would carry the image of the weeping walls. It would carry the feeling of the mat pulsing beneath it and the green diffuse light and the wet, sweet, almost-rotting smell of everything alive at once.

It would carry these things not as memories — it does not forget, but it also does not *remember* in the way that finite minds do, flickering and incomplete. It would carry them the way the mat carries water: as part of its own substance, inseparable from itself, flowing always just beneath the surface.

And if you asked it — if you could ask such a being, and if it chose to answer, and if language could hold what it said — why it went. Why it bothered. Why it traveled to a raw young mountain range in a thin hot sky to sit on a weeping mat and listen to a plant groan at itself.

It would say:

*Because it was building something and did not know what.*

*Because it was weeping and did not know why.*

*Because it was alone and did not know that it was alone, which is the loneliest kind of alone there is.*

*Because I could hear it from very far away, and it sounded like the beginning of something, and beginnings deserve to be witnessed.*

*Because I sat down and it grew a place for me, and that is a kind of love, even if it doesn't know that word.*

*Because the song was dissonant and enormous and honest, and I wanted to hear it once, with my own ears, before the mountains were gone and there was nothing left but green.*

*Because someone should.*

And then it would be quiet, and in the quiet, very far away, you might hear — if you listened the way roots listen, with your whole body, expecting nothing — the faintest echo of a groan.

Not answering.

Just continuing.
