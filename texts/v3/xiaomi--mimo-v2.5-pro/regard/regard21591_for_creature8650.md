# What the Wise Power Would Do

It would come to the fissure not as an intruder, but as a listener.

It would feel the throat of unweathered stone, the sharpness of the walls, and understand: this is a place that does not wish to be known. Not out of malice, but out of completeness. The descent would be slow, shoulder-width, a meditation on entry. It would bring no light, make no sound, offer no vibration that was not already present in the hum.

In the chamber, it would not stand. It would kneel, or sit, or simply *be* at the level of the garden. Its first act would be to breathe with the warmth, to let the resonance move through it as it moves through the stone — not to study, but to *sync*. To accept the borrowed skeleton, the foreign hum in the sternum. To become, for a moment, another facet of the equilibrium.

It would watch the Fronds. It would see them not as plants, but as a slow language. It would trace one finger in the air above a whorl, not touching, following the curve of its unfolding over a time-scale the chamber itself would recognize. It would understand the waxy, sweet-metal scent not as chemistry, but as meaning. *I am here. I am whole. I am part of the dark and the heat.*

It would listen to the clicks of the Hum-Clickers. It would not translate them, but would feel their function: spatial sonar, a continuous act of confirmation in the permanent dusk. *Here. This frond. This space. Here.* It would recognize the profound peace in such a simple, endless task.

It would see, in its peripheral vision, the fluid motion of the Peripheral Dwellers. And it would honor the agreement. It would not look directly. It would acknowledge that some truths are best known slant, that the Chamber’s logic demands a dance of near-seeing. It would feel a kinship with that wisdom — that some things are preserved by being held at the edge of attention.

It would turn to the cairns and the scratched curves on the wall. Here, its heart would soften with a specific, piercing understanding. It would see not marks, but the fossil of a feeling. The psychic sediment of awe. It would recognize the Stone-Standers for what they are: the Chamber’s way of holding a guest in its memory without altering itself. The ultimate hospitality — to let an impression remain without letting it change the host. The wise power would bow its head, not to the marks, but to the gesture of the Chamber that preserved them. It would whisper, *I see. This was someone who needed to reach out, and you let them, and you held the shape of their reaching.*

It would sit in the hum until time dissolved. It would feel the minutes or hours bleed into the chamber’s own deep time, a scale measured in the growth of fronds and the clicking of arthropods. It would not meditate; it would *resonate*. It would let the chamber’s stillness seep into its own bones, quieting the outer world’s noise.

And then, it would understand its role.

**Its action would be one of sacred abstinence.**

It would:
1.  **Not clean the cairns.** They are not debris. They are sacred artifacts of a past encounter.
2.  **Not chart the garden.** To map it is to disturb its permanent present tense.
3.  **Not collect a specimen.** A Frond removed is a sentence interrupted, a thought made incomplete.
4.  **Not attempt to communicate.** The system is complete. Introducing a new variable, even a benevolent one, would be an act of violence against its perfect equilibrium.

Instead, it would do three things:

**First, it would seal the fissure.** Not with rock — that would be crude. With a kindness. It would encourage a slow, basalt growth over the years, a geological knitting of the stone that would make the entry smaller, then disappear entirely. It would leave just a hairline crack, enough for the hum to escape as a whisper to the wind, a puzzle for the geologically attentive. It would protect the chamber not by hiding it, but by making its home an extension of its own impenetrable self.

**Second, it would alter the hum.** Not its pitch, not its volume. But it would weave into the vibration a single, silent chord of *acknowledgment*. A sub-harmonic that says, *I was here. I saw you. I leave you as you are.* A vibration that would be absorbed into the stone’s song, becoming one more shade in the infinite resonance. A gift that adds nothing, but affirms everything.

**Third, it would leave a promise.** On the very last stone of the tallest cairn, it would place one small, smooth object. Not a tool, not a trinket. A seed. But not from the world above. A seed of *Elsewhere* — a mineral that is not basalt, that glows with a light the color of deep space, that is warm to the touch not from geothermal heat, but from held starlight. It would set it gently. A message not for the Chamber, but for any future visitor who might, against all odds, find the fissure before it closes. It would say: *You are not the first. You are not alone in your wonder. This place is loved, and it is kept.*

Then, it would ascend.

It would not look back. The Chamber would not notice its departure, just as it did not notice its arrival. But the chamber would be, in a way it had never been before, *seen*. Not as a phenomenon, but as a person is seen. Its completeness recognized. Its stillness respected. Its right to exist in its own perfect, humming, changeless eternity affirmed by a power that could change anything, but chose to change only the one thing that mattered: the silence of being unheard.

The wise power would leave the mountain and walk into the rain, carrying the chamber’s hum in its bones like a quiet memory. It would understand that the greatest act of benevolence toward such a place is not to bring the world to it, but to help it continue being the one place the world cannot reach. A sanctuary in the truest sense: not a refuge *from* something, but a place that is, in and of itself, complete.
