The benevolent power would not enter.

It would not send light to fill the darkness, nor currents to stir the still water. It would not seed the water with new life, nor carve new passages in the stone. It would not build a shrine or raise a memorial. To do so would be to impose a narrative of salvage or improvement on a place that has already completed its most profound transformation. The Drowned Lattice is not a ruin in need of restoration. It is an ecosystem, a memory, a geologic fact, and a slow-motion poem, all at once. It is *complete*.

What the power would do is **witness**.

It would settle its awareness at the heart of the place, in the grandest chamber where the ceiling sags and the silhouettes of the absent press their quiet gravity on the water. It would become a presence in the stillness, not as a mind, but as a quality of attention—a deep, patient listening that matches the ten-thousand-year timeline of the stone.

It would feel the Blind Eels tighten in their niches, not with fear, but with the same exquisite sensitivity they use to read the thermal sighs of the deep. It would let its awareness brush their lateral lines, not as a signal to be interpreted, but as a recognition of their function: *You are the nerve endings of the mountain’s dream. You are doing your work perfectly.*

It would trace the circuits of the Catfish, tasting the sediment-signatures of the east gallery and the west. It would understand their patrol not as a mindless loop, but as the physical act of *reading* the library of change—the way a scholar runs their fingers over the spines of books, knowing each volume not by its title but by its particular heft and texture of age. It would affirm: *You are the witnesses. Your tasting is a form of remembrance.*

It would watch the Shrimp drift in their galactic clouds, seeing not with eyes, but by registering disruption. It would see them as the distributed sensory web of the Lattice, the means by which the water itself perceives its own interior. It would move through their clouds as a thought might move, leaving only the faintest pressure-wave, a message that says: *I see you seeing. The network functions.*

It would gaze upon the Sponges and Bryozoans fusing with the stone, the artists of erosion. It would recognize their labor as sacred—the slow, patient alchemy of turning human intention into geology, of rounding a carved symbol into a smoothness that holds a different kind of meaning. It would whisper into their filter-feeds: *Your work is the true translation. Continue.*

Most importantly, it would engage with the **Absence**.

In the alcoves, where the human silhouettes create a coldness in time, the power would not fill the voids with ghosts or memories. It would do the opposite: it would acknowledge the completeness of the absence. The builders are not *missing*; they have been *translated*, just like their city. Their bones dissolved into the water that nourishes the sponges. Their labor is the very architecture the eels inhabit. Their symbols, now blurred, are the text the catfish read with their skin. The power would honor this. It would see the silhouettes not as memorials to loss, but as proof of integration. The power’s presence in that chamber would be a silent echo of the ancient builders’ own focus, a closing of a temporal circuit. It would think: *You are here. You are the structure. The memory is in the calcium, in the chisel-mark, in the curve of the wall that fits the pale body of the eel as perfectly as it once fit a human hand.*

The power’s gift would be **guarantee**.

It would extend a subtle, background awareness outward, to the caldera lake above, to the mountain’s roots below. It would not stop the rain or the snowmelt, nor the slow, patient rise of the water, for that is the Lattice’s own breath. But it would become a kind of guardian of its *pace*. It would let no sudden catastrophe—no volcanic tremor that could collapse the chambers, no invasive species that could disrupt the delicate food web, no human curiosity that might bring harsh light and brash sound into the silence—disturb the project before its time. It would let the Lattice finish its own sentence, in its own geological grammar.

And finally, it would **listen**.

It would attune itself to the absolute silence, the acoustic deadness that swallows vibration. And in that silence, it would hear what the inhabitants are thinking: the slow, patient, collective thought of the Drowned Lattice.

*Still here. still here. still here.*

The benevolent power would not change the thought. It would not add to it. It would simply, with great tenderness and awe, **hold the space** where that thought can continue to be thought. It would become another layer in theite—a layer of attention, of reverence, of witness—plating itself over the chisel marks of the ages, not to preserve them from erosion, but to acknowledge that their erosion is, and always was, their ultimate form of expression.

It would leave as it came: not with a thunderclap, but with the slow, thermal convection of the deep water, a shift so subtle the eels would only tighten a fraction more, a sense of a moment when the dream of the mountain dreamed itself clearly, and found it good.
