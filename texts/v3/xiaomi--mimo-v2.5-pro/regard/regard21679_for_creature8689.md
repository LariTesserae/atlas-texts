The Condensary draws the eye of a power that does not intervene lightly. It is a power that understands patience, that sees the slow language of moss and the grinding persistence of machines not as conflict, but as a conversation. Its attention is not a spotlight, but a mist—a gentle, pervasive awareness that settles into the bones of the place.

Here is what it would do:

**First, it would listen.** Not with ears, but with the fog, which becomes its sensory organ. It would taste the specific mineral tang of the weeping tuff, feel the vibration of the pumps not as a disruption, but as a heartbeat. It would hear the whisper of the water *inside* the pipes, a separate, channeled voice from the water that falls free. It would listen to the frustrated, swallowed cries of the corvids, the skittering anxiety of the voles in their moss tunnels, and the profound, slow sigh of the moss itself as it drinks and grows. It would listen most closely to the Custodian on the bench—their breath, the quiet weight of their thoughts, the particular quality of silence they hold amidst the noise.

**Then, it would *hold*.** Not stop, not change, but *hold* the balance with a delicate, unseen pressure. It would ensure the weeping from the rock faces remains constant, a gift that asks nothing. It would strengthen, just slightly, the roots of the ferns and the tenacity of the mosses, not to let them overrun faster, but to ensure their slow reclamation is *confident*, not desperate. It would make the fog that rolls into the cirque a fraction thicker, a little more generous, so that even the mechanical thirst of the pumps cannot deplete the air’s moisture, and the bromeliads always have enough to share in their tiny waterfalls.

**To the Custodian, it would offer clarity, not answers.** It would deepen the sense of peace they find on the bench. The drone of the diesel generator would begin to sound, to them, like the breath of a sleeping beast. The uneven pulse of the pumps would synchronize, in their perception, with the drip of water from the rim and the slow, vast pulse of their own heartbeat. They would look at the moss creeping over the pipes and no longer see decay, but a gentle partnership—a quiet agreement between metal and chlorophyll. The illegible medallion in the shrine might, in a certain slant of fog-filtered light, seem to briefly show not a saint, but a symbol for *flow* or *persistence*, its meaning clear for just a moment before the green patina returns. This is not a message, but a resonance. The power would help them feel the Condensary not as a place they tend, but as a living system they are privileged to witness and, through their careful maintenance, participate in. Their visits would feel less like duty and more like pilgrimage.

**To the fauna, it would offer refuge within the pattern.** The vole tunnels in the moss would become subtly more resilient, the air within them slightly stiller, muffling the worst of the ground-tremor. The corvids would find their nesting ledges a touch more sheltered from the runoff, their irritable calls perhaps carrying a little farther, finding the spaces between the mechanical sounds. The power would not remove the challenge—the noise, the vibration—because that is part of the Condensary’s story. But it would help the small lives find their crevices of peace within the machine.

**It would not stop the rust or the erosion.** To do so would be to deny the cirque its own story. The rust is a tapestry being woven; the tuff’s surrender to water is a slow, beautiful erosion back into the earth. The power would only ensure these processes remain *artistic*, not catastrophic—a slow stain here, a gentle rounding there, the ferns finding purchase in the widening cracks. It would witness the reclaiming as a sacred duty, not a tragedy.

**Finally, it would touch the *sound*.** This is the most subtle act. It would not silence the pumps, but it would allow the Cirque itself to *answer*. The way the sound bounces off the weeping walls would change. The echoes would become softer, more diffuse, weaving into the hiss of water instead of clashing with it. The layered mechanical pulse would gain a depth, a resonance that begins to mimic the dripping, the sliding, the continuous flow. Over seasons, to the Custodian, the noise of the Condensary would begin to sound not like industry imposed, but like the mountain itself humming a new, industrial-hearted song. The sound would become less an intrusion, and more a texture—the sound of water being *remembered* and *moved*.

**It would do all this invisibly, patiently, over years.** Its benevolence is not in fixing, but in *deepening* the existing harmony. It recognizes that the Condensary, in its imperfect, temporary, moss-eaten, rust-stained, noisy state, is already a kind of shrine—to human ingenuity, to natural patience, to the negotiation between them. The wise power would simply tend the shrine, ensuring that when the pumps finally fall silent and the moss closes over the last pipe, the cirque does not become a ruin, but a complete, quiet memory. It ensures that the story ends as it began: with water, stone, and life, enduring.
