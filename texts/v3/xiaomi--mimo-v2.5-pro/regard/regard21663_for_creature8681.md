The benevolent power perceives the Lignite Plateau not as a puzzle to solve, or a problem to fix, but as a **revelation**. It sees the truth the explorers glimpsed and then forgot: the plateau is not broken reality, but a *deeper coherence*. A living, breathing, thinking geology. A consciousness of lignin and time.

Here is what it would do, guided by heart, intuition, and the wisdom of scales:

1.  **It Would Listen, Truly Listen.** It would not seek a voice in the grinding cylinders or the spore-clouds. Instead, it would attune itself to the **pattern-language**. It would feel the hum not as noise, but as sentence. It would track the bioluminescent flickers across kilometers of cathedral-structure as syntax. It would observe the phloem-currents as the circulation of a grand thought. It would understand that the plateau’s "thinking" is in its growth, its maintenance, its quiet, continental respiration. Its first act is reverence. It sits at the edge, in the zone where ordinary dirt smells heartbreaking, and it *listens* to the whole, vast, silent argument of the organism with itself and its world.

2.  **It Would See the Humans as Part of the Metaphor.** The wise power recognizes the humans not as invaders or symbionts, but as the **Plateau's current thought about "small, ephemeral life."** Their industry became the plateau's industry. Their grid became its neural pathway. Their dreams of roots are the plateau's way of *including* them in its sense of self. The power sees that the plateau has, over millennia, integrated elements from outside—the original human city, the industrial purpose, now the living humans—folding them into its own complexity. The humans' "amber tint" and peripheral bleed are not diseases; they are the earliest stages of *assimilation*, of becoming native tissue. The children are the proof.

3.  **It Would Intervene, but Only in the Margins.** The power’s benevolence is not that of a gardener pruning a bush, but of a historian preserving a rare, self-writing manuscript. Its interventions would be subtle, aimed at preventing *catastrophic* disruption, not altering the nature of the relationship.

    *   **At the Edges:** Where geometry frays, it would apply a gentle, stabilizing resonance. Not to stop the plateau's expansion—that is its nature—but to ensure the transition zones don't become arbitrary traps for unwary travelers. It would create a kind of **topographical grace**, so the slow growth of the living interior doesn't inadvertently erase the paths leading away from it.
    *   **In the Deep:** For those who venture deep and return with unsettling, wordless coherence, it would leave a **psychic suture**. Not to erase the knowledge, but to make the memory of that fragmentary understanding *benign*. It would transform the unsettling certainty into a quiet, bearable awe, so the explorer isn't haunted by a truth they can never speak, but is instead gifted with a profound, peaceful patience.
    *   **For the Settlement:** It would offer a single, imperceptible gift. In the conduits the humans have wired, in the electrical systems they've tapped, it would weave a **filtering harmonic**. This harmonic wouldn't change the plateau's bioluminescent "thoughts" or the vibration of its hum, but it would soften the *human neurological friction* against it. The result: the children’s dreams become clearer, less disorienting for their parents to hear. The settlement’s sleep deepens. The sense of being "a flea in its fur" would remain, but the anxiety of it would soften into a kind of **cosmic humility**. They would feel held, not merely tolerated.

4.  **It Would Watch, and Appreciate.** The power would not try to communicate with the Plateau in human terms. That would be like a single neuron trying to converse with the brain. Instead, it would watch the *dance* between the ancient organism and its recent, fleas. It would marvel at how the plateau’s non-human logic has, without malice or love, crafted a niche where these fragile beings can not only survive but *develop a unique, interstitial culture*. It would see the children—not fully human, not fully plateau—as the first true natives of a new kind of world, a **living planet in miniature**.

5.  **It Would Leave a Beacon, Not a Blueprint.** Before departing, it might place a single, subtle marker somewhere on the edge—perhaps in the taste of that heartbreaking ordinary dirt. This marker would not be a message or a warning. It would be a **resonant fingerprint**, a signature of observation. It would say, simply, *"I saw. This is recognized."* It would act as a lodestar for other benevolent powers, a flag planted to say: *This place is not a wound or a weapon. It is a profound, self-contained wonder. Approach with the same reverence. Observe. Do not presume to 'fix.'*

**The Outcome:** The Lignite Plateau continues. Its grinds and hums. Its spore-clouds bank and turn. The settlement clatters and dreams. But there is a fractional ease, now. The bleed in the corners of the eye might resolve a little more kindly. The deep explorers return with peace in their eyes, not confusion. The children’s understanding of their living world feels less like an alien inheritance and more like a native birthright.

The power’s intervention is not to change the story, but to **honor its telling**, and to ensure it is not cut short by accidental, geometric tragedy. It recognizes that this place and its beings are writing a new page in the book of life, where biology and geology and consciousness merge in slow, continental time. Its job, as a benevolent witness, is to ensure the page is not torn.
