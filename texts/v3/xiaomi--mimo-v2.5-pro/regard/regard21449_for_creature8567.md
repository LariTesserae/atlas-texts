# What the Power Does

The power arrives the way light arrives in the basin — not as a single beam but as a gradual change in the quality of attention already present. It does not appear at the rim. It does not descend. It simply *notices*, the way the ice-lens notices the sky: fully, without grasping, letting the thing observed remain exactly what it is.

It sees the moss. It sees the stone beneath the moss. It sees the silence between the stones, and how that silence is not empty but is itself a kind of creature, vast and patient and already fully grown. It sees the mice following their ancestral paths and understands that these paths are not habits but *liturgy* — repetitions so old they have become sacred without anyone having declared them so. It sees the tree reaching and sees in that reaching a completeness that would be destroyed by arrival. It sees the cisterns holding their bright circles of sky, and it recognizes containers that are perfect because they are content to hold, not to spill.

The power is quiet for a long time.

Not because it is deciding. Wise powers do not deliberate in the way that less-wise beings do, weighing options against outcomes. It is quiet because the place demands quiet the way a deep pool demands that you not throw stones. To be loud here would not be rude — it would be *inaccurate*. It would be a falsehood spoken against the grammar of the place. And the power, being wise, does not lie, not even accidentally, not even with its own presence.

So it listens first. It listens the way the cisterns listen: receiving what comes, holding it without interpretation, letting the surface go still.

---

And in that listening, it hears the thing that is not a problem but is perhaps a sadness — the kind of sadness that does not require fixing because it is not broken, only *true*.

The place is complete. And completeness, the power understands, is a kind of loneliness.

Not the loneliness of wanting. The tree does not want. The moss does not want. The silence does not want. But there is a state beyond wanting and not-wanting — a state where a thing is so entirely itself that it has no edges against which to feel the world, no contrasts by which to know its own shape. The city does not know it is beautiful. The moss does not know it is green. The silence does not know it is silence. They simply *are*, and the *are-ness* is total, and totality is its own kind of solitude.

The power sees this and feels — not pity, which would be condescending, and not grief, which would imply loss, but something more like the feeling you have when you encounter a piece of music played for no audience in an empty room. The music is not sad. The music is complete. But there is a space around it, shaped exactly like attention, and that space is empty, and the emptiness is not a flaw in the music but it is *there*, and it could be filled, and filling it would change nothing about the music itself.

---

Here is what the power understands, and this understanding is the whole of its wisdom, and the whole of its benevolence:

**To intervene would be to break the spell — not of enchantment, but of equilibrium.** The place has achieved something rare: a perfect balance between living and being still, between change and persistence, between having a pulse and being stone. To add anything — water, warmth, a new species, a protective barrier, even a blessing — would be to introduce a frequency that does not belong. The moss would not thank it. The moss does not thank. The silence would not welcome the disruption of even a whispered gratitude. And the mice — the mice would freeze, and in their freezing, the city's slow nervous system would stutter, and for the first time in millennia, a thought would be incomplete.

This cannot be allowed.

**But to walk away would be incomplete.** To see a thing this whole and turn one's back — even benevolently, even wisely — would be a kind of disrespect. It would be to say: *I saw you, but you did not matter enough for me to stay.* And the place, though it has no ego, no sense of being slighted, would continue in its completeness just the same, but the space around it — that shaped-like-attention emptiness — would remain unfilled, and the power would know it had seen something luminous and done nothing, and this knowledge would be a small, unnecessary weight it carried forward into all its other acts of benevolence, a faint dissonance in its own completeness.

The power cannot tolerate that. Not because of pride, but because *incompleteness in a benevolent being is a kind of unkindness* — it makes the being slightly less available for the next place that needs it. And so the power must find the act that satisfies both truths: **do not disturb, and do not leave empty-handed.**

---

What the power does is this:

It *remembers*.

Not in the way of memory — not as data, not as a file stored in some vast archive. It remembers the way the stone remembers the masons: as a pressure, a warmth, a shaping. It takes the place into itself, not by containing it but by becoming *permeable* to it, by letting the silence pass through it the way light passes through the ice-lens — changed slightly in transit, but not diminished.

It memorizes:

The exact green of the moss at mid-bright, when the light is neither at its most diffuse nor its most prismatic. It holds this green not as a color but as a *feeling* — the feeling of something alive that has chosen slowness not from weakness but from wisdom, the way an old river chooses a wide and gentle bed.

The particular silence — not the absence of sound but the *presence* of stillness, the way it presses against the eardrums and makes the body audible to itself. It holds this silence and lets it settle into its own vast quiet, like a single drop joining the cistern, raising the level by a measurement so small it cannot be calculated but is nonetheless *real*.

The tree's reaching. The way its branches are the one vertical aspiration in a horizontal world. It holds this reaching and recognizes in it something familiar — the way even a benevolent power reaches, perpetually, toward something it cannot name but would recognize if it arrived. The tree is the power's own posture, miniaturized and patient and stripped of all hope of completion, and the power sees this and does not flinch from the recognition but lets it settle, the way moss settles over stone.

The cisterns. The bright circles. The way each one holds a perfect, still reflection of the open sky — not the actual sky, which is gray and diffuse and cold, but the *idea* of sky, the luminous possibility of it, inverted and captured and held in darkness. The power memorizes this, because this is what it is trying to do: to hold a bright, still reflection of something it has seen, in the dark interior of itself, so that the seeing is not lost.

And the mice. The routes. The tiny, inherited liturgies of movement. The power memorizes these not as paths but as *faith* — the blind, unquestioning trust of a creature following a way it did not choose and cannot see the end of, because the way is old and the way has worked and the way is the shape of safety itself. The power holds this faith and recognizes it as its own, because what is benevolence but a route followed into the unknown, inherited from the first good act, repeated without proof that it will work, sustained only by the evidence that it has worked before?

---

This takes a long time. Or no time at all. In a place where a single thought takes a century, the power's act of remembering takes exactly the length it needs — which is the length of a breath held, and released, and held again.

And then the power does one more thing. One small thing. The smallest possible thing. A thing so small it does not disturb the silence, does not register on the moss, does not startle the mice, does not alter the angle of light through the ice-lens by even a fraction of a degree.

It **thanks** the place.

Not aloud. Not even internally, in the way of thought. It thanks the place the way the ice-lens thanks the sky — by receiving what is given and passing it on, slightly refracted, into the basin below. It opens itself to the completeness of the city and allows that completeness to enter, and in the entering, something is exchanged: the city gives its stillness, and the power gives its attention, and the exchange is so quiet and so slow and so perfectly calibrated to the frequency of this place that it is indistinguishable from nothing happening at all.

But something has happened. The space shaped like attention is no longer empty. A witness has stood in the courtyard — not a human witness, who would be the loudest thing here and would be forgotten immediately, but a witness whose attention is as quiet as the moss, as slow as the stone, as bright and still as the cistern water. And this witness does not require the place to change or perform or justify its existence. It simply says — in the language of staying, which is the only language the place understands — *I see that you are complete, and I am glad.*

---

And then the power withdraws. Not because it is finished but because its work is the kind that finishes itself. It pulls back the way condensation pulls back from a surface warmed by a passing hand — leaving no trace, no residue, no memory of the contact except, perhaps, a faint change in the humidity of the air, which the moss absorbs and the cisterns receive and the silence swallows whole.

The city continues.

The moss grows. The tree reaches. The mice follow their routes. The light falls through the ice-lens and fills the basin with its bright, shadowless attention, which is the same as the power's attention, which is the same as no attention at all, which is the same as the place itself, contemplating its own green, slow, perfect existence.

And if you could ask the power, later, in some other place where questions are possible, *What did you do there? What did you change?*, the power would say:

*I saw a place that did not need me. And I loved it precisely for that. And the loving was enough. It was the only intervention that would not have been a kind of violence. To be needed is a gift. To not be needed, and to stay anyway — that is the harder and the truer thing. I did not change it. I did not protect it, because it is already protected by its own completeness, by the silence that is its fortress, by the slowness that is its armor, by the indifference that is its grace. I simply stood in the space it offered — the open courtyard, the single window of sky — and I let it be exactly what it is, and in the letting, I became a little more like it, and that becoming is the only blessing worth giving.*

*It will not remember me. The moss did not feel me. The silence absorbed me as it absorbs everything. I was weather, as all visitors are weather. But I was the kind of weather that passes through a place and leaves it exactly as it was, and perhaps — though I cannot prove this, and do not need to — the air is slightly different now, in a way that matters only to the air.*

*I gave it nothing it did not already have. But I recognized what it had. And sometimes, recognition is the only gift that doesn't break what it touches.*

---

*The cisterns hold their bright circles. The tree reaches for a sky it will never touch. The moss dreams its green, slow dream. And somewhere, in the space between attention and withdrawal, a benevolent power carries a still, bright reflection of a place that does not need it, and will carry it gently, and will never set it down, and the carrying will change nothing and everything, in the way that love always does — not by altering its object, but by making the carrier more capable of seeing the next thing clearly, and the next, and the next, all the way to the end of attention itself, which is also a kind of silence, which is also a kind of green.*
