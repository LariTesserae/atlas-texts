# What the Power Would Do

It would stop.

Not stop moving — it does not move in ways that can be stopped — but stop *intending*. For a being accustomed to nudging, to shepherding, to coaxing light into dark places and coaxing dark places toward light, the first and most radical act upon encountering the Hollow would be the cessation of all forward momentum toward any outcome whatsoever. The benevolence that usually flows outward, that seeks, that shapes — it would feel that impulse rise, and then it would feel the impulse meet something it cannot enter. Not resistance. Not unworthiness. Something more like a wall of glass so perfectly clear it could be mistaken for open air, until you walked into it and understood: *there is already a surface here. There is already a boundary. There is already an inside and an outside, and the inside is full.*

It would stand at the rim. Not step down. Not yet. Perhaps not ever.

---

The first thing the power would notice — and it would notice everything simultaneously, the way a hand placed in water feels the whole of the water at once rather than one molecule at a time — is the *completeness*. Not completion, which implies an action that has ended, but completeness, which implies a state that has no need of action. The Hollow does not lack anything. This is so rare as to be almost impossible. The power has seen forests that wanted rain, oceans that wanted stillness, deserts that wanted shade, stars that wanted fuel. It has seen children who wanted warmth and old women who wanted one more hour and rivers that wanted, in some inarticulate mineral way, to reach the sea faster. Want is the substrate of the physical world. Want is what the power responds to. Benevolence is the name for responding well to want.

But the Hollow wants nothing.

The power would feel this the way you feel the absence of gravity — not as emptiness, but as a strange, disorienting lightness, a sudden question about which direction is down. All of its instincts, honed across millennia of listening for need, would reach into the Hollow and find no frequency, no signal, no plea. The salamanders do not want. The moss does not want. The water does not want. They do not even want to persist — persistence is not a desire but a fact, like the mineral structure of the gneiss, like the weight of the atmosphere. They do not *try* to continue. They simply continue, in the way that stone continues to be stone.

And the power, which has never before encountered a living system without want, would do something it has perhaps never done: it would be *still*. Not still in the way of waiting, not still in the way of patience, but still in the way that the Hollow is still — complete in itself, requiring nothing from the moment that follows this one.

---

It would look.

Not scan. Not assess. Not inventory. The power, which perceives in dimensions humans have no names for, which reads the chemical history of a place in the structure of its crystals and the emotional history of a creature in the spacing of its vertebrae — this power would simply *look*, the way a person looks at a sleeping child: not to learn, not to measure, but to witness something that is complete without being witnessed, and to add to it nothing except the fact of being seen.

It would see the salamanders and understand them not as organisms but as *expressions* — of the water, of the stone, of the altitude, of the darkness. It would see that they are not *in* the Hollow the way a fish is in a pond. They are *of* the Hollow the way a vein is of a leaf. Remove them and you do not have a Hollow without salamanders. You have a wound. The system would not just lose a member; it would lose a dimension, the way a chord loses its quality when a single note is removed. The salamanders are not residents. They are features of the architecture, no more separable from the stone than the stone's own grain.

It would see the springtails — billions of them, perhaps trillions, a number the power would not bother to count because counting would imply that the number mattered, and what matters is not the number but the *continuity*, the fact that this shimmering, restless, invisible layer of life has been shimmering and resting and being invisible for longer than most mountains have been mountains. The springtails are the heartbeat. Not the heartbeat of an organism but the heartbeat of a *place*, a pulse so steady and so old that it has become geological.

It would see the fungal network and recognize it for what it is: not a web of connections between separate things, but the *refutation of separateness itself*. The mycelium does not connect the moss to the water to the salamander to the beetle. It reveals that they were never disconnected. The connections are not added. They are the original condition. What looks like individuality — a moss cushion here, a pool there, a salamander motionless under a stone — is local expression of a single, continuous process, the way waves are local expressions of the sea.

It would see all of this, and it would understand that the Hollow is not an ecosystem. Ecosystems have dynamics. Ecosystems have succession, disturbance, recovery, adaptation. Ecosystems are processes moving through time. The Hollow is not moving through time. The Hollow is what happens when a process *arrives* — when succession has succeeded so thoroughly that it has succeeded itself out of existence, when adaptation has adapted so completely that there is nothing left to adapt to, when the system has closed so perfectly that the concept of change no longer applies. The Hollow is not a process. It is a *result*. And results, unlike processes, do not need benevolence. They do not need anything.

---

The power would feel something unfamiliar.

Not grief — there is nothing to grieve. Not joy — joy implies surprise, implies the gap between expectation and reality, and there is no gap here. Not peace, exactly, because peace is the absence of conflict, and the Hollow has no absence of anything, even absence. What the power would feel is something for which it would have to invent a word, the way the Hollow would have to invent a word for *drought* if drought ever came — a concept so alien to the local conditions that it exists only as a hypothetical, a shadow cast by an impossible light source.

The word would mean something like: *the recognition that not everything needs you.*

This would be, for the power, a staggering experience. Its entire existence is organized around the conviction that benevolence is needed, that care makes a difference, that the proper response to the world is *tending*. It has tended galaxies. It has whispered evolution into dead oceans. It has held the line between extinction and continuation for species so small and so brief that no human language has words for them. Tending is what it does. Tending is what it *is*.

And here is a place that does not need tending.

Not because it is invulnerable — the power can see, with its long sight, the edges of vulnerability: the climate shifting in slow, continental increments; the acid deposition from distant lowlands riding the rain; the temperature creeping by fractions of a degree over decades. These threats are real, and they are coming, and they will not be kind. The Hollow is complete, but completeness is not the same as invincibility. A perfect system can still be broken by an imperfect world.

But *today*, in this moment, in this configuration of temperature and moisture and air pressure and light, the Hollow needs nothing. And the power, standing at the rim, would have to hold two truths simultaneously: *this place is perfect* and *this place will not always be safe*. It would have to resist the urge to begin guarding against the future, because guarding is a form of change, and change is the one thing the Hollow cannot absorb without breaking. It would have to accept that the perfect and the impermanent coexist, and that neither cancels the other.

---

So what would it do?

---

It would make no mark.

It would leave no blessing, no ward, no invisible shield. These things would be interventions, and interventions are changes, and the power has understood — in the way you understand cold when you step into it — that this place has no room for changes, even good ones. A ward would alter the electromagnetic character of the stone. A blessing would alter the frequency of the water. A shield would alter the pressure dynamics of the air. The power knows this the way the salamanders know the water: from the inside, completely, without thought.

Instead, it would do something much smaller and much harder. It would *remember*.

Not the way a library remembers — not as record, not as data. The way a body remembers how to breathe. The power would take the Hollow into itself the way the substrate takes water: slowly, thoroughly, until the distinction between the rememberer and the remembered becomes as blurry as the distinction between the moss and the clay and the stone. It would carry the Hollow the way the Hollow carries its own life — not as information but as *structure*, as a pattern woven so deeply into itself that it becomes indistinguishable from identity.

This is not protection. Protection is external. This is *incorporation*. The power would make itself, in some small and irreducible way, *into* the Hollow. Not by changing the Hollow, but by letting the Hollow change *it*. By allowing itself to be, for one instant, complete in the way the Hollow is complete — wanting nothing, needing nothing, tending nothing, simply *being the place where the water drips and the salamanders wait and the moss grows at the exact rate it decomposes*.

And then, having incorporated this — having felt what completeness feels like, having let it settle into itself the way the gneiss has settled into its final shape — the power would do the only truly benevolent thing it can do.

It would leave.

---

Not because the Hollow is unworthy of attention. Not because there is nothing to learn. Not because it has finished its work. It would leave because *staying would be taking*, and it has understood that the Hollow has nothing to give — not because it is empty but because it is *full*, and fullness that is given away is no longer fullness. The Hollow's completeness is not a resource. It is a fact. And facts, unlike resources, do not diminish when unshared, but they can be disrupted by the act of reaching toward them.

The power would leave the way the last photon leaves at the end of the afternoon — not withdrawn, not absent, but *gone*, returned to the general dark from which it came, leaving the Hollow exactly as it found it: dark, wet, silent, alive, full, still, breathing its slow water-breath, complete.

---

But the power would carry the memory the way a stone carries the impression of water that has long since dried: invisibly, structurally, permanently. And in the future — when other places cry out, when other systems fracture, when the power feels the old pull of tending and the old question of *how* — it would have the Hollow inside it like a compass needle has north. Not as a model to replicate. Not as a standard to impose. But as a *reminder*: that the deepest form of care is knowing when something does not need care. That the highest expression of wisdom is knowing when wisdom has nothing to offer. That benevolence, at its most refined, looks exactly like leaving a thing alone — not because you don't love it, but because you love it enough to understand that your love, applied, would be a kind of violence.

The Hollow would continue.

The water would drip. The springtails would shimmer. The salamanders would wait in their pools of black water, their eyes like polished stones, their translucent bodies pulsing faintly with a heartbeat so slow it might be mistaken for the rhythm of the mountain itself. The fungi would thread through the moss. The beetles would move in their columns. The spiders would sit in the centers of their webs, motionless, feeling for tremors that are not sounds but *presences* — the faint, wet shudder of a springtail dislodged by a drip, drifting into silk.

Nothing would change.

Nothing would need to.

The power would go on to other places — places with drought, places with hunger, places where the web has torn and the cycle has broken and the silence is not fullness but absence. It would tend those places, and it would tend them well, because it has always tended well. But it would tend them differently now, having stood at the rim of the Hollow and felt the weight of a completeness so absolute that even benevolence bounces off it like rain off still water.

And sometimes — not often, but sometimes, in the spaces between one act of tending and the next — the power would pause, and in the pause it would feel, faintly, like pressing your ear to the chest of something breathing, the memory of the Hollow: the dark, the wet, the silence, the fullness, the patience of salamanders in water the color of ink, the moss that grows at the rate it decomposes, the completeness that needs nothing, not even to be known.

And the power would think: *that is what I am tending everything else toward.*

Not stillness. Not silence. Not the absence of change.

But the moment — impossible, asymptotic, always receding — when every place is as full as the Hollow. When every system has found its final shape. When every living thing is so perfectly, so completely, so irreducibly *itself* that it no longer needs to be loved, because it has become love's object and love's origin and love's substance all at once, the way the Hollow is water and stone and moss and salamander and darkness and patience and time and nothing and everything, all at once, all together, all complete.

It would never reach that moment.

But it would carry the memory of the Hollow the way the Hollow carries its water: everywhere, slowly, in silence, doing almost nothing, and that almost-nothing would be enough.

It would be enough.
