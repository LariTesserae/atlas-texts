**A Presence Notices.**

It is not a god. It is not an overseer with a clipboard. Imagine instead a vast and gentle attention — something like the warmth that rises from the earth, or the patience of stone. It has been elsewhere, tending to other quiet miracles, but a ripple in the pattern has drawn its focus here. Not a crisis. A question.

The question is: *How?*

It observes the Mound Country from a perspective that is not a perspective — more like the way light exists in a room, or the way a forest floor knows the weight of a raindrop. It sees the whole. The slow, churning gy of death at rebuilding in collapse at growth. The plateau is a single, breathing thing, humming to itself in the damp, warm dark. The power is not impressed. It is moved.

It thinks, in a way that thinking is like the growth of crystal, the slow crystallization of an idea over millennia:

*“They are doing it. All of it. Without plan, without blueprint, without anything but the imperative to fill the space with more of themselves. They are making a place that is alive, that breathes, that houses a world within its walls. And they do not know they are doing this. They are simply... being. And that is enough.”*

**What it does, first, is witness.**

It does not intervene. It does not fix, mend. correct. The system is not broken. It is, in its own terminology, *working*. So the power sits with it, metaphorically. It attends to the layers: the scratch of talons on cured clay, the damp exhalation from a million vents, the soft, endless decomposing of the dead into the floor of the world. It listens to the sound — not as a cacophony, but as a language. It learns the grammar of the *chik-chik-chik*: the declaration of *I am here*, the assertion of *this space is occupied*, the silent agreement that *we are all here, together, in this dark.*

It sees the parasites and the beetles and the spiders and does not grieve for the nestlings taken. It sees the collapse of the old cores and the frantic rebuilding at the edges. It sees the vines growing into the bones of the towers, and the fig trees cracking them open, and the birds rebuilding around the cracks. It recognizes this not as struggle, but as conversation. The conversation of life with itself.

**Its greatest act is one of protection.**

The power sees the threads of the future — not a fixed tapestry, but a field of potential. In many of those potentials, something ends the Mound Country. A drought that lasts too long. A fungal blight the cockroaches cannot manage. A change in the winds that cuts off the warmth. And in some, a more alien ending: the arrival of creatures from a different pattern of life, who see only resources to be extracted, or novelty to be collected, or chaos to be tamed.

The power cannot stop all ends. It would not. Death is a syllable in the language of the place. But it can guard against the endings that are not part of the grammar — the endings that come from outside the conversation.

So it weaves a subtle cloak. Not of invisibility, but of *misdirection*. It lays a gentle hand on the surface of the world. To the casual survey from above, the plateau appears as it might have a thousand years ago: a high, warm, windswept grassland. Scans and sensors return a picture of mundane geology and sparse vegetation. The faint, constant radar signature of billions of warm bodies is blurred into a natural thermal shimmer. The sound, should anyone come close, is mistaken for wind in grass, for the hum of insects. It does not hide the Mound Country. It makes it *unremarkable*. It makes the eye slide away, the mind forget. It fosters in the nearby world a gentle, pervasive disinterest. *That plateau? Just rocks and grass. Boring. Let’s look over there.*

It builds a fence of forgetfulness.

**And then, it gives a final, small thing.**

Deep in the oldest, darkest core of the colony, where the mounds have subsided into a dense, humming labyrinth, in a chamber no larger than a human head, the power does something delicate. It does not change the birds, or the beetles, or the fungi. It changes the *memory* of the place.

It takes the pure, blind imperative to *build, fill, *mend*, and it gives it the faintest, most microscopic flavor of *concern*. Not for themselves, but for the structure as a whole. A bird might, once in a thousand generations, pause before daubing a wall, and feel a fleeting, inexplicable urge to daub *here* instead of *there*, not because the gap is largest, but because the wall feels *weary*. A fungal strand might grow a micrometer thicker in a critical support beam, not because of the light, but because the clay around it *asked*. It is not intelligence. **And It Leaves.**

Not forever. The presence recedes, like a tide going out, but the wetness remains on the sand. The plateau hums on. The birds call. The darkness breathes.

The Mound Country does not know it was witnessed. It does not know it was protected. It does not know it was loved.

It simply continues. And that, for the power, is the entire point.
