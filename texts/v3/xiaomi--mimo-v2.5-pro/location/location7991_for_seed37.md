# The Nascence Pockets

You find it by accident, the way you find all of them — by pressing your hand against what you thought was solid tufa and feeling it yield, not like water, not like mud, but like *nothing*, a softness that has no texture at all, and then your fingers are through.

Inside: a chamber the size of a root cellar, though the word "chamber" implies walls, and these are not walls. They are more like the *idea* of walls — surfaces of raw calcium carbonate still wet with their own precipitation, still being deposited molecule by molecule from mineral-laden seepage that weeps down from somewhere above in a mist so fine it isn't water so much as *the memory of water*. The stone here has no age. Press your thumb into it and it resists, but barely, the way soft cartilage resists. It hasn't finished deciding what it is.

And it is **bright**.

Not sunlight. Not firelight. Something else.

---

**THE FAUNA**

They hang from the nascent ceiling and cling to the barely-walls in numbers that don't make sense for a space this small — hundreds, perhaps thousands, in a pocket that a man could cross in six strides. They are the size of a cupped hand, each one, and they are *bright*. Not bioluminescent in any way you could name — no chemical glow, no phosphorescence. They simply **are** light, the way a stone is heavy, the way water is wet. Their bodies are translucent, layered like mica, and each layer holds a different refraction. They shift in slow, tidal rhythms, not quite breathing, more like *tiding*, and when they shift, the light they cast changes — cooler, warmer, casting new geometries on the half-formed walls.

They are not insects. They are not crustaceans. They are not anything taxonomically terrestrial. They have too many joints, or too few, depending on the moment you look. Some have symmetry; most do not. A few appear to have *interior* structures — veined things, tubular things, organs or engines or something without a name — that are visible through their translucent shells and that move on their own schedule, slower than the creature, or faster, or *opposite* to it, as though the parts inside are operating in a slightly different version of time than the parts outside.

You cannot tell if they are one species or many. They look different every time you look back. A cluster that was three distinct organisms a moment ago now appears to be a single organism with three heads. A large one near the floor has split — not reproduced, *split* — into two smaller ones that move in perfect mirror of each other, but with a two-second delay between them, so that the second always seems to be remembering the first's motion rather than performing its own.

They do not make sound. This is the most unsettling thing about them. They move constantly — rearranging, tidying, flowing across surfaces — and they are utterly silent. No click of chitin, no whisper of wing-membrane. Not even the soft friction of body-on-stone. It is as though they exist in a layer of reality adjacent to sound, close enough to be seen, too far to be heard.

---

**THE WATER**

It is not a pool. It is not a stream. There is a low place in the floor — if the floor can be called a floor, if the slight concavity can be called low — where moisture gathers in a skin, a film, a sheen no thicker than a soap bubble. Kneel down and you can see your own face in it, but *wrong* — not reversed, as in a mirror, but shifted. Your left eye is where your right should be, but your mouth is in the correct position. Your hair moves a half-second after you move your head.

The film does not evaporate, though the air is cool and dry enough that it should. It does not pool deeper, though the seepage above should feed it. It simply *persists*, that single molecular layer of water maintaining itself against all physics, as though it has been told to remain.

Occasionally, one of the light-creatures descends to the water's surface and touches it, and the film *rings* — not audibly, but visibly, a ripple that you perceive not with your eyes but with some other sense, a proprioceptive ripple, as though the vibration is happening inside your own chest. The creature then ascends. You cannot determine what it has gained from the exchange. Neither can the creature, as far as you can tell.

---

**THE REMAINS**

In one corner — if the pocket has corners, if the soft geometry of its nascent walls permits the word — there is a structure. Or the ghost of a structure. Three stones stacked in a deliberate cairn, and they are the only hard things in the chamber. They are *old*, genuinely old, not embryonic like the walls. Basalt, dark, pitted with a history that extends backward in time in a way nothing else here does. They have been placed by human hands — you can see the thumb-smear where someone pressed a fourth stone into the top and then removed it, leaving a negative impression.

There is no fourth stone.

The cairn marks nothing. There is no burial beneath it, no cache, no inscription. It is simply a mark. *Someone was here. Someone saw this place before you. Someone stacked three stones and left.* The remains are so slight, so easily missed, that they feel less like evidence of civilization and more like a biological process — the way a bird drops a seed, the way a tide leaves foam. Perhaps the person who placed them did not choose to do so. Perhaps the pocket *requested* it, the way an oyster requests a grain of sand.

Near the cairn, pressed into the soft wall at knee-height, there are other marks. Finger-pressions. Not writing, not symbols — just someone's hand, pressed into the living mineral and left there. The print has not filled in. The wall has grown around it, slowly, over what might be days or centuries — time is uncertain here — so that the handprint is now a shallow relief, a fossil of a gesture that happened once and was remembered by the stone.

---

**THE REALITY**

The pocket is *wrong* in ways that accumulate.

Your sense of direction fails first. Not gradually — suddenly, completely, like a switch being thrown. You are facing the far wall, and then you are facing it again, and you have not turned around, but you are now certain that the far wall is behind you. You spin, slowly, and you find the far wall again, in front of you, exactly where it was. Both experiences are correct. Both are felt with equal certainty in your body. Your inner ear offers no resolution.

Then: scale. The pocket is the size of a root cellar. You know this. You measured it with your strides. But the light-creatures on the far wall appear to be at a great distance, the size of stars, and the ones on the near wall are at arm's length, the size of beetles, and both observations are happening simultaneously, overlapping, superimposed, so that the room is *both* — intimate and vast, close and infinite — and your eyes cannot reconcile it, and you feel a pressure behind them, not painful, just *insistent*, as though your visual cortex is being asked to perform a calculation it was never built to perform.

Then: causality. A creature on the wall near your left shoulder detaches and drifts to the floor. You watch it land. Then, a moment later, you watch it detach and drift to the floor. Then it is on the floor, already there, and the memory of watching it fall has the texture of a dream — something that happened to someone else, in some other pocket, in some other version of this place.

You do not feel threatened. This is important. The wrongness is not hostile. It is *generous*, as though reality in this pocket has been thinned to a membrane — like the water on the floor — and what bleeds through from the other side is not malice but sheer *excess*, too much reality for the available space, spilling over, overlapping, happening twice because there is nowhere else for the second happening to go.

---

**THE DYNAMICS**

The place is alive with process.

The walls grow. You can hear it if you press your ear to the stone — not a sound, exactly, but a *presence* of sound, the way a migraine is a presence of pain. Mineral being laid down. Crystal lattices extending, one molecule at a time, into the damp air. The chamber is slightly larger now than it was when you entered. Or slightly smaller. One of these is true.

The creatures cycle. There is a tide among them, a slow migration from ceiling to floor and back, though no individual creature appears to move in a straight line between the two. They drift, they loop, they vanish at the edges and reappear at the center. The tide takes perhaps twenty minutes. Perhaps two hours. Your watch, if you had one, would not help you; time here is a suggestion, not a law.

The moisture weeps. Down from above, in that impossibly fine mist, coating every surface in a skin of condensation that never thickens and never dries. The air smells of limestone and ozone and something sweet, something biological, something that suggests fruit ripening in total darkness — which is absurd, because it is bright, it is the brightest underground space you have ever been in, and the sweetness is coming from the creatures, or from the walls, or from the water, or from all three, or from the space between them, which may be the most alive thing in the room.

The cairn stands. The handprint fills in, molecule by molecule, one day in the future — near or far — it will be gone, absorbed into the wall, and the pocket will have no memory of the hand that pressed it there, and the hand will have no memory of the pocket, and the stones will sink into the soft floor and become indistinguishable from the embryonic stone around them, and the place will be born without history, as it was always trying to be.

---

**TO LEAVE**

You withdraw your hand. The tufa seals behind you, gently, with the faintest exhalation of trapped air, warm for just a moment against your knuckles.

The hillside is ordinary. Dry scrubland. A limestone slope baking under a flat white sky. You could press your hand to a hundred more stones and find nothing.

But you carry the light behind your eyes for an hour afterward — not as afterimage, not as memory, but as *presence*, a bright cool pocket-shaped space somewhere behind your sternum that takes its time dissolving, that resists dissolving, that asks, gently, with no urgency at all, whether dissolving is really what it wants to do.
