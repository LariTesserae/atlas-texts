# The Antrum Litae

You descend.

Not through water — there is almost none, only the occasional bead of brine trembling in a fissure like a glass eye — and not through soil or root-tangle, because nothing here has ever grown in any direction a botanist would recognize. You descend through *stone*, through a series of vertical shafts that narrow and widen without pattern, their walls slick with a residue that is not moisture but a mineral film, warm to the touch, faintly conductive against your skin. The air is thick, close, and heavy with dissolved heat — not oppressive, not burning, but *present*, the way a fever is present in a sleeping body. It presses against you from every direction, equidistant, as if the temperature of this place has long ago reached consensus with itself and will not be moved.

The shaft opens. You are inside.

---

## The First Vault

The space that receives you is not a cave. Caves are carved — by water, by acid, by the patient grinding of geological want. This space *contracted*. You can see it in the way the walls curve inward at the ceiling, concave and smooth, the way a drying fruit pulls away from its own skin. The stone here is a deep iron-banded something — not quite basalt, not quite gneiss — shot through with veins of a darker mineral that absorb what little light you've brought. The veins are dense. They are everywhere. They branch and re-branch and converge and fork again, and if you let your eyes unfocus, you begin to see them not as veins at all but as something *organized*, something with a grammar, though no reading you attempt will hold.

The floor of the vault is uneven, rising in low ridges and dropping into shallow depressions — not eroded, not smoothed, but *young*, as if the ground buckled recently and then stopped, arrested mid-gesture. The ridges are sharp enough to catch a fingernail. They are warm. You could not sleep comfortably on this floor without padding; it would abrade you slowly, the way sandstone wears a riverbed, except the river is your own weight and the stone would take its time.

The vault is perhaps a kilometer across. Perhaps more. Dimensions are difficult here because the walls do not end where you expect them to — they curve away into shadow and keep going, and the ceiling is high enough that its details vanish into a darkness that feels less like absence and more like *thickness*, a darkness with body and reluctance.

---

## The Inhabitants

You are not alone.

This is the first thing you understand, not because you see them — the dark prevents that, mostly — but because you hear them. Not hear, exactly. You *feel* the disturbance they leave in the quiet, the way you feel someone enter a room behind you even when you cannot turn around. The sound-register of this place sits low: subsonic, infrasonic, the creak of enormous weight shifting by millimeters, the occasional wet click of something adjusting its grip on a wall. The silence between these sounds is not empty. It is *attentive*.

When you do see one — and you will, eventually, because some of them carry their own light — it will not look like anything you have a word for. The closest approximation: imagine a chambered nautilus the size of a draft horse, its shell a spiral of calcium and iron fused into a single dark armature, its interior segments filled not with gas but with a dense, bioluminescent gel that pulses in slow amber rhythms. It moves along the walls on dozens of muscular, translucent feet, each foot tipped with a hard keratinous hook that it sinks into the rock with a sound like a muffled chisel. It is not fast. It is not slow. It moves at the speed of *purpose*, which is to say it does not dawdle and does not rush and does not respond to you at all, because you are not relevant to its schedule.

There are other things. There are things that nest in the high concavities of the ceiling — colonial, quiet, packed tight into crevices like fat white grubs, though each grub is as long as your arm and blind and armored with overlapping plates of a translucent mineral that grows from their own bodies. They are not insects. They are not worms. They are *themselves*, a phylum that exists nowhere else, shaped by the specific thermodynamic conditions of this vault and no other. They feed on the mineral film on the walls. They are patient. They have been here longer than the vault's current shape, and they will be here when it contracts further.

In the depressions, pooled in the shallow concavities of the floor, there are things that might be fungi or might be fauna — the distinction collapses here, in the way it collapses in the deep ocean. They are gelatinous, dark, segmented into lobes that slowly fold and unfold like the pages of a breathing book. They are warm-blooded, or warm-bodied at least, generating their own heat in a place that already radiates it, and they are *dense* — each lobe weighs more than it should, packed with calcium structures that serve no obvious structural purpose but ring like porcelain when struck. They are abundant. They carpet the depressions. You will step on one if you are not careful, and it will not mind, because minding is not something they have developed the architecture for, but you will feel the density of it through your boot — a weight disproportionate to its size, a heaviness that seems to come from inside the geometry of the thing itself.

---

## The Structures

You find the first one by accident: a low wall, waist-high, constructed from stacked blocks of a stone that does not match the surrounding bedrock. The blocks are rough-hewn, cut with tools — you can see the chisel-marks, the places where a blade struck and missed and struck again, leaving a gouge that is deeper than the intended cut. The wall is not straight. It curves slightly, following some logic that made sense to its builder and no sense to you. It is not a fortification. It is not a home. It is a *marker*, perhaps, or a *statement* — though about what, you cannot guess.

There are more. Scattered across the vault floor at no interval you can discern, there are cairns, walls, small roofless enclosures, all built from the same foreign stone, all showing the same imperfect handwork, all empty. Some have collapsed. Some lean. One — deep in the vault, near a cluster of the ceiling-grubs where the bioluminescent gel casts a faint amber wash across the floor — is nearly intact, a small rectangular room with a doorway facing northeast (or what was northeast when it was built; the vault has no compass points, no sun, no orientation except the one you impose). Inside this room, you find nothing. But the floor is worn smooth in a path from the doorway to the back wall, and the back wall has been polished by something — not by hands, but by *bodies*, the slow abrasion of countless repetitions, the same movement, the same path, over time measured in generations.

Whoever built these structures is gone. They are not the current fauna. They were something else — something that used tools and cut stone and oriented their doorways toward something that no longer exists. Their technology was simple: chisels, hammers, patience. The marks on the stone are the marks of hands, or appendages very like hands, applying force in the only way available to them — downward, rhythmic, unceasing. They built without mortar. They built without plan, or with a plan so alien that its organizing principle is invisible to you. They are gone, and the vault has absorbed their absence the way it absorbs everything: slowly, without sentiment, into its own equilibrium.

---

## The Dark

The light situation deserves its own accounting because it shapes everything. The vault is *dark* — not the darkness of a room with the lights off, which is a human darkness, a temporary and reversible condition, but a darkness that is the *default*, the original state, the thing that light is an intrusion upon. You brought a light, and the vault tolerates it, but you can feel the tolerance. Your light does not reach the walls in most directions. It illuminates your hands, the ground within a few meters, the lower bodies of the things on the ceiling, and nothing else. Beyond its radius, the darkness is not empty but *full* — full of warm air, full of slow movement, full of the low creaking of stone adjusting to its own temperature, full of the soft clicking of keratin hooks finding purchase.

The bioluminescence of the ceiling-grubs and the nautilus-things provides a secondary light source — amber, diffuse, unreliable, the color of old honey or slow fire. It does not illuminate so much as *suggest*, giving shapes to surfaces without resolving them, making the vault look like a photograph taken with too long an exposure: everything slightly blurred, slightly doubled, slightly more than it is.

---

## The Weather of It

At this scale — and the scale is vast, continental in its interior space, a network of connected vaults and chambers and passages that extends beyond any boundary you could walk to in a human lifetime — there is weather, of a sort. Not rain. Not wind. But thermal convection creates slow currents in the dense air, and these currents carry *information*: the mineral scent of one chamber migrating into another, the faint electrical charge that builds where certain mineral veins intersect, the subsonic hum of enormous masses of stone transmitting tectonic stress from deep below. The air moves in cycles — warm rises, cool sinks, though "cool" here is relative, a matter of a few degrees, enough to set the air in motion but not enough to feel like a breeze. You notice it as a change in the quality of the silence: the quiet deepens when the air is still, and softens slightly when it moves, as if the atmosphere itself is breathing but has forgotten how to do it with conviction.

---

## What Does Not Change

The defining quality of this place — the thing that makes it feel not just dark and strange and vast but *held* — is its stasis. Nothing here is in a hurry to become something else. The mineral veins are not growing. The vault is not contracting further. The fauna moves, but it moves within patterns so old and so rehearsed that the movement is functionally identical to stillness. The structures are not eroding because there is no water to erode them and no wind to abrade them; they will stand as they are for millennia, their chisel-marks sharp, their imperfections permanent.

You get the sense that this place arrived at its current state a long time ago and has been *maintaining* it ever since — not through any active effort, but through the sheer weight of its own inertia. It is warm because it has always been warm. It is dark because it has always been dark. The things that live here live because they have always lived here, and they will continue to live here in exactly this way until some external force intervenes — and nothing will, because the surface above is far away and indifferent, and the stone below is deeper still, and the equilibrium is so complete, so total, so deeply *negotiated* among all its participants, that it has become something very close to permanence.

You are a disturbance. You will leave. The vault will close over your passage the way water closes over a dropped stone — not quickly, not dramatically, but with the quiet certainty of a system that has no interest in remembering you.
