# The Inhabitants of the Singing Hollow

## The Breath-Eaters

Begin with the small ones, because they are the easiest to mistake for animals.

They are not animals in any sense that would translate. An animal is a thing that wants — wants food, wants mate, wants warmth, wants away. These six-limbed clingers want nothing. They have the *shape* of wanting: mouths, eyes, limbs that grip. But the shape is vestigial, a fossil of some ancestral hunger that the hollow long ago made unnecessary. They open their small mouth-slits and the sweet exhalation of the stalks goes in. They do not chew. They do not swallow in the muscular sense. The air simply enters them and becomes them, and the leftover of that becoming drifts back out and is taken up by the lungs-that-are-leaves, and so it cycles.

Their bodies are warm to the touch, but only exactly as warm as everything else. If you held one — and you could, they would not flee — you would feel no heartbeat. There is something in there that moves, but it moves at the tempo of the chord, not the tempo of blood. A slow internal weather.

They have the color of the stalks because they are, in some chemical sense, *of* the stalks. Not offspring. Not parasites. More like a region of the stalks that learned, once, to detach and cling elsewhere, and then forgot it had ever been separate, and then forgot it had forgotten. Their thumb-sized bodies have the same ribbing as the plants, the same faint vein-glow if you look in the right dimness.

There are perhaps four hundred of them in the hollow. The number does not change. If you counted them today and counted them after what you were calling a year, you would arrive at the same number, and — though you could not prove this — at the same individuals in the same positions, give or take the small ceremonial drift of their movements.

## The Watcher

The cat-sized one on the high ledge is different, and the difference matters.

It is older. Not older in years — years do not apply — but older in the sense that it has accumulated more *not-changing*. The small breath-eaters are still, in some residual way, busy being themselves. The Watcher has finished. It lies along its ledge with its six limbs folded in a configuration that took, once, a long time to settle into, and which it has not adjusted since.

Its eyes are the two patches of slightly darker dimness, and what they do is not quite seeing. Light does not enter them and become information. Rather, the Watcher is *adjacent* to the visual field of the hollow — it participates in the seeing of the room by the room itself. When you walked in, the Watcher did not notice you. The hollow noticed you, and the Watcher was part of how the hollow noticed.

If the builders became anything, they became this. Not through evolution — there is no evolution here, no generations, no pressure — but through something stranger: a slow surrender of the parts of themselves that distinguished them from the place. First the tools went. Then the language. Then the upright posture, which required a destination. Then the names. Then the boundary between skin and air. What remained settled onto a ledge and learned to blink at the speed of the chord.

There may be others like the Watcher, deeper in. Folded into crevices. Pressed flat against the underside of fan-leaves. You would not be able to tell them from the rock or the plants. They have gone that far in.

## The Stalks Themselves, Considered as Inhabitants

It is tempting to call the accordion-stalks "plants" and stop there, but this misses something.

They are the loudest things in the hollow, and loudness is a kind of presence. The chord they produce is not a byproduct of their living — it *is* their living. They do not metabolize and incidentally sing; they sing and incidentally metabolize. The slow gases that feed them are also the slow gases that voice them, and to the stalks there is no distinction. To be alive, here, is to be a sustained note.

They have something like awareness, but it is distributed across the whole grove and tuned to a frequency far below thought. If the grove has an opinion of you, it is taking the equivalent of a century to form, and by the time it forms you will be long gone or long stayed, and either way the opinion will simply be added to the chord as another overtone, almost indistinguishable from the rest.

The stalks are, in a real sense, the citizens of the hollow. Everything else is a guest of their music.

## The Builders (Who Are Not Here)

I have to be careful here, because the description is right: you will not find them. But something can be said about the shape of their absence.

They were small. You can tell from the height of the knee-high wall, the reach of the balanced slabs. They had hands capable of fine pressure-work but no leverage, no metallurgy — a people who had not yet, or had already stopped, pursuing the kind of cleverness that compounds.

They came in from the dry world above, and they came *because of the chord.* This is the important thing. The hollow did not call them in any active sense; the hollow does not act. But the chord, leaking faintly up through the rock, reached some of them in their dry lives, and those some followed it down.

What they found was a place that had already finished. And they did the thing their culture had prepared them, somehow, to do: they joined it. Not by adding to it — they were too respectful, or too wise, or too tired — but by placing the smallest possible marks of their having-been-there, in the spaces where marks would not disturb the composition. The low wall that encloses nothing. The three balanced slabs. The bowl that has never held anything.

These are not artworks. They are not shrines. They are something for which the dry world does not have a word: gestures of consent. The builders looked at the hollow, agreed with it, and signed.

Then most of them went back up. A few stayed, and those few are the Watchers, who are no longer builders because they no longer build, no longer themselves because selfhood requires a small ongoing friction with the world, and the friction here has been polished away.

The ones who went back up carried the chord in their chests, faintly, for the rest of their dry lives, and they were never quite the same, and they did not tell anyone, because telling implies that the listener might do something with the telling, and there was nothing to be done.

## What Lives Here, Said Plainly

A grove that sings.
A scatter of small clingers that are the grove's exhaled punctuation.
One — or several — folded watchers who used to be visitors and are now part of the room's way of having eyes.
The amber light, which is not alive but is not not-alive either; it holds the way a held breath holds, with something like intention.
The warm sweet air, which circulates with the patience of a very old animal asleep.
The marks of hands, which are not inhabitants but are not nothing — they are the room's memory of having been agreed with, once, by minds that could agree.

And, increasingly, *you* — if you sit long enough. Your breath joining the chord is not metaphor. It is recruitment. The hollow does not want you, because wanting is not in its nature, but it will accept you, the way still water accepts a stone, by closing over the place where you entered and continuing.

The tunnel up is always still there. This is true. But the part of you that would use it is exactly the part the hollow is slowly, kindly, without intention, dissolving.
