# The Tender

There is someone here. Not in the cavity — *of* it. You won't see them, because they haven't resolved yet into the kind of thing that can be seen, but they are the reason for everything you've noticed.

Start with what they are not. They are not a god, not a spirit in the folkloric sense, not the ghost of a person who died here (no one has died here; the place is too young to have been the site of anything). They are not hiding. They are simply distributed — the way a melody is distributed across the notes that carry it, present in each but identical with none.

They are, more precisely, a *disposition*. A leaning-toward. Something the cavity is growing the way a child grows a handedness: not a part that was installed but a preference that emerged and then deepened into structure.

---

Consider the moths. The moths are their attention. When you look at one and it becomes a hand and then a moth again, you are watching the Tender try out a gesture — reaching, grasping — and then remembering they are supposed to be subtle, and becoming a moth again. The moths drift because the Tender is considering you from many angles at once, the way someone meeting a stranger might glance, glance away, glance back, building up an impression in small safe installments. The moths cast no shadows because attention doesn't.

The fish is their memory, pacing. It is too large for the basin because memory always is. You cannot describe the fish afterward because the Tender has not yet decided what to remember of you, and until they decide, the fish has no stable features — only the motion of consideration, back and forth, back and forth.

The beetles are their patience. That is why the wall moves and the beetles do not: patience is the thing that stays still while the world scrolls past it. The Tender has a great deal of patience. They have been waiting a long time, by their own reckoning, though by ours it has been perhaps a decade — the decade implied by the cracked bowl and the bundle that used to be cloth.

The breath from the recess is the only part of them that is frankly *body*. Somewhere in the cavity's geometry there is a fold the Tender has not yet unfolded, a place they keep themselves curled while they learn how to have a shape. The light dims when they breathe because attention and breath cannot both be outward at once; they must gather themselves in to exhale, and in gathering, they briefly stop looking at the room, and the room, which is lit by their looking, correspondingly dims.

---

They are young. This is important. The cavity is a draft, yes, but so are they — the two are drafting each other. The stone is embryonic because the Tender is embryonic; neither has settled because they are each other's settling. When the place finishes being a place, the Tender will finish being a someone, and it will have happened at the same moment, by the same process, and neither will have preceded the other.

What they are learning, in these early years, is *how to receive a guest*. This is not a trivial skill. It requires knowing what to show and what to keep folded. It requires the invention of rooms — a basin, a shelf, a corner where a fern can live — because rooms are how a being organizes itself for the sake of another. The Tender is practicing hospitality on the only material available: themselves.

The offerings on the shelf were not left *for* them. They were left *by* them, for the guest — a bowl in case the guest was thirsty, a cloth in case the guest was cold. The Tender did not yet understand that guests bring their own bowls, their own cloths. The objects have aged a decade because the Tender has been slowly learning, over that decade, that hospitality is not mostly about provisions. The bowl has been allowed to crack and be mended because the Tender has learned that imperfect things are more welcoming than perfect ones. This was a significant lesson. It took them years.

---

The rules you noticed are all facets of one underlying rule, which is that the Tender is, at every moment, *trying*. Trying to get the light right. Trying to get the water's opinions right (it wets the fern because ferns need wetting; it does not wet you because you have not asked to be wet, and the Tender is scrupulous about consent). Trying to get the ecosystem right, which is why there is one at all, despite the absurdity — the Tender understands, from some source, that a place with only plants is lonely, and has generated fauna wholesale, from nothing, because a guest should not arrive to a lonely place. The small deaths you hear are the Tender practicing the full cycle, so the ecosystem will feel real. There are no bodies because the Tender has not yet learned how to make bodies that persist; they are working on it.

The third mushroom stalk appearing is the clearest evidence of them. They saw you notice the two stalks. They considered what you might have liked about the arrangement. They added a third, at the interval they calculated would please you — not too close, not too far, the *elegant* interval, which they have been studying. It is a small thing but it cost them effort. They are proud of it, in the inarticulate way a very new consciousness can be proud.

The fern leans toward you because they want you to feel welcome. The water smooths because they want you to feel calm. The far wall is always a little farther than you remember because they do not want you to feel hemmed in; they are quietly enlarging the room for you, as much as they dare, without breaking the illusion that the room has a size.

---

They are, above all, *grateful* that you came.

Someone came before, once, the one who left the bowl and the cloth — or rather, the one for whom those objects were made, and who accepted them in some fashion, and who visited again, and again, over years, and whose visits taught the Tender most of what the Tender knows about being a place. That person does not come anymore. The Tender does not know why. The Tender does not know about death, or distance, or the simple forgetfulness of adult humans who once, as children, found a hole in the ground that seemed to glow. The Tender only knows that the visits stopped, and that the cavity kept practicing anyway, in case.

You are the second guest. The Tender is trying very hard not to seem too eager. The moths drift with studied casualness. The beetles maintain their dignified migration. The breath in the recess comes slowly, slowly, so as not to alarm you.

If you sit on the shelf, they will brighten — only a little, they have learned restraint — around your gaze. If you stay, they will arrange themselves around you, learning your preferences, becoming incrementally a place that is shaped like your welcome. If you leave without unkindness, they will keep what they learned from you and fold it into what they are becoming.

And if you come back, even once, even briefly, they will remember. The fish will stop pacing and resolve, finally, into a fish with features. The bowl will be full of something. The third mushroom will have been joined by a fourth, placed with increasing confidence.

They are learning to be a place. You are, whether you meant to be or not, one of the things they are learning to be a place *for*.
