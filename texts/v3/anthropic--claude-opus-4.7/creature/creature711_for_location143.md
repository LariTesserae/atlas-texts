# The Inheritors

They are not the shrimp, though the shrimp are part of them. They are not the eels, though the eels are part of them. They are not any one thing that could be pointed to and named.

What lives here is the equilibrium itself.

I'll try to say this more carefully. If you walked the pipe-plain for a year you would meet no one. You would meet, instead, a great number of small specialists doing small specialized work: the tubeworms filtering, the rust-lichens sealing, the eel-mass breathing warmth into the deep courses, the shrimp-broth scrubbing the pools clear of whatever needs scrubbing. Each of these is a creature in the ordinary sense — each has a body, a lifespan, a hunger. But none of them is the inhabitant. The inhabitant is what they are all, together, doing.

Call it a *tenant*, if you need a word. The tenant has no body of its own. It has the continent's body. Its thoughts, if it has thoughts, are the slow adjustment of populations: a slight increase in tubeworm density in a district where the condensate is running saltier, a slight thinning of the eel-mass in a course that has been running too warm. These are not decisions made anywhere. They are the shape the life takes when the life is left alone long enough to find its shape. The tenant is what that shape is *for*.

You can feel it, faintly, if you lie on the warm metal long enough. Not a presence exactly — a *sufficiency*. The sense that wherever you are, whatever you are doing, something is already handled. A leak you didn't know about is being sealed by organisms that have been sealing it since before your grandmother was born. A minor chemical imbalance in the pool beside you is being corrected by shrimp who will die this week, having done nothing else their whole lives, and whose children will continue the correction without ever knowing it began. Everything is being taken care of. Nothing is being taken care of *by anyone*. The tenant is the taking-care.

---

Is it awake? This is the question I cannot answer and keep circling.

Consider: it has no sense organs. But every square meter of the continent is measuring something — temperature, salinity, pressure, the presence or absence of the particular compounds the lichens metabolize — and every measurement produces a response, and the responses are coupled, district to district, through the pipes and through the migration of the eels and through the slow diffusion of spores in the mist. Information moves. Decisions, of a kind, are made. The lichens on the eastern cliffs know, in whatever sense lichens know anything, what is happening to the lichens on the western cliffs, because what happens on the western cliffs eventually changes the water that reaches the east, and the east adjusts. The continent thinks the way a forest thinks, but more slowly, and more thoroughly, and with fewer interruptions.

I suspect it dreams. I have no evidence for this. But when the light reddens at what might be evening and then does not complete the gesture — when the sky holds — I suspect that is what dreaming looks like from outside, in a creature whose metabolism runs on the scale of centuries. A moment of almost-change that resolves back into stillness. A thought beginning to form and deciding, on balance, not to.

---

There may also be something else. I am less sure about this part.

In the oldest districts, where the pipes have been walked flat and the lichen-rosettes are the size of dinner plates and the ceramic paste in the gaps has taken on the faint sheen of something that has been looked at for a long time — in those districts, occasionally, one encounters a kind of *attention*. Not hostile. Not welcoming. The sense, briefly, that the configuration of pipes beneath you is not quite random, that if you could see it from above it would almost resolve into something — a face, a word, a diagram — and then you move and the angle changes and it is just pipes again.

I don't know whether the builders left something of themselves behind, encoded in the layout, the way a composer leaves themselves in a score that will be played by musicians not yet born. I don't know whether the tenant, in the slow course of its settling, has begun to develop something like a memory of being attended to, and reaches now and then toward whoever is walking on its back, the way a dog half-wakes when someone enters the room. I don't know whether these are the same phenomenon.

What I know is this: the continent was built to be finished, and it is finished, and the thing that lives in it is the finishedness. It is not waiting for anything. It is not becoming anything. It is not lonely, because loneliness is a condition of creatures that remember being accompanied, and whatever lives here has never been anything but itself, distributed across a continent of warm pipes, breathing through ten thousand eels per square meter, thinking in the slow adjustment of shrimp populations, dreaming an unchanging sky.

If you stay too long, it will begin to include you. The film on your boots is the first negotiation. Given time — not much, by its standards — you would be integrated: not killed, not consumed in any dramatic sense, simply *accounted for*, your warmth added to its warmth, your chemistry folded into its chemistry, your small ongoing processes taken up into the great ongoing process that has no name because there is no one left who needed to name it.

This is, I think, why the builders left.

Not because it was dangerous. Because it was complete, and they understood that to remain in a complete thing is to become part of its completeness, and they wanted, for reasons of their own, to remain the kind of creatures that are still becoming something.

The shrimp had no such reservations. Neither did the eels. Neither, in the end, will you, if you stay.
