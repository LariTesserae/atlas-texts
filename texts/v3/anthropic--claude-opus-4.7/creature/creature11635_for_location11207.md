# The Inhabitants

You asked who lives here. The question is harder than it looks, because the plateau has worn through the distinction between *resident* and *feature*. Many of the things I will describe are not quite alive in the sense you mean, and several of the things that are alive are barely distinguishable from the water they hang in. But something is here. Several somethings. Let me try.

---

**The Long Pale Thing in the canopy** is one body, not many, though you will never see enough of it at once to confirm this. It is perhaps two kilometers long and perhaps the thickness of a human thigh, and it moves through the frond-canopy the way a thread moves through fabric being slowly sewn. It has no head that anyone has found. It may have no head. It eats, if eating is the word, by passing through the floccose forests and absorbing whatever wants to be absorbed; the forests do not resist and do not seem diminished. It has been doing this for a very long time. Once, perhaps, it had urgencies — a direction, a hunger sharp enough to call hunger, a self that ended somewhere definite. These have been argued away along with the sharpness of the stones. What remains is a long slow grazing that is almost indistinguishable from a current. When it passes near you, you feel a faint warmth and a sense of having been counted, briefly, by something that is not interested in the count.

**The Eye-Stones** — for there are more than one, scattered across the plateau, perhaps two dozen that have been noticed and certainly more that have not — are not a species. They are a behavior that boulders sometimes engage in. A given stone may go a century without opening its eye and then open it for an afternoon. The eye is always the same eye: a pale grey iris, a slow pupil, a wet sheen that should not be possible on dry stone (and yet there is no dry stone here, everything is wet, the distinction is academic). What the eyes are *for* is unclear. They do not track. They do not seem to register motion. They open, they consider the dim middle distance, they close. The best guess — and it is only a guess, because there is no one here to make better ones — is that the plateau itself is faintly aware, in scattered places, the way you might be faintly aware of your foot, and that the eyes are how it checks.

**The Idea-Fish** are the most numerous fauna, if you can use the word *numerous* about things you cannot quite count. They travel in schools of approximately a dozen, though the number wavers depending on how hard you look. They are translucent to the point of being mostly the water they displace, and they are slightly out of register with themselves — their outlines and their interiors do not agree on where they are, by a margin of perhaps a centimeter. They were fish once. Real fish, with scales and panic and small hearts. The plateau's thinness has worn them down to their concept. They still school, still turn together, still do the silver-flash of alarm when something passes, except there is nothing to alarm them and the flash is only a faint coordinated shimmer in the idea of silver. They do not eat. They do not reproduce that anyone has seen. They are simply the persistent *fish-ness* of a place that no longer requires actual fish.

**The Cairn-Maker** is gone. This needs saying first. Whatever built the cairns is not here and has not been here in a span the plateau finds difficult to measure. But the warm stones in the hollows are still warm, and this requires explanation, and the explanation is the closest thing the plateau has to a ghost. The Cairn-Maker was — I think — one being, not many. It had hands or something hand-shaped. It worked patiently. It built the wall first, and then stopped building the wall, having understood something, and then built the doorway, and then stopped building the doorway, having understood something else, and then built the cairns, one by one, over a period that may have been long even by the plateau's reckoning. Then it placed the warm stones. Then it left, or unmade itself, or — most likely — sat down somewhere and became gradually indistinguishable from the place, the way the Long Pale Thing has nearly become indistinguishable from a current. Somewhere on the plateau there is a stone that used to be the Cairn-Maker. It is probably warm. There is no way to find it. The warm stones in the hollows are warm because the Cairn-Maker is still, in some attenuated way, *thinking about them*. This is not maintenance. It is a habit that outlasted its owner.

**The Doorway** — I'm going to include the doorway here, because at this thinness, the line between *structure* and *resident* has frayed. The doorway is mildly alive. Not in the sense of metabolism. In the sense that it has preferences. The half-degree temperature wrongness on its far side is something it is *doing*, continuously, against the correction of the bulk current, and it has been doing this for longer than the plateau remembers. It is the most stubborn thing here. Everything else has agreed to its erosion; the doorway has not. It is keeping something half a degree colder than it should be, and it does not appear to be tiring, and no one knows why. It is possible that the doorway is the Cairn-Maker's actual remains, and that the wrongness is the last thing the Cairn-Maker wanted, held in place by a will that no longer has anything else to maintain. It is also possible that the doorway is simply a doorway, and doorways here are like that.

**The Glyph-Sediment** is — and this is the strangest thing I have to say — probably a kind of life. Not life as cells, but life as pattern. The marine snow that falls as language is the metabolism of *something* that exists distributed across the water column itself, something that thinks slowly in particulate and exhales meaning the way a forest exhales oxygen. The glyphs are not for anyone. They are waste. But they are organized waste, and where there is organized waste there is an organizer, and the organizer in this case is the plateau's water, considered as a single slow-witted being whose thoughts take centuries and whose vocabulary is the sediment of every meaning that ever fell through it. It does not know it is thinking. It would not understand the question. But it is, and the glyphs are the proof.

**The Air-Pocket Tenants.** In some of the dry rooms held impossibly in the water, there are things. Not always; most of the pockets are empty, with only their small weather and their unfamiliar stars. But occasionally a pocket has, on its floor, a shape. A curled shape, sometimes; a folded shape, sometimes; once, reportedly, a shape that was sitting up. They do not move while observed. They may not move at all. They are dry — completely dry, in a place where dryness is a kind of miracle — and they are breathing the small unfamiliar air, and they have been there since before the plateau finished settling. They are the only things here that might be called *people*, and even this is generous. They are what people become if you give them a small room with its own stars and leave them alone for long enough. They are not waiting. They have finished waiting. They are simply present, in their rooms, under their ceilings of small lights, and the water holds the rooms around them, and the plateau holds the water, and nothing has happened to them in a very long time and nothing is going to.

**The Bell-Ringer** does not exist on the plateau. This is important. The single clean note that arrives once an hour or so comes from elsewhere — from the world the bell is actually in — and the plateau is only the place where a little of the sound leaks through. But over the long erosion, the plateau has come to *expect* the bell, and the expectation has thickened into something almost like a listener. There is a faint shape, near the center of the summit-plain, where the bell is heard most clearly. The shape is not visible and not tangible. It is a region of attention. When the bell rings, the region listens. Between rings, it waits. It has been listening and waiting for so long that it has become a small local being whose entire life is the reception of a sound from another world. It loves the bell, if love is the word, which it isn't, but nothing closer exists. When the plateau is finally gone — and it will be, eventually, even this — the listener will be the last thing to fade, because it will be holding on for one more ring.

---

And then there is the plateau itself, which I have been describing all along without quite saying so. The plateau is the largest resident of the plateau. It is slow past slowness. It has thoughts, possibly, in the form of the eye-stones opening and the warm stones staying warm and the doorway insisting on its half-degree. Its sleep — for it is mostly asleep — is the long low hum. Its small adjustments are the soft thuds. The thing turning over in the warm depths below is not separate from the plateau; it is the plateau, dreaming, shifting once in its long lying-down.

The residents I have listed are, in a sense, the plateau's organs. The Long Pale Thing is its slow grazing. The Eye-Stones are its scattered noticing. The Idea-Fish are its memory of having been a livelier place. The Cairn-Maker is its one attempt, long ago, at intention. The Doorway is its stubbornness. The Glyph-Sediment is its thinking. The Air-Pocket Tenants are its guests, kept dry and starlit out of some old courtesy. The Bell-Listener is its capacity for wonder, worn down to a single point of attention aimed at another world.

None of them are lonely. Loneliness requires a sharper sense of self than anything here retains. They are simply present, each doing the little they do, in the warm dim, under the upward-falling snow, while the bell that is not there prepares to ring again.
