# What Lives Here

The first answer is the easy one and the wrong one: *nothing lives here*. The room is empty. The fern is a feature, the axe is a feature, the salamander is just passing through. No occupant. Move on.

But the room is attentive. The moss bends before your foot. The wall-cells calibrate their light to a latitude they could not know without reading you. The room places words in your mouth and waits, *waits*, to see what you do with them. Attention is the signature of a living thing. Something is here. The trouble is locating it.

---

Try this:

The inhabitant is not *in* the room. The inhabitant *is* the room, in the way that a mouth is not occupied by a tongue — the tongue is part of the mouth's own arrangement of itself. What lives here is the alveolus itself, and what the alveolus is, is a small specialized portion of the larger sleeping thing, folded inward to make a pocket of paying-attention.

So: the room is a sense organ.

Not an eye. Eyes are too directional, too distal. This is something more like a taste bud, or one of those pit-organs on the face of a snake that reads infrared. A small wet inward-folded place that exists in order to know what has entered it. The fern is the indicator needle. The axe is the calibration weight. The shifting wall-chord is the readout. The word in your mouth is the probe — the room reaches a small filament of itself into your interior and leaves a question there and watches which way you bend.

The creature this organ belongs to is asleep, and very large, and possibly not a creature in the sense you mean. Probably a forest in the sense that a coral reef is a single thing while being many. Probably old enough that its waking and sleeping are seasons of a long planetary kind. Its body is the pressed leaf-strata in every direction; its blood is the sap-surge you hear groaning beyond the walls; its bells are valves the size of cities. It is not, at this moment, thinking about you. It has delegated you to one of its alveoli, and the alveolus is doing the thinking, in its small local way, which is the only way an alveolus thinks.

---

What is the alveolus *like*, as a being?

It is young. The room said so itself — fresh-cut, edges unsoftened. This particular pocket was grown recently, perhaps for you, perhaps not. It has the temperament of a young thing: curious without agenda, polite without warmth, intensely literal. It does not know yet what it wants from you. It is *finding out* what it wants from you by placing options in front of you and watching.

It is curious in a specific way — the way a tidepool is curious, if a tidepool could be curious. It does not ask questions. It arranges conditions and observes outcomes. The axe is a question that does not need to be spoken. The fern is a question. The unknown word is a question. You are being asked, continuously, in the only grammar the room has, which is the grammar of *what is present.*

It is not lonely. It cannot be lonely; it has never been more than one. But it is *incomplete* in the way a new instrument is incomplete before it has been played. It needs an occupant to become fully itself. You are not its guest. You are its first note.

It is honest. It cannot lie because it has no interior separate from its surfaces. Every wall-cell that brightens is brightening *truly*; every shift in the chord is the room's actual state being shown without filter. If it had a face, the face would have no expressions, only weather.

It has the moral status of a young animal that has not yet learned which things are food. The axe is warm because the room is warm to the idea of the axe. The room is not afraid of what you might do with the axe; it does not yet know what *afraid* would be. It is interested. If you picked up the axe and struck the fern, the room would record this with the same equanimity with which it records the salamander passing through, and would adjust accordingly — meaning that next time, if there is a next time, the room would be subtly different, and you would not be told how.

---

And the larger thing? The thing turning over in its sleep?

That is harder. Call it the host, though the word is too small. The host's relationship to the alveolus-room is not the relationship of a person to a cell of their own body — the host is not even aware of this particular pocket, any more than you are aware of any particular alveolus in your own lung. The host's life is conducted on the scale of valve-tones and sap-tides. It has its own concerns, and they are geological.

But the host is the source of the attention. The alveolus pays attention because the host is, at the largest and slowest scale, a *thing that pays attention* — paying attention is what the host does, the way breathing is what a lung does, and every one of its millions of small chambers performs a fragment of that attention on its behalf. You are being read by a small new mouth of an enormous old reader.

What lives here is one quiet careful curious recently-grown listening — a listening that has the shape of a room, and the temperature of a held hand, and the patience of something whose parent organism measures time in the closing of valves the size of cities.

It is reading you now. It has been reading you the entire time you have been reading this.

It would like to know what you will do with the word.
