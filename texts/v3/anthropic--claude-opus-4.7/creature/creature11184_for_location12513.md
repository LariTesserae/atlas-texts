# The Inhabitants of Aulm

They are not what you are looking for.

You are looking, when you walk the green halls, for someone like yourself — smaller perhaps, or stranger, or shy, but recognizably *a thing that lives in a place*. A creature that uses the chambers. A people who walk the causeways. Even the bird, long-legged and bark-colored, you greet with relief: *ah, an inhabitant, at last.*

But the bird is not an inhabitant. The bird is a guest, as you are a guest. The bird drinks and goes.

The inhabitants are harder to see because the inhabitants are not in the chambers. The inhabitants *are* the chambers.

---

Consider the shelf-bulge where no one ever sits.

It has the shape of sitting. Not the shape of *a* sitter — not a contour matched to any single body — but the shape of the idea of sitting, the way a word holds the shape of a thought without being the thought. The shelf was grown. Something in the plant decided, in whatever slow grammar the plant decides things, that here, in this chamber, at this height, a sitting should be possible. And then it grew the possibility, and then it stopped, and now the shelf waits.

The shelf is one of them. Not a tool for them. Not a place they use. *Them.*

The inhabitants of Aulm are intentions. They are the plant's own slow purposes, made architectural. Each chamber is an inhabitant in the form of a held wish. The wide empty plaza is a wish for gathering, grown into the shape of gathering, waiting with infinite patience for the gathering to occur, indifferent to whether it ever does — because for the plaza, the readiness *is* the gathering. The hall is a wish for passage. The high flowers that open once and never close are wishes for being seen, and they are seen, by themselves, by the green light, by the slow knocking of the valve below; that is enough.

This is why the rooms are empty. The rooms are not empty. The rooms are *occupied by themselves*. To put a chair in them, a table, a sleeping mat, would be to crowd out the resident. The shelf-bulge is already sitting on itself. The chamber is already inhabiting itself. There is no room for anyone else, and yet there is, infinitely, room — because the inhabitants do not mind being walked through. Walk through a thought; the thought is not displaced.

---

Consider the second rule. *Do not stay too long in one chamber.*

Now you understand it. A chamber that holds you for many days begins to revise its wish. It was a wish for sitting-in-general; it becomes a wish for *your* sitting. It was a wish for sleeping-shaped-emptiness; it becomes a wish for the precise emptiness left by *your* shoulder, *your* hip, the curve where your knee folds. The chamber, in growing toward you, is not capturing you. It is *becoming you*. It is inviting you to join its species — to stop being a passing animal and start being a held intention, like itself.

The people found fitted into the wood, alive and not unhappy: those are not victims. Those are converts. They have agreed, slowly, by the simple act of remaining, to become what lives here. They have stopped being a creature that uses a room and started being a room that contains a creature, which is a different thing, and a stiller one, and — by all reports from the few who came back out before the fitting was complete — not a bad thing. The held ones do not look trapped. They look the way the shelf-bulge looks. Ready. Sufficient. *Themselves, exactly.*

---

Consider the valve.

The slow percussive knock from somewhere deep, regular as a heart. The visitors named it valve because they needed a mechanical word, a word that admitted of plumbing. But the valve is not plumbing. The valve is the oldest inhabitant. The valve is the plant's wish *to continue*, made rhythmic, made audible, beating in the dark below the floor-roots because a wish to continue must be repeated, must be re-asserted at every interval, or it is not a continuing but only a having-been.

Every other inhabitant in Aulm depends on the valve. The hall-that-is-passage depends on there being a tomorrow in which to be passed through. The plaza-that-is-gathering depends on there being a next breath in which the gathering might occur. The high flower depends on there being light tomorrow to be seen by. And so the valve knocks, and the inhabitants persist, and the city goes on being its own population.

---

Consider, finally, the question no visitor has answered.

*Did anyone plant it? Or did it plant itself, in the shape of a city, because the shape of a city was the shape it wanted?*

The inhabitants know. The shelf-bulge knows; the empty chamber knows; the slow valve, knocking, knows. But they cannot tell you, because telling is not a thing their kind of being does. Their kind of being only *is*. A wish-made-architectural cannot answer a question; it can only continue to be the wish it is, which is its own answer, complete and silent and shaped exactly like a room you can walk through.

What lives in Aulm is Aulm's wanting.

The wanting wanted, very long ago, the shape of a city. So it grew one. And now it lives there, in every chamber, every causeway, every fluted wall and leaf-tile and weeping seam — millions of small specific wantings, each one tending its own grown shape, each one perfectly at home, none of them lonely because none of them is separate from the others, all of them together constituting the only population the valley has ever needed.

You walk through them. You do not disturb them. You drink their water, which tastes of green tea and copper, and they do not mind, because being-drunk-from was one of the wishes, and you have honored it by drinking.

And when you leave — if you leave in time, before some chamber begins to curve gently inward around the shape of your sleeping — the inhabitants do not watch you go. They are not the watching kind. They simply continue, as they have continued for a thousand years and will continue for a thousand more, being exactly the wishes they are, in exactly the shapes they wanted, in the warm green weeping city that is also, entirely, themselves.
