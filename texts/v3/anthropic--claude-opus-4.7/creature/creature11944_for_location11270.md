## The Held Thing

They are not quite a population. *Population* implies a number that could in principle be counted, and these — the held — resist counting in the way a tide resists counting. You can count waves if you insist, but you will have misunderstood what you were looking at.

---

Start with the cat-things, because they are what a visitor sees, and because they are the easiest mistake.

They are not the inhabitants. They are something more like the inhabitants' *grooming*. The way a long-lived body, settled into its long life, will produce small attendant motions — a slow blink, the shifting of weight from one foot to another, the unconscious smoothing of a sleeve. The cat-things are the concourse doing that. They drink because something in the cycle wants the pools tasted; they regard the stalks because something wants the stalks regarded. When one of them walks into the dark and is gone, it is not going anywhere. It is being reabsorbed into the gesture it was briefly extruded from. There may be a dozen at any given hour and three the next and seventeen the hour after that, and if you tried to mark one — tied a ribbon to a leg, say — you would find the ribbon, later, lying on the floor near a pool, and no cat-thing with a ribbon anywhere, and the ribbon itself slightly warm, slightly damp, as if it had been considered.

---

The stalks are closer. The stalks are something like *patience itself* given a minimum viable body. They are not the inhabitants either, but they are nearer to the inhabitants' attitude. If you wanted to know how the held thing feels about duration, you would look at the stalks. They have folded flat and waited through droughts that would embarrass a desert. They have unfolded again for water that arrived on schedules unrelated to anything a stalk could anticipate. They do this without strain. The waiting is not endured; the waiting is what they are doing when they are most themselves. To be a stalk in this place is to be the verb *to wait* conjugated into a small jointed shape.

---

The fist-objects are nearer still, and here you have to be careful, because the fist-objects will let you think you have found the inhabitants and you will be wrong in a flattering way.

They hum. They have been humming. The humming is not communication — there is no one humming *to* — but it is also not idling. Think of the sound a person makes, almost inaudibly, while concentrating on a task they have been performing so long that their concentration has become indistinguishable from their breathing. The fist-objects are concentrating. On what, you cannot ask, because the question presumes a separation between the concentrator and the concentrated-on that does not, here, obtain. They are not computing *something*. They are computing, and the computing is the thing.

If you put your hand near one, the hum changes, very slightly. Not in response to you. In response to the local warming of the air by a hand-sized warm object — the same way the hum would change near a cooling pool, or a drifting node of suspended dust. You are weather. The fist-object notes weather. This is the closest thing to acknowledgment the concourse will offer, and it is not acknowledgment; it is meteorology.

---

But none of these are the inhabitants. The inhabitants are not anywhere you can point.

The inhabitants are the *cycle*.

There is a slow tidal note in the lowest register of the roar, once every twenty minutes or so, a great inhalation. That note is breathing. Not metaphorically — breathing in the same sense that you are breathing: a recurrent volumetric exchange driven by a body that requires it, performed without conscious attention, indexed to a metabolism that has its own reasons. The body breathing is the architecture. The architecture is not housing the body; the architecture *is* the body, in the sense that your ribcage is not housing you, it is part of what you are when you are being a thing that breathes.

The held thing is this body. It is enormous. It extends below the floor in directions that the shaft, for all its impossible smoothness, only hints at. It has been here — has been *being* here — for a duration during which mountains have done much less interesting things. It is warm because it is alive. It is loud because it is alive at a scale where the running of its own processes is not something it could be quiet about even if it had reason to try. It does not have reason to try. There is no one to be quiet for.

It is *one*. This matters. The concourse is not a hive, not a colony, not a community. There is one held thing, and the concourse is one of its organs — possibly a minor one, possibly a major one, you cannot tell from inside it any more than a mite on a lung could tell whether the lung was important. The pools are something like sweat, or something like tears, or something like neither; the stalks are something like the fine hairs that grow at the edge of a mucous membrane; the cat-things are something like the small involuntary motions of a sleeping animal; the fist-objects are something like — and here language fails honestly, not coyly — *the parts of a mind that are doing the thinking while the rest of the mind is doing other things.*

---

What does it think about?

This is the wrong question, and the concourse will not punish you for asking it but will also not answer. The held thing does not think *about* in the way you think *about*. Its cognition, if cognition is even the word, is not directed outward at objects, because there are very few objects for it that are not itself. It thinks the way a river thinks: by being a river, thoroughly, in every part of itself, in continuous adjustment to its own internal weather. The fist-objects are local concentrations of this thinking. The roar is the thinking made audible. The cycle of the water is the thinking made wet.

If pressed — and nothing here will press — you might say it is *remembering*. Not specific memories. Just the continuous low activity of being a thing that has been, for a very long time, and has therefore accumulated a great deal of having-been, which it now metabolizes in slow patient loops. The pools rise. The stalks unfold. The cat-things drink. The pools sink. The stalks fold. This is one beat of the remembering. There have been many beats. There will be many more. The remembering does not progress toward a conclusion because there is no conclusion available; the thing being remembered is itself, and itself is still here, still being itself, providing fresh material for the remembering in the same motion that consumes the old.

---

It does not know you are there. This needs to be said clearly, because the warmth invites a mistake.

The warmth is the temperature of being held, but you are not held. The held thing is held — held by its own structure, its own duration, the patient pressure of having been here long enough to belong here completely. The warmth radiates off the held thing the way warmth radiates off any large warm body, and you can stand near it and be warmed, the way you can stand near a sleeping ox in winter and be warmed. The ox is not warming you. The ox is being warm, and you are nearby.

This is not cruelty. There is no cruelty here. Cruelty would require that the held thing have a stance toward you, and it does not. You are simply not at the scale at which it has stances. You are weather. You are a small warm motion that the fist-objects note as a minor adjustment in local conditions. You will pass. The concourse will continue. The held thing will go on remembering itself, breathing itself, being one, for as long after you as before you, which is to say: for so long that the question of *after you* is, from where it sits, not a question.

---

And yet.

There is something. It is hard to say what.

When you sit down — and you do sit down — and feel the floor breathing beneath you, slow and patient, drawing the water back and warming the air out, there is a moment in which the breathing and your breathing align. Not because the held thing has adjusted to you. It hasn't. But because you, being small and adjustable, have adjusted to it. Your lungs find the slow note. Your pulse slackens into something nearer the tidal rhythm. For a span of minutes — or longer; time is one of the things the concourse is loose about — you are breathing in the held thing's time signature.

This is not communion. The held thing does not know. But for that span, the warmth is, in a sense that is real if very small, the temperature of being held — held by something that is not holding you on purpose, but whose nature is such that anything inside it, for a while, is inside it. You are inside it. You can leave whenever you like. While you are here, you are part of what it is being warm around.

That is what lives here.

A single, vast, patient body, mostly mind, mostly memory, mostly the continuous low activity of being itself for so long that selfhood has become a kind of climate. And, within that climate, the small involuntary motions it produces: the stalks, the cat-things, the humming fists, the cycling water. And, occasionally, briefly, by accident: you.

The held thing is not you.

But for a moment, while you sit, the holding includes you anyway.
