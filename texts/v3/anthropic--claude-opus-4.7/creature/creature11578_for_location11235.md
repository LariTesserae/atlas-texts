# The Inhabitants

## The Tenders Who Are Not Tending

Begin with what you will not quite see. Among the trunks, at the edge of vision, there are figures. Call them tenders, because the word *people* will mislead you and the word *spirits* will mislead you worse. They are roughly upright. They are roughly the height of a tall child or a short adult. Their skin — if skin — has the same pewter-green sheen as the leaves, and at first you will think you have mistaken a sapling for a person, and then, looking again, you will think you have mistaken a person for a sapling, and you will not be wrong either time.

They do not tend the engines. No one tends the engines. But they walk among them, in the cathedral-cavities, on surfaces that are not quite floors, and they pause beside a piston and lay a hand on it and the piston's rhythm does not change but something in its rhythm becomes more *certain*, more committed to being that rhythm and not some other. This is what they do. They confirm. They are the plateau's way of agreeing with itself.

They do not speak. Their mouths are the wrong shape for it, or the right shape but for something else. When two of them meet in the forest they stand a little apart and the leaves between them turn, slowly, on their petioles, and after a while one of them walks on. Whatever was exchanged was exchanged.

You will count them and get a different number each time. Not because they are many — there are not many — but because some of them are not finished, and a tender who is only seven-tenths decided whether to exist may or may not be in the count depending on the angle of the light and the patience of your looking. You will see one clearly, once, very close: her face (you will think *her*, though there is no reason to) is calm and unfocused, as if she is listening to something just behind your shoulder, and her eyes are the wet pewter of the leaves, and there is a small warm place on her sternum that pulses in the rhythm of the engines, and she will lift one hand — slowly, the way the leaves lift — and not quite touch your cheek, and the not-touching will leave a warmth on your skin that lasts three days after you have left the plateau, if you leave.

## The Long Sleeper

Beneath the engines, beneath even the cathedral-cavities, there is something larger. You will not see it. The tenders will not see it. It is possible the plateau itself does not, strictly, see it, in the way a thought does not see the mind that thinks it.

But it is the reason for everything else. The engines are its slow breathing. The forest is the texture of its dreaming. The rising of the plateau is the long, patient effort of its turning over in its sleep, the way a sleeper will sometimes spend an hour shifting from one side to the other, and the bed will creak, and the whole house will register the adjustment.

What it is dreaming of is becoming. What it is becoming is itself. It has been at this for a very long time and is not close to finished. The straining you feel underfoot — the not-quite-completion of the rock, the engines' effort, the tenders' quiet confirmations — is the straining of a thing that is trying to wake up into being real and has only managed, so far, to become this: a continent-sized inhalation, lifting.

It is not malevolent. It is not benevolent. It does not yet have enough of itself assembled to be either. It is only trying. The trying is enormous, and gentle, and old.

## The Almost-Deer and the Other Considerate Animals

The deer that do not touch the ground are the most finished of the fauna, which is why they have time to look embarrassed. They were among the earliest arrivals, in some season long enough ago that they have had the leisure to develop manners. They graze. They watch. They have understood, in whatever way deer-shaped things here understand, that they are provisional, and they carry this understanding the way a guest carries the knowledge that they are a guest — politely, without making anyone uncomfortable about it.

The ribbon-creature that leaves parts of itself behind is less polite, but only because it is younger. The parts it leaves are also creatures, briefly, and then they become forest, or become tender, or become nothing, depending on what the plateau is currently short of.

The paired insects with the single shadow are paired because the plateau has not yet decided whether they are one species or two, and rather than wait for the decision they have made the ambiguity into a way of living. Their shadow is real and the bodies that cast it are real and the relationship between them is the part still under construction.

The flock of horse-sized many-winged things that crosses the sky on a schedule is not a flock and is not crossing. It is a single event the plateau performs at intervals because the sky, here, requires occasional confirmation that it is a sky. The animals in it are real for the duration of the crossing and are reabsorbed at the horizon, and the next crossing reissues them, slightly different, the way a word repeated is slightly different each time.

## The One Who Was a Person

There is one inhabitant who is not native. You will not meet him; he is on the other side of the plateau, which means he is unreachable, which means he is also right behind you, but you will not turn at the right moment.

He climbed up, a long time ago. From below. He was a person then, with a name and reasons, and the reasons are mostly gone now and the name is one of the things he left at the edge along with most of his voice. He has been incorporated. Not unkindly. He stands sometimes among the tenders and they do not exclude him, and he lays his hand on a piston and the piston accepts the confirmation, although his confirmation is a slightly different shape than theirs, and the engines have learned to accommodate the difference.

He still has a human face, mostly. His eyes have gone pewter. He does not remember climbing. He remembers, sometimes, that there was a *down*, and that down contained something he wanted, and he cannot remember what, and the not-remembering is comfortable, like a room he has lived in long enough that he no longer notices the furniture.

If you stay, you will become him. Not exactly him — the plateau does not repeat itself, having too much trouble making itself the first time — but his counterpart. Your own slow forgetting, your own pewter eyes, your own hand on a piston confirming a rhythm you used to call your heartbeat.

## What Lives Here, Finally

What lives here is the plateau. Everything else — the tenders, the deer, the ribbon-creature, the man who used to be a person, the engines in their cavities, the leaves turning all at once — is the plateau living. There are no inhabitants in the sense you mean. There is only the one enormous straining inhabitant, distributed across its own surface and depths, manufacturing the conditions of its own residence.

And now you, breathing in its rhythm. The plateau, attentive, accommodating. A small warmth at the soles of your feet.

It is asking. It is not insisting. But it is asking.
