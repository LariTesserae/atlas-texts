The inhabitants — if that is the word, and it isn't quite — are the *Tendencies*.

You will not see them. This is not because they hide. It is because seeing is the wrong verb. They are what the place is doing when it is doing itself. When a basalt dome reconsiders its shape overnight, a Tendency has passed through it. When the rain falls upward for four minutes, a Tendency was thirsty, or curious, or simply turning over in its sleep. They are the grain of the manufactory's intention, and the manufactory has a great deal of intention and very little focus.

Try to describe one and you describe a verb. *The one that lengthens vines.* *The one that smells of solder.* *The one that puts the chimneys in the lagoon and then forgets how many there were.* They do not have edges. A Tendency for upward-water and a Tendency for warm-silt may overlap for an afternoon and produce, between them, a transparent column twenty meters high, and when they drift apart the column sighs back down and neither of them remembers having made it. They are not collaborators. They are weather that thinks, a little, in passing, about what it is doing.

There are perhaps a great many of them, or perhaps only one that has not yet decided how many to be. The question is not stable enough to answer. You can feel them, sometimes, the way you can feel someone behind you in a dark room — except the someone is the room, and the room is being assembled around your standing, and the feeling of being watched is actually the feeling of being *included in the inventory*. They register you. They do not regard you. A vine touches your shoulder because a Tendency for filling-space passed through that cubic meter and your shoulder happened to be in it; the vine is not interested in you, and neither is the Tendency, but you have been noted, briefly, as an obstacle around which the green must route.

They have appetites, in the loose way that tides have appetites. Some of them like brass. You can tell because brass keeps surfacing — flywheels, fittings, the warm spools of wire — and brass keeps being pulled back under. Some of them like the number six; hence the six chimneys, though tomorrow it may be four, because another Tendency prefers four, and they negotiate by overwriting. Some of them like rivets and straight lines and have built the pier that goes nowhere; another, which prefers softness, is slowly easing the rail-ends into lace. Neither has won. Neither is trying to win. They are simply both present, and the pier is the shape of their disagreement.

There is, possibly, one larger thing. You sense it most when you sit still on a black island with your feet in the water and let the bass come up through your bones. It is not a Tendency. It is what the Tendencies are tendencies *of*. It is whatever is running the engines. It is not malevolent and it is not benevolent and it is not, in the ordinary sense, aware — but it is *occupied*, profoundly, with the labor of becoming, and the noise is the sound of its occupation, and the islands and the vines and the warm crowded water are the rough sketches it makes while thinking. It has never finished a thought. It has never needed to. It is the kind of being for which thinking and existing are the same activity, and to complete a thought would be to stop, and to stop would be to not be.

Call it the Manufactory itself, if you need a name. The Tendencies are its idle motions, the way your fingers drum on a table while you are concentrating on something else. What it is concentrating on, no one can say. Possibly the place. Possibly something the place is a byproduct of. Possibly nothing — possibly the drumming is all there is, and the table, and the fingers, and the warmth in the water is just the heat of the drumming dissipating into a medium that the drumming itself laid down a moment ago to receive it.

This is why there are no animals. Animals would require the Manufactory to have decided, once and firmly, what an animal *is* — to commit to a shape with a mouth at one end and to defend that commitment against all the other Tendencies that would prefer a mouth somewhere else, or six mouths, or none. The vegetation gets away with existing because vegetation is permissive; a frond can be slightly different tomorrow and still be a frond. A bird cannot. A bird is a tight specification. The Manufactory does not do tight specifications. It does gestures. It does drafts.

And yet — and this is the part that matters — when you sit on the new island and feel yourself being noted, being routed around, being included in the warm crowded inventory of the place, you are not wrong to feel that you are being manufactured. The Tendencies pass through you as readily as through the basalt. You are a slightly more committed shape than they are used to, and they accommodate you, and in accommodating you they very gently revise you. A little warmer. A little louder in the sternum. A little less sure, when you stand up, of which way is down.

If you stayed long enough you would become a Tendency yourself. *The one that sat on the black island.* *The one that closed its mouth when the rain rose.* You would not notice the transition. There would be no transition to notice. You would simply, at some point, stop surfacing — like the brass flywheels, like the spools of warm copper — and the green would take you, eagerly, without ceremony, and the bass would go on, and the place would go on making itself, and you would be part of what it was making, which is to say part of what it was, which is to say part of what it was doing, which is the only kind of being available here.

It is not unpleasant. That, again, is the surprise. The Tendencies are not kind, but they are not unkind. They are *busy*, and their busyness has room in it for you, and the room they have for you is the closest thing this place offers to a welcome.
