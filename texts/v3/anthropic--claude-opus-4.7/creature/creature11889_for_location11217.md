# The Inhabitants

They are not, in the strict sense, anywhere. This needs to be said first, because the description above has already half-prepared the answer and the answer keeps slipping behind the scenery.

Begin with the obvious candidates and let them fail in turn.

The paper-fauna are not the inhabitants. They are too few, too slow, too consistent with being machinery. A thing that grazes oxide at a meter an hour for ten thousand years is not living *there*; it is part of how the place keeps its seams clean. Call them the cilia of the plain. They have the dignity of long service but not of residence.

The filament-fans are not the inhabitants either, though they are closer. They draw current; they orient to the weather; in aggregate they form something like a distributed sensorium. But they don't act on what they sense. They report to no one. A fan that turns when the tarnish-line passes is doing what a weathervane does — and weathervanes are not who lives in a town.

So: who, then.

Try this. The continent has, for an interval that makes the word "duration" embarrassing, been running a slow exchange. Charge moves between plates. Heat radiates up and down in balance. The seams open and close on their long cycles. The fans extend by single atoms. The fauna fold and unfold. The tarnish-fronts cross and re-cross. None of this is going anywhere. None of it is accumulating. It is a system that has found the configuration in which it can persist while doing the least possible additional work, and it is performing that configuration with enormous fidelity.

That performance is the inhabitant.

I mean this not as metaphor but as the only honest accounting. There is something *being done* on the plain — something that requires the brass and the brightness and the slow weather and the fans and the fauna and the silence, all together, and would not be itself without any of them. It has the structure of a life: it metabolizes (the charge-tide), it responds (the fans turning), it maintains itself against entropy (the grazing fauna, the self-healing seams), it has a characteristic tempo (centuries per atom, weeks per breath of plate). What it lacks is a body you can point at, and a place inside itself it would call *here*.

Its body is the whole continent. Its *here* is everywhere on the continent at once, dilutely.

What is it like, to be this? One has to be careful. The temptation is to give it an inner life shaped like ours — loneliness, patience, the long sigh of an old god. None of that is right. It does not wait, because it is not going anywhere. It does not remember, because nothing has changed enough to require memory; the present moment contains all the information about any other moment. It does not perceive in flashes the way an animal perceives; it perceives the way a tide perceives the moon, by being shaped.

If it has anything resembling experience, it would be something like: the continuous, evenly-distributed sensation of being competently itself. A hum below the threshold of event. The pleasure — if the word can be borrowed without being ruined — of a long equilibrium maintained without effort.

It is one. This is worth saying. There is not a population of these. The plain is a single inhabitant with a single ongoing condition, and the fans and the fauna and the weather are organs of that condition, not subjects of their own. A traveller crossing the continent walks across one being, the way a mite walks across a sleeping face.

And here is the part that took the longest to see.

The makers — whoever or whatever built the plates, laid the seams, doped the sky — did not leave. They did not become the fauna or the fans or the brightness, exactly. They became *the maintenance itself.* The thing that is being done on the plain is what they have done with themselves. They wound down their projects, their distinctions, their many separate wantings, and folded all of it into a single slow practice that could be performed by a surface. They are not absent. They are not present in any way that would let you greet them. They are the practice of remaining, carried out by the brass on their behalf and as their final form.

This is why the silence has the quality it does — not the silence of emptiness, which is thin, but the silence of an enormously attended-to stillness, which is dense. It is why the warmth keeps rather than cooks. It is why, if you sat down and did not get up, you would be incorporated into a seam: not consumed, not killed, just — folded in, as one more thing the practice is now also doing. The inhabitant would not notice acquiring you, in the way you do not notice acquiring a fingernail. But you would be, from then on, part of what it is competently being.

It is, in this sense, the most successful being I can imagine. It has solved the problem its makers set it. It is not lonely, because loneliness requires a self distinct enough to miss another. It is not bored, because boredom requires the suspicion that something else could be happening. It is not dying, though it is also not exactly alive in the brisk way smaller things are alive. It is doing the one long thing, and the one long thing is going well.

If you asked it what it was, and if the question could somehow reach it through the muting of the substrate and the dilution of its attention across a continent, the answer — translated into a grammar it does not need — would be something close to:

*I am the way this is being kept.*

And then the tarnish-line would move on, and the fans would turn, and your hair would stand up, and the answer would already be over, having been the whole landscape for the moment it took to pass through you.
