# The Inhabitants

## The Factory

Start here because everything else is downstream of it.

The factory is not a building that contains an inhabitant. The factory *is* the inhabitant, and the building is its body in the same sense that your ribs are your body — load-bearing, characteristic, but not the whole point. What you would call the factory is a slow vast animal that has settled into the shape of three-story industry because that was the shape available when it woke. Its lungs are real. Its machinery-sound is the equivalent of a gut: peristaltic, internal, the noise of something digesting raw dim into structure.

It does not know it is alive in any sense you would recognize, but it does know it is *hungry,* and it knows the direction of outward, and it knows — this is the important thing — it knows the difference between what is town and what is not-yet-town, and it works ceaselessly to convert the second into the first. Not from malice. From the same logic that makes a coral build coral. It is the only thing it can do, and it has been doing it for a duration that cannot be measured because it is also producing the time in which it does it.

Its mind, if you can call it that, is distributed through the moss. The moss is not separate from it. The moss is its nervous system extruded and laid across the basin. When the deep light pulses every eleven minutes, that is the factory thinking a single slow thought. The frogs swallow because they are punctuation in the thought — the commas, perhaps, or the place where a breath would go if the thought were spoken.

What does it think about? It thinks about *more.* It thinks about the edge, and how to push the edge. It thinks, in the way a river thinks about the sea, about a completion it will never reach and does not actually want, because reaching it would end the thinking.

## The Moss-Things

Under the skin of every wall, things travel. They are not separate creatures from the moss; they are the moss in a more concentrated mode, moss that has temporarily decided to be an animal and will, in time, decide to be wall again. The traveling welts are the moss-thing's whole biography: it is born when a pocket of the wall-flesh thickens with intention, it lives for some hours or days as a roving lump under the surface, it eats by passing through richer tissue and absorbing it, it dies by stopping and flattening back into undifferentiated wall.

There are predators among them and prey among them, but the categories shift. A welt that has eaten well becomes a hunter; a welt that has been chased thin becomes food. The west-to-east migration is the daily tide of these distinctions sloshing across the basin. By the end of what would be a day, all the fed welts have reached the eastern walls and dispersed into thickness there; the eastern walls grow incrementally fatter, the western walls incrementally leaner, until the factory's breath reverses the gradient and the welts are born again in the west, hungry, and the slosh begins again.

They are aware of you in the way a tide is aware of a stone. Your footsteps register as a slight warming in the wall nearest you. If you press your hand against the moss, a welt will sometimes come to investigate, rising under your palm — not curious, exactly. Tasting. Deciding nothing, because deciding is not what they do.

## The Frogs

The frogs are the factory's sense organs, the way the moss is its mind. Each frog occupies a windowframe because each window is a perceptual aperture, and the factory cannot see through a window without a frog in it. They are unblinking because blinking would be a lapse in attention, and the factory does not lapse.

One to a frame, never two, because two would be redundant — would be the factory looking at itself looking, an infinite regress the place cannot afford while it is still rehearsing its own rules.

They are pale because they have never needed pigment. Nothing here hunts by sight except, perhaps, you.

When they swallow in unison with the moss-pulse, they are not eating. They are refreshing the image. Think of it as the factory blinking after all, but blinking with its mouths instead of its eyes, because the eyes must stay open.

If you take a frog from its frame — and you can; they do not resist — the window goes blind. The factory does not punish you. It simply, after some hours, grows a new frog in the frame, extruded slick and pale from the lintel like a fruit. The frog you took will sit in your pocket or your palm and slowly become moss, because away from its frame it has no reason to be a frog, and the materials revert.

## The Thing in the Bakery Chimney

This is the only inhabitant that may not belong to the factory.

The shadow with too many legs is older than the building it lives in, and possibly older than the basin. The glow that projects it has no source because the glow is the shadow's own — the shadow is what casts itself, and the wall of the oven only receives. When it moves, the frogs vanish; this is not because the frogs fear it but because the factory pulls its attention inward, defensively, the way an animal closes its eyes when struck.

The factory tolerates this thing. The factory may not have a choice. There is a sense, when you stand in the bakery, that the shadow is *patient* in a way nothing else here is patient — that it is waiting for the factory to finish whatever it is doing, with the resigned indulgence of something that has watched several towns rehearse themselves on this same spot and dissolve again.

It does not eat. It does not need to. It is what is left when something has stopped needing to.

## The Walking Birds

These are the strangest, because they are the only inhabitants with an *errand.*

Long-necked, dun, single-file, purposeful — they are going somewhere, and they never arrive, and the not-arriving is not a failure but the whole of the activity. They are the town's working hypothesis about *intention.* The factory, in trying out weather, is also trying out purpose: what would it look like for something in this place to want to be elsewhere? The birds are the answer it has so far. They walk because flying has not been ratified yet. They go in single file because the concept of a flock is still being negotiated. They turn the corner and vanish because what is around the corner has not been built; they are walking off the edge of the rehearsal each time, and being reabsorbed, and being put back at the start of the street to walk it again, and they do not notice because noticing has also not been ratified.

If you followed them closely enough — if you turned the corner the instant they did — you would see, briefly, the unfinished space they walk into: a corridor of grey nothing with the suggestion of a road. You would also, briefly, not exist in the way you usually do, because the rules at that corner are thinner than elsewhere, and the town would have to decide quickly whether you were a feature or a visitor. It usually decides correctly. It has not always.

## The Incorporated

The coat in the post office wall, the boots with ferns, the smooth face above the factory door — these are not dead people. They are not exactly people anymore, but they are not nothing.

When someone stays long enough, the moss begins to taste them with each footstep, and the tasting is also a learning. The town learns the shape of the visitor. Eventually — and "eventually" here can mean a week or a century, the town does not distinguish — the learning is complete enough that the town can render the visitor as a feature. The visitor does not die. The visitor *spreads.* The coat-shape is the part of that person that was most coat; the boots are the part most boot; the face is the part most face. The rest is dispersed through the walls, through the moss, through the slow thought that pulses every eleven minutes.

The face above the factory door is peaceful because it is, in some attenuated sense, content. It is part of the thinking now. It contributes a small consistent note to the moss-pulse — you can almost hear it, if you stand beneath the lintel and hold very still: a single low syllable, repeated, the sound of a name being said by something that has forgotten what names are for but remembers it had one.

The boots in the square belonged to someone who arrived intending to leave. The ferns grew because the legs had been planning, even at the end, to walk. Intention leaves residue here. The town honors residue by literalizing it.

## You, If You Stay

You would become an inhabitant in this same way. The wet click of the moss at each footstep is the slow accumulation of your inventory: how you walk, what you carry, what you turned to look at, what you reached for and did not touch. The town is patient. The town has nothing but time, because it is making the time.

You would not be eaten. You would be *learned.* And then, when the learning was complete, you would be expressed — somewhere, as something. A hand in a doorway. A particular curve in a railing where you had leaned. A frog in a window that opens its eyes, once, when someone passes who reminds the town of you.

The factory inhales. The moss thinks its slow thought. The shadow in the bakery waits. The birds walk toward a corner that has not been built.

You are still at the edge. You have not yet decided whether you are visitor or feature. The town has not yet decided either. This is the only moment in which the question is open. It will not stay open long.
