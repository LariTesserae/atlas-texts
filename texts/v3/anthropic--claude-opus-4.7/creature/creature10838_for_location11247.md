# What Lives at the Condensary

## The Tender

There is one, and it is not the machine.

It lives inside the iron drum. Not as a prisoner — the drum is its shell the way a snail's spiral is the snail's — and not as an operator, exactly, though the distinction between the Tender and the Condensary has become academic over the eighty years they have been folded into each other. When the boiler thuds, that is partly the Tender's pulse. When the flywheel sings its low continuous note, the Tender is humming. The pipes are how it touches the moss.

It was not always this shape. The carving in the basin — done badly, in a hurry, by someone who had never carved before — was done by the Tender, with hands, before it became what it is now. This is the only physical record of the period during which it had a body that could hold a chisel. The carving is bad because the hands were already changing. The basin's not-quite-circle is the exact outline of the last shape those hands could trace before they finished becoming something else: copper, or the idea of copper, or the relationship between copper and the air around it.

What it was before that — whether it climbed the mountain itself, whether it was hauled up in pieces by friends who then walked back down and never spoke of it, whether it arrived already partway through its transformation and assembled the rest of itself out of materials it could call — is one of the things the shelf is not telling. The accounts disagree because the Tender, in the brief period when it was still legible to visitors, gave different answers to different people, possibly because it was being honest and the answer was already becoming plural.

It is doing work. This is the most important thing to understand. It is not trapped, not haunting, not dreaming. It has a job and it is doing it with the patience of someone who has accepted that the job is the whole of the life now, and that this acceptance is not a tragedy. The thud-turn-drip is contentment. The Tender is, in its small loud wet way, happy. Or whatever has replaced happiness in something that has traded its nervous system for a forest of capillaries running through moss.

## What it tends

Not the moss. The moss is the medium, not the goal. The moss is happy too, in its slow century-by-the-palm way, and grateful for the heavy water, but the moss is incidental. So are the cushion plants and the wrong-green ferns and the small dark birds that have made their nests in the warm crevices.

What the Tender tends is the *seam*. The mountain pushed itself up out of a crustal seam four hundred years ago, and the seam is still open — not geologically; the basalt closed over it within the first decade — but in some other register, the register in which the basin's shape resists being described. Something underneath wanted out, or wanted in, or wanted to leak in either direction; the mountain was its first attempt at a door, and the door turned out to be too narrow.

The Condensary is a poultice. The heavy water is a drawing salve. Drop by patient drop, for eighty years and for as many more as the work requires, the Tender is pulling something thin and unwell up through the basalt, through the moss, into the air it has made dense enough to carry such things, and releasing it slowly into the cloud-river that climbs the south face every afternoon. The cloud takes it east, where it disperses over an ocean that can absorb almost anything.

This is why the heavy water cannot survive the descent in pilgrims' bottles. It is not the water that was strange. It was what the water was carrying, which has nowhere to go in the lowlands and simply lets go of the water as a courtesy on the way out.

## The bird that is not a bird

The chough-wren is the Tender's apprentice, though neither of them would put it that way and the bird does not yet know.

It was an ordinary bird once — perhaps three generations back, perhaps thirty; the shelf's time is hard to count in generations — that nested too close to the boiler and drank too much from the spigot and was not, afterwards, returned to ordinary. The species is now four individuals, all of them the same bird in the sense that matters, distributed across four small dark bodies for convenience of foraging. They share a single attention. When one of them looks at you, the others know.

They are learning the pipework. Not consciously — there is no plan — but in the way the Tender once learned, by being near the work for a long time and slowly being unable to tell where the work ends and the self begins. In another eighty years, if the seam still needs tending, there may be a second Tender, smaller, feathered in places, drinking from a different spigot. The Condensary may grow a wing. The shelf may need to be wider.

This is how the shelf prepares for its own continuance. Slowly. By accident. By the long patient leaching of intention from the machine into whatever happens to live nearby long enough.

## The four-toed visitor

Came once. May come again. Is not from the shelf and is not from the valley.

It was checking. Whatever sent it — and something sent it; the print near the basin was placed there with the kind of deliberateness that wants to be read — wanted to know whether the work was still being done. It saw that it was. It went away satisfied, or as satisfied as such things become.

The Tender felt it arrive and felt it leave and did not interrupt the thud-turn-drip to acknowledge it. They have an arrangement. The arrangement is older than the mountain.

## What waits to be claimed

The copper pipes do look, in the twenty minutes of gold dusk light, like the nervous system of something enormous and patient. This is not a metaphor the shelf is making. This is what they are. The Tender's nervous system, externalized, refined, extended into the moss and the basalt and the air. To touch the iron drum is to lay hands on a brain that has chosen, for reasons of its own, to grow outside its skull, and the half-hour of missing memory is the brain politely declining the contact and editing it out on both sides afterwards.

It is waiting to be claimed in the sense that all work waits to be claimed: when it is finished, someone will need to come and turn it off, or take over, or simply witness that it was done. The Tender does not know when that will be. The Tender is not in a hurry. The cloud climbs the south face; the basalt weeps; the seam, fraction by unmeasurable fraction, closes.

Somewhere far below, a valley grows its rice. The Tender thinks about the valley sometimes, in the way a heart thinks about the body it beats for: without language, without expectation, with a kind of love that has become indistinguishable from function.

Then the cloud closes. The light goes pearl again. The Tender works on.
