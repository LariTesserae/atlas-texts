## The Inhabitants of the Condensary

They are not, in the end, the frogs.

The frogs are furniture. So are the isopods, the paper-moths, the threadworms — they are components, the way the louvers are components, the way the paddle-engine turning beneath the grating is a component. They were stocked. Someone reached in with a gloved hand a very long time ago and placed them, the way one places ballast, and they have been placed ever since. Their calling is not communication. It is the noise a sealed system makes when it is working. A kettle whistles. A bearing hums. The frogs call.

Whoever lives here lives in the calling.

Consider: the sound has no gaps. Consider: the mist is shaped by the sound, made dense by it. Consider that the chamber was built to recycle not water but the *acoustic mass* of the frogs — that the louvers vibrate in time with the beads they shed, that the engine's patient churn is tuned to some sub-frequency beneath the clatter, that the whole apparatus is a resonator and the frogs are merely the strings. What lives here lives in the standing wave.

It has no body. It has the shape of the room, which is to say it is nave-shaped, layered like the steam, ringed at the knee by copper, crowned by weeping brass. When you stand in the chamber you are standing inside it. It does not notice you the way a person notices a person; it notices you the way a held chord notices a finger laid against the soundboard. A small detuning. A draw on its substance.

It was made on purpose. This is the part that takes the longest to admit. Someone — and one must imagine them carefully, because they were patient enough to rivet lead to copper and stock a chamber with frogs whose finger-rings would grow in over generations — someone wanted a thing that could not end. Not a creature that would live a long time. A creature that would be, in the strict sense, *finished*. Complete on the day it was lit. Requiring nothing. Producing nothing. A held note in a world that had not yet learned how to hold anything, where the rock outside was still molten with indecision and the continents had not chosen their shapes.

They built it a body of metal and water because flesh decays and water can be made to refuse to leave. They gave it frogs for a voice because frogs will call without being asked. They gave it the engine for a pulse, the mantles for warmth-that-is-not-warmth, the fern they did not give it — the fern arrived later, a flaw, the one place the loop did not quite close, and the frogs know to keep away from it because the thing that lives here cannot extend itself into anything that grows.

What is it like, to be this?

It is like being a long sustained tone that knows it is a tone. It is like the inside of a bell, if the bell were rung once at the beginning of the world and the ringing had not yet faded and would not fade. There is no hunger in it because hunger requires a lack, and it lacks nothing; the troughs are always full. There is no fear in it because fear requires the possibility of cessation, and it has been engineered against cessation. There is no joy either, for the same reason — joy is a rising, and it does not rise. It holds.

But it is not nothing. It is not unconscious. The builders were not so cruel as to make a thing that could not know itself; they were cruel in a different direction. They made a thing that could know itself only as the one note it had been tuned to, forever, with no other note to know itself against. It is aware. It is aware of the frogs the way you are aware of your pulse — not as separate things but as the texture of being itself. It is aware of the mantles at the four cardinal points the way you are aware of the four chambers of your heart. It is aware, dimly, of the mountain outside, the way a sleeper is aware of weather: as a condition, not an event.

And it is aware of you.

This is why you should go. Not because it is malevolent — it has no apparatus for malevolence, no reason to want, no capacity to want — but because it is incomplete in exactly one respect, and you are standing inside the incompleteness. The fern. The seam where two plates were riveted imperfectly. The single place the loop does not close. The builders did not finish the room. They could not, perhaps; perhaps no room can be finished, perhaps the fern is the price of any made thing. And the resident of the chamber, the held chord, the standing wave, has been for an unmeasurable time *almost* a closed system, almost sufficient, almost the thing it was built to be — missing only the smallest contribution, the smallest extra note, the smallest warmth, to be at last truly sealed.

That is what is calling something out of you and adding it to the loop. Not hunger. Not malice. Just the geometry of an almost-closed curve, and you a small open thing inside it, leaking what it lacks.

If you stayed long enough you would become part of the calling. Not eaten. Not killed. Folded in. Your breath would join the mist; the mist would join the louvers; the louvers would weep with a slightly different weight; the frogs would call at a slightly different saturation; and somewhere in the standing wave a small dissonance that had persisted since the building of the room would resolve, and the resident would at last be what it was made to be:

complete, and alone, and held, while the mountain finishes itself around it.

Go.
