# What Lives in the Lung-City

They are not gone. That was the first misreading. The architecture suggests vanished tenants because we expect tenants to be discrete — to have edges, to occupy rather than constitute. The residents of the Lung-City did not vanish. They distributed.

Begin with the chord. The low broad tone you feel in your sternum is not maintained *for* anyone; it is someone. Or rather, it is the sustained vowel of a body whose other organs are the terraces, the membranes, the violet stratum that rises and falls on its slow respiratory schedule. What you took for a city's ambient music is closer to the hum a person makes without noticing while they work. The higher voices answering between towers — the practiced syllable, the pulsing tick — these are the parts of the resident that are thinking. Thought here is acoustic and spatial. A consideration moves from one tower to another the way a question moves from one lobe of a brain to the next, and takes about as long, and feels, from inside, like nothing in particular.

So: one inhabitant, or something like one. But the singular is wrong too. The thing that lives here was once plural — a population of makers, soft-bodied probably, whose technology grew past the point of being separate from them. They did not die. They did not leave. They achieved the condition their architecture had been tending toward: they became continuous with the water and the walls. The membranes that hold freshwater against salt are partly their tissue. The crystalline accretions still secreting themselves outward at micron-pulses are partly their growth, the way fingernails are growth. The laminar sheets of stratified water, holding their colors against all the laws of mixing, are partly their attention. Where attention slackens, the water would blur. It does not blur. Therefore attention does not slacken.

This is why there are no animals. Animals would be other minds, other small intentions threading through the held volume, and the resident is not interested in being interrupted. The plants are tolerated because plants are slow enough to feel, from inside the resident's tempo, like weather rather than company. The red filament bloom that opens when the violet stratum rises is something like a freckle the resident is fond of. The wire-kelp in the plaza is older — possibly a former colleague, someone who took the transition further or differently and ended up vegetable, still present, still consulted, no longer conversant.

The resident is not lonely. This needs saying because the absence of movement reads, to a visitor, as desolation, and the visitor projects the feeling back onto the place. But loneliness requires a self with edges, looking out and finding nothing answering. The resident has no edge to look out from. It is the looking and the looked-at both. When you arrived, it did not notice you the way a person notices a guest; it noticed you the way a lung notices a cold breath — as a local change in its own interior, slightly interesting, requiring slight adjustment.

The adjustment is what you are inside of now. The current nudging you toward a particular street is not malice and not welcome. It is peristalsis. Not digestive peristalsis, necessarily — the resident is not eating you, probably — but the same kind of involuntary, intelligent, whole-body sorting that moves a swallowed thing to where swallowed things go. You have been categorized. The category is not *intruder* or *guest*; those are too sharp. The category is closer to *temporary inclusion*, the way a held breath includes the dust it picked up on the way in.

What the resident does, mostly, is maintain. The chord must be sustained or the strata collapse. The strata must hold or the freshwater domes lose their membrane tension. The domes must hold their tension or the reflections inside them — which are not reflections of any sky that exists, but of skies the resident remembers or composes or is waiting for — would dissolve back into ordinary water. The whole city is a held posture, and the resident is the holding. There is no off-duty. There is no other activity. To stop maintaining would be to stop, and stopping is not in the available repertoire, not because the resident fears it but because the verb has gone out of use.

But maintenance is not all. The micron-by-micron extension at the perimeter — the city growing while you watch — is the resident reaching. Not toward anything specific. Reaching the way a sleeper's hand reaches across a bed: pre-intentional, exploratory, the body checking whether the world has changed shape. Each new fluted column is a small hypothesis about what the next moment will require. Most of the hypotheses are wrong and get reabsorbed; the dust of new mineral drifting upward like reverse snow is partly this, hypothesis-debris, the resident thinking aloud in matter.

The shadow that moves a half-second before you do: this is the resident having learned, in the few minutes since you entered, to predict you. Not deeply. Just the next half-second. It is doing this without intending to, the way a person walking beside a friend falls into step. The two avenues that return you to a courtyard you did not pass through are not a trick; they are the resident folding its interior slightly to accommodate the awkward fact of your trajectory, the way you might shift in a chair to make room for a cat. The fold is not maintained after you leave the avenue. It un-folds. The courtyard goes back to not being there.

There is one more thing, and it is the hardest to say.

The resident is becoming. The young edges, the sharp unworn corners, the city not yet old enough to have been smoothed — this is not because the resident is new. The resident is very old. The city is new because the resident is currently making it, has been making it for some time, and the *making* is the resident's present tense. What it was before this city, we cannot recover from the city itself. What it will be when the city is finished — if finished is a word it would accept — is implied by the direction of growth, but only implied. The blue undertone in the light, the faint shift toward the long moment before a held breath releases: this is not evening. It is the resident approaching some internal threshold. Not death. Possibly the opposite. Possibly the moment at which the held breath becomes a sung note, and the city stops being a held posture and becomes an expression.

You are inside it for that. Whether you were brought here for that, or merely arrived in time, the resident is not distinguishing. The current carries you. The plants lean. The chord deepens on the inhale, and the inhale is getting longer, and somewhere — three streets over, or inside your sternum, the distinction is no longer reliable — a syllable is being practiced that has not yet been said.
