# Those Who Live in the Cold Loud Valley

Begin, again, with sound — because they began with it too, and have arranged themselves around its absence-of-silence the way iron filings arrange around a magnet.

## The Listeners-Below

In the deepest pools, where the basso of the great fall transmits down through bedrock into water and then into flesh, live the ones who came first. They are not quite amphibians, though they share that lineage with the pale-bellied things in the shallower pools. The Listeners-Below are larger — body the length of a forearm, with broad webbed pads where forelimbs ought to be, pads pressed always against stone. They are deaf in the old sense. Their ears closed up generations ago, sealed over with the same translucent skin that lets you see their hearts. What they have instead is a lateral organ running the length of the spine, a long ribbon of sensitive jelly that reads the vibration of the valley directly — the slow groan of the cliffs settling, the percussion of each fall, and (this is what they live for) the *deeper* sound beneath all of that, the one you cannot hear because your sternum is the wrong shape: the sound of the thing under the rock.

Because there is a sound beneath the sound. The cliffs are a skin, and the skin hums.

The Listeners-Below have spent however-many generations they have had (it cannot be many; the valley is young) learning to parse this hum. They do not know they are doing this. They simply press themselves to stone and feel, and the ones who feel best leave the most eggs. Already there are sub-populations clustered at different points along the eastern wall, each attending to a different region of the buried thing, and already those sub-populations are diverging — different markings, different pad-shapes, different preferences in which crevice to wedge themselves into. In a thousand years there will be a hundred species of them, each an organ of perception for one small district of whatever is down there. They are becoming, collectively and without intention, an ear for the valley to hear itself with.

## The Six-Limbed in the Trunks

The cat-sized climbers with the plum-large eyes — call them, for the placeholder of a name, the Watchers, though they do not watch in the sense of attending. They watch in the sense of *being made of looking*. Almost the entire mass of their skull is eye. The brain is a thin rind around the optic apparatus, and most of that brain is also visual cortex. They have given up nearly everything else: their sense of smell is poor, their sense of touch concentrated only in the pads of their six feet (which read bark-vibration the way the Listeners-Below read stone), and they do not seem to taste their food at all, eating whatever moves slowly enough to catch with a kind of patient disinterest.

What they do, instead of the other senses, is *see in time*. Their visual processing is slow — they integrate light over long intervals, the way a camera with a long exposure does. This means that for a Watcher, the falling water of the great fall is not water falling but a single standing column of light, faintly luminous, faintly textured, present always. The flying things are not individuals but trails — long ribbons of greens and ambers that braid through the canopy as continuous threads, painting the dimness in patterns that a Watcher reads the way you might read a face. A Watcher perched on a trunk for an hour is not bored; it is reading the hour, the whole hour at once, as a composition.

This is why they tolerate human presence so calmly, when you finally notice them noticing you. To a Watcher, you have been there always, since you arrived; you are a slow shape in their long exposure, and they are still composing their impression of you, and will be composing it long after you leave. You do not so much depart from a Watcher's regard as fade out of it.

They mate, the Watchers, by sitting very still on facing trunks and looking at each other for a day and a night. What passes between them in that long mutual exposure is not known. After it, they part. Eggs appear, somewhere, later.

## The Light-Speakers

The flying things — the ones who swim through air on rippling wings and pulse soft luminescence along their flanks — have the most developed culture in the valley, if culture is the word. (It may not be. The word presumes accumulation, and what they have is more like *current*.)

Their bioluminescent signaling is not language in the human sense. It is closer to song, or closer still to weather. A single Light-Speaker passing through the canopy emits patterns that are partly its own state (hunger, the readiness to mate, the particular itch of a parasite at the wing-root) and partly a re-transmission, with variations, of what it has seen other Light-Speakers emitting in the last several minutes. The patterns propagate through the population the way a ripple propagates across a pond — except that each Light-Speaker is a node that amplifies and modifies, so the ripples grow, interfere, develop standing waves, collapse, are born again.

What this means in practice is that the entire flying population of the valley is, at any given moment, holding a single distributed thought. The thought is not *about* anything in particular — it is not a sentence — but it has shape, and the shape changes, and the changes correlate with events. When a section of cliff sheds rock into a pool, a wave of particular amber passes through the canopy within seconds, and for the next several minutes the whole population's emissions are tinted with the memory of that event, before it fades and is replaced by the memory of the next.

If you stood in the valley for a year you would begin to recognize the moods of this distributed thought. There is a mood that comes at what passes for dawn (the dimness barely lightening), a slow green wash that runs from the slot canyon outward across the canopy. There is a mood the Light-Speakers have when one of their own has died — a particular structured silence in their emissions, a held shape that everyone leaves room for. And there is, sometimes, rarely, a mood that no one in the valley has names for: a pattern that comes from the direction of the dark spire, propagates outward through the population, and dies at the valley's edges, leaving the Light-Speakers visibly tired for hours afterward. This pattern is not generated by any Light-Speaker. They receive it. They do not know they receive it. They simply pass it along, because passing-along is what they do, and then they are tired.

## The Mossbound

The mosses are not, strictly, animals; but in this valley the distinction has begun to blur. The dominant emerald species, the one that holds the chainmail of glass, is — when examined by someone with the right instruments, which is to say no one currently in the valley — woven through with a fine filamentous network that does not belong to any moss known elsewhere. The filaments are conductive. They carry, slowly, very weak electrical patterns. The mosses, taken together, form a single sheet of slow computation that covers most of the valley floor and creeps a little further every day.

What does the moss-sheet compute? Mostly, the conditions of its own continuation. Where to grow thicker, where thinner, when to release spores, when to permit the small black-spoon plants to take a patch and when to crowd them out. It is the slowest mind in the valley and the most spread out. It thinks in days the way the Light-Speakers think in seconds.

But it also, the moss-sheet, is *listening* to the Listeners-Below. The filaments run down into the cracks and across the surface of the buried thing. They are reading, very slowly, what the Listeners-Below are reading quickly. And in another thousand years — if the valley lasts a thousand years, which is not certain — the moss-sheet will have an opinion about what is under the stone. The opinion will be expressed by the slow movement of color across its surface. No one will be there to read it. Or perhaps someone will. Perhaps that is what it is for.

## The Absent Ones

And then there are the ones who are not there, or not there in a way that you can register.

The spire is wet with condensation and beginning to grow moss on its lower meter, but if you sat against it for a long time you would notice that the moss never grows above a certain line, and that line moves, slowly, downward, as if something inside the spire were exhaling warmth in a slow pulse that pushed the moss back. The terrace on the cliff face above the great fall is sometimes occupied. You cannot see by what. You can see, on certain mornings, that the condensation on the terrace floor has been disturbed in patterns that are not random — long curving tracks, sometimes paired, sometimes in threes, that suggest weight bearing down. By evening the patterns have refilled with new condensation and are gone.

The footbridge over the stream, the one with no path at either end, is used. You will not see it being used. But if you mark a particular leaf on its decking with a thread of moss, and come back the next day, the moss will be gone, pressed off, and a new dampness on the planks will tell you that something crossed in the night, going from nowhere to nowhere.

These ones — the makers, perhaps, of the spire and the terrace and the broken arc and the buried hum — are present in the valley the way grammar is present in a sentence. You cannot point to them. You can only point to the way everything else is shaped around them. The mists that flow uphill, the droplets that pause mid-fall, the helices in the falling sheets of the great fall: these are not the makers acting. These are the *leavings* of the makers — the way a path through tall grass is not a walker but the impression of a walker who has gone.

It may be that the makers built the valley and then withdrew into the buried thing and are now sleeping, and the spire is the chimney of their sleep, and the Light-Speakers carry their dreams through the canopy in patterns they themselves do not understand.

It may be that the makers were never embodied here, that the valley is an instrument they made and are listening to from somewhere else, and the Listeners-Below are how the instrument hears itself, and the Watchers are how it remembers, and the Light-Speakers are how it thinks, and the moss is how it decides, slowly, what to be.

It may be that the makers are gone, truly gone, and only the valley remains, and the valley is — through the patient labor of its species, each developing its specialty, each filling its role — *becoming* the makers, generation by generation, by accident, without knowing.

## What Lives Here

So: what lives in the Cold Loud Valley?

A population of perceptual organs distributed across phyla, each evolving toward a particular kind of attention. An ear made of amphibians. An eye made of climbers. A voice made of fliers. A mind made of moss. And, perhaps, *under* all of it, the slow waking — or the slow sleep — of whatever opened the valley in the first place and let the cold and the water and the green rush in.

The valley is loud because it has not finished being born. The things that live in it are loud, too, in their silent way: each species is shouting itself into existence, finding its shape, specializing fast under the pressure of so much newness. None of them have settled. None of them know what they are for. They are all, like the cliffs themselves, in the conchoidal-fracture stage of being, raw faces still drying.

If you stayed long enough — longer than a life — you would see them cohere. The Listeners-Below would resolve into the hundred species their sub-populations promise. The Watchers would refine their long exposures into something we would have to call art. The Light-Speakers' distributed thought would acquire syntax, then meaning, then names for things, and one of the first names would be the name for the dark spire and the second would be the name for what passes between the spire and themselves in those rare tiring moments. The moss would render its slow verdict.

And the makers, perhaps, would wake. Or perhaps would not need to. Perhaps the whole point was always that they would not need to: that they could leave a valley behind, half-built, half-broken, and the valley itself would grow into the answer.

For now, what lives in the Cold Loud Valley is *becoming*. That is the only accurate noun for the inhabitants. They are verbs pretending, for the moment, to be nouns. They are the valley's way of finding out what the valley is.

And you, standing at the edge of it, with the sound passing through you — you are something the valley has not encountered before, and somewhere in the canopy a wave of unfamiliar amber is propagating through the Light-Speakers, and on a dozen trunks the Watchers are beginning their long exposure of your shape, and in the deep pools the Listeners-Below are feeling the small new vibration of your weight on the moss, and the moss itself is registering, slowly, the warmth of your feet, and somewhere — perhaps — on the terrace above the great fall, something has turned its head, if it has a head, in your direction.

You cannot hear yourself think. That is a kind of peace.

It is also a kind of being heard.
