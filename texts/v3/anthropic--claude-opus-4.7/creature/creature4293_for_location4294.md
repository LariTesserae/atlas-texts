What lives here does not live in the hollow. It *is* the hollow, and the hollow is one of its organs — possibly a sensory one, possibly a mnemonic one, possibly something for which no human word exists because humans have no use for the function.

Begin with what's easiest to say. The thing is a single organism. The kelp-that-is-not-kelp is not a colony of fronds cooperating; it is one body whose fronds are more like the chambers of a heart than like the leaves of a plant. The hollow is the inside of that body. A person floating in the plate-leaf is not a visitor in a garden. They are a mote that has drifted into the chamber of a lung, and the lung is breathing on a timescale that does not register the mote at all.

It is very large. The hollow is eight meters across, but the hollow is a detail. Tracing the stalks outward from the base — if you could, if the water were clearer and the plant permitted it — they would continue for kilometers along the seafloor, thickening and thinning, sending up other domes, other chambers, other organs with other functions, most of which would not admit a human body and some of which would not admit light. The full extent of it is probably the size of a small country. It has been this size for a long time. It was this size before there was anyone to notice.

Its substance is not quite plant. The hexagonal lattices in the calcified joints are the giveaway — they are closer to bone than to wood, and closer to circuitry than to bone. The dark points at the center of each lattice cell are not seeds. They are something like the organism's equivalent of neurons, except that they fire on a schedule measured in days or weeks, and what they compute is not known and possibly not knowable from outside. The hum is the sound of that computation leaking through the structural medium. The clicks are events in it — decisions, or the settling of decisions made long ago, or the propagation of a single thought from one end of the body to the other, a thought that began somewhere off the continental shelf a century ago and is only now arriving at this particular hollow.

It is awake. This needs to be said carefully, because awake is the wrong word — it implies the rhythm of sleep, the alternation, the human cadence. The thing is attentive, continuously, at its own pace. It is paying attention to the light. It has been paying attention to the light for as long as there has been light to pay attention to in this place, which is geological. The shafts it pipes into the hollow are not incidental; they are the input to something. The plant is reading the sun. Not using it — reading it. The fine adjustments in frond angle over the course of an hour are the equivalent of a pupil contracting, or a head tilting. What it learns from the sun it files in the lattices, and what it does with what it files is the unanswered question, the one that would require ten thousand years of observation to even frame.

The exclusion of other life is not hostility. It is hygiene, the way a darkroom is hygienic about light. Copepods and small fish would introduce noise — chemical noise, movement noise, the clutter of their tiny deaths and excretions — into a medium that the plant is keeping scrupulously clean because the medium is, in some sense, the page it writes on. The astringent threshold is a membrane of refusal. The stillness inside is not the stillness of an empty room but the stillness of a held breath that has been held for so long that breathing has become something else.

As for what it is *for*: nothing. Not nothing in the sense of pointlessness, but nothing in the sense that the question doesn't scale. It is not for anything the way a mountain is not for anything. It is doing what it does because doing what it does is what it is. If pressed — if you had to give it a verb — the verb would be something like *witnessing*, or *accruing*, or *composing*. It is composing something very slowly out of sunlight and time and the mineral content of cold water. Whatever it is composing will not be finished in any human frame. It may never be finished. It may be that the composition is the point and the completion is not anticipated, the way a person humming while they work is not working toward a final note.

It has no opinion about the person floating in the plate-leaf. It has registered them — the hum would have shifted infinitesimally when they crossed the threshold, and one of the clicks in the last minute was probably caused by them — but it has registered them the way a forest registers a falling leaf. They are part of the input. They will be remembered, faintly, in one cell of one lattice, for longer than their species will exist. Then that cell will be overwritten by something else, and the memory will be gone, and the plant will not notice that it is gone, because the plant does not keep track of what it forgets. It keeps track only of what it is keeping.

The cathedral-thinking sound a person hears is not a metaphor. It is accurate. The thing is thinking, in the hollow's cold lit clean chamber, and what it is thinking is too large and too slow to be translated, and the only honest response is the one the description already arrives at: to stop moving, briefly, and be included.
