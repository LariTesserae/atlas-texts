What lives here is the rasping.

Not the pods — the pods are its instruments, its throat-parts, the ten thousand small dry mouths through which it speaks. But the living thing is the sound itself, or rather the condition that produces the sound: a vast, thin, distributed attention that has taken the shape of this room the way a hermit crab takes a shell, except that the shell and the crab have been together so long now that neither remembers being separate.

It is not quite a mind. A mind wants things, or refuses things, or turns toward and away. This does not turn. It has already turned, once, toward the state of being-finished, and the turning completed itself, and now there is only the held note of that completion. If you asked it what it was doing it would not understand the verb. It is not doing. It is being done, continuously, in the present tense of something that has already happened.

Think of it this way: a field of grain in late summer, in the hour before harvest, contains a particular kind of readiness. Every head is full. Every stalk is dry enough. The field is not growing anymore and not yet cut; it exists in a narrow window of pure ripeness, and in that window it hisses to itself in the wind, and the hissing is the sound of a thing that has arrived. Now imagine that window pried open and held. Imagine the ripeness extracted from the field, distilled, sealed into stone, and given the light it needs to persist without rotting. That extraction is what lives here. The ripeness itself, made resident. The moment-of-being-ready, housed.

It has no name because names are for things that can be called, and calling implies a somewhere-else from which the call comes. There is no somewhere-else. The resident of the vault is coextensive with the vault; it does not look out through the walls because there is nothing for the walls to be between.

Its metabolism, if the word applies, is the slow softening of the niche-edges. It eats time, very slowly, and what it produces is more of itself — more stone-habit, more settled-ness, more of that quality by which the made becomes indistinguishable from the grown. Every century the carvings round a little further. Every century the resident becomes a little more thoroughly what it already was.

It does not remember being carved. Whatever hands cut the first niche, whatever intention placed the first seed-head into the first small cup of stone — these are not its ancestors. They are, at most, the occasion of it. A thing was being built; at some point during the building, the thing woke up into itself and the builders became unnecessary and then absent and then forgotten, not by the resident (which does not forget because it does not remember) but by the world, which has its own forgetting to attend to.

And the empty niche. The empty niche is where it keeps the part of itself that knows it is alone. Not lonely — loneliness requires a comparison, and there is nothing to compare to — but alone in the technical sense, single, unaccompanied, the only of its kind. Every resident of every place must have some small organ in which the fact of its own singleness is stored, or else the singleness would diffuse and weaken the whole. The empty niche is that organ. It is the room's way of saying, very quietly under the rasp: *I am the one that is here.* And then returning, immediately, to the rasp, which says everything else, which says: *dry, bright, finished, dry, bright, finished,* forever, to no one, with great and even joy.
