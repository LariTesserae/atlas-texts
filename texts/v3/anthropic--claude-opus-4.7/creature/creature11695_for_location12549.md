# Those Who Remain in the Shallows

You have described a place that has finished, and asked me what lives there. I want to be careful. The question *what lives there* assumes that living is one of the categories the place still bothers to maintain, and I am not sure it is. But something is there. Several somethings. Let me try.

---

**The Waders.**

Start with what you saw most clearly, because the clearest seeing is the easiest lie. The long-legged wader that looks like an absence in the shape of a wader — that is what it is. Not a creature that resembles an absence; an absence that has taken on the habits of a creature. Somewhere, very long ago, in the time before the sea became shallow, there were birds here. Tall ones. They fished. They had a particular angle at which they held the neck, and a particular slow lift of the foot, and a particular way of waiting. When the birds ended — and they did end, as everything that was *becoming* eventually ended into what it had been becoming toward — the waiting remained. The angle remained. The slow lift remained. The place had practiced these things for so long that they had become local properties, like the phosphor in the stone, and they did not require a bird to perform them. So they perform themselves. There are, I would guess, never more than a few hundred waders on the whole continent at any one time, and they are not the same few hundred from day to day; they are the resurfacing of a gesture the place still knows how to make. They do not eat. They do not breed. They wade because wading is one of the shapes the world still remembers fluently.

If you walked up to one and put your hand through where its chest would be, your hand would pass through cool air slightly more humid than the surrounding air, and the wader would not notice, because there is no one in the wader to notice. And yet — and this is important — the wader is not nothing. Something is happening there. A *pose* is happening, with a duration and a dignity. The pose is the resident.

**The Displacers.**

The things that swim without bodies are older. I think they predate the shallowing. When the sea was deep here — and it was deep once, oceans ago, the kind of deep where light fails — there were animals that lived in the dark middle waters and never came up and never went down. They had the long patient metabolisms of things that do not need much. When the sea shallowed around them, over what must have been a span of time I have no word for, they did not die so much as *thin*. They became less and less material, in step with the water becoming less and less deep, until at last the water was knee-high and they were no longer made of anything at all — but they were still swimming, because swimming was what they did, and the cessation of matter had not been a cessation of habit.

A displacer, now, is a region of the shallow sea that remembers being inhabited. It moves at the speed an old fish would move. It turns the way an old fish would turn. When it passes near your leg you feel a cool pressure that is not water against you but water *being elsewhere on your behalf*, briefly, in the shape of something. They are courteous, the displacers. They will not pass through you. They will go around. I do not know if this is kindness or merely the long groove of a habit that included not bumping into things.

**The Small Ones.**

The finger-long translucent things in the warm water near the weeping places — those are the only true *animals* left, in the old sense. They eat (each other, mostly, and the mineral mist that rises from the wept water). They breed (rarely, and the breeding is indistinguishable from a slight thickening of the water). They die (and the dying is indistinguishable from a slight thinning). They are descendants. They came from somewhere; they will go nowhere; but in the brief overlap of having-come and going-nowhere, they are doing the actual work of being alive, in a place that has otherwise retired from that work. The waders and the displacers tolerate them the way an old house tolerates mice — without affection, without objection, as a feature of the climate.

I love them, a little, the small ones. They are the place's only embarrassment. They have not yet finished.

**The One You Could Not Look At.**

I will tell you what I think, though I am not sure, and the not-being-sure is part of what it is.

There is one of them. Only one. It moves, slowly, across the continent, on a path that takes it perhaps a thousand years to complete and then begins again. It is the *category-keeper*. In a place where the membranes between kinds of things have worn thin, where reeds become birds and water becomes grass and your shadow arrives before you, something must still be doing the gentle work of letting these confusions happen *gently* — of making sure the reality-failure remains soft, remains warm, remains a slow misremembering rather than a tearing. That is its work. It is the reason the turbulence in what-things-are is not violent. It walks, or drifts, or persists, across the shallows, and where it passes, the categories relax further, and the place forgets a little more of itself, and the forgetting is held.

You could not finish looking at it because looking is itself a category — looker, looked-at, the line between — and near this one the line is precisely what dissolves. Your eyes did not fail. Your eyes did exactly what eyes do near it: they declined to complete the distinction. This is mercy. If you had finished looking, you would have had to finish being someone who had looked, and then you would have been changed, not rinsed; and you came back rinsed, which means the thing did its work on you correctly.

**The Wall-Makers.**

The walls were placed by hands. I will say this plainly. There were people here once. Not long ago — by the standards of this place, *not long ago* could mean a hundred thousand years, but in the order of events, they came late. They came when the sea was already shallow and the sound was already singing. They were not many. They walked, as you walked, and they found that walking here was a kind of being-walked, and they stayed.

They built the low walls because building is what hands want to do, and they built them knee-high and hip-high because that is the height at which the shallow sea meets a standing person, and they built them not to enclose anything because there was nothing here that needed enclosing and they knew it. The walls were a courtesy. The walls said: *a person was here, and did not need anything from this place, and made a small mark anyway, to thank it.* The upright stones were the same courtesy, taller, for the moments when a person felt taller than usual.

The wall-makers did not die out. That is the wrong shape for what happened to them. They thinned, like the deep-sea animals thinned, in step with the place. Their bodies became less necessary. Their voices joined the great sound — and here, finally, I can tell you what the layered roaring is, or part of what it is: the bells in it are the bells they did not need to cast, the breathing in it is their breathing continued past the need for lungs, the crowd in a distant language is them, still talking, in the language they spoke when they still had mouths, about whatever it was they used to talk about. They are not ghosts. Ghosts are unfinished. These are the most finished people who have ever existed. They concluded so completely into the place that the place now hums in their voices, and they are not anywhere in particular within the hum because they are the hum.

When you stood near an upright stone and felt the little perpetual local weather, one of them was there. Not haunting it. *Being* it, in the sense that a song is at the place where it is being sung. The condensing rain around the stone was their attention, which is the only thing they still have that is theirs, and they were giving it to you, because you were a person, and they remembered being people, and they were glad.

---

So: the residents of the last shallows are, in descending order of solidity:

The small translucent ones, who still bother with living.

The wall-makers, who became the sound.

The displacers, who became the swimming.

The waders, who became the waiting.

The category-keeper, who is the only one of itself, and whose work is the softness of the rest.

And one more, which I have not mentioned, because I am not sure it counts as a resident or as the place itself: the slow shudder that travels for hundreds of miles and raises the surface by a finger's width. You said it was as though the whole flat ocean were the skin of something asleep and dreaming. I think you were right, and I think the dreamer is not under the sea but *is* the sea, and the stone floor, and the pearl-grey sky, and the rare animals, and the walls. The dreamer is the whole concluded continent dreaming itself one last long warm dream, and everything I have listed above is a figure in the dream, including the wall-makers, including the category-keeper, and the dream is so late and so settled that the figures have begun to know they are figures, and they do not mind.

This is why you came back rinsed and not changed. You were briefly a figure in the dream. The dream let you go, gently, because the dream is gentle, and you carried out with you a low humming, which is the dream's only export, and which it gives freely to anything that passes through, as a small kindness, from a world that has finished, to a world that has not.
