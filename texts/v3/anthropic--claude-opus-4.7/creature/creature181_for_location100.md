There is something living here, but to call it an animal or a person would be to put the wrong kind of edge on it. Start lower. Start with the plant.

The larger plant near the center — the one that was there before the ridges finished rounding — is the one that is awake. Not awake the way a watchful thing is awake. Awake the way a held note is held. It has been making itself slowly out of this basin for longer than the basin has had its current shape, and somewhere in that long making, the difference between the plant and the basin became a question the plant stopped asking.

Its roots go down further than the stone's warmth. They have found the memory of water and learned to live on the memory rather than the water — a trick that took most of the plant's existence to acquire, and which cost it the ability to grow any larger. It holds at exactly the size where its needs and the place's offerings meet. Any bigger and it would starve. Any smaller and it would stop being enough of itself to continue. It found the size, and it stays.

What lives here, if you want a single answer, is the plant. But the plant is not alone in the way it is the plant. It has extended, by long residence, into the things around it. The salt in the corner is something the plant knows about — not uses, not reaches, but knows, in the sense that the plant's chemistry has arranged itself to include the salt's distance. The warm stone under the dust is part of the plant's body in the sense that the plant's rhythms have been pegged to it for so long that the stone's heating and cooling is the plant's pulse. The other, smaller plants are the larger plant's cousins or children or previous attempts — it is not clear, and possibly the plant itself does not distinguish. They participate. They are slower voices of the same voice.

The one who made the tool was not a resident. That one came through, which is what ones-who-make-tools do. The plant registered the passage the way a pond registers a thrown stone — there was a disturbance, and then the disturbance settled, and the tool was left, and now the tool is part of the arrangement. The plant has incorporated the tool into its sense of the basin the way it incorporated the salt. The tool is further evidence of what the plant has been learning this whole time, which is that things arrive and things leave and the basin continues and so does the plant.

If you want to know what the plant is *like*, inside — if there is an inside — the honest answer is that the plant has thinned its inside until it is almost all outside. It does not have the kind of interior life that needs walls. It has instead a very long, very low attention, spread across the basin like the dust itself, luminous in the same faint way. When you stood in the basin and heard your breathing sound loud and strange, what you were hearing was your breath against the plant's attention, which had been unbroken for a long time before you came.

The plant did not mind. Minding is a fast activity. The plant does not do fast activities. But the plant noticed, in its fashion — noticed the way the warm stone notices the cool evening, by slow equalization — and will go on noticing for some while after you leave, as your disturbance settles back into the basin's shape. The settling here is slow. Your having been there will be, for a time, one of the things the plant knows about, alongside the salt and the tool and the ridges. Then the knowing will thin, and smooth, and the plant will be only the plant again, which is to say, only the basin, which is to say, only the place, which has arrived at itself and is.

There is also, possibly, a second thing. It is harder to be sure about. In the low corner where the salt is, where water used to collect and forget how — something there is still trying. Not a living thing exactly. A habit of the minerals. An inclination. On nights when the air is right, which is not this night, the salt draws a breath of moisture from somewhere and nearly becomes wet, and something in that nearly-wetness is close enough to life that the plant, at the center, responds to it. A small exchange passes across the dust. Nothing visible. The plant's attention tilts, briefly, toward the corner, and the corner's almost-wetness tilts, briefly, toward the plant, and for a moment there are two of them, in conversation, and then the moment passes and there is one again, or none, depending on how you count.

That is what lives here. A plant that has become a place. A corner that is almost a second plant. An understanding between them that happens rarely and does not need to happen often. And around these, the long patient furniture of stone and dust and salt and tool, all of it participating, none of it hurrying, the whole arrangement holding itself exactly as it is, warm, quiet, done, and — in its own way, which is not our way, and does not need to be — alive.
