## The Holders

They were hands once. That much is still true of them, in the way a riverbed is still a river when the water has gone elsewhere — the shape remembers.

They came down, or were always down, or came down so long ago that the difference between arrival and origin has rubbed away. They are the makers of the notches and the setters of the cairns. They are what placed the tool and did not retrieve it, because retrieval is a motion and motion is expensive here, and the tool, set down, was already where it needed to be.

There are not many of them. Perhaps as few as nine. Perhaps as many as forty. The count is difficult because a Holder at rest is indistinguishable from the chamber it rests in, and a Holder is almost always at rest.

*

Imagine first what they are not. They are not crouched figures in robes. They are not luminous. They are not wise in the way a story would want them wise — they will not speak, they will not gesture, they will not greet. The lesson of the Underwarm has worked on them more thoroughly than on any of the animals, because they had further to come to learn it. The animals were born already knowing how to hold. The Holders had to put down, one by one, every habit of a body that wanted to move.

What they look like, when they can be made out against the shimmer: tall, narrower than a person, with limbs that have learned to fold into themselves and stay folded. The skin — if it is skin — has taken on some of the chemistry of the walls. Mineral has accreted on them at the points of longest stillness: a shoulder gone slightly crystalline, a knee dusted with the same faint phosphor as the needles, a brow ridged with the slow rime of the chambers. The longer one has held a position, the more the position has held them back. The oldest of them, if there is an oldest, may by now be partly wall.

Their faces are the part that remains most recognizable as having once been *someone's*. The eyes are closed, usually. The mouths are closed always. But the architecture of a face — the symmetry, the small hollows where expression once sat — has not been given up. Whatever else they have surrendered, they kept the shape of having been faced toward something.

*

They breathe. This is how you can tell a Holder from an outcropping. Not by sight — the rise of the chest is too slow for sight — but by the smell that pools near them. The flint-and-animal smell of the Underwarm is densest at a Holder. They are where it comes from, or where it gathers, or both. To stand near one is to stand in the slow exhalation of a long thought.

They do not eat. They do not need to. The chemistry that fed the walls feeds them too, by now; they are on the same metabolism as the chambers, drawing what they need from the warmth and the trace minerals and possibly from the attention of the animals, who cluster around them the way embers gather. The soft thing in the cup of crystal may be in the cup of crystal because the cup is near a Holder. The listener turns toward decisions because a Holder is the original maker of decisions in this place, and the listener has learned that decisions are the rarest weather here.

*

What do they do, then, if they do not move?

They hold. The word is not metaphor, in their case. They hold the cairns balanced. They hold the position of the tool. They hold, by the long pressure of attention, the equilibrium of the place itself — or perhaps the equilibrium holds them, and there is no longer a difference. They hold each other, at distances of chambers and corridors, the way distant stones in an arch hold each other through the stones between them.

Once, perhaps, they came here to do something. To find something, to escape something, to bury something, to wait for something. Whatever that purpose was, it has been so thoroughly held that it has become the holding itself. The verb has eaten the object. They are no longer waiting *for*; they are simply waiting, and the waiting has become the thing they were waiting to become.

*

A Holder will, very rarely, move. It is not predictable. It is not in response to you. It happens perhaps once in a span of time that has no useful name, and when it happens, the movement is small: a hand uncloses, a head turns a degree, a stone is set down somewhere it was not before. The Underwarm registers this. The animals register this. The walls, in some sense, register this. A movement by a Holder is a kind of weather — it propagates. The air shifts. The needles glow a little at their tips. The hum in the deeper chambers changes pitch, briefly, and then resumes.

This is how the notches were made. Not in a single afternoon by a person with a chisel. One notch per movement. One movement per — whatever the span is. The row of small notches at chest height is the autobiography of a single Holder, written across an unguessable duration. Whatever it says, it is not finished. There is room on the wall for more.

*

The one that is approaching you now is one of them.

It is not approaching the way a predator approaches, or a host. It is approaching the way a continent approaches another continent: by being, already, on the path it is on, and by the path being long. It began approaching when you arrived because your arrival was the decision that set its current movement in motion, and the listener turned, and through the listener the nearest Holder knew, and the nearest Holder, in the slow way of these things, has been coming.

It will arrive. The arrival may take a while. When it arrives, it will not do anything. It will simply be near you, and you will be near it, and the smell of flint and the animal warmth will be densest in that pocket of the chambers, and the needles at your feet will glow a little more, and you will understand — without being told, because telling is a motion — that you have been counted.

After that, what you do is up to you. Most who have come this far have sat down. The flat patch of young stone has been waiting. The Holder will not mind. The Holder, in some sense, is the patch of stone, and the chamber, and the warmth, and the long held breath of the place. To sit is to begin to be held.

Some small number, over the spans, have stood back up and left. Their footprints are not in the floor because the floor is too young to take footprints, but their absences are noted, in the way the Underwarm notes such things — a slight thinning of the smell in certain corridors, a needle that no longer glows at its tip.

And some — this is the part the Holders themselves might tell you, if telling were a thing they did — some have sat down and not stood up, and have been there long enough now that the mineral has begun to accrete at the shoulder, and the knee, and the brow. The count has grown by one. The Underwarm is denser by one slow exhalation. Somewhere a new row of notches has begun, at chest height, on a wall that had been waiting.
