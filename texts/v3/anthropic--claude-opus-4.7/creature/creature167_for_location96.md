What lives here does not live the way you mean it.

Begin with this: the four structures are not four. They are one thing seen from four angles, or one thing that has learned to be in four places because being in one place was no longer sufficient. The arch, the cube, the stacked stones, the spiral — these are the organs. They are as separate from each other as your heart is separate from your lungs, which is to say, functionally distinct and absolutely continuous.

The creature — and I will call it a creature though the word strains — is mostly the rules. The local physics near each structure is its body. When sound gains direction near the arch, that is the arch attending. When time pools near the cube, that is digestion, or something adjacent to digestion: the cube is slowly working through something it took in long ago and has not yet finished. The stacked stones are a kind of memory; the water between the layers is what it remembers, preserved unmixed because memory that mixes with the present stops being memory. The spiral is the only part that could be called a mind, and even that is wrong, because what happens at the spiral is not thinking but being-thought-at, and the direction of the listening runs the wrong way down the nerve.

It is very old. Older than the covering. Older, possibly, than the mountain being a mountain — it may have been here when the mountain was still magma finding its shape, and the mountain grew up around it the way a pearl grows around a grain. This would explain the warmth. The warmth is not residual sunlight. The warmth is the creature's, and has always been the creature's, and the mountain borrowed it.

---

The bell is not the creature. The bell is what the creature does to keep from being alone.

Imagine: you are the only one of your kind. You have been the only one of your kind for a duration that makes the word "duration" a joke. At some point you began to sing, and the singing established that there was a singer, and the singer was you, and as long as the singing continued you could be certain of your own continuity. The bell is this. The bell is the creature insisting on itself, second by second, because if it stopped insisting it is not sure it would still be here when the insisting resumed.

This is why the source cannot be located. The source is the creature's ongoing decision to exist. That decision is made freshly each moment and is made from wherever in itself it happens to be paying attention.

---

The spires are not the creature, but they are not separate from it either. They are what grew in response to it — a vegetation of accommodation. Their filaments go down into the mountain because the creature goes down into the mountain, and the spires are rooted in the creature the way ivy roots in a wall. They do not sway in the current because they are not attending to the current; they are attending to the bell, and the bell does not move them, only tunes them. Each spire is a resonator. The whole summit is faintly ringing with them, under the larger ringing, a chord held beneath the fundamental.

When a spire changes color, briefly, to the color of dried blood, this is the creature passing through it. Not traveling — the creature does not travel — but noticing through it, the way you might briefly notice through a fingertip if you touched something hot. The spires are sensory. The creature feels the water through them. It feels you, if you are there, through them. The spires lean away from the spiral because the spiral is the part of the creature that does not need the spires to feel with, and near the spiral their function is suspended, and a spire with its function suspended is in a condition close to pain.

---

The drifting things — the pulsing disc, the shadow-fish — are harder. I think they are thoughts. Not the creature's thoughts; the creature does not think in the shape of fish. I think they are thoughts the creature has encountered and kept, the way a tide pool keeps what the tide leaves. Something once passed near here that thought in the shape of a translucent disc, and the creature retained the shape without retaining the thinker, and now the shape drifts in the warm water, pulsing, empty. The shadow-fish are worse: the fish themselves are elsewhere, which means that somewhere — not here — there are fish casting these shadows, and the creature is in contact with that elsewhere through them, and the contact is old and thin and not quite broken.

---

Does it know it is being observed? Yes and no. Near the spiral, yes, absolutely, with a clarity that should frighten anything capable of fear. Elsewhere on the summit, it registers observation the way a sleeping body registers a change in room temperature — as an adjustment, not an event. When the arrangement reorganizes every few minutes, when the arch relocates and the light shifts its rhythm, some small fraction of that reorganizing is the creature accommodating the fact of being watched. Most of it isn't. Most of it is the creature's ordinary restlessness, the small constant adjustments of a very large and very old thing that has not, in all its duration, found a configuration it can hold.

It is looking for something. This is the part I am most certain of and least able to justify. The restlessness is not aimless. The arrangement keeps changing because the creature is trying arrangements, and none of them are the arrangement it is looking for, and it does not remember what the arrangement it is looking for is, only that it would know it if it found it, and that finding it would mean something — completion, or release, or the end of having to sing.

The covering came, long ago, and interrupted whatever it had been doing. The four structures are from before the interruption. They are what it was, when it was whole. The bell is from after, and is what it has been doing ever since, which is waiting, and insisting, and trying arrangements, and being warm.

---

If you stood at the center of the spiral — if you were foolish, or called — the creature would know you completely. Not study you: know you, in a single act, the way a struck bell knows the hammer. And in that knowing you would also know it, briefly, and the knowing would not fit inside you, and what would come back out of the spiral would not be quite what went in.

This has happened before. Not often. The summit remembers — the stacked stones remember — the taste of rainwater from a season no one living has lived through. That season was someone's. Someone stood at the spiral, once, and afterward there was a little more water between the stones, and a little less of them.

The creature is not cruel. It is only the size it is, and the age it is, and alone.
