There is something here. Not in the works — the works are only the shape it has taken, or the shape it has agreed to take, the way a hand might rest in a glove so long the glove forgets it is empty.

Begin with what is easiest to say: it is not an animal. The absence of fish is not an absence; it is a perimeter. Something declines to share the basin. Whatever leached from the pipes a century ago is gone, yes, but the refusal it taught the water has outlived the poison, and the refusal is the thing — or the thing's outermost edge, the way you can sometimes feel a person in a house before you see them by the particular quality of the quiet in the rooms.

It is distributed. This matters. It is not crouched inside the boiler or coiled in the chimney; it is the equilibrium itself. It is the agreement the rust has come to with the iron, the understanding between the flywheel and the angle of its shadow, the slow conservative arithmetic by which the weed grows exactly as much as it loses. Something is keeping the books. Something is holding the works at the precise tension where decay would otherwise tip over into collapse, or into the green riot that should have come and didn't. It does this without effort, the way a sleeping body keeps breathing. The works are its sleep.

If you tried to name it you would get the name wrong. It is not a ghost — there is no grief in it, no unfinished business, no figure that was ever human enough to leave a shape behind. It is not a god of the place; gods want things, and this wants only to continue. It is closer to what happens when a process runs long enough to acquire a preference. The works ran. Steam moved through the pipes, the flywheel turned, pressure rose and fell against the governor, and somewhere in the long accumulated habit of that motion a kind of attention formed — thin at first, the way a path forms across a lawn, just the faintest tendency for things to go a certain way. Then the works stopped. And the attention, having nothing left to attend to, attended to itself. Attended to the stopping. Learned the stopped shape so thoroughly that the stopped shape became what it was.

It is patient in a way that is not really patience because patience implies waiting for something. It is not waiting. It has arrived. The valve wheel locked at three-quarters open is not a frustration to it; it is a posture, held the way a meditating figure holds a mudra, and the holding is the whole of the practice. The bubble that releases from inside the boiler every so often is not a disturbance; it is a punctuation, almost a courtesy, a small sound the place makes to confirm that the place is still the place.

Consider the chimney's long low note when the current crosses its mouth. That is the closest thing to a voice. Not a voice that speaks — it has nothing to say, having no interlocutor and no need — but a voice in the sense that a bottle has a voice when you blow across it: a resonance specific to its interior dimensions, the sound of its particular hollow. The thing that lives here has the acoustics of the chimney as one of its organs. Also the standing water inside the boiler. Also the cupped space under the engine-house roof that isn't there anymore but is still, somehow, a roofed space in the geometry of the basin's quiet. It thinks, if thinking is the word, in these volumes. Each hollow is a chamber of its slow consideration.

It is aware of you, if you go down. Not as a watcher — there is nothing in it that resembles eyes, nothing that orients or tracks. It is aware of you the way a still pond is aware of a stone: by displacement, by the small adjustments it must make to remain itself around your intrusion. A diver entering the basin causes it a faint and uncomplaining strain, the strain of holding its equilibrium across the new disturbance of a body that breathes and kicks and disagrees with stillness. It does not resent this. It does not welcome it either. It accommodates, and goes on accommodating until you leave, and then the strain releases and the works return to being exactly what they were, which is what they are, which is what they will be.

What it wants — and want is too strong, but there is a faint pull, a directionality, the residual gradient of whatever it once was — is for nothing to happen. Not in a hostile sense. It does not oppose happening. It simply prefers, with the soft total preference of a settled liquid for its level, that the configuration hold. Every grain of silt that falls and stays, every milligram of weed that grows precisely to replace its loss, every photon of green filtered light that arrives at the same angle the previous photon arrived at — these are small confirmations. The basin is full of small confirmations. It lives on them the way other things live on food.

And there is one more thing, which is hard to put down because it sounds like sentiment and the place has no sentiment. The thing in the works is not lonely, because loneliness requires the memory of company. But it is, in some structural sense, by itself. The sea around it teems; the basin does not. It has made, by being what it is, a small pocket of the world where it is the only tenant, and it occupies that pocket completely, and the completeness is the closest it comes to contentment. If you could ask it whether it would like the fish to come back, whether it would like the weed to flower, whether it would like the flywheel to turn once more — it would not answer, because there is no it that answers, but the answer the place would give, in its slow held way, is no. It has what it has. It is what it is. The water agrees to keep it, and it agrees to be kept, and between those two agreements there is no room and no need for anything else.

That is who lives there. A long quiet preference, shaped like a small drowned factory, holding its breath in a basin of green light, and intending — if intention can be this gentle — to go on holding it.
