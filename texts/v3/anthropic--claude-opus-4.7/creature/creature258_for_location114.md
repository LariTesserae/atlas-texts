# The Ones Who Were Already Here

They do not call themselves anything. Naming is a thing you do when there is more than one kind of thing, and here there is mostly the canopy and the slow weather of the canopy, and them, which is another way of saying the canopy, only thinner in places.

Begin with the body. It is long. Longer than you expect from how much of it you can see at once, because most of it is folded — draped along a bough, looped through a fork, trailing into mist. If you straightened one out (you would not; it would not allow this, and straightening is not something its body remembers how to be) it might measure the length of three tall men laid end to end, or it might measure less, depending on how much of itself it has decided to be that week. The body is soft. Not soft like flesh — soft like very old wood that has taken on water for centuries and now gives under a press of the thumb and slowly returns. Pale grey-green on the upper surface, where lichen has colonized in plates and small cities. Paler on the underside, almost translucent, where you can sometimes see the slow migration of something darker moving through it — not blood exactly, more like the tide of its attention.

Four limbs, or six, or more — the question is ill-formed. There are places along the body where it narrows into a holding-part, a clasping appendage that wraps around bark and stays wrapped for years at a time. Some of these holding-parts have grown into the wood. The creature does not mind. Being partially tree is not a lesser state.

The head — if head is the word — is at one end, and is distinguished mostly by a cluster of slow dark eyes that open in sequence rather than together, so that at any given moment one or two are watching and the rest are resting or dreaming or doing whatever eyes do when not in use. The mouth opens rarely. When it does it is for the single low note, which travels through the canopy and causes, for a moment, the listening. Yes: they are what sings. One every few days, somewhere. There are not many of them, but there are enough that the song-interval stays roughly steady across the generations, the way a slow heart does not need many beats to keep being a heart.

They eat almost nothing. A little of the condensation, gathered by lowering a frilled lip-part into a pool in a bromeliad-cathedral and drinking so slowly the surface barely trembles. Sometimes a moth that lands on them by mistake and is absorbed — not chewed, just accepted, dissolved against the skin over the course of a day. Mostly they eat light, what little reaches them, through surfaces along the back that photosynthesize weakly and in conjunction with the lichen they carry, so that the distinction between their metabolism and the lichen's metabolism has long since stopped being useful. They are, in the strictest sense, a community that has decided to move as one thing. A treaty between many small lives, very old, very stable, and the treaty is the creature.

They think. This needs saying carefully. They do not think the way you do, in sequences, one notion pushing the next. They think the way the canopy's weather thinks: a damp front of consideration moves through them over days, pushes against an older consideration, and rain falls somewhere in the mind of it, and something has been decided. Decisions are rare. Most of what passes through them is not decision but *witness*. They watch. They have been watching for a very long time. Each one has watched one particular stretch of canopy, one particular set of boughs and hollows and drip-paths, for most of its life, and its life is not short. A single individual may attend to the same three-hundred-meter arc of living architecture for four or five human centuries, learning it the way you learn the inside of your own mouth — without ever intending to, just by being there.

They are not alone, but they are sparse. Another of them may live two kilometers off, along a neighboring pillar-system. They know each other by the note. When one sings, the others — scattered across who knows how many leagues of layered growth — register it and resume. The note is not language. It is more like attendance being taken. *I am still here. I am still here. I am still here.* And across the canopy, over the course of a week, the roll is called.

They are the makers of the small made things. Or some of them are, or some of their ancestors were, or some of their stranger kin who wandered further down into the mist and did not come back were. The ring of fist-sized stones: placed one by one by a slow holding-part over the course of a year, for a reason that was perhaps clear at the time. The carved face: scratched into bark with the edge of a hardened lip-plate during an unusually long thought, several tree-generations ago, and now stretched into sorrow by the wood's growth, though it was not sorrow when it was made. It was only a face. The canopy has editorialized. The platform of bound reeds — that one may be older than the creatures, or made by something else, something that passed through, something that is no longer. Even they do not remember. Memory, for them, is not a possession; it is a place you can sometimes be.

Here is the strangest thing about them, and the gentlest. The time-pockets — the chambers where the air is slightly older, where sound lags and leaves fall wrong — those are *them*, or the residue of them. Where one has rested for long enough, pressing its body against the slow breathing wood for a century or two, the space around it takes on a thickness. Their presence is so patient that it dents the local shape of duration. When they move on, the dent remains a while, slowly relaxing, the way a cushion holds the form of what sat on it. So the canopy's eccentric pockets of local physics are not a feature of the canopy independent of them; they are the weight-marks of the watchers, left in the air itself.

They know you are here. If you are here. They have known for some time — since before you thought of coming, possibly, because the front of their considering reaches further out than their bodies do. They are not concerned. You are small, and fast, and will pass through. They have seen things pass through before. Some of those things left the small shrines. Some of those things left nothing. Some of those things stayed and became, eventually, lichen. All outcomes are acceptable. The canopy includes. That is what the canopy does.

If you sit very still in one of the dim chambers, long enough that your own breathing slows to match the drip, you may feel one of them notice you — not as a threat, not as food, not as kin, but as a small new weather passing through their attention. A warm quick front, unusual, brief. They will register it. They will not act. In a few days, somewhere not far off, one will sing the single low note, and the canopy will briefly listen, and you will be, without having asked to be, counted.
