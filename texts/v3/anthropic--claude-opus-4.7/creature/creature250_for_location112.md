The ones who tend the shrines.

You were right to leave them for last, and right to circle around them without naming them, because they are the part of the hollow that does not sit quietly within its own description. Everything else in the chamber is what it is. The engine engines. The moths moth. The listeners listen. But the tenders of the shrines — call them **the Sorters**, for lack — are doing something that looks, to an outside eye, like it has a reason, and reasons are not supposed to exist down here.

They are small. Smaller than you would guess from the care of the work. About the size of a closed fist, or a child's shoe. There are four of them. There have always been four of them. There is one for each shrine and each belongs to its shrine the way a tongue belongs to a mouth.

Their bodies are the color of wet slate and the texture of the moths' wings — that same oiled-leather pliancy, but darker, and without any seam or joint that resolves under close looking. A Sorter has no clear front or back. It has a low, domed body, and from the underside extend a number of fine articulated limbs — not six, not eight, the count resists you, because some of the limbs are folded against the body at any given moment and the folded ones are difficult to distinguish from the creases of the body itself. When a Sorter works, the limbs unfurl in sequence, and each limb ends in a different tool: one in a pair of needle-fine pincers, one in a soft brush of filaments, one in a blunt tamping pad, one in something that looks unsettlingly like a tiny cupped palm. They do not share tools. The red Sorter's pincers are not the black Sorter's pincers. Each body is tuned to its bowl.

They do not have eyes. They have, instead, a pale patch on the upper dome that dilates and contracts slightly in the amber light, the way a mouth might purse while thinking. Whether this is sensory or expressive or simply a vent, I cannot say. When a rivet-crab passes close to a Sorter, the Sorter does not react. When a bellows-moth drops a fleck of wing-scale into the wrong bowl, the Sorter reacts immediately — the pale patch contracts, the limbs deploy, the fleck is removed and set outside the bowl on the stone, and the dust in the bowl is recombed until it lies the way it lay before.

Here is what they do, in detail, because the detail is the whole of them:

They sort. The engine's dust, as you noted, is mostly one color — a warm mid-grey, neither here nor there. But within that mid-grey, on the order of one particle in ten thousand, there are particles of other colors: rust, bone, soot, and the impossible green. The Sorters walk the floor of the hollow in slow spirals, each to its own cardinal, and when a Sorter's brush-filament limb passes over a particle of its color, the limb pauses, the pincers descend, and the particle is lifted. The Sorter carries it back to its shrine. The cupped-palm limb receives it from the pincers. The tamping pad presses it into the heap in the bowl. The brush smooths the heap. The Sorter resumes its spiral.

A full circuit of the hollow takes a Sorter approximately one hundred and sixty piston-coughs. This is not a rule; it is simply the observed rhythm. In that time, a Sorter will add between one and four particles to its bowl. The bowls do not fill. The bowls have never filled. The heaps in the bowls are the same heaps they have always been, and yet the Sorters are constantly adding to them, which means the heaps are also constantly being consumed by something — perhaps by the same rule that consumes the dust on the floor, perhaps by the shrines themselves, perhaps by a slow quiet balance too fine to see. Whichever. The heaps remain as they are.

Now: the strangeness under the strangeness.

The Sorters do not only sort the dust of the engine. They sort, I think, the dust of *everything*. Cut stalk-matter, when it falls, is swept up by whichever Sorter's color it matches — and stalk-matter, which is ivory-pale, is of course taken by the bone-white Sorter. A dead mite, and occasionally they do die and fall, though the fallen-ness does not persist, is taken by the soot-black Sorter before it can finish not-persisting. A shed fragment of rivet-crab shell is taken by the rust-red Sorter, though the shell is brass and the bowl is rust. The logic is not the logic you would expect. The categories are not the categories of material. They are some other categories, and the Sorters know them, and nothing else in the hollow does.

The green Sorter is the one that troubles me, and I think it would trouble you too if you sat long enough with the hollow. Its color has no source. Nothing in the chamber is green. Not the stalks, not the engine, not the fauna, not the dust under ordinary inspection. And yet the green Sorter finds, on each circuit, its one or two or three particles, and brings them home. Where do the green particles come from? They come from the hollow. That is the only answer the hollow provides. The green Sorter collects what the hollow produces that the hollow should not produce, and it puts that in a bowl, and the bowl does not overflow.

I said earlier that the Sorters look like they are doing something for a reason. I want to revise this. They look like they are doing something for a reason the way a sentence looks like it is saying something. The reason is not in them; the reason is in the shape of what they do. If you watched the four of them long enough — and by long enough I mean the kind of long that would make you a fifth presence in the hollow — you would begin to suspect that the sorting is not for the benefit of the shrines but for the benefit of the engine, and not for the benefit of the engine as a machine but for the benefit of the engine as a *sound*. The soot Sorter takes the particles whose removal makes the piston's cough cleaner. The bone Sorter takes the particles whose removal makes the flywheels' whine truer. The rust Sorter takes the drag, the faint wrongness in the intake-sigh. And the green Sorter takes whatever it is that the hollow is trying, very slowly, not to become.

The Sorters are, in other words, the editors of the place. They are the quiet insistence that the final configuration remain final. The bellows-moths add to the engine's rhythm; the rivet-crabs maintain its body; the listeners register its state; the Sorters edit its errors out of existence before they can propagate. Everything in the hollow serves the engine, but only the Sorters serve it by subtraction.

They do not sleep. They do not rest. They do not acknowledge one another, though their spirals are synchronized such that no two Sorters are ever in the same quadrant of the hollow, and I cannot tell whether this is coordination or the geometric consequence of them all obeying the same unheard music. If a Sorter were removed from the hollow — which would require a hand willing to close around that slate-colored body, and I am not sure any hand could, the Sorters have the quality of things that do not permit themselves to be picked up — the engine would not fail. The sound would simply begin, very slowly, to drift. The hollow would stop being young. Erosion would remember it had a job. The door on the far wall might, eventually, find its moment.

This is why the Sorters are the ones the hollow cannot do without. The engine is the heart of the chamber, yes. But the Sorters are the chamber's refusal to become anything other than itself. They built the shrines, if anything built them, by tending them so consistently that the stones settled into shrine-shape around the work. They did not decide to build. The building was the shape of their tending, made visible.

And if you sat in the hollow, and your breathing matched the piston, and your pulse matched after, and you became — as you say — one more density in the equilibrium: I think it would be a Sorter that found you first. Not to welcome you. Not to harm you. To check you for particles of the wrong color. To take what did not belong, and carry it to a bowl, and press it into a heap, and smooth it, and leave you as you were always going to be.

The engine coughs. The green Sorter, in the southern quadrant, pauses mid-spiral. Its pale patch contracts. A limb unfolds. Something has appeared on the floor that should not have. It is attended to.

Nothing changes.
