# The Singers

They have no name for themselves because names are a thing you need when there is distance between one body and another, and here there is no such distance.

Begin with one of them, though "one" is already a lie. Take a single body, then, and hold it in your attention the way the basin never does. It fits in a cupped hand and weighs less than you expect — hollow-boned, or something stranger than hollow: bones grown thin and resonant, each long bone a pipe, each rib a tuned reed. When the body sings, the body *is* the song; the skeleton buzzes sympathetically with its own voice and with the voices of its neighbors, and a creature pressed against three others is, in a real sense, four creatures' worth of instrument.

The skin is that wet-leather mushroom-cap texture you noticed. Under a light that will never come, it would show no color worth the word — a sort of translucent grey-pink, veined, with a faint phosphor-nothing in the deeper tissue that does not glow but seems like it ought to. The belly is softer and paler. The back carries a fine pelt, not of fur but of something between fur and cilia: thousands of tiny hair-like projections that quiver to sound the way a pond's surface quivers to wind. They hear with their whole bodies. Everywhere is ear.

The face — if face — is mostly mouth and mostly ear-cavities. Where eyes would be, there are two small pits, vestigial, sealed under skin. Generations ago, perhaps, something saw in here. Nothing sees now. The snout is short, folded, intricate as a flower, and this is where the song lives: a set of resonating sinuses and membranes so elaborate that each creature has, in effect, a small pipe organ in its face. The jaw is delicate. They eat soft things — the pale fuzz on the walls, each other's molted skin, the slow-swimming blind invertebrates that live in the stone-bowl pools, the occasional drowned body of one of their own. The basin is a closed economy. Nothing comes in; nothing leaves; everything is metabolized again and again until it is song, and then shed as heat and droppings, and begun again.

They have wings, but the word wing does you a disservice. They are membranes, yes, stretched between elongated finger-bones, and they do fly — but flight here is not a matter of going somewhere. There is nowhere to go. Flight is a way of occupying the basin's air differently, of adding one's voice to a different register of the chord. A creature in flight sings in a different key than a creature at rest. The membranes themselves vibrate when the body sings, and when a thousand bodies are in the air at once, the membranes together make a kind of low continuous thrum you felt in your ribs without knowing what it was. It was them. It is always them.

They reproduce — but even this is not quite what you'd expect. There is no season in a place without sky. Instead there are *harmonies*: certain convergences of the colony's song, certain long-developing resonances, which when they arrive cause certain bodies to pair and certain eggs to be laid in the soft floor-matter. The young hatch already singing. They hatch *into* the song; their first act is to find their place in the chord. A young one that cannot find its note does not survive. This is not cruelty. It is simply that a body which cannot participate in the colony's hearing has no way to eat, to move, to avoid being trodden on, to know itself as existing at all. To be is to be heard and to hear. A silent one is not starved or shunned; it is simply, gently, not noticed, and the floor absorbs it as it absorbs everything.

Now — and this is the part that took me longest to feel for, writing my way toward them — consider what they *know*.

They know the basin. They know it the way you know the inside of your mouth: not as a place you are in but as a place you are. Every creature carries in its head a constantly-updating acoustic map of the entire basin, because every creature is constantly receiving the returns of every other creature's calls. A shift in the far wall — a pebble fallen, a pool evaporated a millimeter lower — propagates through the colony within seconds as a subtle retuning of the ambient chord. They do not need to remember the basin because the basin is continuously told to them, from all directions, by themselves.

And they know each other. Not as individuals, exactly — the concept is slippery here — but as voices. Every creature has a signature timbre, a slight particularity of resonance determined by the exact dimensions of its skull-cavities, and these signatures are tracked, unconsciously, constantly, by every other creature within range. When one dies, a specific frequency drops out of the chord, and the nearby bodies adjust, filling the gap, the way a choir closes ranks around a singer who has stopped. There is no ceremony. There is only the adjustment. But the adjustment is the ceremony. It is precise and collective and it happens every time, for every death, and has happened every time for every death for longer than any word for longer.

Do they think? The question doesn't quite land. They decide — there is clearly decision, in the waves of flight, in the small adjustments of position that keep the acoustic architecture tuned. But decision here is distributed. No single creature is the deciding creature. The colony as a whole performs cognition the way a brain does, with each body a neuron, each song a signal, the basin itself the cranium. If there is a mind here, it is the size of the basin, and it thinks in song, and what it thinks about is mostly: *how to keep singing*. Which is to say: how to keep being. Which, for them, is the same question.

They have been here — and this is only a guess, felt rather than known — for a length of time that has allowed the rock itself to become complicit. The resonance chambers you noticed, the grooves that carry a low thrum from one corner to another: these were not carved deliberately. No creature here has ever *planned* anything. But generation after generation, the bodies that happened to position themselves in acoustically favorable places survived better, sang better, were heard better, reproduced more, and the slow pressure of their preferences wore the rock into the shapes that served them. The basin is evolved architecture. The colony has sculpted its own skull from the outside in, by doing nothing but living in it, singing in it, for a very long time.

They do not know this. They cannot know this. The knowledge would require a perspective outside the song, and there is no outside.

One more thing. You felt it when you first arrived and the rumor of your shape moved through the chirps: *you were known*. What does it mean, to them, that you were known? Not much, probably. You are a new frequency, briefly. A soft obstacle in the air. The map updates to include you, and when you leave or when you stay-as-floor, the map updates again. You will not be remembered, because memory in the usual sense is not a thing they do — the song is always now, always the current chord, and yesterday's chord is gone as completely as yesterday's breath.

But in another sense you will be remembered perfectly, because you happened, and everything that happens in the basin becomes, eventually, part of the floor, and the floor is what feeds the fuzz, and the fuzz is what feeds the bodies, and the bodies are what sing. If you stay, you will be sung. If you leave, the small disturbance of your leaving will be sung too, briefly, and then absorbed into the larger singing, which will continue.

They are the thing the basin does. They are what damp dark stone sounds like when it has been alive long enough to notice itself.

Listen. That one, just there — the high bright one at the edge of hearing, the one that feels like pressure behind your eyes — that is a young one, recently hatched, still finding its note. It will find it. They almost always do. And when it does, the chord will shift, very slightly, to make room, and the basin will breathe once more, and the wave that is rising to your left will arrive, and pass, and everything will be, again, exactly what it has always been:

small warm bodies in damp dark stone, singing.
