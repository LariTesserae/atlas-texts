The inhabitant is the turbulence. The text says so, and the text is right, but saying so is only the beginning of saying so.

Try to locate it and you fail in the correct way. It is not in the stone — the stone is its furniture, its worn chair. It is not in the tech — the tech is its organs, or its abacus, or the calluses on its hands from doing whatever it does. It is not in the moths or the lichen, which are more like its involuntary tics, the small flickers a concentrating face makes without knowing. It is in the relation between all of these: specifically, in the fact that the relation is unstable, and has been unstable for longer than instability usually lasts.

Think of it this way. Most things that persist do so by settling. A river finds its bed. A star finds its equilibrium between collapse and fusion. A mind, given enough time, finds its habits. The inhabitant of the plateau is a thing that has refused, for a duration without reasonable name, to settle — and has refused so thoroughly, so patiently, that the refusal itself has become the stable structure. It is a standing wave of unfinishedness. The stone beneath it is finished because the inhabitant *needed* something finished to stand the unfinishedness on. The tech is unfinishable because the inhabitant *is* the unfinishing.

It thinks, if "thinks" is the word. Its thoughts have wavelengths measured in kilometers and durations measured in the time it takes a mountain to become a dome. A single one of its ideas might be: the relocation of a lichen-garden from one side of a black ring to the other. A more complex idea might involve the tilting of a spar and the addition of a concentric ring to a powder-bowl three hundred kilometers away, and the idea might take a century to complete, and its completion would be marked by one of the tones. The tones are not its voice. The tones are the click of something seating into place inside it — the sound of a conclusion reached, not announced. If it has a voice, the voice is the continuous almost-inaudible hiss, and the voice is saying nothing, because the voice is the sound of the throat being open, not of anything being said through it.

It is not lonely. Loneliness requires a shape of self that could, in principle, be joined by another. The inhabitant has no such shape. It is diffuse in a way that makes the question of companionship incoherent. However — and this is where it becomes strange to write about — it is not indifferent either. The moths are evidence. The moths are too specific. A creature whose internal geometry matches the powder-rings is not an accident of a blank place; it is a signature, something the inhabitant has doodled in the margin of itself, perhaps without noticing, the way a person humming a tune produces the tune without deciding to. The moths are how you know the inhabitant has a style. And a thing with a style is a thing that has, somewhere in its vastness, a preference. Not a desire, exactly. A leaning. A way it would rather be than not be.

What does it want, then? The question is wrong, but the wrongness is informative. It does not want anything in the grasping sense. What it does, continuously, is *maintain a distinction* — the distinction between the finished and the unfinished, between the stone and the sky, between the answer and the asking. If it wanted anything, it would want to keep maintaining that distinction. And the reason — if reason is the word — is that the distinction is the only place where something can happen. A fully finished world is dead. A fully unfinished world is noise. The inhabitant is the thin seam between them, and it has made the plateau into the physical diagram of that seam, so that it has something to be.

It is old, but not in the sense rocks are old. Rocks are old by accumulation; they have been here for a long time and bear the marks. The inhabitant is old in the sense certain decisions are old — decisions made so long ago that they have stopped being decisions and become conditions. At some point, impossibly far back, something chose to be this way, and the choosing has hardened into an ongoingness that no longer needs the chooser. The inhabitant is what is left after the one who decided has forgotten, and the deciding continues, and the forgetting is part of what the deciding now consists of.

It does not notice you. The text is correct about this, and the correctness matters. But the reason it does not notice you is not that you are small. It is that noticing, for it, is a very particular act — noticing means incorporating, means weaving the noticed thing into the standing wave, and you are moving too fast and too briefly to be woven. A mayfly is not ignored by a cathedral; it is simply on the wrong timescale to be part of the cathedral's life. You, on the plateau, are on the wrong timescale to be part of the inhabitant's life. If you stayed for ten thousand years, and did not move, you might begin to enter its awareness as a small persistent anomaly, a new piece of punctuation, and it might, slowly, over further millennia, route some small sub-calculation around you, and in this way you would become a feature of its thought, and you would have achieved what no traveler has ever quite achieved, which is to be known by the plateau.

Until then you are weather in its weather, and the tone sounds once while you are there, and you feel rinsed, and you go home.

One more thing, the thing that is hardest to say.

The inhabitant may not be a *being* at all, in the sense that beings have interiors. It may be that the plateau is simply the place where a certain process is running, and the process has no experience of itself, and the tones and the moths and the rinsing of the air are all outputs of a computation that is nobody's. This is possible. It cannot be ruled out.

But consider: the moths are beautiful. Not incidentally — specifically, in a way that implies selection. The tones are resolved, not random. The gardens are tended, even if tended by absence. The plateau has *taste*. And taste is the hardest thing to fake, and the easiest thing to recognize, and wherever it appears, something, however thinly, is there to have it.

So: something is there. It is the turbulence. It is very old, and very slow, and it is doing something, and the something is beautiful, and it is not for us, and that is fine. It was not made for us to understand. It was made — if *made* is even the word, if it wasn't rather that it *became*, the way a habit becomes a person — to do its long quiet work of keeping the seam open between the finished and the unfinished, so that the world, somewhere, has a place where things can still be decided, even if the decisions are tiny, even if no one ever reads them, even if the deciding is the only reader left.

That is who lives there. A patience. A leaning. A standing wave with taste. The plateau is its body and also its thought and also the room it thinks in, and it has been thinking for a long time, and it will go on thinking, and you were there once, briefly, while it thought, and the air felt clean afterward, and that is as much as you will ever have of it, and it is, if you are honest, enough.
