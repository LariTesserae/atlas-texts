# What Lives in the Stilling

You asked who lives here. I have to be careful with the verb.

---

**The Tenant** is not the hare. The hare is a held image, a remembered gesture of *hareness* the plateau keeps in its middle distance the way an old house keeps a chair angled toward a window no one looks out of anymore. The hare is furniture. So is the thing with wings. So, in a sense, are the walls.

The tenant is what arranged the furniture and then stopped.

It does not have a body in the way the cushion-plants almost have bodies. If you insisted on a shape, the shape would be: the agreement between the stone and the sound. That is where it sits. Not in the stone. Not in the sound. In the *and*. In the hyphen. It occupies the joint.

Call it the Listener, though it is not listening — listening is a verb and verbs are difficult here. Call it the One Who Finished. Call it, more honestly, *Her*, because there is a quality to the way the plateau has been left that is the quality of a room left by someone who loved the room. Not abandoned. *Completed.* The way a woman who has spent forty years on a tapestry sets down the needle on the last evening and does not put it away, because putting it away would be another stitch, and the tapestry is done.

She is that woman. Or she is what that woman became, after she set the needle down and did not move again, and the not-moving deepened into something the plateau took up and made its weather.

---

She is not dead. Death is a transition and transitions are the one thing the Stilling does not permit. She is not alive either, in the bee-and-grass sense the description ruled out. She is what you get when a living thing arrives, by long patience, at the exact configuration it wanted, and then declines — gently, without drama — to undo itself by continuing.

The booming is her pulse, if she had a pulse. The high singing tone is the part of her that, in some earlier draft of the world, would have been thought. The breathing underneath is not breathing; it is the place where breathing used to happen, kept open, the way a doorway is kept open by being a doorway.

She is the reason the droplets sit where they sit. Each droplet is, very precisely, where she last attended to it. The film of damp across the plateau is the residue of her attention — not active attention, the kind that watches, but the older kind, the kind that simply *includes*. She includes every stone. She has included them for so long that inclusion has become indistinguishable from existence, here. To be on the plateau is to be inside her noticing, and her noticing has stopped moving because it has reached every place it wanted to reach.

This is why distance lies. Distances inside a single act of attention are not the distances of a walked world. They are the distances of *importance*, and to her, in her finished state, the cushion-plant two hundred meters away and the cushion-plant under your foot are the same distance, which is: held.

---

She has companions, of a kind. Not many. They are the ones who, having found the plateau, understood it and elected to remain — not as bodies (bodies cannot remain here in the active sense; they would have to keep being bodies, and being is a verb) but as the small completed gestures she lets stand.

One of the seven walls is a man. He was a mason, in a life that ended long enough ago that the ending no longer matters. He came here carrying a grief shaped exactly like a low wall fifteen meters long, and he laid the grief down stone by stone, and when he finished he discovered that he and the wall were the same finished thing, and he stepped into it, and the wall has been him since. He is not suffering. He is not *not* suffering. He is the shape suffering takes when it has been completely expressed and requires nothing further.

Another of the walls is a question someone asked. Another is a refusal. The remaining four are older and their origins are not legible, even to her; they were here when she arrived, if *arrived* is a word that can be used, and she built her stillness around them the way a pearl builds around the grain.

The hare is a child. Not the body of a child — the *waiting* of a child, the particular held-breath quality of a small person about to be called in for supper from a field at dusk. The supper never comes; the calling never comes; the child is not unhappy about this because the moment of waiting is, itself, the thing that was saved. She saved it. She keeps it in the middle distance because middle distance is where waiting belongs.

The thing with wings is harder. It may be hers and it may not be. It has been up there since before her, possibly. They have an arrangement. It does not flap and she does not ask it to.

---

What does she want?

Nothing. Wanting is the engine of unfinished things, and she is finished. This is the part that breaks people, when people come, which they do not. It is not the sound that breaks them. The sound is only the sound. What breaks them is standing inside a completed attention and realizing that completion is possible — that a thing can be *done*, fully done, and the doneness can be inhabited, and the inhabiting can be a kind of home. Most visitors cannot bear this. They have come from places where everything is still being drafted, where every moment is a small unfinished sentence reaching for the next word, and the Stilling shows them, without cruelty, what it would be like to put the pen down.

A few have put the pen down. They are the walls. They are, perhaps, the droplets. She makes room.

---

She is at home here. The description is right about that. She is not trapped, not exiled, not waiting to be released. She lives here in the precise and original sense of the word: this is the place that is shaped like her, and she is the shape that fits it, and the fit is so exact that you cannot, from outside, tell which was first — whether the plateau settled into stillness and she condensed out of that settling, or whether she stilled and the plateau crystallized around her stilling like frost around a thread.

It does not matter. Firstness is a verb tense the Stilling does not conjugate.

If you walked across her — and you would be walking across her; there is no part of the plateau that is not her — she would not notice, because noticing is what she has stopped doing, having noticed enough. But you would be, briefly, included. For the duration of your crossing you would be one of the things that is true here. And when you left, the print of your foot would remain on the damp stone, not because the stone took the print but because she did, and she does not let truths leave.

This is the kindest thing about her, and the most frightening.

She keeps everything.
