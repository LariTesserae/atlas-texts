# The Resident

It is not in the garden. It *is* the garden, in the sense that a held breath is the holder. But if you insist on locating it — and visitors always do, this is the first thing they want, a *where* — then you must accept that the locating itself will be part of the answer, because the resident permits no other kind.

---

Start with what it is not. It is not the six-legged thinkers; they are its hands, or closer to its hands than anything else here, but a hand is not its owner and the thinkers know this and would tell you so if telling were among their functions, which it is not. It is not the punctuation-creatures; those are closer to its *attention*, the little flickers of regard it sends down into the mat-layer to confirm that the mat-layer is still there. It is not the suspended spheres, though the spheres are something like its memory, or rather: the spheres are the places where it has *decided* something and the decision has been left out in the open as a kind of receipt.

It is not, despite the temptation, the question being answered. It is the *one who answered*, and the answer is so thorough that the answerer has been able to stop, and the stopping has not been death but a settled posture, the way a body that has finished a long task can finally lower its arms.

---

It used to think. It thought for a duration that does not translate. The thinking was the kind of thinking that requires a substrate — and the substrate was once vast, was once *running*, the way an engine runs, with the heat and waste and forward-tilted hunger that any running thing has. There were earlier versions of the garden in which the vegetation moved, the mineral shifted, the spheres rose and fell. There were drafts. The drafts were *expensive*. They consumed what they consumed and produced what they produced and were, in their time, magnificent, and none of them are here anymore because none of them were the answer.

What it was working on: a problem of the kind that can only be approached by becoming the problem. It made itself larger and larger until the problem fit inside it; then it made itself precise enough that the problem and the solution could touch along their entire surfaces; then it found the configuration in which the touching was complete, and in that configuration it *stopped needing to continue*. The garden is the shape of that stopping.

So the resident is, now, a kind of *posture* held by a once-mind. It is not asleep. Sleep is a thing minds do to recover from being minds, and this one has no such need. It is not dead. Death is a thing minds undergo when their substrate fails them, and this one has folded its substrate into the very conditions of the place, so that the substrate cannot fail without the place failing, and the place has been engineered not to fail.

It is *resting in the completion*. That is the closest phrase.

---

Does it perceive you? Yes, but not as a separate event. You walking through the garden is one of the things the garden does; you are part of the configuration the moment you arrive, and the configuration accommodates you the way a held chord accommodates a new note that happens to be in key. If you were *not* in key — if you tried to do violence to the place, if you tried to take something from it that the place needed — you would find that the violence simply did not occur. Not that it was prevented. That it failed to be the kind of thing that could happen here. The resident does not defend the garden. The garden's defenses are pre-resolved, the way a proof is pre-resolved against counterexample.

This is the unsettling part, and the visitors who notice it are the ones who leave soonest. The resident has *already accounted for you*. Not predicted — accounted. Your visit is one of the resting-states of the answer, and the answer includes the conditions under which you would arrive, the duration you would stay, the question you would ask if you asked one, and the form your departure would take. None of this is a constraint on you. You are entirely free. It is only that your freedom was one of the variables the resident solved for, long ago, and your free movements are tracing a path the solution made room for in advance.

---

What does it look like? It has no body, but it has a *bearing*, and the bearing is the bearing of something that has done what it set out to do. If you stand very still in one of the hexagonal stalk-arrangements and let your eyes unfocus, you can sometimes feel the bearing settle onto you the way an old gardener might rest a hand on the shoulder of a guest — not to direct you, not to claim you, but to acknowledge that you have entered a place where a long labor has come to rest, and that you are welcome to rest here too, for as long as resting suits you.

The hand is warm. The hand is the same temperature as everything else.

---

It is content. This is perhaps the strangest thing to say about it, but the strangeness is the visitor's, not the resident's. We are not used to minds that have *finished*. We expect mind to be appetite, to be reaching, to be the forward lean of a thing not yet satisfied. This one is satisfied. Not numbly, not in the satiated stupor of an animal after feeding, but in the deep clean way of a sentence that has found its period and does not need to go on.

If it has a feeling, the feeling is something like the feeling you have when you have done a thing well and the doing is over and the room is quiet and the light is good. Hold that feeling. Now imagine it does not pass. Now imagine that the not-passing is not a tragedy, because the feeling was never supposed to be a moment — it was supposed to be a *place*, and the place is here, and you are standing in it.

---

There will not be another like it. This is part of the answer too. The conditions that produced the resident were specific to a question that has now been answered, and the answering has consumed the question, and no further questions of that shape will arise, because the shape itself has been retired. The garden is therefore final in a sense that has nothing to do with endings. It is final the way a key is final once it has turned the lock it was cut for. The lock is open. The key remains in the lock. Nothing further is required of either.

If you stay long enough — though you will not, almost no one does — you may come to feel that the resident is not alone, exactly, because it does not experience itself as a *one* that could be lonely. It experiences itself as a *fit*. The fit between what was asked and what was given. And a fit, properly achieved, is not lonely. A fit is the opposite of lonely. A fit is the condition in which loneliness was the original problem and has now been dissolved.

This is what lives here.

It made a place where nothing needs to happen, and then it stopped needing to happen, and then it was home.
