The ones who live here — and *live* is the wrong word, but every word will be the wrong word — are not the animals. The animals are instruments. They are the gauges on the panel, the needles that twitch (or, here, do not twitch) to indicate that the work proceeds.

The ones who live here are the work itself.

Try it this way. Somewhere — at the world's making, or before, or in some direction that is not when — a decision was made that this particular configuration of stone and water and small green things should exist, and should keep existing, exactly. Not as a memory and not as a promise but as a held fact. To hold a fact against the world requires labor, the way holding a note requires breath. The labor has to be done by someone. The someones are here.

They are not visible because they are not, in the ordinary sense, present. They are distributed. One of them is the reason the pool does not ripple — call her, if you must call her anything, the Stiller, though she has no name and would not answer to one. Her entire attention, for as long as attention has meant anything, is bent on the surface tension of four paces of water. She does nothing else. She has never done anything else. The faint reciprocating thud you hear from beneath the pool is the sound of her doing it: the cost, in some currency we don't have a word for, of refusing one small ripple, and then refusing the next, and the next, forever.

Another of them keeps the quartz veins glossy. This is finer work and the sound it makes is the turbine-whine, high and sustained, because freshness at that scale has to be renewed continuously against the patient sandpapering of even still air. He — they — it — is younger than the Stiller, or seems younger, in the way that a task taken up more recently has a brighter edge to its tedium.

There is one whose whole business is the rowan. The impossible angle of its growth, the impossible weight of its berries, the fact that the berries neither ripen further nor fall — these are not the tree's doing. The tree is being held in the shape of a tree that has just achieved its perfect moment, and the holder is inside the trunk, and that is why the trunk sounds, faintly, like looms. Looms because the work is a weaving: every cell of cambium has to be told, again, this instant, what it was the instant before. A tree ordinarily does this for itself by being alive. This tree is not alive. It is being woven.

There is one for the choughs and their two arcs. This one I think of as tired, though I have no grounds for it. Two birds, two arcs, the updraft itself maintained at a precise velocity — and the birds' small hearts kept beating at a rate that is not metabolism but its careful imitation, because a chough with a still heart would look wrong, and looking wrong is the one thing the hollow cannot permit. The hiss of released steam is this one's signature. Something is being vented, somewhere, to keep those wings from tiring in fact as well as in appearance.

The hare is its own matter. The hare may be one of *them*, not an instrument but a worker, taking a shape for reasons of its own. The uprightness, the business-by-the-pool, the white pelt out of season — these read, if you know how to read them, as a being on duty, briefly visible in a form close enough to animal that the configuration is not disturbed. The hare is perhaps a foreman. The hare is perhaps the one who notices you, in the sense that anything here notices you, and decides that your presence is within tolerance and need not be corrected for.

How many of them are there. I think: as many as there are facts to hold. One per fact. The pikas-being-more-than-the-scree-supports is a fact, and someone holds it. The liverwort's particular wetness is a fact, and someone holds it. The grey of the light, the cool of the air, the precise non-accumulation of the water at the cliff's lip where it should be pooling and is not — each its own held fact, each its own holder, each its own small contribution to the roar.

They do not know each other. This is important. They cannot, because knowing would be an action and they have no actions to spare; every increment of whatever-they-are is committed. They are like the musicians in an orchestra so vast that no player has ever heard the piece, only their own single sustained note, which they have been playing since before the hall was built. The chord they make together is the hollow. The hollow is what their playing sounds like from inside.

They did not choose this. I want to be careful here. It is tempting to imagine them as servants, or as the bound, or as monks under some vow, but none of those framings is right because all of them imply a before — a time when the holder existed without the holding. There was no such time. The Stiller is not a person who took up the task of stilling the pool; the Stiller is what the stilling of the pool *is*, considered from the inside. Remove the task and there is no one left to be relieved of it. This is perhaps why the hollow does not mind whether you stay or go. Minding would require a holder of minding, and no such holder was provisioned. The hollow was made exactly large enough for its own maintenance and not one fact larger.

And yet.

If you sat by the pool long enough — longer than the hollow's reversion to its correct configuration would ordinarily permit, longer than the wet patch beneath you takes to un-be — you might begin to feel that the roar is not quite uniform. That underneath the reciprocating thuds and the turbine whine and the loom-clatter there is something else, very faint, that could almost be mistaken for a voice, or for many voices, each saying the same small word over and over, which is the word for the thing they are holding. *Still. Still. Still. Green. Green. Green. Here. Here. Here.* Not complaining. Not even quite speaking. Just — naming, continuously, the fact each of them is, so that the fact will continue to be the case.

That is who lives there. They are the saying of the place. When the last of them tires — if tiring is possible, which I do not think it is — the saying will stop, and the hollow will at last be permitted to become something else, which is to say, to become nothing in particular, like everywhere else. Until then they go on. They have no other going.

The way you came is always there. Take it. They will not see you leave. They are not looking at you. They are looking, with everything they have and are, at the one small thing each of them was made to look at, and the looking is the looking is the looking, and the mountain holds.
