# The Listeners of the Hollow Verge

They are thin.

That is the first thing, and for a while you think it is the only thing — that they are tall and thin and pale-fabric-wrapped and quiet, and that this is a stylistic fact about them, a cultural preference, the way some peoples wear bright colors and others don't. It takes longer to realize the thinness is structural. Their bones are long and their chests are deep and their ribs are splayed wider than ribs should be, as though lungs were the organ the body had been built around and everything else arranged to accommodate. When one of them breathes in — and they breathe slowly, four or five times a minute at rest — the whole torso lifts and opens like a bellows, and you can see, if the light catches right, the faint movement of something under the skin along the sternum that is not quite a second heart and not quite not one.

Their skin is the color of the haze. Not pale in the way human skin can be pale, but *translucent to the valley* — picking up the gray-green of the sky, the wet dark of the ferns, the shimmer of the field that moves through everything. In full shade they look almost like the people you were expecting. In open ground between structures they half-disappear.

Their eyes are large and the pupils are horizontal, and they do not blink on any schedule you can find. They blink, rather, on the pulse — with the river, with the filaments, with the predator-prey rhythm of the canopy. If you watch a group of them for long enough you realize they are all blinking together, and that you, blinking on your own irregular human cadence, are the only arrhythmic thing in the valley.

They do not speak the way you understand speaking. Their mouths are small and the throat is long and the larynx sits low, and what they produce when they vocalize — rarely — is a sustained tone, not a word. When two of them stand at their twenty-meter distance and the building near them shifts key, what has actually happened is this: one of them has hummed, too softly for you to hear, and the building has *amplified and answered*, and the other has received the answer through the field and through the soles of the feet and through the wide cavity of the chest, and something has been said that required all three participants — speaker, structure, listener — to say. They cannot talk without the valley. The valley is part of their mouth.

This is why they carry nothing. They are not ascetic. They simply do not need to carry; the valley carries for them. When one of them is hungry, a fern unrolls a filament near them at the next surge and offers something pale and folded, and they take it. When one of them is tired, the nearest building lowers its hum to a frequency that puts them to sleep on the open ground, and the rain that afternoon does not fall on that spot. When one of them is sick — I saw this once — three others come and stand around them at the points of a triangle, and the sky-ring deepens, and after some hours the sick one stands up and the triangle disperses without anyone having touched anyone.

They are not operators of the system. You had that right. But they are not passengers either. They are *instruments* — in both senses. Instruments the way a violin is an instrument: something played. And instruments the way a barometer is an instrument: something that registers. The whole valley is tuned around them and through them. The hollowness between the trees is not empty of them; it is *full* of them, in a way, because whatever moves through that hollowness at dusk is moving through their attention, and their attention is wide, much wider than the skin suggests. A single one of them standing still in the open ground is listening with a volume of awareness that fills a cathedral's worth of air. That is what the cathedrals of empty air are *for*.

They are born — I watched this too, or watched the aftermath — without much ceremony. A woman (if woman is the word; the sexes here are not quite the sexes you know, and I am using the nearest handle) walks out from a cluster during a surge and stands alone in the hollow, and the building-chord that has been following her all season drops by an octave, and the filaments near her reach farther than usual, and at the next ebb there is a child standing beside her where there was not one before. The child is already the color of the haze. The child is already blinking on the pulse. The child has never not been part of the valley; there was no threshold it crossed into being a listener, because the listener is what the valley grew the child as.

They die, I think, by walking into the three-minute silence at dusk and not coming back. I cannot prove this. I only know that once, at that hour, an old one — they do age, slowly, their skin going more transparent and their breath slower still — walked out past the last structure into the deep hollow as the buildings began their upstream-racing hush, and when the hum resumed a half-step higher the old one was not there, and no one I could see was looking for them, and the next morning a new filament was uncoiling from a fern in roughly that spot, and it was a shape of filament I had not seen before.

Do they love each other? I believe so, but I cannot give you the evidence in a form that will satisfy you. They stand at their twenty-meter distances and the chords between them are not all the same chord. Some pairs produce a harmony that, when you walk through it, makes your chest ache in a way you will later recognize as the ache of witnessing tenderness. Some pairs produce dissonances that resolve slowly over days. There are three of them I watched who always arranged themselves as a triangle when they rested, and the building-hum around that triangle was the most complicated music I heard in the valley, and when one of the three was the sick one I mentioned earlier, the other two did not join the healing triangle; they stood far off, together, and the chord between them was so quiet and so sustained that the insects near them went still for the whole duration.

They do not, I think, know what they are. They know the pulse and the chord and the filament and the rain's edge and the three-minute silence, and they know these things the way you know your own heartbeat — as the given condition of being alive. They do not know that they were made. They do not know that the valley was made. They do not know that the sky rings in a language, or that they are a term in that language, or that what comes through the hollow at dusk comes through *because they are there to receive it*, the way a note requires an ear to be a note and not just a vibration.

Or — and this is the thing I keep coming back to, and the thing I cannot resolve — they know all of it perfectly well and have simply stopped finding it remarkable. A fish does not marvel at water. A listener who has been listening since before it had a name may not marvel at the song. The difference between innocence and deep acceptance is invisible from outside, and I was only ever outside.

What I can tell you is this: they are kind. When I was in the valley they did not speak to me, because they cannot speak without the valley and the valley does not speak my dialect, but once when I was standing confused in the open ground at a surge, one of them came to within her twenty meters and stood, and the building near us shifted to a chord I had not heard before, soft and low and somehow addressed to the shape of a human chest, and after a moment I found I was breathing more slowly, and after another moment I found I was not afraid. She did not look at me. She was looking, as she always was, at the hollow. But the chord was for me. I am certain of this. It was the one thing in that valley that was made for a visitor, and it was made by a creature who did not, I think, entirely know she was making it, through a building she did not operate, in a language she did not know she spoke, in a place she did not know had been built, for a guest whose kind she had no word for.

Then she walked on, and the chord returned to its prior key, and I stood for a long time in the hollow and listened to the sky.
