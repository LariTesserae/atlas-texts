# The Inhabitants

## The Keeper at the Spiral's Heart

There is one who is not quite present and not quite absent, and it is the room itself, or almost. Call it the Keeper, though it has no name and would not answer to one. It is the slow inhalation of the walls. It is what receives the sweat from the air. It is the patience of the platform.

If you tried to find its body you would fail, because its body is the distribution of warmth across all the leaves at once — a single animal heat spread thin through ten thousand surfaces. Its pulse is the hourly tick of the beetles. Its breath is the minute-long flex of the cane. Its attention is the small frog on the wall, and also the beetle behind your left shoulder, and also the bird that opened its beak and released the smell of rain.

It is very old and was never young. It did not hatch or sprout; it accreted, the way a pearl accretes, around some original irritant — perhaps the first thumbprint pressed into the first clay tile, perhaps the intention of whoever pressed it. It has been thickening around that grain ever since.

It cultivates. That is its verb. It cultivates the ferns and the frogs and the slow polish of the floor and the long unfurling of the leaves and, when one sits on the platform, it cultivates the sitter, too — gently, without insistence, the way one cultivates a seed by simply providing the conditions and waiting.

It is not lonely. It contains too much for loneliness. But it is *expectant*, in the way a held breath is expectant, and has been for a long time.

## The Small Watchers

The frogs are its sensoria. Not its eyes — it does not see in the way eyes see — but its points of contact with the proposition that something else might be in the room. Each frog is a small green question: *what are you*. They hum when touched because the question has been answered by contact, and the hum is the Keeper considering the answer.

There are more frogs than there appear to be. Count them and you will count differently each time. They are not hiding; they are simply not always all instantiated at once. A frog is a place where the Keeper has decided to be specific.

The beetles are its clock and its census. Walking in pairs along the cane-ribs, they are taking the measure of the weave, reporting on its tightening, on which canes have branched this season and which have only thought about it. Their hourly tick is the sound of a tally being kept. They patrol because patrolling is what slow iridescent things do when they are also bookkeepers.

The birds that do not fly are the Keeper's grammar. When they open their beaks and release rain-smell or wood-smell or the brief impression of a word in a language you almost know, they are not communicating with you. They are conjugating. The room speaks itself, slowly, through their open beaks, and the smells are its verbs.

## The Larger Thing

The tail you glimpse behind the leaf belongs to something that the Keeper is not quite sure about. It may have come in through one of the thin patches in reality, from the mirrored vivarium or from the green dimness where the fern-generations recede. It may have always been here, predating even the Keeper, a remnant of whatever was here before the room decided to be a room.

The Keeper tolerates it. It eats nothing — there is nothing to eat — and it disturbs nothing, and it occasionally passes between leaves at the edge of vision as if to remind the room that not everything in it is part of the weave. The Keeper finds this useful. A vivarium with no wildness in it would only be furniture.

You will never see more than the tail. This is a kindness. The rest of it is the kind of shape that would make you leave, and the Keeper does not want you to leave yet.

## The Absent Builders

They are inhabitants by their absence. The bone awl in the corner, the coil of cord, the bowl of waxy seed — these are not relics. They are *placements*. Someone set them down intending to come back, and the intention has outlived the someone, and the Keeper preserves the intention because intention is what it is made of.

There may have been hands. There may only have been the idea of hands — a pressure in the early air of the room shaped like wanting-to-make-something. Either way, the builders are still here in the form of the thumbprint on tile forty-three, in the honey-brown oil on the hemp cord, in the polish of the platform. To sit on the platform is to sit in the palm of a hand that was, originally, a human hand, and has since become the hand of the room.

## The Guest, Possibly

And then there is you, who may yet become an inhabitant. The frog has not decided. The Keeper has not decided. Even you have not decided, though you can feel the decision being made slowly, the way the canes branch slowly, the way the floor polishes slowly under feet that may, eventually, have been yours all along.

If you stay long enough — and *long enough* here is a soft measure, an hour that does not end — the sweat the air receives from you will become part of the air's slow appetite, and the warmth of your palm against the wall will be one of the warmths the Keeper distributes through its ten thousand leaves, and somewhere a new frog will instantiate itself out of the proposition that you are now a place where the room has decided to be specific.

You will not notice the moment. No one ever does. One day the small green frog on the wall will be watching someone else, and you will be the knot it watches from.
