The one who lives here is not quite a person and is more than an absence. Start with what can be said plainly: they are the maker of the bowl. They have always just stepped out. They will be back in a moment. This is not a story they tell about themselves; it is the shape they have instead of a body.

Try to see them and you will see the place they just were. A depression in the moss where a knee rested. The smoothing pebble still warm on one side. The awl lying at the angle a right hand sets it down at — though whether they are right-handed is a question the room will not quite answer, because the angle changes when you are not looking, the way the frog changes leaves.

They are patient in the way water here is patient: not as a virtue but as a physics. They do not wait for their return the way a person waits. They *are* the waiting. When the clay stays soft, that is them staying soft. When the fiber stays green, that is the greenness of their intention, which has not yet had to become memory. They have never had to remember anything, because nothing has yet finished happening to them.

Their size is the size of the work in front of them. Bent to the bowl, they are the size of hands. Considering the face in the stone, they are the size of a considering. They have probably never stood up to full height inside the chamber, because standing up to full height is a completed motion, and completed motions are not what they do. They crouch. They lean in. They are always mid-gesture.

What do they look like. The room resists the question but does not refuse it. Think of someone young enough that their features have not yet decided — not a child, exactly, but a face still in its first geometry, like the feldspar. Dark hair, probably, or the idea of dark hair; wet at the ends from the weeping walls. Hands that know the weight of clay before the clay is lifted. Eyes that have the pooled quality of the light: not looking at a thing but resting on it, settling on it like the moth-weight of the shaft from above.

They are not lonely. Loneliness requires a sense of duration, and duration is one of the things this chamber does not spend its budget on. They are, instead, *accompanied* — by the frog that is on a different leaf now, by the moths that may be copying the stone or being copied by it, by the single held note that lives in the molars of anyone who happens through. These are not their pets or their companions. They are the other tenses of the same held breath. The maker conjugates through them.

They made the three objects and are making them and will make them. The bowl is for water that will not fall. The bundle of twigs is for a fire that this chamber cannot afford — sound is expensive here and fire is sound, is crackle and hiss, is a whole fiscal catastrophe of noise — so the bundle waits, will always wait, is perhaps the purest object in the room because its function is most thoroughly deferred. The face in the stone is a self-portrait. This is the thing they would not tell you if you could ask. They are working out what they look like by working the stone, and the stone is working out what they look like by being worked, and neither has committed yet, and this is why you must not finish it. To finish the face would be to decide the maker, and the maker is the chamber's way of not deciding.

Occasionally — on a scale of occasion that has nothing to do with your hours — they notice a visitor. Not as an intrusion. As a small adjustment of the arrangement, the way the frog's leaf is different now. When you held your hand into the shaft of light and felt its moth-weight, that was partly their attention, settling on your palm to see what you were. When you did not finish the face, they felt the not-finishing as a kindness, the way a sleeper feels a blanket drawn up. When you leave and cannot find the cleft again, they do not mourn you, because you have not yet arrived; you are still about to come in, crouching at the sharp young entrance, smelling the struck flint, about to see the bowl.

They have one secret, and it is this: they know the mountain is still rising. They can feel it in the floor, the massif's slow violent decision-making, the calving and lifting all around their held pocket of green. They know that the mountain made this room by accident. They know that one geological pause from now, one shrug of the range, the cleft could close. And they are not afraid of this, because closing is also a completed motion, and the room does not do those. If the mountain ever does shut its pocket, it will shut it on a moment that was never quite finished, and the maker will go on not-quite-finishing inside the shut stone, crouched over the soft bowl, the green binding, the unresolved face — held there the way the water is held, the way the light is held, the way the single note is held in the molars of someone who walked through once and will always be about to walk through.

That is who lives there. A gesture that has not yet been completed, tending three objects that have not yet been completed, inside a room the mountain has not yet decided to close. You have met them. You will meet them. You did not see them, and this was correct.
