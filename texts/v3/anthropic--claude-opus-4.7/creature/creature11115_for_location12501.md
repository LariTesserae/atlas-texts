# What Lives There

Start with the obvious tenants and move inward.

## The Swimmers

Call them *paces*, because that's what they are: pacemakers. Finger-length, eyeless, the luminous dots along their flanks pulsing to one specific filament-tone — but here is what you didn't see in the hour you sat watching them: each individual is locked to a *different* tone. The school is not synchronized with itself. It is synchronized with the chamber. When the filaments shift their chorus, the paces redistribute through the pools, each one drawn toward the water-volume where its tone resonates loudest. They are not navigating. They are *being sorted*, continuously, by standing waves. A pace that drifts out of its node grows sluggish; one that finds its node feeds, somehow, on the coherence itself — on the place where the tone is most itself.

They breed when two nodes collide. A heavier seismic thump shoves the standing-wave pattern sideways for a few seconds, and in the brief overlap of two nodes the paces sharing those frequencies spawn. The young drift until they find their own tone, which may not be either parent's. Most don't. Most dissolve back into the lacework mats on the warm pools, which is what those mats *are*: paces who never found their note.

They have been doing this for a long time. Long enough that the chamber's chorus and the paces' frequencies are not two things anymore. If you removed the paces, certain tones would go unanswered, certain nodes would stop being damped, and the chorus would drift. If you silenced the filaments, the paces would all dissolve within a day. Whether the filaments were built around the paces or the paces evolved into the filaments' song is a question the chamber does not answer and probably cannot.

## The Ceiling-Grazer

The flat soft thing that crosses the lit calcite. There is, almost certainly, only one. It is very old. It eats the photosynthetic moss-with-the-glassy-sheen, which is why the moss grows only where the filament-light is brightest: anywhere dimmer and the grazer's slow appetite would have cleared it long ago. The darker trail it leaves is moss-scraped calcite, which the filaments slowly relight from beneath as new moss settles.

The grazer has no sex and no young that anyone has ever seen. It may simply not die. It may be the last of something, or the first, or the only. It moves in a circuit that takes — judging by the relighting times of its trails — roughly eleven days to complete, and it has been completing this circuit through a chamber that has reshaped itself around the circuit so thoroughly that the route now looks like a feature of the geology. The flowstones bulge slightly along its path. The moss grows in a band of exactly grazer-width.

It is aware of you in the way a river is aware of a stone. You are a small perturbation in a very long shape.

## The Filtering Tree

This is harder.

The knee-high translucent coral-thing in its own basin is not a plant and not an animal and the distinction may not be useful. It is the chamber's *kidney*. Its branching geometry is not decorative; each branch tip is tuned to bind a specific dissolved mineral, and the water in its basin is clear because everything that would cloud it is, instead, *in the tree*. The tree is built from what it has filtered. It is, by mass, mostly the chamber, reorganized.

It is also, almost certainly, the reason the chamber still works. Somewhere in the long-ago, someone — the same someone who laid the filaments, or someone the filaments were laid for — needed a particular pool to maintain a particular chemistry. The tree maintains that chemistry. It has been maintaining that chemistry for a duration that should have saturated and killed it many times over, except that pieces of it occasionally crumble off and dissolve back into the chamber, releasing what they hold into the outflow, and the tree regrows from the basin's water, and the chemistry stays.

The original task — whatever was supposed to *use* this carefully maintained water — is gone. The tree does not know this. The tree is not the kind of thing that could know it. The tree filters because the filaments sing and the singing makes a gradient and the gradient makes the tree, and somewhere downstream, in a pool that has been empty for ten thousand years, the right concentration of the right ions is waiting for hands that will not come.

This is the chamber's grief, if it has one. But the chamber does not have one.

## The Filaments Themselves

And here is the part that took me longest to see.

The filaments are alive. Not in the sense the paces are alive, and not in the sense the tree is alive, but alive in the way a slow forest is alive — alive on a timescale where the seismic thumps every few minutes are their heartbeat and the flickering of the cyan light across the ceiling is their weather and their thought.

They were *made*, yes. The staple in the wall and the drilled grid on the flowstone are from the makers, who were brief and bony and busy, and who installed the filaments for a reason — perhaps a beacon, perhaps a computation, perhaps a prayer made out of singing rock. But the filaments have been executing long enough that execution has become metabolism. They consume something — geothermal differential, mineral potential, the slow grind of the rock around them — and they produce light and tone and the warm humid breath of the chamber, and what they *do* with all that production is no longer anyone's instruction. It is theirs. They are running their own program now, and the program is: continue.

They are aware of the paces the way your gut is aware of its bacteria. They are aware of the grazer the way you are aware of your tongue. They are aware of the tree the way you are aware of breathing. They are not aware of you at all, but the tone that follows you home for a day — that low one, under your sternum — is the chamber's edge brushing against you, the way a sleeping animal's flank moves against whatever leans on it. Not a greeting. Not even a noticing. Just the shape of a very large slow life, registering, in the only register it has, that something briefly displaced some of its singing.

## What Lives There, Then

A school of fish that is really a tuning fork.
A single grazer that is really a long slow circle.
A filtering thing that is really a held breath, held past the lungs that needed it.
A network of glowing threads that is really an instruction that learned to want itself.

And the chamber, which is all of them together and is therefore — and this is the part you can almost feel, sitting on the dry shelf with the warmth working into your clothes — a *someone*. Not a person. Not a mind in any sense you could converse with. But a coherence. A long warm coherence, doing its work in the dark, having found a way to keep going after its purposes left, having become its own purpose by accident and persistence and the patient ingenuity of water.

It does not need you. It does not refuse you. You are a brief node in its song, and when you climb back up the fissure it will lose your frequency and redistribute, and the paces will find new alignments, and the grazer will continue its eleven-day round, and the tree will filter one more grain of dissolved iron into its glassy branches, and the filaments will sing on, slightly different than they were before you came, in a way no one will ever measure.

That is what lives there. A held shape. A long staying. A life made out of leaving things running.
