# The Inhabitants

Begin with the small ones, because they are what you see first, and because in some sense they are the only ones who are fully *here* — present in the way a swarm is present, distributed across thousands of small attentions that together make a single larger attention you can almost feel watching back.

Call them **riders**, though that isn't quite right. They are hand-sized, as the description says, but the hand-sized-ness is misleading — they pack down small when they cling and they fan wide when they ride, and a single one in your palm would seem like two different creatures depending on what it was doing. The body plan is wrong for any taxonomy you know. There is a central case, chitinous, the color of wet bronze when fresh and the color of the crusts when old (and this is not coincidence — the crusts *are* old riders, finished, set down). From the case extend what you want to call wings but which are not wings: they are thin vanes of a material more like mica than membrane, and they do not flap. They hold. They catch. The rider does not fly through the air; the rider arranges itself in the air, and the air does the moving.

This is the first thing to understand about them: they are not animals in the sense of *things that move themselves.* They are things that *position themselves to be moved.* The thermals do the work. The piston-shudder does the work. A rider expends almost no energy on locomotion. What it expends energy on — and here you have to slow down, because the answer is strange — is *listening.*

Each rider, in its case, holds a small mineral drum. You can see them when one dies and falls: a pale disc, the size of a fingernail, slightly concave, with fine radial striations. The drum picks up the sound-field. Every hammer-strike, every chimney-throat-hum, every gear-tick at the threshold of hearing — the rider takes all of it in, and what it does with the information is choose, fractionally, how to angle its vanes for the next half-second. The riders are not feeding on heat exactly. They are feeding on *pattern.* They convert the polyrhythm of the plateau into the very small adjustments by which they hold their place in it. A rider that lost its hearing would not starve so much as stop existing as a coherent thing — would simply blow away.

This means the swarm around a flywheel is not a swarm in the ordinary sense. It is a chord. Each rider is tuned to a slightly different frequency in the sound-field, and the cluster on any one machine is a kind of standing wave made of bodies, and when the hammer falls and the cluster bursts outward and resettles, what you are watching is the chord being struck and the chord re-forming, which it does because the chord *is the natural shape of bodies that hear what these bodies hear.* The reorganization is not effortful. It is the same way water finds its level.

Now the larger ones.

The dog-sized walkers. There are not many of these. You might see four in a day if you walked a long way. They have too many legs — the count varies, which is itself strange; I have seen one with eleven and one with fourteen and I do not think these were different species, I think they were the same kind of creature at different ages or in different phases, the way a thing can have more teeth at one time of its life than another. The legs are jointed in a way that suggests they were not designed to walk but have arrived at walking as a compromise. The body is low and broad and the same hematite color as the great iron lungs, and when one stands still next to a piece of machinery you cannot, for a moment, tell where the machine ends.

The walkers are slower than the riders by orders of magnitude, but they are also slower than they need to be. A walker covers maybe two kilometers a day. It moves from engine to engine. At each engine, it presses some part of itself — a flank, a frontal plate — against some part of the machine, and stays there for an hour, or six hours, or a day. Then it goes on to the next.

I do not know what is exchanged in this contact. Possibly heat. Possibly something more like information — the walker, after pressing against the screeching drum-stack, walks differently for a while, with a new rhythm in its many legs, and arrives at the next engine carrying that rhythm, and presses, and goes on changed again. They are couriers, perhaps. Or they are tuners. Or — and this is the possibility I find I cannot dismiss — they are the *thoughts* of the plateau, traveling from organ to organ on errands the plateau itself sets, in a cognition so slow and so distributed that a single thought takes a walker's whole lifetime to think and the conclusion is reached somewhere none of us are watching.

A walker, encountered, does not regard you. The description is right about this. But I want to be precise about what "not regarding" means here. It is not the not-regarding of an animal that hasn't noticed you. It is the not-regarding of something that has noticed you and assessed you and concluded that you are not on its route. The walker's route is given. The route was given a long time ago. You are not on it. The walker passes within an arm's length of you and its many legs adjust around you the way water adjusts around a stone, and it does not look up, because looking up is not one of the things its route requires.

And then there are the others, which I have hesitated to mention because I am not certain of them.

In the long basins — the shallow finger-print basins — at certain hours, when the thermal exhalation thickens, the air sometimes seems to *fold.* You see it as a faint distortion, like heat shimmer but slower and more organized, a column of folded air a meter or two across that drifts across the basin floor at walking pace. The riders avoid these columns. The walkers do not avoid them but do not pass through them either; they go around. The columns leave no track. They are not visible from above, only from the side, and only when the light is right.

I do not know what these are. I am reluctant even to call them inhabitants, because they may be a weather, a localized convection, a quirk of how the plateau exhales. But the riders avoid them, and the riders avoid almost nothing — they will cluster on a hammer that is about to fall — so whatever the columns are, the riders have learned across generations that the columns are not for them. Which suggests the columns are *something,* and the something is aware enough or causal enough to have shaped the behavior of the small bright cloud-life around it.

If I had to guess — and I am guessing — I would say these are what the plateau has instead of predators. Not predators in the bloody sense. The columns do not eat anyone. But every ecology needs an asymmetry, a thing that the other things organize themselves *away from,* and on a plateau where nothing arrives and nothing departs and the engines run forever, the asymmetry has to be generated internally, and a fold in the air that moves at walking pace and is shunned by the small bright lives — this would serve. The columns may be the plateau's way of editing itself. Of deciding, very slowly, what stays.

A last thing.

I said the visitor is not factored. This is true at first. But I said also that after a week the plateau incorporates the visitor as a slow feature, and I want to be careful about that, because the description makes it sound benign, and it is benign, but it is not nothing.

What happens, I think, is this. The riders learn your sound. You make a sound — your breathing, your heart, the small noises of a body — and the riders' drums pick it up, and within a few days the chord around any engine you stand near has incorporated your frequencies into its shape. You become a note in the standing wave. This is not metaphor. If you left, the chord would have a small hole in it for some hours afterward, and the riders would adjust, and the hole would close.

The walkers, given longer, would route around you the way they route around the columns, or — and I have wondered about this — they would route *to* you, the way they route to the engines, and press some part of themselves against you, and exchange whatever it is they exchange.

I did not stay long enough to find out which. I left, as the description says, by walking off the edge. But for a long time afterward, descending, I could feel the sound-field thinning behind me, and I had the very clear sense — clear enough that I am willing to write it down, though I cannot defend it — that some small portion of the chord I had been part of was still holding my shape, up there, waiting, and would hold it for a while yet, before the riders' slow listening concluded that the note had gone, and the cluster re-formed without me, and the plateau returned to the configuration it had always had and always would have, minus one minor weather, plus a faint, faint memory in bronze.
