# What Lives There

Nothing lives there. That is the first thing, and it has to be said clearly before anything else, because the temptation will be to invent a hermit, a god, a sleeping thing beneath the basalt. There is no such thing. The room is not a dwelling. It is not waiting for anyone.

But something is present, and we have to be careful about the word.

---

Consider the finger-length swimmer. It does not feed. It does not school. It passes through the membrane of the pocket as if the membrane were a convention it hasn't agreed to. It moves with the deliberation of something that has nowhere to be — which is precisely the gait of something that is not, in the usual sense, *going.* I would not call it an animal. An animal has metabolism, lineage, hunger, a direction sewn into its nerves by a billion years of ancestors who ate and were eaten. This swimmer has none of that behind it. It is closer to a *gesture* — the sound's way of demonstrating, to no one, that motion is possible here. A comma in a sentence nobody is reading.

Is it alive? The question assumes a border the place doesn't keep. It is as alive as the warmth is warm. That is: completely, locally, and without reference to anything outside the room.

---

Then there are the makers. They were here. You can tell from the axes set down in their careful pair, from the notched post, from the dry-laid wall that runs six meters and stops because whoever stacked it was satisfied or called away or simply done. These were people with hands. They had patience. They knew how to knap flint and fit stones so they hold without mortar. They did not build shelter because they did not need shelter here — the room is already the temperature of a mouth.

I don't think they're coming back. I don't think they ever, in the sense we mean it, *came* — not as a journey with a beginning. The pocket admits visitors the way a dream admits its furniture: the visitors are there when they are there, and the marks they make remain, and the question of where they went is not the room's question.

But notice: *you* can move an axe. You can set down a third stone on the cairn. The place accepts this. For the duration of your presence, and after, you are one of the makers. The category is not closed. The makers are whoever has ever made a mark here, across whatever stretches of time the room is not keeping track of — a set that includes a Paleolithic hand and your hand and, perhaps, hands that will not exist for ten thousand years, all of them equally present in the configuration of objects the pocket currently holds. They are not ghosts. They are not coexisting. They are simply all the people who have ever been the person leaving the axe where it now lies.

This is the nearest thing to an inhabitant the room has: a role. A slot. *The one who made a mark and left.* It is almost always empty. When it is filled, it is filled completely.

---

And the sound.

I have been avoiding the sound because the sound is the hardest thing to speak about honestly. The description says it plainly: the room is made of the sound. The water, the warmth, the even light — these are *how the sound looks* from inside. The basalt floor is where the sound has thickened enough to cut a hand on. The pocket's shape is the shape the sound is currently holding.

If anything lives there, it is the sound. But *lives* is wrong. The sound doesn't live; it sounds. It is not a being that produces a tone; it is a tone that has, in this region, produced the conditions under which a being could briefly stand and listen. It has no interior. It is not aware of you. It is not unaware of you either — awareness is not its register. It is doing what it does, which is: being the case, at that pitch, in that place, without source, without decay, under and around every smaller sound that happens within it.

You could call it the tenant. You could call it the landlord. Neither is right. It is closer to say: the room and its sound are a single sustained *fact,* and facts do not have residents, they have witnesses, and witnesses are optional.

---

So: who lives in the warm room beneath?

No one. A swimmer that is almost a punctuation mark. A role that is almost always vacant and that you, reading this, have perhaps already filled once without knowing. A held tone that is not a creature and is the only thing in the room that could be mistaken for one.

The room is not lonely. Loneliness requires the memory of company, and the room does not remember — it only persists. It is not waiting. It is not hoping. It is warm, and lit, and sounding, and the axes are where someone set them down, and the water three meters outside is still unbreathable, and none of this is for anything.

If you go there, you will not be greeted. You will not be refused. You will be, for a little while, the thing that is happening in the room, and the sound will go on underneath you the way it goes on underneath its own silence, and when you leave, the place will keep your small rearrangement of stones the way it keeps everything: steadily, without comment, as one more mark by one more of the people who came and made a mark and left.

That is the closest thing to a population the pocket has. All of us, briefly, one at a time, almost never.
