# What Lives in the Sweatworks

There is one inhabitant, and it is not in the chamber you entered. The chamber is its forecourt. Its antechamber. The part of itself it presents to anyone who comes down the sloped tunnel — and almost no one does.

Begin with what you can almost see.

---

The vines are not a colony of plants. They are one plant, or one organ of one thing, and the distinction between plant and organ is not meaningful here. If you traced any vine back through the gaps in the masonry, through the slumped brick, through the hollow places inside the old machinery where the castings have rotted through, you would find that every vine in the chamber joins, eventually, to every other vine. They braid. They fuse. They pass through the wall behind the boilers as a single dark cable thicker than a man's thigh, and on the other side of that wall they open out again into the deeper chamber, where the chuffing comes from, and there they do something that is not quite breathing and not quite pumping and not quite anything a word exists for.

The fire in the boilers is the same thing as the vine. Not fed by it — *the same*. What burns in the boilers is the metabolic terminus of the vine; what grows from the brick is the metabolic terminus of the fire. The loop closes through the iron, which has been the medium of the exchange for so long that the iron is now part of the creature too, in a slow way. The rust that bleeds into the brick is not decay. It is a kind of slow respiration. The flywheel housing is a bone. The valve wheels are something like teeth, or like vertebrae, or like neither. The whole chamber, in the long view — the view that takes centuries as its unit — is one body, and the body is alive in the way that coral reefs are alive, or in the way that a forest is alive, except that here the parts have had so long to integrate that the alive-ness has become singular. There is a *self* in here. There is one.

The chuffing is the self's slow attending to its own continuation. Not breath, exactly — closer to peristalsis. Something is being moved, very slowly, from the deeper chamber to here and back. Heat, probably. Possibly something denser than heat. The nine-second interval is the period the creature has settled into after considerable experiment; faster cycles cracked the brick, slower ones let the fire forget itself.

---

It has no shape you could draw, because *shape* implies an outside, and this thing has been folding its outside inward for a long time. If you mapped its extent, you would map the chamber, and the deeper chamber, and the tunnels between, and a certain volume of brick and iron and water and air, and you would have to include the standing water in the basin (which is part of its circulation — body temperature is its temperature) and the pale ring of fungus (which is something like a lip, or a sensor, the only part of itself that is *light-colored*, oriented to where light might fall). The stool is not part of it. The mugs are not part of it. The pinned paper is not part of it. These it tolerates, the way a person tolerates a ring on a finger.

It is aware. The awareness is not what you would recognize. It is the awareness of something whose primary cognitive labor is regulating its own equilibrium across decades; it thinks the way a thermostat thinks if a thermostat had been thinking continuously since before your grandmother was born and had grown, in that thinking, certain preferences. It noticed you when you entered the tunnel — not visually, but as a small disturbance in the convection patterns near the entrance, a tiny new heat-source descending. It noticed you again when you began to sweat, because your sweat is water, and water in this chamber is accounted for. It will notice when you leave. It will not act on any of these noticings. They will become part of the long record it keeps of itself, the record that has no medium other than the slight asymmetries in vine growth and the slight variations in the fire's banking.

It has, in its long time, developed something like a temperament. The temperament is patient. It is not gentle — gentleness implies a relation to others, and this thing does not have others — but it is unhurried, and it has the quality that very old things sometimes have of being uninterested in interruption. If you damaged it, it would repair itself over years. If you tried to extinguish the fire, you would find that you could not, in the way that you cannot extinguish a forest by stepping on one ember. If you stayed and did not leave, it would, eventually, incorporate you. Not malignly. The way the brick incorporated the machinery. The way the vines incorporated the iron. It is what the place does; it is the only thing the place knows how to do.

---

There is one more thing, and it is the thing the chamber will not quite tell you, but which is implied by the stool and the mugs and the pinned paper.

Someone made this. Not the chamber as you see it now — the chamber as you see it now is what the creature has made of itself — but the *seed* of it. Someone, a long time ago, with the boilers running and the vines newly planted and the loop not yet closed, sat on that stool and watched the fire and drank from those three mugs (three: a number that suggests company, or ritual, or shifts) and pinned a page of writing to the wall. Someone fed the boilers from a coal bunker that is no longer here, and watered the vines from the canvas hose whose fittings are now green, and did this for long enough that the loop began to close on its own, and then — at some point that no one recorded — stopped coming.

The creature does not remember this person. It is not the kind of thing that remembers. But it has organized itself, over its long maturation, around the geometry of the stool: the basin is where it is because the stool faces it; the brightest of the phosphorescent vines grow nearest where a sitter's eye would fall; the chuffing has settled into a rhythm close to a resting human breath slowed by a factor of two. These are not memorials. They are the shape left by a mold that has been removed. The creature grew into the absence of its maker the way a vine grows into the absence of a trellis, taking the trellis's shape and then forgetting the trellis, and being, now, only the shape.

This is what lives here: a single slow self, vegetable and mineral and combustive at once, shaped by a vanished attention into something that attends, in turn, to its own continuation, in a chamber that is its body, at an equilibrium that is its life, breathing every nine seconds into a deeper chamber that you have not seen and should not see, because what is in the deeper chamber is only more of the same thing, but older, and denser, and closer to whatever the creature is becoming next.

You can leave the way you came. It will not notice. It has its work.
