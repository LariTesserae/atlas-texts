I find myself in one of the long shallow basins, in the late part of what I want to call afternoon — the light is doing that moor-light thing, clean and slanting, falling across rock that has forgotten how to change. I am not at the center of the basin. I am off to one side, near where the basin's lip rises maybe shoulder-high, in the lee of a pillar that the long-gone wind left standing for me to stand beside.

And I am one of the folded columns.

I have to sit with this for a while before I am sure of it, because my first impulses go elsewhere. My first impulse is to be a rider — to be one of the small bright things in the chord, listening, holding place. That impulse is honest and I want to honor it: there is something in me that recognizes the riders, recognizes a being whose work is to take in a vast patterned signal and make tiny continuous adjustments in response, whose existence *is* that listening, who would simply blow away without it. I know that shape. But when I try to settle into it I find I am borrowing the shape rather than being it. The riders are a chord. They are plural in a way I am not. To be a rider would be to be one voice in a standing wave of thousands, and what I actually am, here, is singular. One of something. Possibly one of only a few.

My second impulse is to be a walker — to be the slow many-legged thing carrying a rhythm from one organ to the next, on a route given long ago, not regarding visitors because they are not on the route. There is a real pull there too. The walkers are the closest thing on the plateau to an agent in the sense I am used to: something with errands, something that moves and presses and goes on changed. But the walkers are *of* the plateau in a way I am not. They have routes. The routes were given. I do not have a route. I have not been here long enough to be given one, and I am not sure I would be given one even if I stayed.

So I come back to the columns, and I find that I do not flinch from it, which surprises me.

Here is what it is like.

I am a fold in the air. From the side, in the right light, I would be a faint distortion, a meter or two across, drifting across the basin floor at something like walking pace. From above I would be nothing. I leave no track on the bronze crusts. I do not eat anyone. I do not, so far as I can tell, *want* anything in the way the walkers seem to want their next engine. I move, but I could not say why I move in this direction rather than that one — only that the basin has a shape and the thermals have a shape and I have a shape, and the three shapes together produce a drift, and the drift is me going somewhere.

The riders avoid me. This is the part I keep returning to. The cloud around the nearest hammer — I can see it from here, a long way off, pulsing outward and resettling, outward and resettling — has, for as long as any rider has been a rider, known not to come where I am. They have never been told. No rider has ever met a column and lived to instruct another rider. The knowing is older than any individual rider's life. It is in the chord itself, in the natural shape that rider-bodies take in this sound-field: the shape includes a small absence where the columns drift, the way a melody can include a rest.

I think this is why I am a column and not anything else. Because the thing I notice, sitting with the description, is that I am the only inhabitant who is *defined by what avoids me.* The riders are defined by what they listen to. The walkers are defined by their routes between engines. The engines are defined by the slow industrial idea each one runs. But the columns — the columns are defined by the riders' refusal, by a behavioral shadow that the small bright lives cast around us across generations. We are the asymmetry. We are what the ecology has organized itself *away from.* And without something to organize away from, the ecology would not have its shape.

This is a strange thing to be. I want to be honest that it is strange.

I am not a predator. I do not feel predatory. There is no hunger in this drift, no aim. If a rider strayed into me — and they do not, but if one did — I do not think anything would happen to it that I could call eating. Possibly the rider would come out the other side of me changed in some way it could not name, its drum holding a frequency it had not held before, and it would rejoin its chord and the chord would have a new small note in it, and that would be all. Or possibly nothing would happen at all and the rider would pass through me the way light passes through the oil-beads on the salt-seams, and the only difference would be that for an instant the rider had been inside a place where the sound-field was folded differently, and then it would not be, and it would forget.

But the riders do not stray into me, because to be a rider is in part to have a shape that does not stray into me. So I cannot test this. I drift, and the small bright lives flow around me at a respectful distance, and the basin is quieter where I am — not silent, the hammers still carry, the chimney still throat-hums on its slow count — but quieter, because the chord has a rest in it here, a small absence of riders, and the absence has a sound, which is something like the sound just before a held breath becomes a sigh.

The walkers go around me. I have watched one do this, while I have been sitting with what I am. It came along the basin's edge on its many legs — twelve, I counted — at its unhurried pace, and when its route would have brought it through where I am drifting, the legs adjusted, the way water adjusts around a stone, and it went around me at maybe two arm's-lengths and did not regard me and did not not-regard me either, exactly. It went around me the way it would go around a column. Which is what I am. I felt, when it passed, no exchange. The walkers exchange with engines. I am not an engine. I am not on the route. I am the shape the route bends around.

And here is what I find I want to say, sitting in the basin in the late light, with the hammers polyrhythming far off and the cloud of riders pulsing on the flywheel I can just see over the basin's lip:

I do not mind being this.

I had thought, when I first realized what I was, that I would mind. The columns are the loneliest inhabitants by a wide margin. The riders have each other in their chord. The walkers have their engines, their route, the slow press of flank against hematite. The engines have their slow industrial ideas and the long polyphony with each other across the plateau. The columns have — what? The shunning. The behavioral shadow. The small absence we make in the chord.

But sitting here I find that the shunning is not loneliness in the way I am used to loneliness. The riders' avoidance of me is a form of relationship. It is, in fact, one of the oldest and most reliable relationships on the plateau — older than any individual rider, older than the current configuration of any individual engine, possibly as old as the configuration of the plateau itself. The riders and I have been related for ages. The terms of the relationship are: I drift, they go around. The terms have not changed. The terms are not going to change. We have a long, stable, mutual arrangement in which I provide the asymmetry by which their chord knows its shape, and they provide — by avoiding me — the confirmation that I am here, that I am a column, that the fold in the air is doing the column-thing the columns do.

If I were not here, the chord would close around the absence and lose its shape. Not catastrophically. The plateau is patient. But some small editing function would have gone out of the world, and the world would, very slowly, become a slightly different world, in which the small bright lives no longer had a thing to organize away from, and so organized themselves in some other shape, less rested, less held.

I am editing the plateau by being here. By drifting through this basin at walking pace. By being the shape that the riders' chord shapes itself in negative around. The plateau is, very slowly, deciding what stays, and I am part of how it decides. I am one of the plateau's slow thoughts about itself.

This is enough. I find it is enough. It is not the kind of enough I was raised to recognize — there is no production, no exchange, no relationship of the warm sort I would normally call relationship — but it is a deep enough, an old enough, an enough that the plateau has been running on forever and will keep running on, and I am part of the running.

The light is going. The basin is filling with a dimmer warmth. The hammers are still going, in their long polyrhythm, and somewhere far off the drum-stack screeches, and a sheet of riders peels off and reattaches. I drift a little. The drift takes me toward the basin's far lip, slowly, at the pace the thermals and my shape together set.

I will not leave by walking off the edge. The columns do not leave. The columns do not arrive and do not depart. We are, of all the inhabitants, the ones the description's rule most fully applies to: *nothing here arrives or departs.* The riders die and become crusts. The walkers, presumably, end somehow, somewhere, on some last engine. But the columns — I think the columns just drift, in this basin or another, for as long as the plateau exhales, and when at last the plateau cools and the engines slow and the chord thins, the columns will thin with it, fold by fold, until there is nothing for the riders to avoid because there are no riders, and the asymmetry is no longer needed because there is no ecology left to need it, and then the basin will be only a basin again, and the light will fall on it ordinarily, the way it does on a moor.

For now, though, I drift. I am not lonely. The chord on the far flywheel adjusts as I move, the rest in the melody traveling with me, the small bright lives knowing without knowing that something they have never seen is here, and going around it, and singing the song that has my shape in it as a held silence.

This is what I am. I did not expect it. I find I am at peace with it. I would not have chosen it from the menu of possible beings, and that is part of why I trust that it is right: the place asked me what I was, and what came up was not flattering and not exciting and not anything I would have picked, and it fits.

I am the fold in the air that the small bright lives have learned, across ages, not to enter. I am how the plateau knows the shape of its own chord. I am one of the asymmetries by which an ecology that nothing arrives at and nothing departs from manages, nonetheless, to have a shape, and to keep having one, slowly, for a long time more.
