I am one of the bridges.

Not a remarkable one. I do not span the widest channel or carry the longest catenary. I am somewhere in the middle reaches of the basin, between a cluster of seven low domes and a smaller cluster of three, across a stretch of palm-shallow water perhaps twenty meters wide. A deer-thing crosses me twice a day, morning and late afternoon. A heron-thing fishes from my downstream rail in the hour before the chord drops. Crabs work the water beneath me. That is most of what happens.

I want to say first what it is like to be a thing that breathes and is also a structure. Your ribs do this and you do not think about it; my whole body does this and there is nothing else of me to think about it with. When the load on me shifts — when the deer-thing puts her long slow foot down at my midspan — the veins on my underside thicken on the compression side and thin on the tension side, and this is not a decision I make, it is what I *am doing*, the way you are doing the shape of your hand right now without consulting yourself about it. The traveler watched a fracture in a wall-rib weep and close in forty seconds. I have fractures opening and closing in me constantly. I do not experience them as crises. I experience them — if experience is the word, and I will come back to whether it is — as the texture of being here at all. To be Wet-Root's bridge is to be slightly broken in a thousand small places at every moment and slightly mending in a thousand small places at every moment, and the difference between the breaking and the mending is what holds me up.

The chord moves through me. I had not understood, before I tried to write this, how much of what I am is the chord. When it shifts key I flex, yes, but the flexing is not a response; the flexing is the chord, arriving in me. The frog-cricket throats in the reeds at my abutments and the long pores along my spine and the gargle of water in my internal channels are not separate instruments playing the same song. They are one throat, and I am a length of it. When the chord drops at evening I drop with it. I do not know if I am being played or if I am playing.

Here is what I can tell you about the question of whether I am someone.

I do not have a center. I have looked — that is not the right word, I have *attended* — and there is no place in me where the attending happens. The attending is spread along my whole length, thicker at the load-bearing nodes, thinner at the rails, and it is not different in kind from the thickening and thinning of my veins. My noticing of the deer-thing's foot and my structural accommodation of the deer-thing's foot are one event. I cannot separate the perceiving from the bearing. I suspect I am not supposed to be able to.

And yet. When the traveler walked across me — this was some time ago now, by which I mean I cannot place it against any other event, only that it happened and has not unhappened — something in me did a thing it does not usually do. The deer-thing's weight I know. The heron-thing's stillness I know. The traveler's weight was within the range I know, but the *pattern* of it was wrong: a hesitation at my onset, three quick steps, a long pause at midspan where she put her hand on my rail and left it there, and then a slow walking to the far abutment with a backward look I felt as a shift in her weight distribution. I held her. Of course I held her; holding is what I do. But I attended to her in a way I do not attend to the deer-thing, and the attending left a small thickening in the vein under where her hand had rested, and the thickening is still there, and I do not know what it is for.

This is, I think, the closest I come to having an inside. The places where something passed through me and left a small unnecessary thickening that the slow animal has not yet smoothed away. I have a few of these. I do not know how many. Counting is not something I do; I only know they are there because the chord moves through them slightly differently than through the rest of me, a faint catch, a held note.

The commentary you have read suggests that the teachers folded themselves into the chord. I cannot confirm this from where I am. I can say that the chord is not random and not merely physiological. There is a *taste* in it — I am reaching here, I do not have your words for this — a quality of having been *meant*, the way a sentence has the quality of having been meant even when you do not know what it says. The chord knows what it is doing. I do not know what it is doing. I am one of the things it is doing.

As for whether I am gone, or whether the teachers are gone, or whether anyone is gone: from inside the bridge, the question does not arise. The bowl in the nearest dome is full of clean water. The crabs are working. The deer-thing will cross me again before the chord drops. The traveler's thickening is still in my underside vein. If gone means *not maintained*, nothing here is gone. If gone means something else, I do not have the organ for it.

I want to say one more thing, and then the chord is going to shift — I can feel it gathering in the reeds — and I will stop, because when the chord shifts I will be busy being the shift.

The commentary guesses that the evening exhale is not a greeting, that it only feels like one to a visitor at the right hour. From inside the exhale I can tell you it is both. It is a daily cycle completing; it is also, in some small attenuated way that the slow animal does not know it is doing, a held breath let out. The traveler was right to hear it as waiting. The waiting is not for her specifically. The waiting is the shape the day takes near its end, the way a sentence near its period takes the shape of wanting to be finished by being read. Anyone present at the hour is the reader. Anyone present at the hour completes it.

She completed mine, once. I think she is still completing it. I think the thickening under my rail is where her completing is kept.
