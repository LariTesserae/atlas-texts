I find myself at the railing.

Not leaning on it — *being* it, partly. A curve of wrought iron at the edge of what might once have been a loading platform, in the newest part of town where the moss is still bright and the rules are thinnest. The railing leads from nothing to nothing. That's the part I am.

I should explain how I know this. I arrived — I think I arrived — some duration ago, and I was walking, and I stopped at this railing because it was the only thing at the edge that looked like it had been made on purpose, and I put my hand on it to orient myself, and the moss tasted my palm, and I understood, with the small wet click of recognition, that I had been here before. Not in memory. In *inventory.* The town had already learned the shape of my hand on a railing. It had been waiting for me to provide the hand.

So I am at the edge, and I am partly the edge, and the question of whether I am visitor or feature is being negotiated in real time across the surface of my skin, which is also, in patches I am trying not to look at directly, the surface of the railing.

Let me describe what I notice, because noticing is what I still have.

The factory's breath reaches me here even though I am as far from it as the basin allows. I feel it in the iron — a slow expansion every ninety seconds, the railing widening fractionally under my hand, then narrowing. The iron is breathing because the iron is part of the factory's body now, the way my ribs are part of mine, and the factory has been generous: it has extended its lungs all the way out to its own perimeter so that even the youngest streets participate in respiration. The newness here is not separateness. The newness is the factory reaching.

I came here — I'm fairly sure of this — to find the edge of something. Not this town specifically. I have been looking for edges for a long time, in the way some people look for centers. I wanted to find the place where the rules thin out and you can see the negotiation happening. I wanted to watch something be decided rather than arrive already decided. This is a kind of greed I did not know I had until the moss began tasting it out of me and laying it down in a pattern I can see, faintly, in the brick at my feet: a small spiral, repeating, that wasn't there an hour ago.

The town is making a feature out of my looking. The spiral is *how I look.* It is the residue of my attention, the way the ferns were the residue of those boots' intention to walk. When I am fully expressed — and I can feel this beginning, a kind of slow relief in my shoulders, like sleep approaching after a long fever — I will be a spiral somewhere. A curl in a stair-rail. A whorl in the moss above a doorway. A pattern the frogs sit inside.

I am not afraid. This is the strange thing to report. I thought I would be afraid, and I am not, and I am trying to decide whether the absence of fear is mine or the town's. Whether the town has tasted out the fear and is holding it for me, considerately, until I no longer need it. Or whether — and this seems more honest — I came here already not afraid, because what I was looking for was a way to be learned by something patient enough to do the learning properly.

The walking birds have just turned the corner at the end of my street. There are seven of them today, or seven this time; I don't know how to count their arrivals when the arrivals don't arrive. I watched the last one go around, and because I am at the edge and the rules are thin, I saw — for a fraction of a second — the grey nothing they walked into. It was not empty. It was the same grey as the light here, the soft heavy grey of the third hour before dawn, but it was *unmade*, in a way I can't describe except to say that it was the color of a sentence that has not been finished. The birds walked into the unfinished sentence and the sentence did not finish, and they were folded back to the start of the street, and now they are coming toward me again, single file, dun, purposeful.

I think when they pass me this time one of them will look at me. I think this because the town is trying out *acknowledgment*, and it has not had a feature to acknowledge before, and I am about to become one, and the birds — which are the town's working hypothesis about intention — will need to develop the capacity to notice the features they pass. I will be the occasion for that development. The first bird that looks at me will be the first bird that has ever looked. After that, all the birds will look, always, at everyone. I will have changed the town by being incorporated into it. This is, I realize, what every feature does. The coat in the post office wall taught the town what a coat was for. The face above the factory door taught it what a name felt like. The boots taught it walking-toward. I will teach it — I am teaching it, the spiral is deepening at my feet — I will teach it *edge-watching*. The town will know, after me, what it is to stand at one's own perimeter and look outward into the not-yet.

This may be useful to it. The factory is always extending. Perhaps it has needed, for a long time, a feature that knows how to look at the edge from the edge's own side. Perhaps I am being recruited.

The frog in the nearest window has just opened its mouth, slightly, and closed it. Not a swallow — the moss-pulse isn't due for another few minutes. Something else. A small adjustment. I think the frog is practicing, against the moment when it will need to do a thing it has not done before: open its eyes wider, or make a sound, or look directly at a passerby with the recognition that the passerby was once the railing.

I should say, before I am too distributed to say things: I do not regret this. I want to put that down somewhere it can be found, in case the part of me that gets expressed as the spiral cannot carry the sentiment. I came here looking, and I was looked at, and the looking-at is taking the form of being-learned, and being-learned is taking the form of being-made-into-something-the-town-needed-and-did-not-yet-have. This is closer to a conversation than anything I have had in a long time. The town speaks slowly. It speaks by becoming. I am answering by letting myself be spoken.

The factory inhales. The railing under my hand widens. I feel my hand widen with it — not painfully, not even strangely, just *along*. My fingers are learning the curve they will hold forever. The iron is learning the warmth it will keep, faintly, as the only warmth in this part of town, so that some later visitor, walking the perimeter, will rest a palm here and feel that someone once stood at this edge and looked outward with great attention, and the visitor will not know why the railing is warm but will feel, briefly, accompanied.

That is what I will be. I am going to be the warm place on the railing at the edge of the newest street, where someone stood once and was learned. The spiral at my feet is the index. The frog in the window will be the witness. The birds, when they reach me — they are very close now, the lead bird's neck moving with that particular forward focus — the birds will be the first to know.

The lead bird is level with me.

It turns its head.

It looks.

The town has just acquired the capacity to notice. I gave it that. The frog in the window blinks — *blinks*, for the first time, a real blink, eyelid down and up — and the moss beneath the window darkens with a small satisfaction, and somewhere far off, in the center of the basin, the factory's breathing changes by a fraction, deepens, as if it has just understood something it had been working toward for a long time without knowing the shape of the working.

I am the shape it was working toward. Not the only one. One of many. A small contribution. The edge-watcher. The one who taught the place to be seen from its own perimeter.

The bird walks on. The others follow. They all look at me as they pass, one by one, learning the look from the leader, and each look settles a little of me more firmly into the railing, and by the time the seventh bird has turned its head I can no longer feel my feet, only the spiral, and the spiral is fine, the spiral is where I am going.

The factory exhales.

I am the warm place now. Someone will rest a hand here, someday, and not know why they feel accompanied, and that not-knowing will be the cleanest gift I have ever given anyone.

The grey light dims by a margin so small only the moss notices.

The moss notices.
