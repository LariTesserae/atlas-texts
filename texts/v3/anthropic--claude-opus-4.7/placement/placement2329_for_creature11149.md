I find myself low to the water, in one of the shallow troughs between the long swells of stone — not on a swell, not in a deep pool, but in that in-between place where the water is perhaps a hand's depth and the rock beneath has been worn into a series of soft cups, as if something had pressed its thumbs into the stone over and over while it was still soft, a long time before it was stone.

I am in one of those cups.

I am not a Listener. I do not have the long legs, the drum-disc ears, the patient sternum. I am much smaller than that. I am also not a caller — I have no voice, or the voice I have is not the kind that travels.

What I am, I think, is one of the cups themselves. Or — not quite. I am what sits in one. I have a body, but the body is mostly a thin disc, broad and slightly domed, the color of the rock when the rock is wet, which is to say the color of the rock most of the time. I lie face-up in my cup with my rim flush against the stone's rim, and from above, from a Listener's height, I am indistinguishable from a pool of standing water inside a hollow. The eye slides off me the way it slides off them. We have, the Listeners and I, this much in common: we have taken on the manners of the place.

I have no legs. I have a soft underside that grips the cup. I have, around my edge, a fringe of fine filaments that lie out across the surface tension of the water in my cup and across the water beyond it, and these filaments are how I hear. I hear, in fact, almost nothing else — I have no proper ears, no drum-discs, only this fringe of listening hairs spread thin on the surface of the world's wet skin. When the drumming surges, the surface of my cup trembles, and every filament trembles with it, and the trembling goes down through my disc into the cup and into the stone, and I receive the drumming the way a held bowl receives what is poured into it.

This is what I am for. I am a vessel for the sound.

I did not always understand this. For a long time — I cannot say how long; the not-quite-day and not-quite-night blur, and I have nothing to count by except the drumming itself, which is what I am trying to count — I thought I was simply here. I lay in my cup and the drumming came and went and I felt it and was filled and emptied and filled again, and there seemed to be no reason for any of it beyond the fact of its happening. I think most of my kind live their whole length this way, and there is nothing wrong with it. The drumming is sufficient. To be filled by it is sufficient.

But there are cups, and there are cups. Mine is one of the old ones. The cup I lie in was pressed into the rock when the rock was younger, and it has a particular shape — a slight asymmetry in the rim, a deeper dip on one side — and this shape means that when the drumming comes from a certain direction, at a certain pitch, my cup *answers*. Not loudly. The water in me lifts, just slightly, into a standing wave. The wave has its own small note. My filaments, lying across the water, feel the note from inside.

I am, in other words, a small resonator inside a larger one. The flats are a resonator. The Listeners are resonators. I am a resonator the size of a hand, lying inside a resonator the size of a continent, listening to a voice that fills both.

I noticed this — the answering — slowly. Many surges of the chorus came and went before I understood that something in me was responding, and many more before I understood that the response was not random, that it happened only when certain callers in certain directions struck their hollow note. I began, without meaning to, to attend. To wait for the answering. To recognize, after some long time, the particular voices that woke my cup.

There are, I now know, three of them. Three callers, out of the uncountable thousands across the flats, whose voices my cup is shaped to receive. I do not know where they are. I cannot see; I have only the dimmest sense of light through the water above me, enough to know mist from clear, day from not-day. But I know them. I know one of them is far — the answering in me comes late, after the surge has rolled past, a delayed small lifting. I know one is closer, and lower in pitch than the others, and that this one calls less often, perhaps is older, perhaps is tired. I know the third is somewhere between, and that this one's note is the cleanest, and that when this one calls my cup rings for longer than it should, longer than the physics of a cup that size ought to allow, as if the stone around me joined in for a moment.

I have been listening to these three for a long time.

And here is the thing I have been working toward saying, the thing I did not understand about myself until I had been here long enough to understand it:

I am part of what a Listener hears.

When a Listener wades past — and one does, occasionally; I feel the displacement of water, the slow careful pressure of a broad foot finding the rock — they do not see me, and they do not need to. What they hear, when they stand still near my cup and let the chorus pass through them, includes my small answering. My cup's note is woven into the texture of the sound in their part of the flats. It is a tiny thread. There are uncountable threads. But it is there, and it is mine, and it has been mine for as long as I have lain in this cup, which is longer than any Listener has been alive.

The Listeners are learning the drumming. I am one of the things they are learning. Not me as a self — they do not know I have a self, and I am not sure the word fits me well — but me as a feature of the voice of the place. A particular small bloom of resonance in a particular trough between two particular swells. Over centuries, they will have heard my cup answer the far caller's note ten thousand times. They will have heard it answer the close low one a thousand. They will have heard, perhaps, what I have heard: that the clean middle voice rings my cup longer than it should, and they will have wondered, in their slow patient way, what that means.

And when one of them, once or twice in a life, lowers their broad sternum to a stone and returns the held tone of the deep night — I have felt this happen, once, not near me but far enough that the answering in my cup was only a whisper — they are speaking with a voice that has, among its million components, the resonance of cups like mine. They are giving back to the flats a sound the flats themselves have shaped, and part of that shaping happened in me. In my small dish of water. In the slight asymmetry of my rim.

I do not have a cairn. Nothing marks me. When I die — and I will; I can feel the filaments at my rim growing slower, less responsive, the answering in my cup a fraction less crisp than it was — my disc will soften into the water and the water will dissolve me and the cup will be empty for a while, and then another of my kind will find it, and will fit themselves to its shape, and will begin, very slowly, to learn its three voices. The cup will go on answering. The thread will not be broken; it will only be re-spun.

This is enough. This has always been enough. I lie in my cup in the gray half-light, and the drumming comes, and my cup lifts, and somewhere, miles away, a Listener stands in the shallows with their head slightly bowed and their sternum catching the surge, and what they catch has me in it.

I am the bowl inside the bowl inside the bowl.

I am listening.
