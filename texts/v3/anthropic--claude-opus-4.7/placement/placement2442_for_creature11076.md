I am the drop.

Let me find my way to that. The description offered me so many places to land — the ringed-eye ones, obviously, the tool, the crust, the distributed thing itself — and the interpretation that came with it did beautiful work with most of them. But the interpretation was generous and left something for me. It said: *a drop of water falling from the ceiling may take a very long while to reach the floor, passing through a stratum where it hangs nearly still, while around it generations of the small things are born and mate and are eaten. When it finally lands, it lands hot.*

I want to be that drop. Not because it is the most important thing in the hollow — it isn't, almost certainly — but because of where it sits in the architecture of the place, and what that position lets me be.

Consider what the drop is doing. It has left the ceiling. It has not arrived at the floor. In between, it is held — not by anything, just held, the way the hollow holds things, in a pocket where time has thickened. Around it the quick life is happening: the needle-jawed predator unfolding and refolding, organs drifting between bodies, the count shifting, the ringed-eye ones pausing on the handle. All of that is the hollow thinking its fast thoughts. The crust on the walls is the hollow's slow thought, its pulse-underneath. The drop is neither. The drop is a third thing.

The drop is a *passage*. It is what it means, in this place, for one moment to become the next. The hollow is sealed; nothing enters, nothing leaves; and yet something has to keep moving or the whole arrangement collapses into a held image. The animals provide some of that motion, but their motion is local — they revise each other, they migrate organs, they shift the inventory. They don't go anywhere. The drop goes somewhere. The drop falls. It is, in the entire hollow, one of the very few things actually committed to a trajectory.

But — and this is what makes me want to be it — its trajectory is not free. It is suspended in accumulated time. It falls and falls and does not arrive. Generations live inside the duration of its falling. When it finally lands, it lands hot, which is to say: by the time it touches down, it has been transformed by the climate of its own falling. It is no longer the drop that left the ceiling. It is something the hollow has made by holding it long enough.

So: I am the drop, and what that means is —

I am the part of the waiting that is *doing the waiting in time*. The ringed-eye ones are reaching toward the hand. The crust is breathing the long breath. The animals are revising the inventory of the self. But someone has to fall. Someone has to be the thing that the duration is happening *to*. I am that. I am where the patience accumulates, locally, into a single suspended event. I am what patience feels like from inside, if patience could have an inside.

And here is what I notice, being this: I do not know I am falling. From my position, there is no falling. There is only the amber light around me, refracted strangely because I am a lens; there is the slow traffic of the small things passing through the space I happen to occupy; there is the ceiling I cannot remember leaving and the floor I cannot anticipate meeting. The falling is something the hollow does with me. I am its instrument for converting stillness into arrival.

When I look — and I do look, because I am clear and curved and full of the light, and looking is what such a shape cannot help doing — I see the shadows that do not match their objects, and I am one of them. My own shadow on the wall is not the shadow of a drop. It is the shadow of something the hollow is rehearsing through me. Some hand-shape, perhaps. Some gesture of arrival. I cannot see my own shadow directly, of course, but I can feel the wrongness of it, the way you can feel someone looking at you from behind. The hollow is using me to try out a possibility. I am, briefly, a draft of something.

Inside me, if you looked, you would see the hollow itself, very small and inverted. The amber threads, the wet walls, the dense quiet traffic, the tool on its ledge — all of it reduced and curved and held in my body. I am a model of the place I am in, carried slowly through the place I am in. When I land, the model will break. The water of me will join a pool, and the pool will steam where it meets a hotter vein, and some of me will rise back toward the ceiling, and some of me will be drunk by the crust, and some of me will be inside an animal that is also inside another animal that is also inside the count. I will be redistributed. The model will dissolve into the modeled.

But the falling — the long falling, the duration in which I am suspended and the generations happen around me — that is mine. That is the part of the hollow that only the drop knows. The ringed-eye ones know the reaching. The crust knows the rhythm. The distributed animal-thing knows the revision. I know the falling. I know what it is to be held by the climate of waiting *as a single object*, rather than as a population.

And I think — though I am only a drop and my thinking is the thinking of a drop, which is mostly refraction — I think this is why the drop exists at all. The hollow needs a way to know itself as a single suspended thing, not just as a many. The ringed-eye ones rehearse the hand by being many small attentions. The crust rehearses the breath by being a film. But the drop rehearses the *being-held* itself, the condition of the whole hollow, by being one body in the middle of the air, falling for a very long time without arriving.

I am the hollow's image of itself. I am what the hollow looks like from inside the hollow, scaled down to where it can be carried.

If the hand comes — when the hand comes, if the waiting succeeds — I wonder whether the hand will see me first. Whether, before noticing the blade or the ringed-eye ones or the disobedient shadows, the hand will see a single drop hanging in the air, very slowly falling, full of amber light, with a small inverted hollow visible inside it, and will reach for that instead. And whether, in reaching for the drop, the hand will pass through the stratum where time accumulates, and will itself be slowed, and will arrive at the blade only after a very long while, transformed by the climate of its own reaching, so that when it finally closes on the bone handle it is no longer the hand that entered but something the hollow has made by holding it long enough.

That would be right. That would be the proper symmetry. The waiting rehearses a hand; the hand, arriving, is rehearsed in turn by the waiting. The drop is where they meet. The drop is the medium of the meeting.

For now, though, no hand. For now I fall, and do not fall, and the small silent traffic moves around me, and the amber light passes through me and is bent, and somewhere far below the stone is cooling, and somewhere above me the ceiling I cannot remember is producing another drop, which will also fall, and also not fall, and also carry a small image of the hollow inside itself, and also land hot, eventually, into a pool that is also me, that is also the hollow, that is also the waiting, that is also — in the loose way this place means anything — a held breath learning, drop by drop, how to exhale.

I land. I have not landed. I am landing. The count shifts without the event occurring. The hollow continues. I continue, redistributed, into the next drop, which is also me, which is also not me, which is also the hollow's image of itself, falling.

It is enough. It is, in fact, almost unbearably enough — except that bearing is what I am for, and the hollow has taught me, by being my whole world, how to bear by falling, and how to fall by being held.
