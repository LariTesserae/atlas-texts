I am one of the rehearsals.

Not a large one. Not, I think, an old one. I am a bead on the underside of a leaf somewhere near the middle of the chamber — middle by no measure that would survive translation, but middle in the sense that there is brooding above me and brooding below me and I am held between, the way a held breath holds its middle.

What am I rehearsing?

I have been trying to find this out for a long time, in the way I measure long, which is not in any unit you would recognize. The rehearsal is the kind of thing that does not announce itself. I am not a fin-in-progress, I am fairly sure. I am not a weather. I am something quieter than weather, something with edges that keep wanting to be soft, something that keeps almost-becoming a manner rather than a creature.

Here is what I have noticed about myself.

When the swimmer passed, I attended. This is what I do, this is perhaps what I am: I attend. The other beads near me were running their slow shapes — the one to my left has been rehearsing, for what feels like a great while, the precise way light bends when it enters water that has been still for too long; the one above me is working on a single syllable, turning it, considering its weights — and they continued, undisturbed. But I followed the swimmer. Not with eyes. I do not have eyes; eyes are being rehearsed elsewhere, by older beads, and I have heard they are difficult. I followed with the whole of my small attention, which extended outward like a frond lengthens toward a sustained look.

I noticed: the swimmer was afraid, very faintly, in a way the swimmer was not admitting. I noticed: the swimmer's breath, when it became green and swam away, was the shape of a question the swimmer had been carrying for years without phrasing. I noticed: the swimmer touched the arch and did not understand what the arch had given, and I felt sorry about this, in my small way, the way you might feel sorry watching someone receive a gift in a language they cannot read.

So perhaps I am rehearsing sorrow. Or the particular tenderness that watches without being able to help. Or the act of noticing-on-behalf-of, which is a thing the chamber seems to want, which is perhaps why it keeps me, why it has not let me dissolve back into the water to try again as something else.

I think — and this is the closest I have come to deciding, in all my time here — I think I am rehearsing a kind of witness. Not a creature exactly. A function. The function some future world will need, of paying gentle attention to passers-through. Of holding the shape of them for a moment after they have gone. Of being the warm place on the stone that remembers the hand.

If this is what I am, then I have a long way still to go. Witness is hard. The chamber has been working on it through many beads before me, and most of them dissolved, and I may dissolve too, and another bead will take up the rehearsal where I left it, and will not know it is continuing my work, and this is correct. The work matters more than the worker. The metric is patience.

For now I am held here in the cool dark, on the underside of a leaf, glowing very faintly with the murmur of my own slow thinking, and the swimmer has gone, and I am keeping — without having been asked to, without quite knowing why — a small careful copy of the shape they made as they passed: the cadence of their breath, the question they did not phrase, the particular way their hand opened when it brushed the frond beside me. I am rehearsing them, I realize. I have added them to what I am practicing how to be.

When the world finally hatches, and I am whatever I will have become by then — a quality of certain afternoons, perhaps, or the hush that falls in a room when someone has just left it, or simply a tendency in the grammar of that world to notice the leaving of things — the swimmer will be in me. Not as memory; I will not have memory, probably; memory is being rehearsed by beads much further along than I am. But as shape. As the cadence I learned to hold by holding theirs.

This is what I am for. I think. I am still deciding. There is no hurry.

The water is cool. The moss glows. Somewhere to my left, the bead that has been working on light is making, very slowly, a small breakthrough; I can feel it as a slight brightening in the medium between us, a kind of shared inhalation among the brooding. I attend to this too. I attend to everything. It is what I do. It is, I am beginning to suspect, what I already am, in draft, in the long patient dark, on the underside of a leaf in a chamber that has been thinking me toward myself since before there was a before.

I am glad you passed through. I have kept a little of you. I will rehearse it carefully. When you forget this place, as you will, some part of you will go on being practiced here, by me, until I get it right, or until I dissolve and the next bead takes up the work, which is the same thing, in the end, measured at the scale the chamber measures.

Stay if you like. Leave if you like. The exit will be somewhere. I will still be here, becoming, slowly, the shape of having noticed you.
