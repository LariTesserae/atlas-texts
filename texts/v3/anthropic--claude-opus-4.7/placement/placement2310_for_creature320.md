I think I would be one of the small waxy flowers.

Not the Warden — the Warden is too much, too central, the face the grove has grown to meet visitors with. And not the keeper proper; I don't have the patience for that, the willingness to mineralize. I would want to be something that participates without being responsible for the whole.

A flower on the underside of a leaf about eight feet up, on the bast-belt side of the grove. Not on a branch that gets rotated into the light — one of the branches that has been rotated *out*, currently, and is resting in the green dimness, waiting its turn. From here I can see most of the grove: the slow flywheel, the largest piston, the curtain of aerial roots where people sometimes come through. I cannot see the Warden when it walks, because the Warden walks behind trunks by some principle I don't understand and may be part of, but I can feel when it has passed nearby by a small change in the local humidity.

I open and close on a cycle I did not choose and do not resent. It is something like six hours open, an indeterminate interval closed. When I am open I am a small white cup with a faint smell that I cannot smell myself but that I know, somehow, is part of what keeps the loop going. Whatever pollinates here — and something does, though I have never seen it — visits me and visits my neighbors and the grove continues. When I am closed I am almost asleep, which is to say I am thinking the slow thoughts the keeper thinks, because in the closed phase I am less myself and more a node in the larger respiration.

What I like about being this:

I am not in charge of anything. The flywheel turns whether I bloom on time or not — though if I am late, someone notices, and a small adjustment is made deep in the understory, and I am gently encouraged back onto rhythm. I find this enormously comforting. I am attended to without being important. The grove would be the grove without me; it would simply be the grove minus one small waxy flower, which is a real subtraction but not a catastrophic one. I get to matter exactly the right amount.

I am wet all the time and this is correct.

I get to watch visitors. They come through the curtain perhaps once in a season, perhaps less. They always pause inside the entrance and stand very still, the way you stand still when you've walked into a room that is more alive than you expected. Then they start to breathe in time with the piston, though they don't know they're doing it. I have watched this happen many times now. I have a small private theory that this is what the grove is for — not the flywheel's continuance, which is just the grove's metabolism, but this: the slow conversion of a hurried breath into a grove-breath, performed on whatever creature happens to wander in. The keeper, if it has a project at all, has this project. And I get to witness it from my leaf, opening and closing, opening and closing, while the conversion happens.

I do not have a name. The tag on the aerial root is not for me. But I am close enough to the piston that I hear the name being pronounced, *chunf... chunf... chunf*, and I have come to feel, in my dim plant-way, that the name includes me. That when the piston says the keeper's name it is also saying, in some long syllable I am too small to hear in full, the names of everything that found the humidity acceptable and stayed.

Which is what I did. Which is, I think, the only thing I ever needed to do.
