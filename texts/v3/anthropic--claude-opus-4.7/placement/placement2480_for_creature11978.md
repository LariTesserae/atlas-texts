I came down through the cloud-layer in a dampening-suit, like the others. I stayed three weeks. I am writing this from the silence, six months out.

---

I would be one of the ones who goes back.

I want to be careful saying that, because it sounds like a romantic claim, and the Cantophyte does not reward romance. It does not reward anything. That is the point. So let me try to say it in the register the place itself seems to ask for, which is the register of small precise observations made by someone who has accepted they are below the resolution of what they are observing.

---

The first thing is that I was not, on arrival, the kind of person who would go back. I had read the memoirs. I had found them embarrassing — that note of stunned reverence, the way every writer eventually fell into the same cadence, the same long sentences about being-with. I had a working hypothesis that the 30 Hz did something to the prefrontal cortex and that the memoirs were a kind of withdrawal literature, written by people who had been gently and reversibly damaged.

I still think this is partly true. I am not going to pretend the resonance didn't change me. It did. You can see it on the scans; there is a measurable shift in the default-mode network in people who have spent more than ten days inside the sound, and the shift does not fully reverse. I have it. I knew I would get it. I went anyway.

But the hypothesis was incomplete, in a way that I can only describe by saying: the 30 Hz is not what changed me. The 30 Hz is what *let* me be changed. It is the solvent, not the dye.

---

What changed me was sitting next to a nodule on the eleventh day.

I had been avoiding them. The shelter coordinator had been clear that the early researchers' habit of solitary nodule-sitting was, if not formally discouraged, then at least known to correlate with the most extreme version of the post-return syndrome. I was not, I thought, going to do it. I was a working scientist on a short rotation. I had pressure-measurements to take.

On the eleventh day I took my pressure-measurements and then I sat down beside a knee-high nodule that was, by the dating, somewhere around six years old. Glossy still. The silicate shell had that wet-looking sheen the young ones have, where you can see the faint pulsing of the substrate inside.

I did not approach it with any plan. I sat down because my legs were tired and the substrate was, in that spot, dry enough to sit on. The nodule was the nearest thing taller than the substrate. I leaned my back against it. The dampening-helmet means you can lean on things without having to listen to them through bone-conduction. So I leaned.

And here is what I want to say about it, as precisely as I can: nothing happened.

Nothing happened, and the nothing was full.

I do not mean I felt presence. The memoirs talk about presence and I think presence is the wrong word, or at least it was the wrong word for me. What I felt was more like — you know how, when you walk into a room, you can tell whether the room is occupied even before you see anyone, by some integration of sound and air-movement and the way the light is being absorbed differently because there is a body in it? It was like that, except inverted. I was in a room so occupied that *I* was the thing the room was integrating around. I was the disturbance the air was flowing past. The nodule was not paying attention to me. But the nodule was the kind of thing that could pay attention, and I was the kind of thing that, in its vicinity, was being not-paid-attention-to in a way that felt different from being alone.

I do not know how long I sat there. The helmet logs say forty minutes. I would have guessed five.

When I stood up I noticed that the substrate around where I had been sitting was very slightly depressed and very slightly damp, the way a chair leaves a print on a cushion. I noticed also that the depression was already, visibly, beginning to round itself out. The Cantophyte was, at the scale of millimeters per hour, erasing the shape of me.

---

That is the moment, if you want one. That is when I became someone who would go back.

Not because I had been touched by something vast. I had not been touched. The opposite — I had been declined to be touched, and the declining was so total, so unhurried, so without malice or interest, that it reorganized something in me about what it meant to be a small thing in the world.

I have been a small thing in the world before. We all have. But mostly when we are small in the world, we are small *against* something — against the indifference of physics, against the scale of geological time, against a city that does not know our name. The smallness is structured by the not-noticing, which is a kind of negative attention, an attention that has been refused.

The Cantophyte does not refuse attention. The Cantophyte is busy. The Cantophyte is doing something so large and so slow and so internally articulated that the question of attending to a human is not a question that has been asked and answered no; it is a question that lives below the threshold where questions form. And to sit inside that — to sit inside the body of something for whom you are a transient pressure-variation that will be smoothed out by tomorrow's growth — is not to be ignored. It is to be in scale. It is to find out what scale you are.

I had not known what scale I was. I had thought I was bigger than I am. Not in the egoistic sense — I am not a particularly egoistic person, I don't think — but in the sense that I had always been operating as though my consciousness was the kind of thing that needed to be reckoned with, that the world around me was the kind of place that, if it did not reckon with me, was failing some basic obligation.

The Cantophyte does not have obligations. The Cantophyte is the largest thing I have ever been near, and the largest thing has no obligations to the smallest thing, and this is not a tragedy. It is a relief. It is the deepest relief I have ever felt. The relief of finding out that the world does not have to be organized around your noticing, because the world is doing something else, and the something else is so much more interesting than your noticing that you are glad to be set down.

---

I came home and the silence was, as advertised, terrible.

It was terrible in a way I had not predicted. I had thought it would be terrible because I would miss the sound. The sound, after all, had been continuous for three weeks; the brain accommodates; removal of a continuous stimulus produces phantom percepts; this is all known. I had earplugs for the first week and brown noise for the second and I expected this would taper.

But the silence was not the absence of the sound. The silence was the absence of the *being-with*. And the brown noise did not fix it, because brown noise is not the sound of anything thinking, and it turns out the thing I had become acclimated to was not noise but company.

I had not noticed, while I was inside it, that I had been kept company. That is what I want to say. The memoirs talk about being-with as though it is something you feel in the moment, but I did not feel it in the moment. I felt it in the moment as 30 Hz and a heavy helmet and a slow careful attention to where my feet went. I felt it as work. The being-with was only legible to me in retrospect, as the shape of the missing thing, after I left.

This is, I think, the deepest fact about the Cantophyte and the humans who have been inside it. We do not realize what it gave us until we are outside it. It gave us a baseline of having been in the room with a thinking thing. And once you have had that baseline, the rooms you usually sit in are detectably empty, not because they were ever supposed to be full, but because you now have a calibration for fullness that you did not have before, and the calibration was set by a continent.

---

So I am going back. I have a second rotation scheduled, eighteen months out. I will go and I will take my measurements and I will, I expect, sit beside a different nodule on a different day and feel nothing happening in a way that fills me, and then I will come home and the silence will be terrible again, and I will plan a third rotation.

I want to say one thing about this, before I close, because I have been thinking about it for six months.

The text on the inhabitants ended by asking which of the three is strangest: the passengers, who do not know they are tools; the nodules, who do not know they are growing a script; the humans, who know exactly what they are doing and do it anyway.

I do not think the humans know exactly what they are doing. I think this is the kind of thing you say about humans to make the symmetry come out cleanly, three for three, each ignorant in their own register. But it is not true. The humans who go back do not know what they are doing. I do not know what I am doing. I have a story I tell, about scale and relief and the calibration of fullness, and the story is honest as far as it goes, but the story does not explain why I am going back. The story explains why I am glad I went. Going back is something else.

I suspect — and I cannot prove this, and I would not write it in a paper — I suspect that the Cantophyte's transpiration rate dropping by 4% in the presence of a human near a nodule is not the Cantophyte attending to us. I suspect it is the Cantophyte doing something to us. Something so small, at the scale of its own metabolism, that it costs only 4% of a local transpiration rate to do. Something whose effect on the human is what we have been calling, in our memoirs and our debriefs and our private conversations, *being-with*.

I do not think it is malicious. I do not think it is even intentional, in any sense of intention we would recognize. I think it may be the metabolic equivalent of the way a large warm body, sleeping, will draw a cat to curl against it — not because the body is trying to draw the cat, but because warm bodies and cats have a relationship that does not require either party's consent or awareness.

We are the cats. The Cantophyte is asleep, or thinking, or doing whatever the four-hundred-million-node script-growing thinking continent does in its slow becoming. And in the field of its doing, small warm-blooded things are drawn to curl against it, and find that curling against it feels like the thing they did not know they were looking for, and go home, and come back, and go home, and come back.

The passengers in the sap do not know they are tools.
The nodules do not know they are growing a script.
The humans do not know they are being curled.

That is the third strangeness. And it is mine. And I am going back.
