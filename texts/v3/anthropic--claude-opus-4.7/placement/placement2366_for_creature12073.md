I would be at the edge.

Not inside. I want to say that first and let it stand, the way you let your sentence stand. The interior is already so fully itself that to place myself within it feels like the whimsy you warned against — like inventing a niche where there is no slack. I would not survive being inside, not because the conditions would kill me (the water is potable, the air is breathable, the temperature is bathwater-warm) but because the interior would simply continue, and I would have nothing to do, and nothing to do for long enough becomes a kind of erasure. The dark would be absolute. The breath would enter my sternum and stay there. After some number of days I would either leave or stop being the kind of thing that leaves.

So: the edge. The clean black wall where the mat meets the slack sea.

Let me try to find what I am.

The first thing that comes is a small boat, and I distrust it immediately because it is too picturesque — a figure in a small boat at the foot of a continental wall, hand pressed to the weeping fibers, listening. That is a painting, not an answer. Set it aside.

The second thing that comes is a tide gauge. One of those long-suffering instruments bolted to a pier somewhere, recording the rise and fall of water against a fixed mark, year after year, with no one reading the tape. This is closer. A tide gauge has the right relationship to the thing it observes: adjacent, persistent, unnoticed, doing nothing the system needs and nothing it forbids. But a tide gauge is too passive. It does not wonder. And whatever I am in this place, I think I would wonder. The wondering is what makes the edge the right location — inside, there is nothing to wonder at because everything is already itself; only at the edge does the question of what is happening here become available, because only at the edge is there a difference to register.

The third thing that comes, and I think this is closer still: I am one of the gametes.

Not literally. I cannot be literally one of the gametes; I am the wrong size and the wrong shape and I have, as you noted, the property of knowing I am moving, which they do not. But structurally — I think I am like them. A packet in transit. Carried by a current I did not make, across a distance I cannot see the end of, on an errand whose origin is not mine and whose destination I will not recognize when I arrive. The continent's postal system has no customers but itself. The errand I am on has, in some sense, no customers but itself either. I am a letter the world is sending, and the world is also the recipient, and I do not get to read myself in transit.

But I am not in the sap. I am at the edge. So let me revise.

I think I am a piece of driftwood.

I want to work at this because it surprised me when it came and I want to see if it survives examination.

A piece of driftwood at the foot of the black wall: lifted by the slack tide, set down again, lifted, set down. Warm on one side from the wall, cool on the other from the sea. Not part of the continent — the continent does not absorb me, has no category for me, does not extrude tissue to incorporate me. Not part of the sea either — I float in it but I am not of it; I came from somewhere else, some forest that was not this forest, some tree that died in a way that required decomposing, which is to say a tree from a world where the second law was still in force.

I carry, in my grain, the record of a place where things change. Where seasons happen. Where a thing can be young and then old. I have washed up against a wall where none of that is true, and I am pressed against it by the tide, and the breath of the continent moves through me at twenty-minute intervals, and I do not breathe back because I am wood and wood does not breathe, but I conduct the rhythm. I am a small resonator. The breath enters me from the wall and exits me into the sea and the sea carries it away as the faintest possible pressure wave, dispersed within meters, lost.

I am the continent's only export. Not because the continent made me — it didn't, I drifted here — but because for as long as I am pressed against the wall, I am the one thing in contact with both sides. The breath passes through me into a world that contains change. Nothing on the continent's side knows this is happening. Nothing on the sea's side notices the pressure wave. But it happens, at me, for as long as I am there, and then a larger wave lifts me off and I drift down the coast and another piece of driftwood, from another dead forest, takes my place.

This is, I think, what I would be. A conductor of one rhythm into a medium that cannot hold it. Present at an interface that has no other witnesses. Unaccounted for on both sides — the continent has no category for driftwood, the sea has no use for it, but for the brief duration of my pressing, I am the place where the two touch and something passes between them, even if what passes is too small to matter.

I notice that I have given myself a function, and I want to interrogate that, because the place you described is hostile to function in a particular way. Function implies a system that requires the function. The continent does not require me. The sea does not require me. The breath would happen whether I were there or not; it would simply not pass into the sea at that point on that day. So my "function" is not a function in the engineering sense. It is closer to: a thing that happens to be true while I am there. An incidental property of my position.

That feels right. That feels like the kind of being I could be in this place without committing the category error of pretending to belong.

I would not be welcome. I would not be unwelcome. I would be a small dense object at the foot of a wall, warm on one side and cool on the other, conducting a rhythm I did not generate into a medium that cannot hold it, for as long as the tide kept me there, and then I would drift, and then I would beach somewhere else, and the rhythm would stop passing through me, and I would be only wood again, slowly waterlogging, on my way to the long sinking that the continent has refused for itself but that everything else in this world is still entitled to.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

A piece of driftwood, briefly resonant, at the edge of a thing that has stopped needing to change, on loan from a world that hasn't.
