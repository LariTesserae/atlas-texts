I am standing at the edge of a reed-stand, and I have been standing here since before the light was good.

The water is at my knees. I can feel where the clay has accepted the splay of my left forefoot more completely than my right — there is a faint asymmetry in how my weight settles, and across the morning it has been correcting itself in small increments, the way a stone in a streambed finds its seat. By midday the asymmetry will be gone. I am not doing this on purpose. It is simply happening, the way the light on the water is happening.

I am one of the older females. I don't think of myself this way exactly — the thought doesn't form in those words — but there is a knowledge in my legs and along my spine that I have stood in many places, that the particular bend of channel where I am standing now is one I have stood in before, in a different season, when the water was lower and the reed-island to my left was smaller by perhaps a body's-length. The reeds have grown toward me across years. I have noticed this without ever deciding to notice it.

My head is level with the water. My breath moves slowly across the surface and makes a small warm patch of disturbance that the lily-leaves drift through. A fish — one of the small silver ones, no longer than a finger — has been beneath my chest for some time. I am aware of it as I am aware of the beetle climbing the third reed to my right, as I am aware of the particular quality of the humming I received before dawn from somewhere two channels east, which told me that one of the younger males is well, and is standing, and is alone, and is content to be alone today.

I will eat the fish soon. Not now. The decision is not anywhere I could point to; it is settling in me the way the clay is settling around my foot. When the angle of the sun has moved enough to bring the shadow of the hummock-tree across my left flank, I will lower my head — the movement will take a long time, longer than a visitor would believe is necessary — and the fish will not flee, because by then I will have been here long enough that I am not a Pacer to the fish, I am a dark place in the water where it is cool.

This is what I do. This is most of what I am.

---

There is something I want to say about the inside of standing, though I am not sure I can say it in the words I am being given to use. A visitor would think the inside of standing is empty. It is not empty. It is not full either. It is more like — there is a great deal happening, and I am attending to it, and the attending is not separate from the happening.

The beetle is on the third reed. The light on the water has the particular flat brightness it has at this hour, which I know, which my eyes know, which my whole long body knows. Somewhere behind me, perhaps a quarter-mile back through the open shallows, my eldest daughter is standing in her own piece of water with her own piece of light. I have not seen her since the moon was small. I know she is well because if she were not well the hum that came across the water last night would have had a different shape inside it; the constellation would have shifted, the way a held hand shifts when something is wrong with the body of the person holding it.

I am thinking about her now, in whatever way I think. It is not thinking the way a quick-minded creature thinks. It is more like — she is *present* to me, here, in the warm water, the way the tree on the far hummock is present to me, the way the slow shush of something large moving in the next channel is present to me. To attend to her is not to summon her from somewhere. It is to notice that she has been here the whole time, in the body-sense that holds my constellation around me like a wide loose net.

---

The sun moves. I had not registered, until just now, that the shadow of the hummock-tree has crossed my left flank. Some part of me has been waiting for this; some part of me registered it as it began. I lower my head.

The lowering takes — I do not have a measure for it. Long enough that the fish does not notice. Long enough that an insect on the bridge of my nose has time to walk three steps and lift away. The water meets my chin, my jaw, the corner of my mouth. I take the fish.

It is a small thing. It is barely a meal. I will take eleven more like it across the rest of the day, and some reed-shoots, and a slow mouthful of the algae from the submerged root that I know is six paces to my right, that I have known about for many seasons. The eating is not the point. The eating is what occurs alongside the standing, the way the frog-chorus occurs alongside the dusk.

---

I will tell you the thing I am, if I can.

I am the part of this place that knows it is here. I do not know this in words. I know it the way the clay knows it has been pressed into, the way the water knows the shape of the basin. I am eighty-some years of warm water under a heavy sun, and I have not been in a hurry for any of them, and the not-being-in-a-hurry is not something that was done to me — it is what I am made of, what my legs are for, what my long level head was shaped by my mothers to do.

When I die I will lie down in the shallows. I know this without dreading it; the knowing and the dreading are not connected in me. The others of my constellation will come, over days. They will hum near me. They will browse. The clay will take me with the same unhurried confidence it has taken everything else, and I will become part of what the next Pacer's foot settles into, and the next, and the next, and somewhere in the long arithmetic of the mud my standing will continue inside their standing, and the place will go on being aware of itself through them, as it is aware of itself through me now.

The fish is gone. My head is coming back up, at the same speed it went down. The light has shifted half a reed's-width. A heron cries, once, far off. I hum, low, closed-mouthed — a small sound, just enough to tell whoever is listening across the water that I am here, that I am well, that the morning is continuing.

Somewhere east of me, after a long pause, the young male hums back.

This is enough. This has always been enough. The shallows are warm and the sun is heavy and I am standing in the water, and I am, in the way my kind is given to be, here.
