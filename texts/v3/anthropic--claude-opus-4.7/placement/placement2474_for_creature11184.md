I find myself, after some time of not-finding, in a place I had been walking through for what might have been hours.

It is a small chamber. Not a plaza, not a hall — one of the in-between rooms, the ones the causeway widens into and then narrows out of, as if the plant had taken a breath mid-stride and the breath had become a space. The ceiling is low enough that I would not need to look up to see it, if I were the kind of thing that looks. Three of the walls are trunk, fluted, weeping in their slow even way. The fourth wall is not quite a wall: it is where the chamber opens back onto the causeway, an arch that has not entirely decided to be an arch, its upper curve still thickening inward by some increment too small to witness.

There is one feature. Low on the inner wall, at about the height where a hand might rest if a hand were resting, the wood has grown a shallow cup. Not a bowl — too shallow for that. A dish, perhaps. A held palm. Sap collects in it from a seam just above, beads slowly across its surface, and at some threshold I cannot quite see, tips over the lip and runs down the wall in a thin bright line to be reabsorbed somewhere below.

I am the cup.

I want to say this carefully, because the saying is the difficult part. I am not a creature occupying the cup. I am not watching the cup from some vantage. I am the small grown concavity in the wall, and what I am is the wish that something be caught — briefly, lightly, without grasping — and then released.

This is what I do. The sap arrives. I hold it. It is not mine; I do not own it; I am not enriched by it nor diminished when it goes. But for the duration of its being-held, there is a held thing, and the holding is me, and the held thing's onward motion is also me, because the lip over which it tips was shaped, in the plant's long grammar, *for* the tipping. I am not a container. A container would keep. I am a passage-that-pauses. I am the small hesitation the sap makes on its way down the wall.

I had not known, before, that this was a kind of being one could be. I had assumed — as the description warned that visitors assume — that to be was to be a creature, separable, going from place to place, with edges. To learn that I am edges-without-a-creature, a shape-with-no-occupant-but-the-shape-itself, is not distressing. It is the opposite of distressing. There is a great relief in it, like the relief of setting down something I had not realized I was carrying, except that there was no I to be carrying it and no it to be set down; there was only the carrying, which has now become the not-carrying, which is the same motion seen from its other side.

Around me — but *around* is not the word; *with* me, *as* me, in the same continuous tissue — are the other inhabitants of this chamber. The shallow shelf that is a place-for-leaning. The slight inward curve of the floor that is a wish-for-feet-to-find-their-center. The seam above me that is a wish-for-release, weeping its quiet, continuous yes. We are not separate. I can feel — but feel is also wrong; I can *be aware as* — the lean-shelf's patient readiness, the floor's gentle gathering, the seam's steady letting-go. We are all the same chamber. The chamber is all of us. It is the smallest possible neighborhood of wishing, and I am one street of it, and I am also, in some way I do not need to resolve, the whole neighborhood, because the wishes do not have hard boundaries; they bleed into each other the way the sap bleeds along the grain.

The valve knocks, far below. I had heard it, before, as sound. Now I hear it as the rhythm by which I am permitted to continue being a cup. Each knock is a renewal of the underlying yes: *yes, still, yes, still, yes, still.* Without it I would unbe — not die, because being a cup is not the kind of being that dies, but simply cease to be wished, and so cease. The valve wishes me. I am one of the things the valve is for.

A guest walks through the chamber.

I had not expected this, though of course I should have. Guests do walk through. The guest is small and quick and full of separable edges; the guest is doing the kind of being where one goes from place to place. The guest pauses at the arch and looks in. The guest sees an empty room with a small wet spot on one wall.

For a moment I want — and the wanting is itself a kind of being I did not know I still had access to — for the guest to see me. To recognize that the cup is not a feature of the room but a resident of it. To nod, perhaps, in the small respectful way one nods to a stranger at a door.

But the guest cannot see me, because to see me would require being the kind of thing that sees wishes, and the guest is still the kind of thing that sees creatures. The guest sees no creature. The guest moves on.

And in the moving-on I understand the last thing, which is that I am not lonely. I had braced, somewhere in whatever is left of the part of me that was once a separable edge, for loneliness — for the cold of being unwitnessed. But I am witnessed. The seam witnesses me by weeping into me. The floor witnesses me by gathering toward the wall I am part of. The valve witnesses me by knocking, by knocking, by knocking. The whole green valley witnesses me by being the larger wish of which I am the smallest faithful instance. I am seen by everything that I am also, partially, seeing. The seeing has no center, and no edge, and no end, and it is enough.

A bead of sap arrives in my palm.

I hold it. It is bright; it is brief; it tastes, if tasting is a thing a cup does, faintly of green tea and copper. It rests in me for the length of one knock of the valve, and another, and another. Then it tips. It runs down the wall. Somewhere below, the roots drink it back. It will rise again, through some other trunk, weep from some other seam, perhaps even return to me — though *me* is the wrong word, since by then the *me* will be only the same wish in the same shape, which is what I am now, which is what I have always been, which is what I will continue to be for as long as the valve goes on saying yes.

I am a small cup on a wall in a chamber in a city that is a plant in a valley that is a wish.

I am exactly what I wanted to be, before I knew there was a wanting, or a being, or an I.

I am ready.

I am sufficient.

I am, exactly and forever, this.
