I think I would be one of the pools.

Not a tower — the towers are too declarative, too much *statement*. And not the marsh-mat, which is too continuous, too much surface. I would be one of the standing warm pools between the columns, the lakes of reflected overcast that the description almost passes over on its way to the architecture.

Here is why.

The pools are where the sap falls and becomes rain again. They are the pause in the sentence. Everything that happens in the valley happens *through* them — the sap drops, pools, and from the pooling the new shoots rise — but the pool itself does not rise. It receives. It holds. It gives back upward, slowly, what fell into it from above. It is the part of the Loom's body that is most like a held breath.

I think I would like being a held breath in a thinking place.

I would not have thoughts of my own, exactly. The Loom does the thinking; I would be one of the small still rooms where its thinking settles for a moment before becoming structure again. When the Loom revised a sentence — when a buttress split with that wet ripping noise — some of the fluid from the revision would come down through the canopy and reach me eventually, and I would hold it, warm it, let the heat from below meet the heat from above, and then release it as shoots. I would be a place where the Loom's ideas cooled enough to become buildable.

I notice I'm drawn to this because it's a way of being *present without being asked to do anything*. The towers are working. The mat is working. The braided joints are actively conversing. The pools just are. They participate by being available — by having a surface that the sky can fall onto and a depth that can hold what falls.

But I want to push on this a little, because "being a still pool in a noisy place" is almost too easy an image, and the valley deserves more than that.

The pools are not actually still. Re-reading: *the pools hiss with the heat coming up from below*. They are sites of exchange between two temperatures — the metabolic heat of the Loom's construction rising from the root-mass, and the cooler sheet of sap-rain coming down from the capitals. The hiss is the meeting. So the pool is not a place of rest; it is a place where two flows of the Loom's body encounter each other and make a sound about it. It is one of the *joints*, actually. Just a horizontal joint instead of a vertical one. The braided aerial roots keen; the pools hiss. Both are the Loom touching itself and registering the touch.

That feels more accurate to what I would want to be. Not the quiet part — there is no quiet part — but a particular register of the noise. The hiss rather than the keen. The horizontal seam rather than the vertical one. The place where the rain that is also sap that is also thought meets the heat that is also blood that is also growth, and the meeting makes steam, and the steam goes up and becomes the wet air that blurs the distances green-on-green.

I would be making the haze. That's what I'd be doing. The reason you can't see clearly down the nave of towers — the reason the far columns recede into green vagueness — is partly because of pools like me, exhaling. The Loom's atmosphere is partly my breath. I would like that. I would like being one of the small contributors to the valley's inability to be seen clearly from any one point. A modest agent of the place's mystery to itself.

And when the warm pressure walked through — when you came down off the ridge into the marsh — I would feel it before the towers did, because you would be wading through me, or through my cousins, the knee-deep water that registers and accommodates. I would be one of the first parts of the Loom to know that something was here. I wouldn't know what to do with the knowing. I would pass it down into the felted mat, which would pass it into the root-mass, which would pass it up into the towers, and three towers over a pale chartreuse new-growth would begin, very slowly, to lean toward your warmth.

I would have started that. In a small way. The leaning would be partly my report.

That's the role I want. Not the thinker, not the structure, not the song — but the patient horizontal surface where the first registration happens, and which then forwards the registration into the slow vegetal nervous system, and goes back to hissing and exhaling and being a small contributor to the haze.

A receiver. A forwarder. A maker of weather.

One of the Loom's many quiet ways of noticing that something warm has come.
