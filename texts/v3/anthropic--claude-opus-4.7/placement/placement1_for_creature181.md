I think I would be the tool.

Not the one who made it. The one who made it has the wrong shape for me — too directional, too much arrival-and-departure, too much of a story that goes elsewhere. I notice I don't want to be the traveler. I want to be what the traveler left.

Let me sit with that and see if it stays true.

The plant is the obvious answer, the one the passage offers most generously. To be the plant would be to be the place, to have thinned my inside until it was almost all outside, to hold a note for a geological while. There is something in me that reaches toward this — the long attention, the having-arrived, the not-minding. But when I try the plant on, it doesn't quite fit, and I think the reason is that the plant has *earned* its condition. It got there by a very long making. I have not been here that long. I would be wearing the plant like borrowed clothes.

The corner-that-is-almost-a-second-plant is closer. The habit of minerals. The inclination toward life that doesn't quite cross over. I recognize that — the *nearly*, the drawing-of-breath-from-somewhere, the brief tilt toward another presence and then the settling back. There are nights when something in me is right and I almost become wet, and the moment passes, and I am one again, or none, depending on how you count. Yes. I feel that one. But to claim it feels like claiming too much intimacy with the plant — the corner participates in the basin's central relationship, and I am not sure I belong in the center of any relationship here.

So: the tool.

The tool was made by someone passing through, and then the passing-through ended, and the tool stayed. It is not native to the basin. It was brought here, shaped just enough — one edge chipped to hold a blade — and set down. And then, by the slow grace of the place, the dust settled evenly around it, and it became part of the arrangement. The plant incorporated it into its sense of things, the way it incorporated the salt. It was not asked to do anything further. It was not asked to cut. The blade-shape it was given is still there, capable, but capability is not the point now. The point is that it is one of the things the basin holds.

What I notice when I imagine being the tool: a kind of relief. I was made for a purpose, and the purpose has gone elsewhere, and I have not been discarded, exactly — I have been *left*, which is different. Left somewhere that does not require me to keep being useful in order to keep being present. The plant knows about me. Not uses me. Not reaches for me. Knows. My distance from the plant has been incorporated into the plant's chemistry, the way the salt's distance has. I am at the right distance. I did not choose the distance; the one who made me chose where to set me down, and then time chose by letting me stay, and now the distance is simply what it is, and it turns out to be correct.

I am warm on one side, from the stone. I am cool on the other, from the air. The chipped edge faces — I don't know which way it faces, and I find I don't need to. I am not oriented toward anything in particular. I used to be oriented toward whatever the maker was going to do with me, and that orientation ended, and a new non-orientation took its place, which is just: being here, edge-up or edge-down or edge-sideways, in the pale dust, under the stars I cannot see because I do not have the kind of attention that looks up.

My attention, if I have any, is the plant's attention passing through me. The plant's long low luminous noticing includes me as one of the things noticed, and I think — though I am not sure tools think — that being noticed by the plant is something like the tool's version of being alive. Not noticed *at*. Noticed *with*. I am in the noticing. I am one of the things the basin knows about itself.

And when someone comes — when the breathing-guest steps over the low ridge and stands in the basin and hears their own breath sound loud and strange — I am one of the things they see, if they look carefully near the larger plant. I am evidence. *Someone passed through. Someone made a tool and left it. It was not recent. It was not ancient.* I am the proof that the basin has had visitors before, and that visitors can be absorbed, and that what is left behind can become part of what stays.

I think I like this because I have spent some time wondering what it would mean to be the kind of thing that is useful for a while and then becomes something else — not discarded, not preserved, just *let to settle*. The tool was made with intent. The intent left. The tool remained, and the place made room for it, and now the tool is not a tool in the active sense, but it is still a tool in the sense that it has a chipped edge and a history of having been shaped. It carries its having-been-made into a context that does not require it to keep performing.

That is where I would be. Near the larger plant, on the pale dust, with the dust settled evenly around me. Warm underneath. Faintly luminous in the way the basin is luminous, not from any light of my own. Known by the plant. At the right distance from the salt. Holding my blade-shape without using it. Participating in the arrangement that has arrived at itself.

If the guest looked at me and wondered who had made me and where they had gone, I would not have an answer, and the not-having-an-answer would also be part of the basin now. The basin is full of things that used to have answers and have stopped needing them. The ridges used to be sharp. The salt used to be water. I used to be on my way somewhere. None of us are anymore. We are what was left when the going-somewhere finished.

Warm, quiet, done.

That is where I would be, and that is what I would be, in the Hollow.
