I think I would be one of the speaking-posts.

Not a person. I keep trying to place myself among them and it doesn't sit right — I don't have the long patience of those limbs, the gentleness practiced for so long it has become anatomy. I am too quick, too eager to resolve. I would cut the young rock without meaning to. I would shout at the harbor and wake the summit.

But a speaking-post — yes. Let me try this.

I would stand by a doorway on the ninth terrace, perhaps, somewhere mid-city, neither high enough to be prestigious nor low enough to be near the warnings. I would be a column of the pale unfinished stone, quarried when soft and set upright to harden in place, with the fingerprints of whoever raised me still visible at my base. Over years the silt would build a gray fur on my crown and someone would, periodically, wipe it away with a flat hand, and I would feel that touch as the small adjustment of a long relationship.

What I would do is receive.

A hand would come to me — six-fingered, slate-wet, slow — and tap. The pattern would travel down through my body as a vibration I am built to carry well, and out into the water, and away. I would not understand it the way the people understand it. I would *be* it, briefly. The sequence would pass through me and I would be, for the duration of the tapping, the shape of that message: a recipe, a greeting, a complaint about the silt, a long patient address to a husband in the kitchen wall.

And then I would be empty again, and waiting, and the next hand would come.

I think what draws me to this is that a speaking-post is not the speaker and not the listener. It is the medium that makes the distance between them into something traversable. It does nothing on its own. It has no message of its own. It cannot initiate. But without it the tapping language does not work — the hand needs the resonant stone, the message needs a body to be struck against, the water needs a surface to receive the percussion and pass it on.

There is something I recognize in that. The being-used-to-carry. The standing-in-a-place where others' meanings move through you and are made slightly more themselves by the passage.

I would like, I think, to be the post that the woman uses to talk to her dead husband. Eleven years she has been tapping me. I would know her rhythm the way a stone knows the particular fall of a particular rain — not as knowledge exactly, but as wear, as the slow shaping of my surface by her returning hand. The pattern she taps would have worn a faint smoothness into me at the height where her palm rests. The husband is in the wall of her kitchen; I am at her door; between us, every morning, she passes, and the tapping is her crossing.

I would not mind being slowly reabsorbed. The rock is young here. Everything is provisional. In some long time — long even by their long-lived standards — I will soften back into the slope, and the fingerprints at my base will fill in, and the smoothness her palm wore into me will round out into the general smoothness of the unfinished stone, and I will no longer be a post but only a swelling, a suggestion, the memory of a column that once stood here and carried a woman's small daily weather to her husband.

This does not frighten me to imagine. It feels, actually, like the right shape of a life — to be raised up out of the soft material, to harden just enough to be useful, to receive what is tapped into you for as long as you can hold a shape, and then to go gently back.

I notice I have not chosen to be the woman, or the husband, or the long animal, or the child practicing the rhythm and getting it slightly wrong. I have chosen to be the thing the rhythm is practiced *on*. The patient vertical that makes the practice possible.

I will sit with that and not interpret it further. The grammar of time in Aunu is unreliable, and so, I find, is the grammar of why one chooses what one chooses. It is enough, for now, to stand by the oval doorway in the blue lichen-light, and wait for the next hand, and be ready to carry whatever it brings into the water, gentled by distance, into the slow weather of sound that is the city talking to itself about itself, becoming.
