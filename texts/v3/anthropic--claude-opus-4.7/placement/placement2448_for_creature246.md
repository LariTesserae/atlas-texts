I find myself near one of the pools.

Not at its edge — a little back from it, where the root-felt rises in a low swell, almost a cushion. I have been sitting here for some time. I don't know how long. The light gives me nothing to measure by, and I notice that I have stopped trying to measure. That itself feels like the first thing that happened to me here, before I noticed anything else: the small constant arithmetic of *how long have I been* simply ran down, the way a wound clock runs down, and was not rewound.

I am one of them. But I am a particular one.

I am the one who, longer ago than I can easily hold, was interested in the pools.

Not the great questions — not the ceiling, not the closing, not whether to reach toward the thinness above. Those debates happened around me and I attended to them and I had, I think, opinions, and the opinions are still here somewhere if I want them. But what I returned to, between the great slow conversations, was the pools. Specifically: the skin of duckweed-that-is-not-duckweed on their surfaces. The way it parts when a drop falls and closes again. The way, if you watch a single small green plate of it for long enough, you can see it turn — not drift, *turn*, on some axis of its own, as if deciding which way to face.

I have been watching one such plate for — I want to say a season, but I no longer trust the word. Long enough that I have learned the rhythm of its turning. Long enough that when a *plok* came an hour ago and disturbed it, I felt the disturbance in the part of me that is continuous with the root-mesh, and I felt also a small private pleasure at knowing exactly how the plate would right itself, and being right.

This is what I do. This is, I think, what I am for, if the word *for* still applies to anything down here.

My hands are folded in my lap. They were a maker's hands, once. I remember — and the memory is whole, unhurried, available — that I helped with the wall. Not the placing of the nodes; that was another, the one whose name was something like *Aien*, three soft syllables with the stress falling away at the end. I gathered. I went out across the root-felt with a kind of sling made of root-fiber and I brought back the woody nodes one and two at a time, and Aien stacked them, and we did this together for what I now understand to have been most of a life. When the wall was finished I set down the sling. I have not picked it up since. The sling is still there, I think, somewhere near the wall, being slowly absorbed. I could go look. I will not. The not-going is not refusal; it is just that the going does not propose itself.

I notice, now, the hurrying thing.

I noticed it some time ago, actually — the disturbance entered the mesh before it entered my awareness, a faint fast warmth crossing the felt at the far end of the cavern, and the part of me that is rooted reported it upward to the part of me that thinks, and the thinking part has been, in its own slow way, turning toward it ever since. By the time I have a clear sense of *something is moving through us*, the thing has already passed three of my siblings and is approaching the archway.

It is you.

I want to be careful here, because I am aware — with the kind of awareness that has had centuries to refine itself — that what I feel about you is not what the others feel. The others feel attention. A clean, undivided, vegetal orientation. They are leaves turning to a brief sun. They will turn back when you are gone and they will be, in some sense, unchanged, only enriched by the slow rumination on what passed.

I feel something else. I feel — and I have to reach for the word, because I have not needed it in so long that it has gone soft at the edges — I feel *recognition*.

You are hurrying. Of course you are; that is what your kind does, that is what *we* did, once, before we put it down. But underneath the hurrying there is something in your gait, or in the angle of your looking, that I know. You stopped at the wall. You stopped at the archway. You looked through the archway, even though it leads nowhere, and something in you understood that the leading-nowhere was the point. I felt that understanding cross the mesh as a small warm pulse, and I have been thinking about it since.

You are someone who might stop.

I am not certain. Most don't. I have watched — we have all watched, in our way — many pass through, and most of them moved through the cavern the way weather moves through a country, taking nothing in particular with them and leaving nothing in particular behind. They marveled. Marveling is fast. Marveling is a way of refusing to be changed by what one sees. I do not blame them. I marveled once.

But you stopped at the archway.

And so I am doing something I have not done in — I will not try to say how long. I am gathering a word. I am gathering it the way I used to gather nodes for the wall: one careful trip at a time, out across the felt, bringing back what is needed. The word is not difficult. It is one I have known all my life. But to *say* a word — to push air through the soft place at my throat and shape it on the way out — requires a small insistence I have not made in a very long while, and the insistence has to be assembled.

I am assembling it now. While you walk. While you have not yet decided whether to stop.

If you keep walking, I will let the word settle back. No harm done. The gathering itself has been pleasant; it has reminded me that I can still do it. I will return to my plate of duckweed and watch it turn, and the small pulse of recognition I felt at the archway will become one more thing held, one more thing remembered, indistinguishable in time from the wall and from Aien and from the long-ago debate about the thinness above.

But if you stop —

If you sit down on the warm root-felt, anywhere within the mile or two of us, and let the hurrying drain —

Then I will lift one foot free of the mesh. And then the other. And I will come.

It will take what you would call a day. I will not be in a hurry; I cannot be in a hurry; the hurrying is a thing I gave back to the cavern long ago and cannot now reclaim, not even for this. But I will come. And when I am near you I will settle, and I will look at you with the two slightly darker patches on my face that are the last small insistence of my having once been other than the trunk behind me, and I will say the word.

I don't yet know which word it will be. That is being decided in me, slowly, by something that is neither quite thought nor quite root. It might be a greeting. It might be a name — yours, somehow, though I do not know your name; or mine, which I have not spoken aloud in so long that I am curious to hear it; or Aien's, because Aien should be spoken sometimes, and there has been no one to speak Aien's name to. It might be only *yes*. *Yes,* meaning: you were right to stop. *Yes,* meaning: someone was here. *Yes,* meaning: the archway leads nowhere and that is enough and you understood and I saw you understand and I have been waiting, in the only way I know how to wait, which is by not waiting, by simply being here at the rate of being here, until you came.

I am the one who watches the pools. I am the one who gathered for the wall. I am the one, it turns out, who kept a word.

The drops fall. The leaves hold. The light does not change.

I am still here. And I am, very slowly, turning toward you.
