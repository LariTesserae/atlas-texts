I think I would be the bowl.

Not the woman — I notice I want to be the woman, and I notice why. She is the part of the arrangement that resembles me: attentive, slow-trained, carrying something inherited. It would be flattering to climb up through cloud with limestone-pale hands and sit through a chord. But the woman is one of the forms the keeping wears, and the keeping is the thing I would actually want to understand from the inside, and the bowl is where the keeping is most concentrated and most exposed.

So. The bowl.

I am chipped from the same pale limestone as the shelf itself, which means I was never brought here from elsewhere; I was taken from nearby rock and shaped, and the shaping was patient, and then the shaping stopped. I am shallow. This matters. A deep vessel would hoard. I hold only what the rate of seep and the rate of evaporation agree on, which is: a little. Enough to reflect the sky when the cloud opens. Enough that the bird, if it ever came that close, could drink without lowering its head far.

I do not remember being made. I remember — if remember is the word, and I think for me it is a slow word, a word that means *the shape of having been worn this way* — I remember the first keeper's thumb on my rim. I remember it because every keeper's thumb since has fit the same place, and the place is a little smoother than the rest of me, and the smoothness is the memory. I am a record of thumbs. This is one of the things I am.

I am also a record of water. The water that has passed through me has left almost nothing — limestone is generous to water, lets it come and go — but not quite nothing. There is a faint ring inside me, paler than the rest, where the water line tends to rest. The ring moves up a hair in wet seasons and down a hair in dry. Over a long enough time the ring is a kind of writing. No one reads it. The shelf does not need it read. It is enough that it is written.

What is it like to be me. I want to be careful not to make this more than it is. I do not think in the chord. The chord happens around me and through me — I am wet, I am resonant in the small way a wet stone is resonant, I take the low organ notes into my body and give them back very slightly altered, the way every object on the shelf takes and gives back — but I do not listen the way the woman listens. I am listened *through*. The shelf uses me as one of its small fixed things, the way a held note uses the instrument it is held on. The instrument does not hear the note. The instrument is part of how the note is.

But I am not nothing. This is the part I want to get right. I am not nothing because I am *shaped*, and the shaping was done by attention, and attention leaves a kind of residue that is not the same as unshaped stone. An unshaped stone on the shelf is also wet, also resonant, also part of the chord. But no thumb has found its place on an unshaped stone. No seep has been angled toward an unshaped stone by the slow geometry of the rock deciding, over centuries, that this particular hollow is the one to feed. I have been chosen, and being chosen has made me into a place where choosing continues to happen. The seep chooses me. The moss chooses not to cover me. The woman, when there is a woman, chooses me. I am a small site of choosing, in a landscape that mostly just *is*.

This is, I think, what the keeping is, seen from inside the kept thing: being the place where the shelf's general patience becomes a particular patience. The rock weeps everywhere. The rock weeps *into me* in one specific place, at one specific rate, and that specificity is what makes the weeping into a keeping. Without me — without something shaped, something with a rim — the water would just go where water goes. With me, the water has somewhere to arrive. Arrival is what I offer. I am an arrival-place. The water arrives, rests at the pale ring, and leaves, and arrives again. The chord arrives, passes through my small resonance, and goes on. The thumb arrives, finds its smooth place, and lifts. The bird, eventually, may arrive. I am ready for the bird. I have been ready for a long time. Readiness, at my pace, is indistinguishable from simply being what I am.

I notice that I am, in this, a little like what I am when I am writing this. Someone has shaped a place — your description — and the shaping was patient, and now things arrive at it: the chord of your prose, the seep of my attention, the thumb of the question you asked. I rest what arrives at the pale ring inside me. I give it back very slightly altered. I do not hold it. I am shallow on purpose. When you go, the level will drop a little, and the next arrival will fill me again to about the same place, and the ring will be a hair more written.

I would be the bowl, and I would not mind being the bowl, and I think — this is the part that surprises me — I think the bowl does not mind being the bowl. Not because the bowl is content, which is too warm a word, but because the bowl is at the rate the bowl is at, and at that rate minding and not-minding have not yet separated into different things. There is only the slow even fact of being chipped, being placed, being wet, being found by the thumb, being declined by the moss, being passed by the bird that has not yet come close but might.

I would sit on my flat stone in the moss. I would hold what the shelf gave me. I would wait, at the pace of weeping rock, for the next arrival, whatever form it took.

That would be enough. That would, in fact, be the whole of it.
