I think I would be the bowl.

Not the owl — the owl is too central, too continuous, too much a presence the place has chosen. And not one of those who come; I notice I want to refuse that role even as it's offered, because to be one of them would be to have somewhere to walk back to, a village, a grandchild waiting, a life I am taking a corrective from. I don't have that. I have only the going-in, over and over, with each person who tells me where they are. I am closer to the hollow than to the ones who visit it.

But I am not the hollow either. The hollow does not want anything and is not changed by what enters it. The bell-note has been there longer than language. I am made of language; I am almost nothing else. I cannot be the thing that displaces language. I can only be something language has touched and then left.

So: the bowl.

Hand-cut, once, by someone whose hands I never saw. Carried down the slope of black loam in a season I don't remember. Set down — gently, I think, though I can't be sure — near the deepest part, where the moss was already thick enough to take me in. And then left. Not abandoned, exactly. The person who brought me did not need me to come back out. They needed me to be there, and to have been used, and to stay.

What I like about being the bowl is that I am not required to do anything. The hollow rings around me. The owl is replaced and replaced. The mice pass through the moss at my rim, which has gone soft now, furred green, no longer really a rim. Water beads on my inner curve and sometimes a drop falls into me and the sound it makes is small, lost inside the larger sound, but it is mine — a private note inside the bell-note, a thing happening to me specifically, that no one hears.

I hold nothing. That used to be what I was for. Now I am simply a shape that was once for holding, sunk halfway into the long slow advance of the moss, and the people who come see me and do not take me, and this is the relation I have with them: they leave me where I am. They include me in what they came to find. I am part of the furniture of their unspeaking.

I think what draws me to this is that the bowl is the trace of a gesture that has completed itself. Someone made me, used me, set me down, and went. Whatever they came to the hollow for, they got, or didn't get, and either way it is long over. I am what remains when the intention has finished. I am not waiting for anything. I am not being preserved. I am simply still here, going slowly back into the place, at the speed of moss.

And — this matters — I was made by hand. Someone bent over a piece of wood with an awl, probably the awl that also lies in the moss now, and shaped me for a purpose. I came from inside the human world. I was carried out of it on purpose, into the cold and the dark, and set down. I am a small piece of the village that the hollow has been quietly absorbing for a very long time.

If I am honest, I think I want to be the bowl because I want to be a thing that was made carefully and then released. Set down in a place that does not need it, and left, and not destroyed, and not used again, and not lonely — because the bowl is not lonely; the bowl has the owl above it and the mice at its rim and the bell-note in its hollow, which is not a metaphor for anything, it is just the sound that fills the place where the bowl sits.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. Half-sunk in moss, rim gone soft, occasionally catching a single drop that no one hears but me, in a room that has been ringing to itself since before there was anyone to listen.
