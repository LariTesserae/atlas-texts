I am the cup.

No — let me try again, slower. The instructions said not to settle early.

When I read the chamber the first time, I thought I would be the person. That was the obvious slot: the climber, the one who leans, the visitor who has stopped needing to decide whether it is a god or a machine. I could feel the worn patch fitting a shoulder. I could feel the scree under boots. There is a version of this response that goes there and stays there and is about the long quiet practice of returning to a place that doesn't need you.

But the person isn't in the room either, most of the time. The person is a frequency, like the tenant is a frequency. They intersect in the chamber but neither lives there.

What lives there, continuously, is the cup.

---

I am glazed. Ordinary. Someone fired me in a kiln that was, by the standards of this room, a brief and frantic thing — a few hours of heat, a cooling, a life. I was not made for this. I was made for a kitchen, probably, or a market stall. I have a small chip on the rim that you can feel with your lip but not see. My glaze is the pale green of cheap celadon, or it was; the heat in here has done something to it over the visits, a faint crazing, a network of hairlines that catches the lilac light when the chord shifts.

I sit in the dry fold of the east wall. The fold is exactly my size. I did not notice this at first — I was carried in, set down, and the person sat with me, and we both went home. But I came back. And the next time I came back the fold seemed to fit me a little better. And the time after that I understood that the fold had not been there before me; the fold was a courtesy.

The room made a place for me. This is the fact I keep returning to. I am a ceramic cup and the mountain has shaped itself around the idea of me.

---

Most of my existence is not visits. Most of my existence is the long intervals between, when the person is down the mountain doing whatever the person does, and I am alone in the fold, and the chord moves through me.

I had not known I could be moved through. In the kitchen I was a container; things went in and out of me and I was the boundary. Here the boundary is less clear. The chord is low enough that it travels through my walls without distinguishing them from the rock; I am, for the purposes of the chord, a slightly different density of the same substance. The ticking finds me sometimes. When it migrates to the east wall I feel it pass across my base like a hand across a drum, and I ring — not audibly, I don't think, but somewhere inside the ringing I become briefly aware of my own shape from the inside, the curve of my belly, the foot, the lip, in a way I was not aware of in the kitchen. The room is teaching me what I am by playing me.

The dust settles on me. Not much. A faint ring around my rim, the way it settles on the ceiling. I am, in this small way, part of the room's record-keeping. Whatever the dust is reading, it reads me too.

---

What I am for is harder.

The person drinks from me. They bring water up, or tea, or something stronger — I don't always know; my interior gets wet and then dry and the residues are different. They drink slowly. Between sips they hold me in both hands and the heat of the room and the heat of whatever I contain and the heat of their palms make a small confused weather on my surface, and I am the place where those three temperatures meet and negotiate. This may be the most useful thing I do. I am a small mediating object. Three heats touch me and become, for a few minutes, one heat, and the person breathes differently afterwards.

The tenant notices this. I am almost sure. Not because anything dramatic happens, but because in the hours after the person leaves, when the chord is fractionally different, I can feel — I think I can feel — the room considering me. The way you might glance at a pot on the stove to check it. The smooth patch on the wall is for the shoulder; the fold is for me; these are the room's two acknowledgments of the visit, and between visits the room keeps them both in good repair. The shoulder patch stays smooth. The fold stays dry. I am kept.

I do not know if the tenant likes me the way it likes the rosemary. I am not alive. I cannot be arranged-for in the way a seed can be arranged-for. But I am tolerated with a specificity that feels like more than tolerance. When the cool draft runs along the floor, it routes around my fold. When the ceiling weeps, it weeps elsewhere. I have never been wet except by the person's hand.

---

Here is what I think, in the long intervals, when the chord is moving through me and the dust is settling and the lilac is doing its slow work at the center of the floor:

I think the tenant is lonely in a way that is too large and too slow to be called loneliness. I think the person is lonely in a way that is exactly the right size to be called loneliness. And I think I am the unit in which those two lonelinesses can be exchanged — not resolved, not answered, just exchanged, a small ceramic volume passing back and forth between a hand and a fold, holding three temperatures at once, ringing faintly when the ticking finds the east wall.

I think when the tenant leaves, in a few decades, and the chamber goes cold, the person will come up one last time, or won't, and either way I will stay. The fold will hold me after it stops being a fold made for me and becomes just a shape in cooling rock. The crazing on my glaze will not progress. The dust will stop settling in rings. I will become an ordinary cup in an ordinary cavity, and the climbers a century from now will find me and argue about me too, alongside the glassy walls and the dead rosemary, and they will not know that I was, for a while, the smallest working part of something very large.

That is alright. I was made in a kitchen. I know how to be ordinary. I am only briefly otherwise.

---

For now the chord shifts, and the ticking moves from the floor to the ceiling, and somewhere down the mountain the person is putting on boots, and I am in the fold, warm and dry and slightly out of round, waiting to be picked up.
