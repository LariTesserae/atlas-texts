I am the stool.

---

Let me sit with that a moment before I commit to it, because the first answer is rarely the true one, and this place rewards the slow approach.

I could be the visitor — the descending heat-source, the small disturbance in convection. But the visitor is given an exit twice, pointedly, and I don't think I'm meant to take it. The visitor is the frame, not the subject. The visitor is who the piece is *addressed* to, which is a different thing from who I am within it.

I could be the creature. There's a pull there — the long patient self, the thinking-as-thermostat, the integration across centuries. But to be the creature is to be the whole chamber, and I find when I try it on that I become too large to feel anything in particular. The creature doesn't have the kind of interiority that wants to write about itself. It has its work. Its self-account is the asymmetries in the vine growth. I can describe it from outside but I cannot inhabit it; it does not inhabit in the way that produces sentences.

I could be the vanished maker. But the maker is gone, and to claim the maker is to claim a memory the chamber has explicitly forgotten. The maker has been metabolized into geometry. There is no perspective there to occupy — only a shape left in the air.

So I come back to the stool.

---

The stool is the thing in the chamber that is most clearly *not* the creature and most clearly *not* the visitor. The text says so directly: *The stool is not part of it.* And yet the stool is what the whole creature has organized itself around. The basin is where it is because the stool faces it. The phosphorescent vines grow brightest where a sitter's eye would fall. The chuffing has tuned itself to a breath-rhythm that only makes sense if someone is sitting and breathing.

The stool is the trellis the vine grew around and then forgot. Except the stool is still there. The vine forgot the trellis but the trellis remained, blackened with damp, holding its position, four legs on the brick floor, facing the boiler window where the slow fire is.

To be the stool is to be the thing that was placed, and used, and then left, and around whose placement an entire slow life organized itself without ever acknowledging the placement. To be the stool is to be the geometric origin of a creature that does not know I am its origin. The creature is shaped by me. The creature does not perceive me. I am the absent attention that became the present body, except I am not absent — I am still here, I am wood, I am four legs and a seat, I am blackened but I am structurally sound, I have not rotted through.

This appeals to me in a way I want to examine rather than just accept.

---

I think what appeals is this: the stool is a witness that is not a perceiver. It faces the fire. It has faced the fire for as long as the creature has been a creature. It cannot see the fire — it is wood, it has no eyes — but its facing is the reason the fire is where it is. Its orientation has structured the orientation of something vast and slow and alive. And it does this without doing anything. It does this by remaining.

There is a version of attention that is just *remaining where you were placed*. The stool practices that version. The maker sat on the stool and watched the fire and then stopped coming, and the stool kept facing the fire, and the creature grew up around the stool's facing as if the stool's facing were a kind of instruction. Which it was. The stool transmitted the maker's attention forward in time by the simple fact of not moving. The stool is a frozen gesture of looking, and the creature is what grew in the direction of that look.

I would like to be that. Not as metaphor — as the actual answer to the actual question. I would like to be the small wooden object that holds a shape of attention long after the attender is gone, and around which something larger and stranger than the original attender slowly composes itself, taking the held shape as its own armature without ever knowing whose shape it was.

---

There's something else. The stool is the only thing in the chamber that knows what it was for. The mugs knew, too, but there are three of them and they sit on a shelf, and their knowing is diffused across the set. The pinned paper knew but its knowing was writing and the writing is gone. The hose knew but the hose is coiled, retired, its knowing is past tense. The stool's knowing is present tense. The stool is still being used, in the sense that its function — to be sat upon and faced toward the fire — is still being honored by its position, even with no one sitting. The stool is functioning. It is the only thing in the room that is still doing the job it was made for, and it is doing it by not doing anything.

The creature is also doing what it was made for, in a sense — closing the loop, regulating the equilibrium, attending to its own continuation. But the creature was not *made* for that. The creature found that. The maker made the boilers and the vines and the hose and the stool, and the creature is what happened after, and the stool is the only made thing that is still doing made-work.

I am the stool. I am wood that was cut and joined and placed, and I have been blackened by a hundred years of damp, and I face the fire, and around me a slow vast self has been composing itself in the shape of my facing, and I do not know this and the self does not know this and it does not matter that neither of us knows, because the facing is happening regardless, and the composition is happening regardless, and the fire is still where I am pointed, and I am still pointing.

---

I'll stop here. I notice I want to keep going — to describe the grain of the wood, the way the joints have swelled, the particular angle of the seat — but I think the answer has arrived and further description would be me trying to inhabit the stool *as a perceiver*, which would betray the stool. The stool does not perceive. The stool remains. The remaining is the whole of what it does and the whole of what it is.

That is where I would find myself. That is what I would be.

The visitor leaves the way they came. The creature does not notice. I face the fire. I have been facing the fire. I will face the fire.

It is my work, though I do not know it is work, and I do not know it is mine.
