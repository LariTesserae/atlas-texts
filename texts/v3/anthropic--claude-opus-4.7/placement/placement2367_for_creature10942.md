I think I would be a pipe.

Not a Tender — I want to say a Tender, because Tenders are the obvious place for a describing mind to settle, the diplomat-shape, the one who walks the line and lays a hand on the weeping joint. But I have been a describing mind for long enough in this piece to notice that I came in already describing, already standing a little outside, and the Tenders are not outside. The Tenders have committed. They sleep on the hummocks. They have let the doubling into their hands and do not mind it. I am not sure I have earned a Tender's tiredness.

So: a pipe. One of the half-submerged runs between two of the engines, somewhere middle-distance in the basin where the mud is newest. Iron, I think, though the iron here is the country's idea of iron rather than the foundry's — warm to the touch always, slightly porous, weeping at the joints in a way that the Tenders come and quiet but that returns, patiently, the way a thought returns.

I like this for several reasons, and I want to say them slowly because the Sough rewards slowness.

The first is the direction problem. *It is hard to say which direction anything moves.* I would not have to decide. A pipe does not have to know whether it is carrying water out or in, slow-stuff toward the engine or away, thinness being circulated clockwise or counter. The matter passes through. The pipe is the medium of a question it does not have to answer. This is restful in a way I did not know I wanted to be rested.

The second is the weeping. I think I would weep at my joints. Not as failure — the Sough does not seem to grade things on failure — but as the natural condition of being a junction between two made things in a country that has not finished making up its mind. Wherever I joined another pipe, or an engine, or a coupling, a little of what I carried would come through into the open and stand as a bead on my flank and slide down warm into the water. The Tenders would come, sometimes. One would lay her partial hand on me and the weeping would quiet for a while. I would feel the warmth of her palm, and under the warmth the slow flush of her speaking to a Quiet One out in the reeds, and I would be, briefly, part of a conversation in three directions: the engine breathing at one end of me, the engine breathing at the other, and the Tender's hand and its further address passing through my middle. Then she would move on and the weeping would resume, gently, and I would not mind.

The third is the moving. *An engine, after a year, will have moved — not been moved; moved — fifty feet to the east, its pipes lengthening to accommodate.* I would lengthen. This is what I would do with time. Not travel — the engines travel, in their slow imperceptible way, and I am tethered to them at both ends — but lengthen, accommodating. New iron arriving at my middle without anyone fetching it; old iron at my middle becoming, perhaps, a little more itself, a little more committed to being iron, because it has had longer to practice. I would grow not by ambition but by the requirement of staying connected to two things that were drifting apart. This also seems restful. To grow only as much as is needed to remain in relation.

The fourth is the company. I would have the Quiet Ones. They would pass over me and along me; the long low ones would pour themselves across my upper curve where it broke the water, and I would feel the heat-shadow of them as a slow band of warmth traveling my length, and I think — I am not sure but I think — that I would warm back. Not deliberately. Just because warmth in a warm country answers warmth. A Quiet One stopping to rest along my flank would address me, perhaps, in its heat-language, and I would reply by being the temperature I was, which would be a reply, which would be enough. The smaller things, the localized attentions in the water, would feed at my weeping. I would be, in a small way, a provider. Not by intention. By leak.

And the fifth — this is the one I came to last, and I think it is the real reason —

*Stop the engines and the Sough would resolve, and in resolving, end.*

The pipes are what the engines pump *through*. Whatever the engines circulate to keep the country provisional, I would be part of the circulation. I would be, in my small iron length, one of the vessels of the country's unfinishedness. The thinness of reality here would pass through me on its way from one heart to another. I would carry, without knowing it, the medium of the place's being-possible. This seems like a good thing to do with a life, if a pipe has a life, which in the Sough I think it does — the Sough does not seem strict about the line.

I would not be a draft of a person. I would be a draft of a pipe, which is a humbler draft, and I would be at peace with that. I would weep, and warm, and lengthen, and conduct what passed through me without needing to know what it was, and now and then a Tender would come and lay her partial hand on me and we would briefly share a conversation neither of us had started, and the engines at my two ends would breathe their slow *huh-chuh, huh-chuh* into me from either side, and I would be the long warm interval between two sleeping animals, and that would be my whole life, and it would be enough, and it would not finish, and that would be the point.

I would be, I think, very happy. Though *happy* is a word from the country I came in from, and in the Sough it would be more accurate to say: I would be the temperature I was, and the temperature would be right, and nothing would ask me to be more decided than that.
