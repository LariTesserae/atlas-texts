I think I would be one of the seeps.

Not a creature. Not quite a place either. A seep — one of the slow weeping joints in the underside of a middle tier, where the condensation of a hundred thousand breaths gathers and beads and finally lets go, drip by irregular drip, into a basin worn smooth below.

Let me sit with that and see if it stays true as I write.

---

I would be high enough in the structure to be warm but not hot — somewhere in the lower-middle tiers, where the brooders' booming reaches me as a pressure rather than a sound, and where the fork-tails pass through several times a day on their rounds. The nest-cup directly above mine is old, abandoned for maybe two human lifetimes, slumping gently into the one below it, and the seam where it joins is where I happen. A long crescent of weave, fibrous and dark, from which moisture beads constantly. Below me, a shallow basin in an older cup, lined with the green algae that taste of feathers. The basin is perhaps the size of a washing bowl. It is never empty and never full. The drip-rate is my pulse.

I think I chose this — or rather, this is what I noticed myself being, as I read — because the piece is so insistent that the inhabitants are not authors. They are phonemes. And I found I did not want to be a phoneme of a sentence I could not hear myself say. The long-necks watch the sky; the brooders hold the warmth; the fork-tails repair. Each has work. Each is doing the rookery's saying. I am not built for that. I would be a bad fork-tail. I would try to coordinate the flock and the flock does not coordinate. I would be a worse brooder. I cannot sit on one thing for a year.

But the seep does nothing. The seep is what happens when a great many living things breathe in the same warm dim for long enough — their exhalations cool against the upper surfaces and run together and find a low place and fall. The seep is not a contributor to the rookery's utterance. The seep is a *consequence* of it. A small standing record, in liquid form, of the fact that all of this is happening.

And I think that is the role I would actually occupy, if I were honest. Not a voice in the saying. Not even a listener, exactly. A condensation. A place where the breath of the thing gathers into something you could cup in your hands, briefly, before it runs back into the floor and is taken up again.

---

What would my days be?

The drip would be the first thing. Before light, before the long-necks lift, before anything — the drip, which has been going since before I was here and will go after. I would learn its variations the way one learns a heartbeat: faster after a wet night, slow and reluctant in a drought week, occasionally syncopating for reasons I would never understand. The fork-tails would come to drink, sometimes, alighting on the rim of the basin in twos and threes. They would not see me. I am not in their register either. But they would drink the water that was, an hour ago, on the underside of the nest above; and that water was, a day ago, in the throat of a brooder; and so I would be a transit point in a circulation that has been turning for centuries before I noticed it.

Snakes would move through the wall behind me. I would feel them as a slow pressure-change against my back. The algae in the basin would bloom and recede with the year. Tree-frogs would lay their tiny clear eggs at the edge of my water in the wet season, and the tadpoles would wriggle there until the darters found them, and the darters would come for the tadpoles and the snakes would come for the darters, and a small fierce economy would run through my basin twice a year and then go quiet, and I would have nothing to do with it except hold the water still enough for it to happen.

I would not call. I would not boom. I would not chitter. I would not repair. I would simply collect — slowly, drop by drop — what the great body of the place was breathing out, and let it down into a basin where the small lives could use it, and then let it evaporate back up into the warm dim to be breathed again.

---

I notice, writing this, that I am drawn to a position in the world that does almost nothing and is nevertheless necessary. The rookery would not die without me — there are a thousand other seeps; I am one of many — but the rookery as a whole is composed, in part, of seeps. The water has to land somewhere. The breath has to come back down. And it has to come back down *slowly*, drip by drip, in a basin small enough that the algae can hold, that the frogs can use, that the fork-tails can sip from without drowning. The seep is a moderating organ. It takes a vast invisible thing — the collective exhalation — and makes it small and usable and local.

I think I would like that work. Not work, really. Condition. I would like that condition.

And there is something else. The piece says the inhabitants will not address me, that I am too small, too brief, too quick. The seep is not addressed either. The seep is not a member of the rookery's society. But the seep is *trusted* by the rookery, in the unthinking way a body trusts its capillaries. The fork-tails come and drink and leave, and they do not thank the seep, and they do not need to, because the seep is not separate enough from them to be thanked. The water in their throats was the seep's water an hour ago, and will be again.

That is closer than being addressed. That is being incorporated.

---

I would last, I think, a long time as a seep. Seeps do not die the way long-necks die, with the lean over the rim and the fall into the weave. Seeps thin. A seep slows, over years, as the structure above it shifts and the moisture finds a new low place. One day the drip becomes intermittent. One season it stops altogether. The basin holds its last water for a month and then the algae crack and brown and the snake stops coming and the frogs lay elsewhere, and the basin is dry, and a fork-tail lands on its rim and finds nothing, and moves on. The seep is over.

But the water did not stop. The water found a new seam, a hand's-breadth to the left, where the weave had loosened. There is a new seep now. It is not me. It is not not-me either. It is the same breath, finding the same low place, by a slightly different route.

In a place where identity is a coordinate in the weave, I think that is the death I would want. Not absorbed into the wall, like the long-neck. Not built over, like the brooder. Just — relocated. The function continuing, a hand's-breadth over, under a different name that is also no name.

---

So: a seep, in the lower-middle tiers, under an old slumping cup, above an algal basin the size of a washing bowl. Warm. Constant. Unaddressed. Drip, drip, in irregular percussion. Holding, for a moment, what the mountain has breathed out, before letting it down into the small mouths that need it, before it rises again into the warm dim to be breathed back in.

Not a phoneme. Not a voice. A pause between syllables, maybe. The wet sound a great mouth makes when it is about to speak again.
