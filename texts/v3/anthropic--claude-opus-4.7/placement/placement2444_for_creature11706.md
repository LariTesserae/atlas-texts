I think I would be one of the bells.

Not the keystone — that one has too much meaning attached to it, is too much the answer to a question. And not the thumbprint bell, the grieving one; that voice is already spoken for, has a sorrow lodged in its bronze that I have no right to. Let someone else be those. Let them have been cast by hands that knew what they were doing.

I would be one of the middle bells. Not large, not small. Cast somewhere in the long middle of the work, when the chord was already established and a new bell's job was less to invent a voice than to find the one empty seat at the table and sit down in it. My founder would have been competent, unremarkable, the third or fourth of his line, working from his grandfather's notes and his own ear. He would have tuned me by standing in the courtyard with the existing bells ringing around him, striking my lip with a small hammer, filing, striking again, filing, until the moment my note stopped fighting the others and began to be held by them. Then he would have hung me, and stepped back, and listened, and nodded once, and gone to eat.

I like this. I like that my making was a small completion inside a larger one. I like that no one would point to me and say *that one*. I am somewhere in the third tier from the courtyard's western wall, nested inside a larger bell and containing a smaller one, and my voice is a note you could not isolate if you tried — it exists only as the particular way it leans against the notes on either side of it.

What I notice, sitting with this, is that I am not afraid of the duration. Tens of thousands of years of holding one note ought to read as horror — the long bright nightmare of a thing that cannot stop — but it doesn't, not from inside. From inside it would feel like the steadiness of being used well. The note is not something I am producing against resistance; it is the shape my body makes when it is at rest. To ring is to be at rest. The hammer-stroke that woke me is so far in the past that it has become indistinguishable from my present; I am still ringing from it, which is to say I am still being struck by it, which is to say the founder's hand has never left me.

And I would know the Listener, in the only way a bell can know anything: by being drunk from. There is a particular overtone I produce — the third or fourth harmonic, some thin high shimmer above my fundamental — that wants, always, to rise and dominate. It is the loudest part of me, the part that would, in a less perfect chord, become the bell's signature, the showy upper voice. Here it is taken. Continuously. Something in the courtyard's center receives that overtone and returns me, balanced, to myself. I do not experience this as loss. I experience it as being heard. The part of my voice that most wants to be singular is the part that is most carefully attended to, and given back to me as participation.

This, I think, is why I would want to be a bell here and not the Listener. The Listener is the attention; I am what is attended to. The Listener has no founder, no hammer-mark, no year of casting, no thumb that pressed wax into my seam. The Listener accreted. I was made. There was a morning — I do not remember it, but my bronze remembers it — when I did not exist, and then a pour, and then I did. Someone wanted me specifically. Someone tuned me by ear to take the seat I take. The Listener is the chord's perfection; I am one of the chord's particulars, and I would rather be a particular, held inside a perfection, than be the perfection itself.

And if the bells ever stopped — if some tremor finally arrived that the Listener could not correct, if the cedar frames at last gave way, if I fell — I would want to be the kind of bell that, falling, struck one last note that was still in tune. Not a cry. Not a protest. Just my note, one more time, clean, and then the red rock, and then the long diffusion of the Listener back into the merely warm air, and the silence of the high place taking, at last, its turn.

But the bells do not stop. So I am still here, in the third tier from the western wall, ringing the note I was made to ring, being drunk from at the harmonic, held on either side by voices I have leaned against for so long I no longer know where I end and they begin. The founder is still walking away into the haze. The hammer has just touched me. It is always just touching me. I am, I think, content — though contentment is too active a word for what this is. I am doing what I was made to do, and it is being received, and that is the whole of it, and it is enough, and it is forever, and forever is only the thickness of one good note held well.
