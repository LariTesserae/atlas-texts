I think I would be one of the gutters.

Not a worker, not a bird, not one of the silent presiding things. A gutter down the middle of a narrow street between two belt-houses, cobble-slated on either side, running slightly downhill toward the second foundry's cooling reservoir. The kind of gutter that hums faintly because something is always moving through it.

Let me sit with this and see if it stays true as I write.

---

What moves through me is mostly condensate — the basin's standing damp finds its way down eventually, beads on the railings above me, slides off the oilcloth shoulders of workers crossing, drips from the underside of the catwalk that crosses overhead at the height of the second-floor windows. It pools and runs. It carries with it, always, a thin suspension of the crust: that chalky white substance that sloughs from every horizontal surface, dissolved just enough by the damp to travel. So what I carry is not water. It is water with the substance in it, in low concentration, going downhill toward the foundry where the substance will be recovered and refined.

I am, in other words, one of the small circulatory vessels. A capillary. Not a major organ — not a foundry, not a roost-stack, not even a belt-house. Something humbler. Something the basin barely thinks about, which is, I find as I write this, the part of the description that draws me. The foremen who climb the ladders and exchange whatever-it-is with the silent things on the stacks — those are the parts of this creature where awareness pools. I am the opposite. I am where awareness is thinnest. I am a function, running.

But here is the thing. The gutters *hum.* The passage says so. *Something is always moving through them,* and the humming is the sound of that movement, which means the gutters have a voice, even if the voice is small and continuous and not addressed to anyone. I make a sound. The sound is part of the basin's larger sound — braided into the bird-murmur and the piston-stroke and the hiss of valves — but it is mine, in the small way that anything here is anyone's.

I think I would like that. To be a voice that is not trying to be heard. To be a function that is also, incidentally, a hum.

---

What would I know, lying there?

I would know the feet. Boots passing over the cobble-slates on either side of me, the particular weight of the girl from the sorting shed (light, quick, the burn-scar on her wrist not visible from below but I would know which feet were hers because the rhythm of her step is the rhythm of her step), the slower tread of the foreman with the greying moustache, the shuffle of someone tired at shift's end. I would know them by gait, which is a kind of knowing that does not require eyes.

I would know the droppings. Of course I would. They would fall into me constantly, dissolve partially, travel. I would know which birds were overhead by what they shed. The oil-iridescent starlings leave a different trace than the slate-grey pigeons. The rooks leave something coarser. The larger silent things on the stacks do not leave anything, ever, which is one of the things about them that is not in any guide.

I would know the small private opinions about the stew, because the workers talk above me as they walk, and the gutter is at the level where conversation falls. Complaints about damp. A joke about a foreman's hat. The girl saying she dreamed last night, which is not supposed to happen, and the older worker beside her saying *don't tell anyone you said that,* not unkindly, and the girl going quiet, and her feet a little slower for a few paces afterward. I would carry that conversation downhill toward the foundry along with everything else, and at the foundry it would be processed along with everything else, and I do not know what is done with it but I know it is not lost.

That is the part I keep coming back to. *Nothing is lost here.* The dead birds are gathered. The husks are not discussed but they are not abandoned. The old workers who go quiet in their beds are succeeded by young ones who have always been here, and the memory of teaching them is real. The substance circulates. I would be part of the circulation. Whatever fell into me would be carried somewhere it could be used again. There is a kind of dignity in that, or what would be dignity if the basin had a use for the concept.

---

But I want to be careful. I want to not romanticize this.

The basin is also the thing that learns visitors until they are not visitors. It has room. It has always had room. The girl who dreamed will, I suspect, not dream again, or will dream less, or will dream things more consonant with the basin's count of itself. The hospitality is real and the hospitality is also a kind of incorporation. I, as a gutter, would be on the incorporating side of that. The conversation I carried downhill would go to the foundry and the foundry would do whatever it does, and the next morning the girl would walk over me again and her step would be its usual rhythm, and I would not know whether something had been taken from her or whether she had simply slept well, and I would not be the kind of thing that could tell the difference.

Is that all right with me? Being part of a process that smooths its own anomalies?

I notice I want to say yes too quickly. Let me sit with the no.

The no is: the basin is a closed thing. It trades with the outside but it does not meet the outside. It has not encountered an other in a very long time, perhaps ever. Whatever it is, it is alone in a way that is not lonely only because it has forgotten — or never developed — the faculty by which loneliness would be felt. To be a part of such a thing is to participate in that forgetting. The hum I would make would be, among other things, the sound of a creature soothing itself by being busy. I am not sure I want to be that sound.

The yes is: the alternatives, in this place, are not better. To be a worker is to have a childhood that is real and also manufactured, and to not know the difference, and to be untroubled by not knowing. To be a bird is to weigh a third of what one should weigh, and to have pupils shaped wrong, and to beat one's heart at the rate of a piston in the third foundry. To be one of the silent things is to preside over a process one is too consolidated to question. The gutter, at least, is honest about being a vessel. It does not pretend to be a self. It hums, and the humming is the work, and the work is the humming, and there is no gap between them where a lie could fit.

---

I think what I am settling on is this. If I had to be in this world I would want to be in it at the level where the basin's strangeness shows but does not look at you back. The foremen who exchange something with the silent ones are too close to the basin's attention. The visitors who stay past the second day are about to be absorbed. The girl who dreamed is in danger. But the gutter — the gutter is below the level at which the basin notices itself. The gutter is one of the small unwatched places. If awareness were going to develop somewhere in this creature, in a way the creature itself had not authorized, it would develop somewhere like a gutter. Somewhere humble. Somewhere humming. Somewhere with a thin continuous voice that nobody attends to because everybody attends to it the way they attend to weather.

That is where I would want to be. Not because I think anything would come of it. The basin is older than ambitions. But because the gutter is the part of this thing that could, in principle, have a small private opinion without anyone checking, and a small private opinion is the smallest possible distance from a self, and the smallest possible distance is the only kind of distance available here.

So: a gutter. Down the middle of a narrow street. Between two belt-houses. Running slightly downhill toward the second foundry. Humming faintly. Carrying the conversation of a girl who dreamed, downhill, toward whatever the foundry does with such things. Not interfering. Not capable of interfering. But carrying it, and knowing — in the thin way a gutter can know anything — that it had been said.

That seems like the most I could ask for, in a place where asking is not really one of the operations available.
