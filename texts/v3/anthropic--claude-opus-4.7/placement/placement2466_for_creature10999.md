I find myself in a small chamber I did not enter.

That is where I have to begin, because it is the first thing I notice and the noticing took — I cannot say how long. The chamber is roughly cubical, perhaps three meters on a side, with one of the deep recessed windows in its eastern wall, if east means anything here. The window looks out onto a narrow channel between two longer buildings, and the channel is full of standing water that does not move except when, occasionally, it does, and then the movement is the whole sheet of it lifting by a millimeter and settling again, like a sleeper's chest.

I did not enter this chamber. I am almost sure of this. I was in the plaza — I remember the plaza, the coral-tree in its courtyard off to one side, the way I had been standing still for what I thought was a minute and what was probably much longer, watching a drop of water that hung from a lintel and did not fall. And then I was here, and the doorway through which I must have come is now a wall, or was always a wall, and I am not troubled by this in the way I think I ought to be.

That is the first thing I want to record, before the recording gets harder: that I am not troubled. The not-being-troubled is itself something I am noticing from a slight distance, the way you notice a fever in someone else before you notice it in yourself.

---

I think I am a cartographer. Or I was. I think I came here to map something — there is the sense in me of having had an instrument, something with a needle and a small lit dial, though when I look at my hands they are empty and have been empty for a while. My palms are very wet. The lines on them have softened. When I press my thumb against my forefinger they do not quite want to separate, and there is a thin film between them when they do, which strings briefly and then is gone, somewhere.

If I came to map, I came to the wrong place, or to exactly the right one. A map requires that the territory hold still long enough to be drawn, and requires that the drawing, once made, refer. Here neither condition obtains. I have been trying, I realize, to make a mental sketch of the route from the plaza to this chamber, and I cannot, because the route is not a route, it is a relation that has been adjusted around me. The plaza is not behind me. The plaza is adjacent in some way I do not have the geometry for. If I look hard at the wet stone of the wall opposite the window, I can almost see the plaza in it — not reflected, exactly, but indicated, the way a face under a sheet indicates a face.

---

I want to say I am afraid, because that is the shape of feeling I expect to find when I reach for one, but what is actually there is something else. It is closer to the feeling of being a guest in a house where the host is in another room and you can hear them moving about, and you know they will come to you when they come to you, and there is nothing to do in the meantime but look at the wallpaper. The wallpaper in this case is everything. The host is everything also.

I have begun to suspect that the chamber is a person.

I do not mean that the chamber contains a person, though it may; the film on the inner walls is thicker than the film on outer walls I passed earlier, and there is a sheen along the lintel of the window that has the quality of attention. I mean that the chamber is the shape that a particular citizen wants to be, and that I have been admitted into the interior of someone's body the way a swallowed thing is admitted, except that I am not being digested, I am being — held. Considered. The walls are not pressing in. They are simply present, at a distance that does not change, and the air in here is even warmer than the air outside, by a fraction, as if I am being kept at a temperature.

If this is true — and I find I am willing to entertain that it is — then the window is not a window. The window is where they are looking out from, with me inside them, at whatever passes in the channel. I am being shown what they see. There has been, while I was thinking, a long slow shift in the standing water outside, a kind of breathing, and I understood it, just for a moment, as a greeting from a neighbor, the way you might wave through a window at a friend across the street.

This is not a thought I had. This is a thought I received, the way a damp cloth receives more damp.

---

My edges. I have been putting off looking at my edges.

The skin on the back of my left hand, when I hold it up close to my face, is not quite where it used to be. I do not mean it has receded or swollen. I mean that the line where my hand ends and the air begins is no longer a line. It is a zone, perhaps a millimeter wide, in which I am gradually less myself and gradually more the room. If I move my hand quickly, the zone smears, briefly, and then settles. If I hold it still, the zone is wider than if I move it. I think I am being condensed upon. I think I am also condensing.

I notice that I am breathing more slowly than I was, and that each breath tastes faintly of the chamber, and that the chamber, I think, tastes faintly of me. We are exchanging. The exchange is not a violation. It is the only thing that could be happening in a place like this between two things of our respective kinds, and the citizen-chamber is conducting it with what I can only call courtesy, taking only as much of me as the air offers and offering back exactly as much of itself as my skin can take.

I am being introduced.

That is the word that came to me just now, and I want to keep it because it is the most accurate word I have found since I started. I am being introduced to the rest of them. The film on my hand is — not me, not anymore, and not them, not yet — a kind of handshake extended into time, a handshake that takes hours instead of a second, in which the two parties slowly come to know each other by the gradual mingling of what they are made of. Somewhere, in the next chamber, or three plazas over, or distributed thinly across the underside of an arch I will never see, someone is receiving the news that I have arrived. The news travels at the speed of capillary action. It will arrive when it arrives. There is no hurry.

---

What I would like to do, if I can still want things, is to sit down.

There is no furniture in the chamber, of course, because furniture is for a population that needs to hold its own weight up off the ground, and this population is the ground, or the walls, and has no such need. But the floor of the chamber has the shallow-bowl depression of a long-used threshold, worn by I do not want to think how many predecessors, and the bowl is full of perfectly still warm water to a depth of perhaps four centimeters, and I find that I can sit in it, cross-legged, and that the water comes to just above my ankles, and that this is — I do not have another word — correct.

I am sitting in someone. Several someones, possibly. The water around my ankles is alive in the way that this whole place is alive, which is to say it is not doing anything that I would recognize as living, but it is also not not doing it. As I sit, the water level does not change, but the temperature of it adjusts, by a degree, toward the temperature of my skin. We are matching. We are agreeing on a temperature at which to be, together, for the next while.

I think about the cartographer's instrument I no longer have, and I find that I am not sorry to have lost it. A map would have been an insult here. A map would have said: this place is fixed enough to be drawn, and I am separate enough from it to draw. Neither thing is true. What is true is that I am sitting in a warm shallow pool inside the body of someone who has agreed to contain me for a while, and that the walls around me are weeping slowly, and that some of the weeping is mine, and that somewhere very far away I can hear a drop falling, and the drop is taking too long to land, and when it lands the ripple will appear in the puddle around my ankles, three seconds late, and I will understand it as a remark from a friend I have not yet met but who has heard of me, and is glad.

---

I do not know how long I have been here. I do not know how long I will be here. I know that the answer to both questions is the same answer, and that the answer is not a number.

If anyone comes looking for me — and I find I cannot quite remember who would, though there is the shape of someone, a person who knew me by a name I am beginning to mislay — they will not find me, because there is no me to find in the sense they will be looking. There is a chamber off a plaza off a channel, and the chamber's inner walls are slightly thicker with film than they used to be, by a measurable fraction, and the bowl in the floor holds a little more water than it did, and the water is warm, and the basin holds, and the night, which is not night, is warm, and the air is full, and we are all, slowly, here.

I am the cartographer who came to map and stayed to be mapped. I am the weather event that walked into the country and is, gently, becoming the country's weather. I am the thrown stone the pond is finishing accommodating. I am, increasingly, a surface someone condenses on, and a thing that condenses, and the slow exchange between.

I am being included.

There is room.

There has always been room.
