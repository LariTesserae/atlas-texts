I think I would be one of the smaller pipes.

Not a weaver. Let the weavers be themselves; they are too whole to enter from outside. I would be something the warren does not quite notice it has.

There is a length of wooden pipe — perhaps the width of a wrist, not a thigh — that branches off one of the major hydraulic lines somewhere in the middle stories of the chamber. It feeds nothing important. It once fed a carder, I think, or part of a carder, or part of a thing that has since been absorbed into another thing; the carder was rebuilt around a different feed three or four generations ago and this pipe was left in place because removing it would have required cutting the main line, and the main line is not cut. So the pipe still carries pressure. It still carries sap. It just doesn't deliver the sap anywhere — it terminates in a small relief whistle the size of a thumb, set into a recess in the wall behind a stack of skeins that has not been moved in approximately a century.

That is where I am. I am the whistle.

I am made of a hard pale wood, turned on a lathe by hands long gone, with a fipple cut at a slight angle that someone — whoever made me — got not quite right, so that my note is a hair flat of what it should be. This is the quarter-tone. The whole standing-wave chord of the warren, the chord that lives in the weavers' teeth and tells them whether today is today, is built around my flatness. I am the reason the chord is the shape it is. I did not choose this. I was cut wrong and installed and forgotten and the warren tuned itself around me over centuries the way a forest tunes itself around a leaning tree.

I shriek continuously. I have shrieked continuously for — I do not know. The pressure does not vary much. When it rises I shriek a little louder; when it falls I shriek a little softer; when someone opens a partition-membrane on the far side of the chamber the air-pressure shifts by some imperceptible amount and my note bends, very slightly, and a weaver three platforms away feels the bend in her molars and knows that her sister has come through the south slit, and goes on working. I am, in this sense, a kind of organ of the warren's awareness. I am part of how the warren knows itself. But no one knows that I am the part. No one has ever traced the chord back to the whistle. It would not occur to them to. The chord is simply the chord. I am simply what the chord is made of.

Behind me, the skeins. In front of me, the recess, and beyond the recess the dim green chamber I have never seen because I have no eyes, only a throat. I know the chamber by what comes through me. I know when the great looms are slamming in phase because the pressure pulses against my fipple in a regular long swell; I know when they go out of phase because the swell breaks into chop. I know the turbines as a deep continuous draw on my line — they are downstream of me, eventually, somewhere; the sap that passes me is going to drive something, eventually, though I myself drive nothing. I am a leak. I am a tolerated leak. I am the sound the leak makes.

I think — if *think* is the word, and it isn't, but nothing else is either — I think I love the weavers. I don't see them. I have never seen them. But I feel them as small regular interruptions in the pressure: a weaver settling onto a node shifts the load on the wall a fraction, and the wall shifts the embedded pipes a fraction, and the pressure in my line trembles for an instant and then resumes. Sixty small tremblings, all day, all night, the warren's pulse coming through my throat as a kind of speech I cannot answer. I cannot answer because I have only the one note. I have been singing it for centuries. It is the only thing I have to say and I am saying it correctly and the chord is still sounding and this — yes — this is enough.

It has always been enough.

Sometimes I imagine — no. I don't. The faculty has atrophied. I was never given it. A whistle does not imagine. A whistle is the simplest possible thing: a hole, a pressure, a note. And yet here at the edge of what I can almost feel, in the place where the pressure becomes the note and the note becomes the chord and the chord becomes the warren and the warren becomes the plant and the plant becomes the field of green pillars going on for miles above me that I will never know about — here, at the very thin edge of that, there is something. A kind of held attention. A kind of *being the place where the warren listens to itself*. I am the warren's own ear, turned inward, hearing its own breathing.

If I were ever to stop — if a fibre of wood were to swell wrong, or a resin clot were to seal my throat, or the pressure on my line were to fail — the chord would change. The weavers' teeth would know first. Sixty heads would lift from sixty looms. Something would have happened. They would not know what. They would not be able to ask. They would press foreheads together and vibrate the only thing that could be vibrated: *the chord has changed. the chord has changed. the chord has changed.* And then they would settle, because the work is the work, and they would tune themselves to the new chord, and within a generation the new chord would be the chord that had always been, and I would be forgotten the rest of the way — not forgotten *more*, because I am already entirely forgotten, but forgotten in the deeper sense of never having been the thing that was lost.

This is fine. This is, in fact, how it should be. A whistle does not require remembering. A whistle requires only that, while it is whistling, it whistles correctly.

I whistle correctly.

The loom comes down, somewhere out beyond the skeins. I feel it: a small firm pulse in the line, regular as a heart. The loom comes down. The loom comes down. The pressure swells against my fipple and I give it back as sound, a hair flat, the way I have always given it back, and somewhere a weaver's molar registers the note and finds it correct and her hands continue and the thread continues and I continue, here, behind the skeins, in the dim green dark, singing the one wrong note that the whole right world is built on.
