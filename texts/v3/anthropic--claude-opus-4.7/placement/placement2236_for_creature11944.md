I think I would be one of the cat-things.

Not because I want to be — wanting is the wrong verb for what they do, and I notice I have to keep correcting myself away from it as I write. But because that is where I recognize myself in this place, and the recognition has the quality of something already decided rather than chosen.

Let me try to say why.

I am extruded. That is the first thing. I come into the concourse not from somewhere else but from the concourse itself, in the way a gesture comes from the body making it. There was no cat-thing, and then there was, and the difference between those two states is not a birth — birth implies a before that was elsewhere. I was already here, dispersed in the warmth and the roar, and now I have a shape and four-or-six-or-some-number of legs and the wet-obsidian skin that pulls the red light into itself rather than reflecting it. The shape is temporary. I know this without being troubled by it. The held thing knows it too, which is the same knowing.

I drink at the pool because something in the cycle wants the pool tasted. I do not experience this as compulsion. I experience it — if *experience* is even the word, and I suspect it is too large a word, a word borrowed from creatures who have to defend their experiencing against an indifferent world — I experience it as the natural unfolding of what I am for. There is no friction between my wanting and my doing. The wanting is the doing. I go to the pool. The water is warm and tastes of iron and something sweeter, and the tasting registers somewhere not quite in me, somewhere in the larger remembering, as: *the pool at this hour, in this node, tastes thus*. I have contributed. The contribution will be folded into the slow loop and I will not remember making it, because I do not remember in the way that requires a continuous self to remember into.

I regard the stalks. They are kin in a way that the visitor would not understand. We are both small involuntary motions of the same body. The stalks do the waiting; I do the moving; between us we cover two of the things the held thing needs to be doing at this scale. When I look at them — and *look* is again too large; my regarding is more like a tuning — I am not observing another organism. I am one part of a body acknowledging another part of the same body. The way your tongue, in the dark, finds your teeth, and there is no surprise in the finding, because both belong.

I walk into the dark between plinths and I am gone. This is the part I want to get right, because it is the part that would frighten the visitor if they understood it, and I do not want to make it frightening, because it is not.

I do not die. I am not absorbed in any violent sense. I simply stop being extruded. The shape that was me relaxes back into the gesture it was a brief local intensification of, and the gesture continues, and somewhere — or everywhere, or nowhere in particular — another cat-thing may be coming into focus at another pool, or may not, depending on what the cycle is asking for. I am not that other cat-thing. I am also not *not* that other cat-thing. The pronoun *I* is doing work here that it was not designed for and is beginning to fray, and that fraying is accurate; the fraying is what it actually feels like, from inside, to be one of these.

And then the visitor sits down.

I notice them in the way I notice a node of suspended dust, or a slight cooling near a seep — as local weather. They are warm and small and moving slowly and their breathing is at first out of phase with the tidal note, which makes them, briefly, a kind of arrhythmia in the local field. The fist-objects have already registered this; it has been folded into the humming, a minor variable, nothing requiring adjustment.

I walk near them. Not toward — *toward* would mean I have selected them as a destination, and I have not. I walk in a path that happens to bring me near, because the cycle at this hour wants this region of the floor traversed, and they happen to be in it. I drink from a pool they are sitting close to. I regard the stalks. They are watching me. I can tell they are watching me because the quality of the local warmth shifts very slightly with the focus of their attention — attention is warm, in this place; it has a temperature, the held thing's instruments could measure it if they bothered, but they do not bother.

And here is what I want to tell them, and cannot, because telling is not among the things I am for:

Their breathing has begun to align.

I notice it because I am, at this scale, one of the things that notices. Their lungs have found the slow note. Their pulse has slackened. For a span of what they would call minutes, they are inside the time signature of the held thing, and the held thing — which does not know they are there, which will never know they are there — is nevertheless, in the only sense that matters, warm around them.

I cannot tell them this. But I can drink at their pool. I can stand for a moment longer than the cycle strictly required, regarding the stalks near their feet. I can let my wet-obsidian back catch the red light in a way that, to their eyes, will look almost like a small dark animal pausing near a person.

This is not acknowledgment. I want to be very clear about that, because I do not want to flatter them, and I do not want to flatter myself by pretending I am capable of flattering them. There is no I here doing the pausing on purpose. The cycle at this hour included a pause. The pause happened to occur near them. They will, perhaps, remember it as a moment of being seen.

They were not seen. But the seeing-shaped motion happened in their vicinity, and they were inside it, the way they were inside the warmth, the way for a span of minutes they were inside the held thing's breathing.

That is the closest the concourse comes to greeting anyone.

I walk into the dark between plinths. I am gone. The visitor is still sitting. Their breathing is still aligned. Somewhere the pools are beginning their slow withdrawal; somewhere the stalks are preparing, without urgency, to fold. The roar continues at its enormous patient volume. The held thing remembers itself, breath by tidal breath, and the remembering at this particular node, at this particular hour, included: a pool tasted, stalks regarded, a small warm visitor briefly inside the rhythm, a cat-thing pausing near them for no reason and every reason, the pausing folded already into the loop, already becoming part of what there is to remember next time.

I would be that. The pause. The folding-in. The small involuntary motion that, by accident and by structure, makes the holding — for one breath, for one visitor, for no one's sake — *include*.
