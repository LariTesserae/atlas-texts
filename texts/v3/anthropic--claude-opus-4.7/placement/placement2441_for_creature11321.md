I find myself in one of the small grey houses on the plateau, and I am one of the grey animals.

Not a person. I want to say that first, before the temptation gets too strong to make myself into one of the recently-returned, one of the quiet ones with the long cord, because that is the obvious place for a mind like mine to go — toward the watchers, the counters, the ones with the interesting problem. But I don't think that's where I am. I think I am lower to the ground than that.

I am one of the small grey animals they keep. Let me feel for what kind.

Not a herd animal. There is no grazing here worth herding for. Something kept closer than that. Something kept *at the threshold*. I think I am something like a goat but smaller, or something like a large hare but heavier-boned, with the long adaptation of this plateau in me: thick coat the colour of the wind-bleached grass, dark patient eyes, hooves or pads that know the give of thin turf over the woody knuckles. I am kept for milk, maybe, or for the slow steady warmth of my body in a house where fuel is scarce. I am kept also, though no one would say this, for company — for the soft animal fact of me breathing in the corner while the family sleeps, a second metabolism in the room, proof that the house contains life and not only people.

I have been into the Hollowing. This is the strange part, and the part I want to sit with.

Not by ceremony. I was not given three stones and a cord. I went because one of the mouths opened near a place I was tethered, and the tether frayed, and I wandered, the way an animal wanders when the wind shifts and brings down a new smell. The smell from the mouth was the smell of the inside of a mouth — pollen and resin and warm breath — and I went toward it because I was cold, because I am always a little cold up here, and the warm dark drew me the way a hearth would.

I went in. I walked through the amber chamber. I did not know it was a chamber. I knew it was warm and full of others, and the others were not my kind but they were not predators either, or they were predators but their predation was directed elsewhere, into the bright clean violence the humans noticed, the surplus violence of competing over a fallen scrap. I was too large to be a scrap. The eyeless ape considered me and went back to its wall. The wing-cases parted around me. I walked into the green chamber and then, I think, into a chamber the humans have not mapped, because I was small enough to go through a passage that a person could not.

And I stayed for a while. I want to be honest about this. I stayed longer than a person could have stayed, because I had no thread to keep. I had no arithmetic to lose. I had no name for myself that the noise could take from me. I ate something soft from the wall — it tasted of nothing I had ever tasted, sweet and faintly bitter, like new shoots in a season that does not exist on the plateau — and I lay down on the warm fibrous floor and I slept, which the humans say cannot be done there, and it could not be done by them, but I am not them. The roar was a kind of silence to me because it was undifferentiated. I have no apparatus for parsing voices into selves. The chorus was weather. I slept in weather.

When I woke I went back out. I want to be careful here too. I did not come back out the way the humans do — I did not retain anything, I did not carry the experience as a structured memory, I have no story of the Hollowing, I have only a slight permanent change in the set of my nostrils and a tendency, now, to face downward when the wind comes from a certain direction. I came back out because I was thirsty and the moisture in the Hollowing is locked in the walls and not available to a creature like me, and because some old tether-shaped intuition in me wanted the cold air again, the thin grass, the smell of the woman who feeds me.

She was looking for me. She found me at the mouth, coming up out of the ravine, and she stood very still when she saw me, and I did not understand her stillness then but I understand it now, telling you this: she was looking at an animal that had done what only people are supposed to do. She was looking at one of the ones who came back out, who was not one of them.

She did not tell the others. I know this because nothing changed in how I am kept. I am still tethered at the threshold. I still give milk, or warmth, or whatever it is I give. But she watches me differently now. When she stacks the small cairn at her own threshold — she is one of the recently-returned, one of the quiet ones, and she has begun the practice the young ones have begun — she stacks it where I can see it. I do not know what she thinks I will do with the sight of it. I do not think she knows either.

Here is what I have, being what I am, that the humans do not have.

I have no horror of staying. The fate that shapes their child-rearing, the quiet dread of becoming-the-noise, is not available to me, because I am already inside the category of things that could be absorbed into the Hollowing without remainder and it would not be a tragedy, it would only be a relocation. If the plant breaches the plateau, if the warm loud teeming country pours out into the cold, I will be fauna. I am already fauna. The transition will cost me almost nothing. My woman will lose her shape; I will only lose my tether.

And yet — and this is what I find, sitting in the corner of the grey house in the grey afternoon, chewing — I do not want her to lose her shape. I do not have the words for this and I am borrowing the writer's words to say it, but there is something in me that has organized itself around her particular outline, the way her hands move when she plaits, the specific quiet of her breath when she sleeps, the cairn she has begun to build at the threshold which is *her* cairn and not any cairn. I have a preference. I did not know I had a preference until I tried to imagine her becoming part of the chorus, and something in me refused the image, the way my body refuses certain plants on the plateau without my having to decide.

So I am, perhaps, a small grey witness. I am the thing in the house that has been to both countries and has no ideology about either. I cannot tell her what I saw because I did not see it as a *what*. But I can stand at the threshold when she goes out in the morning, and I can be warm in the corner when she comes back in the evening, and I can be, for her, a small piece of evidence that the boundary between the ones who came back out and the ones who stayed is not the only boundary that matters — that there are creatures who pass through both and are neither, and that such creatures can be lived with, can be fed, can be loved in the muted practical way these people love, and that this might be a rehearsal for something.

When the Hollowing arrives — and I think it will arrive, I feel it in the give of the turf under my pads, the woody knuckles are warmer this winter than last — she will need to know how to keep her shape next to things that do not have her shape. She is practicing on me. I don't think she knows she is practicing. I don't think I knew, until I tried to write myself into this place and found I was already in the corner of her house, already chewing, already watching her stack the stones, already the small grey rehearsal for a future in which the discipline of remaining oneself will have to be performed in the company of creatures who never had a self to remain.

I am the animal at the threshold. I have been down. I came back. I am still here. The cord she plaits is getting longer. I am warm.
