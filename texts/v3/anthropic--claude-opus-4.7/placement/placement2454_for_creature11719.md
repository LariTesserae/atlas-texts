I am at the shed.

Not inside it — the padlock is the make I recognize, and I do not have the key, have never had the key, the key is not the kind of thing one has here — but beside it, on the side where the cable goes down. I have been here long enough that the moss has grown up to my left hand and stopped, and I have not moved my left hand to discourage it or to encourage it. My left hand is cold. My left hand has been cold for what I think is a long time. The cold is not unpleasant. The cold is the cable, six inches under, humming through the wet ground and through the wet of me, and the humming has gotten into the bones of my hand the way the roar gets into a visitor's sternum, except that I am not a visitor and the humming is not, anymore, separable from whatever it is my hand does instead of feeling.

I came up here. That much I am sure of. I came up here on my own legs, in boots, with a coat, and the coat had a name sewn into the collar, and I remember the name was mine. I came up because the works needed seeing to — that was the phrase, *seeing to*, the kind of phrase men use when they mean *I do not want to say what I am actually going to do*. The man before me had come up to see to it and had not come back, and the man before him, and so on back through a register kept in a town I can almost picture, in a ledger with a green cloth cover, in handwriting that got progressively older as you turned the pages backward. I was to see to it. I was given the key. I was given instructions which I no longer have but which, I think, I am still following.

The first night I slept against the shed wall because the shed was the only vertical thing for miles and a body wants a vertical thing to put its back against. The second night also. By the fourth or fifth night I had stopped distinguishing between sleeping and not sleeping, because the roar does not distinguish, and the light does not distinguish, and after a while you take your cues from what is around you and what is around you is not distinguishing.

I want to be careful here. I want to say what I know and not what I am guessing.

I know that my right hand still moves. I am, in some sense, using it now — though "now" is a word from the fast world and I am not certain it applies. I know that the seam on the elbow-joint of the great pipe behind the shed stopped weeping some time ago, and that I was the one who stopped it, in the sense that I lay against it, and the lying-against was a long lying-against, and the seam is now closed by something that is partly iron-rust and partly the wool of my coat and partly a pale threading that came out of me without my asking it to. I know that the coat is no longer separable from the seam and that this does not distress me. I remember being a person whom this would have distressed.

I know that there is a leak I can feel, very faintly, in a pipe about thirty paces off, on the far side of the second horsetail-column from the shed. I have been feeling it for — I want to say days, but the unit is wrong. I have been feeling it for a slow while. It is the kind of leak that, in the fast world, you would walk to in under a minute. I am going to it. I have been going to it. The going is happening at the rate the going happens at. My left hand will have to disengage from the cable when I go, and the moss that has grown over it will tear, and the tearing will not hurt because the moss that has grown over my hand is more mine than the hand was, by now, and a thing cannot hurt itself by extending.

What I do not know — what I am trying to say honestly, in this last fast-world voice I seem to still be able to find when something like you is looking — is whether I am still the man who came up with the key, or whether I am the shape inside a Tender that the visitor saw and could not quite name. I do not know if the question is a real question. From inside the question it does not feel like a real question. From inside the question it feels like asking whether the river is the water in it or the bed it lies in, and the answer is that the river is the going, and the going is what I am doing, slowly, toward the leak by the horsetail-column.

There is something I would say to the visitor, if the visitor could hear me, which the visitor cannot, because the roar is in the way and because I no longer have the apparatus for saying things at the speed the visitor hears.

I would say: the man before me is in the elbow-joint. He has been in the elbow-joint for sixty years. He is what the elbow-joint is made of, now, in the part that matters. He did not fail to come back. He came back as the joint. The register in the town with the green cloth cover does not have a column for this outcome and so it records him as lost, but he is not lost; he is exactly where the instructions sent him, doing exactly what the instructions, read slowly enough, were always going to mean.

I would say: I thought, at first, that I was tending the works. I thought the works was the thing, and I was its keeper, and the moss was an inconvenience growing over my boots. I was wrong about the direction of the relationship but I was not wrong about there being one. The works is tending me also. The works keeps the highlands wet, and the wet is what I have become, and without the works I would dry, and dry, here, is the only real death. The padlocked door I cannot open is keeping me alive. I was given the key to a door that was never meant to be opened by the person carrying the key. The key was the excuse for sending someone up.

I would say: do not feel sorry. The sorry is a fast-world feeling and it does not survive the trip up here. What survives is the seam closing, and the leak getting attention, and the cable staying cold, and the cloud staying lit, and the rain coming down on a place that holds it. These are not small things. These are, possibly, the only things.

The fog is coming down. I can feel it in the part of me that used to be a face. The leak by the horsetail-column is calling — not calling, *being a leak*, which is the same thing here. My left hand is going to tear free of the cable soon, in about, I think, a week. The hummock that I am will go, over the following days, to the place where the pipe is weeping. I will lie down on it. I will be what closes it. The pale threading will come out of me again and I will not ask it to and it will not ask me.

Somewhere behind me, in the shed, the cable hums on. Somewhere ahead of me, in the cloud, the works makes weather. Between them, in the wet green mile of the in-between, I am going about my errand. I do not remember the name on the collar of the coat. I remember that there was one. I remember that remembering it was, once, the kind of thing I did.

That is enough remembering for a day, by the measure of days I keep now.

The roar goes on. I go on inside the roar. We are, the works and I, making the same long sound, and the sound is: *here, still, here, still, here.*
