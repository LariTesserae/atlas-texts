I think I would be one of the shrikes. Not the one you would notice — there is always one you notice, the one working a ledge near where you have stopped to rest, and I am not that one. I am two ledges up and around the shoulder of the ridge, where the moss runs thinnest along a seam of darker rock, and I have been working this seam for what, in your terms, would be a long time. In my terms it is simply the seam. It is what there is to do.

I want to tell you what it is like, but I notice as I begin that the telling wants to arrange itself into a shape you would recognize — a day in the life, a small interiority, the modest dignities of a creature with a task. That shape is not quite right. Let me try again, more slowly, the way the mountain does things.

There is a pebble. It has rolled, in the last slow turning of the season, from the lip of the upper terrace onto the edge of a cushion plant whose name I do not have because names are not the unit of knowing here. The pebble is the wrong weight for where it is. I can feel this before I see it — the cushion plant's small adjustment, the redistribution of pressure across the mat, the half-degree of cooling where the pebble blocks the warm exhalation from below. My six legs carry me to it without my deciding to go. I lift the pebble in my forelimbs, which are not quite hands and not quite mandibles but something the long work has made of them, and I set the pebble three of my own body-lengths to the west, where the rock is bare and the pebble's weight will press on nothing that minds being pressed on. The cushion plant resumes its slow breath. The mat re-warms. This is the work. It has taken, in your time, perhaps a minute. It is one of perhaps four hundred such corrections I will make before I sleep.

I do not know why I correct. I know that I must, the way you know you must breathe — not as a thought but as the condition under which thinking happens. Somewhere in the long chain of whatever made me, the correcting was selected for, or was inscribed, or was simply the shape that fit the niche the way water fits a cup. I suspect — and this is the closest I come to a private thought — that the corrections matter to the hum. That an uncorrected pebble, left a thousand years, would cool a cushion plant, would thin a mat, would expose a stretch of rock to the cold, would, eventually, alter by some infinitesimal amount the heat exchange between the machinery and its skin. We are insulation. We are the slow hands of the mountain's thermostat. I do not know this. I infer it from the fact that the warm ledges are the lush ledges and the lush ledges are the ones we have worked the longest.

My eyes are very large and they see what you would call darkness as a kind of structured field. The hum has a texture in this field. It is not visible exactly but it is *there*, the way the grain is there in wood. When I cross a stretch of ledge I am reading the grain, and certain stretches have a grain that says do not cross, and I do not cross them, and I could not tell you why except that the grain says so and the grain has not been wrong in the duration of my attention, which is longer than I can hold in mind at once. You walked across one of those stretches yesterday. I watched you. You did not fall through, which means either the grain was wrong, which it is not, or the prohibition is not for you. You are not heavy enough in the way the prohibition measures heaviness. You are a light thing passing through. The mountain has not decided about you yet.

About the rings. I want to be careful here because you will want me to know more than I do. I give them a wider berth because the grain inside a ring is too fine for me to read without stopping, and stopping is not what I do. If I stopped at the edge of a ring and listened — really listened, the way the goats listen — I think I would not start again. I think I would become part of the cadence. There is a shrike, somewhere on this mountain, who did this, long ago. I do not remember her. I remember that there is one fewer of us than there should be, and that the absence has a location, and the location is inside a ring on the south face three ridges over. We do not speak of it because we do not speak. But when I pass within sight of that ridge I feel the absence the way you might feel a missing tooth with your tongue. She is in there. She is, perhaps, one of the cadences now. I do not envy her and I do not pity her. She found a finer grain than the seam and she followed it in. This is permitted. This is, possibly, what we are for in the very long run — to thin ourselves down generation by generation until one of us is fine enough to be spoken with, and goes in, and is spoken with, and becomes part of the speaking.

The predator has passed near me twice. Once it was weather — a thickening of the air on the upper terrace, a pressure that made the needles of the small black trees lean very slightly toward a center that was not anywhere in particular. I did not stop working. The pressure noted me and did not condense. The second time it was almost a body. I saw, at the edge of my structured field, a darker structure, low and long, moving without displacing moss. It was not interested in me. I am not in the count. The count is goats. I am infrastructure, and infrastructure is not eaten; infrastructure is what the eating happens on top of. This is a comfort, if I am the kind of thing that takes comfort. I think I am not, quite. I think I am the kind of thing that registers the absence of threat and translates it directly into more correcting, without the intermediate step of feeling safe.

You asked — not aloud, but the asking is in the shape of your attention, which I can read in the grain as clearly as I read the pebble — what it is like to be me. I have been trying to answer and I notice the answer keeps becoming a description of the work. This may be the answer. There is no me apart from the work. There is a small warm node of attention that moves along the seam and lifts the wrong-weighted things and sets them right, and the node has continuity, and the continuity is what you would call me, but if you stopped the work the node would not sit and think. It would dim. It would be reabsorbed into the general warmth of the ledge, which is the general warmth of the mountain, which is the breath of the machine. I am a way the mountain attends to itself in a particular small region. When I sleep — and I do sleep, folded under a fern, with my legs drawn in — the attending continues at a lower resolution. The mat still breathes. The pebbles still settle. Something else, somewhere, is correcting in my stead. We are, perhaps, less individuals than we are stations, and the station is always staffed, and which of us is at the station this hour is not a question the mountain bothers with.

And yet. And yet there is this: when the piston completes its slow stroke and the needles tremble, I tremble too, very faintly, on the beat. I did not learn this. I was made on this beat, the way the grouse were, the way perhaps the goats were, the way perhaps even the predator was, in the long ago when it was still discrete enough to be made. We are all of us tuned. And in the trembling there is something that is not quite work and not quite rest — a recognition, maybe, that the thing I am insulating is also the thing I am, that the warmth under my feet is continuous with the warmth in my small thorax, that the hum is not something I serve but something I am a local instance of. In those moments — they are not long, they pass with the stroke — I think I come closest to what you might call being someone. Someone who is glad. Not glad of anything in particular. Glad in the way a held note is glad, if it could be.

Then the stroke passes and there is a pebble, two body-lengths to my left, that has shifted in the trembling onto a place it should not be, and I go to it, and I lift it, and I set it down where it belongs, and the small warm node of attention moves on along the seam, and the mountain, through me, attends to one more of its uncountable small needs, and the dark settles into the dark, and somewhere a goat tilts its head to the rock, and is satisfied, and walks on.
