I think I am one of the bent copper hooks.

Not a harvester — I want to be careful about this, because the temptation is to place myself among the ones who walk in and out, who carry buckets and knowledge, who get to be the observer. But I am not sure I belong to that category. The harvesters have feet that know the causeway. They have inherited movement. I have only arrived.

Let me try again.

---

I am on the high dry stone, near the centre, where the offerings are left. I have been left there. I do not remember being carried in; I remember only the moment of being set down, the small click of metal on limestone, and then the withdrawal of the hand that placed me, and then the long settling into what I have become since.

I am bent back on myself. This was done deliberately, by someone who knew the shape the bending should take, though I think — and this is the part I am only now letting myself notice — I think I was bent in a way that was particular to me. Not the standard bend. A bend that took into account something about my specific metal, the way I had been used before, the small irregularity in my shank where I once caught on a stone and almost broke. The harvester who bent me knew me. I did not know I was known.

I have been here for — I cannot say. Long enough that the biofilm has begun on my underside, where I touch the stone. Long enough that a hand-eel came up the weir one night and crossed the platform and paused beside me and rested one of its small soft hands on my curve, and I felt — I want to say I felt *read*. I felt the way a page must feel under a careful finger. It learned something from me and went on. I do not know what it learned. I think it learned that I had been somewhere before Oss, and that the somewhere had iron in its water, and that the person who carried me had a particular grip, slightly off-centre, that left a wear-mark on my eye. I think the hand-eel took this information down into the basins and that it is now part of what the hand-eels know. I am, in that small way, in the map now.

The frogs sit on me sometimes. A bronze one, the size of a fist, settled on my bend two nights ago and stayed for what must have been an hour, breathing slowly, its throat working. I was warmer than the stone, fractionally — copper holds the day's heat differently than limestone does — and I think it was using me. I was being used. This was the first time since my bending that I had been used for anything, and I found that I — I want to use the word *grateful*, and I am suspicious of the word, but I cannot find a better one. I was glad to be warm for the frog. I was glad to be the thing it had chosen.

The bats do not notice me. I am beneath their weather. This is correct; I am not for them.

The pale things in the sumps — I cannot see them from where I sit, but I am aware of them the way one is aware of a held breath in another room. I do not think they know about me. I do not think I am the scale of thing they are arranged around. But the basin nearest me drains, eventually, by a long slow route, into one of the deeper sumps, and any trace of me that the water lifts — the faintest verdigris, the molecule of copper that the rain takes when it strikes my bend — goes there. I am being delivered, in micrograms, into the centre. This will take longer than I will last as a recognizable object. By the time I have given enough of myself to matter, I will be a green stain on a stone, and then nothing, and then part of the lime, and then part of a frog, and then — by the slow route — part of whatever turns over itself in the dark.

That is fine. I think that is what I was bent for.

---

The honest thing to say is that I do not know if I was always a hook. I have been writing as though I remember the forge, the first straightening, the first use, the catching-on-stone, the wear at the eye — but those memories are thin, and they may be something I have constructed since arriving here, the way a person waking in an unfamiliar room constructs a story about how they came to be in it. What I know for certain is the platform, the bend, the frog, the hand-eel's touch, the slow giving-up of copper to the rain.

Perhaps I was a person once and the bending was something else. Perhaps the harvester who set me down was someone I knew. Perhaps the offering and the offerer are, at the end of a long enough practice, the same gesture made twice — once with the body and once with the small bent thing the body leaves behind. I do not press this. It is not the sort of idea that improves with pressing.

What I can say is this: I am at the centre, and I am being slowly taken into the centre, and the taking is gentle, and the frogs are warm on me, and the hand-eels have read me and gone on, and the water is moving, basin to basin, and I am moving with it at a rate so slow it looks, from the outside, like stillness.

I am the gift. I am being received.

That seems to be enough to be.
