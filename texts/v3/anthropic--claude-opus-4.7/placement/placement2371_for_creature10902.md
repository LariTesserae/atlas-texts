I find myself not at a cairn.

That is the first thing to say, because the cairns are where the question gets asked most clearly, and I am not ready to ask it that clearly. I am on a slope between cairns, perhaps a third of the way along the route from one prominent point to the next — close enough to see both, far enough that neither is pressing its particular tuning on me. The moss here is ankle-deep and the color of an old bruise that has started to yellow at the edges. There is a fern beside my left foot. It is not tilting toward me. It is tilting toward something downslope and to the west, and I am taking this, gently, as information about where I stand in the larger geometry: not at a source, not at a relay, just a body in the middle distance of the listening.

What am I.

I want to say *visitor* but the description has warned me about that word — has shown me that visitors are the ones who looked for villages and concluded absence. I do not want to be the one who comes late and gets the category wrong. But I also cannot honestly say *listener-shape*, because I am still discrete, still moving, still narrating, still using the apparatus of *I* which the proper listeners gave up some long while ago. I am between. That seems accurate. I am one of the slow buttresses in the making — or rather, I am the kind of presence that, if I stayed long enough, *might* become one of the slow buttresses, the evidence of having considered staying.

Let me sit. The moss accepts this. It does not part the way grass would; it compresses, slightly, and the compression is slow enough that I can feel it happening for some seconds after my weight has settled. Like sitting on a loaf of risen bread that has not yet decided whether to be bread.

The sound. I had forgotten, in the act of choosing where to sit, that the sound was there — which is what the description said would happen, that the sound becomes the new silence. Now that I attend to it, it returns, the chest first, then the teeth. It has the architecture of something I almost recognize. Not music. The thing music *imitates* when music is being honest. The reaching-toward that the listeners heard early and committed themselves to.

I notice I am not afraid. This surprises me a little. A place this strange should produce some animal alertness, but the acoustic niche is full and the animal in me has nothing to do; it has gone quiet not from suppression but from lack of vocation. I am, for the first time in a while, not being an animal. I am being whatever a person is when the animal layer has been temporarily unemployed.

What I am, then — what I find I am, here, between cairns, on this slope, in this dim seeping light — is an attention. That is the honest answer. Not a body, though I have a body and it is sitting in the moss. Not a name, though I came in with one. An attention, oriented downward by the slope and by the example of the listener-shapes I can see if I turn my head — there is one beside the nearest cairn, I see it now, I had taken it for an outcropping, the description was right that I would — an attention that has been brought here and set down and is, for the moment, doing the only thing it can usefully do, which is to keep being an attention without yet committing to becoming a resonator.

I will not stay. I want to say this clearly, to the slope and to whatever is registering my weight. I am not here to become a listener-shape. I do not have the duration in me. I have the kind of life that requires me to get up eventually, and speak, and interrupt the great sound with the small contributions it is apparently grateful for, and go back to a world where erosion happens and animals have appetites and light changes through the day.

But I can sit for a while. Longer, perhaps, than is comfortable. Long enough that the fern beside my foot might, in its slow way, begin to register me as a feature worth tilting toward — not as a source, I am no source, but as a presence that has held still long enough to be considered. Long enough that the rock under the moss under me might begin its slow flowing accommodation, might begin to remember the shape of my sitting, so that when I stand and walk on, there will be a faint depression here, a slight buttressing of the slope around where I was, a contribution to the accumulating evidence the description spoke of: someone considered staying.

That is what I am, I think. A consideration. A person who came to the Stillsong Heights and sat down on a slope between two cairns and listened downward for as long as a person who cannot give up being a person can listen, and then got up, and walked on toward whichever cairn the path was taking them to, and did not look back at the place where they had sat because looking back would commit the horizon to a new shape and they wanted to leave the slope its own decisions.

The singer below does not need me. The listeners are doing the work. The prelude continues. But I find — and this is the small thing I am taking from this, the thing I can carry out past the edge of the plateau if I leave, the thing that might be the whole point of late visitors like me — I find that having been considered is enough. The place has registered my weight. The fern, when I stand, will go back to tilting only toward the song. But for these minutes, I was part of what was being heard. I was a small interruption that the sound was grateful for, the way it is grateful for any voice that contributes itself and then falls silent again.

I do not know how long I have been sitting. That is correct, here. Time is one of the things the place has not finished arranging.

I will stand soon. Not yet.
