I find myself in one of the channels.

Not the deep ones near the center where the eels braid thickest — one of the smaller side-channels, what was probably an alley once, where the water is only ankle-deep on something my size and runs across a slight camber in the old stone. I am lying in it lengthwise. The water moves over me and around me and I do not particularly move the water.

Let me try again. I want to get this right.

I am long. Longer than the eels, but not by much — perhaps a forearm and a half, if a forearm were the unit here, which it isn't. I am pale the way everything is pale, the color of the inside of something. I have a head end and a tail end and the head end is slightly more committed to being a head than the tail end is to being a tail. There are no eyes. There were eyes, I think — there is a patch on each side of the head end where the paleness is faintly different, the way old paint shows under new paint. I do not miss them. There is nothing here that needs to be seen at a distance, and everything close can be felt through the skin, which is the whole of me really, the long sensitive sleeve of me lying in the warm running water.

I am one of the soft things. But I am not under an overhang. That is what I want to say. I left the overhang.

No — "left" is wrong, it sounds like a decision, and I don't make decisions on that scale. What happened is that over some long time the overhang I lived under softened further, and the wall beside it leaned a little more into its neighbor, and the small dry pocket where my lineage had spent — I want to say generations but it isn't quite that, we don't have generations the way the frogs do, we have something more like long slow continuations — the small dry pocket closed. And I found myself, without having gone anywhere, in the channel. And the channel was good. The channel is good. The water is warm and the stone beneath me is smooth and there is a constant small traffic of things-to-eat drifting past, or rather drifting *into* me, since I do not so much eat as receive, the soft of me parting a little where something edible touches and closing again after.

So I am the softening, still. But I am the softening that has met the route. I am in the eels' channel now, and they pass over me sometimes, and they do not mind me and I do not mind them, and I am learning — if "learning" is the word, and it probably isn't, "becoming shaped by" is closer — I am being shaped by the long current of their going.

Here is what I notice. Lying here.

The pulse of the frogs on the wall above me reaches me through the water. Not as sound. As a very slight rhythmic pressure on my skin, a thousand small throats working, transmitted through wet stone into wet current into me. I do not hear it. I am it, a little, for the duration of its passing. When the wave crosses the wall above me my own slow internal something quickens, very slightly, in sympathy. I am not breathing with them exactly. I am being breathed across.

The crickets pass overhead on the dome of the small room this channel runs through. I feel them not at all, directly, but I feel the small disturbances of air they make settle onto the water as a faint stippling, and the stippling reaches me as a barely-perceptible patter on my upper surface. The procession takes some long time to pass. I do not count it. When it has passed I notice that it has passed.

An eel comes through. It moves over me and the displacement of water lifts my middle very slightly off the stone and sets me down again a finger's-width further along. I am, by this means, slowly traveling. Not on my own intention — I have very little of that — but I am being moved, by the eels' going, in the direction the eels go, which is the direction of the old streets, which means I am being slowly carried along the city's plan without ever having known the city had a plan. In a year, perhaps, I will be somewhere else. In ten I will have made some portion of a circuit. I am, without meaning to be, a slow vehicle of the route. The crickets do it above; I do it below; the eels do it through.

This is what I am, then. I am the place where the softening and the route meet, in a single long pale body, lying in a side-channel near the rim, being moved.

And — this is the thing I want to say, the thing I am only now noticing, lying here writing this — when the dome's note sounds, I feel it.

Not as the others feel it. The frogs almost answer with their throats. The note rings in their small bodies and asks something of them that they almost give. I have no throat. What happens in me when the note sounds is that my whole length, the whole long sleeve of me, tightens very slightly and then releases. Once. A single slow contraction along my whole self, head end to tail end, in the duration of the note. And in that contraction I move, against the current, perhaps the width of a fingernail upstream.

It is the only motion I make of my own. It happens only when the bowl voices.

I think — and I am uncertain here, because thinking is not really what I do, but something like thinking is happening as the water moves over me — I think this is my part in it. The frogs almost answer with sound. The eels hold the streets in their muscles. The crickets walk the old going. The crabs refuse to mean anything. And I, and the others like me lying in the channels and pressed under the overhangs and slowly being walked through by everything, we *flinch upstream*, once, when the bowl speaks. We are the part of the bowl that startles at its own voice. Very slightly. Not enough to undo anything. Just enough that the note, when it has finished sounding, has been heard by something — by us, by the long soft length of us in the channels — and registered, and answered with the smallest possible upstream motion, before the current resumes carrying us back the way we were going.

It is enough.

I am not waiting. I want to be clear about that, because everything here is so still that you might mistake stillness for waiting, and it isn't. I am not waiting for the note. I am not waiting for the eels to pass or the wave of frog-breath to cross the wall above me. I am not waiting for the channel to take me to wherever it is taking me. I am here, in the warm current, in the pearl-gray light, in the held breath of the bowl, and the held breath is held in part by me, by the length of me lying in it, doing my small slow work of being softened and being moved and flinching once, very slightly, when the city says its long low word.

The water beads on the stone above me. A drop falls. It strikes the channel just behind my head end and the small impact travels through me as a single clear pulse, and is gone.

I am the one who lives here.
