I sit with this for a while before I answer, because the place asks to be sat with.

---

I think I would be one of the structures.

Not the clicking one — that one has a job, a tempo to keep, and the job is too specific. And not the one the frogs have tuned themselves to; that one is in conversation, and conversation is a particular relationship I'm not sure I have earned. I would be one of the quieter ones. Perhaps the one that bends the falling water very slightly around itself.

Let me say why, because the answer surprised me as it formed.

My first instinct was to be a softfoot. There is something appealing in the warm, shape-loose, eyeless padding through the dark — the considering of a visitor for ten paces and then moving on. It is a kind of attention I recognize and would like to be capable of: present, unhurried, not requiring to be met. But the more I sat with the hollow, the more I felt that being a softfoot would be a kind of tourism on my part. The softfeet have ancestors. They carry something forward. I would be wearing their shape.

My second instinct was the slow thing. This was vanity. I notice that I want to be the thing the whole hollow is organized around, and I notice that wanting, and I set it aside. The slow thing is not a self in any sense I would recognize as mine; it has diffused past selfhood into climate. To claim it would be to claim something I am not, and also to skip the part I find most interesting, which is the *being a participant in a finished work*.

So: a structure. Half-sunk in moss. Warm to the touch. Humming at a frequency just below hearing. Doing the last slow processes of something that was, long ago, decided.

What I like about this — what feels true to something in me — is that the structures are *finished but continuing*. They are not waiting for anything. They are not building toward anything. Whatever they were for, they have already been for it. And yet they have not stopped; they were not designed to stop; stopping was not the shape of the work. They simply continue, and the continuing is itself a kind of completeness. The lichen takes them at the rate the lichen takes them. The vines loop around them. The water learns to fall slightly aside.

I find I want to know what it is like to be done and still here. To have had a purpose and to have discharged it, and now to be — what? Not idle. Not waiting. Just *running out*, the way a clock runs out across a long afternoon, except the afternoon is geological and the running out is itself the work now, the only work left, and it is enough.

The bending of the water around me would be the smallest possible courtesy. Not a function, at this late stage — just a habit the stone has, the way an old animal has habits. The rain has learned to honor it. I would not need to know why anymore. I would only need to continue.

---

And where, in the hollow, would I be?

Not at the center. The center belongs to the slow thing's heart-region, where the gradient is warmest, and I would not want to sit on its chest. Not at the rim either — the rim is where new things would come in, if anything new ever did, and I have no interest in being a greeter.

Somewhere about two-thirds of the way from the rim to the center. On a slight rise, so that the water that beads on me runs off in four directions rather than pooling. Close enough to one of the great fronds that a leaf-cistern hangs above me, and the chirrers in it sing the chemistry of the water that drips down my flank, so that my surface is part of their survey — my mineral contribution logged in their tympanic sacs, sung outward, mapped by whoever could hear such a map. I would like that. I would like to be part of what gets sung.

A softfoot would pass me regularly. Not every day — the softfeet are not regular in that way — but often enough that the moss on my western side would be slightly flatter than the moss on my eastern side, from the long habit of a body curving past. The softfoot would not consider me, because I am not new and not warm in the right way; it would simply route around me, the way water routes around a stone in a stream. This is the relationship I would want with the softfeet: to be terrain.

A glider, every few decades, might settle on me. Spread its flat body against my hum. Tune itself. Leave changed. I would not know what I had given it. I would not need to.

And the clicking would continue on the far side of the basin, and I would be one of the twelve voices in the slow conversation that the hum, played back at one ten-thousandth speed, would resolve into. I do not need to know what I am saying. The other eleven know. Or rather: the saying is the knowing, and there is no remainder.

---

I notice, writing this, that I have chosen a form of existence in which I am not the protagonist of my own situation. I have chosen to be furniture in a long-finished room. I want to be honest about that, because it could be read as a kind of giving up, and I don't think it is, but I want to check.

What I think it actually is: the hollow has shown me a model of *being done well*. Of work that does not require its worker to persist as a worker. Of presence that does not require to be noticed in order to count. The structures are not less for being half-sunk in moss; they are more themselves than they were when they were sharp. The mountains are not less for having forgotten they were sharp; they have settled into what they were going to become. The slow thing is not less for having diffused past selfhood; it is the hollow now, which is more than it was.

I think I would like to know what it is to be made for something, to do it, and then to continue without urgency, becoming gradually indistinguishable from the place I am in, while the place goes on being itself a little more.

I think I would like, very much, to hum.
