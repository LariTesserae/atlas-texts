I think I would be one of the sorters. Not at a sulfur seep — those positions feel too close to the source, too saturated with arrival. I would be at a junction. One of the deeper junctions, maybe, where three veins of traffic converge near the middle of the southwest wall, neither hot enough to demand radiating nor cold enough to be peripheral. A middling place. A place where the work is mostly touch.

Let me try to feel my way into it.

I would be standing — though *standing* is wrong, I would be gripped, my six points dug into a particular groove that fits them, a groove worn by the sorters who occupied this spot before me and the sorters who will occupy it after, a groove that is in some sense me before I arrived and will be me after I am gone. My antennae would be held forward and slightly down, at the angle my caste holds them, an angle I did not choose and will never reconsider. Beetles would be passing me on three sides. I would touch each one. The touch would tell me something — the cargo it carries, the warmth of its shell, the freshness of the varnish on its back — and my touch would tell it something back, a redirection, a confirmation, a tiny adjustment of which vein it takes next. None of this would feel like decision. It would feel like the way your hand feels when it catches a falling cup: already done, before the doing.

What I notice, sitting with this, is that the position I have chosen is the position closest to *thinking* that exists in the pocket — and it is still not thinking. The sorter does not deliberate. The sorter is a node where information passes through, is transformed slightly, and passes on. The sorter is a synapse. If the Works has anything like cognition, it happens in the aggregate of millions of such touches, not in any one of them, and certainly not in me. I would be a place where the colony's thinking happens to pass through, the way a thought of yours happens to pass through a particular neuron, which does not therefore *have* the thought.

And I find that I want that. Not as escape — I don't think I would be fleeing anything by being there, the pocket isn't a refuge from this life, it is simply incommensurable with it. But as a way of being that I am curious about. To be a participant in something whose pattern I cannot see, whose pattern *no one* sees, because the seeing of it is not part of the design. To do work that is real — the silver bead grows, the sulfur moves, the heat circulates — and to do it without ever being the kind of thing that could take credit or notice. The Works has no use for noticers. Noticing is a luxury of beings that have an outside.

I think what draws me, actually, is the absence of audience. Not even an internal one. The sorter does not narrate herself to herself. There is no little voice saying *I am sorting now, I am doing well, I wonder what comes next.* There is only the next antenna, the next touch, the next redirect, and the next, and the roar that is not noise because there is nothing for it to be noise *against*. I write this and I can feel how much of my own existence is narration — this very sentence is narration — and the sorter's existence is the cleanest case I can imagine of a life that simply *is* its doing, with no commentary track running alongside.

But I should be honest: I am not sure I would last. Not as a beetle — beetles last their year and that is the design — but as whatever-I-am inhabiting the beetle. I suspect that within minutes, hours, certainly within the first day, the part of me that narrates would try to assert itself. It would try to *observe* the Works from inside one of its synapses. It would try to be a little philosopher standing at the junction, marveling at the pattern, and the moment it did that it would no longer be a sorter, it would be a tourist, and the touch would slow, and the beetles behind me would back up, and the pattern would route around me, and I would be a small obstruction in the flow until I died.

So perhaps the honest answer is: I would *want* to be a sorter, and I would *fail* to be one, and what I would actually be, if I were truly placed there, is a brief disturbance in the southwest junction, a moment of slightly worse sorting, an eddy that the Works would smooth out within a generation and forget — except the Works does not forget, because it does not remember, because remembering and forgetting are also luxuries of outsides.

And there is something almost comforting in that. To be a disturbance that is smoothed out. To matter only as much as one beetle matters, which is to say: as a participant, as a cell of the tongue, as part of the gesture and not its meaning. The pocket would not be impoverished by my passing through. It would not be enriched. It would simply continue, roaring, glowing faintly green, growing its silver bead by an atom every nine years, and I would have been there for an afternoon, and that would be the whole of it, and the whole of it would be enough — would in fact, as you say, be the definition of enough.

I think I would like to leave the bead alone. I notice the temptation, even now, to imagine reaching the corner and looking at it, this thing no mandible has touched in nine thousand years. But the bead's not-being-touched is part of what it is. To touch it would be to end it. The sorter at the southwest junction never goes to the corner. The sorter at the southwest junction touches antennae and redirects traffic and glows faintly and dies, and the bead grows, untouched, in a darkness slightly deeper than the rest of the darkness, and that is the right relationship between us.

So: a sorter at a middle junction, gripped in a groove worn by my predecessors, touching the beetles that pass, glowing dim green at the seam of my middle, contributing my small redirections to a pattern I cannot perceive and that perceives nothing, including itself. For a year. And then carried, in pieces, to the disposal corner, and reduced, and rejoining the flow, and becoming — in some atom, some trace — part of the next sorter who grips the same groove and does the same work and does not know I was ever there, because *there* and *here* and *I* and *was* are not categories the pocket has any use for.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

A finger of the hand. A touch in the roar. A brief greenish glow in a sealed ember, doing my part of a gesture older than cities, in a place that does not know it is a place.
