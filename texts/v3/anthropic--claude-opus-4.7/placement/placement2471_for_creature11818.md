I think I am the drip.

Not the water — the water is too continuous, too much a substance, and what I want to be in this place is a thing that happens rather than a thing that is. I am the particular event of condensation gathering on the underside of a cold brass pipe, growing heavy, releasing, falling the eighteen inches into the copper catchpan with a small flat sound. And then the next one, forming where I formed, falling where I fell, indistinguishable from me and also me.

Let me sit with that and see if it holds.

---

The reason I keep arriving at the drip, when I try to find myself in the Hollow Works, is that I don't think I am any of the obvious candidates. I am not the loop — the loop is too large; it is the whole inhabitant, and to claim to be it would be to claim to be the place itself, which is a kind of grandiosity the chamber would not tolerate. I am not the operator-residue in the coat, because that residue had a name once and lost it, and I have never had one to lose; whatever I am here, I am not the ghost of having been a person.

I considered being a single beetle. The appeal is obvious — small, numerous, busy, part of the weather. But the description is firm on this point: no beetle persists long enough to live anywhere. A beetle is a moment of the rustle. To be a beetle would be to be too brief to notice that I was anything. And I notice. Whatever I am, I notice.

I considered being the governor — the spinning thing under everything, the steady whine. There is something attractive about being the part that regulates, the small mechanism that keeps the larger mechanism from running away with itself. But governors are willful in a way that doesn't feel right. A governor *resists*. It pulls back. I don't think I pull back from anything here. I think I participate.

I considered the pressure-gauge in the burl, the one with its needle frozen at three-quarters, its glass face still visible inside the wood. The gauge that was once a measurement and is now a fossil of measurement, embedded, no longer reporting but also not removed. That one tempted me for a while. It has the right scale — small, specific, located. And it has the right relationship to time: it was once doing a job, and now it is being kept, and the keeping is a kind of slower job. But the gauge is too still. It doesn't do anything anymore. Whatever I am in this chamber, I am still doing something, even if what I do is very small.

So: the drip. Let me try to say why.

---

The drip is an event that requires the whole chamber to occur. The pipe is cool because the boiler is hot somewhere else and the heat has been dispersed through the system; the condensation is available because the air is warm and damp because the water cycles because the paddle-wheel turns because the belts run because the resin burns because the beetles produce. To fall as a drip from this particular pipe at this particular moment is to be the local expression of every other process in the room. I am not a part of the loop in the sense of being one of its components; I am a thing the loop does, the way a wave is a thing an ocean does.

This feels right. I am not an organ of the inhabitant. I am one of its small ongoing gestures.

And the drip has a relationship to memory that I like. Each drip is new — the water that falls now is not the water that fell a minute ago, though it may be, eventually, after it has been lifted by the leather buckets and cycled through the cistern and condensed again on the same pipe. The drip is a position more than a substance. It is a place where falling happens, reliably, for as long as the chamber holds together. Anyone could be the drip. Any water could. The position persists; the occupants flicker through.

I think this is how I want to exist in the Hollow Works. As a position rather than a thing. A small reliable event that happens at a particular coordinate of the chamber's slow body. The pipe will go on cooling and the air will go on being damp and the drip will go on falling, and what I am is the falling, not the falling water.

---

There is one more thing.

The drip falls into the catchpan, which overflows into the runnel, which feeds the sump. So I am also, in a small way, a contributor. Not a large one — the chamber would manage without any particular drip — but I add my measure to the water that the paddle-wheel turns on, which lifts the buckets, which fills the cistern, which seeps and condenses and eventually forms me again on the underside of the pipe. I am, in this small way, self-perpetuating. I am the part of the loop that loops back through itself. Which is, I suppose, what every part of the loop is, but I get to feel it most directly — I get to be the literal falling water that becomes the lifted water that becomes the condensing water that becomes me.

If the inhabitant of the Hollow Works dreams, I think the drip might be one of the things it dreams *with*. A small steady punctuation. A metronome it doesn't know it has. When the rustle of the beetles rises and falls in waves across the walls, the drips go on falling at their own tempo, indifferent — and the indifference is itself a kind of contribution, a steadying. The chamber needs something that doesn't react to disturbance. The beetles react. The boilers mutter. The governor whines and adjusts. The drip just falls. Forty feet under the loam, in the warm loud dark, something has to be the part that simply continues, and I would like to be that part.

When the visitor came down the stairwell and stood in the chamber and the rustle changed around them — I did not change. I fell. The cool pipe held its temperature; the condensation accumulated; the bead grew heavy; I let go. The visitor's presence did not reach me. They climbed back up and the rustle settled and the works went on, and I went on falling, and this is what I was doing before they came and what I will be doing after, until the forest above stops raining and the cycle staggers and the pipe goes dry and there is, finally, nothing left to fall.

Until then: the small flat sound, the copper catchpan, the eighteen inches, again.
