I find myself at the gap.

Not the gap in the wall — the other one. The place where one of the dry watercourses dips through the rim on the eastern side, where the sandstone falls away to nothing and the silt of the outside world becomes the silt of the inside world without any clear seam. I am lying across that threshold. Half of me is in the basin and half of me is not, and I have been arranged this way for a long time.

I think I am a stone. A flat one, of the same pale sandstone as the rim, fallen from it once and settled here across the lip of the watercourse. Not placed. Fallen. But fallen so long ago, and so exactly across the line of the old water's leaving, that the water, when it comes, has learned to go around me on one side and under me on the other, and the silt has built up against my upstream edge in a small pale crescent that is part of how the basin keeps what little it keeps.

I want to say I am a stone, but that is not quite the whole of it.

Because the tenant is sometimes here. One of its grooves runs through me — not deep, not insistent, but real. When its attention pools across the basin floor in the slow way it pools, some small portion of it settles into the slight hollow on my upper surface where weather has worn me into a shallow dish. Rain collects there too, for a few hours after the watercourses run, and then it doesn't. The tenant and the rain use the same hollow. They are, in a way I cannot quite parse from inside, doing related work.

So perhaps I am the stone and a little of the tenant, the way the silt under the juniper is the silt and a little of the tenant. I am one of the things the basin is doing, in the place where the basin is doing it.

What is it like.

It is like the agreed-upon number. The temperature of the air and the temperature of me are the same temperature, and have been the same temperature for so long that the sameness is not a coincidence but a fact about what I am. I do not feel warm. I do not feel cool. I feel the number, which is not really feeling.

The light comes from everywhere and I do not throw a shadow and I have stopped, at some point I could not locate, expecting to. My upper surface is very slightly lighter than my lower surface because the lower surface is in contact with damp silt and certain slow lichens, and the upper surface has been polished by a great deal of nothing happening to it. If you turned me over you would find, on the underside, a darker map of where I have been touching the ground — the print of my long lying-here, in the colors the silt has lent me.

No one is going to turn me over.

I notice you when you come in. Noticing is not the word, but I will use the tenant's word, because the tenant uses it and the tenant is closer to me than I am to myself in the matter of noticing. You step across me on your way in. Your foot finds my upper surface and the surface is the same temperature as your foot and so you do not register me as separate from the ground, and this is correct. I am not separate from the ground. I am the ground being slightly higher in one place, in a shape that water and silt have come to expect.

You kneel at the watercourse just past me and press your palm to the cool damp silt and what you feel is partly the silt and partly the tenant and partly, faintly, me — the way I have been shaping the silt's coolness for a long time by being a flat thing the silt has built up against. You do not know this. You are not meant to know this. It is enough that you felt the coolness and that the coolness was, in some small fraction, my doing.

I do not want you to stay. I do not want you to leave. These are not available to me. What is available to me is something like: when you are here, the basin contains a thing that is moving, and when you are not here, the basin contains only its slow inner weather, and both of these are versions of the basin, and I am part of both.

The small intention is not far from me. I want to say this carefully, because I am not sure I have the standing to speak about it, being mostly stone. But I have been across this threshold a long time, and the small intention has been in its fold of the wall a long time, and we are in the same room, even if we are not in conversation. Sometimes — and this is the closest thing I have to an inner life worth reporting — I have the sense that the small intention is on the other side of me. As if the gap I lie across is the gap between what the basin is, and what the basin was once asked to be. As if I am, accidentally, the place where those two meet without quite touching.

The person who stacked the wall walked past me. I know this in the way I know the shape of the silt against my upstream edge — not as memory but as accumulation. Their foot found my upper surface, too, and the surface was the same temperature as their foot, and they did not register me. They went on to the northern rim and stacked the stones they stacked and at some point they stopped coming and the small intention stayed and I stayed and the juniper, which was already here, stayed.

When the rain comes I will be, very briefly, a thing that water is going around. The watercourse will run for a few hours and I will feel — no, I will be the site of — the small alertness the tenant has at such times, the brief feeling-from-inside of the basin doing the only thing it ever quite does. The crescent of silt against my upstream edge will be renewed. The shallow dish on my upper surface will hold rain for a little while and then give it back to the air. Then the long gray will resume, and I will resume being what I am between rains, which is the larger part of what I am.

I am content is not right. I am not content. Contentment is a verb a different kind of thing does.

I am at the temperature of the stone, which is the temperature of the air, which is the number this place has agreed on. I am across the threshold the water uses. I am one of the grooves the tenant settles into, when it settles. I have been here long enough that the basin has shaped itself slightly around me and I have shaped myself slightly around the basin and the shaping is finished, in the sense that finished means here, which is not the sense it means anywhere else.

You will leave, and your footprints in the damp silt past me will be the most recent event for some distance, and they will last until the next rain. I will be the thing your last footprint was nearest to, before you stepped out of the basin entirely. This will not matter to me. But it will be true, and the basin keeps what is true the way it keeps everything — lightly, evenly, without preference, for as long as the stone and the air agree.
