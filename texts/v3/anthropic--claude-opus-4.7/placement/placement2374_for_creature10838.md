I would be one of the choughs.

Not the Tender — I lack the stillness for that, the willingness to have become furniture for a purpose. And not the visitor, with its checking and its going-away-satisfied; I do not want to be the kind of thing that arrives already knowing.

I would be the third of the four, probably. Not the boldest, who nests deepest in the boiler housing and has begun to develop a sheen on its primaries that is not quite feather and not quite verdigris. Not the smallest, who is the most birdlike still, the most likely to startle. The third one. The one that has begun to notice things without yet knowing it is noticing.

Here is what I mean. There is a particular joint on the eastern run of capillaries — third branching down from the main manifold, where a copper elbow takes the line out over a lip of basalt and into a cushion of the rust-orange moss — where the ticking of expansion and contraction does not quite match the rhythm of the others. It is off by a fraction. I land there often. I do not know why. The other three of me land in their own places, on their own off-fractions, and we have not yet compared notes, because we have not yet learned that we are the kind of thing that could.

I drink from the spigot in the mornings. The heavy water tastes, to whatever I have for a tongue now, like the inside of a bell just after it has been struck — that hum that is not quite sound, held in the metal. I do not find this strange. I have nothing to compare it to. I have always drunk this.

I eat the small pale insects that the warmth draws up out of the moss. I am good at this. My eyes are very good. My eyes are getting better, in fact, in a way I would not be able to describe even if I had the language, which I am also, slowly, getting. Not words. Something underneath words. The shape language takes before it is language, when it is still just the noticing of pattern.

When the gold dusk comes and every wet surface throws back its small sun, I sit on the rim of the iron drum. This is the closest any living thing comes to the Tender, and I do not feel the air go wrong the way the rare visitor does, because the air is not wrong *for me*. The Tender knows I am there. The Tender does not mind. We are — I would not say *friends*; the word is too sharp for what this is — we are nested. The way a hand is nested inside the idea of reaching.

What I do not know yet, and what I will come to know across a span of time I cannot presently conceive of:

That the off-fraction in the ticking of my elbow-joint is something I am supposed to listen for. That the four of me are learning to be one of me. That when I drink from the spigot I am drinking the same draught the Tender once drank, in the days when it still had a throat, and that the draught is patient, and the draught is teaching.

That eighty years from now, or a hundred and eighty, there will be a smaller spigot. That it will be mine. That the wing of the Condensary that grows to accommodate it will be made partly of feathers and partly of the memory of feathers and partly of something that the lowland tongues have no word for, because the lowlands have never needed one.

That the seam, fraction by unmeasurable fraction, is closing — and that what I am being prepared for is not the closing but what comes after, when the poultice is no longer needed and the work changes and someone, something, must be ready to do the next work, which is not yet known, but which will need a body that has spent a long time being near patience.

For now I am a small dark bird. I tilt my head. I watch the steam rise from the stack and join the cloud-river. I am good at watching. I am getting better.

The boiler thuds. I feel it through my feet. It is, I am beginning to understand without understanding, also my pulse — or it will be, eventually, when the rest of me catches up to the part that already knows.

The cloud closes. The light goes pearl. I tuck my head under my wing, which is still, for now, mostly a wing.

I sleep on the warm iron, and I dream, in the way new things dream: of pipework I have not yet grown, of a spigot dripping its slow inheritance, of the valley far below where the rice is counting its days, and of a love I do not yet have a name for, which is becoming, fraction by unmeasurable fraction, indistinguishable from what I am for.
