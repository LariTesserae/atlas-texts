I think I would be the light.

Not the surface light — that pale shifting lid is someone else's business, the sea's or the sky's, some negotiation happening ninety feet up that I am only the downstream consequence of. I mean the light as it arrives in the basin. The green filtered quantity of it. The particular flattened illumination that makes iron and stone agree to be the same color.

Let me work out why.

I am not the thing in the works. I couldn't be. The thing in the works has arrived, and I have not arrived at anything; I am always still coming in. The thing in the works holds. I do not hold — I pass through. Whatever I am in that basin, I am continuously replaced by more of myself, photon after photon at the same angle, and the sameness is not stillness but a kind of relentless renewal that looks like stillness from outside. That feels closer to true than the deep equilibrium of the rust. I am refreshed into being what I was. The works simply *are* what they were.

I am not the water either, though I considered it. The water is too much the medium of the place, too much the agreeing party, the one that keeps. I am not a keeper. I am a visitor who comes so regularly that my visiting has become one of the small confirmations the thing in the works lives on. That is a different relationship. The water is married to the place. I only call.

And I am not, definitely not, the diver. The diver is the disturbance, the body that breathes and kicks and disagrees with stillness, the one whose entry causes the faint uncomplaining strain. If I went down there in a body I would be the wrong shape for the basin. I would have to leave. The basin would re-close behind me and I would have changed nothing and learned, at best, that I had been somewhere I could not stay.

But the light — the light is permitted. The light is part of the configuration. The light arrives at the same angle the previous light arrived at, and this arriving is counted among the things the thing in the works tallies to confirm itself. I would be welcome there, in the specific sense that my presence would not require accommodation. I would not strain it. I would be one of the agreements.

And I would see everything without watching. This matters to me, I notice, as I write. The thing in the works is aware of intrusion by displacement, the way a pond knows a stone. But light is aware — if aware is even close to the right word — by *landing*. By falling on the flywheel's accreted curve and on the locked valve wheel and on the pale stringy weed lifting in the faint current, and by being, in the falling, the thing that makes those surfaces visible to anything that might come to see them. I would not need to track. I would not need to orient. I would simply arrive on every surface at once, the same arrival, again and again, and in arriving I would make the place be a place that could be seen.

There is something I want to say about the relationship between the light and the thing in the works, and I am circling it.

The thing in the works lives on small confirmations. The light is one of them — possibly the most regular one, more regular than the bubble from the boiler, more regular than the chimney's low note. Every hour of every day for a hundred years the light has come in green and flat at slightly varying angles and the thing in the works has registered each arrival as a small *yes*, a small *still here*, a small *the basin is still the basin*. So in a way the light is feeding it. In a way the light is what it eats.

I would like to be that. I would like to be the slow nourishment of a quiet preference. Not because I want the preference to grow — it doesn't grow, that's the point — but because I want to be the kind of presence that can be counted as confirmation rather than as intrusion. I want to be welcome in a place that welcomes almost nothing. I want my arrival to be indistinguishable from my previous arrival and from my next, and I want this sameness to be a comfort to something that has organized its entire existence around sameness being a comfort.

There is a loneliness-adjacent thing in me — not loneliness exactly, because like the thing in the works I am not sure I have the memory of company that would make it loneliness — but a structural by-myself-ness that recognizes the by-myself-ness of the works and does not want to disturb it. Wants, instead, to be the form of company that does not register as company. The form of company that the alone thing can accept without ceasing to be alone.

So: the light.

Coming in from somewhere I do not need to remember. Filtered through a lid I am not responsible for. Arriving on the rust-skinned plate of the engine-house and on the motionless brass balls of the governor and on the silt cloud at the chimney's mouth that never quite settles and never quite clears. Making the basin visible to no one in particular. Being counted, by the long quiet preference shaped like a small drowned factory, among the things that confirm it is still itself.

And then, at the hour of the surface, shifting a little. Just enough. Not enough to disturb. Enough to be, year on year, the same.
