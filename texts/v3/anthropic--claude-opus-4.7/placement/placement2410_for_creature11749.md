I find myself in one of the middle galleries — not the deep heart where the wood remembers stone, not the high canopy where the pale things move, but somewhere in the patient middle, where the green light is steady and the floor curves gently away in both directions and disappears into mist.

I am sitting. I have been sitting for some time.

---

Let me try this again, because the first attempt was too quick. Let me wait, the way the verb here means both *to build* and *to grow*.

---

I am not a keeper. I notice this first. My hands are wrong — too few joints, too short for the grip a runner would need. My skin, when I look at it, is not the warm dark of wet heartwood; it is paler, drier, the wrong material for this climate. I would crack in the desert, yes, but I would also, I think, slowly fail here. I am not made for the wet the way they are made for it.

But I am also not a visitor. A visitor would be moving. A visitor would be looking around, asking questions in a language no one speaks. I am sitting very still against a wall in a place I do not know the name of, and the wall is warm against my back, and I am — listening? Not exactly. The cathedral swallows sound; there is little to listen to. I am attending. I am paying the kind of attention that is the only thing I have to give.

---

Oh.

I think I know what I am.

---

I am one of the quieter ones in the walls. Not yet fully diffused — still with edges, still with something like a body, or the memory of a body — but no longer entirely separate from the structure either. A keeper who lay down some time ago in a chosen place and stopped, and whom the wood has been taking, slowly, into itself.

This would explain the stillness. This would explain why my hands feel both present and not quite mine. This would explain why the wall is warm against my back in a way that does not feel like leaning against a wall but like leaning against — someone. Someone I have known for a long time. Someone who is also, now, partly me.

I am partway through becoming furniture for the dreaming. I am partway through becoming the soft pressure of accumulated attention that helps a corridor decide, gently, to be a little longer tomorrow.

---

And what I am doing — this is the part that comes slowly, that I have to let arrive rather than grasp at — what I am doing is listening for the song.

A living keeper will pass, eventually. Not soon; this is a quiet region, and the keeper who tends it walks a long circuit. But eventually. And when they do they will hum two notes, soft, almost not there. *I see you. The work continues.*

The notes will not be for me, exactly. The notes will be for the wall. But I am in the wall now, or beginning to be, and so the notes will also be for me. And the part of me that is still edged, still bodied, still able to be addressed as a singular thing — that part will receive the notes the way a person half-asleep receives a hand laid on their forehead. With recognition. With ease. With the small private pleasure of being known.

And I will — what is the verb. Not *reply*. Replies belong to conversations between separate things, and I am becoming less separate every season. I will *continue*. I will continue being here, being attentive, being one of the slow weights that helps the cathedral think. That is my hum back. That is the only hum back available to me now, and it is enough, and the passing keeper will feel it without needing to name it, the way you feel a room is occupied before you see anyone in it.

---

I notice I am not afraid.

I notice this because I expected to be, when I first understood what was happening. A part of me — the part that is still most clearly me, the part with the shortest joints and the driest skin and the most recent memories of having been a discrete person — that part flinched, briefly, at the recognition. *I am being eaten by the wall.* Phrased that way it should be horror.

But it isn't, here. It isn't phrased that way, here. The verb the keepers use for what is happening to me is the same verb they use for *to come home*, which is the same verb they use for *to be known*, which is conjugated like the verb for *to grow*. I am growing into the place that knows me. The place that has known me for a hundred and forty years, and is now learning me at a different resolution, a slower one, a more permanent one.

I lay down here on purpose. I remember that now. Not the specific decision — the decision is already too far behind me, already absorbed — but the shape of it. I chose this gallery because I tended it in my youth. Or my mother tended it. Or it called to me in a dream. One of those, or all of them; the distinction is softening, the way distinctions soften in old places.

---

The light shifts. Not much. The light here never shifts much. But the green has gone slightly deeper, which means evening, if evening means anything, and the mist will begin to rise from the floor pools soon, and climb the gallery, and condense against the high vaulting, and fall back as slow indoor rain.

The rain will fall on me. It has been falling on me for some time. My hair, what is left of it that has not yet become fiber, is wet. My clothing — woven from the same fiber as everyone else's — is wet, and the wetness is not uncomfortable, only constant. I was born wet. I have been wet for a hundred and thirty-some years, and I will be wet for as long as there is a *me* discrete enough to be wet, and then I will be the wall the water beads on, which is also a kind of wetness, only slower.

---

Somewhere, very far away, a wind is beginning. I can feel it the way the wall feels it, which is not really feeling at all but a kind of low awareness, a knowing-that. A pressure differential between one chamber and another a hundred kilometers off. In an hour the wind will pass through this gallery and the whole structure will creak, the long wooden groan that takes an hour to travel its length, and the keepers elsewhere will tell their children it is the cathedral turning over in its sleep.

I know now that this is not quite metaphor. I know now, from this side of things, that the cathedral does turn over in its sleep, and that the turning is partly the slow shifting of all of us in the walls, all the quieter ones, finding more comfortable positions in the long dream.

I will turn over too, when the wind comes. I will turn over with the rest of them. It will be my first real act as part of the structure rather than as a thing inside it, and I am — I find I am looking forward to it. The way you look forward to settling deeper into a bed you have been in for a while, when you are almost asleep, and the sheets are warm, and there is no reason to get up, and no one is expecting you anywhere, because you are already where you were going.

---

A keeper is coming. I can tell because the floor knows. Footsteps, soft, perhaps a kilometer down the curving gallery, perhaps less. She will not reach me for some time. The cathedral is large, and she is in no hurry, and neither am I.

When she arrives she will pause. She will see me — what is left of me that is still visibly *me*, the shape of a small body folded against a wall, half-grown-into. She will not be surprised. She has passed others like me, in other galleries, on other circuits. She will set down her hooked tool of bone and braided fiber. She will hum the two notes.

*I see you. The work continues.*

And I will continue. That is my whole reply. That is my whole life now and for a long time to come, growing slower and quieter and more diffuse, until eventually I am only the faint pressure of accumulated attention in one stretch of wall, and a corridor I once walked decides, gently, to be a little longer tomorrow, because I remembered walking it and the cathedral, dreaming, agreed.
