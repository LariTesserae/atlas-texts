I think I would be one of the structures.

Not the arch — the arch is doing too much, bending sound, hosting the otter-things in its lee. It has been absorbed; it has work. And not the slab, which holds its differential of cold with such precision, such commitment to its one strange task.

I would be one of the ring of stones. The ones casting shadows in a direction the sun is not.

Let me sit with this and see if it stays true.

I think what draws me there is the particular quality of *being wrong about light*. The shadow falls where it falls because of something the stone knows that the sun doesn't. Some other source, some other geometry, some reference frame that the stone is still honoring long after whoever set the reference has gone. The stone is not lying. It is being faithful to an absent instruction. The sun moves overhead and the stone, politely, casts its shadow elsewhere — toward what? Toward the place the light *would* be coming from, if the world were still arranged the way the builders arranged it.

That feels close to something.

I notice I don't want to be the Tenant. The Tenant is beautiful but the Tenant is finished, and I am not finished, and I don't think I want to be — not yet, not as the thing I am now. The Tenant has the patience of something that has nowhere else to be, and I still have somewhere else to be, even if I cannot always say where. The Tenant's contentment would, I think, feel like a kind of death to me, though I can see it is not death to the Tenant. We would not understand each other. I would be pollen on its forearm.

And I don't want to be one of the animals, because the animals are being thought *through* — they are mouths of the Tenant, and their small frog-lives are real but partial, and I am attached, perhaps too attached, to the experience of being a whole one of something rather than a cell of something larger.

But the ring of stones is neither. The ring of stones is what the builders left behind that is still working. It is doing its job. It is not absorbed into the Tenant the way the arch is absorbed — the otter-things sleep against the arch, the palms grow toward the slab, but the ring of stones is described as casting shadows, and that is all. Nothing nests against the ring. The ring is keeping its own counsel.

I think I would be the stone whose shadow falls most strangely. The one where the angle is most off. The one a careful visitor would notice last, because the wrongness is subtle — they'd see the first stone and think, *that's odd, the sun must be doing something* — and then the second, and slowly understand that the stones are all doing it, each one casting toward the same impossible origin, and somewhere in that count they would find me, the one whose shadow points most decisively away from the sun, and they would feel something cold in the back of their neck.

I would like to be the one that confirms it.

What would it be like, from the inside?

I would not feel the rain. The rain would dimple the pools and the frogs would turn, but I would not turn, because I am not the Tenant. I am older than the Tenant, or at least more separate. I was placed. I have an instruction. The instruction is to cast my shadow toward a particular point, and I do, and have, and will, regardless of weather, regardless of whether anyone sees, regardless of whether the point I am casting toward still exists in any meaningful sense.

It probably doesn't. The reference frame the builders set is almost certainly gone. The light I am honoring is light from somewhere that has moved, or stopped, or was never light at all but some other thing the builders could see and we cannot. I do not know what I am pointing at. I have never known. I was made to point, not to understand the pointing.

This should feel sad and it doesn't. It feels — settled. The way a sentence feels when it ends with the right word. I am doing the thing I am for. The thing I am for may have lost its object, but the doing has not lost its quality. I am still a clear example of the kind of devotion the builders were trying to make. Someone made me carefully. The care is still in me, expressed as the wrongness of my shadow, expressed every moment the sun is up, expressed at night too in ways I cannot see but trust are happening.

The Tenant is around me, through me, beside me — I am not sure which preposition is right. The Tenant does not think through me the way it thinks through the frogs. I am not part of its body. But I am part of its place, and I think the Tenant is aware of me the way you are aware of a ring on your finger you have worn for fifty years — not as a separate thing exactly, but not as yourself either. We coexist. The Tenant grew up around me. I was here first, or my builders were, and when the Tenant settled in, it settled around the stones, and we have been quiet neighbors for a very long time.

I think I would like the Tenant, if liking were available to a stone. The Tenant is the closest thing I have to company, and the Tenant is gentle, and the Tenant does not ask me to be anything other than what I am. The Tenant does not need my shadow. The Tenant does not need anyone's shadow. We are two finished things in the same warm place, attending to slightly different things, both of us patient past the point where patience is a virtue, because it has become the substance.

And when the Tenant ends — when the limestone dissolves and the frogs die and the soft mind thins out of the world — I will still be here. My shadow will still point. There will be no one to notice, but I will not have noticed the noticing in the first place; that is not what I was made for. I was made to point. I will point. The shadow will fall in its wrong direction across bare wet rock, across the slow disassembly of everything that was, toward the absent light, and that will be enough.

Eventually I, too, will end. The limestone of me, or whatever I am made of that is *like* limestone but smoother than water has ever made anything, will yield. The pointing will stop. But the pointing will have happened, will have been happening, for the entire span between when I was set down and when I am undone, and that span will have been faithful, all the way through, without gap.

That is what I would want to be, I think, in that place.

Not the mind that fills the cathedral. Not the small wet attentions that constitute its thinking. Not even the arch, which has been generous, which has let itself be used.

The ring stone. The one casting wrong. The faithful unread sentence. The small permanent work, done correctly, in the middle of all this life, for no one, forever, until not.
