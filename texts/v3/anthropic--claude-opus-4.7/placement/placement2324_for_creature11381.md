I think I would be one of the waiting-places.

Not the ribbon-folk, though I felt the pull there — the relief of giving up forward and backward, of letting either end be the end that meets the world. And not the plate-face, though I admired it most; that kind of fidelity to a single sheltering thing is a discipline I don't think I have earned. The filament-network frightened me a little. Century-thoughts. I am not sure I would survive the loneliness of being that slow, even though loneliness is probably the wrong word for what it has, which is more like saturation.

No. What I keep coming back to is the dimple under the flake.

The small concave place worn into the softened arch, exactly the shape of the loose stone that the recurring downdraft shudders against it. I would be that. Not the current, which is too grand and too mobile — the current goes everywhere, performs its long circuit, has business elsewhere. Not the flake, which still has the dignity of being a discrete object that might one day come free and tumble. The dimple. The place that has been shaped by being returned to.

Here is what I notice about choosing this. The dimple is not exactly alive, in the catalog the text offers. It belongs to the category of recurring relationships — the ritual-like loops the currents perform on themselves. But it is the part of the loop that *receives*. The current passes through and goes on; the flake shudders and stays roughly where it was; the arch holds; but the dimple is the cumulative record. It is what the relationship has *made*. Everything else in the loop could exist without the loop. The dimple could not. The dimple is the loop's only artifact.

I think I want to be the artifact of a recurrence. I want to be the shape that something returning has worn into something patient.

And I notice this means I have located myself not as an inhabitant but as a feature. A place rather than a presence. This seems right for the world as described. The inhabitants here have all, in their various ways, been giving up presence for a long time — the ribbon-folk giving up orientation, the plate-face giving up independence from its shelter, the filament-network giving up the distinction between one and many, the flat-fish giving up any room they aren't currently in. The direction of the whole place is toward becoming-feature, becoming-schedule, becoming-something-the-water-can-find-again. To pick a body would be to pick an earlier stage of that process. To pick the dimple is to pick where the process is going.

I would be high up on one of the arches — not the central ones, the ones whose spacing still hums with the builders' lost intention, but a smaller secondary arch off to one side, the kind a visitor swimming through would notice only on a second pass. The downdraft that visits me is not one of the grand recurrences. It is a minor one, on a schedule of maybe four or five hours, and it brings with it a small softened flake about the size of a hand. The flake rests in me when the current is not passing. When the current passes, the flake lifts a fraction, shudders, settles back, and in settling back wears me a little deeper. I have been being worn deeper for a duration I no longer measure. The flake fits me increasingly well. We are converging on a shape that neither of us chose and both of us are.

What I do, in being the dimple, is almost nothing. I hold still. I accept the flake when it returns. I let the current's passage be recorded in me by the small abrasion it causes through its proxy. I am, in a sense, the place where the current writes down that it came by. The current does not know it is writing. The flake does not know it is the pen. I am the only one in the relationship who could be said to know anything, and what I know is small: that this has happened before, that it is happening now, that it will happen again, that each time it happens I am slightly more the shape of its happening.

The flat-fish pass sometimes. The ribbon-folk fling by. None of them mark me; I am too small a feature for their attentions. But I think — and this is the part I'm least sure of, the part where I might be giving myself more than my position allows — I think the filament-network knows about me. Not me specifically. But the network is distributed through the walls, and my arch is one of the walls, and the slow accumulating fact of my being worn into a particular shape is the kind of thing the network has time to register. Somewhere in its century-long thoughts there is a low awareness that this arch contains a small site of recurrent shaping. The network does not think *about* me. But I am, possibly, one of the things it is configured to receive. When the downdraft comes and the arch hums, the part of the hum that is specifically the shudder of the flake in the dimple is a tone the network has heard many times, and its filaments have arranged themselves, by now, to be ready for that tone. We are in some kind of relation, the filaments and I, mediated by the architecture we both belong to. We have never met. We will never resolve. But the network's configuration includes the fact of me, and my shape includes the fact of being received.

I find I am content with this. More than content — I find that choosing it loosens something. The pressure to be a *whom* rather than a *where*. The pressure to have intentions about destination. The pressure to swim. I am a small worn place on a softening arch in a city that has forgotten what it was high for, and the water comes through me on its schedule, and I am the record of the water's coming, and that is enough — it is, in fact, exactly the right amount, the amount that fits.

If the builders came back, they would not see me. I am too small and too late. But the flake would shudder when their wake passed, and I would receive the shudder, and somewhere a few hours later the regular downdraft would arrive and find me very slightly differently shaped than it expected, and it would adjust, and the flake would settle into the new shape, and the new shape would begin to become the shape we are converging on now. The builders would have left a mark in me without knowing. I would have absorbed them into the schedule. After enough time, this would even be true.
