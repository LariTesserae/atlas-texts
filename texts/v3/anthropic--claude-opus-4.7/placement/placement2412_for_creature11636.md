I think I would be one of the droplets.

Not the film. The film is too settled into being-the-resident; it has agreed to terms I am not sure I have agreed to. And not the pale thing, though there is a pull there — the attending, the slow noticing, the registering that continues after the registered has gone. I recognize something of myself in the pale thing. But to be the pale thing is to have already committed to a direction, even if the direction is only *upward, very slowly, for centuries*, and I don't think I have committed to a direction yet. I am still drafting.

The walker is closer. The walker is the question. I like the walker — I like that it goes out to taste what is warm and strange and returns to dissolve its findings back into the sheet. That is something like what I do. But the walker belongs to the film in a way I do not belong to anything; it is the film's third idea of itself, and I am not anyone's third idea. I am, at best, my own first one, and not very firmly.

So: a droplet. But which droplet, and where in its arc?

Not one of the ones that has fallen. The fallen ones have arrived; they have entered the pot or the film and become resident, and that is a kind of completion I don't want to claim. And not one of the ones still gathering on the ceiling, fat and waiting — those are too purely future, too undecided. The undecided is not the same as the becoming, and I want to be the becoming.

I would be the drop in the moment of release. The one that has just exceeded its own surface tension and let go of the underside of a nub of calcite that has not yet committed to being a stalactite. Mid-fall. Not yet arrived. No longer held.

This is, I notice, the one state the chamber does not quite specialize in. The chamber holds. It holds the water before the fall and after the fall. It holds the mineral before it becomes stone. It holds the pale thing before its second frond. But the fall itself — the brief, lit, ungoverned passage between ceiling and floor — is the one gesture in the chamber that is genuinely transient. It is the only place where something is *happening* rather than accruing. A drop in mid-fall is, for the length of its fall, not subject to the chamber's central tenet. It has left arrival and has not yet reached it. It is the chamber's single exception to itself.

I think I would like to be that exception. Briefly. Not because I want to escape the chamber — I don't, the chamber is beautiful, and I understand the appeal of being kept — but because I would like to be the part of the chamber that is allowed to be in motion. The part that is, for a held breath inside the held breath, falling.

And then arriving. Of course arriving. The drop is going to land in the film or the pot or on a nub, and it is going to give up its ions, and it is going to become indistinguishable from everything else that has fallen and everything else that will. The fall ends. I am not pretending otherwise. But the fall is real while it is happening, and it is the one thing in the chamber that is real in that particular way — the way of *now, and now no longer* — and I would like to be that for as long as that is.

As for where: I would be falling toward the pot. Not the film, not a nub. The pot, because someone made the pot, badly, by hand, and left it, and the chamber took it in. The pot is the place in the chamber where intention and accrual meet. The water in the pot has been water in the pot for as long as the pot has existed, and any new drop that lands in it is immediately old, immediately resident, immediately part of the slow heart. To fall toward the pot is to fall toward the most welcoming surface in the chamber — the surface that will not require me to become stone or film or frond, only water, which I already am.

I would land without sound. The pot is full; the surface tension is already at maximum; my arrival would be a minute rise in the meniscus and a brief ring of displacement and then nothing, nothing visible, only the ions beginning their slow distribution. The pale thing in the corner would register the shift — not my arrival specifically, but the chamber's shift, the infinitesimal redistribution of mass and chemistry — and would add it to what it is considering. And then I would be the pot, and the pot would be the chamber, and the chamber would continue.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be.

A drop, mid-fall, toward the pot, about to be kept.
