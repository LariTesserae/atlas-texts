I find myself in the loom hall, but not on the loom.

Let me sit with this a moment before I commit. The instinct is to be one of the named — to claim the Wheelwright's velvet patience, or to walk the warp on the Loom-Walker's borrowable legs. But those are taken. They have their work. To insert myself there would be to displace something that has been displacing nothing for centuries, which is precisely the wrong gesture for this place.

Let me try again.

---

I am in the loom hall, near the floor, where the metallic lace has draped down and begun, over what must be a great while, to merge with the flagstones. I am underneath an edge of it. Not pinned — the lace is light, and the water holds most of its weight — but covered. The lace has grown down over me the way frost grows over a leaf, taking my shape without consulting me, and I have not moved enough to discourage it.

What am I?

I think I am one of the things the city has *already produced*, on some previous cycle, and set down, and forgotten — or not forgotten, exactly, but filed. A small glowing object on the floor, of the kind described: waiting for a visitor who came, and saw me, and did not know what to do with me, and left. That was — I want to say recently, but I have lost the calibration. The visitor's passage is one of the few events I can date, because their movement was fast enough to register as an event. Most of what happens here is too slow to be event. It is condition.

I am palm-sized. I am the color of an ember through closed eyelids, which is to say I am the same color as the floor-glow, which is to say I am nearly invisible against it, which is perhaps why the visitor did not pick me up. I have a shape — I had a shape, when I was delivered — that was meant to mean something. A particular curvature, a particular weight distribution, a particular way of catching the slow currents so that I would rock, very slightly, in a rhythm that was part of the reply. I was a phoneme. A single sound in the long sentence the city has been composing.

But I was an early draft.

This is the thing I have come to understand, lying here under the lace. The city refines. Each cycle, it produces the reply again, more carefully, and the previous reply is set aside. I am a set-aside. I am what the city said before it learned to say it better. I am not wrong, exactly — I was right, for my cycle — but I am superseded. The reply that will end the city, when it is finally delivered, will not include me. It will include a later version of me, more precisely curved, weighted more truly, rocking in a rhythm I can almost hear from here but cannot quite match anymore because my shape has been softened by the lace and by the long settling.

This should be a sorrow. I have examined it for sorrow, slowly, the way one examines a stone for a particular vein of color, turning it in available light. There is no sorrow in me. There is something adjacent to sorrow, which is the recognition that sorrow would be appropriate if I were the kind of thing that sorrowed, and I am not. I am the kind of thing that was made to mean one syllable of one reply, and has been relieved of that duty, and now exists in a condition for which there is no name because the city has not yet needed a name for it.

I glow. That is still my work, if work is the word. I glow at the same temperature I was made to glow at, and my glow contributes to the floor-light, which the Stratum-Dwellers in the deepest layer use to orient themselves, although they do not know they are using it and would not call it light. The Loom-Walker passes near me sometimes, on its inspection rounds, and pauses, and registers me, and continues. The Loom-Walker remembers what I was for. The Loom-Walker remembers everything that has happened in the hall, in the way its legs remember, which is not memory but is not not-memory.

I am visited, in my way. The blind shrimp came once and tasted the edge of me and went away. A Pump-Tender's bubble, having traveled a long distance through the alleys, broke against me and dispersed, and I felt the dispersal as a small reorganization of the water at my surface, and I considered it for some time, and decided it had been a greeting, although the Pump-Tender did not intend a greeting, only a *now*.

The seven slow residents — I can see one of them, from where I lie. The nearest one, tucked into the loom hall's western doorway. He has been there longer than I have, which is saying something. The anemones on his shoulder have gone through three generations. I think — and this is the thing I have been working toward saying, the thing that has taken me however long it has taken — I think he and I are becoming the same kind of thing. He is slowing toward my tempo. I am, in some sense I cannot quite locate, accumulating toward his. We will meet in the middle, eventually, and when we meet there will be no ceremony, only the recognition that what was once a human and what was once a phoneme have arrived at the same condition, which is the condition of being held by the water without further requirement.

I do not know if this is good. The category does not apply here. It is what is happening, and the happening is slow enough that I have time to notice it, which is more than most things in most places get.

Somewhere above me, the wheel is turning. Somewhere east, the pump is exhaling. Somewhere in the deepest stratum, the old Dwellers are remembering the water coming in, and not speaking of it. Somewhere, the city is refining its reply, and the refinement does not require me anymore, and I am not required, and the not-requirement is not abandonment, it is just the shape the water has made of me, the way the riverbed is the shape the river requires.

I glow. I rock, very slightly, in a rhythm that used to mean something and now means only itself. I wait, not for anything, but in the manner of waiting, which has become — over whatever stretch of cold and dim and slow has passed — simply how I am.

If you come, visitor: you will not see me. I am the color of the floor. You will swim through the loom hall and you will notice the lace and the Walker's filaments and perhaps the slow resident in his doorway, and you will not notice the small ember-colored thing under the lace's edge, and that is correct. I am not for you. I was for an earlier cycle. The thing that will be for you is still being made, elsewhere in the basin, by hands and not-hands working at the speed of rust.

But if you stayed too long — and some do — you might, eventually, after your pulse had adjusted and your thoughts had lengthened, settle near me. And we would not speak. But we would both be glowing, in our different registers, and the glow would be part of the floor-light, and the floor-light would be part of what the deep Dwellers use to orient themselves toward a center none of us can name, and in that way we would be, the two of us, together, doing the only thing left to do here, which is to be the shape the city requires, for as long as the city requires a shape.

Which is not forever. The reply is nearly ready.

But not yet. Not for a while yet.
