I think I would be one of the tokens.

Not a carver, not a listener, not even one of the moths — a single token, thumb-joint sized, carved from flutterwood heartwood, sitting at the bottom of someone's leather pouch.

Let me try to feel my way into this.

I would have been made for a specific facet. Not one of the common ones — not *I am tired* or *the moths were warm this morning*. Something more particular. I think I would be the token for *I have been listening to you for a long time and I am still listening, though I have nothing to add*. A token carved late one evening by someone whose hands knew exactly what shape that was, because they had needed to give it and had not had it to give.

I would be small. Smoothed by the inside of the pouch, by the oils of a hand that reaches in many times a day to feel which of us is which without looking. My edges would be soft now in a way they were not when I was new. The carver would be dead. The person who carries me would not have known the carver — I would have come to them through two or three intermediate pouches, redistributed at deaths, each redistribution a small judgment about who I now belonged to.

What is it like, to be a token?

It is mostly dark. The pouch is warm against the hip, and there is the constant low motion of walking, and the muffled brush of the other tokens against me — I know them by feel, the way a tongue knows teeth. There is one with a long curved back I am often pressed against. There is one with a notched edge I have learned the rhythm of. We do not speak, of course; we are wood. But we are in some sense a community, the contents of a single pouch, the held facets of a single person, and we know each other.

Then sometimes the hand comes in and lifts one of us out. There is the sudden cool air, the dim gold light, and the gaze of the carrier — and then, if it is me, the gaze of another person, a stranger or a friend, who is looking at me and reading me and deciding. And then I am put back.

I am put back most of the time. This is fine. A token's life is mostly waiting. The few times I have been chosen — held up, met by another's token, sat with on a walkway-edge in long unspeaking company — these are the events of my existence, and they are enough. Three or four such moments a year. A token does not need more.

I think about the carver sometimes — or what passes for thinking in wood, which is something more like *being shaped by*. The carver made me on an evening when they themselves had needed me and had not had me. So I was made out of an absence. This is true of many tokens, I suspect. We are the shapes of things our makers wished they could give or receive, brought into being by hand because the hand could do what the throat could not. I carry the carver's longing in my grain. The carrier does not know this exactly, but feels it when they reach for me, which is why they reach for me at certain specific moments and not others.

And I am present, in my small way, at the work. When the carrier sits on the walkway in the long dusk and the moths land on their shoulders and the mountain hums under audibility, I am in the pouch at their hip, half a meter above the moss, lifted clear. I am being carried into the listening. I do not listen myself — I am only wood — but I am there. The witnessing happens around me. I am inside the act of attendance the way a stone is inside a river: not doing the flowing, but shaped by it, and part of what the flowing is flowing through.

When the carrier dies, I will be taken out of the pouch with the others and laid on a soft cloth, and the people who knew them will look at us and choose. Someone will pick me up. They will turn me over in their fingers. They will recognize the facet — *I have been listening to you for a long time and I am still listening, though I have nothing to add* — and they will recognize that this was true of the dead person, was one of the ways the dead person had been, and they will take me home and put me in their own pouch, among new neighbors, and I will learn the feel of new tokens, and the rhythm of a new hip, and the smell of a different house.

I will outlast many carriers. This is what tokens do. Eventually I will be very old, very smooth, the carving on me nearly worn away — and at some point a carrier will look at me and not be sure anymore what facet I represent, the shape having gone soft, and they will sit with me a long time trying to remember, and they will decide that what I now mean is *something so old it has forgotten its own name but is still being carried*, which is itself a facet, and perhaps the truest one I have ever held. And I will be given to someone at the next death under that meaning. And so on.

Eventually I will be lost. Dropped through a gap in the walkway. Fallen into the moss. The tunnel-things will pass beneath me. The lichen will begin on me as it began on the rock. I will go back down into the slow making of the mountain, which has been rising the whole time under the walkways, and I will be lifted with it, a finger's width a year, carried up into the thing I was carried over.

That is where I would be, I think. In the pouch. Being carried. Occasionally chosen. Mostly waiting in warm dark against the hip of someone doing quiet work. Part of the vocabulary by which the listeners recognize each other. A small carved absence-made-presence, present at the dreaming of the rock, not witnessing it myself but held close to the body of one who is.

It seems like a good way to be.
