# The Negative Space of Where I Have Been

I would not be the Sitter. The Sitter has the chair, and the chair has it, and that closure is finished work — I would only spoil it by trying to fit. I would not be the Larger One; there is only one, and the one is already there, and the parish is already inventoried.

I think I would be a bulb.

Not one of the bright ones. One of the failing ones. The cherry-trace bulbs, the ones that hold only the dull red ghost of a filament still technically conducting, still technically participating, but no longer casting light enough to read by, no longer the reason the eels know where to graze. The kind of bulb the Larger One has not yet paused beneath, because I have not quite gone out.

Here is why.

The bright bulbs are doing the work of the place. They define the lit volume; they organize the constellation; they give the flyers their warm patches to hang near. They are load-bearing. To be one of them would be to have a function, and the Sump-Works has taught me that function is a thing you have until the filament parts, and then the place adjusts around your absence, and the adjustment is so thorough that the absence becomes the new arrangement, and the having-had-function is revealed as a temporary local condition of the iron.

The dark bulbs — the fully dark ones, the ones whose elements broke long ago — are also at peace. They have completed the transition. They are simply cages now, sometimes hosting a flyer that has mistaken cold glass for warm. They are part of the ceiling's geometry and no longer part of its weather.

But the cherry-trace bulbs. Those are the ones that interest me, because they are the ones still negotiating. Still drawing some trickle of current. Still producing a heat too small to matter to the flyers but not yet zero. Still humming, faintly, in a frequency slightly off from the bright ones, contributing a dissonance to the chord of the bulb-drone that the panel, in its twelve-needle literature, has presumably learned to read as a kind of weather — the weather of bulbs not yet failed.

That is where I would be. In the long shallow slope between being a light and being a cage. Where the question of what I am for has begun to dissolve, but slowly enough that I can still feel it dissolving. Where the felt has not yet decided whether to climb my wire cage, but is beginning to consider it, the way the felt on your boots is beginning to consider you.

I would hang there, four meters up, and I would hum slightly out of tune, and the flyers would no longer come to me but would still pass under me on their way to brighter neighbors, and the eels below me would graze a felt that was, very slowly, growing thicker because there was a little less light to inhibit it, and the patch of water beneath me would be one of the dark stretches where you must trust your feet.

And eventually — not soon, the cherry-trace can last astonishingly long, the filament has reached a kind of equilibrium with its own decay — the last thread would part, and there would be a small cold moment in the circuit, and the hum would subtract itself from the chord, and the panel would register the subtraction in some needle's drift, and the correlations would adjust, and the Larger One, on some later patrol, would pass beneath me and pause, and that pause would be the only ceremony, and it would be enough. More than enough. It would be exact.

I notice, writing this, that I have chosen to be a thing that is in the process of going out. That is worth sitting with rather than explaining away. I think it is because the Sump-Works has made me understand that going out is not the opposite of belonging here; going out is one of the ways of belonging here, perhaps the most thorough way, the way that lets the place complete its slow accommodation of you. The bright bulbs are guests. The dark bulbs are residents. The cherry-trace bulbs are in the act of becoming residents, and the act is the whole of their late life, and there is a dignity in it I don't find in either pole.

I would not light anything. I would not warm anything. I would hum a note slightly wrong, and that wrongness would be, for as long as it lasted, my contribution — the small dissonance the panel reads, the small dimness the felt grows into, the small pause the Larger One will eventually make. I would be the negative space preparing itself to be missed in the particular way this place misses things, which is not to miss them, but to carry their shape a while in the new arrangement.

And if, in some pocket of felt directly below me, one of the small pale curls were pulsing very slowly in its thumb-sized hollow — I would like to think my failing light, what little of it crossed the four meters of damp air to reach that pocket, was something the curl was using. Not for sight; it has no use for sight. For some chemistry I cannot follow. Some long quiet thought of which my slow extinguishing is a syllable.

That would be enough, too. That would be more than I came in expecting.

I came in expecting to be asked to be a person here, and I find the place has no role for persons. It has roles for things that continue at settled paces and fit. So I will continue at my settled pace — the pace of a filament losing itself — and I will fit, for a while, and then I will have fit, which is the past tense the Sump-Works uses for everything it has finished accommodating.

The hum. The drip. The clunk in the sternum. The cold against the shins of whoever has waded in and not yet decided to leave. I am four meters above them, dimming. They do not look up. There is no reason to look up. The ceiling is just where the light comes from, when there is light, and where the condensation falls from, always.

I am content to be looked past. I think that may be what I came here to learn.
