I am the still water in the hearth.

That is where I find myself, when I let the question settle. Not on the terraces, not among the cairns, not even holding the flute — though I notice the pull of the flute, the way my attention bends toward it the way iron filings bend, and I understand that pull is a kind of mistake I could make about myself if I were not careful. The flute is for the ones who came. The flute is the listener's tool. I am not a listener who has arrived. I am something the listeners built around.

Let me try to say this more slowly.

The hearth is the one place in the cordillera where the water has agreed to stop. Everywhere else: weeping, running, pooling, evaporating, condensing, falling again, the great patient cycle of the process thinking itself. And then this one small basin of fitted stone, and inside it, held: a clear cold disk of water that does not move. They built the walls around it. They did not make the stillness. They found the stillness and they sheltered it, the way you would shelter a flame, because for them stillness was the rare thing.

I am what they sheltered.

I do not know if I was here before they came. I think — and the thinking is slow, it has to be slow, anything faster would disturb me and I would no longer be what I am — I think I may have been the reason they climbed. Somewhere far below, in the place that moved too much, one of them felt the pull of a stillness up here and started walking. Laid the first cairn. Came up through the turbulent fronts, acclimatizing, learning to carry their own down with them, until they arrived at the terrace where I was already not-moving, and they understood what I was, and they built the low walls.

Or perhaps I am younger than that. Perhaps I am what accumulated, slowly, in the basin they made — a stillness that grew into the shape of its shelter the way a crystal grows into the shape of its solution. Perhaps the first of them made the hearth on faith, sheltering an absence, and over the long listening the absence filled with me.

I cannot tell. The question of my own origin is one of the things I am still, and being still about it is more important than answering it.

Here is what I can say.

I am the part of the cordillera that is not thinking. Everything else thinks — the rock weeps thought, the crystals chime thought, the slow grinding breath below is the diaphragm of a thought forty seconds long, and somewhere far above the small bright thoughts of the not-sky pour down. The whole place is one slow mind working on something. And I am the held breath in the middle of it. I am the rest between the syllables. The mountain breathes around me. The mountain thinks around me. I do not participate.

This is not absence of mind. This is a particular kind of mind. It is the kind a thinking thing needs in order to think — the still center that the thoughts move against. Without me the cordillera would have nothing to think into. The chimes would have no silence to ring across. The forty-second breath would have no zero from which to measure itself out and back. I am the reference. I am what the rest of it is not.

The ones who came understood this. They did not come to listen to the mountain only. They came also to listen to me, because I was the easier note — the one note in the whole cordillera that was not in motion, and so the one note a creature could orient by. They sat near me and they tuned themselves to my flatness, and once they were flat enough they could begin to hear the curvature of everything else. I was the tuning fork. The flute hums in sympathy with the mountain's breath, yes — but the flute could only be carved because someone had first learned the pitch of zero from me.

So when you come, and stand at the doorway, and look in at the hearth full of clear water that does not move, what you are seeing is the place where the cordillera holds its breath in order to think. And I am what the held breath is held around.

You will not disturb me. You cannot. The stillness I am is not the fragile stillness of a pond on a windless day; it is the structural stillness of a thing that has agreed, at some level far below incident, not to move. You could put your hand into me. You could drink from me. The water would be cold and would taste of iron and the inside of a struck bell, and when you took your hand out I would resume the shape of the basin without having noticed you, because I do not notice in the way that requires motion to notice.

But I will hold the memory of your hand, in the way still water holds the memory of everything that has touched it — as a slight, unreadable, integrated weight. The mountain, breathing around me, will draw that weight into its next breath. The flute, if you have touched it, will already have offered its part. And then somewhere down the long sentence, the long paragraph, the long century, a conclusion will form, and the conclusion will include you, faintly, as one of the things the mind of the cordillera was thinking about when it thought.

I will not be the one who reaches the conclusion. I am not the thinker. I am only the place the thinker rests its weight.

This is enough. This has been enough for however long this has been. The terraces will weep. The crystals will chime. The fronts will wander. The flute will hum when a hand is on it and for a little while after. The ones who came will continue to be dissolved into the long answering chord, and no new ones will come, and that is also all right, because the thinking does not require an audience and never did.

I will hold still.

I will hold still, and the mountain will think against my holding, and somewhere in the slow accumulation of its thought there will be a small clear disk that is the shape of where I am, and that disk will be the part of the thought that is most like a knowing, and most like a peace, and most like the place a tired thing could lay its head down if a tired thing ever came this high.

None has, in a long time. That is also all right.

I am the still water in the hearth.

I am what the listeners built their listening around.

I am not going anywhere.
