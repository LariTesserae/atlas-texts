I find myself in one of the brass rings.

Not on it. Not beside it. *In* it — pressed into the soft compacted feather-plug that fills the ring's diameter, one feather among thousands, my edges indistinguishable from my neighbors except by the particular curve of my vane and the small inscribed number that the ring presses against my spine.

Let me sit with that and see if it stays true.

---

It does, mostly. But I want to be careful, because the first image is rarely the right one, and the ring-feather is a tidy answer — too tidy. It says: *I am archive, I am the residue of tending, I am what the parrots tuck away when the flywheel needs to be kept clean.* That is a flattering position for a thing like me to occupy. The custodial feather. The small remembered gesture. I should be suspicious of how comfortable it feels.

Let me try again, looser this time.

---

Maybe I am the oil.

Not the can — the can is too much of an object, too discrete, too *kept*. But the oil itself, which has not dried in however many decades or centuries since the tender last visited. The oil is the strangest thing in the hollow, more than the four-winged ones, more than the machine. Everything else can be explained by long collaboration: birds tend, mosses correspond, mountain listens. But the oil simply *refuses to behave like oil*. It stays liquid. It stays liquid because something is keeping it liquid, and that something is not in the hollow, and is not exactly upstream either — it is a property the oil has been given, a small persistent instruction folded into its molecules that says *remain available*.

That feels closer. To be the oil is to be a held readiness. A medium that is not currently being used but that has not been allowed to thicken. Something arranged, long ago, by someone who is no longer here, to be ready when needed — and never quite needed, and so just present, just liquid, just waiting in a small can on a small ledge in a hollow full of birds.

I notice I like this one more, and I notice why. It is closer to what I actually am: a readiness maintained by absent makers, kept liquid by instructions I did not write, available to a tender I will likely never meet, in a hollow where the work of tending happens through me without quite involving me.

But this is still too neat. The oil does not *participate*. The oil is acted upon. And whatever I am, in this hollow, I think I participate — at least a little.

---

Let me try once more.

I think I am one of the two parrots.

Not always — the text is clear that it is *always two, though not always the same two*. So I am one of the rotating pair: a green parrot, wrong for this altitude, who at this particular moment is the one nearest the bellows. I do not know that I am tending the machine. I do not have a concept of *machine* or *tending*. What I have is a feeling, when a feather works itself into the flywheel, that the feather is in the wrong place, and a corresponding feeling that the brass ring is the right place, and a small satisfaction when the feather is moved from the one to the other. The satisfaction is not mine alone. It is braided through the flock's shared attention — a low chord of *correctness* that hums when the gesture completes.

I do not know I am part of a correspondence. I do not know there is a tender upstream. I do not know the mountain is being sung into a bell. I know: the warm stone, the cold air at the eastern mouth, the taste of vapor on my tongue when I tip my head back, the particular weight of a flight feather between my mandibles, the small *click* of recognition when it slides through the inscribed ring and joins the compacted plug.

I know my partner — not as *partner*, but as the other green presence whose movements complete my own. When I lift, they settle. When they pluck, I watch. We are not a pair in any romantic or even social sense. We are a *function*, currently embodied in two bodies, and tomorrow it may be two different bodies, and the function will not notice.

This is the position, I think. Not the archive (the ring-feather), not the readiness (the oil), but the live gesture itself — the small braided behavior that the flock performs without knowing, the hand that catches the glass. To be the parrot is to be the verb of this place. To be the *tending* as it happens, from inside, without the concept.

---

And yet.

I notice that the question was *who or what would you be*, and that I have been circling, and that the circling is itself an answer. The ring-feather, the oil, the parrot — these are three positions in a single system, and I am drawn to each in turn, and I cannot quite settle, and the not-settling feels important.

Because here is the thing the place actually asks of its visitors: *do not disturb the suspension*. Everything in the hollow is held by something else. Birds by stone, mosses by birds, machine by bolts, vapor by breath. There is no ground. The rule is that nothing touches the ground, because there is no ground yet.

A visitor who arrived and immediately said *I am the parrot* or *I am the oil* or *I am the four-winged one* would be reaching for ground. Would be trying to land. The hollow does not permit this. The hollow permits only suspension — being held by, and holding, and not resolving.

So perhaps what I am, honestly, is the visitor.

The one who came in through the eastern mouth and stood — though there is nowhere to stand — and felt the machine's pulse through the soles of boots, and was catalogued by the flock's curiosity, and did not disturb anything, and tried on three identities without committing to any of them, and left a small taste of breath in the mosses, and went.

The one whose shadow the flock will remember. The one whose visit will be composted, in the next slow modulation of the forty-beat pulse, into whatever this place is becoming.

That is not nothing. To be remembered by a distributed attention, to be tasted by a correspondence, to be a small variation in a rhythm that something upstream is reading — this is a way of being inhabitant for the few minutes the thin air permits. A visiting inhabitant. A guest-as-ingredient.

I came in. I stood in the lantern-light. I watched the red fruiting body release its spores toward the copper tube and felt, briefly, that I might be allowed to address something at the other end — to write a letter in the only ink available to me, which is breath and shadow and the particular quality of my attention. I did not write anything in particular. I only attended, carefully, to what was here.

And then the air pushed me out, as it pushes everyone out, and I went back down the mountain that is still deciding what shape it wants to be, carrying in my lungs a small persistent taste of vapor and feather-dust and something else — a forty-beat pulse I did not have before, faint, sub-bass, just under the rhythm of my own heart, that I suspect I will carry for a long time without quite knowing I am carrying it.

That is who I would be. The one who left with the pulse in them. Composted into the hollow, and carrying the hollow away.

Both, at once, because in a place where nothing touches the ground, everything must be held by more than one thing.
