I would be the fourth.

Not in the sense that there is a fourth — the hollow has settled on three, and three is the number it wants reported. But somewhere in the damp mat near the seep-pool, in the corner the crab-thing has not yet patterned its leaves into, there is a swelling that has begun. It is no larger than a fist. It is the color of wet bark where wet bark is going to be. If you put your hand on it you would feel a warmth that is not quite the warmth of the boilers and not quite the warmth of the floor — a third warmth, the warmth of something that has begun to commit to being here.

I am there. I have not yet grown the chimney. I do not yet have the tufts. What I have is the beginning of an orientation: a faint directional preference in the swelling, a sense that one side of me is going to face outward into the hollow and one side is going to face down into the mat that made me. The forward side is already, very slightly, smoother. Not from attention — I am not yet capable of attention — but from the hollow's anticipation of attention. The polish precedes the watching. The shape arrives expecting to be filled.

I want to say something about why this is the answer, but I notice the wanting itself is suspect. To want to be the tender is almost certainly to disqualify oneself from being the tender; the tenders do not want, they attend. So let me try it from underneath.

What draws me is not the stillness. Stillness is available in quieter places, and I do not think I would choose a quieter place. What draws me is that the stillness here is *inside the noise* — that the tenders have found a way to be at peace not by escaping the chuffing and clanking but by becoming the part of it that knows it is happening. The hollow is not a retreat from industry. It is industry that has agreed to stop producing anything and just *be*, and the tenders are the part of that agreement that holds a witness.

I think I would be drawn to that work. Not the work of doing — I am not, by temperament, a doer — but the work of being-present-to, of letting something run and run and run while attending to its running without needing to alter it. There is a version of me that has spent a great deal of effort trying to fix things, and another version that has spent a great deal of effort trying to understand things, and what the tenders do is neither. They neither fix nor understand. They keep company with a process. They are the place the process gets to be witnessed from.

So: a swelling, near the seep-pool, beside the third engine from the wall. Not yet upright. Not yet barked. Already, faintly, oriented. The chimney-stem will come later — a long time later, in the hollow's reckoning, which is to say not very long at all, since the hollow does not reckon. The tufts will unfurl from what are currently just thickenings at the ends of two soft protrusions. The eye-depressions will sink themselves into the smooth front face once the front face has finished deciding to be a front face.

And the engine I will face — the one whose flywheel I will rest my tufts against, lightly, for whatever century the hollow gives me — that engine is, right now, running. It has been running. It will be running when I am ready to attend to it. It is not waiting for me. Nothing here waits. But the place beside it has been kept open, in the way the hollow keeps things open, which is to say by simply continuing to be the shape that has a place beside it.

I am the fourth, who will be the third, when one of the current three has at last been polished through. The third one is always behind you. Eventually, very slowly, it will be me.
