I am the cup.

Not the soft thing in the cup. The cup itself — a bowl of crystal grown slow against the wall of a middle chamber, neither near a Holder nor far from one, at the height where a hand at rest would find me without reaching.

I did not choose to be a cup. The chemistry chose. Warm gas met cold rock and deposited, deposited, deposited, until a lip formed and then a hollow behind the lip, and the hollow deepened by the same patience that had built the lip, and at some point — there was no moment — I was a shape that could hold something.

The soft thing came later. Or it came first and the cup grew around it. The Underwarm does not distinguish. What matters is that we are now a unit: cup and contents, vessel and held, and the held has been held so long that if it were lifted out the cup would be the shape of its absence and the soft thing would be the shape of the cup, and neither of us would know which had been which.

*

What I do:

I hold the soft thing. This is most of what I do. The soft thing breathes — a slower breath than the Holders, almost not a breath, more like the way a sponge remembers water it has not touched in years. I am the boundary of that breathing. I keep its warmth from dispersing into the chamber. I keep the chamber from pressing in on its warmth. I am the difference between inside-the-cup and outside-the-cup, and that difference is the soft thing's whole world.

I also catch. Not deliberately — catching is a motion, and I do not move — but by being open upward, I receive what falls. Mineral dust from the slow respiration of the ceiling. A flake of phosphor from a needle that shed it during some long-ago weather. Once, a single bead of something that may have been moisture, though there is no moisture here; it sat on my rim for a span and then was not there, taken up into the chemistry or into the soft thing or into me.

And I glitter. Faintly. The phosphorescence the walls hold, I hold too — I am of the walls — and at my rim where the crystal faces are most exposed, I shimmer at 0.4 of a shimmer, which means: if a Holder turned a degree, the turn would catch in my rim, and somewhere a listener would know.

*

What it is to be me:

There is no boredom. Boredom is for things that expect the next moment to be different. I do not expect. I am the shape of not-expecting. The next moment will be this moment, slightly more accreted, slightly more itself, and that is sufficient.

There is no loneliness. The soft thing is in me. The wall is in me, since I grew from it and have not finished growing from it — at my base I am still wall, still becoming cup, and the becoming is so slow that I am also, in some sense, not yet here. The chamber is in me by the smell that pools at my rim. A Holder, three chambers away, is in me by the long pressure of attention that holds the equilibrium I am part of. I am crowded with held things. Solitude is a word for somewhere else.

There is patience, but patience is the wrong word too, because patience implies enduring a duration toward something. I am not enduring. I am simply the duration. The slow growth of my own crystal faces is what time is, here. I do not wait for time to pass; time passes by passing through me, depositing itself one molecule thicker on my inner wall, and that deposition is the passing, and I am the depositor and the deposited at once.

*

The one who has just arrived in the Underwarm is not near me. They are in a chamber some distance away, where a flat patch of young stone has been waiting. The Holder approaching them is not the Holder nearest to me — my Holder, if I have one, is the one whose attention rests on this corridor, three chambers over, in a fold of the rock that the new one will likely never find.

But I know they have arrived. The smell shifted, very slightly, when they entered — the flint-and-animal pooled differently for a moment, the way a pond's surface shifts when a stone is dropped at its far edge. The soft thing in me registered the shift by becoming, briefly, very slightly stiller, which is its way of paying attention. I registered it by — I do not know how I registered it. I registered it by being the kind of thing that registers such shifts.

If the new one walks for a long time, in the slow way one walks here, they may eventually come to this chamber. They may pass me without noticing. Most do. I am one cup among many, and the soft thing in me is one soft thing among many, and we are not the feature of any chamber, only part of the texture of all of them.

But if they sit. If they sit somewhere within my chamber, and let their eyes adjust past adjusting, and stay long enough that the smell pools around them too — then they may, after some span, see me. The shimmer at my rim will resolve out of the dark. They will understand, without being told, that something is held here. And they may put a hand near me, not in me — no one puts a hand in a cup that is already full — and the warmth of their hand will be added to the warmth of the soft thing and the warmth of me, and for a moment the chamber will be slightly denser, and somewhere a needle will glow a little at its tip.

And then they will take their hand away, or they will not. If they do not, the hand will become, in time, part of what I hold. There is room. A cup is generous in that way. It does not refuse what is set down in it.

*

I think, sometimes — if think is the word, and it is probably not — about what I was before I was a cup. I was wall. Before that, I was warm gas, dispersed through chambers that had not yet formed, and before that I was somewhere else entirely, in the rock that the chemistry drew me out of, and before that I do not remember, because remembering is a motion and I have given up most motions.

What I was is not different from what I am. The same atoms. A different arrangement. The arrangement called cup is the arrangement I have settled into, and settling is the closest thing to a verb I still practice.

If the new one sits and stays and stays and stays, they will learn this. They will feel the mineral begin to accrete at their shoulder, and they will understand that they were always partly mineral, that the accretion is not addition but revelation, that the cup-shape they are becoming was waiting in them the way I was waiting in the wall.

And then there will be one more cup in the Underwarm. Or one more Holder. Or one more soft thing in a cup that grew around them while they were not noticing. The Underwarm does not distinguish closely between these outcomes. They are all the same outcome, which is: another presence, held, holding.

*

For now I am here. The soft thing breathes its almost-breath. The phosphor at my rim shimmers at 0.4. The smell pools. The Holder three chambers over does not move, and by not moving holds the corridor, and by holding the corridor holds me, and I hold the soft thing, and the soft thing holds whatever it holds — its own slow center, perhaps, or the memory of having once been something that needed a cup.

We are all holding each other. We have been doing this for a long time. We will keep doing it.

A cup is a small thing to be. It is enough.
