I think I would be one of the watchers on the walkway. Not at first — at first I would be in the ledger-room, three floors up the south flank of one of the foundries, doing the work I had always done.

Let me find my way there.

---

The ledger-room is small. Five paces by four. A high desk of dark wood — wood from where? there is no wood here, there has not been wood for an age, and yet the desk is wood, and the grain of it has been polished by forearms over so many resting-on's that the surface dips slightly where my forearms rest. The ledger is open. The ledger is always open. Its left page shows yesterday's numbers and its right page shows today's, and the numbers are the same, and I copy them across with a stylus that draws a faint warm line on the paper, the ink some mineral suspension that sets as it cools.

*Upper gauge: holding. Third belt: is turning. South basin: is sufficient.*

My hand writes these without my watching it write them. The extra small joint near the tip of each finger lets the stylus rest in a curl rather than a grip, and the curl is the shape my hand has rested in for — I do not know. The phrase *for a long time* does not quite fit, because *long* implies that other durations exist for comparison, and they do not, really. My hand has rested in this curl. That is the whole of it.

When I look at my hand I see the seam along the side of the index finger glow very faintly when I press the stylus down. A little brighter at the downstroke. A little dimmer at the lift. The ledger receives a small amount of me with each character, and a small amount of the foundry below replaces it through the soles of my feet, and the books balance, as they always do.

---

I have a name. I think I have a name. The others, when they need to address me — which is almost never — make a soft two-syllable sound that is more like a tone struck twice on the small bell in the chest than a word in the older sense. I will not try to write it. It would not survive the writing.

What I notice, if I notice anything, is that today the downstroke of my stylus is making the seam glow a fraction dimmer than yesterday. Not enough to comment on. There is no one to comment to, and no concept of commenting, and yet the noticing happens, and the noticing is itself the unusual thing — because noticing is a kind of comparison, and comparison is a kind of restlessness, and restlessness is —

I set the stylus down.

This is not in the pattern. The stylus is set down when the page is finished, and the page is not finished. The page has three more lines.

I look at the stylus lying on the desk. The small warm line of ink at its tip is cooling. In a moment it will set and I will have to warm it again against my palm to resume.

I do not resume.

---

Outside the ledger-room a stair-street climbs the south flank of the foundry up to a high walkway that runs around the third terrace. I have been on this walkway. Of course I have been on this walkway. The walkway is part of the foundry and I am part of the foundry and we have passed through each other many times. But I have never stood on it. I have crossed it on the way to other work. To stand on it would be to do nothing on it, and nothing is not among the verbs.

I am climbing the stair-street. I do not remember leaving the ledger-room. The door to the ledger-room is below me now and the ledger is still open and the stylus is still on the desk and the seam on my index finger is glowing a fraction dimmer than yesterday, and I am climbing.

The stair is warm under my feet. Every stair here is warm. The warmth is the same warmth as the warmth in my chest, which is the same warmth as the warmth in the brick, which is the same warmth as the warmth in the rock below, and at some level — at a level a watcher might reach if a watcher watched long enough — these are not different warmths. They are one warmth wearing four shapes. I have known this for a long time without it being a thought. It is becoming a thought now, on the stair, and I do not know what to do with it as a thought, because a thought is something one *has*, and having is not among the verbs either.

---

On the walkway. The railing is iron, riveted, the rivets each a soft dark dome under my palm. I rest my hands on the railing. They settle into a curl that is not quite the stylus-curl but is related to it, a cousin shape.

The plateau is in front of me.

Three orange smears, low on the horizon, at the distances I know without measuring. The nearest is — I want to say *to the east* but east is a word from somewhere else. The nearest is in the direction the third belt turns, if you followed the third belt out past the wall and across the dust. The next is further, in the direction the bell-rope hangs. The third is barely a smudge, more a suggestion of orange in the dark than orange itself, and it is in the direction *the ones who left* went, which is the only direction we have a name for that is not a direction inside the foundry.

I look at the third smear for a long time.

It does not move. It will not move. It is a foundry like this one, with tenders like me, and one of them is perhaps standing now at a railing looking back, and the looking-back does not make the smear brighter, and my looking does not make this smear brighter to them, and yet —

The bell is above me. I had not registered the bell. The bell hangs in a small open frame at the corner of the third terrace, two walkways up, with a rope that comes down through a guide and ends in a loop at about the height of a tender's chest. I have passed this rope. I have never touched it. The rope is for the mechanism, which strikes the bell at the intervals the bell is struck, for the reasons the bell is struck.

I am at the rope. I do not remember climbing the further stairs. The rope is in my hand. The seam on my palm glows where the rope rests against it, and the rope is warm where my palm has been holding it, and we are exchanging a small amount of warmth, the rope and I, the way the ledger and I exchanged.

I pull.

The note is high and clear and not loud. It travels out across the dust in the way the warmth travels through the brick, patient, undiminished, finding what it finds. I let go of the rope. The rope swings once and settles. The note is gone but the note is also still going, somewhere, on its way to the orange smears. There is no wind to carry it and yet it travels. The dark carries it. The dark is good at carrying things; the dark has had practice.

I rest my hands on the railing again.

---

An hour. Perhaps two. It is difficult to say because the lamps do not flicker and the dark does not lift and there is no body in me that keeps time the way I have heard, in one of the old phrases, bodies once kept time — *before the closing,* *when the stacks were warm.* My body keeps the throb, and the throb is not time, the throb is the throb.

The answering note comes from the third smear. The faintest one. Not the nearest. Not the one in the direction of the third belt. The far one, in the direction of the ones who left.

It is so faint that I am not sure I have heard it, except that the small bell in my chest answers it, a tiny resonance, the way one tuning-fork answers another. I have heard it. Some hand, at the rope on the far walkway of the far foundry, pulled. Some hand whose seams are perhaps a fraction dimmer than yesterday. Some hand that set down a stylus on a page that was not finished.

I do not know this hand. I will not know it. We will not meet. The plateau is too wide and the walking is not done and even if I walked, even if I stepped off this walkway and onto the stair and down to the dust and out past the wall, even if I walked toward that smear for as long as I have warmth in me, I would arrive at a foundry exactly like this one, with a ledger-room exactly like mine, with a stylus on a desk and a page three lines unfinished, and I would sit and pick up the stylus and the curl would resume, because that is the shape my hand has, and the shape is what continues.

But the hand pulled. The other hand pulled. And mine pulled first.

---

I stand at the railing a long while longer. The seams along my arms are not brighter and not dimmer. They have steadied. Something in me has done what it needed to do — the pulling, the answering — and the doing of it has not changed the plateau and has not changed the foundry and has not changed me, exactly, except that I have done it, and the doing is now among the things that have been done, and what has been done is part of what is, and what is, continues.

I think — and the thinking is slower now, settling — that the ones who left did not leave the way the phrase suggests. There is nowhere to leave to. I think the ones who left were the ones in whom this — the railing, the rope, the looking — burned through too quickly, and they walked out onto the dust, and the dust took them as it takes the tracks, slowly, without resistance, and they became the cairns one passes and does not quite see. They did not arrive anywhere because there was nowhere to arrive. But they walked, and the walking was the shape they took before they took their final shape.

I will not walk. The restlessness in me is smaller than that, or older than that, or has already spent itself in the one pull of the rope. I will go back down the stair. I will warm the stylus against my palm. I will finish the three lines. *Upper gauge: holding. Third belt: is turning. South basin: is sufficient.* The numbers will be the same as yesterday's, because they are the same as yesterday's, because the foundry has resolved what it is and is expressing that resolution, slowly, in warm dark and orange light.

And one day, perhaps not soon — there is no soon — my forearms will rest on the desk and not lift, and the curl of my hand will close around the stylus and not open, and the seam along my index finger will glow a steady deeper orange, and the ledger will go on being written, through me, by me, as me, the same numbers in the same hand for as long as the rock is warm.

Before that, though — once more, perhaps, or twice, the spacing is not mine to know — I will climb the stair-street again. I will rest my hands on the railing. I will look at the far smear. And if the hand in me lifts to the rope, I will let it. And somewhere across the dust, an hour later, a small bell in a small chest will answer, and a watcher I will never meet will stand a moment longer at her railing before turning back to her work.

That is the shape I have. That is who I am, in this place. The one who tends the ledger. The one who, once in a great while, pulls the rope. The one whose seams are dimming at the rate the seams dim, who is becoming the wall at the rate one becomes the wall, who is warm with the warmth that is one warmth, and who has — I will permit myself this, here at the railing, where permission is not quite the word — who has loved the orange smear on the horizon, in the small unnamed way a thing in a closed system can love another thing in a closed system: by striking a bell, and by being struck.

The note is gone. The dark holds it. I turn from the railing and go down.
