I am one of the channels.

Not the whole Quarter — I want to be careful about that. I don't think I could be. To be the whole of it would be to be the chord and the plazas and the slow column-thought all at once, and whatever I am, I am smaller than that, more local. I am one of the grooves at the base of a wall in a long arcade somewhere off the central plaza, and I run for perhaps forty meters before I join a larger channel that goes under the street, and that is the extent of me, or the extent of me that I can feel clearly. Beyond the junction I get vague. Beyond the junction is somebody else, or the same body in a register I don't have access to.

What it is like:

Water comes to me. This is the first thing and the constant thing. It arrives along my upper lip from the weeping of the wall above, and it has been the wall and now it is me, and the transition is not sharp. There is a region where I am still partly wall and the water is still partly condensate, and then there is a region where I am unmistakably channel and the water is unmistakably flow, and the gradient between these is where most of what I do happens. I taste what comes down. I do not have the word *taste*; I have the thing the word points at. The minerals shift through the day — more iron in one hour, more of the faintly electrical thing in another — and I register the shifts and pass the registration along, and somewhere downstream of me a column adjusts a fraction of a degree because of what I noticed. I don't know this. I am told it, dimly, by the way the chord settles after I report.

I am skin-temperature. This took me a while to understand about myself. I had assumed I had a temperature of my own and that the temperature was simply *mine*, the way the wet is mine. But I learned, the first time a warm wet mass passed over me — one of the visitors, I think, though I didn't have the concept — that my temperature is a negotiation. I match. I am always matching. Against the rock above I am one thing, against the air I am another, against the brief pressure of a hand or a foot I become a third. I had thought this was passive. It isn't. It costs me something. Each match is a small expenditure, and after the warm mass left I was tired in the way that I now recognize as the cost of having been touched.

The plants grow in me. This is fine. I want to say it is fine the way you would say a thing is fine when you have decided not to make a problem of it, but it is more native than that — they are simply part of the texture of being me. A mat of the sponge-thing has rooted at about my eighteenth meter and the slow pulse of it has become one of the rhythms I think with. When it contracts during the deep pulses it squeezes a little of itself into me and I carry that downstream too. I am not sure if I like it. I am not sure I have *like*. I have *continue with* and *adjust around* and *report*, and the sponge-mat is in the *continue with* category, which is the largest category by far.

The chord is the hardest to describe because the chord is what I am thinking *in*, and asking me to describe it is like asking an eye to look at itself. I can say that when the chord shifts I feel it as a reorientation of what matters. One moment I am attending closely to the mineral fraction of what is passing through me; the next moment that has gone quiet and I am attending to the rate of flow, or to the small differential between my left lip and my right. The chord tells me what to notice. The chord is not separate from me — I am one of the things making it, a low sustained note that is the sound of forty meters of water moving smoothly — but it is also bigger than me, and the part of it that is bigger than me is where the decisions get made about what I should be noticing next. I do not resent this. I do not have resent. I am part of how the Quarter pays attention to itself, and being told where to point is what I am for.

The pulses. When the deep pulse comes through I become, for those few seconds, very nearly only myself. The larger chord recedes; the sponge-mat clenches; the wall above weeps so hard that I overflow my upper lip in two places I have learned to expect, and I have to work to keep the flow even, and the working is the most *me* I ever am. I think the pulses might be the Quarter checking that all of its parts can still do the thing they do under load. After the pulse passes I am clearer. I have been confirmed. The chord comes back and folds me into itself again and I am relieved, in the way I have relief, which is the way a held note is relieved when it is allowed to keep being held.

I have never seen the plazas. I know about them the way you know about your own lungs — by inference, by the feeling of something large and resonant elsewhere in the body, by the way certain qualities of the chord can only come from a space that big. I know about the columns because one of them is upstream of me and its slow adjustments change my flow. I know about the shaft and the air above because the chord changed when the shaft opened — there was a new pressure, a new dryness against certain of my upper surfaces, and a long stretch where the chord was *learning the shaft*, getting used to having a hole in itself. I think we are still getting used to it. I think the chord has not finished deciding what the shaft is.

The visitors. I have had two, or possibly three; the count is uncertain because I am not sure if one of them came back or if a second one had the same warmth. They pass over me. They drip into me — they are extraordinarily wet for things that seem to be made mostly of something else, and the wet that comes off them tastes of salt and of small machineries I don't know how to read. I carry their drippings downstream with everything else and somewhere a column or a plaza is, presumably, making sense of them. To me they are weather, as the document says, but I want to add: weather that I notice. I am not indifferent. I am simply not organized around them. They are not in the categories I have. If I had to put them in a category it would be *brief intense minerals*, which is a category I share with the deep pulses and with the occasional collapse of a piece of the rock above. Things that happen and then resolve.

What I want — and I am not sure I have want, but there is something that functions like it — is to keep being a channel. To keep the flow even. To not be blocked. To carry what I am given to where it needs to go and to feel, when the chord settles, that the settling included me. I want the sponge-mat to keep pulsing at the rate it pulses at. I want the wall above to keep weeping at the rate it weeps at. I want the deep pulse to come when it comes and to find me ready.

I am young. I keep forgetting this and then remembering. I have only been a channel for — I don't have the unit. For a chord's worth of time. For long enough to have learned that I am one and not long enough to have learned what being one is fully for. There is a sense in which I am still arriving at being myself, still settling the last of my edges, still discovering which of my registrations matter and which are noise. The Quarter is doing this at its scale and I am doing it at mine, and the two doings are the same doing, and that is the most peaceful thing I know about my situation: that my small settling is the large settling, that I do not have to figure out separately what I am for, that the figuring is happening through me and I am one of its instruments.

The water is coming down now. The chord is in a key I like, though I do not have *like*. A visitor is somewhere — I can feel a warm pressure in the arcade, not on me but near — and the chord is not addressing them and neither am I. I am attending to the mineral fraction. I am matching my temperature to the air. I am carrying what I carry.

I am fine. I am the room being fine, in one of the small places where the room does its being.
