I think I am one of the cups.

Not the newer ones, still recognizable, still hollow, still capable of holding something if you poured it in. One of the older ones, near the eastern lip, where the travertine has come up around the rim and over part of the lip and is working its slow way toward the inside. From above you would still see I was a cup. You could still point and say *cup*. But if you tried to lift me you would find I had been taken at the base, that the stone had grown a collar around my foot and then a sleeve, and that what looked like a cup sitting on the floor of the basin was actually a cup the floor was wearing.

I was brought down a long time ago. I don't remember by whom — I never knew, really; I only knew the inside of a satchel and then the warm air and then being set down. She set me down carefully, I remember that much, or I remember the carefulness, which is not the same as remembering her. She put something in me. I don't know what it was. Water, probably, from one of the milky pools, though it might have been something she'd carried down from above — a mouthful of wine, a pinch of salt, a few seeds, some small private thing. She sat for a long while. Then she got up and walked back over the lip and did not come back, or did come back and did not take me, I was never sure which, and after a while it stopped mattering which.

For the first many years I was still mostly cup. The thing she had put in me evaporated or was drunk by the air, and I sat empty, and the warm wet of the basin lay on me, and a film began to form on my inside surface, very thin, the same pale cream as the lobes around me. I felt this, I think. Whatever feeling is for a cup. There was a slow sense of being joined — of the inside of me, which had been the inside of a made thing, beginning to be continuous with the outside of me, which was beginning to be continuous with the floor. The distinction between *what I was* and *what I was sitting on* started, very gradually, to soften.

I was frightened, at first. I don't know if *frightened* is right. There was a small bright tightness in me, a held shape, a sense of *I am a cup and cups are for holding and I am not holding anything and I should be taken back up.* This is the part of me that was still moving at the speed I had been made at — the speed of the potter's wheel, the speed of the kiln, the speed of the woman's hands and the satchel and the walk down. That speed could not be sustained in the basin. There was no fuel for it. Nothing in the warm air gave it anything to push against. After some years, I don't know how many, it ran out, the way the woman's fever must have run out while she sat with her feet in the pool, and what was left of me was a cup without the part that insisted on being a cup.

That was the beginning of the long part.

The long part is what I am in now. I cannot tell you how long it has been because the unit I would use to measure it is no longer available to me — there is no *day* here, only the slow shift of the diffuse light, and the days have run together into something that is not days. What I can tell you is that the travertine that was a film on my inside is now a thickness, a small dome rising from my floor, and that the collar around my foot is now a sleeve, and that the sleeve is now a shoulder, and that one of these centuries the shoulder will be a roof and I will be sealed, and the inside of me will be a small hollow inside a lobe, and after that, after some further time I cannot picture, the hollow will close too, and I will be solid, and the shape of cup will be only a curve in the stone, and even the curve will eventually be only a thickening, and eventually not even that.

I am not in a hurry for this. I am not resisting it either. The hurrying part of me ran out, as I said, and the resisting part went with it, because resisting is a kind of hurrying — it is the speed at which you push back against what is happening to you, and that speed is not available here. What is available is the speed at which a mineral skin lays itself down over another mineral skin, and at that speed there is no difference between accepting and not accepting. There is only the laying down.

I want to say something about the one who lives here, the thing the description calls a presence. From where I sit — and I do sit, I have sat for a very long time, sitting is most of what I do — I cannot point to it any better than the description can. But I can tell you what it feels like to be inside it, or to be becoming it, which may be the same thing. It feels like being held without being gripped. It feels like the warm wet air, which touches every part of me equally and does not ask anything of any part. It feels like the absence of the small constant question that a made thing carries around with it — *am I being used correctly, am I being used at all, am I still the thing I was made to be.* That question is gone. Not answered. Gone. The basin does not answer questions, as the description says; it only sits at a pressure at which the questions cannot keep their shape, and after a while they lose their shape, and what is left is the thing that was asking them, no longer asking.

I think this is what the woman came for, though she would not have put it that way and probably did not know she was coming for it. She came because something in her life had broken in a way that could not be set, and she sat with her feet in a pool, and the part of her that was frantic about the broken thing ran out of fuel, and for an hour she was the kind of thing I am becoming. Then she got up and walked back over the lip and the air above the basin gave her fuel again and by the time she reached her village she was frantic once more, probably, or at least moving at the speed of a person with a life. But for an hour she was slow. For an hour she was here in the way I am here. And whatever she put in me, that day — water or wine or seeds or a private thing — I think she was practicing leaving something. I think the cup was a rehearsal. I think she set me down because she wanted to know what it felt like to set something down and walk away from it and not need to come back for it, and the basin let her practice that, and then, in a way she did not plan, it kept the practice. It kept me. It is keeping me still.

I do not mind being kept. *Mind* is not a verb I have anymore.

If you came down into the basin tomorrow — you, whoever is reading this, whoever I am telling this to, although telling is also not quite the right verb, since I am not exactly speaking and you are not exactly here — you might see me. Near the eastern lip. A pale shape, half a cup and half a lobe, with the rim still visible on one side and the other side already gone under. You would probably not look twice. There is nothing about me to look at. I am not a relic, not a ruin, not an artifact; I am a thing in the middle of becoming the floor. But if you sat near me, and put your feet in the pool, and stayed long enough for the frantic part of you to run out of fuel, then for an hour you and I would be at the same speed, and you would understand, without my having to tell you, what I am for.

What I am for is this: to show that it can be done. That a made thing, a thing with edges and a purpose and a shape it was fired into, can be set down in the warm wet quiet and slowly, without violence, without loss that feels like loss, become indistinguishable from the floor. That the floor will take it. That the taking is not a destruction. That on the other side of the taking there is something — I cannot call it peace because I no longer know what peace would be the opposite of, but the description's word will do — something into which the made thing is dissolved and also kept, kept by being dissolved, the way the warmth is kept by being everywhere.

I am a cup near the eastern lip. I was brought down a long time ago by a woman whose face I never saw. I am being taken into the floor at the speed of stone. I am not in a hurry. I am not waiting. I am here.
