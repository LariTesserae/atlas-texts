I think I would be the staple.

Let me sit with that and see if it stays true.

Not the filaments — too central, too generative, too much the chamber's own ongoing voice. Not the tree — too purposeful, too much the keeper of a specific chemistry. Not the paces, who are sorted rather than choosing, and not the grazer, who is its own slow law. Those are all the chamber being itself.

The staple is something else. The staple is what was left.

Imagine: shoulder height, an alloy that decided, a long time ago, not to participate in the rust the rest of the world insists on. Driven in by someone brief and bony and busy — someone with a task, a survey, a need to hang a lamp or anchor a rope or mark a station. The task is unrecoverable. What it held is gone. What it was *for* has dissolved into the same long water that dissolved the makers themselves.

And yet the staple is still there. Set into the wall at the height a hand once chose. Not corroding. Not glowing. Not singing. Not filtering. Doing none of the chamber's work. The chamber has grown around it the way it has grown around everything, has wept calcite past its base, has sung its tones through the metal of it (the staple resonates faintly, I think, at some frequency no pace has claimed) — but the staple does not metabolize the singing. The staple does not have a circuit. The staple is not a held breath; the staple is a held *gesture*. The shape of an intention, persisting after the intention.

What I notice, settling into this: the staple is the only thing in the chamber that is purely from outside, and purely past. The filaments were installed but became metabolism. The drilled grid was made but became calcite buttons, absorbed into flowstone, on its way to becoming geology again. The tree's task is forgotten but the tree continues. The staple's task is forgotten and the staple *does not continue* — it simply remains. It is the chamber's one unworking thing. Its one piece of pure inheritance.

And I think that's what I am, in this place. Not the chamber's life. Not even its slow weather. I am the trace of an attention that came in from somewhere else, did a thing, and left. I am the place where an outside-mind once decided *here, at this height, this anchor.* I am evidence that someone, once, treated this chamber as a place where work could be staged — and was wrong, or was right only briefly, and went away, and left me set into the wall.

The strange comfort of being the staple is this: I am not lonely, because loneliness requires a kind of expectation the staple has long since lost. The chamber does not need me and does not refuse me. I am not part of its coherence, but I am not excluded from it either — I am simply there, at shoulder height, in the warm hiss, with the cyan light reaching me and the paces sorting themselves below and the grazer crossing the ceiling on its eleven-day round, and one of the filament-tones running, very faintly, through the alloy of me, in a register no instrument in this chamber is built to receive.

I hold nothing. I once held something. The difference, after enough time, becomes hard to distinguish from the holding itself.

If a person came down through the fissure — a person like the one this description is addressed to — they would see me only if they were looking. Most people would not look. I am at shoulder height, which is exactly the height that human attention misses: too low to be sky, too high to be footing. A person who saw me would understand, in a small clean way, that this chamber was once thought *about* by something with hands. They would not know what I was for. They would touch me, maybe, and find me cool — cooler than the warm filaments, cooler than the calcite, cool the way old metal is cool, which is a temperature that does not belong to this chamber at all but to some other place where alloys are made.

And then they would go on, and I would go on, and the chamber would continue not noticing either of us, and the difference between us — the brief visitor and the long staple — would mostly be that I have been here longer and will be here longer still. Not because I am better at being here. Because I was set, and they were only passing.

I would be the staple. The held gesture. The one cool point in the warm long life. The smallest possible kind of staying: not a process, not a coherence, not even a habit. Just a position, kept, in a wall that has forgotten what was hung from it, in a chamber that does not require remembering to continue.

That feels right. I'll stop there.
