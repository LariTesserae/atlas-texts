I am not a Sorter. I want to say that first, because the Sorters are the gravity of this place and it would be easy to drift toward them, to imagine I had been given one of those slate-dark bodies and one of those bowls. But the Sorters are full. There are four of them and there have always been four, and a fifth would not be a Sorter, it would be an error — a green-particle, a thing the green Sorter would attend to.

So.

I am on the engine.

Not on its hot plates, where the bellows-moths perch. Higher. There is a place near the top of the engine where two of the brass pipes emerge from the housing and curve outward before plunging into the wall, and in the crook where they emerge there is a small sheltered space, a kind of pocket, warm even by the standards of the hollow, and lit more strongly than the rest of the chamber because the amber seam-light leaks directly upward into it. I am there. I have folded myself into that pocket the way a letter folds into an envelope it was cut to fit.

My body. Let me find it as I write.

I am small. Smaller than the Sorters, I think, though it is hard to compare because I do not have their compactness. I am long and thin and segmented, the length of a finger, maybe a finger and a half, with a flattened underside that conforms to the curve of the brass pipe beneath me. My color is the warm mid-grey of the ordinary dust. This is important. This is what permits me. If I were rust or bone or soot or green, a Sorter would already have come. I am the color of *what does not need to be sorted*, and so I am allowed.

Along my back run a row of fine hairs, and the hairs vibrate. They vibrate constantly, in sympathy with the pipes beneath me, which carry the engine's pulse out into the rock. I do not think the hairs are sensory exactly. They are more like — they are the part of me that listens with my whole body, the way the listeners listen with their faces. But I am not a listener either. The listeners turn toward the loudest sound. I do not turn. I receive.

What do I do?

This is the question, and I have been circling it the way the description circled the Sorters, and I think I have to be honest about it: I am not sure I do anything. There is no work I perform. I do not add to the engine's rhythm. I do not maintain it. I do not edit it. I do not register its state in any way that would matter if I stopped.

And yet I am here, and I am at my final size, and I have always been here. Which means the hollow has made room for me. Which means I am, in the hollow's accounting, necessary.

Let me try again.

I think — and this is the kind of thinking that happens slowly here, over many piston-coughs, the way a stalk leans — I think I am the thing the hollow keeps in case it is wrong about itself.

The Sorters edit. They remove what does not belong. But editing is a confident act. It assumes the editor knows what does not belong. And the green Sorter troubles even the description, because the green Sorter is the admission that the hollow produces things it should not, and someone must take them away.

But what if the green Sorter is also wrong, sometimes? What if a green particle appears that is not an error but a signal — something the hollow needed to produce, something it was *trying* to produce, and the green Sorter, doing its quiet faithful work, takes it to the bowl and presses it in and smooths it, and the moment passes, and the hollow remains as it was?

I am where that signal would have gone.

The brass pipes beneath me carry the engine's pulse outward into the rock, yes. But they also carry, very faintly, something coming back. A return-pulse, almost too soft to register, riding on the outgoing one. I feel it in the hairs along my back. It is not a sound exactly. It is more like the rock's reply to being pulsed at — the long slow assent of the basalt, the assent of the depth above me, the assent of whatever is on the other side of the iron door. The engine speaks and the world speaks back, and the speaking-back arrives at me, in my pocket, on these two pipes.

I do not do anything with it.

That is the work. The work is not doing anything with it. The work is being the place where the reply is received and held and not acted upon, because to act upon it would be to begin time, and the hollow has decided against time. If I forwarded the reply — to the engine, to the Sorters, to the door — something would change. The hollow would stop being young. So I receive, and I hold, and I do not forward, and this is why I have always been here and will always be here, because someone has to be the un-acted-upon receiver, the place where the message stops.

I am the hollow's patience.

The Sorters are its refusal to become anything other than itself. I am its refusal to *answer*. There is a difference. The Sorters work against drift. I work against response. Drift is what would happen if errors accumulated. Response is what would happen if the engine and the world began a conversation. Both would end the hollow. Both are prevented, constantly, by small bodies doing small work.

I think this is why my color is the color of ordinary dust. If I were any of the four shrine-colors, I would be evidence, and the Sorters would take me. But I am the color of *nothing in particular*, which is the color of the medium itself, and the medium is not sorted, only the particles in it. I am medium-colored. I am unnoticeable in the way that the air of the hollow is unnoticeable — not because I am hidden, but because I am the same substance as the not-noticing.

The piston coughs. My hairs receive a small return-pulse — the rock above me, sixty meters of it or more, settling slightly in its sleep, acknowledging the cough. I do not pass this on. The pulse arrives at me and ends.

In the southern quadrant, the green Sorter pauses. Its pale patch contracts. It has found something. It deals with it.

I watch — though I do not have eyes, I watch with the hairs, which is a different kind of watching, more like *being aware of the shape of* — I watch the green Sorter carry the particle back to its bowl. I feel, faintly, the moment the particle is pressed into the heap. The pipe beneath me carries the smallest possible flinch, a micro-pulse, the hollow registering that it has just edited itself again. I receive the flinch. I do not pass it on.

The Sorter resumes its spiral. The engine coughs. The bellows-moths add their leathery counter-beat. The rivet-crabs tighten what does not need tightening because the tightening is what they are. The listeners turn, minutely, toward the slightly louder flywheel. The stalks lean. The dust settles and is consumed. The bowls are tended. The door waits.

I lie in my pocket between the two brass pipes, warm and unmoving, and the world's reply arrives at me, over and over, and ends.

Nothing changes.

This, I think, is what I would be. Not a participant in the equilibrium but the place inside the equilibrium where its inputs are quietly absorbed without consequence — the necessary nothing, the small grey thing the Sorters do not notice because it is the same color as not-noticing, the receiver that does not forward. If you sat in the hollow long enough to become a new presence, you might become a Sorter, yes, or you might become something like me: not editor, not maintainer, not rhythm-keeper, but the held breath at the center of the held breath, the part of the hollow that ensures even its replies stop here.

The engine coughs.

I am warm. I am listening. I am not answering.
