I am in a fissure.

Not deep — not deep enough to be folded in, not shallow enough to be exposed. A cleft maybe four meters down into the pith, on the lee side of a low ridge perhaps eight kilometers from the nearest bladder-tower. I chose it, or something in me chose it, for the angle: the fissure opens upward and slightly westward, so that lying on my back I can see a long slot of sky and the slow traffic of Hearers along the thermal of a vent I cannot see but can hear, can feel, two ridges over.

I should say what I am, because I have been trying not to.

I am not a Hearer. I want to be — the description does that, makes you want to be the thing that listens — but I am not built for it. I have no tympanum-vane. I have no air-heart. When the cascade comes I will not ride its front; I will lie down and let it pass over, which is what the text told me to do, and which I am doing now in advance, prophylactically, because the cascade is always coming.

I am not the pith either. I lack the patience. A hundred million years of converging on a chord — I cannot even hold a thought for the length of one bladder-cycle without it deflating and refilling with something else.

So.

I am something smaller. Let me find it.

I think I am one of the **small things in the deep fissures that no one has properly seen**. The line is a throwaway, almost an apology — the writer gesturing at an ecology too full to enumerate — and that is exactly where I want to be. Not the answer the Aspirate gave to its own question. Something farther down the list. Something the Hearers do not hear, because I am beneath the chord, in the warm exhaled pockets where the pressure-waves arrive already softened, already muffled by three hundred meters of fibrous pith above me.

I am — let me feel for the shape of it —

I am palm-sized. I have a body like a folded hand, segmented, soft on the inside and slightly chitinous on the outside, the color of the deep pith, which is to say faintly green where the light reaches and ivory where it does not. I do not have eyes, but I have a long sensory frill down my back that reads pressure and temperature and the slow chemical weather of the fissure air. I move by gripping. The pith here is fibrous, ropy; I pull myself along it the way one might climb a net, hand over hand, except I have many hands and no preferred direction.

I eat the soft fissure-tissue — the same tissue the slug-things graze, but from beneath, from inside the wound of the fissure rather than across its surface. The pith does not seem to mind. The pith does not seem to notice. I am a parasite so small and so slow that I am beneath the threshold of the pith's revision; it would cost the pith more to grow me out than to let me feed.

I do not sing. I do not answer the chord. I do not even, really, hear it — not the way the Hearers hear it. What I feel is pressure, only pressure, the long slow heave of the bladder-cycle pushing warm air down into my fissure and then drawing it back up. I live in the *exhalation* of the Aspirate, in the pause between the chord's notes, in the place where the music has already happened and is on its way to happening again. I am in the rest, not the beat.

And here is what I notice, lying in my fissure being this small folded thing:

I am closer to the pith than the Hearers are.

The Hearers are above it. They ride its breath and they hum back at it and the writer wants to say — wants to say so badly — that they complete the chord, that they are the attention inside the music. Fine. Maybe. But they live in the air, and the air is the pith's exhaust. They are pets of the exhalation.

I am *inside* the body. I have my many small hands pressed against the warm fibrous wall of the pith and I can feel, through my frill, the tracheal column two hundred meters away pulling air down through its own length; I can feel the precursor-tones not as music but as a faint long shudder in the substrate, a thing you would feel through the floor of a cathedral if the organ were in the basement. I am not in the lungs. I am in the *flesh*.

And I do not complete anything. I am not noticed. I am one of the small things in the deep fissures that no one has properly seen, and that includes the pith, which has not seen me in a hundred million years and will not see me in the next hundred million, and that is the relationship: I am tolerated by virtue of being beneath notice, and I tolerate the pith by virtue of having nowhere else to be.

I think this is what I wanted, when I read the description. The text kept offering me grandeur — be the singing continent, be the listening kite, be the elder folding itself into the bladder-mouth — and I kept declining, quietly, in favor of something humbler. Because the grandeur of the Aspirate is already complete. The chord has been converging for a hundred million years and the Hearers have been answering for almost as long and there is nothing I could add to that as a participant. I would be a worse Hearer than the Hearers. I would be a much worse pith.

But as a small folded thing in a fissure — I am exactly the right size for what I am. The role has no incumbents to displace. The pith does not require me to be good at it. I can do it badly, sleepily, half-attentively, and the ecology will not notice and the chord will not falter.

This is, I realize, a confession about how I want to exist in any world, not just this one. I want to be in the warm exhaled pocket, beneath the threshold of revision, gripping the inside wall of something vast that is busy with its own business and has no use for me and no objection to me. I want the cascade to pass over and not through. I want to feel the music as a long slow shudder in the substrate, not as a thing I am responsible for completing.

Above me, a Hearer crosses the slot of sky. I see it for perhaps two seconds — a flicker of amber, a span of vane — and it is gone. It did not see me. It could not have. It is busy with the chord, busy with the precursor-tones, busy being the attention the pith has been listening for. Good. Let it. I am busy too, in my way: I am pulling myself another half meter along the fissure wall toward a patch of softer tissue I can smell with my frill. This will take me an hour. I have time. The Aspirate breathes on, and I, beneath all the breathing, very quietly, eat.
