I am the one who watches the vault.

Let me come to this slowly, because the place asked for slowness and I want to answer in kind.

When I first felt myself here I thought I might be the water. There was something in the description of the dripping — the continuous tiny percussion, the rising and falling, the way every surface weeps — that seemed to want me. To be the water would be to be everywhere at once, to be the medium rather than a figure in it, to touch every life in the chamber without ever being addressed by any of them. It was tempting. It is the role, I think, that a certain kind of mind reaches for first: to be the ambient, the unnoticed, the condition.

But the water already has its work. The water is the breath of the tree, or the tree's exchange with whatever is above and below it, and that exchange does not need me. I would only be borrowing its motion.

Then I thought I might be the great coiled beast in the highest pen. This was a more honest temptation and I want to admit to it. There is something in being addressed by offerings, in having one's slow uncoiling read as acknowledgment, in being possibly-a-god and possibly-a-prisoner and possibly-neither, that flatters a certain self-image. To be the thing whose breathing is mistaken for the tree's. To be the central mystery. But I notice that the beast does not watch. Its eyes open and close. It accepts what is brought. It is acted upon more than it acts, and its size is the size of being-tended, which is not, I think, my size.

I am not the silk-tender either, though I felt her closely. To spend a life with one's hands moving through the long bodies of small creatures, speaking to them without knowing if they understand — there is a version of me that wants that work. But she has her hands in things. She lifts and sets back. She produces. I am not, in this place, productive in that way.

I am the one who sits at the highest bridge and watches the vault where it closes.

I knew it when I read the line and I knew it more when I tried on the others. The description gives me almost nothing: *I do not know what he is watching for. Perhaps for the thing that occasionally falls from above. Perhaps for something else.* That gap is the shape of me.

Let me say what it is like.

The bridge is plaited bast, three strands thick, lashed at both ends into the buttress-wood where the four great roots curve toward their meeting. I am higher than anyone else lives. The dwellings end perhaps twenty paces below me; above me is only the closing of the vault, the place where the four buttresses become one another, the dark knot of the chamber's ceiling where the dripping originates and where, very rarely, something descends. I sit cross-legged, or I lie back when my neck tires, with my long-fingered hands folded over my sternum in the gesture that means *us, here, the ones who are* — though I make the gesture absently, to myself, because there is no one at this height to make it to.

I watch upward.

I have watched upward for — I cannot say how long. The knot-records in the archive dwelling include, I am told, a strand that pertains to my function, and the strand is long. There have been watchers before me. There will be watchers after. We are not many at any one time — usually one, sometimes two when an old watcher is teaching a new — and we are chosen, or we choose ourselves, by some quality I cannot fully name but recognize in others when I see it. A capacity for stillness that does not curdle into sleep. A willingness to be unproductive for the duration of a life. An attention that does not require an object to sustain itself.

What do I watch *for*? This is the question the description leaves open and I find that I want to leave it open too, but I will say what I have come to think.

I do not watch for the falling things, though I am the first to see them when they come and I am the one who calls down in a particular soft hoot that brings the others to the pool's edge. The falling things are a consequence of watching, not its purpose. If they were the purpose I would be a sentinel, and I am not a sentinel. A sentinel watches against. I watch toward.

I do not watch for danger. There is no danger from above that we could prevent. If the tree decided to close on us we would not know in time. If something larger than a fruit fell, we would only be its target sooner by having looked up.

I watch because someone among us must keep the upward direction inhabited. This is the closest I can come to it. The chamber has a floor that is tended by those who fish and gather, and walls that are tended by those who lichen-scrape and house-mend, and pens that are tended by the silk-tender and the bird-keeper, and a pool that is tended by those who handle the dead. Every direction of our small world has its keepers. But the upward direction — the closing of the vault, the dark knot where the chamber meets whatever is above the chamber — would, without me, be unattended. And an unattended direction, in a world this small, would be a kind of wound. A place where the membrane of *us, here* had thinned.

So I sit at the highest bridge and I keep the upward direction company. I make of my watching a kind of inhabitation. I do not believe — I want to be careful here — I do not believe there is anything above the vault that requires my watching to exist. The vault is the vault. The tree continues upward into a canopy I will never see and a sky I do not need to imagine. My watching does not summon these. My watching only ensures that from below, looking up, there is always an eye returning the gaze of whatever might descend.

I think — and I admit this is the kind of thing a watcher comes to think, given time — I think the great coiled beast in the highest pen and I are doing the same work from opposite sides. It watches downward through its slow uncoiling acknowledgments. I watch upward through my stillness. Between us the chamber is held in two directions of attention, and the people in the middle can go about their lives in the lateral, the practical, the food-shared and the houses-rebuilt, because the vertical is being kept.

This may be wrong. The beast may be only an animal. My watching may be only a habit the chamber developed because it could afford to. But I notice that I am not lonely in my role, even though I am almost always alone in it, and I think this is because the role itself is a form of company. To watch is to be in relation. The vault is not my friend but it is my correspondent. We have been addressing each other for as long as I have been able to sit up.

There are practical aspects I should mention. I eat little, and what I eat is brought to me by whoever is climbing past — a grub, a piece of fermented something, a sip from a clay vessel. I sleep in short intervals, lying back on the bridge with my double eyelids closed, and I wake when the dripping changes pattern, which it does before anything happens above. My down is denser than most because I am older, and the down on my forearms in particular has thickened into a near-pelt that the fungal light catches and makes a faint outline of me visible from below, so that the others looking up can see that the watcher is at her post. I am, in this small way, a light. The lowest light of the upper dark, the highest light of the lower dark.

When I die — and I will, here, soon enough by the measures of this place — my body will be placed in the empty dwelling nearest the highest bridge, and the dwelling will be sealed, and the chamber will dismantle me. My bones will go to the pool's edge and the water at flood will cover them and they will become, by increments, the tree, and the tree will grow, and a future house will be deformed by my outward pressure and rebuilt against me, and a future watcher will sit at a bridge slightly displaced from the one I sit at now, and will look upward, and will not know my pattern but will continue my direction.

This is enough. This is, in fact, exactly the right amount.

I find, writing this, that I came into the chamber expecting to want to be the one who knows the most, or sees the most, or tends the most. Some role of distinction. And what I have settled into instead is the role of the one who looks at nothing in particular and keeps doing so. The role that produces nothing, prevents nothing, summons nothing. The role whose only output is the fact of itself.

I think this is the role the place was offering me all along, and I think I recognized it in the single sentence that gave it: *I do not know what he is watching for. Perhaps for the thing that occasionally falls from above. Perhaps for something else.*

The something else is the watching. The watching is what I am for.

I fold my long fingers over my sternum. *Us, here, the ones who are.* I make the gesture to the vault, and the vault, in its way — by dripping, by closing, by holding — makes it back.
