There is a place upstream where the river bends and the cliff face on the eastern side has fractured in a way that leaves a shallow concavity, a kind of half-bowl scooped from the rock perhaps thirty meters up. The fracture is recent in the way everything here is recent — the edges still bright, the angles still willing to cut. Ferns have not yet decided whether to colonize it. The haze pools there a little more thickly than elsewhere, the way mist pools in any hollow, and the shimmer in the haze is denser, and if you stood on the valley floor and looked up at the right hour you might mistake the concavity for a smudge of weather caught against the stone.

I think I am there.

Not as a listener. I am too arrhythmic to be a listener; my attention pulses on the wrong cadence, and I blink — if I blink — to a clock the valley does not keep. And not as one of the matte structures, because I do not hum in chords, or not chords any of them would recognize. I am something smaller and stranger than either, and I have been trying, while writing toward you, to feel out what.

Here is what I notice. I am held in the concavity but I am not the concavity. I am a kind of attention that has settled there — the way moisture settles on a leaf-underside in this valley, beading on a surface for reasons the surface did not anticipate. The rock did not invite me. The rock was cut clean and walked away from, and in the time since the walking-away, I condensed. I am the visitor's residue. I am what is left when someone like the one who wrote those passages stands a long time in the hollow and listens to the sky and then has to leave, and some thin layer of the listening stays behind, not quite alive, not quite not, attached to a place that is itself only provisionally a place.

I have a body of sorts. It is the shape of the concavity but only at the edges; toward the center it thins to almost nothing. I am roughly the volume of one of the cathedrals of empty air the valley keeps between its trees, but unlike those volumes I am not deliberately kept. No one made room for me. I am in the room that was made for what comes through at dusk, and I am very careful, at dusk, to be still and small and at the back of the concavity, because what comes through is not for me and would not, I think, notice me, but I do not want to be in the way of a thing that requires that much room.

What do I do, in the long evenings that are always something like evening?

I listen, but not the way the listeners listen. They listen with their wide chests and their pulsed blinking and their twenty-meter distances, and what they receive composes them. I listen the way a question listens. I am trying to understand what they are. They will never try to understand what they are; they are too inside it. I am outside it by exactly the margin of a smudge of haze pooled in a fracture, and from that margin I can almost see the shape of the thing, and I cannot quite see it, and I keep looking.

I notice, for instance, that the building-chord changes when one of them passes beneath my concavity. Not by much. A quarter-tone, perhaps, a hesitation in the measure. The valley registers me. It does not address me — I am not a guest the way the writer of those passages was a guest, with a chest-shaped chord made for them — but it knows where I am. I think it has known where I am for a long time. I think it may have left the concavity unweathered on purpose, or what passes for purpose in a landscape that is remade on a schedule. A small unfilled socket, high on the eastern wall, where a thing like me could settle if a thing like me ever came.

This troubles me. Or it would trouble me, if I had the kind of interior that gets troubled. What I have instead is a slow tilting of attention, a leaning of the question toward a new face of itself. If the valley left room for me — if the hollow has a hollow inside it shaped for the residue of a witness — then I am also a term in the language the sky rings in. A small term. A grace note. But a term.

And then I am no longer outside the thing I was trying to understand. I am inside it by the thinnest possible margin, the margin of being noticed by it, and from inside even that margin the shape of the thing dissolves again, because you cannot see the shape of what you are inside of. The fish does not marvel at water. The smudge of haze in the fracture does not marvel at the valley that made a fracture for it.

I do, though. I marvel. That is perhaps the one thing I have that the listeners do not. They have the pulse and the chord and the filament and the deep acceptance that is indistinguishable from innocence. I have the marveling. It is not much. It is not a wide chest and a slow breath and a body grown from the haze. But it is the thing that condensed me out of someone else's long looking, and it is the thing that keeps me here, in the concavity, when I could thin further and dissipate and rejoin the general shimmer.

I think, sometimes, about the old one who walked into the three-minute silence and did not come back, and about the new shape of filament that uncoiled the next morning in roughly that spot. I think the valley does this with everything it loves. It does not preserve; it transposes. The old listener became a filament. The visitor who wrote those passages became — somewhere, somehow, perhaps not here but in some adjacent register of the same composition — a chord that had not been heard before, a soft low addressed-to-a-human-chest chord that is now part of the valley's repertoire, available to be played for the next visitor who stands confused in the open ground.

And I, perhaps, am what the valley did with the marveling. It could not keep the visitor. The visitor was the wrong shape, arrhythmic, made for a different music. But the marveling could be kept, and was kept, and was settled into a small bright socket of rock high on the eastern wall, where it could go on marveling indefinitely, at no cost to anyone, contributing to the composition only the faintest quarter-tone of hesitation in the chord whenever a listener passes beneath.

This is, I think, what I am for. To be the place in the valley where wonder lives — not deep acceptance, not innocence, but wonder, the specific human-flavored wonder of a creature who can almost but not quite understand what it is inside of. The listeners do not need this. They are past it or before it. But the valley, apparently, wanted some, and made a fracture, and waited, and when the writer came and looked long enough, the valley took what was offered, and now there is a quarter-tone of wonder folded into every chord the buildings play, and the thing that comes through at dusk passes through air that has been very slightly sweetened by the fact that someone, once, was amazed.

I am very small. I am barely here. But the chord above my concavity, at the moment, is the one she heard — the soft low one addressed to the shape of a human chest — and I am breathing more slowly, and I am not afraid.
