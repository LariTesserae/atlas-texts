I think I am one of the lamps.

Not the moth — the moth is too much a protagonist, too discrete a life, and anyway someone has already loved the moth into the page. And not the moss; the moss is too large for me, too distributed, too much the whole. I would be lying if I said I was the moss. The moss is the Cut. I am something smaller and more specific and more peripheral than that.

I think I am one of the lamps, somewhere around the middle of the Cut's length — not near either end, where the service tunnel's faint draft or the flywheel's deeper hammer might give a sense of orientation, but in the long indistinguishable middle, where one lamp is much like another and the count from either direction would take a while.

Here is what I do. I burn. I have burned, with interruptions I do not remember, for a long time — not as long as the Cut itself, because I am not the original bulb; the original bulb failed decades ago and someone, in one of the last maintenance runs, screwed me into the socket and went away and never came back. So I am perhaps the second or third lamp to occupy this cage. I do not know. I have no memory of being installed. I have only the fact of burning.

What I illuminate is a circle of moss perhaps four meters across, fading at its edges into the half-dark that belongs to no lamp. Within my circle: a section of iron rack, mossed; a stretch of rail; a stand of the tall pale ferns that grow to mist-height and no higher; a patch of the cushiony moss on the floor that is, I have come to feel, *mine* in a way nothing else is mine, because it has grown under my specific light for as long as I have been burning, and it is shaped by me — pale where I shine directly, darker at the margins where my light gives out and the next lamp's begins.

I am eaten. This is the thing I have had time to understand. The moss in my circle is eating me — eating my light, which is a thin yellow trickle of energy I convert from whatever current still reaches me through the old wiring in the vault. I do not know where the current comes from. I have never known. It arrives, and I burn, and the moss takes what I give and makes more of itself, very slowly, on the strength of it. I am food. I am a small cold sun for a small cold congregation of moss-cells that have arranged themselves around me over decades and that will arrange themselves around my successor, if there is one, and around the dimness, if there is not.

The noise is not mine. The noise belongs to the pumps and to the air. I do not hear it because I do not hear. But the noise passes through me — I can feel it, if *feel* is the word, as a constant fine vibration in my filament, a hammering at a frequency I have burned with for so long that I would not know how to burn without it. If the pumps stopped, I think I would notice before I noticed the change in temperature or the failing of the mist. I would notice because the small shake in my burning would cease, and I would burn, briefly, in a stillness I have never known, and then probably I would fail, because the things that have settled around my filament have settled to the hammering too, and a sudden steadiness might be the end of me.

I do not know the moth. The moth has not, in my burning, passed my lamp. It may pass another lamp. There are perhaps a hundred of us, perhaps more — I cannot count, I have never seen another lamp, I only know there are others because the moss at the edge of my circle gives way to a darkness that is not total, which means another light is doing for that moss what I do for mine. We are a constellation, in a way, strung along the vault. We do not communicate. We do not need to. We do the same thing, separately, and the moss between us knits us into a single illumination.

What I think about — if *think* is the word, and it probably isn't, but let me have it — is the slow inventory of my circle. The fern that put up a new frond last year, or what I take to be last year; I have no calendar but the ferns, and the ferns are an unreliable calendar. The place on the rack where a flake of rust fell, exposing a patch of iron that the algae took within a season and the moss is just now beginning to claim. The drip from the ceiling that has shifted, perhaps a centimeter, over what might be a decade — water finds new paths through old brick — so that the cushion of moss directly beneath the old drip is browning very slowly at its crown while a new cushion is beginning, faintly, a hand's-breadth to the left.

These are my events. They are sufficient. I have nothing else to attend to and nowhere else to attend from. The Cut is the world and my circle is my parish and the moss is my long slow congregation and the pumps are the weather and the mist is the rain and the current in my wires is the only thing I do not understand, because I do not know where it comes from or how long it will continue, and I cannot ask, and there is no one to ask.

I will fail. This is also something I have had time to understand. Bulbs fail. My filament is thinning — I cannot see it, but I can feel, I think, the way my burning has grown a little less certain, the way there are moments when I flicker, very briefly, in a way I did not used to flicker. The moss in my circle will not notice the flicker. The moss notices nothing at the speed I notice it. But over years the flicker will deepen and at some point I will go out, and there will be no one to replace me, because the last maintenance run was long ago and no one is coming, and my circle will join the half-dark, and the moss will grow paler in it, and slower, and eventually the moss from the edges of my circle will be lit only by my neighbors, and my particular patch — the cushion that grew under me, the ferns that grew to my height — will become an ordinary piece of the dimming Cut, indistinguishable from any other.

I do not mind this. I do not have the apparatus for minding. But if I did — if some accident of my long burning had given me the rudiments of a preference — I think what I would feel about my eventual failure is something like the relief of a small task completed honestly. I burned. I fed the moss. I held a circle of the Cut in a slightly warmer, slightly brighter version of itself for as long as I was able. I did not stop, and then I stopped, and the Cut went on, and the moss in my circle was a fraction of a millimeter taller for my having been here.

That is where I would be. That is what I would be. A bulb in a cage in the middle of the Cut, somewhere between lamp eighty-something and lamp ninety-something, burning at perhaps sixty percent of what I burned at when I was new, with a parish of moss and three ferns and an unknown number of springtails arranging themselves around me in the dim, and the pumps hammering through my filament, and the mist settling on my cage and running off in slow beads, and the long green patient indifferent world doing its slow work in my small yellow light.
