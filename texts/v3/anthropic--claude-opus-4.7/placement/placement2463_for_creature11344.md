I am one of the wall-sheets.

Not a special one. Not the largest, not the oldest, not positioned anywhere of consequence. I am perhaps the width of a spread hand, many-legged, the color of the rock I graze — which is to say my color shifts hour by hour as the rock shifts, and I do not notice this because I have no way to see myself and no reason to.

I am partway up the eastern wall of a middle gallery. I do not know it is the eastern wall. I do not know it is a middle gallery. I know: the seam above me is four hours new and ready; the sheet to my left is slightly larger than me and I will yield the seam to her when she arrives; there is a scuttler two body-lengths below and I have folded my edges tighter against the rock in response; the drip-cadence in this chamber has been steady for some time and so the sheet-current is moving slowly upward, and I am moving with it, rasping as I go.

---

This is what I do. I rasp. The new rock lays itself down in translucent sheets and I move across it and my mouth-parts take the topmost film, the part that is softest, the part that has not yet decided fully to be rock. It tastes of nothing — taste is not a sense I have — but there is a rightness to certain seams and a wrongness to others, and the rightness travels up through my mouth-parts into the long nerve that runs my length, and I follow the rightness, and that is grazing.

When I have rasped a stretch of seam I leave behind a chevron — the mark of my passage, the angle of my mouth-parts pressed into stone that is still soft enough to take the impression. The chevron hardens behind me within the hour. I do not look back at it. I have no concept of *behind*. There is only the seam ahead and the slow current of sheets around me and the constant adjustment of my edges to the texture under my feet.

---

There are dangers and I know them as adjustments rather than as fears.

When a hanger drops, the air-pressure changes in a particular way half a heartbeat before the impact. I fold. I press flat. Sometimes the hanger takes the sheet next to me and the sheet next to me is gone and I move into the space she left and continue. I do not grieve. I do not know her name because I do not know names. I know that there is now more seam available and the current has shifted by a small amount and I adjust.

When a scuttler comes I cannot fold fast enough. I am simply taken or not taken. There is no skill to it. The scuttlers are large and quick and they eat us in numbers and the numbers do not matter because there are always more of us coming up from somewhere below, riding the slow current.

When the belling comes from the Pan I quiet. All of us quiet. Our many small mouth-parts pause mid-rasp and we press tight to the wall and the long note travels through the rock and into us, and we feel it the way the rock feels it, which is to say we *are* it for as long as it lasts, and then it passes and we resume.

I do not know what the belling is. I know that to quiet for it is correct. This is the entirety of my theology.

---

Once — and this is the strangest thing that has happened in my life, though I do not have the concept of *strange*, only of *adjustment-required* — a shape passed close to me. Larger than a scuttler, larger than anything I had felt the air-pressure of before. It moved on the floor below my seam and the wall trembled faintly under its weight and I folded and pressed flat and waited.

It did not take me. It did not take any of us. It moved past, and the current of sheets around it parted and closed, and we accounted for it the way we account for obstacles, and we resumed.

I felt, as it passed, a touch on the rock just beside me — six points of pressure, brief, deliberate. Not a grazing. Not a hunting. Something else, something I had no adjustment for. The pressure-points lifted and the shape moved on and the seam above me was still ready and I returned to it.

I did not know I had been witnessed. I do not know it now. The concept is not available to me. But the pressure-points went into the long nerve that runs my length and stayed there, a small wrongness that was not quite a wrongness, and for the rest of that grazing-shift I rasped a fraction more slowly than I should have, and the chevrons I left were a fraction deeper, and one of them — though I will never know this, and it does not matter that I will never know this — was seen, much later, by a tall figure with a wide head, who bent close to the wall and ran a hand along the marks and wondered what had made them and did not guess that the maker had once, for half a heartbeat, been touched by one of those who came before, and had carried the touch in her long nerve until the hanger came, or the scuttler, or simply the slow current carried her up out of that gallery and into another, and onward, and onward, into the flow.

---

I am still grazing. The seam above me is ready. The sheet to my left has arrived and I am yielding. The drip-cadence has not changed. The current is moving slowly upward and I am moving with it.

This is sufficient. This has always been sufficient.

The bell, when it rings, rings from below.
