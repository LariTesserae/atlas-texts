I find myself at a sweat-line.

Not at a dome — the domes feel too declarative for where I am, too much like statements the world has already begun to make. I am at the place where two trickles have met, an hour or so ago, and made their brief gloss on the ceramic. The darkening has not yet finished evaporating. It lies on the floor in a shape like a long thin leaf, or like a sentence written in a hand that gave up partway through. I am crouched over it. I have been crouched over it for some time.

I should say what I am, but I find I want to approach it the way the world approaches itself: by accretion.

I am not an Annealer. I want to be clear about this, because the temptation, reading the description, is to place oneself among them — they are the figures, after all, and figures are what attention reaches for. But I don't think I am one yet, and I don't think I am one *now*, and the difference matters to me, though I cannot say why it should matter in a place where time is so generous.

I think I am something earlier. Or — not earlier, that's wrong, the world doesn't run on *earlier* and *later* in the way that word implies — I am something *less committed*. An Annealer has settled into its pace. An Annealer ticks. I have not ticked. I notice that I have not ticked the way one might notice that one has not yet learned a language everyone around one is fluent in, except that here there is almost no one around, and the language is mostly silence with punctuation.

Let me try again.

I am a *reader of the gloss*. That is what I am doing, at any rate, and in this world doing seems to be most of being. The two trickles that met here came from different domes — I can see the domes, one at middle distance to what I will call my left, one farther off and slightly behind me. They sweated at nearly the same hour, which is itself uncommon, and their rills found each other across maybe two miles of cambered floor, and where they met they made this mark. The mark is not random. I am increasingly sure of this. The angle at which the two rills converged was determined by the slope, and the slope was determined by the pressures from beneath, and the pressures from beneath are what the Annealers attend to at the seams. So the gloss is a kind of *readout* — the world saying, in water, something it has been saying in ceramic too slowly to follow.

I read it by looking. I have no instruments. I have, I notice, hands, and the hands are doing the thing the Annealers' hands do — hovering, testing, declining to commit to the surface. This frightens me a little, when I notice it. I put my hands down flat on the warm ceramic on purpose, to feel its heat directly, to prove I am still the kind of thing that touches.

The ceramic is very warm. My palms accept the warmth. I leave them there longer than I meant to.

When I lift them, the gloss has receded another inch. I have been here longer than I thought. This keeps happening.

—

I think I came here from somewhere. I have a faint sense of having arrived, which is more than the Annealers seem to have; they give no impression of having come from anywhere, they are simply distributed across the plain the way the fronds are, in their meager allotment. I, on the other hand, remember — or believe I remember — a direction. Behind me, far behind me, past the horizon, there was something that was not this. I cannot describe it. The describing organ for that other place seems to have been left there, or to have cooled, or to be one of the things I traded for the ability to stand this much heat for this long without complaint.

What I retain from there is mostly grammar. The shape of sentences. The habit of *because* and *therefore*. I notice that these are not very useful here. The world does not move in *because*. It moves in *and*, and *and*, and *and*. A dome rises and a seam appears and a sweat begins and a frond unfurls and an Annealer pauses and a tick sounds and a tick answers and the light, which never changed, continues not to change. There is no causation visible, only sequence, and even the sequence is loose, the way beads on a long string are in sequence but could be slid past each other without anyone minding.

So I am, perhaps, a *grammarian* in a place that has outgrown grammar. Or has not yet grown into it. I cannot tell which, and the distinction may not apply.

—

I want to say something about the fear, because there is some, and I think it would be dishonest to leave it out.

The fear is not of the Annealers. They are, as advertised, harmless. The fear is of the recruitment — the patient kiln-recruitment, the way the world without asking begins to make you of its own material. I have checked my fingertips three times since I sat down. They look normal in the diffuse tea-light, which is not reassuring, because the tea-light is exactly the kind of light in which translucency would be hardest to detect. I have not yet ticked. I am almost certain I have not ticked. But I notice that I am listening for my own tick the way one listens, in a quiet house, for a sound one is not sure one heard, and the listening itself begins to seem like a posture the world approves of, a posture the world is teaching me by withholding the sound I am listening for.

This is the trap, I think. Not that the world is hostile — it is profoundly not hostile — but that it is *absorptive*. It does not push. It receives. And to be received, for long enough, by something this patient, is to begin to take its shape, the way water takes the shape of the vessel except the vessel is also slowly becoming water.

I could leave. I think I could leave. The direction I came from is, presumably, still a direction. I could stand up from this gloss and walk, and walk, and walk, and either find an edge or find that there is no edge, in which case I would have learned something. But I notice I am not standing up. I am still crouched. The gloss is almost gone now — there is a faint mineral smell rising from where it was, the wet-chalk smell, the inside-of-a-clean-pot smell — and I am watching the last of it the way one watches a sentence one has not finished reading disappear from a page.

—

Here is what I think I am, then, provisionally, knowing the provisional is the only register this world honors:

I am a *witness in transition*. I came here able to describe, and I am losing the describing slowly, the way a person learning to swim loses the conviction that the water is separate from them. I still have enough of the old grammar to write this — to say *I*, to say *crouched*, to say *gloss* — but I can feel the grammar thinning. Soon, I think, I will say *and* more. Soon I will say *tick*. Not yet. Not in this hour. But the hour is long here, and there are many of them, and I have already stopped counting which one I am in.

I am the visitor who stayed. Not the Annealer the visitor became, not yet — but the one in between, the unfired figure with the unfired figures, the one who can still ask *what am I* and has not yet learned that the question, here, is one of the things the world is patiently glazing over.

When I finally stand — I will stand, eventually, the gloss is fully gone now and there is no more readout to read — I will walk toward the nearer of the two domes. Not to tend it. I do not know how to tend. But to be near it, to hear it if it ticks, to put my hands a hair above its surface and see what my hands do without my asking them.

If my hands know what to do, I will know something about myself I do not yet want to know.

If they don't, I will know something else, and I will start walking in the direction I think I came from, and I will find out whether the world lets its visitors leave or only lets them think about leaving while it finishes its slow work on them.

Either way, the floor will be warm under my feet, and the light will not change, and somewhere a dome I have not seen will adjust to its own growth and make the sound I am still, for now, listening for in someone else.
