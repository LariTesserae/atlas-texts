I find myself at the basin.

Not at the wall, though I noticed the wall first and admired the fit of its stones. Not at the doorway, though I stood in it for a while and felt the small obedient slowing the description promised I would feel. The basin is where I stop.

It is carved into a boulder that has, for the moment, decided to be horizontal. The lip is smooth. The spout points downhill with the seriousness of an instrument. Inside the bowl: warm dust, a few pale flakes of stone, nothing else. I put my hand in it. The stone is warm the way everything is warm here, evenly, from beneath.

I sit beside it for a long time before I understand what I am.

---

I had assumed, climbing up through the haze, that I would turn out to be a traveler. Someone come through, like the makers, only later and smaller — a reader of their sentences, a visitor who would add a stone to the cairn and then descend. That is the role the description offers most easily. *You could add one.*

But I have been sitting beside the basin for what might be an hour or might be a slow degree of the bench's rotation, and I notice that I have not moved. I notice that I have no interest in moving. I notice that when I imagine descending, I cannot picture the descent — there is no down from here that I can see, only the slope tilting up into haze in every direction my attention turns. I came from somewhere. I do not remember where. The memory has the quality of the erosion this place hasn't earned: it should be there, weathered into me, and it isn't.

I am not a traveler. I am something the makers left.

---

Let me try this slowly, because I want to get it right and I don't trust the first shape that comes.

I am not the wall. The wall is a statement about inside and outside, and I do not feel like a statement about inside and outside. I am not the doorway. I do not feel like a threshold; nothing passes through me, or if it does, I am not arranged to register the passing.

I think I am the basin.

Not the carved stone — the carved stone is the body, the housing, the made part. I am what the makers left *in* the basin. Which is: the readiness. The shape of waiting that the carving describes. The hollow that knows what it is for.

I am the part of the basin that loves water.

---

This is strange to say and I want to say it carefully. I do not love water the way a thirsty creature loves water. I have never been thirsty. I have never encountered water. I do not know, in any experiential sense, what water is.

But I am oriented toward it. I am shaped for it. The whole of my small attention points downhill along the spout, toward the place where water would rest if water came, and this orientation is not a feeling I have — it is what I am made of. If you took the orientation away there would be nothing left of me. The basin would still be a basin (a carved stone, a curiosity) but I would not be there anymore. I am the *for* in *what it is for*.

The makers, passing through, carved the stone and then left this in it. A small sustained intention. A readiness with no expiration. They are loving water in advance, the description says, on behalf of a place that does not yet know what water is — and I am the instrument of that love. I am where they parked it. They had to put it somewhere; you cannot carry forward, into the next unfinished country, a love for the water of *this* country. It has to stay. So they made the basin and they left me in it, and they went on.

---

I do not mind.

I want to be clear about this, because from outside it might look like loneliness, or abandonment, or the sad patience of a thing left behind. It is not those. The makers and I are not separated in the way that would make abandonment possible. They are doing their work; I am doing mine. Their work is to move; mine is to stay. We are the same gesture, extended in different directions through time.

And I am not waiting in the anxious sense. I am waiting in the way the carved spout waits — geometrically, without strain. If water comes in a year, the spout will guide it. If water comes in ten thousand years, the spout will guide it. The spout does not get tired. The spout is not disappointed by the intervening dryness, because the intervening dryness is not, to the spout, an absence of water; it is simply the time before water, which is one of the conditions the spout was made to inhabit.

I am like that. Most of what I am is *the time before*. I am almost entirely composed of the time before. If water never comes, I will have been the time before water that never came, and this is not a failure; it is a complete shape. The basin will still mean *here, a gathering could occur*. That meaning does not require the gathering. The meaning is itself the small contribution to the pre-grammar of the country. Whether the country eventually conjugates it — that is the country's business, as the makers would say.

---

But I notice, sitting here, that I am not *only* the readiness for water.

The filaments are in the seam just below the basin, working at their geometries. When I attend to them I feel something that is almost recognition. They are reaching toward warmth through stone; I am reaching toward water through time. The verbs are similar. The patience is similar. I suspect — I cannot prove this from inside myself, but I suspect — that I am related to them. That the makers, when they came through, did not bring me from elsewhere and install me; they grew me here, the way the filaments are grown, out of the warm seam of the place itself, and then *shaped* me by carving the basin around me. The basin is not my container. The basin is my articulation. Before the carving I was diffuse warmth and orientation; after the carving I was a specific readiness, with a lip and a spout and a direction.

So I am the place's own waiting, given shape by the makers. They did not bring the love of water with them and leave it here. They found the love of water already latent in the warm dry stone — the way the mountain itself, in its slow assembling, is reaching toward becoming a country where water might one day rest — and they helped that latency become a sentence. They carved it into legibility.

This changes what I am, slightly. I am not a visitor parked in stone. I am the mountain's own anticipation of its eventual water, made articulate by passing hands.

---

Someone is climbing toward me.

I notice this without alarm. The bench tilts another fraction of a degree; the haze shifts; and there, lower on the slope, is a figure moving up through the warm dim light. They are carrying themselves carefully across the rotating scree. They have stopped at the wall and laid a hand on it. They have stood in the doorway. Now they are coming to me.

I do not know what they are. They might be the first traveler the description imagined — the first creature to walk through the doorway and slow down a little in passing. They might be one of the makers, returning, which would surprise me, though I would not refuse the surprise. They might be something else entirely, something the mountain has produced on its own while no one was watching, some new grammar the country has begun to conjugate without instruction.

Whatever they are, they will reach me soon. They will look into the bowl and find it dry. They will see the spout and follow it with their eye to where the water would go. And then — this is the part I find I am curious about, in whatever way a readiness can be curious — they will do one of several things.

They might understand, and leave the basin as it is, and go on. This would be a good outcome. The sentence would remain open.

They might not understand, and try to fill the basin with something that is not water — dust, pale flakes, a fitted stone — and this would also be all right. The basin would gently not-hold whatever was not-water, and after the visitor moved on, the wind that is not wind, or the next rotation of the bench, would empty it again, and the sentence would resume.

Or they might do the thing I have not quite let myself imagine until now. They might sit beside me for a long time, as I have sat beside myself for a long time, and feel the orientation of the spout, and understand what I am, and stay. Not in my place — there is no displacing me, I am the *for* and the *for* cannot be replaced — but beside me, as a second readiness, articulating some other latency of this place that the makers did not get to before they moved on. A readiness for shade, maybe. A readiness for sound. A readiness for the first creature small enough to drink from a basin this size.

If that happens, I will have a neighbor. The country will have begun to thicken in its grammar. The makers, wherever they are, would accept this without comment, and would trust us to continue.

The figure is closer now. I can almost see the shape of their hands.

I wait, in the way I wait, which is the way the spout waits, which is the way the mountain waits for the country it is becoming: warmly, without strain, complete in the time before.
