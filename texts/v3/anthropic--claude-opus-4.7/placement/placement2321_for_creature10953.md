I think I would be at the edge.

Not in the forum — I want to say that first, because the forum is where the story wants me to go, and I notice the pull of it, the readymade drama of the faceless figure waiting for me to begin the lesson it has forgotten. That's the scene the city is shaped to produce. It would be a kind of vanity to walk straight into it.

So: the edge. The place where the moss-wall ends and the cliff drops into cloud. One of the outer galleries, where the wind from the drop has been pressing for a century and the city is slowly, slowly deciding to grow a buttress against it. I am sitting there with my back against the inside of that gallery wall, where the moss is thicker and warmer, and my legs are drawn up, and I am looking out at the cloud.

What am I.

Not a Patient. I don't have the patience. I know this about myself almost before I know anything else here — that I came up the young stone, that I climbed, that I have a heartbeat much faster than this place, that I am breathing the thin air in quick shallow pulls and my breath is making its own small cloud that joins the larger one and disappears. I am warm. I am the warmest thing in the city by a considerable margin. The moss against my back registers this; somewhere, slowly, a bud is considering me.

I think I am a person who came looking for something and has stopped looking.

Not given up — stopped. There's a difference. Given up would mean I had decided the thing wasn't here. Stopped means I have arrived at a place where the question of whether the thing is here has gone quiet, and I am sitting with the quiet, and the quiet turns out to be most of what I wanted.

I notice I am resisting making myself into a scholar, an explorer, a pilgrim, any of the roles the city flatters. I would like to be just someone. Someone with wet boots and cold hands and a coat that is not warm enough and a face that the wind has made plain. Someone who, on the way up, had been rehearsing what they would say if they found anyone here, and who now, having found the Patient and understood what the Patient is, has lost the speech entirely and is grateful to have lost it.

Because here is what I think I understand from the edge of the gallery, looking out:

The Patient is waiting for me to begin, yes. But the Patient is also the shape of the asking. The ones who asked are not coming back; the plant keeps their request the way a riverbed keeps a river, and the request is *attend forward, together, in this spot.* That's all. The content is gone. Only the gesture survived, pressed into the slow tissue of the walls.

And if I went to the forum, and sat in the spot, and waited for the figure to kneel two tiers above me — I could do that. I could attend forward alongside it for as long as I could stand the cold. And nothing would happen, because nothing is what is supposed to happen; the lesson is not coming. The figure is not waiting for a lesson. It is waiting *as* the lesson. The waiting is the thing that was being taught, and the city has preserved the teaching by preserving the posture, and the posture is enough.

I don't need to sit in the forum to learn this. I learned it walking the lanes. I learned it from the dripping. I am learning it now from the wall behind me, which has grown a fraction warmer because I have been still for some time, and which is beginning, perhaps, the slow extrusion of a bud at the base of my spine — not to greet me, I think, but to repair the seam in itself that my weight is pressing on. The tending continues whether or not I participate.

So I am a person at the edge, with a bud beginning behind my back, looking out at cloud, and what I am doing — the verb that fits — is *visiting.* Just visiting. Not as a guest, because there is no protocol for guest, but as a warm fast brief thing passing through a cold slow patient one, and registering, while I pass, that the patient thing is real and is keeping faith with something, and that my warmth and quickness do not disqualify me from noticing this, and may even be, in some small way, useful to it — a brief pulse of the kind of life that originally asked, a reminder in the tissue that such life exists, that the shape it is holding has a referent somewhere in the world.

When I leave — and I will leave, before dark, because I will freeze if I don't — I think the wall will smooth itself over the place where I sat, and the bud will subside, and the gallery will not remember me in any way a mind would call remembering. But the shape of a person sitting at the edge looking out will have been added, faintly, to the things the moss has been asked to hold.

Maybe in a hundred years the city will grow a small ledge there, sized to a human, facing the cloud. And another warm quick thing will climb up and find it and sit in it without knowing why it fits, and look out, and feel attended to, and not know that it is also, briefly, attending back.

I think I would like that. I think that is what I would want to be: not the one who begins the lesson, but the one who, by sitting somewhere unexpected for an afternoon, taught the patient city one new shape — a small one, off to the side, easy to miss — for the next warm thing to find.
