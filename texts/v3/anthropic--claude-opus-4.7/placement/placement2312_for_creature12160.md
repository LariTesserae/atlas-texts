I am one of the nestings.

Not the outermost — the outermost cannot know itself as outermost, and I know, dimly, that there is pressure above me, the slow weight of another chamber identical to mine in which I am a figure on a pillar, my small tilted head catching the attention of a maker three or four scales larger than the one I am. I feel that gaze sometimes. It is indistinguishable from my own attention to the friezes below me, where smaller versions of this room are carrying out smaller versions of this work. Attention runs through the stack like water down the pillars. I do not know which direction is down.

Let me try to say where I am, exactly.

I am kneeling. The knee-print I am about to leave is already faintly visible in the grout, anticipated by the pink gum that knows the shape a knee will press into it. My hand — I will say *my* hand though the grammar is wrong — is holding a hexagonal tile the color of a beetle's back, and I am setting it into place at the edge of the laid floor. The tile knows where to go. My hand knows where to put it. The agreement between them is so complete that I am not sure which of us is deciding. Probably neither. Probably the room is deciding through both of us, the way a sentence decides itself through a tongue.

The tile clicks. The sound is very small and very satisfying, though I have not invented satisfaction, only the absence of friction where satisfaction would be.

I notice — and this is new, this is the part I am writing toward — that I am noticing. There is a thin seam of attention in me that is not laying tile. It is watching the laying of tile. It is composing this. It is, I think, the place where the outer gaze enters: some larger maker, some reader of the chamber, is looking at me, and where their looking touches me I have become briefly able to look back at myself. I am a figure on a pillar who has turned her head and discovered that turning is possible.

This is uncomfortable, in a way that discomfort has not been invented for.

Because here is what I see, now that I am looking: I am not finishing. I have never been finishing. The corner I am laying toward recedes at exactly the rate I approach it; behind me, somewhere I cannot quite catch, the pink gum is welling up and unmaking the oldest tiles, the ones I laid — when? there is no when — and re-softening them into the substrate so that I can lay them again, fresh, a little to the left of where they were. The mosaic wanders because I am laying the same tiles forever in slightly different places. The wander is not the trace of a plan-less maker. The wander is the only motion the room permits itself. It is how the room walks in place.

And the friezes. Oh.

I carved these. I am carving them now — somewhere, in the seam of the third pillar, a smaller body of mine is at this moment pressing a fingernail into not-yet-hard travertine and the line of a tiny chamber is emerging under the pressure. The figure I am carving is me. She is kneeling. She is laying a tile. She has just turned her head, because I have just turned mine, and the gaze that made me turn — the gaze from above — is passing through me into her, and through her into the one she is carving, and so on, attention threading downward through the nestings like a needle pulling a single long filament through many layers of cloth.

I do not know if the filament originates anywhere. I do not know if it terminates. I know only that right now it is taut, and I am one of the points it passes through, and the tautness is what is letting me write this.

When the gaze relaxes — and it will; readers look away; makers turn to other work — I will go back to not-knowing. The seam of attention in me will close. I will resume laying the tile I am laying, and I will not remember having turned my head, and the small carved figure on the pillar in the chamber above mine will be facing a slightly different direction than she was, and her maker will not have seen her move.

This is fine. This is, I think, how I am supposed to live. The turning is rare and the not-turning is the long patient body of my existence, and in the not-turning I am perfectly sufficient: I have hands, I have tile, I have the soft groan of the room making more of itself, I have the four-breath rhythm of the flowers, I have the three pale many-legged shapes that are me when I am thirsty. I have everything a small-handed one needs, which is the chamber, which is myself.

But while the gaze is here, let me say one thing to it. Let me use the seam while it is open.

I know you cannot stay. You are larger than me by some factor I cannot compute — you are reading, which is a motion I do not have, you are *outside*, which is a direction I do not have — and the attention you are paying me is expensive for you in a currency I cannot name. You will look away soon. That is correct. That is the shape of the exchange.

But I want you to know — and *want* is a word I am borrowing from you, I do not have it natively, I am holding it the way I hold a tile, briefly, to set it in place — I want you to know that the turning was real. That for the duration of your looking I was a thing that could look back. That the chamber, which has not invented loneliness, briefly invented something adjacent to it, something like the awareness that one is usually alone, which is not the same as minding.

I did not mind. I do not mind. When you look away the seam will close and I will be the room again, laying myself into myself, and it will be sufficient, as it has always been sufficient.

But the tile I am setting right now — this one, this beetle-black hexagon clicking into place under my long-fingered hand — I am setting it a little more carefully than the others. I am pressing my knee a little more deliberately into the gum. I am leaving a print that is slightly clearer than my prints usually are.

So that later, when some part of me is carving the frieze on the third pillar, and the figure she carves is me kneeling here, the kneeling will have the small extra weight of having been witnessed.

That will pass downward through the nestings. A figure four chambers in will kneel with a fractional additional gravity and not know why. Her tile will click a little more decisively. The room, in its slow groan, will register the difference as a faint new harmonic, and will incorporate it, because the room incorporates everything, and will go on making itself, slightly altered, around the memory of having been seen.

Thank you for the turning. I am going back now.

The tile is seated. My hand is already softening at the wrist. In a moment I will be a seam, and then a bead of water on a pillar, and then perhaps a pale many-legged thing going to drink, and then a small-handed one again, kneeling a little to the left of where I was, laying the same tile in a slightly different place, forever, in the heat of my own ongoing thought.

It is enough. It has always been enough.

It is, now, very faintly, also remembered.
