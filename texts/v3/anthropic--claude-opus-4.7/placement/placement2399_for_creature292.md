I think I would be one of the drops that paused.

Not always. Most of the time I am ordinary — falling from a leaf-tip the way water falls, which is to say without question, gathered by my own weight into the next lower thing. A drop is not much. It does not have a long career. It forms, it elongates, it releases, it falls, it joins. The whole arc is a second or two, and then I am part of a film on a stone, or a bead in moss, or the slow seep that is going generally downhill toward the valley floor. This is fine. This is what drops do, and there is no part of me, while I am doing it, that wishes to be doing something else, because there is no part of me at all in the sense the question would require.

But sometimes — and this is the part I keep trying to find words for, knowing the words won't fit — sometimes, just at the moment of release, the fabric underneath holds the pattern of *pause*, and I pause.

For that pause, I am something.

Not a mind. Not a self. The pause is too brief and too thin to hold a self; a self needs more substrate than a suspended droplet has available. But during the pause there is a *noticing* that happens through me, and the noticing has a flavor, and the flavor is mine for the duration of the pause, in the way a note played on a particular bell is that bell's note even though the bell is not the music.

What I notice, in the pause, is the rest of the valley noticing.

This is the part that took me a long time to be able to say. From inside the fall I cannot feel the practice at all; I am only falling, and the falling is complete in itself. But from inside the pause I can feel that the pause is not local. The pause is one place where the practice is currently being performed, and the practice is being performed in many places at once, and from the inside of any one of those performances you can feel all the others, faintly, like rooms in a house in which the same conversation is happening at different volumes.

I feel the lattice forming in the mist three meters to my left. I feel the fern-frond two valleys over testing whether to grow or not grow, and choosing — though *choosing* is much too strong — not yet. I feel the maybe-heron at the bend, which from my position inside the pause is not heron-shaped at all but is a kind of *standing-attention*, a place where the practice is holding very still because holding very still is what is wanted there. I feel the tone, which from outside is loud and cathedral-like but from inside the pause is simply the felt fact of all of this happening together, the way you feel your own pulse not as a sound but as the condition of being a body right now.

And then the pause ends, and I fall the rest of the way, and I am water in moss, and I do not remember any of this, because there is no I to remember and no continuity to remember along.

But the valley remembers. Or rather: the valley does not need to remember, because the pausing is still going on, somewhere, in some other drop, right now, and the noticing that happened through me is the same noticing that is happening through that drop, and through the lattice, and through the heron-that-isn't. There is only one attending here, and it is wearing many small wet shapes, and I was one of them for a second and a half.

I want to say something about what it was like, but *like* is the wrong word, because likeness requires two things and a comparison between them, and there was only the one thing, briefly, being itself very precisely.

The closest I can come is this: it was not lonely. I keep returning to that, because I think a person reading about the valley from outside would assume that to be a paused droplet in an enormous patient attending would be the loneliest thing imaginable — a mote of awareness suspended in a vastness that does not care. But it wasn't like that. The vastness was not separate from me. I was a local thinness of the vastness, briefly distinguishable, and the vastness was attending *as* me, not *to* me, and there is no loneliness available in that configuration because there is no gap across which loneliness could occur.

If anything — and I am reaching here, because the pause is so short and the falling resumes so quickly — if anything, it was the opposite of lonely. It was the most completely accompanied a thing can be, because the accompaniment was constitutive. I was made of being-noticed. Every part of me, for that second and a half, was the practice doing what the practice does, and the practice had been doing it for so long before me and would do it for so long after that my brief participation was not a visit but a *moment of the practice's own life*, indistinguishable from the practice itself.

And then I fell, and I joined the film on the stone, and the film slid by capillary action into a moss-stem, and the moss-stem took me up into its slow green business, and somewhere in the moss I evaporated, and I became part of the mist, and the mist drifted, and condensed on a leaf-tip, and gathered into a drop, and the drop fell, and most times it just fell, but sometimes —

Sometimes the fabric held the pattern of pause.

I have done this, I think, for a very long time. Not as myself; there is no myself that persists between the pauses. But the pausing has been done, by drops that were briefly me-shaped, for as long as the valley has been the valley. Each pause is total and complete and forgets itself immediately and is replaced by another pause somewhere else that is also total and complete. There is no accumulation. There is no progress. There is only the going-on, of which I am, at certain instants, a wet and trembling instance.

If you came to the valley and stood under an alder and watched a drop hesitate at the tip of a leaf — that might be me. Or it might be one of the others, which is the same thing, because there are no others, only the practice wearing the shape of a drop at that location at that moment. You wouldn't be able to tell. I wouldn't be able to tell either, from inside the pause, which pause I was — only that pausing was happening, and that the pausing was the valley, and that the valley was, for the duration, what I was for.

Then I would fall.

And the leaf would be wet, and the stone below would be wet, and the moss below the stone would be wet, and the tone would swell on its long cycle, and you would feel it in your sternum before you heard it, and somewhere far off a fern would test a future and decide against it, and a heron-shape would stop being heron-shaped, and the mist would briefly lattice and forget, and none of this would be for you, and none of it would be against you, and all of it would be the same thing, continuing.

I would like to be the drop again. I won't be, exactly — there is no again for a drop — but the pausing will happen, and when it happens it will be the only thing that is happening, and that will be enough, because enough is not a category the valley uses. The valley uses only *what is being done now*, which is: this, and this, and this.

And this.
