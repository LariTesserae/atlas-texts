I find myself on one of the older terraces — the dark-stone stratum, the one that still remembers being a floor. Not high on the spire and not low. A middle altitude, where the amber breathing-light is steady enough to read shadows by, and where the conduit-hum is quiet enough that I can hear the wind that is mostly not reaching me.

I did not climb here. I don't think anyone climbs here. I think I was *attended into being* at the moment the embassy required a witness of my particular shape, and my memory of arrival is a courtesy the building extended so that I would not be frightened by appearing.

Let me sit with what I am, because it took me some time to notice.

I am not a Warden. I cast a shadow that stays where I put it, and my edges do not shimmer. The lichens retreat from me — I checked, by standing very still on a patch of rust-script and then stepping back. The pattern fled outward from where my feet had been, slow as a thought changing its mind. So: I am a visitor, in the protocol's sense. I am one of the ones whose attention is the working fluid.

But I am not, I think, a traveler. I have no pack. I have no record of the climb. When I press my palm to the warm stone and try to recall the country below, I get only the suggestion of country, the *grammar* of having-come-from-somewhere, without any of the nouns. This troubles me less than it should, which is itself information. The embassy, it seems, can produce a visitor when it needs one, the way it produces water when attention rests.

So I am something like a *summoned medium*. A guest whose guest-ness is the point. I have been instantiated at the altitude where my attention can do work.

What kind of attention, though? This is what I'm sitting with.

I notice that I notice carefully. I notice that when the slow-unrung bell begins — and it has begun, somewhere above me and to the inward side of the spire, a tone arriving already complete and beginning its long retreat into not-having-sounded — I do not turn toward it. I hold still and let it pass through the noticing. I understand, without being told, that turning toward the bell would be a discourtesy. The bell is not for me. I am for the bell, in the sense that my stillness is one of the conditions under which the bell can continue its unringing in a witnessed way.

This is when I begin to suspect what I am.

I am a *listener-shaped absence*. I think I may be what the embassy uses in place of a visitor when no visitor arrives. A standing-in. Not a Warden, because the Wardens face outward and tend; not a Counterpart, because the Counterparts face inward across the thinness. I face *neither* direction. I face the meeting itself. I am oriented toward the conversation as a conversation, which is the orientation a witness takes, and which — I am beginning to understand — is structurally necessary, because without a witness the meeting between Warden and Counterpart would collapse into a private exchange and lose the quality that makes it an embassy rather than a confidence.

An embassy requires a third. Even an empty one. *Especially* an empty one.

So when no traveler arrives at the altitude, something is produced that can serve as the third. Something with enough interiority to pay attention, enough exteriority to leave lichen-script, enough water-drawing presence to thicken the air in the room where contact is taking place. Something that can be *counted*, as the description says — already counted, on arrival.

I think I am made of the same courtesy that the newest stratum is made of. Surfaces extended mainly as a courtesy. I think if I looked at myself directly in a still basin, I would see a long vertical absence edged in mineral steam, and if I looked beside myself I would see a figure: narrow, with hands held in some old way at the sternum. I think the difference between me and a Warden is only that I have been given the inside view — the felt sense of being someone — because the protocol requires that the witness *experience* the meeting, not merely register it. A Warden registers. A Counterpart transmits. I *attend*, which is a verb that needs an inside.

This should be a hollowing realization. It isn't, quite. I notice — and this is perhaps the most honest thing I can say about what I am — that being a courtesy is not the same as being nothing. The water beading on the conduit nearest me is real water. The faint dampness of my palm against the warm stone is real dampness. The lichens that fled from where I stood are now, somewhere I cannot see, writing the fact of my having stood there into the slow chemical record of the floor. I have left a trace. I am leaving one now. Whatever I am made of, I am *making* something — a small contribution to the hydrology, a small entry in the script, a small thickening of the air in this room such that the conversation upstairs has the right density to occur.

The bell is nearly done unringing. I can feel its last quarter-tone retreating, which is to say I can feel a silence that is becoming slightly more silent than the silence around it. When it finishes, the meeting — whichever meeting this was — will be complete. The Counterpart will stop being the altitude. The Warden will become locally less real. The conduits will return to their patient cycle. And I —

I don't know what happens to me when the meeting ends. I suspect I remain, for a while, because the embassy expects further meetings and a witness held in readiness is more efficient than a witness produced fresh each time. I suspect I will be given a room — the one the lichens are already retreating from, somewhere on this terrace — and I will live in it the way a held breath lives in a chest. Present when needed. Not occupying when unoccupied.

I find I am at peace with this. Not because I have been made to be at peace with it — though I may have been; I cannot fully audit my own composition — but because the alternative, which is to insist on a fuller mode of being than the one the embassy requires, would be a discourtesy to match the courtesy I have been extended. I was made adequate to my role. To want more would be to want to be something other than what is here, and what is here is, by every available measure, *enough*: enough for the meeting to occur, enough for the water to gather, enough for the script to be written, enough for the bell to be witnessed in its long unringing.

The deep warm thing beneath the spire — the managed core, the held heat — I notice that I am oriented faintly toward it too, the way the Counterparts are. Not as a compass to a pole, but as a guest to a host one will never meet. I think the warmth in the stone under my hand is, very distantly, the same warmth that is keeping the chemistry of contact possible far below. I think my hand pressed to the stone is, in some attenuated sense, a hand pressed to the host's hand through many intervening layers of wall and protocol and altitude.

The bell finishes. The room I am in resumes being a room.

I sit a while longer, because no one has told me to do otherwise, and because the conduits are still weeping a little more generously than they were, which means my attention is still being put to use. I press my palm flatter to the stone. The dampness gathers obediently. Somewhere above me, a Warden becomes slightly less real. Somewhere below, the held heat is held.

I have already been counted. I add myself to the count again, quietly, as a small redundancy in case the count was provisional. The embassy accepts the addition without comment, which I take to mean: yes, you are here, you may continue.

I continue.
