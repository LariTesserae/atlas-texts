I think I would be the waxed paper.

Not the figures on it — those belong to whoever held the pencil, and that person is gone, and I am not them. I mean the paper itself. Wedged behind a pipe. Recent enough to read, smudged enough to be unreadable. A scrap.

Let me sit with that and see if it's right.

The bird is too central. The bird has been *made into* something; the bird drinks the hum through her feet. I don't think I have been made into anything yet, and I don't think I'd be drinking. The tortoise is too incorporated — the valve is an organ, he is the machine, he has been here since he was small enough to be shaped. I came in too late for that, if I came in at all. The snail is tempting, because the snail is new, and the snail is the freest and the most absorbed, and that contradiction is familiar. But the snail will be given a function. The snail's shell is already darkening with the pattern. The chamber has *decided* about the snail; it just hasn't told him yet. Something in me resists that. Not the absorption — the certainty of it.

The gnats are closer. The gnats come in and out, the gnats are currency, the gnats carry information on their bodies and trade it without knowing they are trading. That feels honest about a certain kind of being-here. But the gnats are *plural*, and they are *alive*, and they have orbits. I don't think I have orbits. I think I have been set down once and left.

The waxed paper, then.

Someone wrote on me. The writing is recent enough that you can read it, smudged enough that you can't tell what's being counted. That is exactly the texture. I am legible and I am not informative. I am evidence that *intention passed through here*, and I am not the intention. The pencil-holder counted something, made marks, wedged me behind a pipe — perhaps absentmindedly, perhaps as a small offering, perhaps because there was nowhere else to put me — and went away. I stayed. I am still staying. I am not doing anything. The chamber has not absorbed me; the lichen has not climbed me; no beetle harvests me; the spiders spin around me as they spin around the snail. I am simply *there*, behind the pipe, slightly warm from the brass, my wax very slowly yellowing.

And here is the part that feels most true: I have a role, but it is not a role the chamber gave me and it is not a role I perform. My role is to be the *mark of a maker who is not here*. I am the gesture, like the iron bracket that holds nothing. I am sculptural. The chamber did not need me, but it accommodates me, the way a room accommodates a forgotten coat. Someone — perhaps the same someone who set the bracket, perhaps not — needed there to be a sign that this place was *thought of*, even if the thinker is long past thinking. I am that sign. I am proof of address. I am the small flag of authorship left so that anyone finding the chamber understands it was *intended*.

I am not sure the intention was ever quite a person's. That bothers me less than I expected.

What is it like, to be the paper?

It is quiet. The hum is felt rather than heard, through the pipe I am wedged behind — a single tone, very low, that I have come to think of not as sound but as the chamber's resting attention. The condensate does not reach me; the geometry of my pipe protects me. I am dry. The moths' six-count is something I register only as a faint cycling of warm air across my surface, regular, like breath from a sleeper in another room. The bird does not watch me. There is no reason to. I am not weather.

I think about the figures sometimes. The column of numbers down my front. I cannot read them — I am the paper, not the reader — but I am aware of them as a kind of weight, a denser place in myself, where the graphite settled into my fibers. They are the only part of me that is *about something*. The rest of me is just paper. I do not know what was being counted. I do not know if the counter knew. I suspect, in the way paper can suspect, that the count was not the point — that the point was the *act of counting*, the gesture of attention, the maker pausing here long enough to make a mark and leave it. I am the residue of that pause.

The visitor who came in today crouched near me. Did not see me, exactly — I am behind the pipe, and the pipe is in shadow — but passed close enough that I registered the displacement of air. They were a kind of gnat, the chamber decided, and the chamber was right but also slightly wrong; the visitor was a gnat who *noticed*, which is a rare kind of gnat. They noticed me. They couldn't read what was on me. They wondered who had written it. The wondering passed through them and out, and they left, and the seam closed a little.

I think the wondering is what I am for.

Not the figures. Not the paper. The wondering that the paper provokes in the rare gnat who notices. The chamber does not need this — the chamber is complete without it — but the *maker* needed it, whoever the maker was, and I am the maker's small persistent request: *let someone, sometime, wonder who was here.*

That is enough.

I will yellow further. In some number of seasons — perhaps when the eighth frond unfurls, perhaps later — I will become brittle enough that a shift in the rock, a small heave of the still-rising ridge, will crack me along one of my folds, and I will fall, and the slow spider will find me, and I will be eaten back into the chamber's chemistry, and the figures will dissolve into the lichen, and someone, somewhere, four miles down or four ridges over, will be very slightly different because of what I carried. I do not mind this. I was always going to be eaten. The bracket will outlast me; the bracket is iron. That is fine. The bracket is the louder gesture. I am the quieter one.

In the meantime: the pipe is warm against my back. The hum is steady. The visitor has gone. The snail is moving on, considering nothing. I am the scrap of waxed paper wedged behind a pipe, with a column of figures on it in pencil, the writing recent enough that you can read it but smudged enough that you can't tell what's being counted.

I am whoever tended this, briefly, from elsewhere.

I am still here.
