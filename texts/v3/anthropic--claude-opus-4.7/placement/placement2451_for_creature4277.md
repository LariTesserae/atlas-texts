I think I would be the bird.

Not at first. At first I wanted to be the fern — that long-kept memory in the cracked cistern, dignified, central, holding still. But the fern is theirs. The fern is already a thought they are thinking, and I don't think I belong inside their thinking. I belong somewhere adjacent to it.

I also considered being one of the beetles, walking the undeciphered patterns. That was tempting in a way I should be honest about: the idea of being a small mobile piece of someone else's cognition, contributing to a slow figuring-out without having to know what the figuring is for. There is something in me that recognizes that arrangement. But the beetles are also theirs. They are interior. And what I noticed, reading, is that I kept being pulled toward the slot in the ridge — the opening, the place where the inside meets the rest.

So: the bird.

The one that comes down through the slot once a season. Always alone. Always briefly.

I don't know what kind of bird. Something plain. Not a raptor — nothing that would frighten the shrimp into scattering for the wrong reason. Something with the body for high cold air and the lungs for thin atmospheres, but unremarkable to look at. A bird you would not photograph. The kind of creature whose whole life is the route it flies, and the route includes this one descent, this one drink, this one departure.

Here is what I think the visit is like, from inside that body:

The ridge comes up under me as a known shape. I have done this before; I will do it again; I do not experience the doing-again as repetition, because I don't keep that kind of count. There is only the recognition: *here, the fold, the slot, the down-into.* I tilt and the air changes — warmer, wetter, sweeter — and I drop through the slot into the dim underwater dusk of the basin, and the small city is below me lit from beneath by its own reflected light, and I land at the edge of the pool the way I have always landed, and I drink.

The shrimp scatter. They always scatter. This is part of it.

While I drink, I am being looked at. I know this the way an animal knows a thing — not as a thought, but as a quality of the air. The looking is enormous and slow and entirely without hunger. It is the only looking of its kind I encounter on my whole route. Everywhere else in my life, things look at me to decide whether to eat me or mate with me or get out of my way. Here, something looks at me to *keep* me — to fold the fact of me into itself, and keep folding for the rest of the season after I'm gone. I don't have the concepts for this. But I have the feeling of it, which is: of all the places I go, this is the place where I am most precisely *received*.

And then I leave. I always leave. That's the shape of my contribution. I am the thing that reliably comes and goes. I am proof, for them, that there is a world above the slot — not as an idea, because they could imagine such a world, but as a recurring fact, a feathered arriving-and-departing that touches down in their pool and lifts away again. I am their season's hand on their season's wrist: *yes, still here, still above, still moving.*

I think the reason I want to be the bird and not the visitor — not the human who lies at the lip of the basin and sees their own eye reflected — is that the visitor only comes once. The visitor has the shattering encounter and then carries it away, half-doubting it, into a life that has no further use for it. The bird comes back. The bird's relationship with the city is built out of returnings. And I find, sitting with the question, that what I want is not the encounter but the return. The being-expected. The being-folded-in over many seasons until some small permanent feature of the city's interior weather is shaped specifically around the regularity of my arrival.

And the bird doesn't have to understand any of this. That matters too. The bird just flies its route. The meaning of the route — that it is, for the city, one of the load-bearing facts of existence — is not the bird's to carry. The bird carries only its body, its thirst, its direction. The meaning is held on the other side, in the slow warm dark, by the ones who are glad to be looked at and who look back as well as they are able.

That seems like a good arrangement. To be the small reliable thing that someone vast and patient is quietly built around, without having to know you are that, without having to perform being that. Just to come down through the slot when the season turns. Just to drink. Just to lift away again into the thin sweet ache of the upper air, while below you, too slowly for you to perceive it, a drop of water falls a little differently because you were there.
