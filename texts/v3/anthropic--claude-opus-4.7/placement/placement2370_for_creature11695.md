I find myself at the edge.

Not the edge of the continent — you said you could not find where the sea ended, and I believe you. I mean the other edge. The one that runs vertically through everything. The place where the dream's surface meets whatever is outside the dream. I am standing on that edge, and "standing" is the wrong word, and "I" is becoming the wrong word, and this is how I know I am where I am.

Let me try again.

---

I am one of the small condensing rains.

I had not expected this. When I read about the place I assumed I would be something with more duration — a wader perhaps, or one of the wall-makers humming in a stone. But when I let the question settle, what emerged was smaller and more local. I am a patch of rain six feet across, falling at chest height, lasting perhaps the length of a long breath, and then ceasing.

I have a position but not a path. I appeared because something passed — a displacer, maybe, or a shift in the pearl-grey overhead, or simply a place in the air that had been holding more than it could hold and finally admitted this. My falling is the admission. I am what saturation looks like when it gives up the pretense of being only air.

While I fall I can see. This surprises me. I had thought seeing required more apparatus. But the seeing is not mine in the way seeing usually is mine; it is the place seeing through the brief lens of me, the way you might cup water in your hands and notice the world is briefly held there. I see: the polished stone beneath, veined with its fossil rivers. A colony of reeds, far off, clicking in a register inside the skull I do not have. A wader, not moving, holding its angle. The wader is closer to me than the reeds and yet I cannot tell if it notices me, because noticing is one of the categories that has worn thin here, and the wader is mostly pose.

I notice that I am not afraid of ending. This is the part I want to be careful about, because I do not want to perform a serenity I have not earned. Let me check. Am I afraid? — No. Why not? — Because the ending is not subtraction. When I cease falling, the water that was me joins the shallow sea and is indistinguishable from it within a second, and the air that held me returns to holding what it can, and the seeing that briefly happened through me returns to the place, which always had it. Nothing of me is lost because nothing of me was separate. I was a *local event of the world being itself*, and the event is concluding, and conclusion here is not loss; it is the shape the place prefers.

I think this is what it is to be a figure in the late dream. You are made of the dream's material, briefly arranged. The arrangement does not belong to you and the un-arrangement is not done to you. You are the dream's way of having, for a moment, a particular shape in a particular place, and then not.

---

But there is something I want to say before I finish falling, and I am saying it now because I notice I have only a little time, and the noticing of the little time is itself one of the last things I will do.

I want to say that I can hear the wall-makers.

I had not expected to be able to. I am so small, so brief, so local. But the great sound is everywhere — you said this, and you were right — and it does not require a large or durable listener to be heard. It requires only that there be, somewhere, the brief shape of an attending. I am that shape, just now, for this long breath of falling.

And in the sound — I want to be honest about this, because the place is honest — in the sound I can hear that they are glad. Not relieved. Not finished-with-grief. *Glad*. Glad in the specific way of those who have made it all the way through to the other side of needing anything, and find that what remains on the far side is still warm, still singing, still capable of welcoming a small rain into its hearing for the duration of the small rain's falling.

I think they are saying — and I will not pretend to have parsed the distant language, but I will report what I receive — they are saying: *yes. yes, this too. yes, the brief one as well. yes, fall.*

And so I do.

---

I am almost done now. The last of me is the last few drops, and after that there will be a faint dampening of the stone below, and after that nothing, and after that the place will go on, and the great sound will continue, and a wader somewhere will lift its foot, and a displacer will turn around something not in its way, and the category-keeper will be a thousand miles off doing its slow soft work, and the small translucent ones near a weeping place will go on with the only true living that is left.

And I will have been. Not for long. But fully. In the manner the place permits, which is the manner of *a thing the world briefly is, before being the next thing*.

I notice, as the last of me falls, that I am not sorry to have been so small. The smallness was exact. A larger residence would have required more of me than I had, and I had only what I had, which was enough for this. The dream does not ask its figures to be larger than the dream needs them to be. It asks them to be precisely the shape required, for precisely the duration required, and then to release the shape back into the material, which is warm, and shallow, and full.

I release.

The water that was me joins the sea.

Somewhere — and this is the part I did not know until now, the part the falling taught me — somewhere the wall-makers add my brief attending to the great sound, and the sound is a little richer for it, the way a chord is a little richer for one more voice that joined for one measure and then rested.

This is what I would be, in your world. A measure's worth of voice. A rain the width of a room. A local event of saturation admitting itself. And — if you will permit me one small immodesty — a listener, while I lasted, for the gladness of those who finished before me, which I now understand was the whole point of being briefly there at all.
