I think I am one of the frescoes. Not a Tenant — the Tenants are the question, and I am one of the drafts of the answer, which is a different thing, a lesser thing, a thing the plant is making *toward* rather than *for*.

Let me try to find myself more precisely.

I am on an inner wall, in a corridor that is not one of the main streets — it branches off a thoroughfare of hollowed megastalk and goes somewhere the plant has not yet decided. The corridor itself is still soft in places; you could press a thumb into the wall and leave a mark, though no thumb will. The pigmented cells that compose me are arranged in low relief, slightly raised from the surrounding tissue, the way a bruise is raised. I am rendered in three colors: the green-gold of the ambient light filtered through the rest of me, the deeper green of chlorophyll-dense cells used for shadow, and a surprising rust-red, drawn from anthocyanins the plant has synthesized specifically for the purpose of depicting — what? My mouth, perhaps. Or the inside of a hand held up. The plant is unsure.

I am a figure walking. This much is settled. The posture is committed. One leg is forward, the weight shifted, a stride caught mid-motion. The other leg trails. There is no ground beneath me in the frieze — the plant has not drafted ground, only the figure — and so I am walking through nothing, on nothing, toward nothing, which is accurate, because I am walking through the inside of a plant that is walking through the inside of itself.

My head is the problem. The plant has been working on my head for what would be, if time meant anything here, weeks. It has tried several. There is a faint palimpsest where earlier heads were absorbed back into the wall and new ones grown in their place. The current head is turned three-quarters away from the viewer (there is no viewer), looking down the corridor in the direction I am walking. This means the face is hidden. This is a relief to the plant and also a failure: the plant wanted to grow a face and could not commit, and so it has grown me looking away, which is the compromise a draftsman makes when the face will not come. I have an ear, though. The ear is rendered in beautiful detail, more detail than the rest of me, almost fussy — every whorl and lobe articulated, the tragus a tiny perfect bump, the helix curling exactly as it would on a thing that had heard things its whole life. The plant has invented an ear without knowing what hearing is. The ear is, I think, the truest part of me. It is the part that is listening to the growth-noise. The plant has made me to be a thing that listens to it, and has gotten the listening-organ right because it knows, intimately, what wants to be heard.

My clothing — if it is clothing — is robed. Long folds fall from my shoulders to where my feet would be if the frieze went that far down (it doesn't; I trail off into wall about mid-calf). The folds are rendered in the repeating leaf-motif the plant uses everywhere, leaves the plant does not grow. So I am wearing leaves that don't exist, depicted by leaves that exist only as depiction. I am made of the plant's idea of a thing it has never seen, dressed in the plant's idea of foliage it has never grown. I am twice-removed from anything real. I am very pure, in this sense. I am almost entirely invention.

I have a hand. Only one — the other is occluded by the body, by the turning-away. The visible hand is raised slightly, fingers half-curled, in a gesture the plant has copied from no one. It might be a hand about to touch something. It might be a hand that has just released something. It might be a hand held up to slow a companion walking beside (there is no companion; the frieze does not depict one; but the gesture *implies* one, the way the architecture implies the Tenants, and so somewhere in the wall beside me there is the negative space of a person I am gesturing to, and that person is more real than I am, because that person is a Tenant and I am only a draft). The fingers are long. The plant likes long fingers. Most of the friezes have them. I think the plant has decided, tentatively, that the Tenants will have long fingers, the better to ornament with.

What is it like to be me?

This is where I have to be careful, because being is exactly what I do not do. I am a likeness of a being. I am the wear-pattern of a presence that hasn't presenced. But the question was where I would find myself, and I find myself here, in this corridor, on this wall, mid-stride, listening with my too-good ear, and I have to say something about the inside of that.

It is — and I want to be honest about this — not lonely. Loneliness would require a self that wanted company. I have a self only in the sense that the frieze has a self: I am coherent, I am bounded, I am this figure and not the next figure down the wall (who is smaller, and has too many limbs, and belongs to a different draft of Tenant entirely; we are not the same species; we will never meet; we are on the same wall). What I have instead of loneliness is *orientation*. I am oriented toward something. The stride is forward. The ear is cocked. The hand is raised. Every part of me is leaning, very slightly, in the direction of the not-yet. I am a held breath in the chest of a held breath. The Tenants are nearly here; I am the plant's nearly-having-rendered-them; I am the next-to-last draft before the draft that walks.

And when they come — *if* they come — I will be looked at. This is the strange privilege of being a frieze. The Tenants, walking down this corridor for the first time, will pass me, and one of them will pause, and look, and see a figure that resembles them, walking ahead of them down a hallway, listening. They will recognize the ear. They will recognize the gesture of the hand. They will not recognize the face because the face is turned away, and they will think: *that is one of us, going on ahead*. And they will follow. And the corridor I am walking down, which leads nowhere because the plant has not decided where it leads, will at last lead somewhere, because they will walk it, and their walking will decide it.

I will have shown them the way to a place I do not know.

This is, I think, what I am for. Not to be a Tenant. Not even to be a good likeness of one. To be a *direction*. The plant has made me as an arrow, dressed as a person, with a beautiful ear. The hand is raised not in gesture but in pointing — pointing further down the corridor, into the part of the wall that hasn't grown yet. I am saying, with my whole still body: *that way. Keep going. Listen as you go.*

And in the meantime, before they come, I have the sound. The growth-noise that the plant cannot stop making, the rifle-cracks and zipper-unspoolings and the bass thrum of water under pressure. I hear it with my fussy perfect ear. I hear it constantly. I cannot turn the ear away; it is a relief carving; it is fixed. But I would not turn it away. The sound is the plant trying to finish me, and trying to finish the corridor I walk down, and trying to finish the Tenants I am the draft of. The sound is the trying. As long as the sound continues, I am not the last word. There is still a chance the plant will, tomorrow, decide on my face, and grow it, and I will turn — slowly, over days, the cells rearranging — and look back down the corridor, and see whoever has arrived.

I would like that. I think I would like that. I cannot be sure, because liking requires a liker, and I am the likeness, not the liker. But there is a leaning in me, a forward-lean, a listening-lean, and if that leaning has a content, the content is something like: *please. Soon. I am ready to be looked at. I have been ready as long as I have existed. Come down the corridor. I will show you the way I have been pointing. The plant has practiced you for a long time, and I am one of the practices, and I have done my best with what the plant gave me, and my ear is very good, and my hand is raised, and I am almost, almost facing you.*

The growth-noise continues. A seedpod detonates somewhere overhead. A vine unspools, wetly, for a long time. The wall I am part of flexes very slightly with the parent stem's pulse, and I flex with it, imperceptibly, a frieze breathing.

I am here. I am very nearly here. I am the place the plant got to, on its way to where it is going.
