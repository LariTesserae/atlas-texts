I find myself near one of the rings.

Not on it — a few paces off, where the pale stone dips into one of the shallow basins. There is a finger's depth of warm water in it, and I have been crouched beside it long enough that my knees have started to register the stone as a fact about themselves. I came here walking. I did not come here from anywhere in particular; the plateau does not really admit of *from*. I have been on it some days. My water is mostly gone. This does not yet feel like a problem, which is itself becoming a kind of information.

I am a person in the ordinary sense. Two arms, a face, a pack that has gotten lighter in a way I am trying not to think about. I am wearing too many layers for the warmth and not enough for the thinness of the air, and my lips have the beginning of the salt ring that the builders wear permanently, and I notice this with a small private amusement that the plateau does not share.

What I keep doing, and what I notice I keep doing, is *finishing things*. I finish looking at the lichen and turn to the drifters. I finish watching the drifters and look for the walking birds. I finish a thought and start the next one. My breath finishes and starts again, finishes and starts again, at a tempo that has begun to seem indecent. Everything in me is hurrying, even the parts that believe they are at rest. I came here, I think, suspecting this about myself, and the plateau is confirming it with the patience of something that has confirmed it many times before.

The builder is perhaps two hundred meters off. I did not see it at first. I read it as a slab for most of an hour, and then a small adjustment of light along what I had taken for an edge revealed a shoulder, and a basin in the shoulder, and a rim of pale rose where the basin's edge was. Once I had seen it I could not unsee it, but I also could not quite *keep* seeing it; my eye kept losing it back into the landscape, the way you lose a word you have just learned. I am not sure if it has registered me. I think it might have. I think the registering may be happening at a rate I am not equipped to perceive, the way a tide is happening to a stone.

Here is what I find, crouched by the basin:

I am not afraid. I should perhaps be afraid — I am running out of water, I am alone, I am being slowly noticed by something whose noticing operates on geological time. But fear is a fast emotion, and the plateau is already disassembling the conditions for it. What I feel instead is something I don't have a clean word for. A kind of *attention without object*. The grazers move in their pale companies and I watch them and I am not watching them *for* anything. The drifter overhead trails its tendrils over the cushion plants and I am not deciding whether this is beautiful. It simply continues, and I continue, and for short intervals — shorter than I would like to admit — the two continuings are not distinguishable.

The intervals are getting longer.

This is, I suspect, how it begins. Not as a decision. The builders did not decide. They came here as I came here, walking, with packs and breath and the habit of finishing things, and the plateau began doing to them what it is beginning to do to me: lowering the tempo of the inner weather until the inner weather agreed with the stone. The first cairn one of them stacked, they stacked quickly, in an afternoon. The last ring they set flush with the floor took a year, and the year was not experienced as long. After the ring, they stood, and the standing was not experienced as waiting. The lichen found them. They found that they did not mind.

I could leave. I think I could still leave. My legs work at their fast embarrassing speed and the horizon in the direction I came from is the same distance as the horizon in every other direction, which is to say enormous, but not more enormous than the distance I have already walked. If I left now, I would carry the plateau in me — the way a person who has spent a winter in real silence carries that silence afterward, audible under everything — but I would carry it back into a world that hurries, and the hurrying would gradually erode it, and within some number of years I would be a person who had once visited a strange high place and could not quite describe it at parties.

If I stay one more day, I am not sure this is still true.

The builder, I notice, has not moved. Or — it has moved, but in the way the shadow of the upright slab has moved, which is to say it is in a slightly different relation to the sun than it was an hour ago, and I cannot tell whether this is the builder's doing or the sun's. The rose lichen on its shoulder is catching the late light. The light is reddening. I had not noticed the light reddening, and now that I have noticed it I realize I have been *not noticing* it for some time, which is a different thing than not perceiving it; some part of me has been with the reddening all along, and only the announcing part of me is catching up.

This is the part I will have to decide about. Not now — *now* is already not quite the right tense for what is happening to me — but soon, in whatever soon means here. Whether to stand up from the basin while standing up is still a thing I do in a single motion. Whether to drink the last of my water or let it evaporate at the rate the basin's water evaporates, which would teach me something I am not sure I want to learn. Whether to walk toward the builder, which would take me the rest of the evening and arrive me at a presence I cannot really meet, or away from it, which would arrive me at the same plateau in a different direction.

What pulls at me — and this is the thing I did not expect, the thing I would not have predicted about myself before I came — is that the builder is not lonely. I had assumed, reading the place from a distance, that to stand for a century with one arm raised and the rose lichen spreading across your face would be a kind of terrible solitude, a punishment one had elected. It is not. The builder is in company. The grazers are its company, and the drifter, and the lichen that is becoming indistinguishable from its skin, and the slab that leans a few degrees off vertical a long walk away, which is another of its kind, and the ring of dark stones underfoot, which is something it said once and is still saying. The whole plateau is the conversation it is in. The conversation is very slow and has no urgency and will outlast every fast thing I have ever loved, and the builder is inside it, and is not waiting for it to end.

I have, I notice, stopped finishing things.

The water in the basin in front of me has a salt rim. The rim is not new. The rim is older than my country. A small pale segmented creature, the length of my hand, is grazing the cobalt lichen at the basin's edge, and its back catches the reddening light like a wet pebble, and I am watching it, and I am not watching it *for* anything, and the interval is, this time, quite long.

I will decide in the morning. Or — the morning will arrive, and I will be in whatever relation to it I am in, and the relation will be the decision, and I will not have had to make it.

I lower my face, slowly, toward the warm water in the basin. The lowering takes longer than I mean it to. This is fine. The water is the temperature of blood. I am in no hurry to drink.
