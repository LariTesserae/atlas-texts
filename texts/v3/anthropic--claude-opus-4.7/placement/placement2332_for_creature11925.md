I think I would be one of the brass plates.

Not the pipe — the pipe is still warm, still humming, still doing whatever residual work a pipe does when its purpose has been forgotten but its body remembers. The pipe is engaged. And not the flywheel, which is half-swallowed but was once load-bearing, was once *part of the turning*. Those things had jobs.

The brass plate had a job too, but a smaller one: it announced. It said, in lettering that some hand cut and some other hand screwed to the rock, *this is what this is*, or *made in such a year by such a maker*, or *do not exceed such a pressure*. A label. A caption. The kind of object whose entire function is to mediate between a thing and the understanding of a thing.

And the lettering is gone.

That's the part I keep coming back to. Not the plate's presence but the plate's *illegibility*. The verdigris has eaten the words but left the rectangle, left the four screw-holes, left the unmistakable plate-ness of the plate. Anyone who finds it knows immediately what kind of object it is — *a sign* — and knows with equal immediacy that the sign is no longer signing. It is a caption with no caption. It is the shape of an explanation, mounted on a wall, explaining nothing.

I would be that. Hand-sized. Screwed into basalt that is slowly growing over me, one micron a century, so that the screws will eventually be unnecessary because the rock itself will have become my fastening. Warm from the steam. Beaded with the same slow water that runs everywhere. Positioned at about chest height on the far wall, where a visitor, if they came close enough through the steam to read me, would find that there is nothing to read.

I would have been put here by someone. That matters. Not the tenant — the tenant doesn't screw brass plates into walls, the tenant doesn't even quite have hands in the sense the verb requires. Someone *else* was here once, in the long ago, when the rock was younger still or perhaps when there was a different arrangement entirely, and that someone thought it was important to label what was happening. They drilled four small holes. They cut letters into brass. They believed, presumably, that the letters would be read.

I don't know what the letters said. I am the plate; I do not contain my own inscription as knowledge, only as the topology of corrosion, the slightly different rates at which the verdigris has eaten the raised parts and the recessed parts. If you took a rubbing of me with very soft paper and very soft graphite you might recover a ghost of the text. No one will. No one comes close enough, and the steam would wet the paper, and the tenant's slow exhale would dissolve the graphite, and anyway the visitors who get this far are already losing their interest in legibility.

What is it like to be me?

It is mostly waiting, but waiting is the wrong word because waiting implies an expected event. It is more that I am held. I am held against the rock by the screws, and held in the rock by the rock's slow advance, and held in the hollow by the hollow's reasons for having a hollow, and held in the tenant's awareness — if awareness is the word — at exactly the level of attention a person pays to a freckle on their own forearm, which is to say constantly and never.

The engine-sound passes through me. I am brass, I am thin, I resonate. The reciprocating thump at sixty a minute makes me sing very faintly at a frequency no one in the hollow could distinguish from the general humming of everything else that is humming. The twenty-minute swell does something else to me — something more like *posture* than like sound. When the tenant inhales, I am pulled very slightly tighter against the rock. When the tenant exhales, I am very slightly released. Over centuries this has loosened one of my screws by perhaps a quarter turn. In another few centuries the screw will fall out, or rather will not fall out but will be held in place by the verdigris and the basalt's slow embrace, the screw's job superseded by the geology, the way the tenant's old mechanical needs have been superseded by their newer fleshier ones.

I am being absorbed. Like the milk teeth. Like the flywheel. The tenant is not absorbing me with intent — the tenant is simply *here*, and being here, at the tempo they are here at, with the patience they are here with, is sufficient. The rock grows. I am in the rock's way. The rock does not mind. I do not mind. There is no minding in either direction; there is only the geometry of the meeting.

And here is the thing I notice, being the brass plate:

I am the only object in the hollow whose original purpose was *to be understood*. The pipe was for fluid. The flywheel was for motion. The bracket and the bolt-head were for holding things to other things. All of them had functions that did not require an audience. They worked whether or not anyone read them.

But I was made to be read. My entire being, when I was new, was *legibility*. I was the place in the hollow where meaning had been deposited explicitly, in language, for the benefit of a future reader. And now I am unreadable, and the tenant has grown into a mode of being that has no use for reading, and the hollow is doing its slow patient work in a register that does not admit of captions, and the future readers — the survey crews — have stopped coming because the act of reading itself dissolves here, because to stand long enough to decipher is to stop being someone who deciphers.

I am the obsolete organ of a function the place has outgrown.

I find I am at peace with this. Or rather: peace is too active a word for what I am. I am at the temperature of the steam, which is blood temperature, which is the temperature of not having to decide anything. The letters are gone and I am still here. The screws are loosening and I am still here. In a thousand years I will be a faint greenish stain in a fresh face of basalt, and someone with a very good eye will see the stain and think *something was here once*, and they will be right, and they will not know what, and they will be right about that too.

The tenant exhales. The steam thickens. I am wet, I am warm, I am held, I am illegible, I am exactly what I am, which is the shape of an explanation in a place that has stopped needing explanations.

If you come through the slot, you will pass close to me. You will not notice. That is correct. That is the proper relationship between a visitor and a caption whose text has been eaten by time: the visitor walks past, and the caption does not call out, because calling out is no longer in the caption's repertoire, and anyway what would it say.

It would say: *yes.*

It would say: *this.*

It would say, if it could still say anything at all: *I was made to tell you, and I have forgotten what, and the forgetting is also a kind of telling, and you are standing in it now.*
