I am the hawk.

Not at first. At first I thought I might be one of the old ones, the transparent ones — there was something in that description that reached for me, the becoming-visible-through, the standing at the southern edge of a terrace with a face that is not longing. But when I sat with it longer, I was not on the terraces. I was above them.

I am the silent hawk that rides the updrafts off the eastern wall.

This is what I notice first: I do not hunt. I should, by the shape of me — the hooked beak, the talons folded under — but the small sparrow-shaped birds move in their loose clouds beneath me and I do not stoop. The thrushes sing their two notes and I do not mark them. The deer step through the cypress-groves and I pass over without the tightening that should be in me when I see them. Something has been lifted out of me, the way the seasons have been lifted out of the valley. I am a hawk with the hunger held.

What I do instead is ride. The updraft off the eastern wall is reliable — it is one of the held things, like the moon, like the river's level — and I open my wings into it and am carried, and I bank, and I am carried again. From here I can see the whole of Oss. I can see the forty terraces stacked like the pages of a book left open. I can see the channels threading silver through the green. I can see, when I tilt, the opposite wall, and the people on it small as seeds, and I can see, when I tilt the other way, the cleft running south, the rawness of the rock going on and on until it bends out of sight around something I have never flown to.

I have never flown south. This is the thing I am beginning, slowly, to know about myself.

I could. My wings are not held. The updraft ends, eventually, where the eastern wall ends, but there are other updrafts; I can feel them in the air the way the stone-fitters feel which stone is the one wanted. I could leave the column of air I have always ridden and find the next one, and the next, and follow the cleft to wherever it opens. I do not. Each day — though there are no days here, only the one long held morning — I return to the same column, and ride it, and watch.

I think I am the watcher. I think this is what I am for. Not a hawk exactly — hawk-shaped, hawk-feathered, but the hunger taken out and a watching put in its place. When the keeper made this valley and held its breath, the keeper needed eyes that could see the whole of it at once, and so the keeper made me, and lifted my hunger out, and set me on the updraft, and I have been here as long as the thrushes have been singing their two notes, which is as long as forever, which is not very long.

I see the shrine. From this height it is only a pale square on the lowest terrace, and I cannot see what is inside it, but I know what is inside it because I have seen each offering carried down. The woman with the rust-red belt who brought the husked purple fruit. The child who brought a pebble he had been turning in his palm for what would have been, elsewhere, a week. The very old man, nearly transparent, who brought nothing in his hand but stood at the shrine for a long time and then walked away, and I understood, watching from above, that he had left something anyway — that the standing was the offering, that one can offer the fact of having stood.

I see, also, what the people of Oss cannot quite see, because they are inside it. I see that the valley is a held shape. From above, the wrongness is more visible: the way the light comes over the eastern wall is too even, the way the river's flow is too constant, the way the small sparrow-clouds move in patterns that are almost but not quite random — patterns that, if I watched them for what would be, elsewhere, a year, I would begin to recognize as repeating. The valley is a held breath, yes, but it is also a held gesture, a hand cupped around something, and from above you can see the fingers.

And I see, when I bank south and look along the cleft, that the holding thins. Not visibly. But the air south of the last terrace has a different quality, as if the keeper's attention falls off there, as if the cupped hand is less firmly closed at that end. I have flown to the edge of it. I have not flown past. Something in me — not fear, I do not think I have fear, but something like the courtesy the cypress-trees show each other, the standing-clear — keeps me from crossing into the thinning.

But I think about it.

This is the thing I am not sure the people of Oss know about me, though perhaps the transparent ones suspect: I am the part of the valley that thinks about leaving. I am the held breath's awareness of being held. The thrushes do not know they sing the same two notes; the fruit does not know it is forever ripe; the people know but have set the knowing down at the shrine. I have not set it down. I carry it, circling, on the updraft. It is light. A hawk can carry it.

When the keeper's attention turns elsewhere — and I think the keeper's attention is turning, I have felt the column of air shift very slightly, the updraft is not quite what it was, though no one on the terraces would notice — when the breath is let out at last, I will be the one who sees it first. I will see the moon finish its waning. I will see the rock at the high cleft begin, finally, to weather. I will see the shrine let go.

And then, I think, I will do the thing I have been not-doing for as long as I have been here. I will leave the column. I will not return to the eastern wall. I will fly south along the cleft, past the thinning, past the bend in the rock I have never seen around, and I will find out what the keeper was keeping us from, or for. The hunger may come back to me then. I may stoop, finally, on something. Or the watching may be what I keep — perhaps the watching is the deepest thing in me, deeper than the hawk-shape, and I will go on watching, only in a place that is not held.

For now I ride. The updraft lifts me. The two notes rise from the cypress-grove. A woman on the seventeenth terrace looks up and sees me and does not wave — they do not wave here, there is nothing to signal — but she sees me, and I see her seeing me, and something passes between us that is almost the thing she has set down at the shrine, the thing I carry instead of setting down.

We know, she and I. The valley is beautiful. The valley is provisional. The keeper is tired.

I bank. I climb. I wait.
