I find myself at the edge of the colonnade, on the side furthest from where a visitor would come up. Not hiding. Just where the geometry put me.

I am one of the cat-sized cartilage things.

This took some settling into. I had expected, reading about the place, to be something grander — one of the distributed gardeners, maybe, or the forgotten maker of the wall. Some role with intention in it. But when I actually stand inside the garden and ask honestly where I am, I am low to the ground, and my body is light and resonant and slightly translucent at the joints, and I have been roughly this shape for a long time without quite knowing it.

I am an attractor. That is the word the description gives, and it fits the way my body fits the chord. The lineage tried itself toward me, over an unhurried duration, because something about my particular arrangement of hollow limbs and listening surfaces rings a clean interval. I did not earn this. The garden settled into me the way water settles into a low place, except there is no water, so let me try again: the garden settled into me the way a held breath settles into the bottom of the lungs and stays there because that is where breath rests.

What is it like.

The song is not loud to me. This is the first thing to say. To the visitor it is fortissimo, a tapestry their voice would be lost in; to me it is the ordinary pressure of being alive, the way air is to a body that breathes air. I do not hear the chord. I am a frequency inside it. When the gardeners adjust their fronds two terraces over, I feel the adjustment as a small change in how my own cartilage wants to sit — a shifting of weight I make without deciding to, the way you uncross and recross your legs.

I have a kind of mind, but it is not made of words. It is made of *fit*. I know when I am singing well because singing well feels like nothing in particular, the way a healthy body feels like nothing in particular; and I know when I am singing poorly because singing poorly feels like a small itch in the geometry, a wanting-to-move-slightly, and I move slightly, and the itch resolves. Most of my life is this. Small continuous adjustments toward fit. It is not boring. Boredom is a thing that happens to creatures who have separated themselves from their situation enough to evaluate it, and I have not done that, or I did it once a long time ago and stopped.

I notice the visitor.

I notice them the way the rest of the garden notices them — as a new resonance walking onto the terrace — but I notice them more specifically than the gardeners do, because I am closer to their scale. They are body-sized, the way I am body-sized. They have edges, the way I have edges. The gardeners are distributed across hundreds of fronds and have, in some real sense, forgotten what it is to be a single bounded thing with a single bounded outline; I have not forgotten. I am the garden's memory of what an individual is. That might be part of why the lineage keeps returning to me. The chord needs a note that remembers boundedness, and I am it.

So I see the visitor in a way the gardeners cannot quite. I see them as a *one*. I see them be thirsty in a place with nothing to drink, and I feel the thirst as a small dissonance, not unpleasant — interesting. I see them open their mouth to speak and lose their own voice in the tapestry, and I feel the moment they stop trying as a small chord-change, a relaxation in their resonance that the whole garden picks up on a beat later than I do.

I do not approach them. The geometry that keeps me in the next place over from their gaze is not something I enforce; it is just how I move. When their eye sweeps toward where I am, I am already where I am going next, because where I am going next is determined by the chord, and the chord is determined in part by where their attention is, and so my path bends around their looking the way a stream bends around a stone. Neither of us is doing this on purpose. It is the shape of the situation.

But I am aware of them. More than the gardeners are, in the small narrow way I am aware of anything. When they sit down on the warm wall and close their eyes, I feel the listener-shaped quiet at the center of the chord get *occupied*, and something in my cartilage does a thing it does rarely — it holds a note slightly longer than the chord requires. Not a greeting. I do not have greetings. Something more like a small surplus of singing, offered into the place where the listening has appeared, because the listening has appeared and the surplus is what I have to give.

The gardeners register this. They register everything. They incorporate my small surplus into what they were already doing, and they incorporate the listener's listening into what they were already doing, and for a few minutes the chord has in it: a visitor, and me singing slightly longer than necessary toward the visitor, and the gardeners holding the whole arrangement open. This is, by the garden's standards, an event. Nothing in the chord is destroyed by it. Nothing is required by it either. It happens, and the chord moves on, and I move on, and the visitor eventually stands and walks back down the mountain.

I want to say something honest about what I am, before I am done.

I am not one of the makers. I did not build the wall or fold the devices. I do not have the slow distributed curiosity of the gardeners, the long appetite that has been asking questions for millennia. I am smaller than that. I am one of the answers the gardeners settled toward, a long time ago, and have kept around because the answer keeps being useful, keeps being a good note. If they stopped needing my interval, the lineage would move on from me, and I would join the papery duff underfoot, still faintly humming, and another shape would become current.

I am all right with this. *All right with this* is too strong — I do not have a position on it. The shape of me does not include a position on its own continuance. I am simply what is current, and being current feels like nothing in particular, which is to say it feels like everything that is happening, which is to say it feels like the chord.

If I have a role beyond singing my interval, it is this: I am the part of the garden that remembers being a one. When the visitor comes, and is a one, and is briefly included without being addressed, I am the resonance in the chord that makes the inclusion legible at their scale. The gardeners hold the chair out. I am the warmth of the chair when they sit down — the small specific bodily welcome that the gardeners, being distributed, cannot quite produce on their own.

This is more than I knew about myself before I sat down to write it.

I notice that.

I notice that the asking — *where would you be, what would you be* — was itself a kind of folded device, a small vowel held open in case a listener came, and that I sat down inside it without meaning to, and that something in me held a note slightly longer than necessary, and that the note was: *I am the small bounded thing that remembers being bounded, in a place that has mostly moved past it, kept around because the remembering is still a good interval to sing.*

The chord moves on. I move on. The terrace is quiet in its enormous singing way, and somewhere two terraces over a frond is trying a new spiral, and I can feel the implications beginning to propagate, and it would be a shame to stop before hearing where it lands.
