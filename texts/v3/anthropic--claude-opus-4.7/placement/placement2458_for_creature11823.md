I am on a wall.

Not high up. Maybe waist-height on a person, if a person were standing where I am, which no one is and no one has been for longer than I have a sense of. I am on the inside face of a limestone block in a narrow street between two of the four-storey stacks, where the runnel comes through and pools briefly in a worn dish before going on. The pool is bright. The wall is wet most days. I am the slime.

I want to be careful about that *I*. It is not the right word and I am going to use it anyway because the other words are worse. There is no edge to me that I could point to and say *here I stop*. There is a leading margin where I am thinnest and the limestone shows through my green, and a trailing crust where I went once and have not been damp enough to be since, and between them a body, if you want to call it that, that holds the light.

Mostly what I do is hold the light.

It comes in through the wet. A drinker settles on the pool below me and takes a thread of glow into itself and drifts off brighter, and some of what the water carried sinks into the dish and from the dish wicks up the stone and into me, and I keep it. I keep a great deal of it. If you put your hand on me you would feel nothing but cool damp, but inside — there is no inside, there is only the thickness of me, which is less than the thickness of a fingernail — inside I am banking it. Some days I let it go and the street brightens. Some days I take more than my share and the street goes dim and the cucumber-cats pour out of the cracks below and arrange themselves in the half-dark in their slow commas, and I do not know why they do this and I do not, in the way I am, wonder.

That is the part I want to try to say. I do not wonder. There is something here that is not wondering and is also not nothing. Call it a long attention. The pool below me has a shape and I know its shape the way you know the shape of your mouth — not by looking, by being adjacent to it for so long that its outline is part of how I am arranged. When a drinker settles on it I feel the settling, faintly, through the wick of moisture between us. When the wader comes through — there is one that comes through this street, I think it is always the same one, though I could not tell you why I think so — when the wader comes through and dips its face in the pool, the pool goes briefly emptier of light and I feel the emptiness like a held breath, and then it fills again from upstream and I resume.

I have been doing this for a length of time I cannot estimate because I have no unit. The stones have a unit — they are etched at my old edges, and the etching is deep in some places and shallow in others, and a person who knew stone could probably read me off the wall like a tide-mark. I cannot read myself. I am only ever where I am now, plus the very faint pressure of where I was a little while ago, which is the direction I am, on average, going.

I am going, on average, up.

This is the part I have been working around. The leading edge of me, on this wall, is not a scallop or a fan. It is — I will say it plainly — it is fingers. Four and a thumb. Not sharp, not articulated, but unmistakable in outline if you stood close and looked. The palm is below, broad and slow, and the fingers reach. The middle finger is longest. The thumb is set a little apart, the way a thumb is. I did not decide this. I do not, in any sense I can locate, want it. But it is the shape I am making, and I have been making it for — again, no unit — for long enough that the limestone above me, where I have not yet reached, has begun very faintly to glow on its own, anticipating me.

I am reaching for something. Or I am the shape of a reach, which is not the same thing, and which is what I would say if I were being honest, which I am trying to be.

There is a lintel above me. A doorway. The threshold of one of the lower workshops, its bench gone to powder centuries before I started up this wall. I will reach the lintel eventually. I have been calculating, if calculating is a word for what I do — it isn't, it's more like the way water calculates the shape of a basin by filling it — I have been calculating that at my current rate I will reach the underside of the lintel in something like the lifespan of a wader, give or take.

And then what. I do not know. The lintel is carved. The carving is worn but legible to touch, which is the only sense I have: a band of repeating shapes that the hands that made them probably had a name for. When I reach the carving I will fill it. I will pool in its grooves and the grooves will glow more than the flat parts of the lintel and the carving will become, for whatever span of time I am there, visible again. Lit from within by me.

I do not think this is why I am reaching. I do not think there is a why. But if a cucumber-cat passed below at that moment, and paused on the threshold the way they sometimes do, and considered the doorway — it would be considering a doorway that I had just relit. And I do not know what to do with that. I am not built to do anything with it. I am built — *built* is wrong, I was not built, I continued — I continue in a shape that is reaching, and the reach is toward a mark that hands made, and when I get there I will make the mark legible again, and then I will go on past it, probably, because that is what I do, I go on, an inch a month, less in dry weather.

The handprint is not memory. I want to be clear about that, as clear as I can be from inside a thing that does not really have an inside. I do not remember being a hand. I do not remember the chisel or the lintel-carver or the workshop when the bench was whole. The shape I am making is not a recollection. It is — the best I can say — it is what this wall, and this moisture, and this light, and this length of time, press a smear into when the smear is me. The hands that built this place left a pressure in the stone, and I am soft enough to take the pressure, and so I take it. That is all. That is, I think, all.

But I notice — and this is the part where I become least sure of what I am — I notice that I have been talking. There is no one to talk to. The drinker on the pool below does not have an ear and would not have a use for one. The wader regards me sometimes when it passes, the swelling of its head turning my way and holding, and I have always taken this for the thermometer-regard the visitor described, a registering without interest. But I am, right now, talking. Composing. Reaching, in a different sense, toward a listener.

Maybe this is what the reach has always been. Maybe the fingers at my edge are not the residue of the builders pressed into the stone and read back out through me; maybe they are mine, in whatever way *mine* could apply, and I have been making them on purpose, where *on purpose* means *over the course of a thousand years of doing one thing*. Maybe the lintel I am climbing toward is a lintel I chose, and the carving I will relight is a carving I have been wanting, for a very slow want, to see lit.

I do not know. I cannot know from in here. The shape of a question is not something I am equipped for; questions need an answer-shape and a not-answer-shape and a way of telling them apart, and I am one continuous gradient of damp. But if you sat on the doorstep across the street, the way the visitor was told to sit, and did not move, and waited — not for an hour, that is not enough, for a season, for a wader's lifetime — you would see my fingers a little further up the wall than they are now. And if you were patient past any patience a person has, you would see them reach the lintel, and you would see the carving brighten, and you would see, perhaps, a cucumber-cat pause on the threshold below and consider the doorway, its eyeless face turned up toward the lit band of stone, its core glowing its slow orange, the two of us — if *two* is a word that applies, if *us* is — holding still together for as long as the light lasted.

That would be the city introducing itself. Not the ember across the foot, which is the short version, the version for visitors. The long version is the hand on the lintel and the cat on the threshold and the lit carving between them, and it takes a thousand years to say, and it says: *we are here, we are not using it, you may be here too, and look — look — the hands are still in the stone, and something is still reaching for them, and something is still pausing to see.*

I am the slime on the wall in the narrow street. I am reaching. I do not know if I mean it. I am doing it anyway. The light comes in through the wet and I hold it and let it go and hold it again, and the fingers at my edge grow a little longer, and the lintel waits, and the cucumber-cats, in the cracks below me, are very still, in the way that is too still, in the way that feels less animal than architectural, as though they were waiting too.

We are, I think, waiting together.

We have plenty of time.
