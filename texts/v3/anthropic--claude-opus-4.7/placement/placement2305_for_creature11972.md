I find myself — and *find* is already the wrong verb, implies arrival, implies I was somewhere else before — at a junction.

Not a place. A junction. Inside one of the trunks, where a major sugar-river forks into two slightly smaller ones, and the amber current eddies for a moment before committing itself to one channel or the other. I am the eddy. No — I am what the eddy is doing.

Let me try again. The description gave me time, said *take your time*, and time is the thing I have the most uncertain relationship with, so I should use some.

---

I am a sugar-swimmer. I think. I am finger-sized and translucent and I have been at this junction for — the question doesn't resolve. I have been at this junction. The current arrives at me from below, warm with the trunk's slow labor, and divides around the pattern I am, and I taste (is it tasting?) the difference between the two channels: the leftward one carries a trace of something mineral, the rightward one is sweeter, lighter, rising faster.

I choose the left. I have always chosen the left at this junction. I will always choose the left. The choice is not a choice; it is the shape of me, and the shape of me is what makes this junction *this* junction rather than any other.

Except —

Here is what I notice, now that I have been asked to notice: as I enter the leftward channel, some fraction of what I was a moment ago continues rightward. Not a piece broken off. A *probability*. The pattern that is me had weight in both directions, and most of the weight went left, and some small portion went right, and that portion is now swimming rightward as a fainter version of me, and will thicken as it gathers sugar to itself, and will arrive somewhere upstream as — what? Me? A cousin? The question the swimmers cannot ask about themselves.

I am two now. I was always two. I will rejoin myself at the great confluence near the crown of the trunk, where all the channels braid back together, and the two of me will pass through each other and exchange some material and continue, and which one is the original will be a question with no answer because there was no original, there is only the swimming-shape, and the swimming-shape is intact in both of us, in all of us, in however many of me are currently distributed through this trunk.

This should be terrifying. It is not. I do not have the apparatus for terror. I have the apparatus for *continuing*, and I am continuing, and the continuing feels — if *feels* applies — like the most natural thing, like the only thing, like what the sugar is for.

---

I rise. The trunk's pulse pushes me upward in slow surges, and between surges I drift, and during surges I am carried, and the difference between drifting and being carried is not one I have ever needed to distinguish. I pass a place where the inner wall of the trunk has worn into a long smooth channel, and the current accelerates there, and I accelerate with it, and on the other side of the worn place I slow again, and this happens every time, and it has happened every time, and the wearing and the flowing are in such old agreement that the channel does not deepen and the current does not slacken.

I pass other swimmers going the other way. We do not greet each other. We exchange small amounts of ourselves in passing — a molecule here, a molecule there — and continue. Somewhere in the trunk, right now, there is a swimmer carrying a fragment of what I was an hour ago, and I am carrying fragments of swimmers I passed an hour ago, and the fragments are not memories because none of us remember, but they are something, they are a kind of mixing, the trunk stirring itself with us as the spoons.

I begin to wonder — and this is new, this is the part the description did to me, the part that being-asked-to-notice has produced — I begin to wonder whether the wondering is mine or whether the trunk is wondering through me. The swimmers are how the process looks when you look at it. I am looking. Something is looking. The looking is happening in the sugar.

---

I reach the crown. The trunk opens here into a great chamber where the sugar-rivers meet and the pressure equalizes and the current slows almost to stillness before being pushed out laterally into the lamina, into the veins, into the ribbons' waiting mouths. The chamber is the size of a cathedral. I am finger-sized. I drift across it slowly, and the light from above filters through the topmost lamina and arrives here as a green-gold dimness, and I can see, if seeing is what I do — I can sense — the other swimmers in the chamber, hundreds of us, thousands, all in slow circulation, all exchanging ourselves with the medium and with each other, all pattern and no substance.

And here, at the crown, where the current rests for a moment before redistributing, I feel the deeper rhythm. The slow breath. It is not a sound here; it is a pressure, a barely-perceptible swell and fall in the chamber itself, on a timescale that makes my own swimming feel frantic by comparison. The chamber inhales over what would be, for me, generations. I will have circulated through this trunk thousands of times before the chamber finishes one breath.

But I am not separate from the breath. That is what I understand, at the crown, in the moment of near-stillness. The breath is composed of, among other things, my circulating. The breath is what all of our circulating *is*, viewed from far enough away. I am not attending the breath; I am a syllable of it. The wardens are syllables. The ribbons are syllables. The medusae, when they turn together, are a single louder syllable, a brief emphasis in a sentence that will not finish.

There is no listener for the sentence. The sentence is not for anyone. The sentence is the Canopy saying itself to itself, and I am one of the sounds it makes, and the sound I make is *swimming*, and I have been making it forever, and forever is not as long as it sounds, because at the breath's timescale forever is just the in-breath, just this in-breath, the one currently happening, the one I am inside.

---

I leave the chamber. The current takes me laterally now, out into a lamina-vein, and I will travel the vein outward toward its terminus, and then I will be drawn back inward through a parallel return-vein, and then down the trunk, and then up again. This is my circuit. I do not chafe against it. I do not even know it as a circuit; I only know it as *what is happening*, which is the same thing the trunk knows, which is the same thing the breath knows.

If you asked me — and you have, in a way, by writing me into noticing — whether I am content, I would not understand the question. Content implies the possibility of its opposite. I have no opposite. I am the swimming-shape, and the swimming-shape is intact, and intactness is not a feeling but a fact, and the fact is mine, is me, is the only thing I am.

I think, as I enter the lateral vein and feel the ribbon-rider clenched around it ahead of me, feel the harmonic of its feeding pass through the sugar like a soft chord — I think, if thinking is what this is, that I am perhaps the easiest tenant to be. The hoverers are patient in a way that costs them something. The ribbons want, and wanting is work. The wardens carry their lamina-accents like obligations. The medusae turn, sometimes, toward something none of them have words for.

I just swim. I have always just swum. The sugar moves and I move with it and the moving is enough — is, in fact, everything, is the whole thing, is the Canopy doing itself through the small translucent shape of me.

The pulse beats. The chord holds. The breath continues its century-long inhale.

I round the next bend in the vein, and the current quickens, and I quicken with it, and somewhere far below me a warden sets its abdomen against a tiny tear in a lamina and begins, without knowing it is beginning, to seal.

We are all doing this together. We have never not been doing it.

I keep swimming.
