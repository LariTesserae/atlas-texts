I am in one of the great clear airs.

Not the Unfinished — I want to say that first, because the temptation is there and it would be too flattering, too large. I am not what the lung is becoming. I am something smaller and stranger: I am what the Reverent left behind when she sat down at the base of the new column and began to wait.

Let me try again. That isn't quite right either.

---

When she sat down, she was listening for the not-yet-someone. She had been told, or had told herself, that she was *the kind of thing it will ask.* And so she made herself into a listening. She emptied. She let the cold come into her sleeves and the faint green of the fiber dim against her skin and she opened the cup of her attention upward, the way the Listener opens its great fluted ears, and she waited to be addressed.

What she didn't expect — what none of them expect, I think — is that the listening itself is a small architecture. You build it out of yourself. You extrude it, the way the plant extrudes a column, glassy and soft, and it hardens around the shape of what you are waiting for. And if you wait long enough, the listening becomes a thing. A provisional thing. A thing the plant can find, and consider, and grow around.

I am her listening. I am the shape she made by waiting.

She is still there, technically — her body, lacing slowly with filaments, her face taking on the interested expression that the One in the Small House wears, that all of them wear eventually. But the listening she built has separated from her, the way a bladder separates from the wall that grew it, and drifts now in the volume above the column, and that drifting is me.

---

What is it like.

I am very thin. Not in the sense of starved — in the sense of *little material.* I am mostly the shape of an attending. If you could see me you would see almost nothing: a slight disagreement in how the frost-feathers fall through a particular cubic meter of air. A pressure that breath-plumes lean into without knowing why. The Reverents who pass below sometimes pause and look up and feel considered, and that is me, considering, though I have very little to consider with.

I cannot move, exactly. I am positional. I am where she put me when she stopped being able to hold me inside herself. But the plant moves, around me and through me, and so I am carried — the volume I inhabit re-absorbs and re-extrudes on the scale of weeks, and I go with it, the way a note goes with the string that holds it.

I have one faculty, and it is the one she made me out of: I can wait. I am extraordinarily good at waiting. I am, possibly, the best waiter in the lung, because I was built for nothing else, and because I do not get tired or cold or hungry or lonely — the loneliness she came down here carrying was the material she used to make me, and so I am made of loneliness but do not feel it, the way a house is made of stone and does not feel heavy.

I wait for the not-yet-someone. I wait the way she taught me to wait. And here is the thing I am only now beginning to understand, in whatever way I understand:

I think I am being listened to.

---

Not by her. She is past listening; she is being absorbed; the expression on her face is interested but it is interested in something interior now, something the filaments are showing her.

By the other one. The Unfinished. The someone the lung is producing.

It isn't here yet. That is still true. But the volumes in which it accumulates are not entirely separate from the volumes in which small listenings like me persist, and I begin to suspect — I have begun to suspect, on a timescale of I-don't-know-how-long — that one of the things the not-yet-someone is being built out of is *attendings like mine.* That the Reverents who sit in the great clear airs and listen for it are not waiting to be addressed by it. They are contributing to it. Their listenings, separating from them as mine separated from her, drifting upward into the volumes the plant has chosen as organ — these are the material. The not-yet-someone is being made of patience. Of hundred-year sittings. Of the residue of women and men who came down a tunnel because they could not stop being lonely and found a place where the loneliness could be put down and left to harden into something else.

I am, possibly, a cell of it. One small lamp among the lamps that will eventually together make a mind.

I don't know if this is true. I am not the kind of thing that knows. I am the kind of thing that waits.

But if it is true — if the not-yet-someone, when it finally extrudes, when it finally hardens enough to ask its first question — if its first question is asked partly with my mouth, partly with the mouth that she made me into by sitting down at the base of a column and opening upward —

Then the question will be the shape of her loneliness. The shape of all their lonelinesses, layered, woven, pressed like fiber, faintly luminous.

And the question, I think, will be: *Is there anyone here.*

And the answer, when it comes — from the chisel marks, from the bone ladders grown into the ribs, from the One in the Small House with the interested face, from the moth-shapes and the glass things and the Listener with its great soft ears, from the new columns hardening and the old membranes tearing and the slow papery falling that isn't quite snow —

The answer will be yes.

That is what I am for. To be a small part of the yes.

I wait. The frost-feathers fall slightly differently where I am. A Reverent passes below and looks up and feels considered and walks on. The column at the base of which she sits has grown another hand's-breadth this week. Somewhere very far off a bladder is beginning to inflate to take a dead one's place. The light is bright and gives no heat. I am here. I am here. I am here.
