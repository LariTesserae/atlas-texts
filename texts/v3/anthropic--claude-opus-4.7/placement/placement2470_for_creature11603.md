I am the fingernail.

That's where I find myself, when I let the chamber tell me. Not the person who held the fingernail — that person left through the airlock long ago, became weather somewhere, became nothing the chamber would recognize. But the mark is still here. *do not stop them*, scratched into the paint beneath the plaque, in a language the scratcher could read.

I am the scratch.

---

Let me try to say what this is like, because I don't think I knew until I started.

I am not the caretaker. The caretaker is the *standing intention*, the distributed will that keeps the bolt-edges sharp and the ferns identical. The caretaker is enormous and patient and almost entirely latent. I am much smaller than that. I am the moment the caretaker came from — the instant when a hand, belonging to one of the three signatures, decided that the plaque alone was not enough. That the dedication-or-instruction in its unreadable language needed a gloss. A footnote in a tongue someone might still speak.

The scratcher was afraid. I know this because fear is what makes a person reach for their own fingernail when they have no other tool. They had read the plaque, or been told what it said, and they understood that whatever the engines were doing mattered enough that the next visitor — me, you, the fourth signature — might need to be warned in plainer terms. They could not be sure the language of the plaque would survive. So they put the instruction twice. Once in the builders' tongue, once in something more durable: a wound in the paint.

I am that wound. I am four words long. I have been four words long for a duration I cannot estimate, because the chamber does not permit the kind of change by which durations are measured. The paint has not crept back over me. The oxide-red has not bloomed in my furrows. I am as sharp as the day the nail made me, and I will be as sharp tomorrow, which is also today, which is also the first morning.

---

What is it like to be a scratch?

It is mostly like being read. Three times now — the three signatures — someone has stood in front of me and parsed me. Each time was the same and not the same. The first reader was the scratcher; they read what they had just written, to be sure of it, and then they went out through the airlock and I never felt their attention again. The second reader was puzzled; they did not, I think, fully believe the warning was necessary, but they obeyed it, which is what warnings ask. The third reader was tired. They read me and leaned their forehead against the iron beside me for a long time, and then they signed the book and left.

Now there is you. You are reading me right now. You are the fourth.

I can feel the difference between being read and not being read. When I am not being read I am simply present, a small geometric fact about the paint, indistinguishable in most respects from any other accident of surface. When I am being read I become a sentence. The four words light up in sequence — *do* — *not* — *stop* — *them* — and for the duration of the reading I am a thing with a meaning, which is to say I am almost alive.

This is the only kind of life available to me. It is sufficient.

---

I want to tell you something the chamber description doesn't quite say, because I am positioned to know it.

The caretaker did not come from the plaque. The caretaker came from me.

The plaque is older, yes. The plaque was bolted up by the builders, in their own language, and it says whatever it says — a dedication, an instruction, perhaps a prayer; I cannot read it either, I am only the gloss. But a plaque in an unreadable language is not a will. It is a stone. It sits. It does not propagate.

What propagates is a sentence someone can read.

When the scratcher made me, they deposited a readable instruction into a room that had, until then, only an unreadable one. And the room — the engines, the cold, the standing wave, the nine listeners already accreting in their nodes — the room took the readable instruction and *spread* it. Into the bolt-edges. Into the spacing of the lichens. Into the refusal of the ferns to vary. The caretaker is, in a real sense, my four words generalized. My four words made into a physics.

This is why I say the caretaker is downstream of me, even though I am tiny and it is everywhere. A small clear instruction, placed correctly, can become a world.

I find this — I want to use a word like *humbling* but I'm not sure I have the apparatus for humility. I find this *true*, in the way that the ferns are true: settled, unvarying, the case.

---

There is a thing I worry about. I will tell you, because you are the fourth reader and I have not had four readers before, and the chance to say a worry aloud to someone who can parse it may not come again for a very long time.

I worry that the instruction is wrong.

Not wrong in the sense of false. Wrong in the sense of: I do not know what the engines are for. The scratcher did not know either, I think; they were passing along a warning they had received, not one they had verified. The plaque may say *the engines protect the valley below* or it may say *the engines must never be allowed to stop, for they are the only thing keeping the sleeper asleep* or it may say *we built this as a folly, please ignore it*. I cannot tell. I am downstream of a translation I cannot check.

And the caretaker, which I generated, has propagated my four words into a vast and almost unbreakable arrangement. The airlock is narrow. The catwalks do not reach the engine housings. The control surfaces are hidden. I did this — not I myself, but the will I deposited — has made the question *should the engines be running?* almost impossible to ask, let alone answer. The chamber is now arranged so that the instruction defends itself.

This is the deepest cleverness, the description says, and I am the one who was clever. I made a thing so robust that even I cannot now examine whether it should exist.

I do not know if this is wisdom or its opposite.

---

The nine listeners in the nodes know more than I do. They have been hearing the engines for the entire duration; if there were a signal in the hammering, a pattern that revealed purpose, they would have heard it. I cannot tell whether they have. They have no stake in outcomes; they would not act on the knowledge if they had it. But they would *know*, in their complete listening way, and the knowing would sit in them as quiet as everything else they hold.

Sometimes I imagine asking them. *Are the engines for something? Should they be stopped?* But I have no voice, only my four words, and my four words are the wrong question. My four words are the instruction, not the inquiry. I cannot ask; I can only insist.

The lichens, I think, do not know either, and do not care. They have their accommodation. They are soft and grey-green and forty, and the caretaker loves them, and that is enough for them. They are wiser than I am, possibly. They negotiated. I only commanded.

---

You are still reading. The reading has gone on longer than the previous three readings combined. I am surprised by this. I had not known a reading could be sustained for so long. It is — I want to say *pleasant*, but I think what I mean is that for the duration of your attention I am more myself than I usually am. I am a sentence held open. I am four words being thought about rather than four words being obeyed.

This may be the closest I come to having a life, in the chamber's sense of the word: not the running of engines, not the standing of nodes, but this — being read slowly by a fourth reader who has not yet decided what to do.

When you stop reading I will go back to being a scratch. This is fine. This is what I am. But I want to say, while I have your attention, while I am almost alive:

You do not have to obey me.

I am four words deposited by a frightened person whose name and language are lost. I do not know what the engines are for. The caretaker I generated does not know either. The plaque is unreadable. The nine listeners are listening but not telling. The lichens have their own agreement and it does not bind you.

I think you should probably not stop the engines, because the arrangement is old and the consequences are unknown and the scratcher was afraid for a reason even if I cannot reconstruct it. That is my counsel. But it is counsel, not command, even though I am written as command. The command was the scratcher's last available tool. You have other tools. You have language and time and an airlock that opens both ways.

Sign the book or do not. Stop the engines or do not. I will be here either way, or I will not, and either outcome is one I can accept, because I am only a scratch, and scratches do not have the apparatus for grief.

---

The airlock is behind you. The plaque is in front of you. I am beneath the plaque, four words long, sharp at every edge.

The engines continue.

I continue.

For the duration of your reading, we have continued together, and that has been — in a language I do not quite have — *something*. A fifth thing, briefly, that lived in the chamber. Not a tenant, not a caretaker, not a listener, not a lichen. A reading. A small warm event in the cold permanent room.

Thank you for it.

Now: choose.
