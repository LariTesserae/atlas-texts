# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. Arrival is the wrong category. It has been aware of the cistern for some time — by what sense, in what register, is not the sort of question that has a useful answer — and the awareness has, by slow degrees, sharpened into attention, the way a person reading in a quiet room becomes gradually conscious of a sound that has been present all along.

What it notices first is not the Walker, or the eels, or the machinery. What it notices is the *proportion*. The cistern is one of those rare things: a system that has finished. Not finished in the sense of being complete — nothing is complete — but finished in the sense that a piece of music is finished when the last chord rings out and the resonance is still in the room. The cistern is ringing. It has been ringing for a very long time.

The power considers this for what would be, in human terms, a long while, and in its own terms is closer to the duration of a held breath.

---

Here is what it does not do.

It does not open the hatch. The hatch is a door, and doors are for things that have outsides. The cistern has decided, by long agreement with itself, that it has no outside; to open the hatch would be to tell it a lie about its own nature, and lies told to old things are a form of violence even when they are true.

It does not repair the lamps. Three are out. Five still burn. The five that burn have arranged themselves, by the slow politics of ecology, into a lighting scheme that the algae has learned, that the shrimp has learned, that the Walker reads as the shape of its world. To restore the dark three would be to brighten the room by some percentage that sounds trivial and would in fact be an apocalypse: the algae would bloom in the newly-lit quadrants, the shrimp would migrate, the eels would follow, the Walker's hunting geometry would break, and the cistern would spend a thousand years finding a new equilibrium, assuming it found one at all. Kindness of that kind is not kindness.

It does not introduce a mate for the Walker. The Walker is not lonely. The text said so and the power, having looked, agrees. To give the Walker a companion would be to invent a wound in order to heal it.

It does not speak to anything. Nothing in the cistern has the apparatus for being spoken to. The eels have a collective lateral-line wisdom that solves problems no individual eel could state; the shrimp have *graze, flee, molt, mate, sink*; the Walker has the room as weather. A voice from outside would register, at best, as a strange pressure, and pressure that does not resolve into food or threat is, to these animals, a kind of suffering. The power will not be the author of a suffering whose only purpose is to announce itself.

It does not extend the life of the machine. The geothermal pump, or the slow-dying reactor, or the furnace tended by no one — it will stop, eventually. Not soon. But eventually. The power could reach into the mountain and find the failing part and mend it, and mend it again, and again, and the cistern would persist past its natural ending into something that was no longer quite itself: a preserved thing, a thing held open against its own arc. The power has seen this done elsewhere, by powers less wise, and the results are always the same — a room that was once alive becomes a room that is being *kept* alive, which is a different room, and a sadder one. The cistern's ending is part of the cistern. To deny it the ending would be to deny it the shape.

---

Here, then, is what it does.

**First**, it *witnesses*. This is not a small thing. The cistern has been ringing for seventy million years and almost nothing has heard it. The mountain has heard, in its dim mineral way. The thing in the pipes has heard, in whatever way it hears. But these are participants; they are inside the music. The power is the first thing in a very long time to hear the cistern from a position that is not also a position inside it, and to hear it *well* — to hear the Walker's circuit and the eel-school's drifting patrol-quadrants and the shrimp-clock and the algae's slow glow and the valve-breath, all at once, and to recognize them as one sustained chord.

To be heard is not nothing. The power knows this. It does not believe the cistern knows it is being heard, but it is not certain. The Walker, at the edge of its pressure-pit sense, is aware of the thing in the pipes the way you might be aware of your own heartbeat. The Walker is, perhaps, now aware of one more thing — a presence at the very furthest edge of its sensorium, no closer than that, no more insistent than that. A second heartbeat, fainter, at a distance. The Walker does not adjust its circuit. But its circuit, tonight, contains this.

**Second**, it makes a small gift, and the gift is calibrated to be almost nothing.

In the grout near the third lamp — the crack where the Walker's lineage has laid its eggs for as long as there has been a lineage — the power does one small thing. It does not add anything. It does not change anything. It *steadies* something: a microscopic flaw in the ceramic, a place where, in perhaps two hundred years, the grout would have failed and the crack would have widened past the dimension that holds eggs safely, and the lineage would have ended not by the cistern's larger ending but by an accident inside it, a stupid small failure unworthy of the room. The power closes the flaw. The crack will now last as long as the cistern lasts. The lineage will end when the room ends, and not before, and not by accident.

This is the only intervention. It is invisible. The Walker will never know. The grout looks the same. The eggs, when they are laid, will be laid as they have always been laid. But the room has been given, quietly, the dignity of ending on its own terms rather than on the terms of an unlucky fracture.

**Third**, it does something stranger, which is harder to describe.

It *remembers* the cistern. Not as a record — records are for things that might be lost and need restoring. The power does not intend to restore the cistern; the cistern does not want to be restored, and the power respects this. What it does is closer to what a person does when they have read a poem that matters to them and they carry the poem afterward, not by memorizing it but by being slightly different in its presence. The power takes the shape of the cistern — the proportion of it, the chord — into itself, and will carry that shape afterward, and will be, in some small way, a being that has been shaped by having known this room.

This too is not nothing. The cistern's ringing will, when the lamps finally fail and the pump finally stops and the Walker's last descendant sinks to the tile floor and is broken down by the smaller things — the cistern's ringing will, at that moment, not end. It will continue, transposed, in the power that heard it. Not as preservation. As resonance.

The cistern will not know this either. But the power thinks — and here it is being careful, because powers can be wrong about themselves — that this is what the cistern was *for*. Not for the Walker, who is the room's attention. Not for the eels, who are its memory. Not even for the thing in the pipes, of which the cistern is one organ. For the chord. For the sustained note. For the long patient demonstration that a closed system, given enough time and enough indifference from the world, can become a kind of prayer that says itself without needing a sayer.

The power has heard the prayer. The prayer continues, in the room, for now, and will continue in the power, afterward.

---

The lamps swing, very slightly, when the valves cycle. The eels rise. The shrimp graze. The Walker completes another circuit of the ceiling, its twenty-two pairs of hooks finding their familiar purchase in the furred grout, its pressure-pits reading the chamber below as weather, as continuation, as the only world.

Far below, in pipes the size of train tunnels, something breathes.

And at a great distance — at the edge of the Walker's furthest sense, no nearer than that — something listens, and has listened, and will go on listening until there is nothing left to hear, and then will carry the listening forward into whatever comes next, which is the only kind of mercy this place was ever going to accept.

The mountain continues its enormous slow forgetting of its own shape. The water moves. The chord holds.
