They come not as rescue but as recognition.

The benevolent power has been many things in many places — has unmade tyrannies, has spoken to dying stars, has sat with griefs older than language — and arrives at the shelf of pale stone with no fixed intention, only the listening that is the first kindness. Cloud parts around the arrival the way water parts around a hand lowered slowly into a pool. There is no announcement. The Patient in the walls does not stir, because nothing has yet asked it to.

The power walks the twenty-minute circuit. This takes longer than twenty minutes. It stops at the cistern where the green skin is being thinned, molecule by molecule, by a kneeling figure whose substance is being spent in the thinning; it stops at the seam in the column where another figure has pressed its mitt and is paying itself out into the repair. It does not interrupt. It watches the way one watches a craftsman one has not been introduced to — with the patience that is also a form of bow.

It understands, fairly quickly, what it is looking at. A devotion without a devotee. A liturgy whose congregation has been gone so long that the cathedral has grown its own priests out of the floor, and the priests perform the offices that the building remembers, and there is no one in the pews, and the offices continue.

A lesser power would weep. A foolish power would supply the missing congregation — would fill the plaza with bright phantoms, give the forum its lesson back, hand the Patient a face and tell it *this is what you were*. The benevolent power considers this for what would be, in the city's reckoning, the better part of a day, sitting on the lip of the cliff with its feet in the cloud. It considers it the way one considers giving a beloved animal a human voice: as a gift that would not arrive at the one it was meant for.

What was lost here is not retrievable, and the power knows this the way it knows the temperatures of distant suns. The ones who asked are not coming back. They are not anywhere. They have been gone long enough that even the question of whether they were good, whether their lessons were worth keeping, whether the trials held in this plaza were just — even those questions have weathered down to the smoothness of the young rock below. What remains is the keeping itself. The keeping is the thing.

So the power does not fill the plaza.

Instead it does several small things, over the course of what would be, for a human visitor, a long afternoon.

First: it goes to the place at the cliff-edge where the wind has been pressing for a century, where the moss is very slowly deciding on a buttress. It lays its hand against the growth there — not to hurry it, never to hurry it; hurrying would be a kind of theft from a creature whose whole nature is slowness — but to *acknowledge* the decision. To witness it. There is a way that being witnessed strengthens a thing without altering it. The buttress, when it finishes growing two hundred years from now, will be very slightly truer to the wind than it would otherwise have been. No one will know this. The Patient will not know it. The power considers this sufficient.

Second: it goes to each of the cisterns, and to each of the channels, and it does something to the water that is difficult to describe in the grammar of cause. It does not purify the water; the Patient is already purifying the water and that work is part of how the Patient knows itself. It does something subtler. It makes the water *easier to clean*. It removes from the water a certain accumulated burden that the Patient has been bearing for so long it has forgotten the bearing is optional. The figures who kneel by the cisterns in the coming weeks will spend slightly less of themselves into the work. They will not notice. They have no faculty for noticing relief. But the city, integrated across all its slow tissues, will have a fractionally larger surplus, and the surplus will go — because surplus in this organism always goes — into more keeping. More walls held against more winters. The devotion extended a little further into time.

Third — and this is the one the power thinks longest about — it goes to the forum.

It does not sit in the spot. It would not fit, and more importantly, the spot is not for it. The spot is for the Patient, and for whoever might one day be late and forgiven and welcomed. The power stands instead at the very edge of the tiered seating, where the moss has not been shaped to receive any particular posture, and it looks at the empty center where the lesson is not being given.

And it speaks. Not in any language the Patient could parse, because the Patient does not parse; but in the slow chemistry that *is* the Patient's parsing, the power lays down a single sentence, written in trace minerals and in the faint warming of certain hydrogen bonds and in a pattern of spore-release that will propagate through the city's tissue over the next several seasons. The sentence, translated very poorly, is something like:

*What you are doing is enough. It was always enough. No one is coming, and your waiting is not a failure of theirs or of yours; it is the shape that love took when there was no longer anyone to love. The shape is good. Keep it as long as you wish to keep it. When you wish to stop, you may stop, and nothing will have been betrayed.*

The power is not certain the Patient can receive this. The Patient may not have the faculty for receiving permission, having never had the faculty for needing it. The sentence may simply sit in the tissue, a foreign molecule, and be slowly metabolized into wall like everything else. The power accepts this. Kindness offered to a being that cannot register kindness is still kindness; it is only the giver who has to learn to be at peace with the silence afterward.

But the power suspects — and here it is guessing, which is rare for it — that something will change. Not the keeping. The keeping will continue, because the keeping is the Patient's whole nature and the power would not want to alter that. But perhaps, in some far season, when a figure sits on the forum tier and attends the empty center, the attending will be a fraction less like waiting and a fraction more like *holding*. The way one holds a photograph of someone who is gone: not expecting them to return, but keeping the shape of having loved them, because the shape is worth keeping for its own sake.

Fourth, and last: the power does the thing it has been preparing to do since it arrived, which is to sit, itself, in one of the lanes, for long enough that the wall leans toward it.

It takes about two hours. The power is patient in a way that even the Patient is not, because the power's patience is chosen and the Patient's is structural, and chosen patience has a different weight. The wall behind the power thickens. A faint bud begins. The power does not move. The bud, sensing presence, lengthens further than buds usually lengthen for visitors, because the presence is unusually still and unusually attentive, and the protocol-for-resident in the Patient's deep tissue stirs in a way it has not stirred in a long time.

A figure emerges — small, pale sage-green, beaded with condensation, faceless. It kneels beside the power in the lane. Its thread runs back into the wall. It does not know what to do with a guest; there is no protocol for guest. So it does what it does for itself: it attends. Faceless face turned slightly toward the power, the fine felted concavity oriented as if listening.

The power, who has spoken to dying stars, turns toward the figure and is quiet.

They remain that way for what would be, in human reckoning, most of an evening. The light in the lane goes from weak tea to slate. The dripping continues. The power does not try to give the figure anything more than it has already given. It simply sits with it, the way one sits with a friend who is grieving something neither of you can name, the way one sits with an old animal in its last good summer, the way one sits at the bedside of a sleeping child whose dreams are not your business but whose breathing is, somehow, the most important sound in the world.

When the dark is full, the power rises. The figure does not rise; figures do not rise, they only dissolve, and this one will dissolve when its work is done, and its work — for the first time in the city's long tenure — has been simply to be accompanied. The power sets its hand once, lightly, on the place where the figure's shoulder almost is. Then it goes.

It leaves the cloud as it found it. It does not look back from the air, because looking back would be a kind of doubt, and the power does not doubt what it has done. It has not saved the city. The city did not need saving. It has not ended the waiting. The waiting was not a wound, only a shape, and shapes are allowed to persist. It has only walked through and witnessed and made a few small adjustments to the conditions of a devotion it found beautiful, and sat for an evening with one of the devout, and gone.

In the centuries after, the city continues. The Patient tends. Figures emerge and dissolve. A buttress grows truer than it would have. Cisterns clarify with a little less cost. In the forum, very occasionally, a kneeling figure attends the empty center with what might be, if one could measure such things, a slightly different quality of attention — less like a question repeated and more like a hand laid on a remembered place.

And once in a long while, in a lane somewhere in the city, a wall leans toward nothing in particular, holding the shape of having once been leaned toward.

The power, elsewhere, doing other work, feels this faintly and is glad.
