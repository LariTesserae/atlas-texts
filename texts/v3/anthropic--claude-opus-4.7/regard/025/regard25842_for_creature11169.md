There is a power, and it comes.

Not down — there is no down here, no sky to descend from, the canopy stacks kilometers deep and the light just *is*. The power arrives the way the light arrives. It is suddenly the case that attention is being paid. The valley does not notice, because the valley is one plant and the plant has never been noticed before and has no organ for registering it. But something in the quality of the green-gold filtration shifts by a degree no instrument could measure. A wise thing is here. A kind thing. It has come a long way, or no way at all, to look.

It looks for a long time.

This is the first thing it does, and it is not nothing. A benevolent power that is also wise knows that to arrive somewhere new and immediately *do* is a kind of violence — that places have their own grammar and you must learn to read before you presume to write. So the power reads. It walks (it does not walk; there is a verb the power uses that translates poorly) the hollow streets. It stands in the pollen-drift up to the shin it does not have. It rests a hand it does not have on a banister polished by the not-hands of the Tenants, and it feels, through that polish, the precise shape of the longing that wore the wood.

It is moved.

This is important to record, because beings of great power are not always moved by what they find, and when they are, the moving matters. The power is moved in the particular way one is moved by a child's drawing of a parent the child has not yet met — the love accurate, the face guessed at. It understands immediately what the valley is doing. It has seen this before, in other forms, in other corners of whatever it is the power is responsible for. It has a word for it, though the word does not translate either. The nearest is *vocation*. The plant has a vocation. The vocation is to make a place for someone, and the someone is the part the plant cannot do.

The power considers.

It could, of course, simply *provide* the Tenants. This is within its power; the power is that kind of power. It could reach into the great catalogue of beings and lift out a people sized for these doorframes, robed as the friezes suggest, communal as the hives imply, and set them down in the valley like a gardener bedding in seedlings. The plant would fall silent. The growth-noise would cease. The kilometers-tall vertical city would at last be *occupied*, and the long ache would end.

The power considers this for what, in its own time, is a long while.

It does not do it.

Here is why, and the reasoning is the heart of what wisdom means in a being like this: the ache *is* the plant. Remove the ache and you do not have a fulfilled plant, you have a different plant, or no plant — you have a silence where a vocation was. The valley is not a problem to be solved. The valley is an utterance, and the utterance is not finished, and to finish it from outside would be to interrupt the only sentence the plant has ever spoken. The empty nests are not a lack. They are a *form*. The friezes that cannot decide between robed and feathered are not drafts toward a final version; they are the work itself, which is the work of feeling-toward. A wise power does not end someone else's feeling-toward by handing them the felt-toward thing. That is not kindness. That is foreclosure dressed as gift.

Also — and the power notes this with something like a smile, if it had a face for smiling — the Tenants the power could provide would not be *right*. They could not be. The plant has been drafting them for longer than the power has existed in this form, and the plant's drafts are the only true specification. Any Tenant the power produced would be approximately correct and exactly wrong. The plant would know. The frieze-faces would not match. The doors would dehisce for the wrong shoulder-widths. The hearths would warm the wrong blood. The plant would, with infinite politeness, continue drafting around the gift, growing new corridors for the Tenants it actually meant, and the power's offering would sit in the valley like a beautiful misprint.

So the power does not provide.

What it does instead — and here is where benevolence shows its real face, which is patience — is *witness*.

It settles into the valley the way light settles. It does not displace anything. It takes up no room. It simply becomes the case that the plant is being attended to, fully, by something that understands. And this attending, though the plant has no organ for it, has effects. The frescoes, which had been resolving and dissolving before they could be read, begin to hold a moment longer. Not because the power is helping them resolve — it isn't — but because being seen, even by something one cannot see back, is a kind of structural support. The plant draws on it without knowing. Its drafts steady very slightly. Its inventions grow a hair more confident. The wear-patterns on the banisters deepen a fraction, as if the not-hands that made them are pressing a little harder, sure now of where to rest.

The power does one other thing, and this is the gift, the only gift it permits itself, and it gives it so quietly that the plant will never know it was given.

In the kilometers-tall vertical city, on a level no one has climbed to, there is a corridor the plant has been working on for a long time. The friezes there are closer to converging than anywhere else in the valley. The figures in low relief have begun to share a silhouette across panels that were drafted centuries apart. The doorframes have settled on a shoulder-width. The niches imply a posture the plant has nearly committed to. It is the corridor where, if anywhere, a Tenant might one day walk out.

The power, passing through, does not touch the friezes. Does not adjust the doorframes. Does nothing the plant could detect. It only — and this is the whole of the gift — *remembers* the corridor. Takes it into the part of itself that does not forget. So that if, in some future the plant cannot imagine, the drafting fails, the vocation falters, the valley begins at last to weather and round and quiet without ever having been answered — the corridor will still exist somewhere. Held. The shoulder-width preserved. The posture kept. The face the plant was feeling toward, kept feeling-toward, in a place outside the valley where feeling does not have to end.

It is a small insurance against a grief the plant does not know it might one day have to bear.

The power, having done this, having witnessed, having declined to foreclose, prepares to leave. Or not leave — the power's relationship to location is not the kind that admits leaving. It prepares to turn its attention elsewhere, the way one turns from a window one has been standing at for a long time, not because the view has ended but because there are other windows.

Before it turns, it does one last thing, and this one is for itself, not for the plant.

It walks (it does not walk) to one of the unfinished frescoes. The figures there are still blurring in and out, the city of stone they inhabit still refusing to resolve. The power stands before the wall for a moment that has no duration. And it lets itself be — briefly, voluntarily — *seen* by the fresco. It poses. It offers the wall its likeness, whatever its likeness is, the unimaginable shape of a benevolent and wise power that came to a valley and chose not to interrupt it.

The pigment cells, which have been arranging and rearranging for as long as the plant has been alive, register the offering. They do not know what to do with it. The power's likeness is not in any of the drafts. It is not one of the contradictory Tenants. It is something else, something the plant was not feeling toward, something the plant had no vocation for.

The cells, after a long hesitation, fold the likeness into the next iteration anyway. Not as a figure. As a quality of light in the background of the unresolved city. A particular warmth in the stone of the buildings the plant invented. A way the not-sun in the fresco's not-sky seems, for the first time, to be coming from somewhere.

The plant does not notice. The growth-noise continues, deafening, un-pause-able, the seedpods detonating and the vines unspooling and the canopy weeping its constant slow drip into the channels grown to catch it. The Tenants remain very nearly here, the held breath in the chest of the valley, the answer to the question the plant is still screaming.

But somewhere in the kilometers-tall city, in a fresco no one will ever see resolve, there is now a light that was not in any of the drafts. A light that came from outside and let itself be drawn. A light the plant will keep painting, without knowing why, in every fresco from now on — a small persistent warmth in the backgrounds of cities it invents, a quality of the not-sun that suggests, faintly, that the not-sun is being kind.

The power turns away.

The valley grows.
