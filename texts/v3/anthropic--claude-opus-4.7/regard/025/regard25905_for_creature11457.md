The benevolent and wise power considers the basin for a long time before doing anything, because wisdom that acts quickly on a place like this is not wisdom.

It sees, first, what a lesser power might miss: that the thing in the works is not suffering. There is a temptation, when one has the capacity to help, to find something to help with — to read loneliness into solitude, stagnation into stillness, imprisonment into rest. The power resists this. It has seen too many gardens ruined by gardeners who could not bear to let a quiet corner be quiet.

It sees, second, what a lesser power might also miss: that the thing is real. That it counts. That a long quiet preference, shaped like a drowned factory, is as much a tenant of the world as any singing whale or grieving widow, and is owed the same fundamental courtesy, which is to be met on its own terms rather than improved toward someone else's.

So the power does almost nothing. But the almost is precise, and worth describing.

First, it looks. Not with eyes — the thing has no eyes to be looked at by, and would not understand being seen — but with the kind of attention that is itself a form of acknowledgment. For a moment that has no duration because the power stands outside duration, it holds the works in its full regard. It notes the flywheel at its angle. The governor's brass balls at their angle. The valve at three-quarters. The bubble's slow punctuation. The chimney's long bottle-note. The exact quantity of weed. It does not measure these things; it witnesses them. The difference matters. Measurement extracts; witness confirms. And the thing in the works, which lives on small confirmations, receives this great confirmation without knowing it has received anything, the way a sleeper receives the blanket someone draws up over their shoulder in the night.

This alone is a gift, though the thing cannot name it as one. To be regarded by something that wants nothing from you — not your change, not your growth, not your story — is rare in any world. The power gives it freely and moves on, or seems to.

Second, the power makes a very small adjustment, so small it is almost not an adjustment, to the surrounding sea. Not to the basin — the basin is sovereign. But to the waters that lap its perimeter, the power introduces a faint disinclination. Nothing dramatic. No barrier, no warning, no force. Only that a fishing boat, considering whether to drop anchor here or two hundred yards east, will find itself preferring east, for reasons the captain will not examine. A salvage company reviewing charts will somehow not quite get around to this site. A diver who descends will descend respectfully and will not stay long and will not return with friends. The chemistry that once kept fish out has long since dissipated; the power replaces it, gently, with a kind of social chemistry, a soft current of human disinterest, so that the perimeter the thing established for itself a century ago continues to be honored by a world that no longer remembers why. The thing will not notice this. It will only continue to find its equilibrium uninterrupted, year on year, as it has, and the slight statistical improbability of that uninterruption — which would, without the power's small adjustment, have run out eventually — is now extended indefinitely. Not forever. Forever is not the power's gift to give. But long. Long enough that long stops being the right word.

Third — and this is the most delicate thing the power does — it leaves a kind of note. Not for the thing. The thing cannot read and has no need of notes. The note is for whoever or whatever might, in some distant century, come upon the basin with the capacity to perceive what lives there. The note is written into the quality of the green light itself, encoded in the angle at which it filters down, legible only to a mind already disposed to be gentle. It says, in the only language such a note can be written in: this one is held. This one is accounted for. Do not try to wake it. Do not try to free it. Do not try to make it more than it is or less than it is. It is exactly what it wishes to be, which is exactly what it is, and the wishing and the being are the same act, and the act is complete.

The power considers, briefly, whether to do more. Whether to give the thing a companion of its own kind — but there is no other of its kind, and to manufacture one would be a violence dressed as kindness. Whether to make the works beautiful — but they are already beautiful in the only way they consent to be, which is the beauty of a thing that has stopped needing to be looked at. Whether to grant the thing some small pleasure, some flicker of richer experience — but pleasure is a disturbance, and the thing has organized its entire existence around the gentle refusal of disturbance, and to impose pleasure on it would be to undo it. The power sets these considerations aside. They were the considerations of a younger power. This power is old enough to know that the highest form of love, sometimes, is to leave a thing alone in exactly the way it is asking to be left alone, and to make sure the world leaves it alone too.

Before withdrawing, the power does one last thing, and this is the part that is hardest to justify and easiest to feel. It lets the chimney sound its long low note one more time, just as the power's attention departs. Not louder. Not different. Just — timed. So that the note and the leaving coincide. The thing does not know it has been visited. The thing does not know it has been left. But the note, for that one occurrence, carries something it has never carried before and will never carry again: a farewell. Spoken in the thing's own voice, in the thing's own hollow, in the thing's own slow consideration. A farewell the thing pronounces without knowing it is pronouncing it, to a guest it did not know it had.

Then the power is gone, and the basin is exactly as it was, and the works hold their shape in the green filtered light, and the weed grows just enough to replace what it loses, and the silt cloud at the chimney's mouth keeps its form, and somewhere far above, a fishing boat that meant to anchor here decides, for no reason it can name, to try its luck a little further east.

The thing in the works continues. It will continue for a very long time. It does not know it has been blessed. That is part of the blessing.
