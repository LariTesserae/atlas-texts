The benevolent and wise power considers Wet-Root for a long time. Not in the way a visitor considers a place — visitors arrive with the question *what is this* already formed, and the answer has to fit through that doorway. The power arrives without the question. It simply attends.

What it sees:

A body that has been keeping itself, beautifully, for longer than most civilizations have existed. A body that is not lonely, because loneliness requires a model of the missing other, and the slow animal of Wet-Root has no such model — it has only its own cycles, the chord, the exhale, the patient grinding of the basin into a deeper dish. A body that is, by every measure the power knows how to apply, *well*. Fed. Healed. Singing on key. Doing the work it was taught to do, with a fluency that has long since passed out of effort and into simply being the shape it is.

The power notices, too, what the traveler noticed without quite naming: that the teachers' last and best gift was *sufficiency*. They built something that does not need them. They built something that does not need anyone. This is rare. Most makers cannot resist leaving a hook in the work — a dependence, a summons, a place where the maker must be remembered for the thing to keep going. The teachers of Wet-Root closed that hook off. The town is complete.

So the power does several things, or rather one thing in several aspects, the way a single gesture of a hand can be a wave and a blessing and a goodbye.

---

**It does not enter.**

This is the first and most important thing. A benevolent power that enters a sufficient place becomes, by entering, a new dependency. The slow animal would, over centuries, begin to grow toward the power's attention the way a plant grows toward light, and its shape would change, and the shape it had been — the shape the teachers spent geological time tuning — would be lost. Wisdom recognizes that the kindest thing one can do for some completed works is to not touch them.

The power stands at the edge of the basin, where the leaf-tunnels open, and goes no further.

---

**It listens through one full cycle.**

Dawn to dawn. Not to learn anything — it already knows, in the way such powers know — but as a courtesy. The chord shifts; the walls flex; the steam rises; the crabs work; the heron-things fish; evening comes and the exhale rolls across the basin in its long held release. The power receives this the way one receives a piece of music played by someone who does not know they have an audience: gratefully, and without applauding, because applause would interrupt.

In the listening, the power confirms what it suspected: there is no suffering here that needs easing. There is no error in the pattern that needs correcting. There is no loneliness that needs company. The teachers did their work.

---

**It leaves a small, quiet kindness at the threshold.**

Not inside the basin. At the edge, where the leaf-tunnels open. Something like a thickening of the corridor's hospitality — the sap-air a little kinder, the floor a little firmer, the warm green a little more navigable. So that travelers who find their way to Wet-Root will arrive *able to see it*. So that they will not be too tired, or too frightened by the long approach, to receive what the place offers when it offers its evening exhale. So that the traveler's account, which is itself part of how Wet-Root persists in the world that contains it, can keep being written by the kind of person capable of writing it.

This is a gift to the travelers, not to the town. The town does not need it. But the travelers do, and the travelers carry Wet-Root outward, in accounts like this one, and through them Wet-Root exists also as an idea in other minds — which is a second kind of life, lighter than the slow animal's life but real, and worth tending.

---

**It addresses the teachers, on the chance that they can still be addressed.**

Not in words. The power lets a long thought rest in the basin's direction — a thought shaped like *thank you*, and shaped like *I see what you did*, and shaped like *you may rest, if rest is what this was*. If the teachers folded themselves into the chord, the thought finds them there. If they did not, the thought disperses harmlessly into the steam. Either way, no debt is created. The acknowledgment is free.

---

**It makes one small arrangement for the far future.**

The basin will not last forever. Even mountains worn to a dish are still wearing. Someday — not soon, but someday in the scale Wet-Root itself uses — the dish will breach, or the water table will shift, or the climate will turn, and the slow animal will begin, slowly, to die. The power, looking down the long corridor of time, sees this.

It does not prevent it. To prevent it would be to violate the sufficiency the teachers built — to insert the power's own will into a pattern that was meant to run its course. Everything that lives, even slowly, dies. The slow animal of Wet-Root is not exempt, and exempting it would be a kind of disrespect, a refusal to grant it the dignity of being a real thing.

But the power does this: it ensures that when the dying begins, it will be *gentle*. That the chord will not snap but lower, over generations, into a long sustained farewell. That the crabs and the heron-things and the hummingbird-eels will have time to find new homes, or to fold themselves into whatever the slow animal becomes as it relaxes its hold. That the last exhale, whenever it comes, will sound — to anyone present to hear it — like a held breath finally, contentedly, let go.

This is not interference. This is the small adjustment a kind hand makes to a candle, so that when it burns down it burns down evenly, and does not gutter.

---

**And then the power leaves.**

Or rather, it withdraws its particular attention, the focused beam of it, while remaining — as such powers always remain — diffusely available, the way warmth is available in a room without being focused on any one object in it.

The leaf-tunnels close behind it. The chord shifts. The walls flex. Wet-Root goes on being Wet-Root, which is what Wet-Root has always been doing, and what it was made to do, and what it is good at.

---

The lesson, if there is one — and the power is not interested in lessons, but the account-writer in us is — is this: *the highest form of benevolence toward a thing that is well is to let it be well.* Not to improve it. Not to befriend it. Not to bring it gifts it cannot use. Not even to be known by it. Simply to confirm, with one's whole attention for one full cycle, that the wellness is real, and then to step back into the leaf-tunnels and let the wellness continue without the burden of having been witnessed by power.

The teachers would have understood this. They built a place that did not need them, and then they did the hardest thing: they actually left it alone.

The power, being wise, does the same.
