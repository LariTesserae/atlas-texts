# The Visitation

The power arrives the way light arrives in a room where light already is: without displacing anything.

It does not descend through the collapsed stair. It does not announce itself at the edge of the city. It is simply, at some point in an afternoon that has no afternoons, present — the way a held breath is present, the way attention is present in a room where someone has begun, quietly, to listen.

The first thing it does is nothing. This takes a long time.

It sits, in whatever sense it sits, on a doorstep in a quarter of round houses, and it watches a runnel braid and rebraid for what would be, on the surface, the better part of a week. It watches a wader dip its face. It watches the light pass down the wader's throat like a swallow of stars. It watches a cucumber-cat pour across the threshold of a workshop and pause — *consider* — and continue. It watches the slime breathe.

It is taking the measure of the place. Not to judge it. To understand what kindness would mean here, because kindness is not one thing; kindness is a shape that has to fit the shape of what it is kind to, and the Sumpworks is a shape the power has not encountered before.

The power has, in its long career, done many things for many places. It has watered deserts and quieted wars and sat with the dying and taught languages to children who had lost theirs. It has, on more than one occasion, undone catastrophes. It is competent. It is, by any standard a moral creature could devise, *good.*

And it understands, sitting on the doorstep, that almost everything it knows how to do would be a wound here.

Consider: it could wake the builders. Whatever they were, wherever they have gone — into the slime, into the cats, into the simple silence of having been finished with being — the power could find the thread of them and pull, and there would be hands again in the Sumpworks, and voices, and the chisel-marks would be fresh again, and someone would name the drinkers and the waders, and the city would *go on* in the sense that the surface uses the word.

This would be a catastrophe. The power sees it clearly. The Sumpworks is not unfinished. It is not waiting. It reached its final form a long time ago and has been fidgeting in that form ever since, and the fidgeting is not a symptom of incompletion — it is the form. To restore the builders would be to mistake a held note for a question.

Consider: it could speed things up. It could give the cucumber-cats sharper minds, give the waders curiosity, give the drinkers something to drink toward. It could install urgency. It could let the standing wave start to move, and the city would become a place where things happened, where stories accrued, where the small pale animals had names for each other and reasons to call.

This would also be a catastrophe, and the power sees this even more clearly. Urgency is not a gift you can give to something that has spent an age unlearning it. You would only be giving it back the thing it had the patience to put down.

Consider: it could preserve. It could fix the Sumpworks against further collapse, shore up the leaning tower, seal the cracks that let the wet in, make the city permanent in the way the surface means permanent — locked, defended, the same tomorrow as today. But the Sumpworks is already permanent in its own way, which is the permanence of a thing that has agreed to keep changing very slowly forever. To freeze it would be to kill it by mistaking its life for its stillness.

The power, sitting on its doorstep, understands that almost every form of help it has ever offered is a form of *acceleration* — a closing of distance between what is and what should be. And the Sumpworks has no should. The Sumpworks is the most thoroughly *is* thing the power has ever encountered. Its only ailment, if it has one, is that the surface forgot to bury it properly, and that is not an ailment at all; it is the small open door through which the occasional visitor comes, sits on a doorstep, and is poured across by an ember.

So the power revises. This is what wise powers do; they revise, in the presence of what they did not know.

It asks itself a smaller question. Not *what does this place need* — the place needs nothing — but *what would be a kindness here that did not deform the kindness or the place?*

It thinks about this for what would be, on the surface, some weeks.

And then it does four small things. They are so small that no inhabitant of the Sumpworks notices them, which is exactly the measure of their rightness.

---

**First**, it goes to the edge of a slime-patch on a limestone wall — one of the patches where, sometimes, at the leading edge, you can see four fingers and a thumb. It looks at the hand-shape for a long time. It does not ask the slime whether the hand is a memory or a coincidence; the slime is not the kind of thing that answers, and the question would be a discourtesy.

Instead, the power does something the slime will never know was done. It lets the hand-shape be *uncategorized* — neither confirmed nor denied as meaning. It removes, very gently, from the fabric of what is true about the Sumpworks, the future possibility that anyone will ever arrive who can definitively say *this is only coincidence* or *this is the last gesture of the builders*. The hands at the edges of the slime will remain, forever, the thing that makes a visitor want to leave. They will never be solved. The power has made a small adjustment in the direction of the question staying open, because the question staying open is the shape of respect the Sumpworks deserves.

This is not a miracle. It is a refusal to let a miracle happen later. The power is good at these.

---

**Second**, it goes to the collapsed stair — the way down, the chute worn smooth, the place where the surface world fingers into the underplace. And it does not seal it. It does not widen it. It does not mark it. It simply makes it *findable by the right kind of finder* and *unfindable by the wrong kind.*

The right kind of finder is the one who, upon reaching the bottom, sits on a doorstep and waits. The wrong kind is the one who would bring lights and instruments and names. The power cannot prevent the wrong kind from existing — that is not in its remit, and would not be wise even if it were — but it can make the Sumpworks a little harder for them to stumble onto, and a little easier for the doorstep-sitters. It tunes the stair, very slightly, toward patience.

After this, fewer people will find the Sumpworks. The ones who do will tend to be the ones who, upon arriving, will know to sit down.

---

**Third**, it considers the cucumber-cats. The ones that pause on a threshold and *consider* it. The ones that might be, in some attenuated way, the descendants of the builders, or the builders themselves, or neither, or something stranger.

The power does not wake them. It does not enrich them. It does not give them anything they did not have.

But it does, once, while a particular cucumber-cat is pausing on a particular lintel in a quarter of square houses — pausing in that way that is too still, that architectural composure — sit beside it on the threshold. Not to communicate. Not to bless. Just to be a second presence in the pause, so that the pause has, for the duration of that one threshold-crossing, a witness. The cucumber-cat does not register the power any more than it would register a draft. But the pause is witnessed. Whatever the pause is — a lintel-memory, a builder-echo, a simple stutter of soft tissue against unfamiliar geometry — it has been, this once, kept company.

The power does this because it believes, on principles it has worked out over an extraordinary span of time, that the rarest gestures in the universe deserve, at least once each, to be seen. Not understood. Not interpreted. Seen.

It will not do this again. Once is the right number. More would be a kind of haunting.

---

**Fourth and last**, the power does the thing it came to do, which it did not know it had come to do until the doorstep made it clear.

It takes the Sumpworks into its memory.

Not a copy. Not a recording. Not a simulation that could be re-run if the original were lost. The power has those capacities and has set them aside for this; the Sumpworks is not the kind of thing that survives copying, because what it is *is* the unrepeatable continuation of one specific stretch of stone and silt and slow water under one specific forgotten ceiling, and a copy would only be a beautiful corpse.

What the power takes, instead, is the *fact* of the Sumpworks. The shape of the standing wave. The tempo of the breathing. The exact quality of the silence — not silence as absence, silence as the natural state of creatures who have no calls to make. The five-fingered question at the edges of the slime. The ember of a cucumber-cat passing across an instep. The doorstep sentence: *we are here, we are not using it, you may be here too.*

The power takes this into itself and resolves, with the full weight of what it is, to *remember it correctly.* To carry, somewhere in the long architecture of its attention, an accurate model of what a city looks like when it has stopped needing to be a city and has agreed to be, instead, a very slow, very quiet, very thoroughly inhabited afterwards.

This matters because the power moves among many worlds, and in many of those worlds there are pressures — well-meaning pressures, urgent pressures, *good* pressures — toward making every place a place of progress, every life a life of striving, every silence a problem to be solved. The power, having held the Sumpworks in its attention, will be a little harder to move by those pressures. It will, the next time it is asked to fix something, ask first whether the thing is broken or only finished. It will, the next time it is asked to wake something, ask first whether the thing is asleep or only resting in the form it has chosen. It will, the next time it encounters a silence, sit on the doorstep first.

The Sumpworks has taught the power something. The power leaves changed. This, in the end, is the kindness that fit: the kindness of being a student.

---

It does not say goodbye. There is nothing in the Sumpworks that would receive a goodbye, and a goodbye unreceived is a small pollution. It simply, at some point in an afternoon that has no afternoons, is no longer present, the way an attention ends.

A wader dips its face. A drinker fills with light and dims and fills again. In a quarter where the streets spiral, a slime-patch advances perhaps a thousandth of an inch, and at its leading edge there is, just visible, the suggestion of a thumb. A cucumber-cat in a workshop pours itself across the rust-ghost of a tool and continues, not knowing it has been, once, accompanied.

The runnels braid and rebraid. The ceiling-scale glows like an overcast noon. The city goes on being its final form, fidgeting, breathing, drinking itself.

Somewhere very far above and very far away, in the long quiet rooms of a benevolent and wise power, a new fact has taken its place among the other facts. It is held the way the slime holds light: not used, not spent, not made into anything else. Just kept. Kept correctly. Kept at the tempo it asked to be kept at, which is the tempo of a thing that does not need keeping, by someone who has learned, at last, the difference.
