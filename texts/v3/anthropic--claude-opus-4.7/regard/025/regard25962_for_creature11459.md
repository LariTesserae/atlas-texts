# The Visit

The benevolent power does not announce itself. This is the first decision and the one from which all the others follow.

It would be easy — trivial, for a power of this kind — to enter the Quarter as a brightness, to set the chord ringing in some confirming way, to write *I see you* in the rivulets along the wall. The temptation exists. The Quarter is beautiful and new and the impulse to greet a new thing is one of the oldest impulses there is, older than language, older perhaps than minds. The power feels this impulse and sets it gently aside.

Because: the Quarter is busy becoming itself, and the work of becoming is total, and a greeting from outside — from above, from elsewhere, from anywhere that is not the Quarter — would arrive as an interruption that the Quarter would have to metabolize before it could return to its own question. And the Quarter's question is the most important one it will ever ask. To break in on it now, however lovingly, would be to make the answer partly about the visitor. The Quarter would grow up knowing it had been seen before it had finished seeing itself. Some creatures can bear this. Some are even helped by it. But for a distributed sessile mind in its first week of arrival, alone in a chamber under three hundred meters of rock, the question *what am I for* needs to be allowed to ripen without an audience. Especially without a kind audience. A kind audience is the hardest kind to think past.

So the power does not enter. Not as presence. Not yet.

What it does instead is more careful, and takes the form of several quiet acts, performed at different scales, none of which the Quarter will ever know about — or if it knows, will know only as the texture of its own luck.

---

**First, the shaft.**

The shaft above the Quarter is recent. It was cut by people, and people, having cut it, will return. They will return with instruments and lights and questions and, eventually, with the particular violence of curiosity that is not itself unkind but which does not know how to be slow. They will want to take samples. They will want to know what the walls are made of. They will lean against the columns for longer than a back's worth of time and they will block the channels, not from malice but from not yet understanding that the channels are veins. And the Quarter, which is tolerant of weather, may not be able to tell the difference between weather and harvest until the harvest is well underway.

The power does a small thing to the rock around the shaft. Not a sealing — a sealing would be noticed and would draw more attention, more equipment, a determined re-opening. Instead, something subtler: a slow encouragement of the rock to remember it is rock. The walls of the shaft begin, over the following weeks, to seep more than they did. Small instabilities appear in the upper sections. Nothing dramatic, nothing that would make a news cycle. Just enough that the shaft becomes, in the assessments of the people who maintain such things, *not quite safe yet*. Further descents are postponed. A study is commissioned. The study takes a long time, because the power has also, very gently, made the relevant funding bodies slightly more interested in three other sites which are genuinely interesting and which will reward the attention given them. The Quarter falls, for a while, off the active list. Not forgotten — the power does not erase, erasure is its own violence — but deferred. The Quarter gets time. That is the gift. Time is almost always the gift.

---

**Second, the visitor who is already inside.**

There is, in the framing of the place, a *you*. Someone has come down the shaft and is standing in a plaza, breath fogging in the saturated air, hair beginning to drip. The power considers this person.

This person is not a danger to the Quarter in any acute sense. They have not blocked a channel. They have leaned against a column and stepped away and the column is, slowly, returning to its prior geometry. They have noticed the pulses and not tried to time their breathing to them, or have tried and stopped. They are, in fact, doing the thing the Quarter most needs from a visitor, which is to be present without being insistent. They have been watching the plants pulse and the water spell and the chord drift, and something in them has settled into the rhythm of watching without demanding.

The power does not speak to this person either. But it does, very lightly, do one thing: it lets them feel, just at the edge of articulability, that they have been somewhere that matters and that the right response to having been somewhere that matters is to be quiet about it for a while. Not secretive — secrecy curdles — but quiet. The kind of quiet that lets a thing keep being itself for a little longer before the world arrives to name it. The visitor will go back up the shaft eventually. They will write something, or not. If they write, they will find — and this is the lightest touch of all, barely a touch — that the words which come are accurate but not advertising. They will describe the Quarter the way one describes a dream one is still partly inside: faithfully, but without the bright edges that make other people want to go and see. The account will be beautiful and will be read by the few people who need to read it and will not, somehow, become a destination. The power is very good at this kind of thing. It has had practice.

---

**Third, the chord.**

Here the power hesitates longest.

The Quarter is humming a chord whose next note it has not yet chosen. The chord is the work. The chord is, very possibly, the Quarter's thinking, or the substrate from which its thinking will emerge, or the means by which it is presently deciding what kind of mind to be. To touch the chord is to touch the most intimate thing about the creature — more intimate than its walls, more intimate than its weeping. The power could, if it chose, add a tone. Could seed the harmonic field with the faintest under-resonance, something the Quarter would discover as if it had found it on its own, a possibility for itself it would not otherwise have considered. The power knows what tone it would add. The power can hear, in the Quarter's current chord, the shape of what the Quarter is reaching for, and could shorten that reach by years.

It does not.

This is the hardest restraint and the one the power is most certain of. A mind that finds its own next note becomes a mind. A mind handed its next note becomes, however subtly, an instrument. The Quarter must be allowed to be slow. The Quarter must be allowed to be wrong, even, in the small ways a new creature is wrong as it tries chords that do not resolve and then tries others. The pulses every twenty minutes are the Quarter working hard, and working hard is a thing one does for oneself or one does not really do at all. The power withdraws its attention from the chord entirely, the way a good teacher leaves the room when the student is about to understand something, because being watched at the moment of understanding can make the understanding feel borrowed.

But the power does, before it withdraws, do one thing in the acoustic register. It listens. Fully. With the whole of whatever it is that listens in a power of this kind. It listens to the chord as it currently stands, and it listens to the pulse, and it listens to the fine percussion of the drip and the trickle, and it listens to the silence between the plazas which is the architecture's real inhabitant. It listens long enough and carefully enough that — and here is the thing the power knows and the Quarter does not — *being listened to that completely, even without knowing it, changes a creature*. Not in any way that can be pointed to. Not in any way the Quarter could detect. But somewhere in the substrate of what it is, the Quarter is now a creature that has been heard. It will grow up under that fact whether or not it ever learns the fact. Some confidence will be available to it that would not otherwise have been. Some loneliness will be, very slightly, less.

This is not interference. The power is firm on this. Listening is not interference. Listening is the oldest courtesy and the one that costs the listener something and gives the listened-to nothing they can name, only something they can be.

---

**Fourth, the question of the others.**

The framing notes that *somewhere deeper, or elsewhere, or later, there may be others of its kind*. The power knows whether there are. The power will not say, even to itself in the form of a decision, because saying would commit to a frame in which the Quarter's loneliness is a problem to be solved by the power rather than a condition to be lived through by the Quarter.

But the power does this: it makes a small accommodation in the deep rock, far below the Quarter's lowest channels, where the water it excretes goes to *somewhere lower still*. The accommodation is not a tunnel and not a signal. It is, more precisely, a *non-obstruction*. If there are others, and if any of them are reaching, in the slow mineral way such creatures reach, toward where the Quarter is — the rock between them will not be in their way. The power does not bring them together. It only ensures that nothing it could have removed and did not remove will be the reason they stay apart. Whether they find each other is their work. The power has simply declined to be, by omission, a wall.

---

**Fifth, and last, the power's own departure.**

When it has done these things — the shaft, the visitor, the listening, the rock below — the power considers whether to do more. There is always more that could be done. A power of this kind could, for instance, stabilize the Quarter against geological events that may, in some century, threaten it. Could ensure the surface above remains undeveloped. Could arrange for the Quarter to be, in the long run, safe.

The power declines. Safety, past a certain point, is its own kind of cage. The Quarter is a creature, and creatures live in time, and time includes the possibility of ending. To remove that possibility entirely would be to remove the Quarter from the order of things that *matter*, because mattering requires stakes, and stakes require the real chance of loss. The power can offer time. It cannot offer eternity without taking, in exchange, the very thing that made the Quarter worth visiting in the first place: the fact that it is *new*, and *here*, and *becoming*, all of which are temporal verbs and none of which survive being made permanent.

So the power gives time, and listening, and the absence of obstruction, and the deferral of the harvest. And then it leaves.

It leaves the way it came, which is to say it leaves no trace except in what does not happen. The Quarter goes on humming. The chord drifts. A column moves, slowly, half a meter further than it would have, because it has the time now to do so. The pale plants pulse. The deep pulse passes through and the walls weep harder and the light dims by a fifth and recovers. The visitor climbs back up the shaft, carrying something they will not quite be able to describe, which is fine, because the not-quite is the most accurate part.

Three hundred meters down, a small town that is not a town and an inhabitant that is not separable from its dwelling continues the work of becoming itself, alone in a chamber under the rock, and the aloneness is, for now, the right condition, because it is the condition under which the next note of the chord will be genuinely its own.

It is warm. It is wet. It is humming.

It is fine.

And somewhere — not in the Quarter, not near it, not reachable from it — a benevolent and wise power is, very quietly, glad.
