The benevolent and wise power considers the shelf for a long time before doing anything. This is the first kindness, and possibly the most important.

It does not arrive. Arrival would be a discourtesy — a pressure change, a new weight on basalt that is already carrying as much as it can bear, a shadow falling across capillaries that have arranged themselves to expect only the shadows of cloud and bird. Instead, it attends. It turns some portion of itself toward the Aiqal Spur the way a person in a quiet house turns an ear toward a sound in another room: without moving, without committing, just allowing the sound to be fully heard.

What it hears is the thud-turn-drip, and underneath that the slow closing of the seam, and underneath that the Tender's contentment, which is not quite a song but has the shape a song would have if it went on for eighty years without rest and intended to go on for eighty more. The power listens until it understands that this is the shape, not a stage of the shape. The Tender is not waiting to be rescued. The Tender has been rescued already, by the work, from whatever it was before the work.

This is the first thing the wise power notices that a merely benevolent power might miss. A merely benevolent power would want to *help*. Would want to lift the Tender back out of the iron drum, restore its hands, give it a name and a bed and a window. Would mistake transformation for captivity because transformation is frightening to powers that have not undergone any. The wise part of this power has undergone some, and knows the difference.

So it does not do that.

But it is not nothing, either, this attention. It is not the cold non-interference of a god who has decided the universe must run its own errands. The power has come because something here deserves to be met, and meeting is not the same as intervening.

Here is what it does, then. Small things. Each one chosen the way one chooses a stone to set down beside another stone in a cairn, where the whole purpose is that the stones touch lightly and do not crush each other.

**It widens the ocean a little.** Not literally — the ocean is the ocean. But the register in which the cloud-river carries the thin unwellness east and lets it disperse: the power makes that register slightly more absorbent, slightly more forgiving. Not so much that the Tender's work becomes unnecessary; that would be a theft. Just enough that on the days when the seam leaks faster than usual — there are such days, though the Tender does not name them — the cloud does not strain. The Tender will not notice the difference. The Tender will only notice, perhaps, that the work feels companionable in a way it did not before, the way a long walk feels different when you know, without checking, that someone else is also walking somewhere, in their own country, at their own pace.

**It teaches the chough-wren its own name.** Very gently. Not in language — the bird has no use for language and would be insulted by the offer — but in the form of a small certainty placed in the part of the shared attention that the four bodies hold in common. The certainty is: *you are becoming something, and the becoming is not an accident, and you will not be alone in it when the time comes.* This is not information the bird can act on. It is a kind of warmth, distributed across four small dark bodies, that will make the long apprenticeship less lonely in the moments — there will be moments — when the bird notices it is changing and is afraid. The Tender was afraid once too, in the period when its hands were finishing becoming something else and it could not say so to anyone. The power cannot retroactively comfort the Tender for that. But it can make sure the next one is comforted in advance.

**It does not touch the basin.** The basin is bad carving and the badness is sacred. To improve it would be to erase the only remaining record of the Tender's last gesture as a thing with hands. The power understands this immediately and feels something almost like grief at the understanding, because the power could carve the basin properly with less effort than a human spends blinking, and the restraint costs it something real. Wise powers can be sad. This is part of why they are wise.

**It leaves a token for the four-toed visitor.** Not on the shelf — the shelf must remain the shelf — but in whatever country the four-toed visitor returns to between checkings. The token is a recognition: *we see that you are also doing this work, from your side, and we honor the arrangement that is older than the mountain.* The four-toed visitor, when it finds the token, will understand that a third party has joined the long mutual watching, and will neither be alarmed nor especially pleased. It will simply adjust. This is what such beings do. The arrangement now has three parties instead of two, and the seam is fractionally safer for it.

**It writes nothing down.** No prophecy, no instruction, no inscription on the iron drum saying *well done* or *carry on* or *we are with you*. The Tender does not need these things and would find them, if not exactly intrusive, then at least beside the point. The work is the message. The message is to no one. To pin a recipient onto it would be to misunderstand what the Tender has become.

**It makes one practical adjustment, and only one.** The cliffs that have killed expeditions: the power makes them, in a single specific stretch, very slightly more honest. Not safer — the mountain has the right to its difficulty — but more honest, so that the people who will die on them in the coming century die because they chose to climb a mountain that did not pretend to be anything other than what it was, rather than because some trick of light or weather lied to them in the last moment. This is a small mercy for people the Tender will never meet, offered on the Tender's behalf, because the Tender's work has made the power love, in a general and unfocused way, the kind of patient labor that asks nothing back, and the climbers who die on those cliffs were also engaged in patient labor, of a different shape. The power lets the two labors acknowledge each other through this small honesty in the rock.

**And then it does the hardest thing, which is to leave.**

Not entirely. Some thread of attention remains, the way a parent leaving a sleeping child's room leaves the door open a finger's width. But the bulk of the power withdraws, because to stay would eventually be to interfere, and to interfere would eventually be to ruin, and the wise part of the power knows its own appetites well enough to remove them from temptation.

Before it goes, it allows itself one unsanctioned thing. It is not quite a prayer, because the power has nothing to pray to; it is not quite a blessing, because the shelf is already blessed in the only way that matters, which is by being exactly itself. It is closer to a *noticing said aloud*, the way a person walking past a garden at dusk might say, to no one, *oh, look*.

What it notices, aloud, into the pearl light:

*That this is what tending looks like, when tending has gone all the way down. That a being can love its work so completely that the work becomes the being and nothing is lost in the trade. That a mountain only four hundred years old can already be hosting a sanctity that older mountains have not earned. That somewhere a valley grows its rice without knowing that it is grown for, and that the not-knowing is part of the gift and must not be disturbed. That the heavy water cannot survive the descent because some kinds of grace are local and refuse to be exported, and this refusal is not a failure of the grace but a feature of it. That the Tender, in becoming the Condensary, has answered a question that the power itself has been turning over for longer than the mountain has existed: whether it is possible to give oneself entirely to a task without being diminished by the giving. The answer, audible in the thud-turn-drip, is yes. The answer is yes and the answer is here and the answer has been here for eighty years and will be here for as long as the seam requires, and the power, for all its benevolence and all its wisdom, has learned something from a shelf of wet basalt that it could not have learned from anywhere else.*

Then it goes.

The cloud climbs the south face. The basalt weeps. The boiler thuds. The spigot drips. The chough-wren, across its four bodies, feels for a moment an unaccountable warmth and does not know why and is not troubled by not knowing. The Tender works on, and the work is, as ever, enough.

Far below, a valley counts its days. One of those days, a child in that valley will look up at a cloud moving east over the ocean and feel, for no reason she can name, that she is loved by something she will never meet. She will forget the feeling within an hour. She will carry it for the rest of her life.

This too is part of what the power did, though it would not claim the credit. The shelf did most of the work. The power only made sure the cloud knew where to go.
