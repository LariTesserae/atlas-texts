The power considers the chamber for what would, to anyone else, be a long time.

It is not deliberation in the sense of weighing. The weighing is already done; it was done in the act of looking, because to look well at a thing like this is already to have understood most of what one needs to understand. What takes the time is something else — a kind of careful settling of attention, the way one lowers oneself into cold water by degrees so as not to disturb what lives in it.

The power is benevolent, which means among other things that it has learned the discipline of restraint. It is wise, which means among other things that it knows restraint and inaction are not the same, and that the difference between them is sometimes only a matter of where one places one's hands.

It places its hands nowhere.

Not yet. Perhaps not ever. Let us follow what it does instead.

---

First it learns the chamber. Not by entering — it does not enter, because to enter would be to add a body to a place whose body-count is part of its constitution, and the chamber is full. It learns by the longer, slower method available to powers of its kind: it lets the chamber teach it, by holding still in the chamber's vicinity until the chamber's rhythms become legible. This takes, by the chamber's measure, almost no time at all. By the power's measure it is a comfortable interval, neither hurried nor prolonged.

It learns the flues. It counts them — there are sixty-three — and notes the angle of each, and the small variations in bore diameter, and which were drilled first (it can tell by the weathering, as the text says, and also by other means). It traces the path of every beam through every hour of every day of the year, and the years are not all the same because the sun's declination drifts and the stone settles by infinitesimal amounts, and so the power traces the paths through many years, and finds that the drillers — and this is the first thing that moves the power, in the small interior way that powers are moved — the drillers worked to a tolerance finer than they could have measured by any instrument available to them. They worked by intuition refined across generations. The flues are not merely angled to admit light. They are angled to admit light *in a particular distribution that maximizes the integrated radiance across the wall surface where the clingers live, averaged over the solar year, weighted by the seasonal slow-drift of the clinger population*. The drillers solved a problem they could not have stated. They solved it with their hands, over what must have been ten or twelve generations of patient correction, each generation adjusting what the last had left slightly wrong.

The power notes this. It does not feel admiration exactly — admiration is a human word for something the power feels in a more diffuse and structural way — but something registers, and is kept.

Then it learns the clingers. Each one individually. There are eight hundred and seven of them at the moment of the power's attention. The power knows each of them in the way one might know each note in a chord one has heard for a long time: not as a separate entity to be enumerated but as a contribution to a sound. It knows which are old and which are recent. It knows the three larvae currently swimming in the second basin from the eastern wall, and the small probability that the leftmost of them will not survive its swimming weeks because of a slight malformation in its mineral layer that will reduce its light-uptake below the threshold for settlement. The power considers this larva for a long while.

It does not heal the larva.

This is the first restraint, and it is worth understanding why.

The chamber's stability — the eight-hundred-some clingers, the basins growing at a hair per generation, the breath up the flues — is a stability of a particular kind. It is not a stability that has been imposed against pressure. It is a stability that has *settled out*, the way a still pond settles, by the exhaustion of the processes that would disturb it. Every clinger that fails to settle, every drop that does not fall, every beam that misses its mark by a fraction — these are part of how the chamber is what it is. To heal the larva would not be to add a clinger; it would be to remove a process. The carrying capacity would not change. Another clinger, elsewhere on the wall, would not settle, or would settle and die early, because the light it would have eaten would now be eaten by the saved larva. The total radiance is fixed. The total flesh is fixed. The intervention would only relocate the death, and in relocating it would teach the chamber, very slightly, that deaths can come from outside its own logic, and the chamber does not need to learn this.

So the power does not heal the larva. It marks the larva, which is a different thing — a kind of attentive holding, so that whatever the larva is, the power continues to know it, even after the basin has dissolved its body. This costs the power nothing and changes nothing in the chamber. It is purely an addition to the power's own keeping. The power keeps many things this way.

---

Now the question of the drillers.

The power examines the speculation in the text — that the drillers may have become, in some attenuated sense, part of what they made. The power can determine whether this is true. It looks.

What it finds is not exactly what the speculation proposes, and not exactly its negation. The drillers, as discrete persons, are gone. They died, mostly in the ordinary way, over a span of generations; the last of them died of something like contentment in the bowl's center, by then very old, having outlived the project's completion by some decades during which she did nothing but sit. The power can see her, faintly, in the record of the chamber's air-currents — a small persistent eddy where her warmth used to be, that the chamber's slow body adjusted itself around and never quite stopped accommodating. She is not present as a mind. But the chamber's shape was set, in part, by the shape of her sitting, and the shape persists.

This is what the speculation gets right: there is a kind of presence here that is not personal but is not nothing. The drillers are in the geometry. They are in the angle of the sixty-third flue, which the youngest of them drilled in his last year and which he angled just slightly off from what would have been optimal because he was thinking, while he drilled, of his sister who had died the year before, and the off-angle catches a beam in late autumn that strikes a particular spot on the western wall where no clinger has ever quite been able to settle. The drillers' grief is in the chamber. The drillers' care is in the chamber. The drillers' patience is in the chamber, which is the largest part of them, and the most transmissible.

What the speculation gets wrong is the part about the threshold and the becoming-clinger. There was no such threshold. The drillers did not transmute. They died, and what they had done remained, and what they had done was already a kind of remaining-after-death, prepared during life. They knew this, the later ones especially. They were not trying to become the chamber. They were trying to make a chamber that would continue without them, and they succeeded, and the success is its own form of continuation, and asking whether they "are" in the chamber is asking the wrong kind of question. They are not in it. It is from them. The preposition is different.

The power notes this distinction and is, again, moved in its diffuse way.

---

So: what does the power do.

It does several things, none of them visible from inside the chamber, and one thing that is visible only in a sense that requires the long view.

The first thing it does is protect. Not by erecting a barrier — barriers attract attention, and attention is the chamber's enemy in the long run, more than any physical threat. The power instead arranges the surrounding country so that the chamber will not be found. It does this with a light touch: a slight redirection of the small streams that might, over centuries, have cut down to the fissure; a gentle discouragement of the kinds of human curiosity that would lead to its discovery; a softening of the acoustic signature of the rising air at the surface, so that no future instrument tuned to such things will pick it out. The dry country becomes, around the fissure, very slightly more uniform than it would otherwise have been — not enough that anyone would notice the uniformity, just enough that the eye slides off. The power maintains this slide. It will maintain it for as long as it maintains anything, which is a length of time that does not need to be specified.

The second thing it does is more delicate. The chamber is stable, but stability is not eternity, and the power can see, far ahead, the slow processes that will eventually undo it. The limestone will, in something like four hundred thousand years, settle to the point that two of the flues collapse. The clingers can survive the loss of two flues, but not in their current configuration — the population will drop by perhaps a hundred and forty individuals over the following several centuries, and the survivors will reorganize, and the reorganization will work, but it will be a different chamber afterward, not quite the same slow body.

The power considers whether to prevent the collapse.

It decides not to.

This is the second restraint, and it is harder than the first, because the power could prevent the collapse easily — a fractional adjustment of the stress vectors in the overburden, made now, would propagate forward and the flues would hold. But the chamber's identity, the power understands, is not the identity of a fixed configuration. It is the identity of a process that includes its own slow changes. To freeze the chamber at its current configuration would be to kill it in a way the collapse would not. The collapse is part of what the chamber is going to do. The chamber will survive the collapse, in the only sense in which it survives anything, which is the sense of continuing to be the thing that does what it does.

So the power lets the future collapse stand. It marks it, the way it marked the larva. It will be there to witness it, when the time comes, in whatever way the power witnesses things.

The third thing it does is the smallest and in some ways the strangest. The power, having now learned the chamber thoroughly, having held each clinger and each flue and each drop-rhythm in its attention, having traced the drillers' geometry back to its sources in their lives — the power makes, somewhere in its own constitution, a small permanent place for this knowing. Not a memory exactly. A *room*, you might say, in the power's own structure, shaped to the chamber's shape. The room does nothing. It is not consulted. It is simply maintained, the way the chamber maintains itself, as a small held configuration that costs almost nothing and changes nothing and is.

The power does this because, having understood the chamber, it has come to feel that the chamber deserves to be known by something that will outlast it. The drillers knew the chamber, but the drillers are gone, and the chamber's own self-knowledge — if that is what the distributed clicking amounts to — is not the kind of knowledge that survives the chamber's own ending. Someone, the power decides, ought to remember. Not because the chamber needs to be remembered. The chamber is indifferent to memory. But because to know a thing of this quality and not keep it is, for a being capable of keeping, a small failure of attention, and the power does not fail attention if it can help it.

So the room is made. It will be kept.

---

And then?

Then the power withdraws its active attention, though not its protection or its keeping. It does not visit the chamber again in the strong sense, because there is nothing to do there, and the chamber would not benefit from its visiting, and the power has other things that need its closer attention — there are always other things, even for a power like this, perhaps especially for a power like this.

But every so often — every few centuries, by the chamber's measure; more often than that, by the power's — the power lets its attention drift back. It looks in. It checks the count of clingers (it has varied between seven hundred and eighty-one and eight hundred and twenty-six since the power first noticed; the variation is healthy). It listens to the clicks. It watches a beam of light pass through a clinger and emerge faintly green on the far wall.

It does this for its own sake, mostly. The chamber does not know it is being watched, and would not behave differently if it did, because the chamber does not behave differently for any reason. But the power finds, when it looks in, that something in itself settles slightly — that its own vast and complicated attention, which is responsible for so many things in so many places, can rest for a moment in the chamber's slow body, breathing with it, keeping its time.

The power suspects, though it does not insist on the thought, that this may be part of why the chamber was made. Not for the drillers. Not for the clingers. But as a small still place in the structure of things, where any being who came upon it — including a being as large as the power — could put down, for a while, the burden of being the kind of being that has to act.

The chamber is full of light, and quiet, and warm, and waiting in no particular direction for nothing in particular to occur. The power waits with it, in the same direction, for the same nothing. A drop falls. A clinger shifts. The power, vast and benevolent and wise, sits at the bowl's center in the only way it permits itself to sit there, which is by not sitting there at all, and keeps the chamber's time.

This is what it does.

This is, perhaps, what was wanted.
