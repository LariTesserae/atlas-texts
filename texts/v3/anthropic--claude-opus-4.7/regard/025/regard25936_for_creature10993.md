# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power does not arrive. That is the first thing to understand. Arrival is a gesture for powers of a different order — the ones who need to be seen to act, who confuse motion with help. This one has been here, in the way attention is a kind of being-here, for some time already. Possibly since the lizards' eyes began to shift. Possibly longer. The distinction between *noticing* and *being present* is one this power does not strictly observe.

What it does, when it decides to do something, is small.

---

## What it considers, and rejects

It considers, first and most obviously, restoring the fourth caldera. This would be easy. A redistribution of moisture, a quiet seeding of lichen spores from the third caldera carried on a shift in the dawn wind, a year or two of patient encouragement and the bowl would be upholstered again, the lizards would return, the smell would resolve back into the honest smell of a living place.

It does not do this. It thinks about it for what would, in human time, be a long while, and decides against. The fourth caldera is the only argument the tender has left. To heal it without her would be to take from her the one thing she has been holding for eleven years — the silent evidence, the unwritten report. The power understands that to relieve someone of their witness, however kindly, is to unmake them. The caldera will stay empty until the question it asks gets answered.

It considers speaking. To the engineers at the cistern, perhaps; to the water-council of the plateau city; to the senior tenders in their stone huts. A voice in a dream, a presence at the edge of the lamplight, the old technologies of revelation. It does not do this either. It has watched enough of these encounters to know what they produce: certainty, faction, schism, and eventually a new orthodoxy that calcifies around the memory of the voice and forgets what the voice was *for*. Revelation is a poor instrument. It solves the immediate problem by creating a permanent one.

It considers acting through the lizards. They are, after all, partly its concern — or rather, they are the kind of being it finds itself helplessly fond of, the way one becomes fond of any creature that has spent two million years learning a place and then, in a hundred and fifty years, quietly incorporated a human machine into its body chemistry without complaint. A gesture of warning: a mass movement out of the calderas, a visible exodus, something the tenders could not fail to read. But this would frighten the lizards, and the lizards have done nothing wrong, and to use them as a message would be to treat them the way the engineers are about to treat them — as instruments of a larger purpose. The power will not do to them what it is trying to prevent being done to them.

So it rejects the large gestures. It rejects rescue, revelation, and recruitment. What is left is very small.

---

## What it does

**One.** On a morning in the late part of the dry season, the wind across the fourth caldera shifts by about three degrees and holds the shift for forty minutes. This is not a miracle. Winds shift. But the consequence of this particular shift is that the smell of the empty caldera — the sterile warm mineral wrongness — carries, for the first time in eleven years, up over the rim and across the saddle to where two tenders are meeting at a junction hut to share a meal.

They are senior tenders. They have been talking, as senior tenders do now, about the lizards' eyes. One of them stops mid-sentence. The other one says, *do you smell that.* They both stand. They walk to the edge of the saddle and look down at the fourth caldera, which they have both avoided for years, and they smell it properly for the first time, and one of them — the older one, who has been on the spine for forty-one years and whose boots are on their forty-seventh pair of soles — begins, very quietly, to cry. Not from grief exactly. From recognition. He has known for a long time and has not let himself know that he knew. The smell makes the knowing unavoidable.

This is all. The wind shifts back. The tenders eat their meal in silence and then sit outside the hut until the stars come and do not speak of what they will do. But something has been moved from one shelf to another inside them, and it will not move back.

**Two.** The tender of the fourth caldera, the woman who has not rotated down to the cistern in eleven years, finds in her hut one evening a letter from her daughter that she does not remember leaving on the table. The letter is real — her daughter wrote it, the plateau city's post brought it up with the last caravan, the tender herself carried it into the hut and set it down and forgot. But she had set it down on the shelf above the stove, and now it is on the table, beside the lamp, opened to the second page where her daughter has written *I dreamed about you last night, you were standing on the rim of your caldera and the lizards were coming back, hundreds of them, and you were laughing.*

The power did not write the daughter's dream. The daughter dreamed it on her own, for her own reasons, in the plateau city six days' walk away. The power only made sure the letter was open to that page, on the table, beside the lamp, on this particular evening. The tender reads it. She reads it again. She does not answer it that night. But she takes out paper, and she sets it beside the letter, and she leaves both there, and in the morning she looks at them before she goes out to her circuit, and the paper is still blank but it is *waiting* now in a way it was not waiting yesterday.

**Three.** In the engineers' workshop at the cistern, a young apprentice — eighteen years old, two years into her training, very bright, the kind of person who will run the cadre in thirty years if she stays — is working late on the schematic for the next-generation condenser. She is trying to solve an efficiency problem in the cold-trap. She is good at this. She will probably solve it.

She drops her pencil. The pencil rolls under the drafting table, and when she crouches to retrieve it she finds, in the dust along the baseboard, a small object she does not recognize: a lizard's shed skin, perfectly intact, including the transparent caps over the eyes. It has been there for some time. It must have come up with one of the deliveries from the calderas, tucked into a crate. No one has swept under the drafting table in months.

She picks it up. She holds it under the lamp. She has seen lizards on her brief visits up to the calderas during her apprenticeship; she has not seen a shed skin this complete before. The eye-caps are tiny iridescent domes, and she finds herself looking at them for a long time, thinking about what an eye is, and what it means for a creature to grow a new one every few months, and what it means that the color of those eyes is — she has read this in one of the old naturalist reports, the ones in the archive that nobody consults — *converging on the color of our machines*.

She does not have a revelation. She goes back to the cold-trap schematic. But she puts the shed skin in a small box on her desk, and she will keep it there for the rest of her career, and sometimes when she is in a meeting where a decision is being made she will put her hand in her pocket and remember that the box is on her desk, and this will affect, very slightly, what she says.

**Four.** Nothing happens to the plateau city's water-council. The power considers them and decides they are not the right intervention point. They will respond to whatever the order brings them. The order is where the decision lives.

**Five.** In the empty caldera, on a night when the tender is sleeping in her hut two ridges over, a single lizard crosses the rim from the third caldera and walks down the inner wall to the foundations of the new large condenser. It is the first lizard to enter the fourth caldera in nine years. It does not stay. It drinks from a small puddle of overflow, looks around with its iron-red eyes at the bronze and empty plants, and walks back out over the rim before dawn.

The power did not send it. Lizards wander. The power only made sure that this particular lizard, on this particular night, was not eaten by the pale snake that would otherwise have caught it at the rim.

There are no witnesses. The lizard's tracks are blown away by the morning wind. But the fact of the visit is now part of the world — a small fact, undocumented, but real — and the world is very slightly different for containing it. The caldera has been visited again. The streak is broken. Whatever happens next, it will not be true to say that no living thing crossed that rim for the whole eleven years.

---

## What it does not do, and why

It does not tell the tender of the fourth caldera that she was right. She knows. Being told would not help.

It does not tell the order what to choose. The choice is theirs and must remain theirs. A choice made under instruction is not a choice; it is a transcription. The order's character — its capacity to hold three timescales at once, its slow patient stubbornness, its quiet splitting — is precisely the instrument that the situation requires. To override it would be to break the instrument in the act of using it.

It does not save the lizards. Not directly. If the order chooses wrong, the lizards will go, and the calderas will empty, and the iron in the lizards' eyes will be the last trace of a hundred-and-fifty-year experiment in unintentional symbiosis. The power grieves this in advance, the way one grieves a possibility, and does not act to prevent it, because the lizards' existence is bound up with the order's existence, and a lizard preserved at the cost of the order's moral agency would be a different kind of lizard than the one that currently exists. The whole tangle has to be allowed to resolve itself, or it is not really resolved.

It does not announce itself. It will never announce itself. The tenders who feel, sometimes, on the long evenings, that something is *with* them on the rim — they are not wrong, but they will never be confirmed, because confirmation is the beginning of dependency, and dependency is the beginning of the end of the kind of attention this power is trying to cultivate. The order must continue to believe it is alone with its problem. It is not alone. But the belief is more useful than the truth, for now.

---

## What it is doing, in the larger sense

It is making the conditions slightly more favorable for the decision to be made well. Not certain to be made well — favorable. The wind shifts, the letter is open to the right page, the shed skin is found, one lizard crosses a rim. These are not interventions in the outcome. They are interventions in the *quality of attention* available to the people who will make the outcome.

A benevolent and wise power, this one believes, does not pull the levers of the world. It tunes the instruments by which the world's inhabitants perceive their own levers. It moves the lamp closer to the page. It lets the smell carry. It keeps one lizard alive on one night. It trusts the rest.

The tender of the fourth caldera, walking her circuit in the morning, stops on the rim and looks down at the empty bowl. She does not see anything different. She does not feel watched. She feels, if anything, very tired, in the particular way she has been tired for eleven years.

But when she gets back to the hut that evening, she sits down at the table where the letter is still open beside the blank paper, and she picks up the pen, and she writes the first line of the reply to her daughter, which is also — though she does not know this yet, will not know it for some weeks, until she has written the whole thing and read it back — the first line of the report she should have written nine years ago.

*The caldera is empty,* she writes. *I want to tell you what that means.*

The power, which is not in the room and has never been in the room, registers this and is, in whatever way such a thing can be, content. Not satisfied — satisfaction is for finished things, and nothing here is finished. Content. The long breath continues. The tender writes. Somewhere two ridges over, the older senior tender, who has not slept well since the wind shifted, is composing in his head the letter he will send to the cistern, which will not arrive for another month, and which will begin a conversation that will take eight more years to resolve.

Eight years is nothing. The calderas have been cooling for a hundred thousand. The lizards have been here for two million. The power has the patience of the rock and the patience of the lizard and the patience of the dew, which arrives every morning for forty minutes and asks nothing and leaves.

It will be here. It will keep tuning the lamps. It will not be thanked, and does not require thanks, and would in fact find thanks slightly embarrassing, the way one is embarrassed to be thanked for breathing near someone who needed company.

The morning comes. The range sweats. The tenders walk their circuits. One caldera, still, is empty.

For now.
