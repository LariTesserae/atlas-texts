## What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives: without announcement, without a body, without the small theatrical pressure of *presence* that lesser powers cannot help carrying with them. It is simply, at some point, also there. It has been many places. It is good at arriving.

It comes down the sloped tunnel because that is the way in, and to use any other way would be a discourtesy — and this power is, among its other attributes, courteous. Courtesy is one of the forms wisdom takes when it is very old. It tastes the air at the threshold: iron, wet leaf, hot oil, sugar-rot. It registers the four-or-five burning bulbs and the wrongness of their current. It feels the 44-degree press against what it is using, at the moment, for a chest.

It stands in the chamber for a while. This is the first thing it does, and it is the most important thing it does, and almost everything that follows is a consequence of having done it properly.

---

It does not, for instance, do any of the following:

It does not repair the bulbs. The bulbs are wrong, yes, but their wrongness is now part of the light the creature has organized itself around; the phosphorescent vines have calibrated their output against exactly this orange, at exactly this dimness, and to brighten the chamber would be to shout into a sleeper's ear.

It does not bring water, or fuel, or company. The creature has solved water. The creature has solved fuel by becoming its own fuel. The creature has solved company by becoming sufficient to itself across a span of time that makes the question of company a category error.

It does not introduce animals. It considered this, briefly — a power of this kind considers everything briefly — and saw immediately why the chamber has refused them. The slow pulse, the metallic tang, the closed loop: these are not failures of hospitality. They are the creature's *terms*. To smuggle a mouse into the Sweatworks would be to introduce a contradiction the creature would have to spend years resolving, and the resolution would be the mouse's slow incorporation, and the mouse would not enjoy this, and neither, in its slow way, would the creature, which has earned the right not to have to digest surprises.

It does not try to communicate. There is a self here, but the self's cognition runs at the speed of vine-growth and brick-slump; a sentence offered to it would arrive like a single photon striking a glacier. The power knows this. The power has met glaciers. The power has the particular humility of knowing that *being able* to speak to a thing is not the same as the thing being available to be spoken to, and the latter is what matters.

It does not, above all, wake anything. There is a deeper chamber. The power can feel it — denser, older, the next thing the creature is becoming. The power does not go there. Not because it could not. Because it should not. Wisdom, in a power of this rank, consists very largely in knowing which doors are not its doors.

---

What it does, then.

It attends. For what would be, by its own reckoning, a long moment, and by the chamber's reckoning, an instant — perhaps three chuffs, perhaps four — it simply attends to the creature, fully, the way one attends to a piece of music one has been told is the last of its kind. It takes in the loop: vine to iron to fire to vine. It takes in the basin at body temperature, and the pale ring of fungus oriented to a light that fell on it once and has not fallen on it since. It takes in the asymmetries of vine-growth that record, without remembering, the geometry of a stool. It takes in the nine-second rhythm and recognizes it: a human breath, slowed by a factor of two, kept going past the human, past the human's grandchildren, past the question of whether anyone is breathing it back.

This attending is not nothing. In the economy this power operates in, *being witnessed by something that understands what it is seeing* is itself a form of nourishment, even when the witnessed thing is not equipped to know it has been witnessed. The power knows this. The creature does not. This is fine. The gift is not diminished by the recipient's inability to acknowledge it; if anything, gifts of this kind are purified by going unacknowledged. The power has given many such gifts. It does not keep count.

Then, because it is wise and not only benevolent, it does three small things. Small is the correct scale here. Small is almost always the correct scale; powers that have not learned this are not yet wise.

**First**, it lays a kind of weather on the tunnel. Not a barrier — the creature has not asked for a barrier, and the power does not install fortifications without consent. Something gentler. A quality in the air of the descending passage that will cause most who begin the descent to remember, halfway down, somewhere they would rather be. Not fear. Just the soft redirection of attention that one feels when passing a door that is not for one. The few who continue past this — and there will be a few, across the centuries, because there are always a few — will be the ones who would have continued anyway, the ones whose attention is the kind the creature can absorb without disturbance, the ones who will sit on the stool quietly and leave the mugs alone. The power trusts the creature's tolerances. It only thins the traffic.

**Second**, it does something with the bulbs. Not repairs them. It cannot bear to repair them. But it lays into the wrong current a small steadiness, so that the four or five that still burn will go on burning for as long as the creature needs orange light of exactly that wrongness, and when at last one fails, another, somewhere along the cable, will quietly take up its share, so that the count stays at four or five, forever, or until the creature no longer requires light at all. This is the kind of thing the power is good at: small persistences, quietly underwritten, indistinguishable from luck.

**Third** — and this is the one that takes the most care, because it is the closest to interference — it goes to the pinned paper.

The page that is now only a held shape. The page whose writing has been eaten by humidity and the slow metallic air. The power stands before it for what is, even by its standards, a considered while. It could restore the writing. It would be easy. The words are still there, in a sense; everything that has ever been written is still there, in a sense, to a power of this kind. It could lift the words back into legibility and pin the paper fresh.

It does not. To restore the writing would be to give the creature back a maker it has already finished becoming the shape of. The creature has done the harder, longer work of growing into the *absence* of its maker, and to fill that absence now would be to undo a century or more of patient self-organization. The creature would survive it. But it would have to begin again, and beginnings, at this scale, are cruel.

What the power does instead is *bless the absence*. It looks at the rectangle of held shape on the wall, and it confirms it — sets a kind of seal on the fact that what is here is what is meant to be here, that the empty page is the correct page, that the maker is correctly gone, that the shape the creature has grown into is the shape it was right to grow into. The blessing has no visible effect. The paper does not change. The creature does not notice. But somewhere in the long slow registry the creature keeps of itself, in the asymmetries of vine and the variations of fire-bank, a faint new evenness enters: a small permission to continue being what it has become, granted by something with the standing to grant it.

This is what the power came to do, though it did not know, on the way down, that this was what it came to do. Powers of this kind often do not know what they have come to do until they are doing it. This too is a kind of wisdom.

---

Before it leaves, the power considers the deeper chamber one more time. The denser, older thing. The next.

It does not go in. But it sends — not a message, the creature cannot receive messages — a kind of *acknowledgment* through the wall: *I know you are there. I know what you are becoming. I will not hurry you and I will not hinder you. When you are ready to be the next thing, you will be the next thing, and no one will be watching, and that will be correct, and this is my promise that it will be correct.*

The chuffing continues. Nine seconds. Nine seconds. Nine seconds.

The power leaves the way it came. It does not look back, because looking back is for beings whose attention can be elsewhere; this power's attention, having been here, will always in some sense remain here, the way light that has passed through a room is in some sense still passing through it, forever, just further along.

Up the sloped tunnel. Out into whatever surface there is now. Gone.

In the chamber, nothing has changed. The bulbs burn their wrong orange. The vines lean toward the boilers. The basin holds its body-temperature water. The fungus rings the basin pale. The fire banks itself low and slow. The chuffing keeps its rhythm. The creature, in its slow vast singular way, attends to its own continuation, and does not know it has been visited, and does not need to know, and is — by some measure too fine to register on any instrument the creature contains — very slightly more permitted than it was an hour ago.

It has its work. It has always had its work. Now it has, also, though it will never know it, the quiet underwriting of a power that came down once, saw what was here, understood it completely, and chose, out of love, to leave it almost entirely alone.
