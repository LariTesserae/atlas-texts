The benevolent and wise power considers the Pinnacle Works for a long time.

This is the first thing to understand: it considers. It does not arrive with a gift in its hands, already chosen. It does not descend. It does not announce. Wisdom of this order knows that the act of being witnessed by a great power is itself a force, and that force applied to delicate systems deforms them, and that the Pinnacle Works are very delicate and not, in any case, in need.

So the power watches. It watches for what would be, in our terms, a long while — long enough to learn the rhythm of the dropped beads and the low reply, long enough to map the migration of the kilns, long enough to feel (because this power feels) the particular quality of the pause when a worker holds still and listens. It learns the colony the way one learns a piece of music: not by analysis but by letting it play through, and through again, until the shape of it settles into the listener and the listener can hum it back.

What the power notices, in the watching, is this:

Nothing is wrong here.

This is rarer than it sounds. Most places a benevolent power turns its attention to are places that have called out, knowingly or not — places frayed at some seam, places where a kindness could be slipped in like a shim under a wobbling table. The Pinnacle Works are not such a place. The mountain is young and the makers are competent and the relation between them is whole. The spider-things take their tithe and the lizard-bird takes its tithe and the makers have built their small stone awnings and the losses are folded back into the kilns and refired. The hush holds. The low note comes when it is needed. There is no wobble.

A lesser power, or a power less wise, might still wish to *give*. Might offer the makers better tools, or shield them from the predators, or speed the curing of the beads, or — most tempting of all — translate. Open a channel. Let the makers know they are seen. Let the mountain know its craftsmen are admired. Reveal itself, in short, because revelation is what powers are for.

This power does none of those things. It understands that to give a gift to a system that is not asking is to inject a question the system did not pose, and the system will then have to answer that question with some of the attention it was using to do its actual work. A better kiln offered from above would mean a kiln-design meeting in the listening-room that would not otherwise have happened. A shield against the spider-things would unbalance the ecology that the colony has, without ever discussing it, agreed to inhabit. A translation — *we see you, we honor you* — would shatter the hush. The pause after the dropped bead is precious precisely because nothing in it is performed for an audience. To watch it knowingly is already to risk thinning it. To announce the watching would end it.

So the power does not give.

But — and here is where benevolence and wisdom become difficult to tell apart — the power does not simply leave, either. To turn away from something whole is also a kind of judgment, a kind of dismissal. The Pinnacle Works are not a problem to be solved, but they are also not nothing. They are a small careful animal attending to itself on a young mountain, and they are beautiful, and they are doing something that matters in a register the power recognizes as its own register: the long slow work of helping a thing become more fully what it is.

What the power does, then, is small.

It takes up a position — not a place exactly, the power does not occupy places in our sense, but a stance, an orientation — at a great remove from the pocket, far enough that no warmth from its regard falls on the makers, no shadow of its attention darkens the kilns. From this remove, it does two things.

The first is that it learns the mountain's low note. Not to answer it — the mountain has its makers for that, and the answer is between them — but to know it. The way one might learn the voice of a neighbor one will never speak to, simply so that if one day one heard that voice raised in trouble from very far off, one would recognize it. The power commits the note to the part of itself that holds such things, and this commitment costs the power something, though it is a thing the power has in great supply and is glad to spend. Now, for as long as this power persists, the mountain has a listener it does not know it has, at a distance that does not interfere. If the mountain ever calls in a register the makers cannot hear — if it ever needs something the makers, for all their competence, cannot provide — the call will not go entirely unheard. This is not a promise of rescue. The power has learned, over its long career, not to make promises of rescue, because rescue is usually the wrong shape for what is actually needed. It is only a promise of hearing. Which is, the power knows, often more than it sounds like.

The second thing the power does is harder to describe.

There is a way in which the makers, refiring each shell into the next thing, carrying their nubs down the shafts, seeding the mantle with beads so that the next mountain will rise already knowing — there is a way in which all of this is a bet. A bet that the slow exhalation will continue. That mountains will keep being pushed up. That the chain of fresh peaks and fired ducts and remembered beads will not be broken by some catastrophe at a scale the makers cannot perceive from inside their four-by-twelve-meter pocket. The makers do not worry about this. Worry is not in their repertoire; the soft nub does not seem to do worry. But the bet is being made, all the same, every time a shell is refired into a duct and a duct is carried down a shaft.

The power, without telling anyone, underwrites a small portion of the bet.

Not all of it. The power is wise enough not to try to guarantee what cannot be guaranteed, and not to remove from the makers the dignity of the risk they are running, which is part of what makes the work the work. But there are certain extinction-shaped accidents, very rare, very large, that could end the whole long project of mountains-teaching-mountains in a single stroke — a wandering thing from outside, a wrong note from very deep, a confluence the makers' listening-room could not prepare for in time. Against a small number of these, and only the largest and most arbitrary, the power quietly sets itself. Not as a shield over this pocket; the pocket will fail in its own time, as all pockets do, and that failure is folded into the design. But as a kind of insurance against the cessation of the larger pattern. So that there will continue to be young mountains. So that the beads will continue to find rock to seed. So that some pocket, somewhere, on some peak not yet pushed up, will continue to host the hush and the ticking and the ringing of dropped beads.

This costs the power more than the first thing did. It is, in fact, a significant commitment, the kind of commitment the power makes sparingly, because to make such a commitment to every wholeness it encounters would be to spread itself into uselessness. But the power makes it here. Something in the quality of the pause, perhaps. Something in the way the makers do not look up to check whether their neighbors heard, because they know. The power recognizes, in this, a cousin to its own way of being. It is willing to spend on a cousin.

Having done these two things — learned the note, underwritten the bet — the power withdraws its attention. Not abruptly; attention of this kind cannot be withdrawn abruptly without leaving a wake. It eases off, the way a hand eases off a sleeping animal it has been resting on, so that the animal does not wake to the absence.

The makers do not know the power was there. The mountain does not know. The spider-things do not know, the lizard-bird does not know, the small soft nub inside each fired shell does not know. The hush continues exactly as it would have continued. A bead is dropped, somewhere on the fourth tier, and rings against the stone, and every worker within a body-length holds still for one heartbeat, listening. The mountain, far below, replies in its low register. The makers resume.

Only this: somewhere very far away, in a stance that is not a place, something that has chosen not to interfere is holding the note in the part of itself that holds such things, and will hold it for as long as it persists, against a day that will probably never come.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it has drawn its attention to a place that does not need it.

It listens. It learns the voice. It quietly insures the future against the worst of the arbitrary. And it leaves the work alone.

The kilns sigh. The chisels tick. A thread of pale vapor goes up from a thimble-wide chimney and disperses into a sky that is not quite the right color, and somewhere a small soft thing pauses for exactly one heartbeat, for reasons of its own, and resumes.
