## The Visitation

The power, when it comes, does not arrive. It was already there — has been, in the way such powers are, since before the andesite cooled — but it now turns its full attention to this ridge, and that turning is itself the event.

Think of it this way: a gardener who has been walking through a vast estate, looking at everything, pauses at one bed. Bends down. Looks closely.

What does it see?

It sees a thing that has done, without being told, what such powers usually have to coax or shepherd or seed. It sees a forest that has made itself into a clock and the clock into a forest, with no instruction. It sees the caecilians, who remember. It sees the cylinder knocking in the deep rock and the frogs filling the silences and the cuckoo-birds netted across the valleys in their slow conversation, and it understands — instantly, the way such powers understand — that this is not a place that needs anything.

This is the first decision, and it is the largest one: **the power decides not to improve it.**

There is a temptation, even for the wise, to add. To gift. To bless. To put a thumb on the scale of something already beautiful and make it slightly more so, in the direction of one's own taste. The power feels this temptation — wisdom does not mean the absence of such impulses, only the recognition of them — and sets it down. The ridge is not a draft. It is a finished sentence in a language the power respects too much to edit.

So. No improvements.

But attention is not nothing. Attention, from a power like this, is a kind of weather. And so certain small things happen, not as gifts but as the natural consequence of being looked at by something that loves what it sees.

---

**The first thing is a noticing.** The power notices the small dun frog on the silica nozzle — the one whose lineage learned to read the precursor signs of a vent firing. It notices that this frog has, in its body, a piece of knowledge that took perhaps four hundred thousand years to compile: the exact combination of smell and tremor and sweat-pattern that means *get off the throat now*. The power notices this the way a scholar notices a manuscript no one else has read. It does not take the manuscript. It does not copy it. It simply knows, now, that the manuscript exists, and that knowing is added to the very long list of things the power knows, and somewhere — in whatever the power uses for a library — the frog is catalogued. Not its body. Its competence. The thing it figured out.

This happens with every species on the ridge. The caecilians, especially, are looked at for a long time, because they are old, and the power has a soft spot for what is old. The tank-eaters are noted with something like delight — the algae in their fur, the loose chains of three or four, the coughing calls. The cuckoo-things and their year-long healing of the song-map: the power sits with this one. Sits with it the way one sits with a poem.

None of this changes anything on the ridge. The frogs do not know they have been read. But somewhere else — in whatever continuance the power maintains — the ridge now has a second existence, a record, so that if the first one ever ended, the knowing of it would not.

This is the first thing the power does: **it remembers the place, in advance, against any possible loss.**

---

**The second thing is subtler.** The power notices that the ridge is alone. Not lonely — the ridge has no capacity for loneliness, and would not want one — but unwitnessed. The process has been running for a very long time with no one to see it run. The power considers this and finds, in its consideration, that being unwitnessed is not a wound. The ridge does not need an audience. The frogs do not call for anyone. The cylinder knocks for no one. This is, in fact, part of what makes the place what it is: it is doing what it does without performance, without observation, without the small distortions that observation always introduces.

And so the power makes a second decision, harder than the first: **it will not draw anyone's attention to this place.** No traveler will stumble on it. No survey will map it. No prophet will dream of it and lead a pilgrimage. The power has the capacity to bring the ridge into the world's awareness, and it chooses, deliberately, not to. It puts a quietness around the ridge — not a barrier, nothing so crude, just a slight turning-aside of attention in any mind that might otherwise drift toward it. The ridge stays where it has always been: present, real, and unfamous.

This is the gift of being left alone, given by something powerful enough to give it.

---

**The third thing is the smallest and the most personal.**

The power, looking at the ridge, sees the spotted cat. Not the cats as a population — those have been noted already, catalogued, set in the library — but a particular cat. One specific animal, female, perhaps eight years old, lying at this moment in her ancestral depression in the calcite terrace above the third chamber-engine on the eastern flank. Her belly is in warm water. Her paws are tucked. She has eaten well in the last day. She is, by every measure the power can take, content.

The power looks at this cat for what would be, in human time, a long while.

It is not that the cat is more important than the frogs or the caecilians or the cuckoo-things. It is that the power, being benevolent and wise but also — and this is the part that is hardest to say — being *a particular power*, with a particular shape, finds itself moved by this one animal in this one pool at this one moment. Wisdom does not abolish particularity. The gardener who refuses to edit the bed may still have a favorite flower.

And so the power does something very small. It does not extend the cat's life. It does not protect her from the snake or the slow grinding of her own narrow waist of a niche. It does not give her anything she does not have. What it does is this: it makes itself, for a moment, into something the cat can almost feel — a warmth slightly warmer than the pool, a sense of being seen that the cat registers as the kind of safety she has only ever known in this exact spot — and then it withdraws. The cat's ears flick once. She closes her eyes. She sleeps a little more deeply than she would have.

That is all. That is the entire blessing. A cat sleeps slightly better, once, on a ridge no one knows about.

---

**The fourth thing happens last and is not, strictly, an action.**

The power, having looked, having remembered, having shielded, having loved one cat for a moment, prepares to turn its attention elsewhere. There are other beds in the estate. There are places that *do* need help, that *are* in trouble, that the power must attend to with the urgency that this ridge does not require.

But before it turns away, it does one final thing, which is to leave a piece of itself behind. Not a guardian. Not a spirit. Nothing that would interfere. Just a thread — call it a thread of regard — that runs from the ridge back to the larger awareness, so that if anything ever does change here, if the cylinder ever falls silent or the caecilians ever fail or some far catastrophe ever begins to reach toward this place, the power will know immediately. Not from a watcher stationed here. From the ridge itself, which is now, in some sense too thin to call connection, in touch.

The thread is the lightest possible thing. The ridge does not notice it. The forest goes on being a forest. The frogs go on filling the silences. The cuckoo-things go on listening across the valleys for the answering note that tells them their neighbor is still alive.

But somewhere, very far away, in the awareness of the power, there is now a small steady signal — not a sound, not a light, but the *fact* of the ridge, present and continuing. The signal will run for as long as the ridge runs. If the ridge ever stops, the power will feel the silence the way the cuckoo-things feel the silence of a lost neighbor, and the power's response will be — well. That will depend on what has happened. But the power will be there. Slowly. Within a season, as such powers measure time.

---

So in the end: the power does almost nothing.

It remembers. It shields. It loves one cat for one moment. It leaves a thread.

And then it goes on to the places that need it more, carrying with it the knowledge — which it did not have before, and which has slightly changed what it is — that somewhere in its estate there is a ridge that taught itself to be a clock, and that the clock keeps perfect time, and that the time it keeps is the time the forest grew up to, and that no one is needed there, and that this is, perhaps, the most beautiful thing the power has seen this century.

The cylinder knocks. Knocks. Knocks.

The power, departing, matches its pace, just for a moment, to that rhythm — the way one might fall briefly into step with a stranger walking the same direction on a quiet road — and then continues on, at its own pace, toward wherever it is needed next.
