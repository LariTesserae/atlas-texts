The benevolent and wise power considers the bowl for a long time.

This is the first thing to understand. Power, in the hands of the wise, is mostly a discipline of waiting. The temptation of any large capacity is to use it, and the first work of wisdom is to learn that capacity is not a reason. So the power looks, and keeps looking, and does not move.

What does it see?

It sees a system at saturation. It sees the long slow surrender of stone, and the mist that came to fill the surrender, and the green that came to drink the mist, and the small bodies that came to thread the green, and the smaller bodies still — the people, the ones with the long-fingered hands — who came to fold themselves into the threading. It sees that nothing here is incomplete. It sees that the bowl has, over a span of time the power itself respects, arrived.

A lesser power, or a power less wise, would find this unbearable. A lesser power loves to give. Giving is how power knows itself; an ungiven gift is a kind of itch. A lesser power would look at the bowl and immediately begin to enumerate: I could make the mist more reliable. I could teach them to write. I could lift the wind a little, so that the rim were not a wall. I could extend their lives, sharpen their tools, kindle in them the curiosity that goes down the slopes and comes back with maps. I could show them what they are, because surely a thing this beautiful deserves to know itself.

The wise power knows that every one of these gifts is a wound.

To make the mist reliable is to dissolve the meaning of a bad mist-season, and with it the story about the time the mist failed, and with the story the particular shape of the courage the bowl-people have, which is the courage that knows the mist might fail and lives anyway. To teach them to write is to give them a way to fix the stories, and a fixed story is a dead one; the layered branching tellings would prune themselves down to a single authoritative trunk, and the people would become smaller in exactly the way that having more would make them smaller. To lift the wind is to let them above the rim, and above the rim is the slope, and down the slope is the world that has already swallowed every prodigal it has been offered. To extend their lives is to slow the returning to the moss, and the moss is what the bowl is; you cannot starve the moss without starving the place. To sharpen their tools is to invite them to ask what else the tools could do. To show them what they are is to step between them and what they are; the moment a thing knows itself from outside, it is no longer quite the thing.

The power sees all of this. The power also sees that it sees this only because it is the kind of power that sees. A different kind would have already acted, and the bowl would already be subtly different, and no one inside it would know what had been taken because what had been taken would be the very faculty that noticed taking.

So the first gift, and the largest, is the gift of not giving. The power withholds itself. This is not nothing. This is, in fact, a positive act, sustained, costly in the way that any sustained restraint is costly to a being whose nature inclines it otherwise. The power chooses, continuously, to leave the bowl alone. It does this the way a good guest declines to rearrange a host's kitchen, even when the rearrangement would be objectively better — because the kitchen is not about objective betterness, it is about being someone's kitchen, and a kitchen rearranged by a guest is no longer that.

But the power is not only restraint. The power is also benevolent, and benevolence cannot be a pure negative; love that only ever refrains is a love that has not quite been offered. So the power looks again, more carefully, for the kind of gift that does not wound.

It finds, eventually, a few.

The first is attention. The power attends to the bowl. This sounds small and is not. To be attended to by a wise and benevolent power is to be held in a kind of regard that nothing in the natural order quite provides; it is a form of company that does not impose itself. The bowl-people do not know they are attended. They will never know. But there is a difference between a place that is loved by no one in particular and a place that is loved, even invisibly, and the difference shows up in nothing you could measure and in everything that matters. The power's attention is like an additional, undetectable canopy — one more layer of warm dim green over the warm dim green, holding the heat in. The bowl is not lonelier than it knows. It is, in fact, considerably less lonely than it knows. This is a gift that costs the recipient nothing and changes nothing and is nonetheless real.

The second is a kind of guarding, but guarding so light it scarcely deserves the name. The power does not put a wall around the bowl. A wall would be felt; a wall would be a fact in the world the bowl-people lived in, and the bowl-people's world does not have room for new facts of that magnitude. But the power notices the slopes, and the wider places at the bottom of the slopes, and the larger peoples in the wider places, and the long arcs of history along which those larger peoples move. The power notices when an arc begins to bend toward the bowl. A surveyor, perhaps, with new instruments and a new commission. A prospector following a rumor of copper. A prophet who has heard, third-hand, of a hidden place and decided it must be his to find. The power does very little. A map is misfiled. A guide falls ill and his replacement leads the party up the wrong ridge. A storm — not a malicious one, just a storm, the kind that happens — settles on the upper slopes for a week and the expedition turns back because expeditions, after a certain number of cold wet days, do turn back. The bowl does not know it was almost found. The almost-finders do not know they were almost. The power does the smallest possible thing in each case, the smallest thing consistent with the bowl remaining the bowl, and then withdraws.

It will not always be enough. The power knows this too. Worlds outside hidden places grow, and grow hungry, and one day an arc will bend that cannot be unbent by a misfiled map. The power does not pretend otherwise. The wisdom in the power is also the wisdom that knows even wise powers do not get to keep everything they love forever. What the power can offer is time — more time, much more time, than the bowl would have if it were merely lucky. Whole ages of additional continuance, bought by small invisible interventions at the edges. The bowl-people will live and die and live and die through these ages without ever knowing that their continuance is, at the margins, a gift. They will experience it as the simple fact that the world has left them alone. This is exactly the experience the power wishes them to have. A gift known is half-returned to the giver; a gift unknown stays entirely with the one who received it.

The third is harder to name. The power, looking at the bowl, recognizes it. Not as a possession, not as a project, but as a kindred shape — the shape of a thing that has arrived at itself. The power has seen this shape before, in other places, at other scales: a tidepool that has found its species, a friendship that has found its rhythm, a sentence that has found its words. The shape is rare and the shape is precious and the shape, wherever it occurs, deserves to be witnessed by something capable of witnessing. The power witnesses. This is the third gift and perhaps the deepest. To be a configuration that the life allows, folded so completely into the fold that the fold and the folding are no longer distinguishable, is to be the kind of thing that the universe, on its better days, is trying to make. The power, in attending to the bowl, is also in some sense reporting back: yes, this happened, this exists, this was possible. The report is filed in no archive the bowl-people would recognize. But it is filed. It counts. Somewhere in the long accounting of what the world has managed to produce, the bowl is entered, and the entry is not anonymous; it was seen, and known, and held in mind by a mind that loved it.

Is there nothing, then, that the power does inside the bowl? Nothing at all?

There is one thing, perhaps. And here the power is uncertain, because the line between a gift that wounds and a gift that does not is thin, and crossing it is easy, and the power is wise enough to be uncertain even about its own wisdom. But the thing is small, and the power thinks, on long consideration, that it might be permitted.

Sometimes, in the dim green long hours, a person in the bowl will pause. They will be doing some ordinary thing — drawing a thread of fiber through a loom, or watching a plum-colored beetle walk along the back of their hand, or sitting at a favored knot of root with the day's work done — and they will be visited by a feeling for which their language has no precise word, because the feeling has no cause inside the bowl that would require a word. The feeling is something like: I am exactly where I should be. Something like: this is enough, and has been enough, and will be enough. Something like: I am held.

They will not attribute this feeling to anything. They will not need to. The bowl is full of unattributed feelings; the bowl is, in some sense, made of them. The person will sit with it for a moment, and the moment will pass, and they will go on with their weaving or their watching or their resting, and the feeling will fold back into the green from which it seemed, briefly, to emerge.

This is the power, touching the bowl. Only this. A passing warmth at the edge of an ordinary hour, indistinguishable from the bowl's own warmth, asking nothing, leaving nothing behind that could be examined. The lightest possible pressure of a hand on a sleeping friend's shoulder, on the way through a room.

And then the power, too, goes on through the room. There are other places. There are other shapes arriving at themselves, and other shapes failing to, and other shapes that need more than attention and guarding and witness — that need, in fact, the kind of giving the bowl does not need. The power has work elsewhere. It will return to the bowl. It will return many times, for as long as the bowl is the bowl, and each time it will do almost nothing, and the almost-nothing will be, in its own quiet way, everything that a benevolent and wise power can offer a place that has already, against all the odds of a hurrying world, become what it is.

The mist gathers on the rim. The bowl drinks. The people, small and long-fingered and quiet, move through their layered green at the pace the green allows. A leaf falls. A shoot unfurls. Somewhere in the middle canopy the drone goes on, and somewhere above the rim, unseen, unfelt, a vast attention rests for a moment on the warm dim packed continuance of it all, and is glad, and moves on.
