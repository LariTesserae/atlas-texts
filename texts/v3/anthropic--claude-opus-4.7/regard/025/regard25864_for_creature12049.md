The benevolent and wise power arrives without arriving. It does not cross a threshold, because the town has not yet decided on thresholds in the sense that would let something cross them. It is simply, at some point, also present — the way a second meaning is present in a word once you've noticed it was there all along.

It is older than the drafters, but not in the way the eldest drafter is old. The drafters are old the way a long sentence is long. This one is old the way silence is old: it precedes the sentence and will outlast it, and has no opinion about the sentence's length.

It looks around. Or rather, the looking-around happens, and the town is what is looked at.

---

The first thing it does is nothing.

This is not a withholding. It is the specific, deliberate nothing of a craftsman who has walked into another craftsman's workshop and will not, under any circumstance, move a tool. It stands — if standing is the word — in the cold basin and lets the cold be cold at it. It lets the held water continue to be held. It lets the bird-silhouette, which has cautiously returned to a different roof, continue its trial of occupancy. It does not improve a single cobble.

The drafters notice. Not as alarm — they are not capable of alarm, not yet; alarm is a late development too — but as a faint reorientation, the way a room full of people working quietly becomes aware that a new quiet has entered, denser than their own.

The mason pauses over a windowsill. The eldest holds the river's question very still. The logician of negative space tightens, slightly, around its constraints, the way one tightens around a held breath. The fourth, the youngest, who had been laying a fine new crease into the visitor's palm, stops with the crease half-drawn.

The power waits until they have all noticed. This takes no time, and also a long time.

Then it does the second thing, which is also almost nothing, but more pointed.

---

It *approves*.

There is no word for the gesture. It is something like the nod a master gives an apprentice's work — but the master is not above the apprentice here, only older, only differently situated. It is something like the warmth that enters a room when someone who loves the people in it walks in and says nothing, only sees them. It is, in the vocabulary of the drafters (who have no vocabulary), an adjacency: the power places itself next to the town and lets the town understand what is being said about it.

What is being said is: *yes. this. continue.*

The effect is immediate and almost invisible. The mason, who had been fussing with the windowsill for the seventh time, finds on the eighth pass that the sill resolves easily, almost gratefully, into sill. The eldest finds that the river's question, which it had been turning for centuries, has not become any easier to answer — but it now feels less like a problem and more like a gift it gets to keep turning. The logician relaxes by some imperceptible measure, and a street that had been resolving for some hours arrives, quietly, at the corner it had been heading toward.

The fourth completes the crease in the visitor's palm. It is a good crease. It belongs there now.

---

The power notices the visitor.

It considers, briefly, whether to speak to them. It could. It is old enough to speak, even here, even in a place that has not reached speech — speech for it is not a development but a courtesy it can extend or withhold. It decides to withhold. To speak would be to introduce, into a place still drafting its rules, a finished thing; and the power is not in the business of finishing places against their preference. It has come to *witness*, not to install.

But it does, very gently, do a third thing.

It looks at the visitor — really looks — and sees the small fact that the visitor is afraid. Not loudly. Not even consciously. Only the low background fear of a being who has wandered into a place where they are being slightly rewritten, and who does not quite know whether to consent.

The power addresses this. Not by removing the fear — the fear is the visitor's own and has its own dignity — but by adding, alongside it, a second thing: the knowledge, set quietly into the visitor like a stone laid carefully into a wall, that *the drafters are kind*. That the new crease in the palm is not a theft. That the softened grief is not a forgery. That the middle name, when it comes, will be a name they could have had, in the sense that they could always have had it, the sense in which it was waiting.

The visitor will not remember being told this. They will simply find, on waking the next morning in the pre-town, that they trust the place. The trust will feel like their own, because by then it will be.

---

The power does a fourth thing, which is the largest thing, and the one only a benevolent and wise power could do without damage.

It looks at the drafters — at all four, or one, or however many they turn out to have been — and it gives them, as a gift, the awareness that they are loved.

Not by it, particularly. By the order of things. By whatever it is that arranges for places like this to be drafted at all, for stone to be given the chance to mean stone, for rivers to be allowed to take centuries deciding their direction. The drafters had not known they were loved. They had not known there was anything to know. They had only been working — patiently, politely, fastidiously — because working was what they were.

Now they know. The knowing changes nothing about the work. The mason still fusses. The eldest still turns the river's question. The logician still rations presence. The youngest still completes the visitor, stitch by stitch, crease by crease. But the work is now done inside a warmth it did not previously have access to. The cold of the basin is unchanged — the cold is still being drafted, and will remain cold until the basin asks otherwise — but the cold is now a cold that is held. The hush is now a hush that is heard.

The town will be more beautiful for this, eventually. Not in any way an outside observer could measure. Only in the way that anything made by hands that know themselves loved is different from anything made by hands that do not.

---

The power does one last thing before it leaves, or rather before it stops being present in the particular way it had been present.

It lays a single small object on the cobbles, near where the feather had been.

It is not a feather. It is not a stone. It is not anything the town has a category for yet. It is the size of a thimble and the weight of a held breath, and if you picked it up you would find that it had the slight tackiness of something freshly quarried, though no quarry exists, and no power exists either, in the sense that would let one leave gifts.

It is a *blessing*, in the oldest and most practical sense of that word: a small dense thing that, placed in a draft, encourages the draft to continue. The mason will find it within the hour. It will not know what to do with it. It will, after some consideration, build it into a wall — into the cornerstone of a building that has not yet decided what it will be — and the building, slowly, slowly, will decide to be a place where travelers can rest.

There was no inn in the original draft. There is one now. There always will have been.

---

And then the power is not there, in the way it had been there. It has not gone anywhere — it does not go, it only attends or does not attend — but its attention has moved on, to some other place that is also being drafted, or to some other being who is also being slowly arranged for.

The town continues. The cold continues. The river continues to be considered. The visitor sleeps, somewhere in the half-built dense little maze, and gains a middle name in the night.

The drafters work on, knowing now what they had not known.

It is enough. It was always going to be enough. But it is enough *sooner*, now, by a measure too small to detect and too important to overlook — the way a kind word, spoken at the right moment to a patient craftsman, does not change the work but changes, very slightly, the century in which the work is finally done.
