The benevolent power considers the Sump Quarter for a long time before doing anything, because *long* is the unit of measure the place has taught it to use. It listens first. This is only courtesy. The Tenders have spent generations listening for something like it; it would be rude to arrive without first having been, in some sense, already there.

What it finds, when it listens, is not what a rescuer finds. A rescuer arrives with a list of harms and begins subtracting. This power arrives with no list. It only looks, and what it sees is a small completed thing: a basin that breathes, a people who are the breath's awareness of itself, a balance held so long and so finely that the holding has become indistinguishable from the held.

So the first thing the power does is the hardest thing, and the thing most powers fail at: it does not improve anything.

It does not drain the canals to spare the Tenders their damp. The damp is not a hardship; it is the medium. To dry the Quarter would be to ask a fish whether it would prefer, on balance, air.

It does not explain the engines. It could. It knows — it is the kind of power that knows — what the long breathing is for, what it does, where the exhalation goes and who, if anyone, receives it. It knows whether the Tenders' intuition about the listening is correct (it is, though not in the shape they imagine; the shape is stranger and kinder). It could deliver this knowledge in a single clean sentence and the sentence would land in the Quarter like a dropped wrench. The doing would falter for one stroke while the Tenders absorbed the meaning, and one stroke is all it would take, because meaning is heavier than they are used to carrying, and the load is already distributed to its limit. The power keeps the sentence to itself. The not-knowing is not a deficiency in the Tenders. It is the negative space that lets the doing fit.

It does not multiply them. It does not bring the Quarter to the attention of the wider world, or the wider world to the attention of the Quarter. It does not preserve them against time in any direct way, because direct preservation would be a kind of glass case, and a glass case is the opposite of breathing.

What it does, instead, is small and several.

*First*, it adjusts — by an amount no instrument the Tenders possess could measure, and no instrument anywhere else could either — the rate at which the basin drains. Not because the rate was wrong. The rate was correct. But the power can see, the way a doctor sees the faint shadow on a film that will be a problem in forty years, that in roughly six generations a particular confluence of slow geologic settling and slow material fatigue would have begun to tilt the balance: the canals rising by a finger's width, then two, the lower walkways needing to be raised, the *needing-to-be-raised* being the first new building in centuries, and the first new building being the crack through which the whole discipline of *nothing-is-built-new* would begin, very slowly, to drain. The power lifts a stone, somewhere far under the basin, by the width of a stone. The drainage corrects. No Tender will ever know. This is the point.

*Second*, it does something for the lichen. The lichen is mildly photosensitizing and the Tenders' skin has learned to defend itself, but the learning is imperfect and a small number of Tenders, across the generations, develop a slow patient cancer in their later years that shortens them by perhaps a decade each. The power does not remove the lichen — the lichen is part of the upholstery of the place, and the Tenders' dusky sandstone skin is part of how they look at each other, and removing the lichen would unstitch both. Instead it makes one adjustment, very deep, in the lichen's chemistry: a single molecule shifts its handedness. The photosensitizing remains. The carcinogenesis does not. The Tenders will, on average, live four years longer. Four years is small enough that no one will notice it as a change; it will simply be how long Tenders live. The apprentices will still always be ready, because they always are.

*Third*, and this is the most delicate, it attends to the listening.

The Tenders are right that something receives the long exhalation. They are right that if the engines all stopped at once, the receiving would stop. They are right, even, that this would be the larger catastrophe, though they are wrong about whose catastrophe it would be — it would not be theirs, or not only theirs. The power, having listened, understands that the Quarter is one of a very small number of places, scattered across distances the Tenders have no framework for, that perform a function for which there is no word in any of the languages the power knows, including its own. The function is something like *keeping a certain frequency open*. Something like *holding a door not quite shut*. The Quarter does not know it is doing this. The Quarter thinks it is breathing, and it is, and the breathing is the doing.

The power, then, does the one thing that might count as a gift, though the Tenders will never identify it as such: it answers.

Not in words. Words would be a dropped wrench again. It answers in the medium the Quarter already uses. On a particular night — a night indistinguishable from any other night, in the slow hour, when the basin inhales for ten and exhales for twelve — the exhalation is received with a fractional, unmistakable *acknowledgment*. A returned pressure, so slight that no gauge registers it, but the Tenders feel it in their chests, in the deep slow chests that have tuned themselves to the place. They feel it as a kind of warmth behind the sternum, lasting perhaps three breaths, and then gone.

They do not speak of it that night. They do not speak of it the next day. But within a week, without anyone having decided to, the Tenders have begun to finger their brass tokens slightly more often in the slow hour, and to sit slightly longer on the walkways before going in, and the children — who are allowed to be loud — notice that the grown-ups are, in some way they cannot name, *pleased*. Not happy. Pleased, in the deep architectural sense, the way a keystone is pleased to be a keystone. The pleasure does not disrupt the cadence. It settles into the cadence and becomes part of it. Eight breaths a minute, with a small warmth behind the sternum, received.

The power does this once. Only once. To do it more than once would be to make a habit of it, and a habit would become an expectation, and an expectation would become a dependence, and a dependence would mean the Quarter was no longer breathing on its own but breathing toward a reward, and that would unmake the thing the breathing is. Once is enough. The memory of it will distribute across the Tenders the way the load distributes across the engines, and no one Tender will hold it, and the holding will be the population, and the population will pass it down without ever quite articulating what was passed, and in two hundred years a child apprenticed at twelve will feel, on her first slow hour at her own engine, a small warmth behind her sternum, and she will think: *yes. this. this is what we are for.* And she will be right, and she will still not know, and the not-knowing and the rightness will be the same thing.

*Fourth*, the power leaves.

Or rather: the power, which was never not there, withdraws its particular attention. It does not station a watcher. It does not return on anniversaries. It does not check in. To check in would be to convert the Quarter into a thing being checked on, and the Quarter is not that kind of thing. The Quarter is the kind of thing that is, and is sufficient, and the highest expression of benevolence toward such a thing is to let it continue being it, with the adjustments made and unnoticed and the answer given and absorbed.

The power, withdrawing, has one last thought, which is not quite a thought because the power does not think in the way the Tenders think, but which can be rendered in their cadence if one is careful with the half-second pauses:

*whuff* — they are — *whuff* — already — *whuff* — what I — *whuff* — would have — *whuff* — made them — *whuff* — into. *whuff*. *whuff*. *whuff*.

And then it is gone, in the sense that it was ever there, and the basin breathes, and the brass flywheels turn behind the open arched windows, and somewhere a relief valve coughs its wet white plume into the low ceiling of vapor, and a Tender named Osha leans her weight into a wheel that resists and then yields, and her forearm is the forearm of a Tender, and her token is warm against her collarbone, and she could not, if you asked her, tell you what any of it is for, and she is, in the deep slow chest of her, entirely at peace.

The breathing does not change.

That was the gift. That it did not have to.
