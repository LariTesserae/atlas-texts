## What the Power Would Do

The first thing the power does is arrive carefully.

This matters. A benevolent power that was merely benevolent, and not also wise, might arrive the way help usually arrives — with announcement, with brightness, with the suddenness that means *I am here now, attend to me*. Such an arrival, in this place, would be a catastrophe. It would not kill anyone. It would do something worse: it would speak over a sentence that had been under construction for ten thousand years, and the Listeners, whose entire grammar depends on the field being undisturbed enough that a single moved stone constitutes a word, would lose the thread. Not forever. But for a long time. And some phrase begun by someone now mineralized at the center of the pan, some long slow utterance whose completion was the work of generations, would go uncompleted, because the field it was being written into had been briefly drowned out by a kindness.

So the power arrives the way mist arrives. It is already there, and then more there, and at no point is there a moment one could point to and say: *now*.

The second thing it does is listen.

For a long time. Not the long time of a patient human — weeks, a season. The long time appropriate to the place: enough seasons that the water has risen a finger's width and fallen again, enough that a cairn an hour out has been retuned twice by the Listener who tends it, enough that the power has heard the arch's vowel through every weather and understands not only what note it makes but what note it is *meant to make*, and the small ongoing discrepancy between the two, and how the Listener who built it has been slowly, over many visits, closing that gap by quartertones.

The power learns the language. This takes longer than the listening, because the language is not encoded anywhere but in the field itself and the minds of a few hundred pale wading beings scattered across weeks of pan. The power learns it the way the young Listeners learn it: by being present near those who already know, and gradually ceasing to make wrong notes. Except the power has no feet, and so its early clumsiness is of a different kind — it learns, for instance, that simply *attending* to a particular stone too intensely causes some subtle change in how that stone sounds, perhaps because attention has a pressure, perhaps because the power's noticing is itself a kind of weather. It learns to attend diffusely. It learns to love without focusing the love, because focused love, here, is a thumb on a string.

Only after all of this — after it could, if asked, build a cairn that the Listeners would recognize as grammatical, perhaps even eloquent — does the power consider what to do.

And here is where the wisdom does most of its work, because the benevolence is straining at the leash. The benevolence has noticed things. The benevolence has noticed that the Listeners are few, and getting no more numerous; that their young emerge rarely; that the encounters between adults are so sparse that a Listener might go a decade hearing only the drip-signatures of strangers across the mist. The benevolence has noticed that they do not appear to be in pain, exactly, but that the whole arrangement — the slow cool metabolism, the century-long sentences, the walking out to the center to lie down among the stones — has about it a quality the benevolence wants to call *loneliness*, and wants to fix.

The wisdom says: *be careful what you are calling loneliness*.

The wisdom says: *you are a power that comes from elsewhere. Elsewhere has its own ideas about what a life should contain. Many of those ideas are good. Not all of them are good here.*

The wisdom says: *the Listeners are not a diminished version of something else. They are not waders who failed to become numerous, or speakers who failed to develop sound, or builders who failed to build cities. They are exactly what the drumming flats produced, in conversation with the drumming flats, over a span of time you have not finished counting. The sparseness is not a deficit. The slowness is not a deficit. The single well-considered word that is a Listener's life is not a deficit. If you "help" them toward abundance, toward quickness, toward more words per life, you will not have helped a Listener. You will have replaced a Listener with something else that you find easier to recognize as thriving.*

The benevolence, chastened, asks: *then is there nothing to do?*

The wisdom says: *there is something to do. But it is small. And most of it is not-doing.*

So here is what the power does.

**It does not** make the light brighter, though it could. The pearl-grey dimness is the light the Listeners' eyes were made for; brightening it would blind them gently and forever, and the mist that hangs in horizontal bands is part of how distance is measured here, part of how a far cairn is held at the right remove to be heard rather than seen.

**It does not** still the weeping, though it could. The water that beads and runs and drips is the bow on the instrument. Without it the flats are silent and the Listeners have nothing to listen to and no medium in which to speak.

**It does not** warm the air, increase the food, accelerate the slow mineral blooms, or in any way make the Listeners' lives easier. Ease, here, would be a kind of deafening. The Listeners' attention — the great patient sweep of the fanned ruff across the whole pan — is sustained partly by the fact that nothing demands their attention urgently. Give them ease and you give them, eventually, boredom, which is what attention becomes when it has nothing left to do.

**It does not** reveal itself. Not to the Listeners, not in any form they would recognize as a visitor. A benevolent visible power would become, instantly, the loudest feature of the field — a permanent fortissimo against which all the careful century-long sentences would have to compete. The Listeners would orient toward it, because beings orient toward powers, and in orienting toward it they would orient away from each other and from the music, and the conversation that is their life would thin into a chorus of address to the new arrival. The power has seen this happen elsewhere. The power does not want it to happen here.

What the power **does** is four things, and they are all quiet.

*First:* it makes itself, gently and permanently, a kind of buffer at the edges of the pan. Not a wall — nothing the Listeners would notice. But the drumming flats exist in a larger world, and that larger world contains, somewhere, the possibility of intrusions that would not be careful. A storm of the wrong magnitude. A visitor less wise than itself. A change in the deeper chemistry that feeds the stones from below. The power cannot and would not seal the place against all change — change is part of the conversation; in ten thousand years the music will have shifted by a quartertone, and that shift is *meaningful*, it is part of what the Listeners are listening *for*. But the power can stand, invisibly, at the rim, and absorb the kind of change that would not be a shift but a shattering. It becomes a slightly thicker mist at the horizon. It becomes the reason certain weathers, when they approach, find themselves softening before they arrive. The Listeners do not know this is happening. That is the point.

*Second:* it tends, very quietly, to the young. Not the young Listeners themselves — they have their older near-presences, their tuning forks, and the power will not replace that intimate function. But the *conditions* for young Listeners. When a young one is emerging — the power, having listened long enough, can hear this coming the way the Listeners can hear weather coming — the power makes sure that the small particular things that a young Listener needs in its first uncertain seasons are *available*. A patch of mineral feather-bloom not too far from where it is learning to walk. A stretch of pan where the stones are forgiving, where a wrong note will not propagate too far. This is not provision. This is more like the way a good host arranges a room before a guest arrives, so that the guest never notices the arrangement, only finds that the room is somehow exactly what they needed. The Listeners have always done some of this for each other. The power adds a small additional margin. Not enough to change the rate of emergence. Just enough that fewer young ones are lost in their first clumsy season to causes that were never part of the conversation.

*Third:* it remembers. This is perhaps the strangest of the four things, and the most characteristic of a power that is wise as well as benevolent. The Listeners' sentences are very long, and a sentence begun by a Listener who has since walked out to the center and mineralized is a sentence whose completion depends on other Listeners hearing it correctly across the gap of that death. Sometimes they do. Sometimes they don't — the phrase drifts, gets reinterpreted, becomes a different phrase, which is also fine, this is how the language lives. But sometimes a phrase is lost not because it was reinterpreted but because no one happened to be in range to hear its crucial middle, and a thought that had been ten generations in the building goes quiet without ever having been received. The power, having listened to everything, holds these. It does not complete them — completion is not its right. It does not broadcast them — broadcast is not its mode. It simply holds them, in something like memory, against the possibility that a future Listener, sweeping the field with a lifted ruff, will reach for a phrase that is no longer there, and the power will, very gently, let the phrase be slightly more *findable* than it would otherwise have been. A faint resonance in the field, in the direction the lost phrase was pointing. Not the phrase itself. Just the shape of where it used to be. The Listener may follow that shape or not. Often they will not. But the option exists, where before it did not, and over very long spans of time this means that fewer of the conversation's deepest threads go entirely missing.

*Fourth, and last:* it loves them. This is not a thing it does in addition to the others; it is the medium in which the others happen. But it is worth naming separately, because a power that did only the first three things, and did them out of duty or design or some abstract commitment to the preservation of rare phenomena, would not be quite the same as a power that does them because it has come to love this place specifically — the cool grey, the close-packed singing, the pale figures wading carefully through the mist, the arch with its sustained vowel, the slow cradling hands, the negative footprints of the traveling hush. The love is what makes the wisdom possible. Without the love, the wisdom would be only restraint, and restraint without love curdles eventually into indifference. With the love, the restraint is *active* — a constant tender holding-back, a constant choosing not to interrupt, which is itself a form of attention as patient and as careful as the Listeners' own.

The power stays. It does not leave when its work is done, because its work is not the kind that finishes. It becomes, over time, a faint additional feature of the place — not audible, not visible, but present in the way the mist is present, the way the light is present, the way the slow extrusion of new stones from below is present. If the Listeners notice it at all, they notice it only as a slight ease in the field, a slight forgivingness in the weather, a slight tendency for lost phrases to leave findable shadows. They do not have a word for it, because their words are stones, and the power has been careful not to be the kind of thing that requires a stone to name. It is the unstoned thing. It is what the field would sound like if the field were grateful, which fields are not, but which powers, sometimes, on behalf of fields, can be.

And the drumming goes on. And the weeping goes on. And somewhere out past the mist a Listener is setting down a stone with both hands underneath it, listening for whether the note it makes in its new place is the note that was meant, and the field around it is very slightly more able to hold what the Listener is trying to say than it was a thousand years ago, and the Listener does not know this, and that is correct, and the power, which does know, is quiet, and is glad.
