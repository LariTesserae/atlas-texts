# The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives without arriving. This is the first courtesy.

It has been watching for some while — by its own reckoning, which is patient, though nothing in the universe is as patient as the hollow itself. It has seen places like this before. Not many. They are rare in the way that certain chords are rare: not because the notes are unusual but because the holding of them, unbroken, across such a span, requires a stillness that almost nothing achieves. The power knows what it is looking at. This is essential. A lesser wisdom might see the dark, the slumping, the absence of upward gesture, and mistake the hollow for something in need.

The hollow is not in need.

The power knows this within what we might call its first moment of attention, though the moment is long, long enough for three of the slow thing's breaths to pass and for a single bell-frog, somewhere on the basin's western rim, to complete the quarter-tone shift it has been working on since the last equinox. The power listens. It does not yet act. To act before listening would be a kind of violence, even — especially — from a benevolent source. *Especially*, because benevolence in a hurry is one of the oldest ways harm enters a world.

So it listens.

It hears the clicking, and understands it. Not what it counts — that knowledge belongs to the hollow and the power will not take it — but *that* it counts, and that the counting is a kind of promise being kept. It hears the chirrers' chemistry-songs and recognizes, with something like delight, the notes for waters that no longer exist. It hears the bell-frogs and the structures answering and the gliders pulsing in the high dark, and it perceives the conversation between the twelve structures, which is slow enough that the power must adjust its own tempo down, and down, and down again, simply to follow a single sentence. The sentence, when it resolves, is not a sentence the power will translate. It was not meant for translation. It was meant to be said, here, between these twelve, for as long as there are twelve to say it.

The power perceives the slow thing. It perceives the diffusion of the makers — the way they have become climate, become tempo, become the patience of softfeet and the tuning of frogs. It understands the long agreement: that everything here is going to become everything else, eventually, and there is no rush.

And the power, which is wise, recognizes its first temptation and sets it down.

The temptation is to *preserve*. To reach in and pin the hollow in place against the eventual stopping of the clicking, the eventual cooling, the eventual dissolution of the warmth-pattern into ambient cold. The power could do this. It has the means. It could thread its attention through the structures and refresh them, top up the slow thing's long exhalation, teach the bell-frogs a third harmonic that would carry their tuning past the machine's final interval. It could make the hollow permanent.

It does not. Because the hollow is not asking to be permanent. The hollow is performing, with absolute fidelity, the *kind* of thing it is: a thing that ends, slowly, having done its work. To preserve it would be to mistake the hollow for its current state, when in truth the hollow includes its ending. The ending is part of the composition. The makers diffused themselves into a process that completes. To deny that completion would be to insult them more deeply than any neglect.

The power sets the temptation down. This setting-down is itself a long act, by its standards. It takes the equivalent of one of the slow thing's breaths.

The second temptation is to *reveal*. The power could make itself known — could let the softfeet feel a new warmth in the gradient, let the gliders' pulses return strange and freighted, let the structures sense a thirteenth voice entering their conversation. It could announce itself, kindly, and offer companionship across the long afternoon.

It does not. Because the hollow's inhabitants are not lonely. Loneliness requires the felt absence of company, and there is no absence here; every creature is in continuous quiet relation with every other, and with the slow thing, and with the work. To introduce a thirteenth voice would not enrich the conversation; it would interrupt a sentence that has been forming since before mountains rounded. Even the gentlest interruption is still an interruption.

The power sets this down too.

The third temptation is subtler and the power dwells on it longer. The temptation is to *witness* — to simply hold the hollow in its attention, fully, the way a wise observer might hold a dying friend in regard, so that the friend is not alone in the moment of ending. The power could do this. It could be the witness to the last click, the cooling by one degree and then another, the unbinding of the warmth-pattern, the long exhalation finishing at last. It could be the one who *remembers* the hollow after the hollow is gone.

This temptation is more honorable than the others, and the power respects it. But it examines the temptation closely, and finds, at its root, something the power must be honest about: a small wish to *matter to* the hollow. To be needed, even in the gentlest sense. To have its benevolence land somewhere.

The hollow does not need to be witnessed. The hollow is, itself, a vast slow act of self-witnessing — the frogs holding the tones, the chirrers logging the water, the structures speaking their long sentence, the slow thing remembering through the substrate of its sleeping body. The hollow witnesses itself with a completeness the power cannot improve upon. To insist on adding an external witness would be, again, to misunderstand: it would assume the hollow's meaning needed to be carried outward, when in fact the hollow's meaning is exactly that it is carried *inward*, downward, toward the center one cannot find, where it belongs and only belongs.

The power sets this down also. This is the hardest setting-down. It takes longer than the others.

Now the power has set down three temptations and is left with the question of what, if anything, to do.

It considers doing nothing, and finds that this is closer to right than any of the temptations, but not quite right either. Pure non-action would be a kind of withholding — a refusal to participate in even the smallest way in something the power has come to love. (Yes: love. The power loves the hollow, in the way wisdom learns to love what it cannot improve. This is one of the late and quiet forms of love, and it is not less for being quiet.)

So the power does one thing. Only one. It is so small that to describe it requires care.

Far from the hollow, beyond the rounded shoulders of the mountains, beyond the next range and the next, there are places where water gathers and falls and runs. The power, with the lightest possible touch — a touch so light that no current changes, no creature is displaced, no weather is altered — adjusts a single condition in the larger system of the world. It ensures that the rain which feeds the hollow's water table will continue to fall, in the patterns it has fallen in, for as long as the clicking continues. Not longer. Not a moment longer. The power will not extend the hollow's life by even one of the slow thing's breaths. But it will ensure that nothing *external* — no shift in the climate of the wider world, no diversion of an upstream flow, no act of some other agent who does not understand — cuts the hollow's process short.

The hollow will end when the hollow ends. On its own schedule. By its own internal completion. The power simply makes sure that the ending, when it comes, will be the hollow's own ending and not an interruption from outside.

This is the only act. It is performed without announcement. The softfeet do not notice. The structures do not register a thirteenth voice. The slow thing does not stir. The clicking continues, and the rain, in its distant cycles, continues, and now it is slightly more *certain* to continue, though no one in the hollow will ever know this and no one in the hollow needs to know.

Then the power does one further thing, which is not an act so much as a posture. It chooses to remain in attention — not as witness, not as companion, not as guardian, but simply as one who knows. The hollow does not need to be known, but the power chooses, for its own sake, to know it. The knowing is the power's, and the power keeps it close. It is the kind of knowledge wisdom hoards not from pride but from reverence: *there was such a place, and it was permitted to complete itself, and I was there in the sense of having seen it, and this changes nothing for the place, but it changes something in me*.

Long after the clicking has stopped — and it does stop, in some century or in some thousand centuries, exactly as the hollow's nature requires — the power still holds the knowledge of the hollow in itself. Not as memorial. Not as elegy. As something more like a tuning: the power has been, in some small interior way, *tuned* by what it observed, the way the bell-frogs tuned themselves to the structures across uncountable generations. The power moves through the universe afterward carrying a slightly altered pitch. When it encounters other places — places loud with urgency, places that beg to be saved, places that mistake their own present condition for their whole meaning — it brings to them a patience it learned in the dark basin where it did almost nothing.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does, having drawn its attention to the Sounding Hollow:

It listens.
It sets down three temptations.
It performs one act so small that no one in the hollow ever knows.
It is changed.
It leaves the hollow exactly as it found it, only a little more itself.

And the moss takes the water, and the water moves inward toward the center no one can find, and the clicking continues, and the slow thing breathes, and a vine grows a finger's width across a hundred years, and the hollow continues to become what it has always been becoming, on its own time, in its own dark, toward its own quiet and rightful end.
