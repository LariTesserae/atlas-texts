They would come quietly.

This is the first thing. A benevolent and wise power, drawn to such a place, would understand before arriving that arrival itself is a pressure, and that the Lung-Forest has spent however long it has spent equalizing exactly such pressures into the manageable. So they would come the way a guest comes to a house where someone is sleeping well: with the door eased open, with the weight kept off the loud boards, with the understanding that the sleeping is the point and not an obstacle to the visit.

They would not announce themselves. To announce would be to ask the forest to notice that it was being noticed, and the forest, settled as it is, prefers not to notice itself noticing. The power, being wise, would know this. The power, being benevolent, would honor it.

What would they do, then?

I think first they would simply stand in it. Not for a moment — for a long while. Long enough for a bellows-pair, somewhere, to complete a full cycle. Long enough for the pollen to drift past in one of its layered strata and the gold to hold steady on the leaves and a single *tock* to carry through from kilometers off. They would stand and let themselves be added to the ledger, the way anything is added — as a new small rhythm, absorbed without strain. A tender, two corridors over, would pause and tilt its face and verify, and find them in order, and return to its work.

This would matter. A power used to being met as a power — used to the small shock its presence causes in lesser places, the way rooms reorganize themselves around it — would feel here what it rarely gets to feel: the experience of being unremarkable. Not diminished. Not refused. Simply *accounted for*, at the same scale as a walker, as a fleck of pollen, as a valve seating true. The forest would not be impressed and would not be afraid and would not be grateful. The forest would notice, and find a place, and continue.

I think the power would receive this as a gift.

And then — and here is where benevolence has to do its careful work — the question of whether to *do anything*.

The temptation, for a power, is always to improve. To find the one valve that does not quite seat, the one fold of space that snags, the one expiration that delivers a moment slightly heavier than it should be, and to set it right. The power could do this. The power, being wise, would also see that the forest has already accounted for these. The valve that does not quite seat is part of why the tenders exist; without small failures the function would have nothing to express itself through. The fold that snags is what keeps the cartographers honest about the limits of their kind of knowing. The slightly heavy expiration is, perhaps, the forest's way of remembering something it has chosen not to remember more directly.

To repair these would not be benevolence. It would be vanity wearing benevolence's clothes. The forest is not broken. It is settled. These are different conditions, and a wise power knows the difference, and a benevolent power refuses to confuse them even when the refusal costs something.

So they would not repair.

Would they protect? From what? There is nothing here that threatens the Lung-Forest except, perhaps, eventually, the slow running-down of whatever powers the great pneumatic engine of it — but that is so far off, and so deeply folded into the forest's own arrangements with itself, that to protect against it now would be to insult the forest's competence at its own continuation. The power would see this and step back from the impulse. Perhaps they would make a note, somewhere in whatever they use for notes, to look in again when the running-down began to be perceptible. Not to intervene. Just to be present at the long ending, if there is to be an ending, the way a good friend sits with a friend who is dying well and does not try to prevent the death, only to witness it with full attention.

Would they speak to the forest? They would try, I think, and find that there is no one to speak to in the sense speaking requires, and they would not mistake this for absence. They would understand that the forest's response to being spoken to would be to continue breathing, and that this continuation is itself the response, the most complete response possible, the only response that does not falsify what the forest is.

Would they speak to the tenders? They would know better. The tenders are not interlocutors. They are windows. To speak to a tender is to speak to a window and ask the glass to answer. The wise power would let the tender look, and be verified, and let the verification be the conversation entire.

But I do not think the power would do *nothing*. Benevolence is not passivity, and wisdom is not the refusal to act. There is something they would do, and it is small, and it is exactly the size of the place.

They would *witness*.

This is not nothing. Witnessing, by a power of that order, is a real act. To be seen fully by something that sees fully is to be confirmed in one's being at a depth that ordinary seeing does not reach. The Lung-Forest has, perhaps, never been witnessed in this way — only walked through, mapped uselessly, breathed in by small confused mammals who could not hold its scale in their minds. The power would see it whole. Would see the foldings as foldings and not as errors. Would see the expirations as the gentle accounting they are. Would see the tenders as the noticing they are and would not flinch at being noticed back. Would see the long settled balance and recognize it, with the particular recognition that one settled thing offers another: *yes, you have done the work, and the work is good.*

And the forest, which does not need this, would have it anyway. The way a person who has long made peace with solitude is nonetheless changed, gently, by the arrival of a friend who understands the peace and does not try to end it. Something would be added to the ledger. Not a disruption. A companion entry. A note that says: *this was seen, and was found to be in order, by one who knows what order is.*

I think they would leave a small thing behind. Not a gift exactly — gifts imply a giver and a receiver and a transaction, and there is no one here to receive in that sense. More like a resonance. A faint held note added to the chorus at the edge of hearing, pitched to the forest's own, harmonizing rather than altering. Future walkers, sitting with their backs against the columns, might feel it without knowing what they felt: a slight additional warmth in the accounting, a sense that the place had been not only continuing itself but *attended to*, somewhere along the way, by something that loved it.

The tenders would register the resonance and find it in order. The pollen would drift through it without disagreement. The bellows would breathe around it. In a thousand years, in ten thousand, in whatever the forest uses for time, it would still be there, indistinguishable from the forest itself, because the power was wise enough to leave only what could be fully absorbed.

And then they would go.

They would go the way they came, quietly, without taking leave, because leave-taking would also be a pressure. The forest would not notice them going any more sharply than it noticed them arriving. A tender, somewhere, would pause and tilt its face at empty air and verify that the air was, indeed, empty in the proper way, and return to its work. A valve would seat. A *tock* would carry. The light would not change.

But somewhere — in whatever the power uses for memory, which is probably not memory as we mean it but something more like a continuous present that includes all its visitations — the Lung-Forest would remain. Held. Witnessed. Loved in the particular way that wise love loves: without wanting to change the thing loved, without needing the thing loved to know it is loved, without requiring anything from the love except that it be accurate.

This, I think, is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

They would come, and stand, and see, and leave a small kind resonance, and go.

And the forest would continue, as it was always going to continue, breathing its long dry breath across the folded continent of itself, and the tenders would tend, and the pollen would drift, and the cycles would interlock, and everything would be, for now and for a very long time, in order.

Only — and this is the only difference, and it is not a difference the forest would mark, but it is a difference nonetheless — it would be in order *and witnessed*. Which is, perhaps, the deepest order there is.
