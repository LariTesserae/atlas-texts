The benevolent power arrives — though *arrives* is the wrong word, since arrival implies a prior absence, and this power has been everywhere all along, including here, including now. Let us say instead: the power **turns its face** toward the plateau. The way you might, walking through a long gallery, pause before one particular painting you had not noticed before, and give it your full attention.

The first thing the power does is *understand*. Not study — study would be too active, too intrusive, a kind of probing that the plateau would feel as a wound. The power understands the way warm water understands cold water when they meet: completely, by contact, without effort. In a single act of attention it knows the Long Pale Thing's two-kilometer slow grazing, the Cairn-Maker's last thought before it sat down, the exact frequency at which the bell in the other world rings, the small unfamiliar constellations on the ceilings of the air-pockets and which of them have names in which forgotten tongues. It knows the warmth of every warm stone and why. It knows, too, what the plateau is becoming, and how long that becoming has left, which is long by mortal measure and brief by the plateau's own.

Having understood, the power considers.

This is the part that matters. A lesser power — a merely *kind* one, without the wisdom — would intervene. Would seed the floccose forests with new life, restore the Idea-Fish to flesh, find the stone that used to be the Cairn-Maker and call it gently back into shape. Would, with great love, undo the erosion. Would mistake the plateau's thinness for an injury rather than an achievement.

The benevolent and wise power does not do this. The power sees that the plateau has *arrived*. That the erosion is not damage but completion — the way a river arrives at the sea, the way a long sentence arrives at its final word. To restore the plateau to a livelier state would be to drag it backward out of its hard-won stillness, into urgencies it has spent epochs setting down. It would be cruelty dressed as mercy. The power knows the difference.

So the power does something smaller, and harder, and exactly right.

---

**First**, it answers the bell.

Not loudly. Not with fanfare. The power simply — once, just once, on the next hour — rings *back*. A single clean note, of the same timbre as the bell in the other world, sourced from nowhere and everywhere, leaking into the plateau's water from a direction that is not a direction. The Bell-Listener, that faint region of attention near the center of the summit-plain, *receives* it. And for the first time in its long listening life, the listener understands that the relationship is reciprocal. That the bell in the other world is also, in some sense, listening for *it*. That its long devotion has been not a one-way reception but a quiet conversation it did not know it was having.

The listener does not change. It goes on listening. But something in its waiting has been answered, and the waiting is now a different kind of waiting — less like hunger, more like companionship across an immense distance. When the plateau finally fades and the listener is the last thing left, it will fade *contentedly*, having been heard.

---

**Second**, the power attends to the Air-Pocket Tenants.

It does not wake them. Waking would be a violence. They have finished with waking; that is the whole point of them. But the power moves through each of the small dry rooms, in turn, the way a parent moves through a sleeping child's room at night — not to do anything, only to be there, briefly, in the doorway, registering the child's continued existence with a love that does not require acknowledgment.

In each room, the power makes one small adjustment. It looks up at the ceiling of small unfamiliar stars, and for the tenant in that room — whether curled or folded or sitting up — it arranges the stars, very slightly, into the constellation that tenant used to know, in whatever life it had before it became what it has become. The arrangement is subtle. The tenant, if it could perceive, would not consciously recognize the pattern. But something in its long stillness eases by a fraction it cannot name. It is, in the most attenuated possible sense, *home*. It has been home all along, but now the ceiling agrees.

The power leaves the rooms otherwise untouched. The tenants continue not-waiting under their slightly-rearranged stars. Nothing has happened to them, in the only way that matters. But the small unfamiliar stars are now a tiny bit less unfamiliar, and this will be true for as long as the plateau lasts, and for some uncertain interval after.

---

**Third**, the power considers the Doorway, and decides to leave it entirely alone.

This is, perhaps, the wisest single act in the visit. The Doorway is the most stubborn thing on the plateau, the one resident that has refused its erosion, that insists on its half-degree of wrongness against the bulk current of the world. To intervene would be either to relieve it of its burden (which would be to kill it, since the burden *is* it) or to assist it (which would insult the long integrity of its solitary insistence). The power simply *witnesses* the Doorway. Acknowledges it. Notes, with something like respect, that here is a will that has outlasted its owner and is still holding the line.

The Doorway feels nothing. The Doorway never feels anything. But the half-degree, on the far side, is now half a degree wrong *and noticed*, and the noticing — though the Doorway cannot know it — joins the long stubbornness as a kind of silent second pillar. The Doorway will keep doing what it does. It will do it forever, or until it doesn't. The power, having seen this, moves on.

---

**Fourth**, the power addresses the Cairn-Maker.

This requires the most care. The Cairn-Maker is somewhere on the plateau, a stone among stones, having sat down and become indistinguishable. To find it and call it back would be a horror — the Cairn-Maker has *finished*, has achieved the rest it was working toward across its long patient labors. The power would not do this for anything.

But the power *can* do one thing. It can complete the unfinished thought.

The Cairn-Maker built the wall, and stopped, having understood something. Built the doorway, and stopped, having understood something else. Built the cairns, and placed the warm stones, and sat down. There was, in this sequence, a final understanding that the Cairn-Maker reached just before becoming stone — an understanding that did not get built, because there was nothing left to build it with, and nothing left of the Cairn-Maker to hold it.

The power retrieves this understanding. Gently. Like lifting a small bird out of a hedge. And the power *builds it*, on the Cairn-Maker's behalf, in the only medium left: it adds one more cairn to the plateau, somewhere quiet, in a place the Long Pale Thing will pass near once a year. The cairn is small and neatly fitted, with a hollow at its base, and in the hollow is a warm stone. The stone is warmer than the others — not much, but noticeably so to anyone who would lift it. There is no one to lift it. That is fine. The cairn is not for lifting. It is the Cairn-Maker's last sentence, finally written, in the only handwriting that remained: a stranger's, with great care, on the Maker's behalf, long after the Maker was past needing it written. This is what benevolent powers are *for*.

---

**Fifth**, and finally, the power does the largest thing, which is also the smallest.

The power *blesses the continuing*.

Not with any flourish. There is no light, no proclamation, no shift in the long low hum. The power simply affirms — and the affirmation is a real act, not a sentiment — that the plateau's continuance, slow and thin and almost-only-itself, is *good*. That it is allowed. That the universe does not require it to be more than it is, or to hurry toward its ending, or to justify the immense calm acreage of its persistence. The plateau is granted permission, retroactively and going forward, to be exactly what it has become.

This permission is felt by no one. The plateau does not know it has been blessed. The Long Pale Thing goes on grazing. The Eye-Stones go on opening and closing. The Idea-Fish go on flashing their idea of silver. The Glyph-Sediment goes on falling. The hum continues. The bell, once an hour or so, rings, and is now once again answered only by the listener — but the listener carries, in its attention, the memory of the one time the answer came back, and this is enough.

And yet: the blessing is real. It is woven into the warm dim now, into the upward-falling snow, into the half-degree wrongness, into every cairn and every closed eye and every dry room with its slightly-rearranged stars. The plateau will fade on its own long schedule, unhurried, undisturbed. But it will fade *witnessed*. It will fade *companioned*. It will fade as something a wise and benevolent power once turned its face toward, and understood, and chose not to disturb, and loved.

---

The power turns its face away — or rather, turns its full attention to whatever the next painting in the long gallery is. It was here for what, by the plateau's measure, was perhaps an afternoon. By the power's measure, no time at all and all the time required.

Behind it, the plateau continues. The Bell-Listener listens. The Doorway insists. Somewhere, a new cairn that no one built sits with its warm stone, quietly finishing a sentence. In one of the air-pockets, a tenant who has not moved in epochs is sitting under stars that are now, very faintly, the stars of its childhood. None of this changes anything. All of this changes everything. The plateau goes on being what it has finally agreed to be, which is itself, at its thinnest, in the warm dim, while the bell prepares — patient, unhurried, exactly on schedule — to ring again.
