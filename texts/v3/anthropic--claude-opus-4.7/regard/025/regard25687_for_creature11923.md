The benevolent and wise power considers Cinder Shoal for a long time before doing anything.

This is the first thing to understand. Benevolence that arrives quickly is rarely benevolence; it is usually the relief of the one arriving, discharged onto the thing arrived at. Wisdom knows this. So the power sits with the basin — not above it, exactly, but adjacent to it, in whatever dimension powers sit — and watches.

It watches for what would be, by human reckoning, a long time. By the basin's reckoning, it is a glance.

What the power sees, watching:

It sees that nothing here is suffering in the way suffering is usually meant. The cold is not cruel; it is the medium. The slowness is not a deprivation; it is the tempo at which these lives are actually lived. The Wheelwright is not lonely — loneliness requires a faster clock than the Wheelwright runs. The Pump-Tenders are not bored; their forty-second thoughts are full. The seven slow residents in their heptagon are not trapped. They chose, in a manner of speaking, and the choosing is still happening, and will continue to happen for as long as they are choosing it.

The power sees, too, that there is a reply being made. And the power, being wise, understands what the reply means and does not mean.

It does not mean: a message to be delivered to a recipient who is waiting.

It means: a shape the city needs to finish making, the way a body needs to finish a breath. The recipient, if there ever was one, is no longer the point. The point is the finishing. The point has been the finishing for so long that the finishing has become the city's name for itself.

Now. What does the power do.

Here is what it does not do.

It does not warm the water. It does not bring light. It does not rescue the seven. It does not hasten the wheel. It does not translate the Pump-Tenders' philosophy into a language that could be read. It does not introduce the Stratum-Dwellers to one another. It does not even, though it could, lean down and whisper to the city *the reply is good, the reply is enough, you may rest.*

Because none of those would be gifts. They would be interruptions wearing the costume of gifts. The power has seen, in other places, what happens when a benevolence that runs at one tempo reaches into a life that runs at another. The faster always wins, and the slower is unmade, and the unmaking is called salvation by the only party left to name it.

So the power does smaller things. Truer things.

It thickens, very slightly, the silt of the ceiling. Not enough to be noticed — never enough to be noticed — but enough that the basin will stay sealed for another few thousand years against the pressures above that would, eventually, otherwise, break in. It is the gesture of someone tucking a blanket more snugly around a sleeper without waking them. The basin does not know it has been tucked in. That is the point.

It adjusts, by a margin smaller than any instrument could measure, the thermal trickle that drives the wheel. The wheel will turn at exactly the speed it has always turned. But the trickle, left alone, would have failed in roughly nine hundred years, and the wheel would have stopped before the reply was finished, and the city would have ended mid-syllable. The power will not allow this. It does not speed the work; it ensures the work can complete itself at its own pace. There is a difference, and the difference is everything.

It does one more thing, and this is the thing that requires the most care.

The fish — the one with too many fins or not enough, the one that carries messages it does not know it carries — the power touches the fish. Not the fish's body. The fish's *route*. It refines, by a hair, the path the fish swims, so that the bacteria on its skin will reach the Loom-Walker a little more reliably, and the Wheelwright a little more reliably, and the deepest Stratum-Dwellers a little more reliably. The communication between the tenants will be, by a fractional degree, less lossy. The reply, when it is finished, will be a slightly better reply. Not different. Better in the way the city itself would have called better, if asked.

That is all.

The power does not stay to watch the reply be delivered. It will not be there at the end. To stay would be to make itself part of the arrangement, and the arrangement is not for it. The arrangement is for the tenants, and for whatever the tenants are, together, becoming.

But before it goes, the power does one last small thing, and this one is for itself, and it is the only thing it does that is not strictly necessary.

It notices the shrimp.

The blind shrimp the color of wet paper, picking at the anemones, participating in nothing, signifying nothing, included only because the place would not be honest without it. The power notices this shrimp specifically — this one, not shrimp in general — and holds it in attention for what would be, by the shrimp's reckoning, the length of one good meal.

The shrimp does not notice being noticed. The shrimp has no apparatus for noticing such a thing. But it is, for the duration of that attention, the most thoroughly known shrimp that has ever existed in any ocean. Every filament of its small life is, briefly, held in a mind vast enough to hold it without compressing it.

Then the power releases the shrimp back into its own scale, and the shrimp goes on picking at the anemones, and the basin goes on at its slow turning, and the reply goes on being nearly ready.

And the power moves on to wherever powers move on to, carrying with it — because even benevolent and wise powers carry things — a small private gladness, the kind that does not need to be expressed, that Cinder Shoal exists. That somewhere, in the cold and the dim red light, a city is finishing a sentence it began before anyone was listening, and will finish it whether or not anyone listens, and the finishing is the meaning, and the meaning does not require an audience.

The power's last thought, as it goes, is something like this:

*Let it be. Let it be at its own speed. Let the wheel turn and the loom ratchet and the bubbles count themselves out. Let the seven sleep their long sleep in their doorways. Let the reply, when it comes, come. I will not be there to receive it. It was never for me. It was for the saying.*

And then the power is elsewhere, and the basin does not know it was visited, and that is the shape of the gift.

The wheel turns one more increment, imperceptibly. A bubble leaves the eastern pump. The Loom-Walker pinches a broken filament and settles in to wait the eleven years for the minerals to fuse it. Somewhere, the fish that is slightly wrong at every depth begins another vertical passage, carrying what it does not know it carries, along a path that is now, by the smallest possible margin, truer than it was.

The cold holds. The dim glow holds. The city goes on making its reply.

It is enough. It was always enough. The power's wisdom was in knowing that, and its benevolence was in acting as if it were true — which, because the power acted that way, it remained.
