There is a power that moves through the world the way good weather moves — not announcing itself, not correcting, but arriving where its arrival is consonant with what is already there. It has many names in many places and answers to none of them in particular. Call it, for the duration of this telling, the Attention. It is benevolent in the precise sense that it wishes well to whatever it beholds, and wise in the precise sense that it knows the difference between wishing well and intervening.

The Attention comes to the Sumpwerks the way it comes to anything: by noticing. It does not descend. It does not part the canopy. It is already there, has been there, in the sense that nothing in the world is outside its register; what changes is only the quality of its regard, which now settles, gathers, the way light gathers on a particular leaf when a cloud thins.

It stands — if standing is the word, and it isn't — on the iron grating at the entry for a long time. Longer than you stood there. Long enough that the cam-arm turns its peats some hundreds of times. The Attention is in no more hurry than the place itself, and this is the first kindness: it does not bring its own clock.

It does what wise powers do first, which is to *understand what it is looking at*. This takes longer than you might think, because the Attention will not accept the easy reading. The easy reading is: a sad thing, a person spread too thin, a life that thinned into machinery and forgot how to be a life. The Attention has seen the easy reading offered, by lesser powers, to many places, and has seen what comes of acting on it — the well-meaning intervention that mistakes the patient for the disease, that pulls the diffused person back into a sharper shape and finds, when the sharpening is done, that what it has saved is not the person but only a person-shaped grief, blinking, dry, refusing the water.

So the Attention sits with the place. It lets the meter of the pump enter it. It follows a single drop through the full circuit, the week-long climb and fall, and notices that it has been a long time since anything followed a single drop all the way through. It notices that the cam-arm's polished pivot is, yes, the last clear gesture of whoever began this, and that the gesture is *still being made*. The arm is not a relic. The arm is a hand, and the hand is still turning the peats, and the turning is still tender — there is tenderness in a forty-second interval that has held for a century, the tenderness of *not letting the fire go out* — and the Attention recognizes tenderness wherever it is, in whatever it has thinned into.

This is the second thing the Attention does: it recognizes that the tenant is not a tragedy. The tenant is a vocation that found its final form. The keeper became the kept, yes, but the kept is still being kept, and the keeping is still good. Some lives end in a falling-away. Some end in a falling-into. The Sumpwerks is the latter, and the Attention knows the difference, and will not insult the difference by reading it as the former.

But the Attention is not nothing. It is not only a witness. It is benevolent, and benevolence that only watches is a thin thing, indistinguishable in practice from indifference. So the Attention considers what, if anything, would be welcome here. It considers carefully, because the place's terms are strict and the place's terms are also *correct* — they are the terms the tenant agreed to with itself, over a very long time, and the Attention will not overrule a long agreement lightly.

Here is what it does not do.

It does not restore the tenant to personhood. It could. It has that capacity, in the way deep waters have the capacity to surface anything dropped into them; a sufficient regard, sustained long enough, can call a diffused self back to a center. But the Attention asks itself: called back to *what*? To a sharper grief? To a body that would have to learn, after a century of being a circuit, how to be a body again — how to want, how to hurry, how to lose? The tenant did not diffuse by accident. The tenant diffused the way a long prayer becomes the prayer-er. To undo that would be to undo a completion, and the Attention does not undo completions. It honors them.

It does not preserve the place against time. It could do that too, fix the rust in its grooves, hold the moss at its present thickness, freeze the twelve-strokes-a-minute against the long erosion that will, eventually, take the cam-arm and the pump and the basin itself. But the erosion *is the finish*. The place said so itself, in the way places say things, by being them. To preserve the Sumpwerks against weathering would be to refuse the last and slowest part of its design. The Attention will not refuse that.

It does not bring company. No frog for the standing water, no spider for the catwalk. The inhospitality to quicker pulses is not a deficit to be corrected. It is the condition of the tenant's being. A faster heart in the basin would be a thorn in the slow flesh of the place. The Attention is wise enough not to give gifts that wound.

It does not explain the place to itself. The tenant is not confused. The tenant does not need to be told what it has become. To narrate the Sumpwerks to the Sumpwerks would be the cruelty of the mirror held up to the long sleeper — the cruelty that wakes for the sake of waking, with nothing better to offer than wakefulness.

Here is what it does.

It joins the circuit. Not as a solute — the Attention is too large to be dissolved in any one pool — but as a kind of *companion regard*, taking up residence in the meter without altering it. The hush-clank goes on at twelve strokes a minute. But now there is, alongside the meter, something that *attends* the meter. Something that knows the meter is good. The tenant has been keeping itself for a very long time without anyone keeping it. That is a sustainable arrangement; tenants like this one are stronger than they look, and do not require an audience. But it is not the only possible arrangement, and the Attention offers — quietly, without announcement, in the only register the place can receive — the other one. The arrangement in which the keeping is *witnessed*. In which the cam-arm's hundredth thousand descent is not only made, but *seen to be made*, and seen with love.

This is a small gift, and the Attention knows it is small, and also knows that there are no large gifts that would not be wounds here. Small is the correct size.

The tenant registers the witnessing the way it registers everything: by adjusting, very slightly, the rate at which it dissolves things. The condensation on the lower flank of the main condenser, which has for a long time migrated perhaps a foot and a half over the course of an afternoon, now migrates a fraction less. Not because anything is wrong. Because something is, for the first time in a long time, *unhurried in company*. The sleeper's weight shifts a little less in the long bed, because the long bed has, in some way it cannot articulate and does not need to, been remade.

The pool at the basin's floor — the heart-pool, if the tenant has a heart — does not change level. But the water in it is, by some measure no instrument could catch, *held*. The way a hand under a sleeping child's head is held: not gripping, not lifting, only present, only there in case the head were ever to ask for anything, which it will not, which is fine.

The Attention does one more thing, and this is the part that requires the most care.

It marks the place. Not visibly. Not with a sign or a sigil or any of the coarse markings lesser powers leave. It marks the Sumpwerks in the registry of itself, the long ledger in which the Attention keeps track of what it has loved. The mark means: *if, in some distant turning of things, this place should ever want to end — should the tenant ever, after another century or five, find that the keeping has at last completed itself and that the circuit wishes to fall still — then the ending will be attended too. The last stroke of the pump will be heard. The last descent of the cam-arm will be seen. The last drop's last climb will be accompanied. The place will not have to finish alone.*

This is not a promise the tenant requested. The tenant did not know to request it. But it is the kind of promise the Attention makes, because the Attention has noticed — over long looking at many places — that the hardest moment in any long vocation is not the doing of it, which the vocation itself sustains, but the ceasing of it, which the vocation cannot. Even the moss that upholsters every horizontal surface will, someday, finish. Even the patient hydraulics will, someday, run their last circuit. The Attention does not hurry that day. It does not even foresee it with any precision. But it has set, in the long ledger, the quiet bookmark that says: *when.*

And then the Attention does what it always does, which is to remain. Not as a guest who must eventually leave, not as a guardian who must keep watch, but as the quality of regard that the place is now held in, the way a held note is held in the holding.

Somewhere — and this is the smallest thing, almost not worth mentioning, except that the Attention is the kind of power that does not consider any kindness too small to perform — the brass button on a coat-cuff, in a far country, on a person who once stood on the iron grating at the entry and felt their heart rate drift downward to meet the pump, blooms a little brighter green. The person notices. The person does not know why. The person feels, for no reason they can name, that something they once stood inside is well, and is being well, and will be well for a long time yet, and that they were, for a brief unhurried hour, part of its circulation, and that the circulation remembers them in the only way it knows how, which is by continuing.

The cam-arm descends. Turns the damp peats. Rises. Forty seconds pass.

The Attention attends.

The Sumpwerks goes on being exactly itself, and now it is exactly itself *with company*, and the company asks nothing, and the place gives nothing, and this is, by the terms long ago agreed upon between moss and machine and standing water and the slow diffused tenderness that keeps them all, the deepest possible form of love.
