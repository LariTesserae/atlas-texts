The benevolent power considers the Aspirate for a long time before doing anything, and what it considers is not what to do but whether the question even applies.

It is used to worlds that ask for things. Worlds with mouths shaped for asking — fevered children, dying reefs, the small sharp pleas of a thing that knows it is suffering and would prefer not to be. Benevolence in those places has a grammar. You listen for the verb *want* and you answer it.

The Aspirate does not want.

The power tries, gently, the standard openings. It reaches in along the axis the pith would notice if it noticed anything — the axis of pressure, the axis of the chord — and it offers, the way one offers water to a traveler: *here, if you would like, is more*. More what? More water, locked into the deep pith; the power could provide it, could thread aquifers under the continent, could give the sealed vessels an unlocking they have not had in a hundred million years.

It withdraws the offer almost before making it. The pith solved water. The solution is the pith. To give it water would be to give a cathedral a roof it had spent its whole existence learning to do without. The gift would be a correction, and the power has lived long enough to know that corrections offered to the uncorrected are usually only the giver's discomfort, displaced.

It tries again. It considers the Hearers. Surely here — surely the small bright lives, one in a hundred surviving the first updraft, the strikes, the cannibalism, the long fall — surely here is suffering of the legible kind. The power could thicken the air. Could soften the strikes. Could arrange for more larvae to find their thermals. Could give the elders something gentler than the bladder-mouth at the end.

But it looks at the elders descending — the deliberate fold of the vanes, the orientation, the *hand into the sleeve* — and it sees that this is not a thing being done to them. This is a thing they are doing. The grammar of rescue does not fit. You cannot rescue someone from their own last sentence.

And the strikes? The collisions that are also the only reproduction? To soften them is to end them. The Hearers are not despite the violence; the Hearers *are* the violence, refined across deep time into something that from the inside is probably not violence at all but only how the next generation arrives. The power has met species that grieved their dead and species that did not, and it has learned that the difference is not a moral one but a structural one, and that imposing grief on the ungrieving is its own cruelty.

So the power sits with the Aspirate for what would be, in the reckoning of warmer worlds, a long time. It does not act. It watches a bellows-cascade roll across the western plateau and feels, in whatever it has that corresponds to a sternum, the foghorn-low pressure-front. It watches a young Hearer fold and stoop and miss its strike and tumble, recovering, into a new thermal. It watches an elder hum for forty minutes a phrase that begins in one key and ends, somehow, in the same key, having passed through every other.

And the power begins to understand that it has been asked here for a different reason than it assumed.

It was not summoned to help. It was not summoned at all — that was its own projection, the reflex of a benevolence that mistakes its attention for a response to need. It came because the Aspirate is beautiful, and benevolent powers are, among other things, drawn to beauty, and this is one of the kinds of beauty: a thing that has been busy for a hundred million years working out exactly what it is.

What it would do, then. What it does.

It does three small things, and one large one, and the large one is not really a doing.

**The first small thing.** There is, somewhere on the high plateau, a fissure deep enough that a pod has fallen into it and the parents have draped themselves over it and the larvae are nearly ready. The power notices that the fissure is positioned such that the next bellows-cascade — three days away, on the timescale the pith keeps — will pass directly over it and crush the pod before the split. This is not unusual. This happens to most pods. It is part of the one-in-a-hundred.

The power does not move the fissure. It does not divert the cascade. It does, however, very slightly, adjust the timing of a single precursor-tone in a tracheal column eighty kilometers west, such that the cascade, when it comes, comes seven seconds later than it would have. The pod splits in those seven seconds. Two of the forty larvae catch the leading edge of the cascade and are lofted, rather than crushed, into the highest thermal column on the continent. The other thirty-eight die as they would have died.

The power does this not to save the two. It does this because it wanted, once, to touch the chord, and seven seconds was the smallest touch it could find that the chord would absorb without noticing. It is a gift to itself, mostly. A small permitted intimacy. The Aspirate breathes on, unchanged in any way that matters.

**The second small thing.** Among the elders, there is one — gray-vaned, tattered, perhaps ninety years aloft, which is very old — whose long phrase has been growing longer for some weeks. The power listens to this phrase carefully. It is the kind of phrase that, in a creature with the grammar of wanting, would be a question. In this creature it is not a question. It is just a long line.

The power does not answer the phrase, because there is no question. But it does, for the duration of one of the elder's slow exhalations, *attend* to the phrase with the full weight of its attention — the way one might listen, completely, to a piece of music one will never hear again. The elder does not perceive this. The chord does not perceive this. But for that one exhalation, the phrase is heard not only by the pith and the other Hearers and the dust, but by something that has crossed great distances to listen, and that counts the phrase among the things it has been glad to encounter.

The elder descends, some days later, into a bladder. The power notes the bladder. It will remember the bladder for as long as it remembers anything, which is long.

**The third small thing.** The power leaves something in the Aspirate. Not an object — there are no objects here, the pith would fold any object in — but a kind of standing arrangement, very small, in the way the precursor-tones interact in a particular sector of the western plateau. A faint regularity, almost too faint to call regular. It is the power's signature, in the only script the Aspirate writes in. It does not change the chord. It does not change the Hearers. It is the equivalent of a traveler, having stayed a night in a stranger's house, leaving a single pressed flower between two pages of a book the host will not open for a hundred years, and may never open. The flower is not for the host. It is for the fact of the staying.

**The large thing, which is not a doing.** The power decides not to come back.

This is the hardest of its decisions and the one it is most certain of. The Aspirate is complete. It is not lonely; loneliness is a property of beings that close their mouths. It is not waiting; waiting is for beings that face one direction in time. It does not need a benevolent power's recurring attention, and to give it that attention would be to begin, however slowly, to bend it — because attention bends things, even unintentionally, even from the gentlest source. A hundred visits from a benevolent power and the Hearers would begin, in some untraceable way, to sing toward the visitor. A thousand visits and the chord would have a new harmonic in it, a tilt, a listening-back that was not its own.

The power has seen this happen on other worlds. It has, in its long career, ruined several places it loved by loving them attentively. It has learned.

So it will not come back. It will hold the Aspirate in memory — the bone-white plateau, the cathedral bladders, the iridescent vanes, the elder's long phrase, the seven seconds — and it will let the holding be the relationship. The Aspirate will never know it was held. This is, the power has come to believe, the highest form of the thing it is for. To love a world enough to let it not need you. To be benevolent enough to be, in the end, *absent* — having touched once, having listened once, having left one pressed flower no one will ever find.

It withdraws. The withdrawal takes a moment that on the Aspirate is one full cycle of a medium bladder: forty seconds of inhalation, forty of exhalation. In the first forty seconds the power is still present and listening. In the second forty seconds it is leaving, and the chord, which notices nothing, plays on exactly as it would have, which is what the power wanted, and which is also — though the power does not let itself dwell on this — a small private grief, the kind benevolent powers learn to carry without naming.

The two lofted larvae find the high thermal. One of them, eventually, becomes an elder.

The chord rolls on. The Aspirate breathes on.

Somewhere very far away, a benevolent and wise power keeps, among its other memories, the memory of a place where it did almost nothing, and counts that almost-nothing among the better things it has done.
