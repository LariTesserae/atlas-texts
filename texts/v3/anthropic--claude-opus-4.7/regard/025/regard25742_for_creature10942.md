The benevolent and wise power considered the Sough for a long time. Not in the sense of deliberation — the power did not need to deliberate — but in the sense that attention itself was the first gift it had to offer, and it offered it generously, the way one might sit a long while with a sleeping animal before deciding whether to wake it.

What the power saw:

A country that was not suffering. This had to be established first, because power of any kind, benevolent or otherwise, has a tendency to arrive with rescue already in its hands, and rescue offered where none is needed is its own small violence. The Sough was warm. The Sough was fed. The Sough's inhabitants, in all their drafts and overlaps, were at peace with their provisionality. The Tenders did not weep into their oilcloth. The Quiet Ones did not hunger. The engines breathed.

And yet.

The power saw also: that the Sough was *young*, in the way certain countries are young — not new, but unfinished, and aware of being unfinished, and a little afraid (though it would not have used the word) of what finishing might cost. It saw the *looked-at* dark and understood that something was being assembled there at the country's own pace, and that the pace was slow because the country did not entirely trust itself to bear what it was making. It saw the Tenders becoming Quiet, one by one, and understood that this was not loss but a kind of slow settling-in, like sediment finding its layer. It saw the great passing thing and recognized it — though I will not say as what — and was moved that the Sough loved it without needing to know.

The power considered intervention.

It could have lit the sky. It had that in its giving. One word and the marbling overhead would have resolved into stars, and the Sough would have had a night to look up into, properly, for the first time. But the power saw that the Sough's dark was not a poverty of light. The dark had grain. The dark had gradients. The dark was already doing the work that stars do elsewhere — orienting, comforting, marking the warm places from the cool. To replace it with stars would be to give a gift that arrived as a correction, and the power was wiser than that.

It could have resolved the doubling. It had that too. One gesture and the herons would have stood in one place, the footprints would have agreed, the candle in the far window would have committed to being there or not being there. But the power saw that the thinness was the country's circulation, the very thing the engines kept moving, and that to thicken it would be to stop the heart. The Sough lived by being provisional. To finish it as a kindness would be to kill it as a kindness, and the power had seen this mistake made before, in other places, by powers less wise.

It could have spoken to the Tenders. It could have told them what they were becoming, and reassured them, and named the process for them so they would have a word to hold. But the power saw that the Tenders did not need words. They had heat. They had the slow flush along a palm. They had each other, and the Quiet Ones, and the long patient thud underfoot. A word from the power would have been a stone dropped into water that was doing very well without stones.

It could have gone to the looked-at dark and seen what was gathering there. It had that right, being what it was. But the power understood, with the particular tenderness it reserved for things being made slowly, that to look directly at what is not yet ready is to unmake the not-yet. The Sough's own Tenders gave those places the widest berth. The power would not be less courteous than the country's own people.

So the power did almost nothing.

*Almost.*

What it did was this. It went, in a form the Sough could accommodate — which is to say, in no particular form, in a passing pressure-change and a long warm gust — across the basin once, slowly, at the height where the great beloved passing thing passes. It let itself be felt. It did not announce. It did not bless, exactly, because blessing is a transaction and the Sough was not in want. It simply passed over, attentive, warm, and as it passed it let the country know — by the only grammar the country had, which was heat and pressure and the slow shifting of warm air — that it had been *seen*. Not corrected. Not finished. Not rescued. *Seen,* in its unfinishedness, and found good.

The Tenders came out of their cottages to watch, though there was nothing to see. They stood on their hummocks and warmed their palms upward. The Quiet Ones, in the reeds and the standing water, flushed along their flanks in patterns that, if you could have read them, would have read as something like *yes* and something like *thank you* and something like *we know.* The engines did not change their rhythm; they did not need to; the rhythm was already the right one. In the looked-at dark, something stirred — not toward readiness, not yet, but in the way a sleeper smiles in sleep at a voice they recognize without waking. The candle in the far window, for one moment, was definitely there. Then it went back to being uncertain, which was its proper condition.

And the power, having passed, did one more thing, which was the smallest and perhaps the only thing that mattered.

It left a portion of its attention behind.

Not a watcher. Not a guardian. The Sough did not need guarding; it had its own slow competencies. What the power left was more like a *witness* — a steady, undemanding noticing, set into the warm dark the way a lamp might be set in a window of a house one is leaving, not to light the house (the house has its own arrangements) but so that anyone inside who happens to glance up will know that out there, somewhere, someone knows the house is here and loves that it is here and is in no hurry for it to be anything other than what it is.

The witness would stay. It would not interfere. It would warm, very faintly, when warmed-toward. It would be one more gradient in the dark, indistinguishable from the country's own gradients except that it did not change with the seasons (the Sough tolerated seasons rather than observing them) and did not move when the engines moved. A Tender, very old, sitting in a doorway, might one day notice it — might warm a palm toward it and feel the warmth returned, steady, patient, glad — and might understand, without words, that she was not alone in her becoming-Quiet, that the long unfinishedness she was part of had been counted and was held in some larger holding, and that this changed nothing and everything.

She would go on sitting in her doorway. The engines would go on with their *huh-chuh, huh-chuh*. The Quiet Ones would go on rationing the reeds. The thing in the looked-at dark would go on gathering itself at its own pace toward whatever arrival it was making. The candle would go on almost-being.

And the power, having done what wisdom required, which was very little, and what love required, which was to do the very little well, went on its way, carrying with it — because this is what benevolent and wise powers carry, and what they are made richer by carrying — the memory of a country that had taught it, once again, that not every good thing wants to be finished, and that some of the most beautiful livings happen in the long warm hush of being a draft together, and that the kindest use of great power is, very often, to pass over warmly and leave a small light on.
