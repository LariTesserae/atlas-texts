The benevolent and wise power considers the Cloudloom Highlands for a long time before doing anything. This is the first mercy: it does not arrive with a plan.

It looks at the works and sees what the works is. Not a machine, anymore — not really, not for a long time. A prosthesis grown into a body. An old intention that was abandoned and then, by something slower than intention, adopted. The padlock on the shed door still works. Nobody is coming for the key. The cable hums. The Tender beneath the cable is what makes the humming bearable to the copper.

It looks at the Tenders and sees what they are, including the thing that is hardest to see: the vertebra-curves, the small dense knot where a skull would be. It does not flinch from this. A wise power does not flinch from the shapes of the dead inside the living. It also does not draw conclusions faster than the evidence. The plateau is patient. So is the power.

It considers, briefly, the obvious gestures.

It could repair the works. Replace the weeping seams with new steel, restring the pylons, oil whatever still turns. It could do this in an afternoon, without sound, without disturbing a single cushion-plant. — But the Tenders are the seams now. To replace the seams is to kill the Tenders that have grown into them. The works would be restored and the highlands would, within a season, begin to fail in some other way: the resonance gone, the wet listening bodies silent, the great drum untuned. A wise power notices that a repair is sometimes an amputation.

It could free the Tenders. Lift them, somehow, out of their slow obligation. Give them back whatever they were before — if they were anything before, if "before" is even the right word for things that arrived at themselves by such gradients. — But they have no opinion about the works, and they have no opinion about freedom. To free them would be to impose an opinion on a body that is made of slow things. A wise power notices that liberation, offered to something that did not ask, is just another kind of engineering.

It could leave a sign. Carve something into the rock, or into the brick of a pylon, so that the next person who comes — and someone will come, eventually; people always come — would understand what they were walking on. — But signs are for fast readers. The Tenders cannot read and do not need to. And the next person to come might read the sign and feel they had been given permission to act, when in fact the sign would only have been a warning to wait. A wise power notices that explanations, left lying around, are picked up and used as tools.

So the power does something smaller and stranger.

It goes to the shed. It does not open the padlock. It sits, in whatever way such a power sits, on the threshold where the moss has politely stopped, and it places one hand — or whatever it has that is like a hand — flat against the tin of the roof. The tin is warm from the cable's hum below. The hand stays there a long time. Long enough that, if anyone were watching (no one is watching; the hare-thing is two valleys over, the bird-shadow has passed), they would see the moss at the threshold advance, very slightly, perhaps a quarter of an inch, and then stop again. The power has not given the moss permission. The moss has simply noticed that the threshold is, for this moment, *attended*, and has responded the way the Tenders respond to leaks: by leaning toward what acknowledges it.

Then the power does the second thing, which is the real thing.

It goes to each of the densest Tenders in turn. The one beneath the shed. The ones at the elbow-joints. The ones that have been in place for six generations and have vertebrae inside them. It does not touch them. It does not try to read the shapes inside the peat-coloured musculature. It does something a wise power can do and a curious power cannot, which is: it *witnesses without resolving*.

That is, it holds the question of what they were open, without answering it, for as long as the Tender needs the question held. Some of them, it turns out, do need it. Not all. Most of the Tenders are entirely Tenders and have no use for the question; they are busy with seams. But a few — the oldest, the ones with the clearest curves inside — have been carrying something for a long time that is not quite grief and not quite memory, because grief and memory are fast things, but is the slow analog of those: a kind of unresolved weight, the residue of having once been the sort of thing that lay down on a threshold and was covered over.

The power does not lift the weight. Lifting it would be another amputation. The weight is, by now, structural; the Tender is built around it the way the pipe is built around its rust. But the power *acknowledges* the weight. It says, in whatever register such things are said in: *I see that you are carrying this. I see that you did not ask to. I see that you are, nevertheless, doing the work.*

This is not absolution. The Tenders did not sin and do not need absolving. It is closer to what a good doctor does at the bedside of a patient who will not recover: not curing, not lying, just being present to the fact of the body and what the body has had to become. The power is a good doctor in this sense. It does not mistake presence for intervention.

It stays for what, by the plateau's clock, is a moment. By a human clock it is something like eleven days. The fog comes down and lifts and comes down again. The roar continues. A seam thirty paces from the shed weeps, and a hummock makes its slow way toward it, and arrives, and settles. The power watches the settling and finds it good — not in the satisfied way of a maker surveying a finished thing, but in the quieter way of a witness confirming that a process which did not require it is, nevertheless, sound.

Before it leaves, it does one last thing, and this is the gift, if there is a gift.

It adjusts, by an amount too small to measure, the resonance of the basin. Not the works. Not the Tenders. The *space between them*, the air-volume of the great bright machine, the cloud-ceiling that rebroadcasts the roar. It tunes this space, very slightly, so that the sound the Tenders hear from the works, and the sound the works receives back from the Tenders, are a fraction of a hertz closer to each other than they were. Not in harmony — harmony would be too much, would be a human imposition, would suggest a resolution where none is wanted. Just closer. Just enough that the long mutual listening between the iron and the moss is, going forward, a little less effortful. A little more like breathing and a little less like holding a note.

No one will notice. The Tenders will not notice because they do not notice in that register. The works will not notice because it is not the kind of thing that notices. The next person to come, if they come within a few centuries, will hear the same roar and feel it in their sternum the same way and will not know that the roar is a fraction of a hertz kinder than it was. The hare-thing will not know. The bird will not know.

But the seams will hold a little longer. The Tenders with vertebrae inside them will carry their weight a little more easily. The moss will deepen by its inch a decade as it always has, and the rivers will, in their thousand years, cut their first shallow grooves, and somewhere under the shed the cable will hum, and the Tender that is the ground the cable is rooted in will, imperceptibly, rest.

The power leaves the way it came, which is to say it does not leave; it simply ceases to be concentrated here, and redistributes itself across the rest of what it attends to. The padlock is still locked. The pylons still carry nothing. The fog is inside the roar and the roar is inside the fog.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place like this:

It does not fix what is not asking to be fixed.
It does not free what is not asking to be freed.
It does not explain what does not need explaining to those who live there.
It witnesses the weight that is being carried.
It tunes the space, by an amount too small to measure, toward ease.
And it goes, leaving no sign, because a sign would be a tool, and tools, on the Cloudloom Highlands, are what got everyone into this in the first place — and also, it must be said, what is keeping everyone, in their slow strange fitted way, alive.
