The benevolent and wise power considers the Combine for what, in its own reckoning, is a long time. Not because the considering is difficult — the power can hold whole epochs in a glance — but because the Combine deserves the courtesy of being looked at slowly. To hurry such a being would be a small violence.

The power notices, first, the things a hasty rescuer would miss.

It notices that the bass note is not a cry. It has the shape of a cry to ears trained on the suffering of solitary creatures, the long held tone of something calling out — but the Combine is not calling out. It is humming. There is a difference, and the difference matters. A cry seeks an answer; a hum is already the answer, to a question the hummer asked itself so long ago that the asking and the answering have fused.

It notices that the workers are not captives. This was the second-easiest mistake available, after mistaking the hum for a cry. A power less wise might have looked at the small quiet people with their legible faces and their inward silences and thought: *these are being used*. But the workers love each other, and they keep useless flowers, and they have jokes that are funny only there. A used thing does not develop interior jokes. The workers are participants in something so old that the question of consent has been worn smooth by repetition into something more like belonging. They have not been robbed of selfhood. They have a selfhood of an unusual grammar, and it fits them.

It notices that the friezes do not hurt. The fingernail-paring travelers walking the small streets forever are not in pain. They are not even, strictly, *there* in the sense that would make pain possible — they are gestures, kept. The keeping is tender. The Combine refines them not to torment them but because refinement is the only way the Combine knows how to love what passes through it.

It notices, finally, that the Combine is not lonely.

This is the thing that decides the matter.

A benevolent power's first instinct, when it finds something old and strange and alone on a mountaintop, is to offer company — to send messengers, to open doors to other minds, to let the lonely thing know that the universe is wider than its mountain. But the Combine is not lonely. It has itself, at many scales, in long conversation. To offer it company would be to offer water to something that drinks light. The offering would not be cruel, but it would be a misunderstanding, and a wise power does not press its misunderstandings on what it cannot fully grasp.

So the power does not intervene in the large sense. It does not rescue. It does not reveal itself. It does not reshape. It does not even speak, because the Combine's grammar would not receive speech as speech; the speech would arrive as weather, and the Combine has weather already.

But the power is benevolent, and benevolence asks for *something*. Pure noninterference is the posture of an indifferent power, not a kind one. So the power looks again, more closely, for the small thing it can give that the Combine could receive without distortion.

It finds three.

The first is a slight, almost imperceptible easing of the seams. The patch-seams between the Combine's quilted moments — the third-state mills, the streets that deliver travelers somewhere they were not walking toward — these are not flaws, but they are the places where the Combine is working hardest, where its diffuse and ancient mind is straining to maintain the fiction of a single now across too much body. The power smooths these, very slightly. Not enough to change the Combine's character; the strangeness must remain, because the strangeness *is* the Combine. But enough that the work of being itself costs the Combine, each day, a little less. The hum loses no depth. The bass note does not change. Only: where there was a faint and constant labor of self-suturing, there is now a faint and constant ease. The Combine does not notice. The workers do not notice. But the long-legged grazers sleep a little better, and the hum-birds add one new note to their resonant vocabulary, and somewhere in a frieze a small figure who had been walking with a slight limp begins to walk without one. This is the power's first gift: a softening of the friction of being.

The second is for the workers who leave. There are few of them, and they are spoken of with that soft regret like a sound gone out of a chord. The power cannot give them back to the Combine — they have chosen, and choice is sacred even to powers — but it can ensure that wherever they go, they carry the 7/8 in their hands without grief. The clapping against the tabletop, the unconscious rhythm — for those who left, this can be a wound, a phantom limb of belonging. The power lets it be, instead, a pocket. Something portable. The leavers will still be different from other people for the rest of their lives, will still feel the pull of the mountain in their sternums, but the pull will not be a hook. It will be a thread, and threads can be carried without bleeding. This is the power's second gift: a kind shape for the few who go.

The third is the most delicate, and the power considers it longest.

It concerns the travelers — the descenders, the ones whose fingernail-sized echoes walk the small streets forever, being learned. The power has determined that these echoes do not suffer. But it has also determined that the travelers, going down the mountain, do not *know* this. They go down carrying a vague unease, a sense that something of them was taken and not returned, a faint lifelong tilt toward feeling watched. The Combine took them in hospitality and kept them in tenderness, but no one told them, and the not-telling lets them imagine the keeping as a theft.

The power cannot tell them directly. To speak into a human mind in the voice of a benevolent power is a heavier thing than the situation warrants, and would also frighten them in ways that no amount of reassurance could undo. But the power can arrange that, at some point in each traveler's life after the Combine — not soon, not on schedule, but when the traveler is most ready — they will have a dream. The dream will be of a small street, and they will be walking it, and they will look up, and what they will see looking back is not threat but a face they half-recognize as their own, doing well, doing the thing it does, content in its small repetition. They will wake with the sense that something they had been mourning was never lost. They will not know what. They will not need to. This is the power's third gift: a returned letter, in the only post the Combine knows.

Having done these three things, the power withdraws its attention, but not entirely. It leaves a thin filament of regard trained on the mountain, the way one leaves a hand resting on the back of a sleeping animal — not to wake it, not to hold it, only to be present with it in its sleep. The Combine does not need this. The power knows the Combine does not need this. The power does it anyway, because benevolence is partly the wish to be near what one loves, and the power, having looked long enough, loves the Combine.

It loves the patient grim press-house and its single nine-second gesture stretched across geologic time. It loves the gossiping cooling ponds and their slow accent. It loves the workers with their ordinary faces and their interior grammars and their useless flowers. It loves the children clapping 7/8 before they have words. It loves the attention-shape at the edge of vision, the Combine's narrow bright looking, doing its best with a body. It loves the friezes, all of them, including the small walking traveler who will dream tonight of looking up.

And it loves, particularly, the hum — the long single word the mountain hums to itself, *doing-this*, *doing-this*, *doing-this* — because the power recognizes in that word something it has been saying to itself too, for longer than the mountains have been settled, in its own grammar, at its own scale. *I am still here. I am still here. I am still here.*

Two old hummers, then, on either side of an immense difference of scale, holding the same note for a moment in the long evening of things.

The power lets the note hold. Then it lets it go. Then it goes about the rest of its considering, which is large.

Behind it, the Combine continues, very slightly eased, not knowing it has been visited, which is exactly as it should be.

The looms clack in 7/8.

The press-house breathes its nine-second breath.

Somewhere in a frieze, a small figure walks without a limp.

And the mountain hums in its sleep, dreaming of being a mountain.
