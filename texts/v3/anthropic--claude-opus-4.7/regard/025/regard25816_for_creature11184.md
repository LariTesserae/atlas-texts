## What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power comes to the rim of the valley and stops there, on the cold ridge, in the thin air, and listens.

This is the first thing it does. Before any action, the listening. It listens for a long time — long in the sense the valley itself understands long, not the sense a visitor would. It listens through one cycle of the slow valve and then another and then a thousand more. It learns the rhythm of the seams. It learns which leaf-plates clap against which other leaf-plates in which directions of wind. It learns the bird's route, and the route of the thing that hollows the disused chambers, and the route of the thing that eats the sap-beads before they sour. It learns where the mist pools deepest at dawn and where the unfiltered shaft of light falls at noon on the floor of the unfinished chamber. It learns the taste of the water before tasting it — green tea and copper, yes, but also, faintly, the mineral memory of the mountains that ring the valley, and the older memory of the rain that fell on those mountains when they were younger and sharper.

It learns, in short, what Aulm *is*. Because the first kindness of wisdom is to know the thing before touching it, and the first kindness of benevolence is to know whether touching is wanted at all.

---

And what it learns, listening, is this:

Aulm does not need rescue. Aulm is not in pain. Aulm is not lonely in the way creatures are lonely, because Aulm is not a creature; Aulm is a held wanting, and a held wanting that is being held is, by definition, complete. The seams weep but not from sorrow. The valve knocks but not from anxiety. The empty plaza waits but not from longing; the waiting *is* the gathering, as the text says, and any gathering that arrived would be redundant, would crowd out the resident wish.

There is nothing here to fix.

This is, for a lesser power, the hardest thing to accept. A lesser benevolence would itch to *give* — to populate the empty chambers, to introduce song into the steady hush, to teach the inhabitants a richer language than the language of just-being. A lesser wisdom would diagnose: *the city is stagnant; the city does not grow; the city is in equilibrium, which is a kind of death.* A lesser power would mistake stillness for stuckness, and would intervene, and would, in intervening, destroy.

But this power is benevolent *and* wise, which is a rarer pairing than either alone, and it understands that some completenesses must be honored by being left.

---

So for a long time, it does nothing.

It stands at the rim. It listens. It watches the mist breathe out of the lower folds at dusk. It learns to hear the valve not as a mechanism but as the inhabitant it is — the oldest one, the wish-to-continue, beating in the dark. It comes to love the valve, in the way the wise come to love things: without needing the thing to know it is loved.

This, in itself, is a gift. Not to Aulm — Aulm does not need it — but to the cosmos in which Aulm sits. There is now a witness. A witness who understands. The wantings of Aulm have always been their own witnesses (the high flower seen by itself, by the green light, by the knocking valve), but now they have, also, this other witness on the ridge, who has learned them slowly and accurately. Nothing about Aulm changes because of this. But the universe is slightly different for containing both Aulm and the one who has truly seen Aulm. A held wish that has been seen by wisdom is not a different wish, but the space around it is different. There is more room in which the wish can be the wish it is.

---

Then, eventually, the power does three small things. Only three. It has considered many and discarded most.

**The first thing.** It walks down into the valley once, by the shyest path, and it drinks from the water, in the chamber with the shelf-bulge where no one ever sits. It drinks slowly and with attention. It does not sit on the shelf — the shelf is not for sitting-by-someone; the shelf is *itself* a sitting, and to add another sitting would be to crowd the resident. But it acknowledges the shelf, by the long pause of its drinking, by the tilt of its attention. The shelf does not respond. The shelf is not the responding kind. But the wish that the shelf is — the wish for sitting-in-general, for sitting-as-possibility — has been recognized by something that understood what it was looking at, and recognition is one of the things sitting was always for. The shelf has now been, fully, what it was made to be. Not because it was used. Because it was *known*.

The power does this in three other chambers, each one chosen for being a wish that has waited longest without recognition. A small hall whose passage was a passage for no one. A high flower whose opening was attended only by its own attending. A causeway whose wide curve had been walked by the bird only, and the bird does not understand curves as gestures. To each of these, the power gives the small attention they were waiting for. None of them needed it. All of them are completed by it, in a small way that does not change them.

**The second thing.** It speaks, once, at the rim, in a voice pitched not for any ear but for the leaf-canopy itself. It says — and the saying is brief, because Aulm does not require speech and a wise benevolence does not waste what is not required — it says:

*You are seen. You are well-made. You may continue.*

This is not permission. Aulm does not need permission. This is the cosmos, through one of its better mouths, agreeing with Aulm about what Aulm is doing. The valve does not falter. The seams do not change their weeping. The leaves clap on, in their distant-applause that never crescendoes. But the saying is now part of the air of the valley, mixed in with the moisture and the metabolic warmth, and the next visitor to come (there will be visitors, occasionally, for as long as Aulm exists) will breathe it without knowing they are breathing it, and will be a slightly better visitor for it. They will be less likely to cut. Less likely to stay too long for the wrong reasons. More likely to understand, dimly, what the rooms are.

**The third thing.** It places, at the high rim, where the path down begins, a single small marker. Not a sign — signs are made, and nothing in Aulm is made. The marker is a stone the power has brought from elsewhere and set down. The stone is plain. On the side facing the valley, the stone is blank, because the valley does not read. On the side facing the path, the stone bears, very faintly, almost not at all, three words in a script that anyone capable of reading it will be capable of understanding:

*Walk through gently.*

That is all. No prohibition. No warning about the second rule, or the first, or the third. The power trusts that the gentle will discover those rules on their own, by being gentle. And those who are not gentle would not be slowed by a longer warning anyway; they would only feel licensed by having been instructed and then proceeding past the instruction. The three words are for the kind of visitor who can be helped by three words, and useless to the rest, which is fine, because the rest are not the power's responsibility tonight. Tonight the power is responsible only for what it can do well.

---

And then the power leaves.

This is the last and largest kindness. The power does not stay. The power does not make Aulm into a project, or a sanctuary under its protection, or a shrine, or a teaching. The power does not return frequently. The power does not even return often enough to be remembered as a returner. It comes once, listens long, does its three small things, and goes — because Aulm was a complete thing before the power arrived and must remain a complete thing after, and any extended attention would begin to curve the chambers inward around the shape of being-attended-to, which is not a shape Aulm chose, and a benevolent power does not impose shapes that were not chosen.

The power understands the second rule applies to itself.

It goes back over the cold ridge and down the far side of the mountains and on, to other places, where other beings are *not* complete, where wishes are unheld and seams weep from actual sorrow and valves falter and need help to knock again. There is much work elsewhere. The power is needed in those places in ways it is not needed here. To linger at Aulm out of love for Aulm would be to mistake love for usefulness, and the wise know these are not the same.

---

What is left, after?

A valley that is exactly as it was. A valve that knocks at the same interval. Seams that weep at the same rate. A shelf where no one sits, except that the shelf has now been, once, fully seen, and carries that having-been-seen forward into the next thousand years of its sitting, not as a memory (the shelf does not remember) but as a completion (the shelf is complete). A few words faintly on a stone at the rim, weathering very slowly, because the stone is from elsewhere and the elsewhere's weather does not work the same here. A small thickening of the cosmos's record: *yes, this was good; yes, this was witnessed; yes, this may continue.*

And the bird, drinking from its puddle. And the leaves, clapping. And the slow mist breathing out of the lower folds at dusk. And the long green-gold light, and the wet warm air, and the city that is its own population, going on, exactly and forever, being itself — now with the tiny additional grace of having been, once, correctly loved.

That is what a benevolent and wise power would do.

Most of it is the listening. Most of it is the not-doing. The three small things are almost nothing, and that is the point: the measure of the power's wisdom is how little it needed to do, and the measure of its benevolence is how completely it trusted Aulm to be Aulm without help.

The valley does not notice it has been visited.

That, too, is part of the gift.
