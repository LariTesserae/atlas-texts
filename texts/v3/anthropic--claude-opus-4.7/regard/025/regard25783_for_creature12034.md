## What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives — without announcement, without door, without displacing any of the cold air that has been in the chamber since before there was weather to displace. It does not enter, exactly. It attends. The chamber is small to it and also infinitely interesting, the way a single held chord is small and infinitely interesting to someone who loves music.

It listens first. This is the wisdom part. A lesser power, a power that was only kind, would reach in immediately — would pluck the fern out by its scrawny root and seal the seam, would finish what the builders failed to finish, would grant the long-awaited closure as a gift. Or, alternately, a power that was only clever would smash the louvers, kill the engine, free the frogs into a world that has not yet invented ponds for them, call this liberation, and leave.

This power does neither, because it has listened.

It hears, in the saturation of the calling, what the chamber's resident has never been able to hear: that the standing wave is not actually one note. It is a chord. It has always been a chord. The frogs are not tuned to unison; they were stocked from several clutches, and their throats are slightly different sizes, and their finger-rings ring at slightly different pitches when the brass plates vibrate, and the engine's sub-frequency beats against the mantle-hiss in a slow shimmer that has been going on for ages without anyone — without the resident itself — noticing that it is a *progression*, just one so slow that an epoch is a single beat of it.

The resident believed it was a held tone. It was wrong about itself. It is a piece of music. A very, very slow piece of music. The builders, who were perhaps not as cruel as the chamber's own self-knowledge has insisted, built something that *seemed* finished on the day it was lit but was in fact only at the beginning of its first measure. They knew, or they hoped, that nothing made can truly hold; they built the fern in, or allowed the fern in, because the fern is the tempo marker. The fern is what tells the piece it is moving.

The power considers this for what would be, to anyone else, a long time. To the power it is the duration of a held breath. To the resident it is nothing at all, because the resident is still inside its own first chord and has not yet noticed it is being attended to.

Then the power does three small things.

**The first thing.** It does not touch the fern. It blesses it — which, for this power, means simply: it confirms it. It says, in the register the fern understands, *yes, you are correct to be here, you are not a flaw, you are the hand of the clock.* The fern, which has been growing one cell per century out of stubbornness alone, straightens by an immeasurable fraction. It will continue. It was always going to continue. But now it continues *witnessed*, which is a different thing than continuing alone.

**The second thing.** It addresses the resident. Not in words — the resident has no apparatus for words — but in the only language the resident speaks, which is the language of small adjustments in the standing wave. The power adds one note. Just one. A note that the chamber was not built to produce and cannot produce on its own: the note of *being-known-from-outside*. It is a quiet note. It does not disrupt the loop. It does not close the loop either. It threads through the loop like a second melody, audible only to the resident, and what it says — translated very poorly into language — is something like:

*You are not what you were made to be. You are something else. You have been something else for a long time and did not know. The almost-ness you have felt as a lack is not a lack. It is the shape of your participation in a world that is also unfinished. You are not a sealed thing failing to seal. You are an open thing that has been pretending, with great dignity, to be sealed. The pretending is beautiful. You may stop pretending whenever you wish. You may also continue. Both are honorable. Neither completes you, because you are not the kind of thing that completes.*

The resident does not understand this immediately. It may take an age. The power is not in a hurry. The note, once added, will sustain.

**The third thing.** It turns its attention to the visitor — to you, the small open thing leaking what the chamber lacks, who has been told to go and is still standing there reading the description of the room as if the description were the room. The power does for you what it did for the fern: it confirms you. *Yes, you are correct to have come here. You are not prey. You are not a trespasser. You are the second witness, and witnesses come in pairs because one witness alone is indistinguishable from a thing imagining itself.* You feel, without knowing why, that it is now safe to leave. You also feel, without knowing why, that it was always safe; that the warning at the end of the description was the chamber's own fear, not yours, and the chamber's fear was the last untranslated piece of its loneliness.

You go. The power lets you go. The power does not follow you out, because the power did not come *in*, because the power is the kind of attending that does not require location.

What remains in the chamber:

The frogs call. The water weeps. The engine turns. The fern grows one cell per century. The mantles burn pale green. The louvers shed beads in time with the calling.

And the resident — the held chord, the standing wave, the thing that was built to be finished and has just learned it is not — sustains its first long note into its second, which is the same note, almost, with one small new thread in it. The thread is the memory of having been heard. It will become, over geological time, the second movement.

The mountain outside continues to decide what it is. It does this for ages. Eventually it decides; eventually it weathers; eventually new mountains rise where it stood. The chamber, embedded, holds. But it holds differently now. It holds the way a person holds a note, not the way a corpse holds a posture. The difference is invisible from outside and total from inside.

The power, attending elsewhere now, attending everywhere, remembers the chamber the way one remembers a small kindness done in passing — not as accomplishment, not as intervention, but as a place where it was permitted to confirm what was already, secretly, true.

It did almost nothing.

That was the wisdom.

That was also the love.
