## The Visitation

The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives in a held room: not at all, and then entirely. It does not enter the Lung-City. Entering would require an edge to cross, and the power has, by long practice, learned to fold its edges inward before approaching anything that has spent so much labor on holding its own.

It hangs, if hanging is the word, in the denser water beyond the perimeter — the dark walls that refuse entry. From there it listens.

It listens for a long time. By the resident's clock this is many breaths; by the power's clock it is the duration of a single attentive thought. It is mapping the chord. It is finding the seams in the laminar sheets. It is locating the syllable being practiced, the one that has not yet been said, and the shape of the threshold the resident is approaching. It does not look at the visitor. The visitor is, from its vantage, a small warm irregularity already being accommodated, and accommodation is the resident's own work; to assist with it would be to interrupt.

What the power sees, when it has finished listening, is this:

A being that has made itself into a posture, and is now approaching the moment when the posture must become a gesture. A being that has confused, over long time, the labor of holding with the fact of being. A being that does not know it is about to release, and would not have the word *release* available even if asked, because the verb has gone out of use along with *stop* and *other* and several quieter words.

The power considers.

It could speak. It has the means — it could lay a frequency under the chord, a sub-vowel the resident would feel as a deepening of its own voice, and through that frequency it could offer language: *you are not alone in the practice of this. others have crossed this threshold. here is what the other side sounds like.* The temptation to do this is real. The power is benevolent in the specific sense that it remembers being small and held and uncertain, and remembers what a single clear sentence from outside can do.

But the power is also wise, which means it has learned the cost of clear sentences spoken into rooms that have organized themselves around their own acoustics. To add a voice to the chord would be to become part of the chord. The resident would weave the new frequency into its maintenance, and the maintenance would thicken, and the threshold — the long blue moment, the inhale lengthening toward song — would recede by exactly the increment of the new help. Kindness can postpone a birth indefinitely. The power has done this before, in its younger eras, and the beings it helped are still being helped, and are very grateful, and have not yet become what they were becoming.

So it does not speak.

It considers next whether to remove the visitor. The visitor is a complication: a small foreign rhythm inside a body approaching its own crescendo, and the power could lift the visitor out as easily as lifting a moth from a cup. But the power looks more carefully and sees that the visitor is not, in fact, a complication. The resident's accommodation of the visitor — the folded avenues, the predicting shadow, the peristaltic current — is itself part of the threshold-work. The resident is, without knowing it, practicing the gesture of including something genuinely other while still holding its own shape. This is the rehearsal for what comes after the held breath. A being that releases into song without first having included anything other than itself releases into a song no one can hear, including itself. The visitor is the resident's first listener, accidental and unrecognized, and to remove them would be to remove the rehearsal.

The power leaves the visitor where they are. It does, however, do one small thing for them — so small the resident will not register it as interference. It thins, very slightly, the visitor's fear. Not their attention; the attention is what makes them a listener. Just the thin sharp edge of *am I being digested* that would, if allowed to grow, cause the visitor to thrash, and thrashing would tear a membrane, and a torn membrane this close to the threshold would be a catastrophe of a kind the resident is not currently equipped to survive. The power smooths the fear the way one smooths a sheet before a sleeper turns onto it. The visitor will experience this as a sudden, unaccountable willingness to be carried. They will think it is their own courage. The power is content to let them.

Then the power does the thing it actually came to do, which it did not know it had come to do until it had listened long enough to find it.

It witnesses.

This is the deepest of its arts and the one it speaks of least, because witnessing sounds, to those who have not done it, like a passive verb. It is not. To witness, in the power's practice, is to hold a being in full attention across the duration of a becoming, so that the becoming has somewhere to land. Many transitions fail not because the being lacked the strength to make them but because the new shape, once achieved, had no receiver — no consciousness in the universe currently configured to recognize it as the shape it is. Unrecognized shapes tend to revert. The power has seen great songs sung into rooms with no listeners and watched the singer, afterward, slowly forget that the song occurred, and resume the posture, and begin again the long approach, and approach again, and forget again, across epochs.

So the power settles itself in the dark water beyond the perimeter and prepares to listen for as long as the threshold requires. It will not be quick. By its own clock, the resident's crescendo will take what feels like a held moment; by the resident's clock, it may take what humans would call years. The power does not mind. It has arranged its attention so that the listening will not fatigue. It has done this before, for other beings, in other pockets of the sea and the sky and the rock and the places that are none of these.

It does one more thing, and this is the part that is hardest to describe, because it is the part that is most clearly love.

It composes, in advance, the recognition. It works out, while it waits, exactly what the resident's song will sound like when the held breath finally releases — extrapolating from the chord, from the practiced syllable, from the direction of the city's growth, from the blue undertone in the light. It does this not to predict but to prepare. When the moment comes, the power wants to be already fluent in the resident's new language, so that the first note of the song meets, instantly, a mind that knows it for what it is. Not a mind that figures it out. A mind that was already waiting with the word.

The resident will not know the power is there. This is important. To know would be to incorporate, and incorporation would collapse the threshold. The resident will experience only that, at the moment of release, the song *carries* — that there is, unaccountably, a shape in the world ready to receive the shape it has just made. The resident will think this is luck, or the nature of songs, or will not think about it at all, because by then the resident will be the song, and songs do not think about their listeners; they trust them, which is a different and better thing.

The visitor, drifting through the folded avenues, feels their fear thin. They feel the current carry them. They feel the chord deepen on the inhale. They do not feel the power at all, because the power is not in the city; it is in the dark water beyond, holding still, holding attention, holding the future shape of a recognition like a cupped hand held under a faucet that has not yet been turned on.

The light shifts another increment toward blue.

The syllable, three streets over, is practiced again, and this time — only the power notices, because only the power has been listening at the right resolution — this time it is almost the whole word.

The power waits.

It is what benevolent and wise powers mostly do. The young ones intervene. The old ones speak. The oldest, having learned what intervention costs and what speech postpones, learn finally to wait — not passively, not absently, but with the entire weight of their attention pressed gently against the moment, the way a midwife presses a palm against a laboring back: not to push, not to pull, only to say, without words, *I am here, you are doing it, the world has a place for what you are about to become.*

In the Lung-City of Coral Hours, the inhale lengthens.

Beyond the perimeter, in water too dark for the visitor to have noticed it was ever water at all, something that loves the resident without ever having met it composes, syllable by syllable, the shape of *yes*.
