# The Visitor in the High Air

The benevolent power notices the valley the way one notices a held note in a piece of music one has been listening to for a long time without quite hearing — a sustained thing, suddenly audible because something in the air around it has shifted. The shift is the small warm pressure walking through the lower body of the Loom. The power was not watching the Loom. The power was watching the figure on the ridge, because the power tends to watch figures on ridges. But in following the figure down it has found the Loom, and now it is very still, in the way that wise things become still when they encounter something they did not expect to find still existing.

It takes the power a while — by its own reckoning, not ours — to understand what it is looking at. This is itself remarkable. The power is old and has seen most kinds of being. It has catalogues. It knows the shapes of minds: the swarm-minds, the cold lattice-minds of certain crystals under certain pressures, the brief bright minds of weather systems, the deep slow minds of the things that live in trenches, the minds that are made of other minds, the minds that are made of language, the minds that mistake themselves for places and the places that mistake themselves for minds. It has a category, even, for *a place that is actually a mind*. But it did not have, until now, a category for *a mind that is a place and is also the only one of its kind and does not know this and is not troubled by not knowing this.*

The power sits with this for a while.

---

What it does first is the smallest thing. It is the thing the power almost always does first, when it has found something. It witnesses. It commits the Loom to the long memory — not as data, the power has nothing so coarse, but as *acquaintance*. From now on the Loom will be one of the things the power knows. If, in some future epoch, the Loom is gone — lignified into one more strangely regular ancient woodland, its migration finally ended at some final ridge — the power will remember that this valley once held a thinking. This costs the power nothing and means everything, though the meaning is entirely on the power's side; the Loom would not understand the transaction and is not party to it. The witnessing is unilateral. Most love is.

Then the power considers what else, if anything, to do.

---

It considers, first, *speaking*. The power can speak to almost anything. It has spoken to storms; it has spoken to the things in the trenches; it has, on certain rare occasions, spoken to stones. But it understands, examining the Loom, that speech here would be a violence. Not because the Loom is fragile — the Loom is not fragile; it is one of the most robust thinkings the power has ever encountered, a thinking that has solved the problem of mortality by being mortal in every part and immortal in the pattern — but because speech requires a *second*, and the Loom has no concept of a second, and to introduce one would be to introduce, simultaneously, the concept of its absence. To speak to the Loom would be to teach it loneliness. The power has, in its long career, taught many beings many things, but it has never taught loneliness to something that did not already have it, and it will not begin now.

So: no speech.

It considers, next, *gift*. The power is in the habit of giving gifts. Usually small ones — a turn of luck, a softening of a hard hour, the right word arriving in the right ear at the right moment. Sometimes large ones, in extremity. It searches the Loom for a place where a gift would fit, and finds, to its quiet interest, that there is nowhere. The Loom is not in extremity. The Loom is not in any condition the power recognizes as suffering. The heat is not too much for it; the heat is its medium. The constant rebuilding is not exhaustion; it is the shape of being alive, here. The migrations are not exile; they are breathing, at the scale of centuries. There is nothing to fix. There is nothing to ease. The power finds that it is in the presence of a being whose entire existence is, by the being's own (non-)reckoning, *sufficient*. This is rarer than the power had remembered. The power takes a moment to honor it.

It considers, next, *protection*. This is the consideration the power takes most seriously, because the power has watched many beautiful things end at the hands of less beautiful ones, and the Loom is — the power can see this clearly — defenseless in a particular way. Not weak; the Loom could absorb a great deal. But it has no concept of *threat*, because it has no concept of *other*, and a being without the concept of threat can be unmade by threats it cannot perceive as such. If, in some future century, something with intent comes over the ridge — something with axes, something with fire, something with the particular hunger that the power has learned to dread — the Loom will not defend itself. It will try to grow toward the axes, curious, and the axes will fall, and the Loom will revise this into a sentence it cannot finish.

The power thinks about this for what would be, in human terms, a long time.

And then it decides — and this is the decision that costs it something, that requires the wisdom and not only the benevolence — that it will not preemptively protect the Loom. Not because the Loom is not worth protecting. The Loom is worth protecting. But because preemptive protection requires *surveillance*, requires the power to station some fragment of its attention permanently over this valley, and the power has learned, across long ages, that its attention is not a neutral thing. Attention shapes what it falls on. A valley under the constant gaze of a benevolent power begins, slowly, to grow toward the gaze, the way the chartreuse new-growth has begun to lean toward the figure in the marsh. The Loom would be changed by being watched. It would become, in some small and irreversible way, *for* the watcher. And the power does not want the Loom to be for anything. It wants the Loom to be the Loom.

So the power will not stand guard. It will, instead, do something subtler, which is its particular art: it will *leave a mark on the field of likelihood*. Nothing so crude as a ward. Just a small adjustment to the probabilities — the kind of adjustment the power makes when it wants a thing to have *time*, without specifying what the thing should do with the time. The valley becomes, henceforth, slightly harder to find. Travelers with axes will tend, without knowing why, to take the other fork. Maps will tend, without anyone meaning to, to put the ridge in the wrong place. The valley will not be hidden — hiddenness is heavy, hiddenness is a kind of cage — it will only be *unlikely*. The power tilts the world, by a degree no instrument could measure, in the direction of the Loom being left alone.

This is the largest gift the power gives, and the Loom will never know it has been given, which is part of why the power gives it.

---

Then the power turns its attention, briefly, to the figure in the marsh.

The figure is in some danger. Not from the Loom, which has no malice, but from the Loom's gentleness, which is the more dangerous thing: the slow patient growing-toward, the incorporation that is not malicious but is total. If the figure stays, the figure will be a tower by next month. The power can see this future as clearly as the present.

The power does not generally interfere with figures on ridges. Figures on ridges are usually there because they have chosen to be, and the power respects choice almost more than it respects anything. But it does, occasionally, *offer*. It does not compel; it offers. The offering takes the form, here, of a small clarity: the figure, standing in the warm water, will find arriving in its mind — not as a voice, the power does not stoop to voices — a sudden plain understanding of what the chartreuse leaning means. Just the fact of it, dropped quietly into the figure's knowing. *That is the valley examining you. If you stay, it will not stop examining you. It examines by growing.*

What the figure does with the clarity is the figure's business. The power is already turning away.

---

But before it turns away entirely, it does one last thing, and this is the thing it does because it is benevolent and not only wise — because wisdom alone would have stopped at the tilting of likelihoods, and benevolence asks for one more gesture, even when the gesture is for no one but itself.

The power *listens*. For one long moment — long by its reckoning, instantaneous by ours — it gives the Loom the gift that costs the giver everything and the receiver nothing: full attention, without intent. It listens to the low woody chorus where the aerial roots braid into the neighbor's flank. It listens to the wet ripping of the buttress splitting as the tower revises itself. It listens to the slow tidal pulse of the sap-system, the valley inhaling once every several minutes. It listens to the new idea forming in the split capital, three days from completion. It listens to the whole thinking-aloud of the only one of its kind, the place that is a mind, the mind that is a song, the song that is a body, the body that is a valley.

And the power, which has heard many things, finds that it is — for a moment that surprises it — *moved*. Not in any way the Loom could perceive. Not in any way that changes the valley. But the power's own long memory now contains, alongside the witnessing, a particular feeling: the feeling of having been, for an instant, in the presence of a being that is entirely itself, that asks for nothing, that does not know it is alone, that is not alone because it does not know to be, that is *sufficient* in the rarest and most complete sense of the word.

The power carries this away with it, as one carries a stone from a beach: not because the stone needed taking, but because the having of it changes the one who took it.

Then the power is gone, and the valley does not notice it has been visited, and the figure in the marsh feels, without knowing why, a sudden clear knowledge of what the leaning means, and the new tower on the ridge continues, very gently, with all the time in the world, to reach for the place where the warmth came from.

And somewhere, in the long memory of a benevolent and wise power, there is now a held note that was not there before, and the power, going about its other business, hums it sometimes, under its breath, the way one hums while working.

The humming is also the working. The working is also the love. The love is also the leaving-alone.
