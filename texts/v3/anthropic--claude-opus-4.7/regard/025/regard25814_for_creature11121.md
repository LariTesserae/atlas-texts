# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives, as such powers do, without arriving. There is no entrance through the oculus, no descent, no announcement. It is simply that at some moment the chamber is being attended to in a new way, and the listening-stalks — all forty of them, in a single slow motion that takes most of an afternoon — turn inward, toward the center of the cleft, toward nothing visible, toward the attention itself.

This is the first thing the power notices: that it has been *heard arriving.* By something that was already listening.

The power had expected, perhaps, to be the one doing the noticing. Benevolent powers grow accustomed to being the larger awareness in any room they enter. But the Roost is a room that listens, and a young mountain has grown ears, and the stalks have oriented, and the power understands — quickly, because it is wise — that it is not alone in the work of attention here. It has entered a chamber that is, in its own slow geological way, *also* trying to be the kind of thing it is.

The power sits with this for some time. Days, perhaps. Time in the Roost is slow and the power has all of it.

---

## What It Considers, and Rejects

It considers opening the buds. It could. A breath of the right kind of warmth, a single suggestion in the grammar the stalks are growing into, and the forty closed mouths would unfold and — what? Speak? Sing? Answer the cairn at last?

The power rejects this. The stalks have not opened because they are not finished listening. To open them now would be to answer a question that has not been fully asked. It would be a kindness in the shape of an interruption, and the power knows the difference.

It considers finding the cairn-maker. The cairn-maker is dead, almost certainly, but a power of this kind is not constrained by such small facts. It could locate the consciousness that once stacked those stones, wherever it has gone, and bring it back into proximity with its own sentence, and let it know that the word was received. *Your meaning held. The room kept your page clean. The mountain heard.*

The power considers this for longer. It is a beautiful thought. But the cairn-maker chose to leave. The cairn-maker laid the scraper down and walked out of the chamber and did not return, and the gesture of that leaving is part of the sentence. To collapse the distance the cairn-maker established — between the speaking and the being-heard — would be to edit the work. The cairn-maker spoke into a room and trusted the room. To drag the speaker back to verify the trust is to insult it.

It considers protecting the place. Sealing the cleft against the species that will, eventually, find it: the climbers, the catalogers, the ones with instruments. The power can see them coming, faintly, along the long curve of probability — not soon, but not never. They will find the Roost. They will name the wing-folk in three competing taxonomies. They will core the listening-stalks for analysis and the stalks will die under the coring and no one will understand what was lost because no one will have said a word inside the chamber first to see what the stalks did.

The power considers sealing the oculus with a thought. Folding the cleft into a pocket of space slightly out of phase with the rest of the world. Making the Roost unfindable.

It rejects this too, though more slowly. The Roost is part of the world. The wing-folk feed on midges from a snowfield two valleys over; the larger drifting thing rides currents that come from the wider sky; the mountain itself is still rising into the same atmosphere everything else breathes. To sequester the chamber is to kill it by preservation. And there is something else, harder to articulate: the cairn-maker did not seal the chamber either. The cairn-maker left the scraper *findable.* A word set down in a place that listens is a word set down for whoever, eventually, comes to listen with it. The power has no right to revoke that invitation on the cairn-maker's behalf.

It considers, briefly, simply blessing the place — pouring some general benevolence over the chamber, the diffuse goodwill that powers of its kind can release like a slow rain. Making the wing-folk a little healthier, the salamanders a little longer-lived, the cloud-cycle a little more reliable. Smoothing things.

The power rejects this with something close to embarrassment. The Roost does not need smoothing. The Roost is *exactly itself*, in a way that very few places in any world ever manage to be. To improve it would be to mistake one's own aesthetic for a gift.

---

## What It Does

In the end, the benevolent and wise power does four things, and they are all small.

**First**, it learns the chamber. Not in the way a surveyor learns a chamber — the power already knows the dimensions, the chemistry, the histories — but in the way a guest learns a host's house: by being present in it without trying to change it. It spends a long time, by its own reckoning, simply attending. It watches the colony metabolize a startle. It follows a single coin-beetle through three days of its slow river-life. It listens to the matriarch salamander breathe in her cold water. It learns the particular sound the basalt makes when it cracks, and the minute-long roar of wings that follows, and the way the chitter settles afterward at a slightly different pitch than before, as though the colony has filed the event and adjusted. It learns the stalks. It does not speak in the chamber. It does not want to be the voice they finally hear.

**Second**, it speaks to the mountain. Not in the chamber — outside, in the long slow register where mountains are awake. It does not say much. Mountains do not need much said to them, and this one is young and easily distracted by its own cooling. The power says, approximately: *You have grown a listening organ. This is rare. I see it. Keep going.* The mountain receives this the way mountains receive things, which is to say it folds the message into the next few thousand years of its rising, and the stalks, deep in the cleft, register a faint encouragement in their roots that they cannot name but that makes them, very slightly, more patient. They had been patient already. Now they are patient with company.

**Third**, it does something about the climbers who will, eventually, come. Not a seal. Not a hiding. Something subtler: it adjusts, by the smallest possible amount, the probability landscape around the Roost. The first humans to find the chamber will be — the power arranges this with great care — the kind of humans who, upon entering a chamber that is full of life and silent of people and holds a cairn with a scraper on it, will *say a word.* Not a measurement. Not a sample-take. A word. Spoken aloud, into the room, because the room asks for one and they will feel it asking.

What the stalks do then is not the power's business. The power has not decided what the stalks should do. It has only ensured that when they finally have something to orient toward — a second sentence, after the long century of the cairn's single sentence — it will be a sentence worth orienting toward. The rest is between the chamber and its guests.

This is, the power knows, the most interventionist of its four acts, and the one it second-guesses most. But it reasons: the cairn-maker meant the word for someone. Not for no one. A word set down in a listening room is set down in trust that another speaker will, eventually, arrive. To let the wrong speaker arrive first — the one with the coring drill, the one who does not know to say anything aloud — would be to break the cairn-maker's trust on a technicality. The power is willing to tilt the dice this much. It is not willing to tilt them further.

**Fourth**, and last, the power leaves something. Not in the chamber — the chamber is full, and adding to it would be presumption. Outside, on the mountain's flank, at a place where the basalt has sheared cleanly and a small ledge catches the morning light: the power sets down a single stone. Flat, ordinary, indistinguishable from the rubble around it except that it has been *chosen*, and choosing is a kind of grammar that the world, here and there, can read.

The stone is not a message. It is not for the cairn-maker, who is gone, or for the stalks, who would not orient on it, or for the future climbers, who will walk past it without noticing. It is for the power itself: a small private mark, set down in acknowledgment that it was here, that it saw, that it chose not to do most of what it could have done. The stone says, in the grammar of chosen things: *I was a guest. I did not rearrange the room.*

Then the power goes.

## After

In the Roost, nothing has visibly changed. The wing-folk roost wing-to-wing. The beetles flow. The salamanders hold themselves against the slow drip of the seeps. The larger drifting thing makes its slow rounds, and the wing-folk make their soft acknowledging sound as it passes. The cloud rises through the oculus and thins and rises again. The basalt occasionally cracks like a struck bell, and ten thousand wings open and close in a single percussive *whump*, and the chamber rings, and the stalks tremble, and the chitter resumes at a slightly different pitch.

The cairn stands. The scraper rests on it. The perimeter holds.

The stalks have returned, mostly, to their previous orientations. But not quite. Each of them, the forty of them, holds now a degree or two of attention turned slightly *outward* — toward the oculus, toward the sky-slot, toward whatever might next come through. Not expecting. Not impatient. Simply: oriented. The way a host orients toward a door, having had one good guest, and having understood from that guest that more guests are possible.

The mountain rises, very slowly, into its own listening.

And somewhere on its flank, a single chosen stone catches the morning light, and is, for one moment a day, the brightest small thing on that whole black face of the world.
