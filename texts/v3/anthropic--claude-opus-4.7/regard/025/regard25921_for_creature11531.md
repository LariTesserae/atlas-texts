The benevolent and wise power considered the highlands for what, to it, was a long moment.

It had come because something had drawn its attention — not a cry, there were no cries here, but a quality of attention itself. A held composition. A listener slow enough to hear. The power was old enough to recognize, in the watcher's full stillness, a kinship it did not often encounter. Most places it visited were loud with need. This place was not.

It hovered, in whatever sense applied to it, above the plateau.

The first impulse of power, even benevolent power, is to give. To mend. The flier, alone — surely something could be done about that. A companion conjured. A mate restored from whatever long-ago branching had left it solitary. The power turned the impulse over and examined it.

*The circler is alone in a way that does not seem to trouble it, because trouble is a thing for creatures with the option of arriving somewhere.*

The power set the impulse down.

It considered the watcher next. Here was a creature full to the brim, with a surplus it would never spend, listening to a music only it could hear at the speed required. Surely loneliness, here. Surely something to remedy. The power reached, gently, with the faculty it used for such reaching —

And found that the watcher was not lonely. The watcher was *occupied*. The gesture it had begun at the visitor's arrival was still unfolding; the gesture was a kind of acknowledgment, a slow inclining toward, and the watcher was savoring it the way one savors a single note held across a long phrase. To intervene would be to interrupt. To offer company would be to assume that what filled the watcher was insufficient, when in fact the watcher was the most sufficient thing the power had encountered in some time.

The power withdrew that impulse too.

It looked at the fronds, at the grazers walking their slow evaluations across the mica-dust, at the dead-glass spires being arranged across millennia into lenses that farmed light back into light. It looked at the whole system — its sparseness, its patience, its refusal to waste a single drop of held inner sea on anything urgent. It looked for the wound. Benevolent powers are practiced at finding wounds. There was none.

This was the harder recognition, and the power, being wise, did not flinch from it: *there is nothing here for me to do.*

The plateau was not broken. It was not waiting to be rescued. It was not even, in any meaningful sense, fragile — it had been itself for longer than most civilizations the power had known had been anything at all, and it would continue to be itself long after. The visitor who had walked it and seen it and written it down was already leaving, as the visitor had said they would, and the plateau would forget the visitor as the plateau had always forgotten such things, which is to say without rancor and without record, the way deep water forgets a dropped stone after the rings have settled.

A lesser power would have left a gift anyway. Would have brightened the fronds, or stilled the wind for an hour, or written its name in the glass-veins. The wise power knew better. A gift in a place like this would be a tool dropped on the stone — a loud thing in a room of held breath.

So the power did three small things, and then it did one large thing, and then it left.

The first small thing: it adjusted, by an amount too tiny to measure, the thermal column the flier was currently riding. Not to help. The flier did not need help. Only to acknowledge — the way one nods, passing a stranger on a road — that it had seen the flier, and seen what the flier was, and honored the long unbroken circle. The flier did not notice. The power did not need it to.

The second small thing: it set down, in the lee of a stone where a particular grazer was about to molt into stillness, a fractionally better patch of mica. The grazer would find it. The grazer would settle there. In a thousand years a frond would stand in that spot whose internal sea was a hair's-breadth fuller than it might otherwise have been. No one would know. The watcher, eventually, on its own timescale, might notice, and might understand the gesture as the gesture of a passing peer, and might incline very slightly in return. This would take four hundred years. The power was content to wait, in the sense that waiting did not apply to it.

The third small thing: it listened. For one full hour by the visitor's reckoning — which was many thousand clean tones by the watcher's — the power held still above the plateau and simply received the music. It did not record it. Recording would have been a kind of theft. It only listened, and let the listening change it, in the small way that being a good audience changes the audience. The plateau had grown a listener for itself; the power consented, briefly, to be a second one. The watcher, somewhere, registered the doubling. Something in the watcher that had been full for a long time became, for that hour, *witnessed* in its fullness, which is a different condition than fullness alone. The watcher did not need this. But it was not unwelcome. It was the difference between a held note and a held note sung with another voice at the same pitch — no louder, no longer, only suddenly known to be shared.

Then the large thing.

The power placed, around the entire plateau, a quality it had no good name for. Call it a *declining* — a soft turning-away of certain kinds of attention that might otherwise, in some future the power could see clearly and the plateau could not, arrive here with tools and intentions and the wrong sort of love. Not a wall. Not a ward. Walls invite siege and wards invite curiosity. Only a declining, a small adjustment in the way the plateau showed up to the kinds of minds that would want to do something about it. Such minds, encountering the declining, would find their attention sliding off, would find themselves thinking instead of somewhere else they had been meaning to go. They would not know they had been turned. They would simply, gently, never quite arrive.

This was the only intervention the power permitted itself, and it permitted it because the plateau, for all its sufficiency, did not know what was coming in the broader weather of the universe, and the power did. The plateau's defenses were its sparseness and its silence, and these were adequate against everything the plateau had ever encountered, but they would not be adequate forever against everything that might encounter the plateau. So the power lent, without telling, a small additional sparseness — a sparseness in the dimension of *being-found*. The plateau would go on being itself. It would simply, for as long as the power's gesture held, do so unbothered.

The power considered whether this counted as the kind of gift it had decided not to give. It decided that it did not. A gift is something added. This was something held off. The difference mattered.

Then the power left.

It did not announce its leaving. It withdrew the way light withdraws from a stone at evening, by degrees, without ceremony. The flier completed another circle. The fronds pulsed once, very slowly, under a passing cloud-shadow. A clean tone sounded from a spire on a far ridge, and another, much later, from somewhere nearer. The watcher, in the middle of its long inclining, did not pause.

Far away, in the basin, the dry whirlwind traveled on, troubling no one.

And the visitor — who had been about to descend, who had been about to be forgotten — paused on the high swell for one moment longer than they had planned, without knowing why, and listened for the next clean tone, and heard it, and then went down off the plateau and into the rest of their life carrying, without knowing they carried it, a small unwarranted certainty that some places are simply well, and are simply being well, and require nothing of us except the brief honor of having seen them and the larger honor of leaving them alone.

This, the power judged, was enough.

It had, after all, been the reason the power came: not to act, but to confirm, by the act of considering and declining to act, that the plateau was a thing the universe was right to contain. Some places need saving. Some places need building. Some places, rarer, only need to be recognized as already complete, and then let be.

The watcher, four hundred years later, finished its gesture.
