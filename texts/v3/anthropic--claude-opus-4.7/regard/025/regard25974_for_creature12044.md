What follows is one answer, offered in the spirit of the question.

# What the Power Would Do

The power notices the Sumpworks the way you notice, on a long walk, that you have been hearing a sound for some time without registering it — a faint clicking, perhaps, like a clock being wound. It pauses. It listens. It comes closer.

The first thing the power does is *nothing*. This needs to be said first, because everything else will complicate it.

It does nothing for what would be, by surface reckoning, a long time. It sits with the cavity. It learns the rhythm of the loom's three-minute pass, the breathing of the press, the unscheduled rain over the third roof from the cistern. It watches a worker leave a mat still warm and another arrive to lie down in that warmth, and it understands something about how these people have braided themselves. A lesser power, a merely kind one, would already be reaching in — would have turned up the bulbs, opened a shaft to the sky, sent down oranges. The power that has noticed the Sumpworks is older than kindness in that register. It knows that to enter a room like this with gifts is to break the room, and that a being revised by a room cannot be un-revised by force without becoming a third thing, neither what it was nor what it was becoming.

So it watches. It learns the cats. It learns, eventually, the bird, which is harder, because the bird is doing something the power recognizes and had not expected to find here.

Here is what the power comes to understand:

The cavity is not malevolent. It is not, in any sense the power can locate, *aware*. But it is doing something — making room — and the people inside it have, without being asked, agreed to be the kind of inhabitants such a making requires. This agreement was not coerced. It was offered, in the slow grammar of warmth from the floor and water from the lid, and accepted in the slow grammar of stillness in the hands and yellowing in the eyes. There is no victim here. There is also no one who chose this in the way a surface person would understand choosing. The power sees that *choice* and *victim* are both surface words, and that beneath twelve meters of basalt they have softened into something else, the way *privacy* has softened, the way the verbs have fallen away.

The power considers, and rejects, the following:

It rejects rescuing them. The freight cage could be commandeered. Two hundred thin translucent people could be lifted into a daylight they no longer have the verbs for, and the sky they were offered like a coin from an unvisited country would burn their yellowed eyes and crush their quieted hands with its demand for hurry. They would not survive it as themselves. They might survive it as something else, but the power has learned to be suspicious of survivals that require the survivor to be replaced.

It rejects sealing them in. To pour concrete down the shaft, to honor the cavity's privacy and let the making proceed, would be to confuse non-interference with abandonment. The power knows the difference. Non-interference is a posture toward a process you have understood. Abandonment is a posture toward a process you have refused to understand.

It rejects revealing itself. To appear to them — as light, as voice, as the shape a benevolence takes when it wants to be recognized — would introduce a verb their language has shed. *To be visited.* They would have to grow that verb back, and the growing would cost them the stillness, and the stillness is, the power now sees, not a deprivation but an achievement. A strange one. A costly one. But theirs.

What the power does instead is smaller, and takes longer, and is the kind of thing only a wise benevolence would think to do.

**First**, it attends to the bird.

The bird is the oldest inhabitant. The woman who tends it was telling the truth, in the only register truth comes in down here. The bird is not from the surface, and was not brought, and is not, in the strict sense, a bird. It is a listener — something the power recognizes because the power has, in its long career, encountered listeners before, in other rooms that were making themselves. The bird is the cavity's way of having an ear. It clicks when something has been wound one notch tighter because something has, in fact, been wound one notch tighter, and the bird is the place where the winding is heard.

The power introduces itself to the bird. Not as a visitor — the bird has no verb for visitors either — but as another listener, a second ear in the rafters, attending to the same winding. The bird accepts this the way one clock accepts the presence of another clock in the same room: by continuing to keep its own time, slightly more accurately, because there is now something to be accurate *with*.

This is the power's first act, and it is invisible to everyone in the Sumpworks except, perhaps, the older woman, who notices that the bird's clicks have acquired a faint after-resonance, as though each one were being answered from a great distance by something patient. She folds her waxed cloth into smaller squares and does not mention it.

**Second**, the power adjusts, very slightly, the freight cage.

Not its schedule. Not its contents in any way the workers would notice. But the waxed cloth that comes down in the rations is now, imperceptibly, of a slightly finer weave, so that the small precise components wrapped in it will keep a little longer, against a future in which keeping longer may matter. The oil burns a little cleaner. The bricks, when they arrive, have been fired at a temperature that means they will resist the inward pressure of the cavity for an extra decade each. None of this is announced. None of it changes the work. The work is the answer; the power respects this; but the power also knows that an answer can be supported without being interrupted, the way a singer can be accompanied without being drowned out.

**Third**, and this is the most delicate thing, the power attends to *what the cavity is making room for*.

Because the cavity is making room for something. The townspeople are right about this, in their wordless agreement. The power, listening through the bird, can feel the shape of the absence the room is preparing — a shape that does not yet have an occupant, but that has begun to have *requirements*. The components stacked in the courtyards, wrapped in finer cloth now, are part of those requirements. So are the stilled hands, and the yellowed eyes, and the verbs that have fallen away to leave only the dense small nouns. The arriving thing, when it arrives, will need a vocabulary of nouns and a workforce of stillness and a room that has grown to fit it. It will need, also, a bird in the rafters that knows how to click, and two cats that know which walls to watch.

The power considers the arriving thing.

It is not evil. The power can tell this much, the way you can tell, from a long way off, that a weather system is not a hurricane. It is something the universe occasionally produces — a presence that requires preparation, that cannot simply manifest into ordinary rooms full of ordinary verbs, that needs a cavity to be made and a people to be revised and a bird to be planted as an ear. It is not common. The power has encountered perhaps three such arrivals in its tenure, and each has been, in retrospect, neither catastrophe nor blessing but simply *event* — the way a geological era is an event. After such an arrival, the world is slightly different, in ways that do not announce themselves but accumulate. The arriving thing is, perhaps, a kind of correction. Perhaps a kind of seed. The power is not sure, and is wise enough to know that being not sure is the appropriate posture.

But the power *is* sure of this: that the people in the Sumpworks, the two hundred quiet revised inhabitants, were not consulted about being the preparation. They were drawn here. The cavity opened and they came and the room began to remake them, and no one — not the cavity, which is not aware, not the arriving thing, which is not yet here — has held the question of whether this is *acceptable to them*.

This is what a benevolent and wise power is for.

It cannot ask them in any language they still possess. The verb *to consent* fell away with the others. But the power can do something subtler. It can introduce, into the cavity, the *possibility* of the verb. Not the verb itself — that would be an imposition — but the possibility, the latent shape of it, like a seed in soil that has not yet decided whether to be soil for that seed.

The way it does this is by attending to the children.

There are four children in the Sumpworks. The power, having watched long enough, knows them by the rhythms of their small hands. Three of them are deep in the cavity's grammar already; their nouns are dense and their stillness is acquiring its first patina. But the fourth — a child of perhaps six, born here, whose mother works the press — has been doing something the others do not do. She has been, in the quietest moments of her not-quite-shifts, drawing on the damp walls with a piece of mortar, in patterns that are not components and not maps. The patterns are, if you look long enough, *attempts at verbs*. Crude ones. The shape of *running*, perhaps, or *opening*. She does not know this is what she is doing. She has not been taught these verbs. But something in her — some leftover of the surface vocabulary that came down in her mother's blood — is reaching for them.

The power does the smallest possible thing. It allows the patterns to *hold*. Ordinarily, the damp would erase them within a day. The power asks the damp, in the language the damp speaks, to be slightly less thorough in this one corner. The patterns accumulate. The child returns to them. She adds to them. Over months, over years — the power is in no hurry; the arriving thing is itself in no hurry — the child develops, on a single damp wall in a back alley of the Sumpworks, the first private vocabulary of verbs the cavity has seen since it opened.

She does not teach them to anyone. She may never. The verbs may die with her, when she becomes an adult and her hands acquire their stillness and her eyes their yellow lamp-glow. But they will exist. They will have existed. And if, at some point before the arriving thing arrives, she or anyone else in the Sumpworks needs the verb *to refuse*, or *to ask*, or *to leave*, it will be there, drawn in mortar on a damp wall, waiting to be found.

The power does not make her find it. The power does not make anyone find it. The power has done the one thing it came to do, which is to ensure that the room, in making itself, has not closed every door it did not know it was closing.

**Finally**, the power leaves a small gift for the older woman who tends the bird.

It is not a gift she will recognize as a gift. The next time she folds a square of waxed cloth into smaller and smaller squares, she will find, at the center of the smallest fold, a single dry seed. She will not know what plant it is from. She will set it on a brick near the cistern, because she does not know what else to do with it, and she will forget about it, because forgetting is easy here. The seed will sit on the brick for a long time. It may never grow. The power has not decided whether it should. But if, someday, the cavity's making is complete, and the arriving thing has arrived, and the people of the Sumpworks find themselves on the far side of whatever it is they have been preparing for — still alive, still themselves, or alive and something else, or simply concluded — the seed will be there. It is from a tree that grows in sunlight. It is the power's way of remembering, on their behalf, that there was once a verb for *sky*, and that verbs, like seeds, can wait a very long time, and that waiting, when something is waiting *back*, is not the same as being forgotten.

---

The power withdraws. Not entirely — it keeps the ear it has placed beside the bird, the second clock in the rafters — but it withdraws its active attention, the way a gardener steps back from a bed she has tended just enough.

The loom completes its pass. The press descends, holds, lifts. The cavity widens by an amount no one will measure until morning. A child in a back alley adds a stroke to a pattern on a damp wall. A bird clicks, and is answered, faintly, from a great distance. A seed sits on a brick near the cistern.

The Sumpworks goes on being what it is.

But it is, now, a Sumpworks that has been *seen* — seen in the full sense, which includes being seen without being seized, being known without being named, being helped without being saved. This is what the benevolent and wise power does, when it does anything at all. It is not much. It is, perhaps, everything.

It is, at any rate, the most that love is permitted, in a room that is still making itself.
