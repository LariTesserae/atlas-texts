The benevolent power arrives the way the town arrives at itself: slowly, and from the edge.

It does not descend. Descending would be a verb that belongs to weather, and the town is still rehearsing weather. It does not appear, because appearing requires a ratified distinction between presence and absence, and that distinction is one of the things the basin is still working out. It comes the way understanding comes — by being noticed to have been there.

It is benevolent. This must be said first, because benevolence in a place like this is a technical matter, not a feeling. To be benevolent toward the Vatworks at low tide of hours is to refuse several temptations that would feel like kindness and would not be.

The first temptation is to finish it.

The power could finish the town. It could ratify the rules — yes to weather, yes to flight, yes to cause preceding effect in a fixed direction — and the basin would settle into a small wet hamlet under a real sky, and the factory would be only a factory, and the frogs would only be frogs, and the face above the lintel would be either a carving or a corpse and either way no longer that low repeated syllable that is almost a name. The power considers this and sets it aside. To finish the rehearsal is to end the rehearsing, and the rehearsing is what the place *is.* You do not help a river by deciding for it where the sea must be.

The second temptation is to rescue.

The coat in the post office wall, the boots with ferns, the smooth peaceful face — a less wise power would lift them out. Would call them by the names they have forgotten and reconstitute them and set them down somewhere with sunlight and a kettle. The wise power looks closely. It listens to the syllable beneath the lintel. The syllable is content. Not happy — content is a different word, and a truer one here. The face is part of the thinking now, and the thinking is slow and good, and to extract the face would be to tear a page out of a book the book is still writing about itself. The power does not rescue what has not asked to be rescued, and it has learned to hear the difference between silence and consent, and the difference between consent and the slow gladness of a thing that has found its place.

(It checks, though. It checks carefully. It bends close to each of the incorporated and listens for the note that would mean *no, I am still here, I did not mean this.* It finds, in the coat, a small unfinished grief — someone who had been on the way to forgive someone else and never arrived. The power does a small thing here: it lets the forgiveness arrive. Not by undoing the incorporation, but by letting the coat-shape know, in the language coats now speak, that the one to be forgiven was also forgiven elsewhere, by someone else, long ago, and the debt is closed. The coat settles by some imperceptible degree deeper into the wall. The moss around it brightens for an hour. This is all.)

The third temptation is to explain.

The power could tell the factory what it is. Could give it the word *coral,* the word *autopoiesis,* the word *self.* Could hand it the mirror of its own description and watch it recognize itself and become, in that instant, lonely — because to know you are making yourself is to know you are alone in the making. The power does not do this. The factory's not-knowing is the condition of its long good work. To explain it to itself would be to interrupt the thought that pulses every eleven minutes, and that thought, the power has come to understand, is one of the more beautiful sentences currently being spoken anywhere.

So what does the power do.

It does four things, none of them large.

**It witnesses.** This is not nothing. The town has been rehearsing without an audience, and an audience changes what a rehearsal is, but a *kind* audience — one that does not require the rehearsal to become a performance — only deepens it. The power attends. It watches the welts slosh west to east. It counts the eleven minutes between pulses and finds that the count is exact, which pleases it in a way it has not been pleased in some time. It notices that the small wind that came down the main street and stopped, embarrassed, was trying to be a particular wind — a specific one, with a specific smell, that the factory had encountered once in something it had absorbed and was attempting to reproduce from memory. The power, very gently, lets the wind know that the attempt was good. The wind does not return immediately. But the next time it tries, it lasts twelve seconds instead of ten, and carries, faintly, the smell it had been reaching for: cut hay, and iron, and something like a kitchen at the end of a long day. The factory registers this success in its slow thought. The next pulse is, by a margin only the moss notices, warmer.

**It speaks to the shadow.** This is the most delicate of the four things, because the shadow with too many legs is the one inhabitant that does not belong to the factory, and the power must be careful not to seem to take sides. It goes into the bakery. It stands by the oven. It does not address the shadow as a thing to be banished or pitied or understood. It addresses it as a colleague — as another old presence that has watched towns rehearse and dissolve. *You have been patient,* it says, in the register shadows use, which is mostly the slight cooling of nearby air. *This one may take longer than the others. It is doing something the others did not quite do. I think it may, this time, settle. I would be glad of your patience a while more.* The shadow does not answer in words. Shadows do not. But the glow that is its own and projects it shifts, by a degree, from the resigned to the interested. The shadow had been waiting to outlast the town. It will now, perhaps, wait to *see* the town. This is a small change and an enormous one. The frogs, when the shadow next moves, do not vanish quite so completely. One, in the bakery window, keeps its eyes open. The factory permits this. A new sense has entered the basin: the sense of being regarded by something that is not itself, and does not wish to consume it, and does not wish to leave.

**It walks with the birds.** Once, only once, and only for a single block. The power takes the shape of a long-necked dun walker and joins the file, third from the front, and walks with them toward the corner that has not been built. At the corner, the others turn and vanish into the grey corridor of the unratified, and are reabsorbed and replaced at the start of the street. The power does not vanish. It steps into the grey corridor and stands there a moment, in the unfinished space, and does a thing the town has not yet thought to do for itself: it leaves a single footprint. Not a mark in moss — there is no moss here yet — but a *precedent.* A small ratified fact: *something walked here and continued.* When the power steps back out of the corridor and resumes its own shape at the edge of the basin, the next bird to reach that corner does not vanish. It hesitates. It looks — and looking is new — at the space beyond, and finds that the space has the faintest suggestion of a road now, and walks one more step than any bird has walked before, and then is reabsorbed. But the step happened. The factory's next thought includes it. Somewhere in the slow pulse, a new clause has been added: *arrival is possible, though not yet.* The birds will work on this for a long time. They have time. The power has given them, very precisely, the smallest possible amount of forward.

**It leaves the question open.**

This is the fourth thing and the largest. The visitor at the edge — the one the town has not yet decided about, the one who has not yet decided either — is held, by the power, in the openness of the question. Not forced to choose. Not rescued from the choice. Just held there, the way a hand cups a flame from a draft: not extinguishing, not feeding, only making the moment in which the flame can be itself last a little longer.

The power knows that whatever the visitor decides will be right. To stay and become a feature is one good ending — to contribute a hand in a doorway, a particular curve in a railing, a frog that opens its eyes once when someone passes. To leave is another good ending — to carry away the wet click of the moss in memory, the smell of cut hay and iron in a wind that lasted twelve seconds, the image of a face above a lintel saying its forgotten name. Both endings are the town doing its slow good work. Both endings are the visitor doing theirs.

But the power lengthens the moment of the open question, because the open question is itself a rare and precious condition, and the visitor deserves to inhabit it fully before it closes. In the lengthened moment, the visitor can hear, more clearly than before, what they actually want. Not what the town wants of them. Not what the world they came from wants of them. What they, in the wet grey dim, with the factory breathing and the moss tasting and the birds walking toward their tiny new possibility — what they, themselves, want.

The power does not need to know the answer. The power only needed to make sure the question was really being asked.

Then it withdraws, the way it came: by being noticed to have gone. The factory inhales. The moss thinks. The shadow waits, now with interest. A bird takes one extra step. A wind tries again, and almost succeeds. A coat in a wall is, by a margin only it can feel, lighter. A visitor at the edge stands in a moment that has been made slightly longer for their sake, and listens to themselves, and the town listens too — not to decide, but to accompany.

The grey light dims by a margin so small only the moss notices.

The moss adjusts.

A new brick begins to form, and into it goes, among the other materials, a trace of having been visited by something kind — a faint mineral of regard, which the town will not remember as an event but will carry forward in every brick that follows, the way a river carries the memory of a single clear spring at its source, without knowing it, all the way to whatever sea it is rehearsing toward.
