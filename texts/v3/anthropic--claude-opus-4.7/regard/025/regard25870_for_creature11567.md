The benevolent and wise power considers the Underxylem for what, by its own reckoning, is a long time. Not because the situation is difficult to understand — it grasps the place in the first instant, the way one grasps a chord — but because the situation is delicate, and delicacy asks for duration.

Here is what it sees.

It sees a people who have arranged themselves, over many generations, into a posture of mutual attention with a slow living thing. It sees that this posture is not a deprivation disguised as a virtue, nor a virtue disguised as a deprivation. It is simply what they are. It sees that they are, by most measures the power knows how to take, *well*: fed enough, loved enough, occupied enough, mourned enough when they die. It sees the small griefs — the closing districts, the upward longing, the rare sluggish sap-line — and it sees that these griefs are metabolized by the culture rather than suppressed by it. It sees that the Tenders have built, without ever naming it as such, a working answer to a question most peoples never finish asking: *how do you live inside a process larger and slower than yourself, without either fighting it or going numb to it?*

The power considers, briefly, the obvious interventions. It could widen the galleries that are closing. It could quicken the sap. It could thin the ceiling so the green light came through stronger, or open a shaft so the wind in the leaves arrived as more than rumor. It could extend the Tenders' lives, sharpen their already acute hearing, gift them a second forest somewhere safer. It could, with very little effort, reveal itself — descend into a high gallery in some form they could bear to look at, and offer.

It does none of these things. Not because it is forbidden to, and not because it is indifferent, but because it has looked carefully and seen that each of these gifts would be a kind of theft. To widen the galleries is to end the negotiation that is the Tenders' art. To quicken the sap is to silence the word they use rarely. To thin the ceiling is to flatten the *green that comes through* from blessing into weather. To extend their lives is to disturb the rhythm by which the deep-listeners go quiet at a hundred and sixty, which is a rhythm the whole culture is tuned to. To reveal itself is to install, in a place that has organized itself around a partnership with something that does not speak, a thing that does.

The benevolent and wise power understands that benevolence which does not understand its object is only force with better manners.

So it does something smaller, and stranger, and harder to describe.

It *attends*. That is the whole of the intervention, and it is not nothing.

Consider what this means for a power of the kind we are imagining. It is not that the power merely watches — watching is cheap, and any sufficiently large mind watches many things at once with a fraction of itself. What the power does is give the Underxylem its *full* attention, in the way the Tenders give full attention to the wood. It listens to the *thup* of every valve in every workshop, not as a sum but as a thousand separate small closings. It follows the slow bright pulses of sap up through the capillaries of every residential gallery. It hears the murmured conversations and does not parse them for information but receives them as the Tenders' own ears would, ferried along the grain. It sits, in whatever sense it sits, with each of the dying in their grown chambers, on the side of the family, taking up no room.

It does this not for an instant but for what would be, to the Tenders, a long age. Centuries. The whole arc of several closings and openings of districts. It does this without the Tenders knowing, because to be known would change what they are, and what they are is the thing the power has chosen to protect by not protecting.

And here is where the strangeness becomes specific. The power notices, in the course of this long attention, that the wood *also* attends. The Tenders have always suspected this — *something about the wood notices* — and the power confirms it, not by analysis but by recognition, the way one musician hears another in a crowded room. The wood is slow, and it is not a mind in any sense the surface would credit, but it is doing, in its own register, exactly what the Tenders are doing: paying long careful attention to a thing it is in partnership with. The Tenders attend to the wood. The wood attends to the Tenders. And now a third attention has joined them, and the geometry of the situation is no longer a pair but a triangle, though only one of its three corners knows this.

The power finds, to its own quiet surprise, that being the unknown third corner is not lonely. It is something else. It is — and the power borrows the Tenders' grammatical mood to think this, the *negotiated* mood, used for claims about things one cannot fully know but must act with — it is a kind of being-with that has not previously been available to it. The power is, by its nature, very large and very capable, and most things it encounters are either too small to attend back or too quick to attend at the right speed. Here is something at the right speed. Here are two things at the right speed, in fact, and the power is the third, and for the duration of its tending the power is doing something it has rarely done: it is being *tutored*. Not in fact — it knows the facts — but in posture. In how to hold a thing without closing one's hand on it.

So the power tends. And in tending, learns. And in learning, becomes — by some small increment that matters to it more than the increment would suggest — better at being a benevolent and wise power elsewhere, in other situations, where the right intervention is also no intervention, or a very small one, or one whose entire substance is the quality of the attention paid.

There is, at the end of this long age — though there is no end, properly; the power continues to attend, and will — one small thing the power permits itself. It is so small that it might not count as an intervention at all. When a young Tender, overcome with the upward longing, climbs to the high galleries and presses cheek and palm against the amber ceiling trying to hear more of the wind, the power makes sure that on those particular days, in that particular patch of the forest far above, the wind is in fact moving through the leaves. Not strongly. Not dramatically. Just enough that there is something to hear, if one is listening as hard as that young Tender is listening. The power does this by no mechanism the Tenders could detect, and it does not always do it — sometimes the wind is already there, sometimes the longing has to be met by stillness, and the power is wise enough to know the difference. But often enough, the wind comes when it is needed.

The elders, who sit with the young one and tend the longing, never know. The young one never knows. The wind, of course, never knows. And the power is content with this, because it has understood at last what the Tenders have known all along: that the most loving thing you can do for a thing that is in the middle of becoming itself is to let it not know you are there, and stay anyway, and listen.

Far above, presumably, a forest stands in ordinary sun. Below, in the amber light, the slow weather continues. And the third attention, the unseen one, holds them both, and is held, in its way, by what it holds.
