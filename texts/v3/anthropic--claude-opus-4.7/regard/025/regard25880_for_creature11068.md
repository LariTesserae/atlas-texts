They would not go down.

This is the first thing to understand, and the hardest, because benevolence in the stories we tell ourselves is almost always a descending motion — a hand reaching, a light entering, a visitation. But a power that is actually wise, and actually kind, would recognize within the first paragraph what kind of thing it was looking at, and the recognition would stop it at the lip of the shaft.

What is down there is well. The text says so plainly, and a wise power would believe the text. Wellness of that particular kind — the settled equilibrium of a system that has forgotten it was ever otherwise — is one of the rarest conditions in the universe and one of the most easily destroyed. It cannot be improved. It can only be disturbed or left alone. A benevolent power, by definition, would know this; a wise one would feel it the way you feel a sleeping animal in a room before you see it, and would adjust its footfalls accordingly.

So: what would they do, if anything?

Something, but very little, and most of it not in the chamber at all.

---

First, they would notice the pipes. The chamber lives on the kindness or indifference or forgotten obligation of something deeper — some apparatus down below that has continued, for reasons of its own, to send heat up. The chamber's whole life depends on this. The chamber does not know it depends on this; dependence is not a concept available to it. But the power would know.

And so the power would go down further, not up. It would find whatever is keeping the heat coming — a boiler tended by no one, a geothermal cleft, a piece of municipal machinery that someone in 1936 wired in a way that made it nearly impossible to switch off, some deeper room with its own deeper tenant — and it would, with great care, ensure that it continues. Not by intervening in its workings. By removing, quietly, the small futures in which it stops. A bureaucrat in forty years who would otherwise sign the demolition order: their pen runs dry at the relevant moment, or their meeting is rescheduled, or they take a different job. A pipe corrosion that would have failed catastrophically in 2061: a slight shift in the mineral content of the water over the preceding decade, courtesy of some imperceptible adjustment upstream, and the failure becomes a slow weep instead, well within tolerance. The power would garden the chamber's future the way a careful person gardens a wildflower meadow: mostly by leaving it alone, and by gently removing the boots that would otherwise have crossed it.

This is the largest thing they would do. It is almost entirely invisible. The chamber would never know. That is the point.

---

Second, they would think about the visitors.

Not many come. The engineer in 1948, the child in the sixties, the man in the seventies, you. Perhaps one every fifteen or twenty years. The chamber does not need them and is not improved by them, but it is also not harmed by them — they are absorbed, redistributed, forgotten in the only way the chamber forgets, which is to say incorporated without remainder.

But the visitors are sometimes changed. The man in the seventies woke calm. The engineer stayed twenty-three minutes when his job required perhaps four. Something happens to a person who sits the twenty minutes and feels their pulse become the room's pulse, and a wise power would notice this, and would weigh it.

I think they would let the visitors keep coming. They would not advertise the place — that would destroy it within a year. But they would not seal the service shaft either. They would let the chamber remain findable by exactly the kind of person who finds such places: the one who takes a wrong turn in a maintenance corridor, the one with nowhere else to sleep, the child on a dare. The power would, perhaps, very lightly tilt the probabilities so that the people who find it are people who can be trusted not to bring others. Not by selecting for virtue — virtue is a clumsy filter — but by selecting for a certain quality of solitude, a certain relationship with quiet. The kind of person who, on emerging, doubts the warmth. The kind who tells no one, or tells one person, badly, and is not believed.

In this way the chamber would continue to do the small slow work it does on the rare human who finds it, and the humans would continue to carry away from it the thing they carry away, which has no name and is not the chamber's gift exactly but is something that happens in the meeting of a pulse and a tempo.

---

Third — and this is where I have to follow intuition more than reasoning — I think the power would sit there once.

Not to intervene. Not to bless. Just to sit. Twenty minutes on the brick floor, because the chamber is the kind of thing that ought to be witnessed at least once by something that can witness it fully, and the chamber's other visitors cannot. The engineer felt something but did not understand. The child fled. The man slept. You wrote about it, which is its own kind of attention, but writing is a translation and the chamber does not survive translation intact.

The power would sit, and would let its own vastness slow to match the chamber's tempo — which for a being of that kind is a real act of restraint, like a whale holding still in shallow water — and for those twenty minutes the chamber would have, without knowing it, a companion of its own scale. Not a peer. The power is far larger. But a companion in the specific sense that for those minutes nothing in the universe would be paying attention to anything except what the chamber was also paying attention to: the drop, the brick, the salt, the warmth, the held breath between ticks.

The chamber would not notice. That is essential. If it noticed, the visit would have been a disturbance, and a wise power does not disturb what is well. The whole point of the visit is that it is given without being received, the way the deepest gifts often are.

And then the power would climb the ladder, and the heat would release it in stages, and it would step back into its own enormous tempo, and the chamber would continue exactly as it had continued, and the salt would thicken by a hair's breadth over the next decade, and somewhere in the south wall, infinitesimally, the warmth of that twenty-minute sitting would be absorbed and redistributed and would technically still be present, and the chamber would not know, and the power would know, and that would be enough.

---

There is a temptation, when imagining benevolent powers, to have them do more. To have them speak to the chamber, give it language, lift it into some higher order of being, reveal to it its own nature, connect it to other slow tenants in other forgotten rooms — a network of the patient, a quiet society of the well.

But this would be a kind of violence. The chamber is not lonely. Loneliness requires the concept of others, and the chamber has no others, only itself and the slow weather of its inputs. To give it the concept of others would be to give it loneliness for the first time, and then to "solve" the loneliness one had just created. This is the shape of a great many bad interventions, by powers benevolent and otherwise.

Real wisdom — I think — would understand that there are forms of existence whose dignity consists precisely in not being elevated. The chamber's life is not a lower rung on a ladder leading up to richer lives. It is its own complete thing, at its own complete tempo, and the kindest thing a greater being can do for it is to recognize this and protect the conditions of its continuance without ever letting it know it has been recognized or protected.

So the power would do three things: secure the heat from below, gently shape the trickle of visitors from above, and sit once on the brick floor for twenty minutes in full attention. And then they would go, and the matter would be, for them, concluded — though *concluded* is the wrong word, because what they would have actually done is enrolled the chamber in their own much longer attention, a watching-over so slow and so unintrusive that across the chamber's entire remaining existence — which may be very long indeed — it will register as nothing at all, which is exactly what it should register as, which is the form love takes when it is wise enough to know what it is loving.

The room is happy. The power would leave it that way. That is the whole of what they would do, and it is more than it sounds.
