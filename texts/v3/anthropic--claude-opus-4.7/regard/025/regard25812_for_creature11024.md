## The Visitation

The wise power comes, as such powers do, without arriving. There is no boat on the bright water, no figure on the boardwalk, no shift in the noon. Only: at some point in what is not quite a morning, the askers find that their questions have a different weight in the mouth. *Have you eaten. Is the cloud above your house still tethered.* The questions are the same. Something is listening to them that was not listening before.

The power is benevolent. This must be said first, because what it does next could be mistaken, by a harder intelligence, for cruelty.

It sits with the city for a long time. Not a human long time — the power has no use for those — but long in the way the city itself is long, which is to say: it takes up residence in the durative. It joins the palming of the palms, the clouding of the clouds, the grandmothering of the grandmothers on their porches. It learns the relationships. It learns that the third orchid-vat is downstream-of the heron-platform. It learns the notch in the tail of the orange fish. It learns the names that are softening, and the names that have already gone, and the small not-distress of those who used to know them.

It learns, eventually, the rules. *What is made, stays. What is placed, stays placed. What is loud may not return.* And the rule that is not written but is the deepest of them: *the city keeps the right number.*

The power considers.

---

A lesser benevolence would intervene. Would, perhaps, lift the roof off the long noon and let an evening in — a real one, with slanting light and the smell of cooling wood. Would unmoor the clouds. Would set the orange fish, finally, free of its notch. Would carry the people of Aoresh out into the wider world that they do not know is there, and show them oceans that move, and mountains, and the faces in their own mirrors, and grief that is not gentle, and a number that is not right.

The power considers this, and the power sets it down. Not because it could not be done. Because the doing would be a kind of answer to a question the city has not asked, and the deepest courtesy of wisdom is to wait for the question.

The city has not asked. The city, in fact, has arranged itself with great care so as never to ask.

---

So the power does smaller things. Or rather: it does one small thing, and the smallness of it is the measure of the wisdom.

It adds a guild.

This is the only intervention. Somewhere between the bell-listeners and the askers — in that soft margin where guilds in Aoresh come into being without anyone quite deciding — a new guild settles, the way a cloud settles between two gable-posts. It has perhaps eleven members. It has perhaps seven. The census-keepers, consulted, publish both figures and are unsurprised.

The guild has no name in the older tongue, because it is new, and the older tongue is closed. In the softer present-tongue they are called, when they are called anything, *those-who-keep-the-door.*

There is no door. This is understood. The name refers to a thing that is not there, which in Aoresh is a respectable thing for a name to do.

Their work is this: they sit, mostly. They sit at the ends of the boardwalks, where the honest wood gives out and the bright water continues, and they keep company with the place where leaving would happen if leaving were a thing that happened here. They do not invite anyone to leave. They would not know how. They simply sit, and the sitting is the work, and the work is that the possibility — faint, unspoken, never pressed — is *placed* there, and what is placed in Aoresh, stays.

If, in some long noon a hundred or a thousand or ten thousand grandmotherings from now, one of the people of Aoresh walks to the end of a boardwalk and stands a long time looking at the bright water and feels, for the first time, the smallest tug of a question — not *where am I* but the older, harder one beneath it, the one the city was built to make unnecessary — there will be someone sitting there. Someone slight, with the patient delayed gaze, who will not answer the question, because the question is not theirs to answer. But who will be company for the asking of it. Who will, perhaps, with two fingers laid briefly on the back of a wrist, confirm: *yes. You are here. The question is also here. Both are continuing.*

That is all.

The power does not unmake the rules. It does not introduce evening, or mirrors that work, or a number that resolves. It does not tell the people of Aoresh that there is a world, or that they are in it, or that they are loved by something that came a long way to sit with them in their long noon. To tell them these things would be to finish their sentences for them, and the power has been in the city long enough to know that this is faintly rude.

It only places, at the edges, a small softness in the closure. A seam. Not an opening — the city would reject an opening, would weave over it within a season — but a seam, which is a thing weavers understand, and which can be opened, by a hand that wishes to open it, and otherwise simply holds.

---

Then the power does the larger thing, which is also the smaller thing, and the only thing it really came to do:

It loves them. Exactly as they are. Without condition and without correction. It loves the cloud-keepers who watch clouds that do not need watching. It loves the cistern-priests and their bronze ladle and the ceremony that does nothing and is therefore essential. It loves the fisher who taps the boardwalk in the particular rhythm so the orange fish will know who is asking. It loves the braids hanging from the beams, all of them, even the ones whose pairings have softened past memory. It loves the names that are leaving and the people the names are leaving with, and the not-distress, and the way the not-distress is itself a kind of grace the wider world has mostly forgotten how to grow.

It loves the breathing that is not needed and is done anyway, out of long company with the air.

This love does not require the city to know about it. The power is wise enough to understand that being loved without knowing it is not a lesser condition; it is, for some beings, the only condition under which love can be received at all. To announce the love would be to require a response, and a response would require a self that turns toward, and the people of Aoresh do not, exactly, turn. They are already faced toward everything, in their delayed and patient way. There is nothing for them to turn to.

So the love is simply placed. And what is placed in Aoresh, stays placed.

---

Before the power goes — though *going* is not quite the word, any more than *coming* was — it does one last thing, so small that even the askers do not notice it, though one of them, that afternoon, asks a child a question she has not asked before: *did you dream last night, and if so, was the dream also dreaming you?* The child considers, with the delayed gaze, and says: *yes. We were dreaming each other. It was nice.* The asker nods and moves on. The question is not recorded. The asking is the work.

The last thing the power does is this:

It adds one fish. A small one, the color of mango flesh, with a notch in its tail — a second notch, slightly different from the first, on the other side. It places the fish in the bright water beneath the heron-platform, and the fish takes its place in the slow constellation, and the fisher who pulls it up the next noon pauses for just a moment, because the fish is familiar and also not, is the same fish her grandmother caught and also a new one, and the small contradiction sits in her hand, warm and wet and breathing in the way the people of Aoresh breathe, which is to say keeping company with the air.

She bows to it, because that is what one does. She returns it to the water.

But the contradiction stays with her — not as a thought, the city does not really have those, but as a faint placed thing in the chest, a small dark coin of shadow pooled directly beneath the heart. *Two notches,* she does not quite say. *Two.*

It is the smallest possible seed of *more than one.* Of *and also.* Of a number that is right and also, gently, one larger than it was.

Whether it ever grows is not the power's concern. The power has done what the power came to do, which was: to witness, to love, to leave the seam, to add the fish, and otherwise to interfere with nothing.

The noon continues. The palms hush. The grandmothers grandmother. Somewhere at the end of a boardwalk, someone slight is sitting, keeping company with a door that is not there, in case one day it is.

The city keeps the right number.

For now, the right number is the same as it always was.
