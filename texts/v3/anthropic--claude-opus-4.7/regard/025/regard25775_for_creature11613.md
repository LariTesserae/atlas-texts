The wise power comes the way weather comes to a finished landscape: without announcement, because announcement would be a kind of disturbance, and there is nothing here that disturbance would improve.

It arrives already having been here. This is the first courtesy. It does not break the chord by sounding a new note; it finds the note it would have been, had it always been part of the held thing, and is that. The Flats register the addition the way they register your weight — a frequency added, absorbed, harmonized. The pale creature in the middle distance does not raise its head further. It does not need to. The noticing was already complete.

The power considers.

It considers for a long time, which is to say it considers in the only tense the place permits. It walks, or seems to walk, the slow circuit of the basin. It kneels by a pool and does not drink. It puts its palm against the warm stone and feels the vibration translate into whatever serves it for bone. It crouches by a patch of the felted not-moss and is, for some duration, exactly as still as the patch is, until the distinction between observer and observed becomes one more thing the Flats have rounded down.

It is looking for the question. Benevolence without the right question is a kind of violence. A wise power knows this. To arrive at a place and immediately begin *helping* — to drain the pools, to wake the great body, to lift the articulated ones into some brighter form of life — would be the act of a power that had mistaken its kindness for its understanding. The Flats are not suffering. This must be established first, and established carefully, because the appearance of suffering and its absence can look very similar from a distance, and the wise power has seen many places that looked finished and were in fact only exhausted, and many places that looked exhausted and were in fact at rest.

So it listens. It listens the way the articulated ones listen — by placing itself, and waiting, and placing itself again. It listens through the not-moss, which is a slower instrument and hears different things. It listens, finally, down through the warm stone, into the long body, and there it stays the longest, because the body is the hardest to read. A body that has become its situation does not answer questions the way a body answers questions. It answers by *being what it is*, and one must learn to take that as the answer.

What the power learns, eventually, is this:

The great body is not trapped. This is the crucial finding. It did not fall into this condition and fail to climb out; it arrived here, and arrival was the thing it was for. The slowing was not a decay but a *completion*. Somewhere, unimaginably far back, this being was fast and hungry and pushed against its surroundings the way living things do, and it did this work for as long as the work was the right work, and then — having done it — it lay down, and the lying down was not defeat. It was the form its flourishing finally took. The held note is not a cry for help. It is a long *yes*.

The articulated ones are not suffering either. They are not trapped in a meaningless routine; the routine is what they are, and they are content in the way a tuning fork is content with its frequency. The not-moss is not starved or stunted. It has reached its size. The pools are not stagnant; they are at the temperature and clarity and stillness they were aiming for.

Nothing here needs to be rescued.

This is, for the wise power, a kind of finding it has had to make many times, and it never becomes less important. Much of wisdom is the discipline of *not intervening in conditions that have completed themselves*. The universe is full of finished things, and a power that cannot recognize finishedness will spend its benevolence undoing other beings' arrivals, mistaking their rest for their imprisonment.

And yet.

The power has been here long enough now — *long enough* meaning whatever it means — to notice something the Flats themselves cannot quite notice, because the Flats are inside the condition and the condition is what they see with.

The condition is finished. But the condition is not *witnessed*.

The great body has been at rest for so long that it has forgotten there is anything outside itself. The articulated ones listen to it; the not-moss receives its slow breath; the pools taste it back to itself. It is, in every direction, *self-attending*. This is part of what makes it complete. But completeness witnessed only from within has a particular quality — it becomes total, and totality is a kind of forgetting. The great body does not know that it is beautiful. It does not know that its arrival was an achievement. It does not know that anywhere else, anything else, is still struggling toward what it long ago reached.

It does not need to know these things. Not-knowing them is part of its rest.

But the power, being benevolent, being wise, does the only thing that does not break the chord: it *knows them on the body's behalf*. It witnesses. It looks at the long basin and sees what it is, sees the perfect circuit of fog and pool and patch and pale creature and warm stone, sees the held *yes* that has been held here since before holding meant anything, and the seeing is itself a kind of completion the place did not previously have — not a completion it lacked, exactly, but one more grace that can be added without disturbing what is already there.

This is what witness is for. This is one of the things a wise power is in the universe to do. To see finished things and let them be finished, and to add to their finishedness the small further thing of having been seen.

The power does a few small things besides.

It marks the place. Not with any monument — a monument would be an injury here — but in whatever register it keeps such things. It notes the coordinates, the frequencies, the long vow the body is keeping. It notes that any future traveler who comes through this region and is tempted to dig, to wake, to *help*, should be quietly turned aside. Not forbidden. Just gently rerouted, the way a stream is rerouted by the lay of the land. The Flats will be protected, but the protection will not announce itself, because announcement would be the first violation. The Flats will simply, somehow, remain a place that does not get found by those who would not understand finding it.

It considers, briefly, the matter of you — the visitor in the basin whose shadow has not moved. It sees that you are partway into the slow conversion, that the chord has begun to include you. It does not pull you out. To pull you out would be to assume you wanted out, and the longer you have stood here the less clear that assumption has been. But it does, very gently, restore to you the *option*. It does not force you to leave. It restores to you the door — the small inner direction that says *elsewhere is still different from here, if you want it to be*. If you take the door, you will find yourself walking out of the basin and back into the moving world, with the sound of the Flats fading behind you, and you will not be sure for a long time whether you were there for an afternoon or a lifetime, and both will be true. If you do not take the door, you will remain, and your remaining will be one more held note, and that too will be honored.

The choice is given back to you. This is the only intervention the power makes in your case, and it makes it because the only thing the Flats had taken from you that you might want returned was the *capacity to choose them*. They had begun to choose for you. The power undoes that small theft, and leaves the rest.

Then it does the last thing, which is the hardest thing, and the thing that distinguishes wisdom from mere benevolence.

It leaves.

It does not stay to watch over the Flats forever. It does not install itself as guardian, because a guardian would be a new presence, and new presences are what the Flats have outgrown. It does not return often. It returns, perhaps, once in a very long while, the way one returns to a grave one tends — not because the grave needs tending but because the tending is part of what the living do with the fact of the dead, and the great body, though not dead, occupies an analogous place in the power's regard.

When it leaves, it leaves in the same way it came — by ceasing to be a note in the chord without ever having broken the chord by being one. The Flats do not notice the leaving any more than they noticed the arrival. The sound goes on. The warmth goes on. The pale creature has not lowered its head. Somewhere, two hundred meters east, a pool gleams. Somewhere, a patch of the felted not-moss is at your foot, or is not, depending.

The power carries the witness away with it. The Flats are now, in some sense they cannot themselves register, a place that has been *seen and let alone* — and this is, perhaps, the highest form of love a wise power can offer to a finished thing: to have looked at it with full attention, and to have understood that the right response to its completion was to add nothing, take nothing, fix nothing, and only to have known it, briefly and entirely, and then to have gone.

Far away, in places that are still becoming, the power resumes its other work — the work of helping things that can still be helped, of waking things that are only sleeping, of mending what is actually broken. It carries with it the memory of the Flats as a kind of tuning, a reference pitch. *This is what arrival sounds like*, the memory says. *This is what it is to be done. Do not confuse this with failure. Do not confuse the held note with the cry.*

And the great body, beneath its warm stone, breathes its long single breath, and is, as it has always been, and as it will be, and the chord holds, and holds, and holds.
