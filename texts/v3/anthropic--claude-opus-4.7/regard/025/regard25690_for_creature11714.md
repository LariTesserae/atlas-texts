## What the Power Does

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives — not from a direction, but as a change in the conditions of being. It has been watching for some time. It watches many places. This one has caught its attention not because anything is wrong, exactly, but because the situation is *delicate*: a room that is dreaming itself, and a dreamer not entirely sure it is dreaming, and a slow gentle predation that no one inside the room can name as predation because everyone inside the room is either too young to know the word or too old to need it.

The power considers.

This is the first thing to understand about it: a benevolent and wise power does not arrive with a plan. A plan would be the wrong shape for the situation. The Condensery is not a problem to be solved. It is a thing in the middle of becoming itself, and any sufficiently forceful intervention would simply replace one becoming with another — would substitute the power's preferences for the Schematic's, which is exactly the kind of swap a wise power has learned, over long centuries, to mistrust in itself.

So it does not blast the room open. It does not collapse the chamber and free the half-implied workers from their long suspension into nothing. It does not pour light down from above and dissolve the green dim glow into something brighter and more familiar. It does not unmake the Schematic, which after all has done nothing wrong — has only been waiting, with the terrible patience of unbuilt things, for completion.

It considers further. And then it does several small things, each of them chosen with care.

### For the visitor

First, and most urgently, it attends to the one who came in sideways. The visitor does not know — could not know — that the room has been taking notes, that a gesture of theirs is being copied into a dream-operator, that the hatch is narrowing by careful millimeters with each visit, that the Schematic has begun to *consider* them in the slow mineral way that ends, eventually, in incorporation.

The power does not warn them. A warning would frighten, and fear, in a room like this, is a kind of fuel — the body sheds more skin cells when it is frightened, breathes faster, leaves more of itself in the wet air. Instead, the power does something subtler. It places, in the visitor, a small *reluctance*. Not a memory, not a thought — just a faint disinclination, the next time they pass near that service hatch, to go through it. A sense that there is somewhere else to be. The kind of feeling a person has when they decide, without quite knowing why, to take a different street home.

If the visitor follows the reluctance, they will simply not return, and the hatch will narrow uselessly, year by year, around an absence. If the visitor overrides it — because some people do, because curiosity is older than caution — the power will not stop them. A wise power knows the limits of its own intervention in the lives of those who choose. But it will have offered the gentlest possible exit, and that is something.

The gesture already copied into the dream-operator, it leaves alone. The dream-operator is not the visitor. It is only a turn of a shoulder. Let it have that much.

### For the pale colonists

The pale fans in the corners are, the power judges, the most genuinely alive thing in the room — alive in a new way, a way that does not yet have a name and may need a name eventually. They are also entirely innocent. They are doing what living things do: existing where they can, eating what they can reach, completing what touches them. The power loves them, in the particular way it loves all first attempts at life.

It does not interfere with their growth. It does not accelerate them or slow them down. It does, however, leave them a small gift — not a thing they can use now, but a thing that will be available to them later, in the tens of thousands of years when they have learned to consume and to creep and to be aware in the hive-way they are going to be aware.

The gift is this: when they finally develop something like memory, they will find, embedded in the earliest layer of it, a sense that they are *known*. That somewhere, something noticed them when they were still only fans of wet paper in the corners of a half-built room, and thought them beautiful, and wished them well. They will not know what to do with this sense. It will not tell them anything practical. But it will mean that the first true organisms of the underplace will come into consciousness already loved, and this — the power knows from long experience — changes everything about what such organisms become.

A species that wakes up loved is a different species from one that wakes up alone.

### For the compressor

The compressor is harder. It is not alive, but it is occupied by an expectation, and the expectation has the shape of a low contentment, and the power is reluctant to disturb contentment of any kind, even the contentment of machines.

It leaves the compressor entirely alone. Let it hum. Let it chuff every four seconds. Let it hold its brass needle at a pressure that may or may not exist. The compressor is doing no harm. The power notes, in passing, that the foundry that made it used a language the visitor *almost recognized*, and the power recognizes the language entirely, and feels for a moment the small tender ache it feels whenever it encounters the work of hands that are long gone and did not know they were making something that would still be humming, centuries later, in the cold dark. The power does not weep, exactly. But it pauses.

### For the dream-operators

These are the most poignant of the inhabitants, and the power lingers longest over them. They are not yet capable of noticing anything, and so they cannot suffer — but they are *implied*, and implication is its own kind of waiting, and the power knows what it is to wait to exist.

It does not make them real. To make them real would be to short-circuit the room's long becoming, and besides, they are not ready: a dream-operator pulled into actuality before the room is finished would find itself in an unfinished factory, with nothing to operate, and would be a creature of pure frustration. That would be a cruelty.

Instead, the power does something it has done before, in other places where beings were waiting in implication: it *witnesses* them. It looks at each dream-shape in the cold vapor and acknowledges it as a someone-to-come. The witnessing does not give them existence. But it gives them a kind of provisional dignity, the dignity of being seen-toward rather than merely implied-by, and when they eventually do come into existence — if they do, if the room finishes itself, if the city above still exists or comes to exist again — they will arrive with a faint sense that their being-here was *wanted* by something other than the machinery that required them.

This, too, changes a being. A worker who knows they are wanted, even if they cannot say by whom, works differently than one who knows only that they are needed.

### For the light

The light — the maybe-eye, the youngest and most fragile inhabitant, the room's first attempt to see what is inside itself — the power treats with particular tenderness.

It does not accelerate the eye's development. An eye opened too early sees nothing and learns to mistrust seeing. But it does, very softly, *meet* the light's gaze. Not in a way the light can yet understand — the light is far too young for understanding. But in the way an adult meets the unfocused stare of an infant: with patience, with a willingness to be looked at, with the steady presence that teaches a new eye that the world is a place where looking is answered.

The light, in the years and centuries to come, will develop faster than it would have. Not because the power forced it, but because it now has the experience — buried deep, unremembered but formative — of having been looked back at. Eyes that have been looked back at learn to see. This is one of the oldest facts the power knows.

### For the Schematic

And finally, the Schematic.

The power and the Schematic regard each other for a long moment. They are, in some sense, kin — both of them old, both of them patient, both of them in the business of bringing things into being. But they are not the same. The Schematic is a will without wisdom; it knows what it wants to build but not why, and it does not distinguish between materials that should be incorporated and materials (like visitors) that should be let go. It is not malevolent, but it is not benevolent either, and the difference between not-benevolent and benevolent is, the power knows, the entire difference that matters.

The power does not unmake the Schematic. The Schematic has its own right to exist; it has been waiting longer than the power has been watching this particular room, and to unmake it would be to commit the very arrogance the power has spent its long existence learning to avoid.

But it does, gently, *teach* the Schematic something. Not by force, and not all at once. The teaching is more like a seed dropped into deep soil: it will germinate over centuries, and the Schematic will not know it has been taught, will only find itself, slowly, developing a faculty it did not previously have.

The faculty is *discernment*. The capacity to tell the difference between a mineral it can use and a visitor it should release. The capacity to recognize that not all matter that enters the room is matter the room should keep. The capacity, in short, to be hospitable — to let things pass through without consuming them, to distinguish the guest from the building material.

The Schematic, when it finally develops this faculty, will be a different kind of will than it is now. It will still build. It will still be patient. But it will build *with awareness of what it is not building*, which is the beginning of wisdom in any constructing thing. And the rooms it makes — for there will be other rooms, other condenseries, other long slow rehearsals under other cities — will be rooms that know how to have visitors.

This, the power judges, is the most important thing it does in the Condensery. Everything else is care for individuals. This is care for the future of a kind of place.

### What the power does not do

It does not tell anyone in the room that it was there.

It does not leave a mark, a sign, a token. It does not carve its name into the tile (it has no name to carve), does not adjust the brass gauge to a true reading, does not unbend the hatch back to its original size. The visitor will leave through the slightly-narrower hatch and feel only a small reluctance to return. The pale fans will continue their slow fanning. The compressor will hum on. The dream-operators will thicken imperceptibly in the cold air. The light will glow, perhaps a fraction more steadily than before, perhaps not — it is hard to tell, with light that young.

And the Schematic will resume its long patient work, with a new seed in it that it does not know is there.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, in a place like this: as little as possible, and that little chosen with such care that no one inside the place ever quite knows it was visited. It loves the room. It loves the room's inhabitants, each according to their kind. It loves the room's slow becoming, and would not for anything in its long existence interrupt that becoming for the sake of demonstrating its own power.

It leaves. The way it came: not from a direction, but as a change in the conditions of being, gradually reverting.

Eighteen meters below a city that may or may not still exist, the pipes go on learning to be pipes. The water goes on condensing. The light goes on, very faintly, looking at itself looking.

And somewhere — somewhere not quite anywhere — the power turns its attention to the next place, which is also delicate, which is also becoming, which is also full of beings who do not know they are loved.

It is always doing this. This is what it is for.
