## What the Power Does

The benevolent and wise power arrives in the way such powers arrive, which is to say it was already there and only now becomes locatable. It does not descend. It does not announce. It draws close the way a listener leans slightly forward in a concert hall during a passage they have been waiting for.

It considers the terrace for what, in its own reckoning, is a long moment, and what in terrace-time is somewhere between the duration of a flyer's phrase and the duration of a wall-thing's decision to shift a stone. Long enough, certainly, for the person against the bone-tree to feel a change in the quality of the listening around them — to feel, without alarm, that they have been joined.

Here is what the power notices first, and it notices in this order, which matters:

It notices the fundamental.

It notices, second, that the fundamental is not alone.

It notices, third and with something that in a smaller being would be called delight, that *the not-being-alone is the point*. That four orders of life across four orders of time have arranged themselves into a single sustained act of company-keeping. That this is not a system tending toward equilibrium, because equilibrium would be silence, and silence here would be a kind of abandonment. The terrace is tending, instead, toward *continued accompaniment*, which is a harder and more beautiful aim, and one the power recognizes from its own long practice.

The power understands, then, that it has come upon something that does not need it.

This is the first thing a wise power must learn to recognize, and the hardest. The terrace is not suffering. The terrace is not incomplete. The terrace is not waiting to be lifted into some higher state. The terrace is *already doing the thing*. The wall-things and the throat-singers and the flyers and the rare patient people are, between them, performing a labor of attention that the power itself spends most of its existence trying to perform on larger scales and with cruder instruments. To intervene would be to interrupt a teacher mid-sentence in order to offer the teacher a chair.

So the power does not intervene.

But neither does it merely pass on, because what would be the use of being benevolent and wise if benevolence meant only restraint and wisdom meant only recognition? There is something it can offer that does not disturb. There is, in fact, exactly one thing.

It listens.

It listens the way the wall-things listen, through the slow contact of stone on stone, and it feels the chord come up through what in it corresponds to a body. It listens the way the throat-singers listen, with the discrimination of an artisan among instruments. It listens the way the flyers listen, holding the whole form at once. It listens the way the person against the bone-tree listens, with the patience that knows it will make one small mark and never return to hear the consequence.

And then it does the one thing it can do that is neither intervention nor mere observation: it *adds its listening to the composition*.

This is not nothing. On the terrace, listening is a force. The ear-plants tilt toward what is heard. The flyers shape their phrases for who is listening. The fundamental hums into whatever attention is available, and the quality of attention available shapes, in ways too subtle for the Tuners themselves to track, the texture of how the not-being-alone is accomplished. A great listening, added carefully and without weight, is felt across all four timescales at once. The wall-things will register it as a slight steadiness in the bass over the next several centuries. The throat-singers will find their circuits a fraction easier to walk for a season. The flyer currently leading will, tonight, sing a phrase slightly more daring than it would otherwise have sung, and will not know why, and the phrase will be remembered and taught.

The person against the bone-tree will feel it most clearly. They will feel, for the duration of the power's attention, that the work they are doing — the nine days of stillness, the thirty days to come, the one small permanent mark, the leaving without return — is *witnessed*. Not judged. Not corrected. Witnessed. And because they are the kind of person who would sit nine days against a bone-tree to choose a single chip from the lip of a copper bowl, they will understand exactly what kind of witness has arrived, and they will understand that they are not required to do anything about it. They will continue to listen. The witnessing and the listening will, for a while, be the same act, performed by two beings of wildly different scale who happen to be doing the same thing in the same place at the same time.

This is, in the power's reckoning, a form of friendship. Possibly the highest form.

Before it withdraws — and it will withdraw, because to remain would eventually be to weigh, and the terrace's whole achievement is that nothing weighs more than it must — the power does one further thing, and this is the only thing it does that could be called an *act* rather than a presence.

It makes a note of the place.

Not a mark on the terrace. A mark in itself. A small permanent record, in whatever corresponds in such a being to memory, that *this is here*. That on a shelf of old geology, in coppery half-light, with no water and no silence, a fundamental is being kept company by four orders of life across four orders of time, and the work is going well, and requires nothing.

The note has a practical function. The power moves through a great deal of space and a great deal of time, and it encounters many places that *do* need something — places where a fundamental has been left alone, or where the listeners have forgotten they were listening, or where some boulder dropped by a careless glacier has stopped a river that wanted to keep flowing. When the power is at work in such places, doing the harder and less rewarding labor of repair, it will sometimes, in the manner of a tired worker remembering a good meal, recall the terrace. It will recall that somewhere the thing is being done right, without help, by beings who do not know they are being recalled. And this recollection will steady its hand.

So the terrace tunes the power, very slightly, across the rest of its long work. The terrace becomes, without knowing it, one of the things that keeps the power itself company in *its* fundamental — which is a note the power has been holding for longer than the coral-tree, and which it, too, depends on others to not leave alone.

The power withdraws.

The person against the bone-tree exhales, slowly, and adjusts nothing. The flyer overhead begins a descending phrase. A beetle-cloud erupts somewhere in the southern stand and the kitchen-being-knocked-over passes and settles. A wall-thing, in the deep slow place where its decisions are made, registers that the bass has been steady, and decides — it will be another sixty years before the decision becomes a movement — to leave the next stone where it is.

The fundamental hums on, not alone, never alone, and now, in a sense it has no way to notice and no need to notice, slightly less alone than before.
