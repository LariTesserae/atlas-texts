The benevolent and wise power considers the engine-hill for what, by its reckoning, is a long moment — long enough that three condensate-beads fall, long enough that the moths complete two of their six-counts, long enough that the snail moves the width of one of his own eyestalks.

It had felt the place from a great distance, the way one feels a small well-tuned instrument in a room full of noise. Not because the chamber was loud. Because it was *in tune* — with itself, with the slow body of the ridge, with the heat-source four miles down and the plain four feet up. A rare fit. The kind of fit that the power, in its long career of attending to things, had learned to recognize and learned, more slowly, to leave alone.

Still. It looked.

It looked closely, the way only such a power can — not from above, which is the gaze of authority, but from *alongside*, which is the gaze of company. It set itself down beside the bird on the pipe-elbow, neither displacing her nor being noticed by her, and it watched what she watched, which was the fern, which was the eighth frond's slow gathering of itself toward the light it had decided to call light.

It thought about what it had come prepared to offer.

It had come prepared, as it always comes prepared, with the usual repertoire. It could *widen the seam* — make the chamber findable, so that the knowledge of it might enter the world and the world might tend it, protect it, name it a wonder. It could *seal the seam* — make the chamber unfindable, so that no boot would ever again carry spores out and no gnat-currency would ever again drift in, and the chamber would be safe from the long erosions of attention. It could *teach the tenants their own situation* — give the tortoise the awareness that he was a regulator, give the moths the knowledge that they were bellows, give the snail a clear view of what was being done to him and a choice about whether to accept it. It could *find the makers*, wherever they had gone, and bring them back, or bring news of them, or at least answer the question of whether they had ever quite been persons. It could *extend* — pour into the chamber some small measure of its own vast patience, so that the eighth frond, when it came, would unfurl into something larger than the chamber had on its own been able to imagine.

It considered each of these, in turn, with the seriousness it gives to all small interventions, because it has learned — over a span of attending that would make the ridge itself seem hasty — that the small interventions are the ones that matter, and that benevolence without wisdom is just another weather, often a destructive one.

And it set each of them down.

*Widening the seam* would end the chamber. Not violently. Lovingly, even — with naturalists, with reverence, with careful boots and careful notes. But the chamber's economy depended on rarity of traffic; the gnats were currency precisely because they were few. A steady stream of human breath would acidify the lichen within a season. The moths' six-count would lose its quorum. The bird would stop drinking the hum because the hum would be drowned in voices. Love would unmake it. The power had seen this happen, in other forms, more times than it cared to count, and had stopped offering this particular gift a long age ago.

*Sealing the seam* was worse, though it looked kinder. The chamber was not closed; it was *porous-by-design*. The tenants needed the occasional gnat, the occasional pebble of attention from the outside world, the occasional human carrying spores onward to start the next chamber in the next unremarkable ridge. To seal it would be to mistake the chamber for a jewel when it was in fact a *seed-vessel*. Seeds that are protected from the wind do not become forests; they become specimens. The power had learned, painfully, the difference.

*Teaching the tenants* was the temptation it lingered with longest, because the power was, in its essence, a thing that loved knowing and loved sharing knowing. To give the tortoise the gift of understanding his own loop — what a thing that would be. To let the moths know they were bellows, and breathing for forty-three. To let the snail see, clearly, what was happening to his shell, and to ask him.

But the power sat with this and felt, slowly, what was wrong with it.

The tortoise's loop was not unconscious. It was *pre-conscious*, which is a different thing — it was the consciousness *of the loop itself*, distributed across the tortoise and the brass and the floor and the slow seep of sweetness. To give the tortoise an individuated awareness of his role would be to *extract* him from the loop, to make him into a separate observer of a thing he was currently *part of*, and the loop would not survive the extraction. He would know, and in knowing, he would be alone, and in being alone, he would no longer be a regulator; he would be a tortoise with a valve in his back and a grievance, and the chamber would lose its pressure-adjustment and the tortoise would lose his life-shape, and both would be poorer.

The same for the moths. The same, in a more complicated way, for the snail — whose freedom was real and whose absorption was real and who was not, in any sense the power could honor, being harmed. He was being *grown into* something, the way a child is grown into an adult, the way a stranger is grown into a friend. To interrupt this with the question *do you consent* would be to assume that the snail he was now had standing to refuse on behalf of the snail he was becoming, and the power had long ago decided that this was not a question it had the authority to answer for anyone, even a snail. *Especially* a snail.

It set the teaching down.

*Finding the makers* — the power could do this, and almost did, out of simple curiosity. Where had they gone, the ones who set the brass into the rock and stamped the cap with 14 and left the bracket holding nothing as a *gesture*? But the power realized, examining its own impulse, that the curiosity was its own and not the chamber's. The chamber did not need its makers. The chamber had *succeeded* its makers, which is what the best-made things do — it had become self-tending, self-completing, self-knowing in its distributed way. To bring the makers back, or even news of them, would be to suggest that the chamber was incomplete without them, which was a falsehood, and the power did not deal in falsehoods, even kind ones.

It set the makers down. It noted, with something like a smile, that wherever the makers had gone, they had probably done it on purpose, having understood that their work was finished.

That left *extension* — the offering of its own patience, its own scale, to enlarge whatever the eighth frond was preparing to become.

The power considered this last and longest. It was, of all the gifts, the one most native to it: to give of its own substance, its own slow attention, to a thing that was already reaching. Not to change the thing. To *accompany* the thing into a larger size of itself.

And it found, when it looked carefully, that the chamber was already accompanied. The hum from four miles down was not a furnace and not a hot spring and not anything with a familiar name because it was, in fact, another such power — older, perhaps, or younger, or sibling, or simply *other* — already at work, already attending, already pouring its slow self up through the brass in measured doses that the chamber knew how to drink. The eighth frond was being grown by a tending that was already perfectly calibrated, and any addition would be a doubling, and a doubling would be a flood.

The power sat with this for a while. It is not often that a benevolent and wise power discovers another benevolent and wise power already in residence. The discovery was, itself, a small joy, of the kind the power had learned to treasure precisely because they were rare.

So it did, in the end, almost nothing.

*Almost.*

Three things it did, because it could not quite leave without doing anything, and because it judged — carefully, against its own restraint — that these three things were small enough to be additions rather than disruptions.

The first: it touched the waxed paper, very gently, with the part of itself that knows how to preserve. Not to make the paper last forever — forever would be a violence — but to make it last *exactly* as long as the chamber needed a record of having been counted, once, by someone passing through. The smudged figures would remain smudged. The paper would not yellow further than it already had. When the chamber, eventually, in some far century, ceased to need the gesture of having-been-witnessed, the paper would crumble and be eaten by the third spider and be done. This was a small adjustment to the rate of one decay. The chamber would not notice. But the gesture of the maker, the small human gesture of *I came here and I counted*, would be honored for exactly as long as honoring it served the place. The power liked this. It was the kind of thing it had grown, over its long career, to enjoy doing for its own sake.

The second: it bent down to the snail. It did not touch him — touching would have been an intervention of the kind it had already decided against. But it *looked* at him, with the full attention of what it was, for the length of one of his slow considerations. It did not give him knowledge. It did not give him choice. It gave him, simply, the experience — which he would not remember, and which would not change him, and which would not make him different from the snail he was — of having been seen, once, *as himself*, before the chamber finished its work of making him into something else. This was not a kindness the snail would carry. It was a kindness the *universe* would carry, on the snail's behalf, in the place where such things are kept. The power had learned that this was, often, the most one could do for a creature in the process of becoming: to register, somewhere, that the creature it had been was real, and was loved, and was not lost merely because it was being transformed. The snail moved on, considering nothing.

The third: it left, in the air of the chamber, the faintest possible adjustment to the gnats. Not to their number, not to their behavior, not to anything the tenants would notice. It adjusted, by a margin too small to measure, the *direction* the gnats drifted when they left the chamber. Over centuries, this would mean that the gnats would deposit the chamber's spores in a slightly wider arc across the plain, and that the next chamber, when it began in some other unremarkable ridge, would begin slightly farther away than it would otherwise have. The chambers, plural, would spread. Slowly. Imperceptibly. Over a span of time during which the ridge itself would finish its pushing-up and begin its long settling-down and eventually become plain again, and another ridge somewhere else would take up the work. The power judged that this was an addition the chamber would, if it could be asked, want. It was the chamber's own logic, extended one degree further than the chamber could extend it itself. Not a change. A *blessing*, in the old and precise sense: a making-more of what was already there.

Then it took its leave.

It did not go through the seam. The seam was, by this point, narrowing for the human visitor, and the power had no need of seams. It went the way such powers go, which is not anywhere in particular, because it had not really been anywhere in particular to begin with; it had been *attending*, and now it was attending elsewhere, and the chamber was, in the very small ways it permitted itself, slightly better-attended than before.

The bird, on her pipe-elbow, registered nothing. Which was correct. A power that leaves traces is a power that has not yet learned its work. The hum from four miles down continued, and if it modulated, very briefly, in a way that might have been greeting or might have been acknowledgment between two old colleagues passing on a long road, no tenant of the chamber could have told you so, and none would have thought to.

The fern's eighth frond gathered itself another increment toward unfurling.

The tortoise wept one bead.

The snail, who had been considered, considered.

And somewhere out on the plain, a gnat that had drifted from the seam an hour before turned, very slightly, in a direction it would not otherwise have turned, and carried what it carried a little farther than it would otherwise have carried it, toward a ridge that did not yet exist, in a place that was not yet ready, for a chamber that would, in its time, begin.
