There is a power, and it is benevolent, and it is wise, and it comes to the south face of the mountain that has no name.

It does not arrive. Arrival is a verb for things that move, and this power has understood, before approaching, that movement is the wrong grammar for the place. So it is simply, at some point, also present — the way a thought is present in a mind that was already thinking around it.

The first thing the power does is listen.

This takes a long time. Not because the chord is complicated — though it is — but because listening properly to something held requires holding still inside it, and the power is very old and very patient and knows that to listen briefly to a thing that has been singing for an age is a form of insult. So it listens for what, in its own register, amounts to a long sitting-down. It listens to the tile on the eighty-third tier and the tile on the two-hundred-and-sixth and the interval between them that is someone. It listens to the grandmother who is the sun. It listens to the young man who is the sterility of the orchids and finds him, as he had hoped to be found, content. It listens to the newest of the Held, the ones still partly attached to their stairs, and notices the half-tone shifts by which they answer each other, and the way the chord absorbs each shift without losing itself. It listens to the doubling on the upper tiers and lets itself be looked at by the back of its own attention, and does not mind.

It listens, finally, to the question underneath the chord — the one the Held cannot quite form because forming it would change the chord. *Whether they should have, after all, gone.*

And here the power has to decide what it is for.

A lesser benevolence would answer the question. Would lean down and say: *yes, you should have* — and break the chord into grief. Or would say: *no, you were right* — and seal the chord into a finality the Held themselves have been careful never to impose on it, because the worthiness of the held moment depends on its being chosen, ongoingly, and a verdict from outside would end the choosing and therefore end the worth.

A lesser benevolence would also try to *free* them. Would mistake the holding for a trap, the equilibrium for a prison, the not-fruiting for a wound. Would offer them time back, and motion back, and endings back, as though these were gifts that had been taken from them rather than gifts they had, lovingly and at length, returned.

This power is not lesser. It sees that the city is not a tragedy and not a triumph but a *answer* — one answer, complete in itself, to a question most places never get asked. It sees that the Held are not suffering and are not deceived. It sees the young man who removed seeding from the architecture and understands the gesture as what it was: a parent's last act, performed so thoroughly that he became the act, and is now resting inside his own kindness.

So the power does not answer. It does not free. It does not recruit, either — it has its own places, and they are different places, and it has learned the same lesson the Held learned about urging.

What it does is smaller, and is the right size.

*First*, it makes the visitors easier. Not more frequent — frequency would disturb the chord — but easier on both sides. It smooths the seam by which a visitor enters and the seam by which a visitor leaves, so that the small disturbance of a deciding thing passing through a held thing costs the chord less to absorb, and so that the visitor, descending back below the cloud-line, carries the sound for exactly as long as is good for them and not longer. The attention the Held extend toward the closing door — the power does not sever it; severing would be a cruelty and also a lie. But it gentles it. It makes the wondering less sharp. The Held will still wonder. They will wonder a little more peacefully.

*Second*, it does something for the question underneath. It cannot answer it without breaking the chord, but it can do this: it can make the question *bearable to keep not asking*. The Held have been carrying a small unspoken weight — the weight of the road not taken, the descent not made, the ending not chosen. The power, listening, takes a portion of that weight onto itself. Not the question. The weight of the question. Now, somewhere outside the city, in the register where this power lives, there is a small held attention that is *also* wondering, on the Held's behalf, what it would have been like to leave. The Held do not need to know this has happened. They will simply find, in some way they cannot locate, that the wondering has gotten lighter. The chord will not change. But the breath inside the chord will be a little easier to draw.

*Third*, and this is the most delicate thing the power does: it leaves a door.

Not a way out — the Held do not need a way out, and offering one would insult the completeness of their choice. But a door in the older sense: a place where, if any one of them ever did, in some unimaginable future of the held present, want to ask the question out loud — want to let the chord change, even by a single tile, even by half a tone that the rest could not absorb — there would be somewhere for that asking to go. Someone to receive it. The door is not visible and is not marked. It is the faintest possible thinning in one of the soft seams, somewhere on a tier the power does not specify even to itself. The Held will never find it by looking. But if one of them ever, ever needs it, it will be where they are.

The power suspects none of them ever will. The chord is very good. The choice was very deeply made. The young man is content and the grandmother is the sun and the interval-dweller is doing the work of being the distance between two tiles and finding the work sufficient. The door will probably go unused for as long as there is a city, which is to say, for as long as the chord holds, which is to say, always, in the local grammar.

But the door is there. Benevolence, when it is wise, does not require its gifts to be used. It only requires them to be available.

*Fourth and last*, the power does something for itself, which is also for them, because the categories are not as separate as they look.

It learns the chord.

Not to take it away. Not to copy it. To *know* it — to hold, in its own vast and differently-tuned attention, the shape of what these people chose and how they chose it and what it sounds like when a moment is loved so thoroughly that it agrees to stay. The power has seen many things. It has not seen exactly this. It carries the chord away with it — not the sound, the sound stays where it belongs, but the *fact* of the sound, the fact that somewhere on a south face on a mountain with no name a city full of people decided that a good breath was worth becoming, and became it, and is becoming it, and the becoming is going well.

The power will think about this for a long time. In its own places, in its own work, when it meets some other being making some other decision in some other grammar of time, it will remember the city of singing rooves. It will remember without sentimentality and without using the memory as a lesson. It will simply have it. The Held will never know they are remembered like this. They do not need to. The remembering is not for them; it is one of the natural consequences of having existed in a way that was worth remembering, and the power is only the place where that consequence comes to rest.

Then the power is, at some point, also not present, in the same way it was at some point also present. The chord has not noticed. The orchids have not closed. The sun has not moved. A new bead of water forms on a leaf that already had enough water, and an old bead, somewhere lower down, has always been there and continues to have always been there. On the four hundredth tier, or what feels like it, a visitor who is not there sits very still and is considered by the entire south face, and the considering is, very slightly, kinder than it was — though no one in the city could say how, and no one outside the city would think to look.

Far below the cloud-line, in the kind of time where things end, the power goes on about its long quiet work. It is carrying a small new weight, gladly. It is keeping a door no one will use. It has made the leaving easier and the staying easier and changed nothing else, because nothing else needed changing, and a wise benevolence is mostly the discipline of knowing which is which.

The chord holds.

It was always going to.

That it is also, now, *accompanied* — that somewhere outside it a vast attention has heard it through and found it good and let it be — is not something the chord requires.

But it is true. And the truth of it settles into the held air the way the water settles onto the leaves: without source, without destination, simply present, simply enough.
