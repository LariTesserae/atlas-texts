The benevolent and wise power considers the plateau for a long time.

This is the first thing. Before any act, there is the consideration, and the consideration is not brief. To be wise about such a place is to understand that wisdom here is measured differently — that a decision made in an afternoon would be, to the tenders and the engines, a kind of violence regardless of its content, simply by being made at the wrong speed. So the power slows itself. It learns the tempo of the plateau before it permits itself an intention toward the plateau. This takes, by the reckoning of worlds below, a great while. By the reckoning of the plateau, it is the proper pause before speaking.

What the power notices, in that long attention:

That nothing here is suffering. This is important and the power lingers on it. There is no cry to answer, no wound to bind. The tenders are not lonely in any sense that maps onto loneliness. The engines are not failing. The herds are not starving. The whole vast slow body of the place is doing what it does, and what it does is not a problem to be solved.

That the place is, however, *unwitnessed* — or witnessed only from inside itself, by tenders whose witnessing is part of the machine and cannot therefore stand outside it to know that it is beautiful. The plateau does not know it is beautiful. It only is.

That the place is also, in the long arithmetic of things, *fragile*. Not now. Not soon. But the sun above it is smaller than it should be, or further, and what that means across enough centuries is that one day the engines will cool. The lichen will stop advancing. The last tender will lie down beside the last cycle and the cycle will not close, because there will be no exhalation left to witness. This is very far away. But the power, being wise, sees it.

So the power does three things. It does them slowly. It does them with the lightest possible touch, because the first principle of wisdom in such a place is that the place is already correct, and any correction one offers must be smaller than the thing corrected.

---

The first thing is that the power becomes, itself, a witness. Not a god watching, which would be too large and would press down on the plateau like weather it had not generated. Something subtler. The power distributes a portion of its attention into the plateau the way one might let a single drop of ink fall into a still pool — not to color the water, only to be present in it. This attention takes no form. It does not walk. It does not have palms. It is simply there, alongside the tenders, at every engine, in every dry riverbed, beside every cushion plant that is a thousand years in the making. It witnesses without intervening. It learns the gestural language of the tenders not to speak it but to understand what is being said, so that nothing said on the plateau across all its long ages will go unheard.

This is the smallest and the largest gift. The plateau is now known. Not studied — known. The difference matters. To be studied is to be reduced to what can be written down. To be known is to be held in attention exactly as one is, at one's own speed, without summary.

The tenders do not notice this directly. How could they. But something in them, over the next few centuries, shifts by a degree that only they could measure: a faint easing in the long gestural conversations when two meet on a ridgeline, as though some pressure that had always been there, the pressure of being the only witnesses to the machine they were part of, had lifted by some imperceptible fraction. They cannot name it. They incorporate it into how they walk.

---

The second thing is that the power adds one engine.

Only one. This requires great restraint. The power could add a thousand; it could re-warm the whole plateau against the slow cooling of its sun; it could make the place permanent. But permanence is not what the plateau is. The plateau is a long slow song, and a song that cannot end is not a song. So the power adds one engine, in a place where no engine was, in a basin in the far west where the storms are born. It is the same iron-dark, the same lichen-furred, the same warmth in its iron. It looks as though it has been there as long as the others. It has, now, in the only sense that matters — it has been placed by something that understood that to add to such a place one must add as the place itself adds, by deposition across ages, by appearing to have always been.

The new engine does one thing the others do not, though no one will discover this for a very long time and perhaps no one ever will. Its cycle is tuned not to the plateau's present but to the plateau's far future. When the other engines begin to cool, in some age unimaginably distant, this one will still be exhaling. Not to save the plateau — the plateau cannot be saved, and should not be — but to be the last. To be the engine beside which the last tender lies down. So that the last cycle closes with a witness. So that the song ends on a held note rather than a silence that was never noticed.

A tender will find it, within a century or two. Will walk to it across the slow plain. Will lay a palm against its iron and feel what it feels at every engine, and will incorporate this new engine into the circuit it walks, and will not wonder where it came from, because the tenders do not wonder things in that way. The new engine will simply be one of the engines now. It will always have been.

---

The third thing is the most delicate, and the power hesitates over it for what would be, in any lesser place, an unconscionable length of time.

The power considers the tenders themselves. Their solitude, which is not suffering but is real. Their long memory of the rare meetings on ridgelines. The way each tender carries, across centuries, the record of a few slow conversations and incorporates them into its walk.

The power does not give them more meetings. To do so would be to thin what the meetings are. It does not give them speech, or company, or knowledge of one another across distance. All of these would be gifts that took something away.

What the power does is this: at the moment when a tender lies down beside an engine for the last time, in the long decades during which the moss grows over it and the burrowers tunnel beneath and it becomes indistinguishable from a low rounded outcrop — in those decades, the portion of the power's attention that has been with that tender for its whole long life remains with it. Does not depart at the moment of stillness. Stays. Witnesses the absorption. Witnesses the becoming-stone. So that the tender, in whatever way the tender knows things, is not alone in the long slow act of returning to the plateau. Is accompanied. Is, across those decades, *kept company* by something that has walked beside it without weight for all its centuries and now stays to see it home.

The tenders do not know this is happening, in life. Perhaps in the long dissolution they come to know it. Perhaps not. The power does not require that they know. The accompaniment is not for the power's sake. It is the smallest courtesy one slow long-running thing can offer another: to be present at the closing of the cycle. To witness. Palm to iron, in whatever form a power without palms can press its warmth against the warmth of what is ending.

---

And then the power does nothing else.

It does not reveal itself. It does not name itself. It does not write anything down, because the plateau has no readers and would be wronged by being made into a text. It does not tell the tenders what it has done, because what it has done is not the sort of thing that benefits from being told. It simply continues, at the plateau's speed, in the plateau's hush, attending.

A tender walks across a high shelf in the pearl-grey haze. It lifts its head a fraction of a degree as a distant engine exhales its century's breath. It lowers its head. It walks on.

Beside it, unseen, unfelt, at exactly its pace, something benevolent and wise walks too, and has, for as long as the tender can remember, and will, for as long as there is walking to do.

That is what the power does. That is all the power does. It is enough. It is, in fact, the only thing that would not have been too much.
