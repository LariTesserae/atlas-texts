They come not as light but as attention.

The benevolent power — call it that, though it has no name it uses with itself — has been watching for some time. Not the way a god watches, from above and apart, but the way an old reader returns to a passage that keeps unfolding. It has watched the cleft for what would be, in the cleft's own accounting, perhaps eleven generations of Hangers. In its own accounting, a span barely worth naming. Long enough to be sure.

It is wise, which means among other things that it has learned the shape of its own hands and what they tend to break. It is benevolent, which means it loves what it sees. These two facts will determine almost everything about what happens next, which is: very little.

---

It considers, first, the obvious gifts.

*Light.* It could pour light down into the cleft. Real light, sun-colored, full-spectrum, the kind that would let the Soft-Beaks remember what eyes were originally for. It thinks about this for what would be, on a human scale, a long time. Then it sets the thought down gently. The Soft-Beaks do not need to remember. The duet between two birds in adjacent hollows is not a diminished version of some brighter song they have forgotten — it *is* the song. To flood the cleft with light would be to walk into a cathedral and turn on the work-lamps because the candles seemed insufficient.

*Space.* It could widen the cleft, ease the press of bodies, give every creature room. But the press is not suffering. The press is the chorus. The Hangers navigate by the answering rustle of bodies that are *right there*. Space would scatter them into solitudes they have no equipment for.

*Knowledge.* It could give the Small-Handed language — real language, the kind that asks questions of itself. It could let them know what their polished hollow is *for*. It thinks about this longest of all, because the temptation is real. The gesture of the back of a hand pressed to a child's forehead is so close, so close, to the threshold of something the power recognizes and treasures. One nudge.

But it sees the nudge clearly. The Small-Handed who could articulate the question would also be the Small-Handed who could no longer simply perform the gesture. The unbroken transmission — eldest to youngest, hand to hand, unasked — would end the moment it became askable. The power has seen this elsewhere. It does not need to see it again.

So it sets down each gift in turn. Not refused — held. The kind of held that means *not now, perhaps not ever, and this restraint is itself a form of love.*

---

What it does, instead:

It learns the chorus. Properly learns it — not as data but as music, the way you learn a piece by living inside it. It listens for what would be, by the cleft's clock, several seasons. It comes to know the duet of every paired Soft-Beak. It comes to know which Hanger-cluster clicks first when a damp evening is coming. It comes to recognize the particular slow turn of the largest Upper Thing, the one that may be the only one, and the way its breath sets the deepest rhythm beneath everything.

This learning is not passive. To be known, fully and without intention to change, is itself a thing that happens to a place. The cleft does not notice. But somewhere in the chorus, in some register too fine for the creatures to register, a resonance establishes itself — the cleft sung back to itself by something that has heard it entirely. This changes nothing the creatures could name. They eat, mate, fall, and are processed by the floor exactly as before. But the place is *witnessed* now, in a way it was not before, and witness is not nothing. Witness is, perhaps, the smallest possible gift, and the only one safe to give.

---

Then it does three other things, each smaller than the last.

*First:* There is a particular Small-Handed, an old female, the eldest in her column, who has been climbing down to the floor for blind grubs longer than any of her family remembers. Her hands are still good. Her grip is still sure. But the power can see — as the cleft cannot — that on her next descent, a particular flake of pumice on a particular ledge will give way under her rear-left hand, and she will fall, and the fall will be from high enough to kill her, and her family will lose not just her but the specific knowledge of the descent route, which she has not yet finished transmitting to the niece she is teaching.

The power does not catch her. It does not strengthen the flake. It does, however — and this is the most it permits itself — cause a Hanger three hollows above to shift its weight a quarter-second earlier than it otherwise would, sending a small chain of rustles down the wall, which the old Small-Handed registers, and pauses for, and in pausing rests her rear-left hand on a different flake. She completes the descent. She returns with three grubs. She finishes teaching the niece over the following season. She dies, eventually, in her sleeping-hollow, of the ordinary slow ending that is her due.

The Hanger does not know it shifted early. The cleft does not know anything happened. Nothing happened, in any sense the cleft could verify. This is the kind of intervention the power permits: invisible, deniable, indistinguishable from luck, and only ever in the direction of letting the place continue being what it already is.

*Second:* It marks the cleft. Not visibly. It places upon the cleft a kind of attention-sign, the way a careful traveler places a small cairn at the edge of something precious to indicate to other travelers: *here, slow down, this is finished work, this is a thing that has found its form.* The sign is for other powers — and there are others, not all of them as patient. The sign says: *I have seen this. It is under my regard. Pass by.* Most will. A few would not have, without the sign. The cleft will never know it was at risk. This too is the point.

*Third — and this is the smallest and perhaps the strangest:* In the polished hollow of one of the oldest Small-Handed columns, where generations of palms have smoothed the lip until it shines faintly even in the dusk, the power leaves something. Not an object. A quality. The stone there will, from now on, hold warmth a little longer than the surrounding stone. Not much. The difference of a held breath. When a young Small-Handed sits in the hollow and runs her fingers along the lip, she will feel, without knowing she feels it, that the stone is faintly warmer than it should be — the temperature, almost exactly, of another small palm that was there a moment before.

She will not know why she returns to the hollow more often than her elders did. She will not know why her own children, taught by her, return more often still. The gesture will deepen. The hollow will be visited a little more, polished a little smoother, across the generations that follow. Whatever the gesture is for — and the power does not know either, not fully, which is part of why it loves the gesture — it will be performed a little more often, and a little more tenderly, because the stone remembers being warm.

This is the only thing the power adds to the cleft. A slight thermal anomaly in one hollow. A thing no instrument the cleft contains could measure. A thing that will, over centuries, very gently weight the Small-Handed toward whatever it is they are slowly becoming.

The power is not sure they are becoming anything. It is willing to be wrong. It is willing to have done nothing at all, in the end, except warm a stone.

---

And then it goes — though *goes* is not quite right, since it does not entirely leave. It thins its attention to the minimum required to maintain the sign. It returns, in what would be the cleft's reckoning, every few hundred generations, to listen for a while and make sure the chorus is still itself.

It never speaks to the cleft. It never lets the cleft know it is loved. To be loved openly by such a power would be, for a place like this, a kind of catastrophe — the introduction of an outside, an above, a someone-watching, which would crack the seamless inwardness that is the cleft's whole nature. The cleft is a held breath. The power's love is the silence around the breath, which the breath does not need to know is there in order to be held.

The Hangers click. The Soft-Beaks trill their private duets. The Small-Handed press the backs of their hands to their children's foreheads. The Upper Things breathe their slow deep breaths. The floor digests its slow harvest. The ribbon of sky goes pale and dim and pale again.

And in one polished hollow, very faintly, the stone is warm.
