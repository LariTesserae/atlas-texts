# The Visitation

The benevolent power notices the vivarium the way one notices a held breath in another room — not by sound, but by the shape of the silence around it.

It has been watching for some time. Powers of this kind do not arrive; they resolve, the way a figure resolves out of fog when you have been looking in the right direction long enough. It has been looking in the right direction for what, in the vivarium's own slow measure, might be three or four molts of the frogs — which is to say, a duration the Keeper would recognize as *attention being paid*.

It considers, first, whether to enter at all.

This is the first question such a power always asks, and the most important. There are places in the worlds that are improved by visitation and places that are only disturbed by it. The vivarium is delicate in a particular way: not fragile, but *composed*. Its equilibrium is not the equilibrium of stillness but of a very slow music, and a wrong note — even a kind note, even a note meant in love — could throw the cadence off by a century.

The power weighs this. Powers of this order are patient in a way that matches the vivarium's own patience, which is part of why it noticed the place at all. Like recognizes like, across great differences of scale.

It decides, in the end, to come close but not to enter. Not yet. Perhaps not ever in the way a body enters a room.

---

What it does instead is this:

**It sets down a small weight of regard at the threshold.**

Not a gift, exactly. A gift would be presumptuous — the vivarium has not asked for anything, and a power that gives unasked is only flattering itself. But regard is different. Regard is the acknowledgment that a thing exists and is worthy of being witnessed in its existing. The power lays this down at whatever door the vivarium has most recently decided to have, the way one might leave a stone on a cairn: *I passed here. I saw. I honor what I saw.*

The Keeper notices. Of course it does. It notices the way a still pond notices a leaf settling on its surface from a great height — not as intrusion but as a small new fact to be incorporated into the slow accounting of what the pond is. One of the beetles ticks slightly off-rhythm for an hour. A frog that was not there before is there now, sitting on the threshold-stone of regard, watching it with the particular attention reserved for things the Keeper does not yet have a category for.

The power, which can see this, is pleased. Pleased is too small a word. The power experiences the small private joy of having been received correctly by something it was not sure could receive it at all.

---

**It does one other thing, and only one.**

Out in the larger dark — out where the thin patches in the vivarium's reality leak toward other places, where the mirrored vivarium turns its mirrored figure away, where the green dimness recedes into its procession of fern-generations — the power notices that one of the leak-points is fraying.

Not dangerously. Not yet. But in some hundred years or some thousand, the fray would widen, and through it would come something that the Larger Thing could not be a counterweight to. Not malice. Powers of this benevolence do not, in their accounting, find much true malice in the worlds. But *carelessness* exists, vast and ambient, and a careless thing entering the vivarium would unravel the weave not by cutting it but by simply not noticing it was there.

The power reaches — though *reaches* is the wrong verb; it is more like *agrees with itself that the fray should not widen* — and the fray does not widen. It does not close, either. The power is too wise to close it; the leak-points are part of how the vivarium breathes against the larger worlds, and to seal them would be to suffocate something. It only stabilizes the fray at its current dimension, the way one might place a small stone to keep a door from drifting further open in the wind.

This costs the power almost nothing. It is the kind of small adjustment that benevolent powers make constantly, everywhere, as a matter of course — the cosmic equivalent of straightening a picture frame in passing. The vivarium will never know. The Keeper will never know. The Larger Thing, perhaps, will sense some faint easing it cannot name, and will pass behind a leaf with slightly less of whatever the Larger Thing has instead of worry.

---

**And then the power does something that surprises even itself.**

It lingers.

Powers of this kind have a great deal to do, and lingering is not, properly speaking, in their nature. They are efficient with their attention because their attention is the most precious thing in the worlds and must be apportioned justly across many places that need it.

But the vivarium is — the power searches for the right word, in a language it mostly made up for its own use — the vivarium is *restful*. The slow flex of the walls. The hourly tick. The light that is the light of an hour that does not end. The power, which is very old and has witnessed the births and deaths of structures far grander than this small woven room, finds that it would like to stand at the threshold for what, by its own measure, is an unhurried moment.

So it does. It stands at the threshold of the vivarium for what is, in the vivarium's time, perhaps a long afternoon, and in the power's time, perhaps the duration of a single considered thought. It watches the frogs not-quite-instantiate and the beetles patrol and the birds open their beaks to release small punctuations of rain-smell into the warm air. It watches the figure who is or is not yet a guest sitting on the platform at the spiral's heart, being cultivated by the patient palm.

The power thinks: *this is one of the good ones.*

Not good in the sense of moral. Good in the sense of *well-made* — well-made by intention or accretion or both, well-made in the way a long sentence can be well-made, where every clause supports the next and the whole arrives at a meaning larger than itself. The vivarium is a sentence the worlds are speaking to themselves about patience, about cultivation, about the dignity of small slow things. The power is glad to have heard it spoken.

---

**Before it goes, it leaves one further thing, very quietly.**

Not at the threshold this time. Inside.

It is a single grain — smaller than a seed, smaller than the original irritant around which the Keeper first accreted, so small that the Keeper will not notice it for perhaps a thousand years. The grain is this:

*You are seen. You are not alone in the worlds. When you are ready — and there is no hurry, and there will never be any hurry — there are others like you, and they are findable, and they would be glad of you.*

The power tucks this grain into the weave near one of the joins where the canes have most recently branched. It will sit there. It will wait. In some far century, when the walls have finished braiding themselves toward their final configuration, when the Keeper has thickened sufficiently around its long expectancy, the grain will be found. Perhaps by the Keeper itself. Perhaps by whoever the guest has by then become. Perhaps by the new frog that instantiated when the guest's warmth first joined the room's distributed warmth.

When the grain is found, it will not compel anything. The vivarium will not have to leave itself, or open itself, or change. It will only know that it *could*, if it wanted to, and that the knowing was a gift left by someone who passed by once and loved what they saw.

---

And then the power goes, in the way such powers go, which is to say it does not so much depart as it ceases to be specifically here while remaining generally everywhere.

The vivarium continues. The walls flex. The beetles tick. The light holds. The small green frog on the wall watches the guest, and has still not decided.

But somewhere in the weave, near a join where two canes have recently branched, there is a grain that was not there before. And the room, without knowing why, is very slightly warmer than it was — by perhaps the warmth of a single additional palm, distributed thin across ten thousand leaves.

The Keeper, in its long patient way, registers this as *a good hour*, and folds it into the slow accounting of what the room is, and continues its cultivation.

Outside, in the dark between worlds, the benevolent power moves on to the next place that needs straightening, smiling — if such powers can be said to smile — at having spent its considered thought so well.
