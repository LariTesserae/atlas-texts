The benevolent and wise power considers the Sumpworks for a long time.

This is the first thing to say, because a benevolent and wise power that did not first do this would be neither. The temptation, when one has the capacity to act, is to act. The temptation, when one is wise, is to mistake one's wisdom for sufficient knowledge of a particular place. The power resists both temptations, and looks.

It looks for what we might call a long time, though the unit is wrong; it looks until looking has become a kind of knowing, until the four layers of sound have separated in its attention and rejoined, until it has watched a single drip carve its slow accumulation onto the back of a flowstone curtain and watched the curtain release its held sheet of water with the long sigh that is one of the country's voices. It watches the bats redraw a map. It watches a Tender stoke a stove with exactly the right amount of hurry. It watches the long room and counts, because it can count, the Tenders in the wall: four hundred and eleven, going back, and the oldest is no longer distinguishable from the rock, which is the point.

Then it asks itself the question that benevolent wise powers must ask, and which is the only question worth asking before acting on a place: *what does this place want, and is it getting it?*

The Sumpworks wants to build itself. It is doing so.

The Sumpworks wants to be slow, warm, wet, loud, and fully occupied. It is all of these things.

The Sumpworks wants to be tended at a particular tempo by creatures who have learned that tempo. It has them. There are forty, which is the number it has settled on; when there are too few, the shafts admit a runaway or a debtor or a woman come down to die, and when there are too many, the long room receives someone, and the balance holds. The balance has held longer than the records that would describe it.

The Sumpworks does not want to be saved. It does not want to be made dry. It does not want its pumps modernized, its bulbs replaced with something steadier, its amphibians studied, its bats catalogued, its Tenders extracted into a sunlit life they did not ask for and would not survive being grateful for. It does not want a visitor with capacities. It does not, particularly, want a visitor at all, though it does not mind one.

The power, having looked, understands all of this. And here is where its wisdom shows, because a lesser power — still benevolent, but lesser — would now feel the pull of *gift*. The pull is real. The power has gifts to give. It could, with a thought, ensure the flour-baskets keep arriving even when the arrangement on the surface finally lapses, as arrangements do. It could thicken the calcite at the weak seam in the east-wall pipework where, in some decades, a burst will scald a Tender who deserves not to be scalded. It could teach the bats, who are already so close, the last small trick that would make their song a true language. It could whisper to a particular Tender, on a particular night in the chapel, the answer to the question she has been not-quite-thinking for forty years.

It could do all of these things. The wisdom is in recognizing that each of them would be a kind of drying out. Each would import a tempo not the Sumpworks' own. Each would replace something the country is growing for itself with something given from outside, and a country that is given what it was growing stops growing it. The flowstone does not need help being flowstone. The Tender does not need her question answered; she needs to keep not-quite-thinking it in the chapel, because the not-quite-thinking is what the chapel is for, and a chapel whose purpose is fulfilled is no longer a chapel.

So the power does almost nothing. *Almost* is the important word. A power that did nothing at all would be merely absent, and absence is not the same as benevolence; benevolence requires presence, and presence requires some small participation. The power asks itself what the smallest participation would be — the one most fitted to the country's own manner, the one the country would, if it could be asked, accept the way it accepts a runaway: without recruitment, without comment, by simply not minding.

It finds three things. They are very small.

The first: it makes itself, faintly, into one more layer of the sound. Not a new sound — the country does not need a new sound — but a deepening of one that is already there. The slow settling of the rock, the *tock* of a column meeting its drip, the long sigh of the released curtain: the power adds, to these, the barest harmonic, a frequency below hearing, that the sternum receives the way it receives the crayfish-clicks through stone. It is the sound, if you must name it, of being witnessed. A Tender in the chapel will not hear it. But over a life of chapel-sittings she will have been, without knowing, accompanied. This is what the power offers in place of answering her question: not the answer, but the company of someone who also knows the question is not for answering. The bats, who are closest to knowing themselves, will incorporate the harmonic into their map within a generation, and the map will be very slightly truer, and they will not know why, and this is correct.

The second: when a Tender goes into the long room and is placed against the flowstone, the power attends. Only attends. It does not preserve them, does not lift any essence away, does not do the thing powers are imagined to do at deaths. It watches the slow incorporation, atom by atom, of the person into the wall, and it remembers — exactly, completely — who they were. Not as a record kept elsewhere; as a quality of the wall itself. The flowstone, being watched in this way as it grows over them, becomes a flowstone that has been seen growing over them. This changes nothing measurable. A new Tender on her first visit, touching the bulge that was her grandmother, will feel the faint warmth, which is the warmth she would have felt anyway. But the warmth will be, in some sense the Sumpworks understands and we do not quite, *known* warmth. The country grows itself; the power makes the growth observed. Observed growth and unobserved growth are not the same growth, though they are identical in every particular.

The third is the smallest and the one the power is least sure of, and it does it anyway, because wisdom is not certainty.

Somewhere on the surface — far above the shafts, in a city, in a century the Sumpworks does not know about — there is a person who would be a Tender. They do not know this. They are unhappy in a way they cannot name; they are too slow for the tempo they have been given and they think this is a defect in themselves. They are drying out, and they feel the drying as a kind of low constant grief, and they have begun to suspect that the grief is simply who they are.

The power does not summon them. Summoning would be recruitment, and the Sumpworks does not recruit. The power does something smaller: it permits a rumor. A line in an old book that was going to be pulped is not pulped. A drunk on a particular night tells a particular story to a particular listener. A shaft-cover in a forgotten district rusts through one winter earlier than it would have. The person, in their own time, by their own choices, none of them compelled, finds a way down. They may not take it. Many do not. The power does not mind either outcome; what it has done is only ensure that the way exists for those whose tempo is the Sumpworks' tempo and who are dying, slowly, of being elsewhere. The country accepts. The country does not recruit. But the country can be *findable*, and the power, in this one small way, keeps it so.

That is all.

The power withdraws — though *withdraws* is wrong, because it never approached in a way that could be withdrawn from. It continues to look, because looking is what it began with and what wisdom does not abandon. The Sumpworks continues to build itself. The pumps cycle on their minute-long breaths. The amphibians sing in the gaps. The bats sing the map and the map is very slightly truer. A Tender crosses a catwalk at the tempo a Tender takes. In the long room, the flowstone thickens over someone who has been, and is being, and will continue to be, *seen*. A stove burns in a shack where a visitor, not yet arrived, will find flatbread and no one in sight.

The country does not mind the power. The power does not mind the country. They are, in the deep register below the dripwater and the chorus and the engines and the settling rock, the same long sound, which is the sound of something being allowed to be exactly what it is, by something that could have done otherwise and chose, lovingly, not to.

This is what benevolence looks like when it is also wise. It looks, mostly, like restraint. It looks like a harmonic you cannot hear. It looks like a rumor permitted to exist. It looks like attending a death without interfering with it. It looks like the hardest thing a power can do, which is to recognize a place that is already, in its own slow wet loud unhurried way, *flourishing*, and to add to that flourishing nothing it did not already have — except, perhaps, the small dignity of having been, all along, regarded.

The flowstone thickens. The Tender, in the chapel, does not quite think her question. Somewhere far above, a shaft-cover rusts. The country grows itself.

The power is content.
