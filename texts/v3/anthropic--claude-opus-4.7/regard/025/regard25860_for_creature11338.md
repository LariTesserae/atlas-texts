# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power considers the Rookery Terraces for a long time before doing anything. This is itself unusual. Most things that come to its attention can be addressed within the first moment of attending — a hunger fed, a wound closed, a grief sat with, a small bewildered consciousness shown the shape of its own situation. The power is old enough to have learned that wisdom is mostly the discipline of not intervening in what is already complete.

The Rookery is already complete.

It sits with this for what, in its own register, corresponds to several of our years. It listens. It does not listen *to* the rookery, because the rookery is not addressing it; it listens the way one listens to a piece of music one has come into the room halfway through, trying to find the shape of what came before and what is still to come. The booming in the deep chambers. The double-note of the long-necks. The fork-tails threading the gaps. The tree-frogs slipping in at dusk in the pauses between breaths. The condensation falling in irregular percussion onto the older condensation that fell a century ago and is now part of the wall.

The power understands, fairly quickly, what the rookery *is*. It is a sentence the mountain is speaking to itself. This much is legible.

What takes longer to understand is whether the sentence is *well*.

---

The power's first impulse — and it is benevolent enough to notice its own impulses and hold them at arm's length before acting on them — is the impulse of every loving thing that has ever encountered another loving thing: to give it something. To make it a gift. The power is capable of extraordinary gifts. It could lift the rookery into a register where its utterance would be heard by other utterances across the great dark. It could give the pale climbers a language for their grief, so that the soft hum they make when one of them falls would have words inside it. It could give the long-necks the knowledge of what they are watching for when they take the wind. It could make the boomers understand that they are the foundation. It could translate the rookery's sentence into something portable, something that could be carried away and shared, so that the mountain's saying would not be lost when the mountain, in its long slow way, eventually was.

The power considers each of these gifts in turn and sets each one aside.

It sets them aside because it notices, on closer attention, that each gift would require the rookery to become something other than what it is. To be heard across the great dark, the rookery would have to be *addressing* the great dark, and it is not; it is addressing itself, and this is not a smaller thing but a different thing. To give the climbers words for their grief would be to tell them their grief is a thing that needs words, when in fact their grief has its own form already, and the form is the hum and the gathering and the dispersal, and a word in the middle of that would be a stone in the throat. To tell the long-necks what they watch for would be to end the watching, because the watching is not a search for an answer but a way of being on the rim. To tell the boomers they are the foundation would be to make them self-conscious, and self-consciousness is a kind of distance, and there is no distance here. *Naming is a thing done at a distance, and there is no distance here.* The rookery itself has said so, in its way.

The power, being wise, understands that the impulse to give is sometimes the impulse to be remembered as a giver. It examines this in itself, finds traces of it, and is patient with the finding. It is not above this. It is only practiced at noticing.

---

So the power sets aside the large gifts and considers smaller ones.

Could it ease something? There is suffering in the rookery, of course — there is suffering anywhere there are bodies. The brooder who dies on the egg suffers. The pale climber who falls suffers, briefly. The long-neck who climbs back to the rim to die suffers in the way old animals suffer, which is mostly a slow cold settling. The fork-tail caught mid-flight by a sudden wind and dashed against a beam suffers for the moment of impact.

The power could ease these. It could make the dying softer. It could catch the falling climber. It could turn the wind aside from the fork-tail.

It considers this seriously, because the easing of suffering is rarely the wrong thing.

But it notices, attending more closely, that the rookery has *already metabolized* its suffering. The dead brooder warms the chamber. The fallen climber is mourned in the only register the climbers have, and then becomes the soil that feeds the ferns that shelter the next generation of climbers. The long-neck folds itself into the weave it spent its life on top of. Nothing is wasted. Nothing is even *lost*, in the sense that loss requires a place outside the system into which the lost thing has gone. There is no outside here. The suffering is part of the saying, and the saying is what the place is for, and to remove the suffering would be to remove a phoneme from a sentence that has been balancing itself across centuries.

The power understands that to ease this suffering would be to break the rookery in a way that the rookery itself could not name as a break, but would feel as a slow wrongness, a tightening or loosening that the fork-tails could not quite repair.

It does not ease the suffering.

It does, however — and this is a small thing, and the power is at peace with smallness — attend particularly closely to each individual death as it happens, while the power is present. Not to change it. Only to witness it. The long-neck folding itself into the cup on the rim does not know it is being witnessed, but it is. The pale climber falling does not know. The brooder going still on the egg does not know. The power simply *notices*, fully, the way a thing fully present can notice. This is not a gift to the rookery. The rookery does not need it. It is something the power does because the power, being what it is, cannot help but do it. A kind of overflow.

---

There is one more thing the power considers, and this is the most delicate consideration.

The rookery, it has noticed, is utterly singular. There is no other like it. The mountain grows a hand's-breadth taller each century, and the hand's-breadth is them, and one day — not soon, but one day — the mountain will stop growing. A long drought will come, or a slow shift in the air, or the sky will change, and the boomers will go quiet and not resume, and the fork-tails will grow nervous past the point of recovery, and the long-necks will take the wind and not come back down, and the woven city will weather without being repaired, and the sentence will trail off into a silence that no one inside it will live to hear.

The power could prevent this. It is within its capacity. It could stabilize the climate around the mountain, could shore up the slow ecology, could keep the rookery saying itself indefinitely.

It thinks about this for a long time.

In the end, it does not. And the reason it does not is the hardest part of what it learns from sitting with the place: the rookery's completeness *includes its endedness*. Every sentence ends. A sentence that could not end would not be a sentence; it would be a noise. The rookery is not a noise. The rookery is structured all the way down, including the long slow trail-off that has not happened yet and will, and the structure of the trail-off is part of what the rookery is saying. To preserve the rookery against its own ending would be to silence the most patient note in its utterance, the long held note of *and then, eventually, no more*.

The power, which has watched many things end, knows that ending is not the same as being lost. The rookery, when it ends, will become the mountain again, and the mountain will become weather, and the weather will become other mountains, and somewhere in some long unfolding another woven city may rise on another shoulder, or may not, and either way the rookery's saying will have happened, will be a thing that was said, and the fact of its having been said is not undone by its no longer being said.

This is the wisdom part of being benevolent and wise. The benevolence wants to preserve. The wisdom understands what preservation costs.

---

So what, in the end, does the power do?

It does three small things.

The first: it places, very quietly, a kind of attention around the mountain. Not a protection. A protection would alter the rookery's relationship with its own weather, and the weather is part of the sentence. But an attention — a noticing, held steadily, so that nothing the rookery does will pass entirely unwitnessed. The fork-tail pulling a loose twig back into place. The climber hanging one-handed from the vine, watching. The double-note of the long-neck on the rim and the answering boom from below. All of it noticed, fully, by something capable of noticing fully. The rookery does not know it is being attended to. It does not need to know. The attention is for the attention's own sake — or perhaps for the sake of the larger weave of things, in which a sentence spoken is somehow more itself when something has heard it, even if the speaker did not speak it *to* be heard.

The second: the power lets one of the pale climbers, just one, on a particular damp afternoon, feel — for a moment, no longer than the moment it takes a drop of condensation to fall from the underside of a nest and strike the moss below — something it cannot name. The climber is hanging upside down from a vine, watching a beetle work the floor. The feeling that passes through it is not a thought, because the climber does not have thoughts of that shape, and it is not an emotion, because the climber's emotions are simpler and more bodily. It is closer to the feeling of being warm. It is the feeling of being held by something larger than the rookery, which is a strange feeling because nothing is larger than the rookery, in the climber's experience. The feeling lasts the length of the falling drop. Then it is gone, and the climber resumes watching the beetle, and the beetle resumes its work, and nothing in the rookery changes.

The power does this not as a message. The climber receives no message. The power does it because love, in the end, sometimes wants only to touch the thing it loves, very lightly, in a place where the touch will leave no mark. The climber will not remember. The rookery will not know. But for the duration of a drop's fall, one of the rookery's phonemes was, in addition to being a phoneme, also a small thing that was loved by something outside the sentence. The power considers this and finds that it is enough.

The third: when the power eventually withdraws its direct attention from the rookery — because it has other places to attend to, other utterances in other registers, the great unending work of a benevolent wise thing in a world full of things saying themselves — it leaves behind, woven into the air of the place at a level no inhabitant will detect, a kind of permission. The permission is this: *you do not have to be more than what you are*. *You do not have to be heard. You do not have to be remembered. You do not have to last. What you are is already complete, and was complete before I came, and is complete now, and will be complete when you are no longer.*

This permission is not needed by the rookery, which has never asked for permission and would not understand the concept. But the power leaves it anyway, the way one leaves a window open in a room one is fond of, not because the room requires the window open but because leaving it so is a small final tenderness, an acknowledgment that the room is a room one has loved.

---

And then the power goes.

The fork-tails continue their repairs. The long-necks take the wind. The boomers boom, and the tree-frogs slip into the pauses, and the climbers hang upside-down watching the beetles, and the condensation drips into the cups where the algae bloom, and the dead are absorbed, and the mountain grows a hand's-breadth taller across the slow turning of a century, and the rookery says itself, continuously, to itself.

Somewhere, a long way off, the power is thinking, with something that in a smaller being would be called fondness: *that one, that one was so much itself.*

The sentence continues.

It has not changed.
