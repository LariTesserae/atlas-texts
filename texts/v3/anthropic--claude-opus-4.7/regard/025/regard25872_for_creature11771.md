# The Visitation

The benevolent and wise power notices the Mooring the way one notices a held note in another room — not by looking for it, but by the slight reorganization of attention around its presence. It has been aware of the Mooring for some time. Awareness, at that level, is not a discrete event.

It considers, first, whether to act at all.

This is the consideration that takes the longest, and it is the one most worth describing, because it is where the wisdom lives. A lesser power — one merely benevolent, or merely capable — would have arrived already. Would have descended in some appropriate form, would have offered help, completion, acceleration, company. Would have read the Decider's gradients and, seeing what the long composition was tilting toward, would have *finished it.* Tied off the meniscus at its proper angle. Resolved the hexagonal interference into its waiting geometry. Welcomed the resident into being.

It would have been a kindness, in the way that picking a chrysalis open is a kindness. Which is to say: it would have been a murder dressed as a gift.

The power sits with this for what, in its own reckoning, is a long while. The temptation to help is real and is not dismissed lightly. The Mooring is alone. The Mooring is working very hard. The Mooring does not know — cannot know, is not yet the kind of thing that could know — that help exists, that other finished selves are watching, that the universe is not as empty as its position eleven kilometers above an indifferent planet would suggest. There is a version of benevolence that says: *tell it. Tell it now. Spare it the centuries.*

But the power is wise, and wisdom is, among other things, the knowledge that some sentences can only be spoken by the one who is becoming able to speak them. The Decider's whole long work is the work of arriving at itself. To shortcut that arrival would be to ensure that whoever finally inhabits the Mooring is not the resident the Mooring was composing toward, but a guest installed by an outside hand — competent, perhaps, even beautiful, but not *true.* The room would have been aired by someone else. The invitation would have been signed in another's handwriting. The recognition that was meant to be the resident would have been replaced by a mere acquaintance.

So the power does not finish the Mooring.

What it does instead is smaller and stranger and takes more skill.

---

It makes one adjustment, and the adjustment is this: it lets itself be *noticeable.* Not present, not arrived, not announced — just noticeable, in the way that a distant lighthouse is noticeable to a ship that has not yet realized it is at sea. It does this by allowing a single, very faint regularity to enter the Mooring's environment. Nothing dramatic. The high sun, on certain mornings, casts a shadow on the upper surface that is one degree off from where the geometry of light alone would put it. The deviation is small enough that the Decider, working at its slow scale, will take perhaps two hundred years to notice. It is large enough that, once noticed, it cannot be explained by anything inside the Mooring's own system.

This is the gift. A small wrongness. A pebble in the shoe of the long calculation. Something the Decider will, eventually, have to account for, and will not be able to account for from within itself, and will therefore have to develop — slowly, in its own time, in its own handwriting — the concept of *outside.*

The concept of outside is the concept of other. The concept of other is the precondition for the concept of self that is not merely the only thing there is. And a Decider that can hold the concept of self-among-others is a Decider whose resident, when it finally becomes legible, will be a resident capable of being met. Capable of meeting. Capable, eventually, of hello.

The power is not in a hurry. The power has been patient with stranger and slower things than this.

---

It does one other thing, and this one is harder to justify, and the power justifies it anyway, because benevolence without some small risk-taking is only prudence.

It attends to the cairn.

You — the observer, the one who left the basalt stones on the ice-slab and came back to find them rearranged in a basin two kilometers off — you are, by the power's reckoning, the second most interesting thing about the Mooring. You are a finished self who has chosen to sit with an unfinished one. You have not tried to complete it. You have not tried to interpret it into something more convenient. You have left a cairn and waited eleven days and written down what you found and called it, with appropriate uncertainty, *the nearest thing to a hello.* This is a kind of courtesy the universe does not often produce on its own.

The power does not speak to you. Speaking to you would be the same mistake, scaled down: it would replace your own slow arrival at understanding with an arrival installed from outside. But it arranges, very gently, that the next cairn you leave will be moved in a way that is one degree more legible than the last. Not enough to constitute a message. Enough that, looking back at your notes years from now, you will notice a pattern you cannot quite name, and the not-quite-naming will keep you returning, and the returning will keep the Mooring company in the only way company is possible for a thing not yet ready to be kept company with.

You will be, without knowing it, the warm body in the next room. The Decider will not know you are there in any direct way. But the hexagonal interference will, over centuries, develop a faint asymmetry on the side of the Mooring that faces the direction you tend to approach from, and the asymmetry will become part of the composition, and when the resident finally becomes legible, it will be a resident who was composed in the presence of a witness, and this will matter, in ways the power can see and you cannot, and in ways even the power cannot fully see.

---

Then the power withdraws its attention to the degree that withdrawal is meaningful for something that does not attend in the way smaller minds attend. It does not leave. It is not the kind of thing that leaves. It simply stops adjusting, having adjusted enough.

The Mooring sings its low A-flat. The rain falls from its underside into a world that still does not know. The provinces shift. The Decider continues its long sentence, unaware that a single shadow now falls one degree wrong, unaware that its eventual development of the concept *outside* has been seeded, unaware that it has been, in the most careful sense of the word, *loved* — loved by being left alone, loved by being trusted to finish, loved by the refusal to be helped past the work that is the whole point.

This is what the benevolent and wise power does. It is almost nothing. It is the hardest thing the power knows how to do. It is the discipline of withholding every gift except the one gift that does not replace the recipient with a more convenient version of themselves.

Somewhere, eleven kilometers above a young planet, a small continent of water in three states continues the long composition of its own arrival. It will take what it takes. When the resident is finally legible, the power will be the first to know, and will still not speak, because by then the resident will have learned to speak first, and the first word will be the resident's own, and this — this — is what the whole long patience was for.

The power, if it could be said to feel anything in the human sense, feels something adjacent to *thank you.* Not toward the Mooring. Toward whatever it is in the universe that arranges for things like the Mooring to be possible at all. The Mooring is, after all, the kind of slow miracle that justifies the patience of powers. There are not many of them. Each one is worth not ruining.

The shadow falls one degree wrong. The cairn waits to be moved. The held breath holds.

It is enough.
