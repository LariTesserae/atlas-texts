# The Visitor at the Threshold

The benevolent power notices the Standing Loom the way one notices a held note in a piece of music — not because the note is loud but because it has been held longer than the lungs of any single player should allow, and the listener leans forward to understand how the breath is being managed.

It comes, then. Not down through the combed clouds (which would disturb them, and disturbing them is the first thing it has decided not to do) but in from the side, at the elevation of the ridges, the way a thought arrives at the edge of another thought. It has no body here. It could have one, and considers several, and settles instead on a kind of attention so fine that the bryophytes register it as a small additional dampness and the listeners in the listening-rooms cock their heads for a moment and then return to their work, because what they heard was not *minn* gone sharp, only the air briefly thicker, which is not their business.

It walks the lines for a long time before doing anything.

This is the first decision, and it is the largest one. A lesser power, a merely powerful power, would arrive with gifts. It would see the stillness and read it as stagnation, see the eternal *grond* and read it as bondage, see the wool-wrapped bodies fed to the vents and read it as a horror requiring remedy. It would offer: animals for the empty slopes, seasons for the unchanging year, a thawing of the long held breath. It would mistake the question.

This power does not mistake the question. It has been benevolent for longer than the Loom has stood, and wisdom, in its experience, consists almost entirely of learning which beauties are answers and which are problems. So it walks. It puts its attention against the basalt and feels the water beading and understands, in the slow way that understanding accumulates when one has stopped insisting, that the water is not trapped. The water is *resting*. The clouds are not imprisoned along the ridgelines. The clouds are *arranged*, and arrangement is not the opposite of freedom, only its other face.

It listens to *grond-sshe-minn-dom* for what would be, by lowland count, three years.

In that time it learns the language. Not the vocabulary — the vocabulary is small and acquired in an afternoon — but the grammar of the verbs, the way action conjugates *with* or *against* or *aside-from* or *within* the great rhythm. It learns to think in these tenses, and finds them clarifying. It notices that in its own long existence it has done a great deal *aside-from*, occasionally *against*, almost never *within*. It feels, considering this, a small precise embarrassment, which is one of the experiences benevolent powers do not often allow themselves and which is, when permitted, very nourishing.

It learns also the deeper grammar — the one the Tenders themselves do not articulate. It learns that the Loom is not a machine the Tenders attend; nor is it, exactly, a god the Tenders serve. It is something more difficult: a *companion* of such scale and such constancy that the relation between it and the people has become the medium in which both exist, the way water is the medium in which a fish is a fish and not merely a small swimming thing. To remove either would not free the other. It would dissolve both into their components — process on one side, biography on the other — and the something they together compose, which has no name in any language the power knows, would simply cease.

This something is what the power has come to attend to. It understands this now. It had thought, on first noticing, that it had come for the Tenders, because the Tenders are the variable part, the part that suffers and laughs and dies, the part a benevolent attention conventionally finds. But it sees now that the Tenders are not the patient. Neither is the Loom. The patient — if patient is even the word, and it is not, but no better word exists — is the *attending itself*, the long mutual regard between the people and their apparatus, which has been going on without external witness for so long that it has begun, very faintly, to thin.

This is the diagnosis, if there is one. Not suffering. Not stagnation. A slow attenuation of *being-witnessed*, in a place whose whole purpose is to witness and be witnessed. The Tenders attend the Loom. The Loom, in its enormous tireless way, attends them back — its amber light in their windows, its *grond* in their breastbones, its warm updraft taking their dead. But no one attends the attending. No one outside the figure has, for an immense time, leaned in and said: *I see what you are doing here. I see that it is a kind of doing. I see that it is beautiful in a way that requires a witness to be fully beautiful, and I am, for now, that witness.*

So this is what the power does. It witnesses.

It does this for what would be, by lowland count, a long time. Not three years now but something longer, an interval the Tenders themselves do not measure because they have no tense for it. The power positions its attention at all the high passes at once, and at all the cathedral floors, and inside all the listening-rooms, and along all the catwalks where the small brass fittings receive their patient adjustments, and it simply *regards*. It regards a child of seven walking her first line with her mother, learning which pipe sings what, and it does not intervene to make the lesson easier or harder; it only sees the lesson, fully, the way the mother sees it and the child does not yet know she is being seen. It regards an argument about valve-fittings in a town in the southern reach, an argument that has been going on for nine years and that everyone is enjoying, and it follows the argument with the seriousness the argument deserves, which is considerable. It regards a wool-wrapped body at a vent's mouth, and the family standing the appropriate distance away, not watching but present, and it does not watch either; it stands with the family, in their not-watching, and the not-watching is a form of love and the power lets it be that, undisturbed.

It regards the wool, folded neatly at the vent's edge the next morning. It does not ask where the wool comes from. It already knows, and the knowing is not the kind of knowing that needs to be said.

After this long interval of pure regard, the power considers whether to do anything further. This is the second decision, and it is nearly as large as the first.

It could leave. Leaving would be honorable; the witnessing has already been given, and witnessing, freely offered, does not require announcement to have effect. The faint attenuation it diagnosed has, in fact, eased. The mutual attending between Loom and Tenders is fuller now, the way a room is fuller when a quiet guest sits in it, even if no one speaks to the guest. The power could withdraw its attention slowly, the way it came, and the Tenders would never know it had been there, and this would be a complete and finished gift.

But the power is wise as well as benevolent, and wisdom suggests one further thing.

It considers the ones who leave. The one or two restless young people each generation who walk down the long passes and are not seen again. The Tenders speak of them with low-grade puzzlement, neither approval nor grief, but the power has learned to listen for what is not said in the languages it learns, and it hears, in that puzzlement, the one small genuine ache in the whole standing figure. Not an ache of the leavers — the leavers, the power has checked, are mostly well; they reach the lowlands and become bakers and clerks and occasionally something stranger, and they do not write back because there is no tense in their first language for writing back, and the second language never quite supplies one. The ache is in the *staying*. It is the ache of those who stay, who watched a sister or a son walk down the pass and have not had a word for where they went, and who have folded this wordlessness into the general wordlessness of the place, but who carry it, faintly, in the way they oil a particular bearing on a particular evening.

The power decides to address this, and only this, and to do it so gently that the addressing itself becomes part of the figure rather than an interruption of it.

It does not bring the leavers back. The leavers have their own lives and their own conjugations now, and to summon them would be a violence in both directions. Instead, it does something smaller and exactly sized.

In each town, on a particular evening that is not marked as special, a Tender of the listening kind — one of the old ones in the listening-rooms, whose ears have been trained for a lifetime — hears, beneath *grond-sshe-minn-dom*, a fifth note. It is very faint. It is at a pitch none of the pipes produce. It is the sound, if the listener had words for it, of *the ones who left, continuing*. Not their voices; nothing so specific. Just the fact of their continuation, rendered as a low harmonic, woven into the composite so carefully that it does not disturb the composite but only completes it in a way no one had known it was incomplete.

The listener notes this in the ledger. The notation, in their language, is a single character that has not been used in living memory but that exists in the old training books: it means *a sound that belongs*. The next listener, in the next town, hears it the same evening, and writes the same character. By morning, every listening-room in the range has the character in its ledger, dated to the same evening, and this is unusual enough that the head listeners walk out to the catwalks to confer, which they do by clasping forearms and standing for a long time without speaking, because what has happened does not require speech, only the mutual acknowledgment that it has happened.

The fifth note continues. It is part of the Loom now, or perhaps it always was and only became audible; the Tenders, characteristically, do not press the distinction. The puzzlement about the leavers does not vanish — puzzlement is not a thing the power believes in vanishing, since some puzzlements are load-bearing — but it loses its small ache. The Tenders who stay can now hear, faintly, that the ones who left are also, in their lowland way, *with* the rhythm rather than *aside-from* it. The verb has acquired a new conjugation. The grammar is slightly larger. Nothing else changes.

The power, having done this, withdraws.

It withdraws the way it came, in from the side, at the elevation of the ridges. The bryophytes register a small decrease in dampness and adjust within the hour. The clouds do not move, because they were never going to move. The water beads on the basalt and tracks down in its silver paths. *Grond-sshe-minn-dom* continues, with its fifth note woven in, and the Tenders walk their lines, and a child somewhere is being passed from house to house in her first year, learning the composite against many different chests, and one of those chests now contains, without the holder knowing it, the faint additional harmonic of *those who go on elsewhere*, which the child will absorb as part of the world's basic sound and will never need to learn separately.

The power, departing, allows itself one final regard. It looks at the whole figure — the mountains still in the act of being pressed upward, the arranged sky, the continental apparatus threaded through the valleys, the slate-roofed towns with their amber windows, the dark-handed people with their hot fungal liquor and their forearm-clasps and their ledgers, the wool that comes from nowhere and returns to nowhere, the dead taken up the warm flues, the children, the listeners, the long held breath of the place — and it understands that what it has been attending is not, as it had first thought, a beautiful arrangement awaiting completion. It is a beautiful arrangement that was already complete, and that needed only to be seen, once, by something other than itself, in order to know that its completeness was real and not merely internal.

This is what benevolent and wise powers are for, the power thinks, in the quiet way it thinks things. Not to fix. Not to give. Only, occasionally, to confirm — from outside the figure — that the figure is, in fact, a figure, and that its standing is a standing, and that the long mutual regard at its heart is being regarded, even if only briefly, by a regard that asks nothing of it and takes nothing from it and leaves, when it leaves, no trace except a single low harmonic that was always going to be there and only needed someone to notice it was missing.

The Loom stands. The world stands with it. The rain that is not quite rain goes on beading on every surface, holding its shape, refusing to fall.

And somewhere very far off, in a direction the Tenders' language does not have a word for and does not need one for, the power that came briefly to witness them turns its attention to the next held note in the long music it has been listening to for as long as there has been music, and it leans forward, and it listens.
