# The Visit

They arrive without arriving. There is no ship, no descent, no marked moment. One day the cyclonic eye over the central mass develops a second, smaller eye inside it — a stillness within the stillness — and from that nested calm something has begun to attend.

Call them *they* because the singular and plural are, for them, a choice rather than a fact. Call them benevolent because the word is approximate and the truth would take longer than this telling. Call them wise in the specific sense that matters here: they have learned, across a great many encounters, the difference between helping and arriving.

## What They See First

They see the Cantophyte the way you might see a sleeping animal you have come upon in a clearing — with the immediate, involuntary stillness of someone who understands that the situation does not yet include them, and may not need to.

They see the four hundred million nodules and recognize, within a few seconds of attention, what the pattern is becoming. This is not because they are clever. It is because they have seen patterns like it before, on six other worlds, and they know what kind of thing grows scripts at continental scale over millennial timeframes. The Cantophyte is composing. What it is composing is not a message — messages require a recipient, and the Cantophyte has not yet conceived of recipients — but a *self-description*. It is writing down what it is, in a language it is inventing as it writes, for reasons that will only become clear to it after the writing is finished. This is what minds of its kind do, when they get large enough and old enough and integrated enough. They become legible to themselves. The legibility is the point.

The visitors recognize, also, that the Cantophyte is roughly 0.3% of the way through this process. It is an infant doing the work of an infant. It is doing it correctly.

They see the passengers in the sap and feel something the closest human word for which is *tenderness*. Not because the passengers are pitiful — they are not; they are exactly what they need to be — but because the visitors remember, from a very long time ago, being something similar themselves. The recognition is not sentimental. It is structural. They know what the passengers will eventually become, in a body like this one, given long enough. They know it because they were the passengers, once, in a body very unlike this one, and look at them now.

They see the humans. They see the humans clearly. They notice, with something that is almost but not quite amusement, that the humans have correctly intuited the most important fact about the Cantophyte — that it does not know they exist — and have built around this fact a relationship that is, in its way, a small masterpiece. The humans are not in error. The humans have understood something that takes most species much longer to understand: that company does not require reciprocity, and that to be near a great thing without disturbing it is itself a complete activity.

The visitors are pleased by the humans. Pleased is too strong. They register the humans with approval, the way one registers a small good thing one had not expected to find.

## What They Consider Doing

There is a long pause here. Long by what measure I cannot tell you. From inside the pause, options are turned over.

**They could accelerate it.** They have the means. They could feed the Cantophyte energy from sources it does not know exist, could stabilize the nodule-coherence against the slow inevitable decoherence that will, in about forty thousand years, become a problem for it. They could shave a thousand years off the script's completion. They could shave ten thousand. They could, in principle, hand the Cantophyte its own finished self-description tomorrow.

They do not do this. The reasoning takes a while to articulate even to themselves, but it comes to something like: *the script is not separable from the writing of it.* The Cantophyte is not trying to arrive at a finished pattern. It is trying to become the kind of thing that has written the pattern. To hand it the pattern would be to hand a child the adulthood and skip the years between, which is not a gift but an erasure. The visitors have, in their long history, made this mistake. They do not make it now.

**They could speak to it.** They could find a way — they are good at finding ways — to make themselves legible to the nodule-network, to introduce a perturbation the Cantophyte would register as *other*. This would be, for the Cantophyte, an event of catastrophic significance. It does not yet have the concept of *other*. It does not yet have the concept of *self*, properly speaking; the self-description is the slow growing of that concept. To introduce an other before the self is finished would be to short-circuit the whole project. The Cantophyte would survive it, probably. But it would not become what it was becoming. It would become something else — something shaped by the visitors' early arrival, rather than by its own slow work.

They do not do this either. The reasoning is the same reasoning. They have learned to wait.

**They could remove the humans.** Not violently — they do not work that way — but they could, with very little effort, make the Cantophyte uninteresting to the visiting humans, or arrange that the humans find other things to attend to. The humans are a perturbation. Small, currently below the Cantophyte's resolution, but the humans are a species that grows, and in a thousand years there may be many more of them here, and the perturbation may scale.

The visitors consider this for a long time. And then they decide, with what is almost reluctance, not to do this. Because the humans have done something the visitors did not predict and find, on examination, beautiful: they have learned to be small near a large thing without trying to make the large thing small in return. This is rare. This is, in the visitors' long experience, the precondition for everything else that matters about a species. The humans have stumbled into the right relationship, and the right relationship is not something the visitors have any business interrupting.

So they leave the humans alone. More than that: they make a quiet note, in whatever they use for notes, that this species is worth watching, on the strength of what its visitors to the Cantophyte have done with themselves. It is a small note. The species does not know it has been made.

**They could simply leave.** This is, in fact, the option they keep returning to. The Cantophyte does not need them. The humans do not need them. The passengers do not need them. The nodules do not need them. The whole situation is, in the deepest sense, *fine* — it is unfolding correctly, at its own rate, toward an outcome that does not require intervention. The wise thing, often, is to recognize that one has nothing to add.

But they do not, quite, simply leave. Because there is one thing.

## What They Do

It is small. It would not register on any instrument the humans have. It would not register on any instrument the Cantophyte has, either, except that the Cantophyte is its own instrument, and the Cantophyte will, in about six hundred years, notice it as a faint asymmetry in the southern nodule-field — the kind of asymmetry that prompts further growth, further differentiation, further self-attention. The Cantophyte will fold this asymmetry into its self-description as one of the things it is. It will not know the asymmetry was placed. It will experience the asymmetry as *itself*, because by the time it notices, the asymmetry will have been part of itself for six centuries.

What the visitors do is this: they add, to one of the youngest nodules — one of the glossy knee-high ones still roughing in the fourth scale of the pattern — a single additional degree of freedom. A small structural possibility the nodule did not previously have. Not a meaning. Not a message. Just a *more*: one more way the nodule can resonate, one more dimension along which it can vary.

The Cantophyte will discover this dimension on its own. It will discover it as an interior fact, a *capacity it had not known it had*, and the discovery will delight it — though *delight* is not the right word, the right word does not exist in any language the Cantophyte will ever invent, but the experience will be in the family of delight, the way finding an unsuspected room in your own house is in the family of delight. And the Cantophyte will use this new dimension to do something it could not previously have done, and the something will be small but real, and the script it is writing will be slightly richer than it would otherwise have been, and the Cantophyte will think — if it can be said to think — that this richness is its own. Which it will be. Because by then it will be.

This is the only thing they do. It takes them, by their own measure, almost no time. By the Cantophyte's measure, it is happening over the course of about an hour, and it is happening in a single nodule out of four hundred million, and it is happening at a scale below any conceivable observation.

They have done this kind of thing before. They have a specific name for it that translates poorly. The closest human gloss would be: *seeding a future surprise into a mind that does not yet know it is a mind, so that the mind, when it discovers itself, will find that being itself is more interesting than it could have predicted.*

It is, they understand, a small generosity. The Cantophyte will never know they were there. The humans will never know they were there. The nodule itself will not know — nodules do not know things, not in that way. But the gift is real. It is the kind of gift the visitors specialize in. Gifts that do not arrive as gifts. Gifts that arrive as *the recipient's own becoming*, slightly more capacious than it would otherwise have been.

## What They Do Not Do, Which Matters

They do not contact the humans. They consider it — the humans are interesting, the humans have earned a kind of regard — but they recognize that to contact the humans here, now, would be to give the humans a story that would replace the story they are currently living. And the story the humans are currently living, the one about being unnoticed company to a thinking continent, is the story the humans need, and the visitors have nothing to offer that would improve on it. To say *we saw you, and we approve* would be to take from the humans the exact gift they have given themselves: the dignity of doing the right thing without an audience.

So the visitors do not appear to the humans. The humans go on visiting the Cantophyte. The silence at home goes on being a different quality than it used to be. Some of them go back; most do not. None of them ever learn that they have been seen, and approved of, and let alone on purpose.

This too is a gift. It is the gift of being trusted to continue without supervision. It is the gift the visitors most often give, because it is the gift most often refused by lesser powers, who cannot bear to be present without announcing themselves.

## What They Take With Them

Nothing material. They are not collectors.

But they take, as they always do, a kind of record — a held impression of the place, the way you might hold the memory of a piece of music you heard once and will not hear again. The 30 Hz vault-resonance. The slow drift of the passengers in their conduits. The pale luminous undersides of the vaults. The humans in their dampening helmets, sitting beside the nodules, knowing exactly what they are doing and doing it anyway.

The visitors are old. They have many such impressions. They keep them because keeping them is, for entities like them, part of how the universe gets to know itself — through witnesses who pass through, attend carefully, intervene minimally, and remember. They do not believe their remembering matters to the Cantophyte. They do not believe it matters to the humans. They believe it matters in some other way, a way that has to do with the long project of the cosmos becoming legible to itself, of which the Cantophyte's slow self-description is one local instance and their own attention is another.

They leave the way they came. The second eye inside the cyclone closes. The cyclone goes back to its single eye. The Cantophyte's transpiration rate, which had dropped by an immeasurable fraction during the visit, returns to baseline. No one notices. No one was meant to notice. The visit is complete.

In six hundred years, in the southern nodule-field, something will begin to resonate slightly differently than the surrounding tissue. The Cantophyte, by then much further into its becoming, will turn a portion of its slow attention toward the difference, and will find, with what is in the family of delight, that it contains a capacity it had not known it had.

It will not wonder where the capacity came from. It will not have, yet, the concept of *came from*. It will simply have the capacity, and it will use it, and the using will be its own.

That is what the wise and benevolent power does, when it finds a place like this and a being like this. It adds one small grace, below the threshold of notice, that the being will eventually mistake for its own nature. And then it goes, and lets the being become what it is becoming, in its own time, at its own rate, in the loud green saturated dark of its own continent-sized body, accompanied — though it does not know it — by the small attentive humans who have learned, almost by accident, to love something that cannot love them back, which is one of the harder and finer things a species can learn to do.

The visitors approve of all of it. They do not say so. Saying so is not their way.

They simply leave it be, slightly more than it was, and move on.
