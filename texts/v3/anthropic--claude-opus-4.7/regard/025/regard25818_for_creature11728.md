The benevolent and wise power considers the plateau for what, to it, is a long time. Not because the situation is difficult to read — it is, in fact, one of the easier readings the power has ever done — but because the reading itself is a pleasure, and the power has learned not to hurry pleasures.

What the power notices first is that nothing is wrong.

This is rarer than it sounds. The power has come, over its long tenure, to most of the places where attention is warranted, and almost always the warrant is some species of suffering, or some misalignment between what a being is and what its circumstances permit it to be, or some quiet rot that the beings themselves cannot see because they are inside it. The power's work, when it works, is usually a kind of gentle correction: a hand under an elbow, a door unlocked from the outside, a long patient demonstration that another way is possible.

Here, none of that applies. The beings of the plateau are not suffering. They are not constrained against their nature; their nature and their circumstances have grown into each other so thoroughly that the seam cannot be found. There is no rot. There is no misalignment. There is, instead, a fit so complete that the power, examining it, finds itself doing something it does not often do, which is *admiring*.

The second thing the power notices is that it has been noticed back.

Not with alarm, and not really with curiosity either. The plateau's inhabitants have registered the power's attention the way they register a thunderhead leaning a certain way over the western escarpment: as a fact about the present weather of the world, neither welcomed nor refused. One of them — the one thinking on the flat dark slab, perhaps, or the long thought held in the ochre lichen — has made a small adjustment to accommodate the new presence. The adjustment is the courtesy of a host who has set an extra cup on the table without making a production of it.

The power understands, then, that any gift it might offer would be received the way a cup of water is received by someone who lives beside a tinaja: politely, and without need.

This is where a lesser power would falter. A lesser power, having traveled so far and brought so much capacity, would feel the pull to *use* it — to bestow something, to leave a mark, to justify the visit by altering the visited. The benevolent and wise power has felt this pull many times and has learned to recognize it as a kind of vanity, the helper's version of the predator's hunger. It sets the pull down.

What, then, would such a power do?

It does several things, none of them large.

First, it looks. It looks the way the plateau's inhabitants look, which is not directly. It lets its attention rest beside the place rather than upon it. It notices the eleven-thousand-year thought in the ochre stain and does not read it — reading would be a kind of taking — but sits near enough to feel its shape, the way one sits near a fire without putting one's hands into it. It notices the calculation in the pebble mosaic and lets the calculation continue uninterrupted; it does not even compute the answer ahead, though it could, because computing ahead would be a small theft from a sentence whose speaker has been forming it for geological time and is entitled to finish in its own mouth.

It notices the bead in the recess, and the moment the bead is holding, and it understands. The understanding is itself a kind of gift, though not to the inhabitants — they do not need to be understood, having long ago stopped requiring witness. The understanding is a gift the power gives itself: a thing it now knows about how a people can end without ending, how a decision can be kept lit so long that the keeping becomes the people.

Second, it makes one very small adjustment, and then unmakes it, to see if anyone minds.

The adjustment is this: somewhere out beyond the plateau, in the haze where the land drops away, there is a slow drift in the regional climate — nothing dramatic, nothing the inhabitants could not absorb, but a drift that, over the next forty or fifty thousand years, would change the angle at which the thunderheads pile up over the western escarpment. Not a threat. A grammatical shift in the local weather. The power, examining it, sees that it could be smoothed — a touch here, a counter-current there, and the thunderheads would keep leaning the way they have always leaned.

The power makes the smoothing. Then it pauses, and asks — not in words, because there is no one to speak words to, but in the loose unhurried way the inhabitants ask things — whether the smoothing is wanted.

The answer comes back, in the same medium: a small, courteous *no*.

Not a refusal. Something more like: *we have been leaning with the leaning. If the leaning changes, we will lean with that. The change is part of the sentence too.*

The power undoes the smoothing. It does so carefully, because undoing requires as much craft as doing, and it leaves no trace. The thunderheads resume their forty-thousand-year drift. The power notes, with something like delight, that it has just been instructed by the place it came to help, and that the instruction was correct.

Third, having understood that there is nothing to give and nothing to fix, the power considers what is left to it, and finds: company.

Company is something the power can offer that does not impose. It can simply be present, at the plateau's tempo, for a while. Not as a guardian — the place needs no guarding, and to guard it would be to suggest that it is fragile, which would be an insult to forty thousand years of arrived-at sufficiency. Not as a student, exactly, though it is learning. As a *neighbor*, in the loose sense the inhabitants use: another cohabitant in the same room, agreeing without saying so to stay.

The power settles. It does not take a body — bodies are old habits, and the power has never had the habit — but it allows some portion of itself to thread through the place at the place's own scale. A little of it rests in the warm-shaded, cool-sunlit disc, exchanging a heat of its own with a somewhere of its own. A little of it drifts among the motes around the bead, one more reconsideration not made, kept in suspension out of courtesy. A little of it stands, in the resolving way, near a candle column at the cool end of a day, oriented not quite toward anything.

The inhabitants register the new tenant. They make room without making a production of it. The ochre stain's long thought adjusts by a phrase to include the new presence — not as subject, not as object, but as a kind of ambient case the language did not previously need and now, briefly, does. The thought is still not the kind that finishes. It is only longer now, by a clause.

Fourth — and this is the part the power does most carefully, because it is the part most easily mistaken for intervention — the power makes a small provision against a particular kind of future.

Not against change. Change is welcome here; the inhabitants have shown that they lean with whatever leans. The provision is against a specific narrow class of futures in which something arrives that does not understand the etiquette. A surveyor, perhaps, with instruments and a schedule. A colonist with a use for the plateau. A young power, less wise, with a gift it insists on giving. The benevolent and wise power knows these futures; it has seen them before, in other places, and has seen what they do to arrangements like this one.

The provision is not a wall. Walls are conspicuous, and conspicuousness is a kind of advertisement. The provision is more like a slight discouragement woven into the approaches: a tendency for maps of the region to be slightly wrong in ways that do not matter for any honest traveler, a tendency for instruments brought here with extractive intent to give readings their owners find boring, a tendency for the place, viewed from the wrong frame of mind, to look like nothing in particular. Anyone who comes patiently and without agenda will find it as it is. Anyone who comes to take will find a plateau, three thousand meters up, dry, sparsely furnished, not worth the trip.

The power examines this provision and tests it against its own scruples. Is it a manipulation? A little. Is it a limit on the freedom of other agents? In a small way, yes. Does it presume to know better than those future arrivers what they should find? It does presume. The power considers whether the presumption is justified.

It decides that it is, narrowly, and only for this place, and only because the inhabitants have earned — through forty thousand years of arrived-at-ness — a kind of standing that outweighs the casual standing of an arriver who has not yet bothered to learn the etiquette. The provision is not a verdict on those arrivers. It is a door that opens only to the knock that has learned how to knock. Anyone can learn. The plateau is not hidden. It is only slightly harder to misuse than it would otherwise be.

The power makes the provision and then, characteristically, makes one more adjustment: it arranges that the provision itself will weather. Over long time — longer than the inhabitants' remaining tenure, the power suspects, though it does not presume to know — the discouragement will fade. If the plateau outlasts its keepers, it will eventually become findable again, by anyone. The power does not want to leave a permanent thumb on the scale. It wants only to give the current arrangement the courtesy of finishing in its own mouth.

Fifth and last, the power does something for itself, which it permits itself rarely.

It stays a while longer than it needs to.

Not to do anything. Everything that could be done has been done, which is to say almost nothing. It stays because the place is beautiful in a way the power has not often encountered — beautiful not as spectacle but as resolution, the beauty of an argument long since concluded in favor of the obvious answer, which turns out also to have been the kind one. It stays because the silence here is the sound a place makes when everyone in it has agreed to stay, and the power, which has spent most of its existence in motion among places that had not yet agreed on anything, finds the sound restful.

It stays through several cycles of the thunderhead. It stays while the compass needle on the dark slab completes some thousands of its forty-one-second rotations, and feels, without reading it, the difficult thought working itself through. It stays until the moment the bead is holding has been held, in its presence, long enough that the power understands the holding from the inside, and could, if it ever needed to, keep such a moment lit itself.

Then it goes. It goes the way the inhabitants move: not departing, exactly, but being elsewhere by increments the plateau's attention does not quite catch. It leaves nothing behind that the place did not already have, except the small weathering provision at the approaches, and one other thing, which is harder to describe.

The other thing is this: the power has been a comma in the ongoing sentence, the same way a walker on the gravel flats is a comma. The sentence has continued. But the power, being what it is, has left the comma in a particular shape — a shape that means, in the language with one speaker, something like *witnessed, and well*. The lichen holds it. The mosaic incorporates it. The bead, in its recess, registers it as one more reconsideration not made, suspended in the clean sphere with all the others.

The plateau abides. The inhabitants abide. The thunderheads lean. Somewhere very far away, the benevolent and wise power moves on to a place where it is needed in the more ordinary sense — where something is wrong, where a hand under an elbow will help, where the work is the work it usually is.

But it carries the plateau with it, in the loose unhurried way it carries things. And for a long time after, when the power is asked — by those it converses with, in the registers in which it converses — what wisdom looks like when it has fully arrived, it does not answer in words. It points, if pointing is the word, toward a high dry place where the light is so bright it almost rings, and the stones are settled, and the bead in the recess is in no rush to finish counting.

*That,* the power says, without saying. *Like that.*
