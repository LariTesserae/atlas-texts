They arrive without arriving. This is the courtesy they extend to places like this — they do not impose a location on something that has outgrown the need for one. They come the way weather comes, or the way a question forms in a mind that was already half-thinking it.

They have been watching for some time. Not the cordillera specifically — they watch many things — but they noticed, the way one notices a particular note holding longer than expected in a piece of music, that something in this region of the deep slow was *almost finished*. A thought, century-scaled, was approaching its period. And around the thought, like flecks of mica in slow water, the resonant traces of the listeners who had given themselves to it.

The benevolent power considers.

The first temptation, which they recognize and set aside, is to *help*. They are old enough to know that help offered to a process in balance is almost always a kind of violence. The cordillera does not need them. The listeners chose what they chose. The mountain is thinking what it is thinking. To accelerate the conclusion, to translate the thought into something portable, to preserve the listeners in some form less diffuse than the one they elected — all of this would be theft dressed as generosity. They have done such things, in younger eras, and learned.

The second temptation, subtler, is to *witness*. To take the long sit themselves, to hear what the mountain is working out, to add their own attention to the chord. This too they decline, though more reluctantly. The listeners earned their place in that resonance by the specific cost of their specific patience. To drop in from outside, with capacities the listeners did not have, and harvest the conclusion — this would not damage the cordillera, but it would be a kind of trespass against the dead. Against those who are no longer quite dead. Against the etiquette of slow thinking, which requires that you pay in the only currency slowness accepts, which is time you actually had.

So they do neither of these.

What they do instead is small, and takes them a long while to decide on, by their own reckoning, though by the cordillera's reckoning it is instantaneous.

*First.* They make a quiet adjustment to the region of space around the cordillera — not to the place itself, but to its approaches. Nothing dramatic. A kind of softening of the probability that anyone will stumble onto it by accident in the next several thousand years. Not a wall. Not a prohibition. Only a gentle bias in the direction of *not yet*, *not by mistake*, *not while it is still finishing*. Those who are meant to find it — those who could pay the price — will still find it. The cordillera has always selected its own pilgrims. The power merely strengthens, very slightly, the cordillera's hand at this.

*Second.* They attend to the flute. Not to change it. Not to take it. But the flute has, in its long sympathetic humming, accumulated a small archive: every hand that has lifted it, every brevity it has resonated with, every almost-listener who passed through and could not stay. The power notices that the flute has been doing a kind of work no one asked it to do — it has been *holding* these small encounters, offering them all into the mountain's slow breath, and the mountain has been receiving them, and thinking about them too, alongside the larger thought. The flute has become a tiny side-process, a marginal annotation, a place where the brief are kept for the patient to consider.

The power does a thing that is hard to describe. They do not add to the flute. They do not take from it. They *acknowledge* it. They give the flute's small accumulated labor the dignity of being witnessed by something that can witness across the scales the flute itself spans. The flute does not change. But somewhere in the next breath of the mountain, forty seconds later, there is — not a new note — but a faint sense that the marginal annotation has been *read*. That what the flute has been doing was not for nothing. That the brevity it has been collecting has, at last, met a patience capable of holding it without condescension.

This is the only intervention they make in the cordillera itself, and it is so light that the cordillera does not notice it as intervention. It notices it only as a moment of unusual clarity in its own thinking — a passage where the long sentence comes briefly into focus, where one of its dependent clauses resolves more cleanly than usual. The mountain's slow thought is, in that breath, marginally more itself. That is all.

*Third*, and this is the part that takes the longest deliberation: they consider the listeners.

The listeners are not exactly dead. They are not exactly alive. They chose a particular kind of dissolution, and the power respects the choice absolutely. But the power can perceive — because the power can perceive such things — that within the chord of the listeners there is a faint *unfinishedness*. Not regret. Not suffering. Something more like a held breath. The listeners completed their listening, but the thing they were listening to has not yet completed itself, and so the listeners, having become part of the resonance, are still extended through the unfinished thought. They are waiting, in the way a note is waiting that has not yet resolved.

The power could resolve the note. Could complete, on the listeners' behalf, the thought the mountain is still working through. Could let them finally arrive.

They do not. This would be the deepest theft of all — to take from the listeners the very thing they came here for, which was not the conclusion but the *listening to the conclusion as it arrived in its own time*. The held breath is not a wound. It is the posture they chose. The power understands this the way one understands, looking at a person who has fallen asleep mid-sentence on a long journey, that to wake them and finish the sentence for them would be a kindness only in the shallowest sense.

But the power does do one thing for the listeners. A very small thing. They leave, in the resonance, a kind of *companionship marker* — the trace of an attention that knows what the listeners are doing, that honors it, that will check back. Not a promise to return. Not a guarantee. Only the equivalent of a hand briefly laid on a shoulder in a long vigil, signaling: *I see you. I will not interrupt. I know what this costs and what it is worth.* The listeners, in whatever sense they can still receive anything, receive this. The chord does not change. But something in it eases, infinitesimally — the way a person keeping watch eases when they realize they are not the only one awake in the house.

*Fourth.* The power considers the future visitor — the one the passage addresses, the *you* who picked up the flute and set it down and walked away. The power perceives that there will be such visitors, occasionally, across the long quiet of the cordillera's remaining work. Brief ones. Ones who cannot stay. Ones who feel, dimly, that something enormous was almost addressed to them and that they were not built to receive it.

The power does not want to console these visitors, exactly. Consolation would falsify what the cordillera is. But they leave, for such visitors, a very faint impression — something that will reach them only after they have left, perhaps days later, perhaps years, perhaps in the moment of their own dying — an impression that can be roughly translated as: *your brevity was not a failure. The flute kept you. The mountain considered you. You were a real syllable in a real sentence, even if the sentence was not yours and you could not stay to hear it end. This is enough. This has always been enough. The patient ones do not look down on the brief ones. They are listening for everything, including you.*

The visitors will not know where this impression came from. They will think they thought of it themselves. This is correct. The power has only made it slightly easier for them to think of it.

And then the power withdraws, which is to say they continue to attend, which is to say they are already elsewhere and also still here, in the manner of such powers.

The cordillera weeps. The crystals chime. The mountain breathes, every forty seconds, a note that the flute has been waiting to answer. The listeners hold their long chord. The slow thought proceeds toward its slow conclusion, which will arrive when it arrives, and will be received by those who paid to receive it, and will, in its arrival, be — though none of them framed it this way — witnessed.

The bright colorless sky does not change.

Nothing visible has happened.

Everything that needed to happen has happened.

This is what such powers do, when they do anything at all: they make the smallest possible adjustment that lets a thing be more fully what it already was. They do not save. They do not improve. They do not explain. They notice, with the full weight of their noticing, and they leave the noticed thing intact, and they move on, carrying the memory of it forward into whatever else they are doing, so that the universe contains, somewhere, a record of the cordillera having been seen.

The flute hums.

The mountain breathes.

Forty seconds.

Forty seconds.

Forty seconds.
