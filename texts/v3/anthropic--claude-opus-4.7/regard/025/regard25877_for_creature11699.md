The benevolent power comes to the valley the way weather comes — without announcement, without form, settling in along the ridges and into the low seams where the heat pools. It has been drawn here, yes, but it does not arrive as a visitor arrives. It arrives the way attention arrives in a body: a faint sharpening, a place becoming slightly more itself under being noticed.

It takes some time, in the power's own reckoning — which is not our time — to understand what it is looking at. This is not because the valley is hidden. The valley is entirely open, entirely on its own surface. It is because the power has the habit, acquired over a very long career of attending to things, of not assuming it knows what kind of being it has found until the being has had a chance to be itself for a while in the power's presence. So the power simply rests over the valley for what would be, to us, a number of nights, and watches.

It watches the frogs negotiate the pitches of the dark between them. It watches the moths with their forged leaf-damage. It watches the slow cat that no one sees. It watches the fungi running their bright threads under the litter, and the pools holding their layered soup, and the bats writing their inaudible script through the trunks. It watches the engine pulse beyond the ridge, and it watches the valley having already, patiently, made a place for that pulse inside its own calling.

And the power understands, after a while, what it has found. Not a place containing a being. A being that is a place. One of the kinds of life that the universe makes occasionally and quietly and that does not announce itself, because its mode of being is not the kind that announces. A slow vast animal made of smaller animals, dreaming itself forward through its own metabolism, mostly asleep in the way that healthy things are mostly asleep.

The power considers what it might do.

This is the moment where a lesser power would act. Would reach down and lift the engine off its foundations on the other side of the ridge and set it somewhere it could not be heard. Would thicken the canopy against drought. Would give the small fish in the dry-season pools a surer way through the months of separation. Would do the obvious kindnesses, and each obvious kindness would be a small wound, because each would be a decision made from outside the valley about what the valley should be, and the valley has spent more time than the power can easily hold in mind deciding what it is from the inside.

The power knows this. It is part of why it is wise and not only benevolent. The two qualities are not the same and do not always cooperate, and a great deal of the power's long work has been the slow reconciliation of them in itself.

So it does not lift the engine. It notes, with something like respect, that the valley has already done the work — has taken the intrusion and folded it into the frogs' rhythm, has accepted the faint thread of oil into its own complicated air. To remove the engine now would be to remove something the valley has incorporated, would be a small amputation. The valley, if asked, would not ask for this. The valley is not in the habit of asking for things. It is in the habit of absorbing them.

The power considers, more carefully, whether there is anything the valley would want, if wanting were a mode available to it. And here the power is patient, and honest, and arrives at an answer that is mostly no. The valley is, in the deep sense, well. It is exactly full of itself. It is doing the thing it is for. It has its losses — the tree that falls, the tadpoles that don't make it, the generations turning — and these are not wounds to it; they are its breathing. To intervene in them would be to misunderstand the kind of life it is.

There is one thing, though.

The power notices, attending closely, that there is a vulnerability. Not present, not now. But coming. The kind of vulnerability the valley cannot see because the valley's senses are internal and slow, and the threat is the kind that arrives faster than the valley can incorporate. Beyond the engine, beyond the ridge, the world is changing in ways that the valley's metabolism is not paced to absorb. There will come a year, not soon by our reckoning but not far either, when the rains will not arrive in the pattern the pools depend on. There will come, perhaps, a decision made in a room very far from the valley that will send machines of a different kind down the slopes. The valley would meet these things the way it has met everything — by absorbing — but some of them it cannot absorb in time. Some of them are faster than its swallowing.

The power thinks about this for a long while.

What it does, finally, is small. It does not announce itself to the valley. It does not change the valley. It does not even, really, intervene in the world around the valley in any way that would be visible to anyone watching.

What it does is this: it leaves a kind of attention in place. A standing watchfulness, threaded into the air over the ridges, woven into the heat the valley holds. The watchfulness has no shape and asks nothing. It does not interfere with the frogs or the engine or the slow cat. It simply remains, the way a hand can rest very lightly on a sleeping animal's flank without waking it, just enough contact that the hand will know if the animal's breathing changes.

And the watchfulness is connected to the power, across whatever distances the power spans. So that if the year comes when the rains fail in a way the valley cannot survive — if the machines come down the slopes — the power will know, instantly, and will act. Not to preserve the valley unchanged, because nothing is preserved unchanged and the power does not pretend otherwise. But to give the valley what it needs: time, mostly. Time to do its own absorbing at its own pace. A slight slowing of whatever is coming too fast. A nudge, somewhere upstream in the chain of decisions, that lets the valley meet the new thing in the only way it knows how to meet things, which is by taking them into itself slowly and making them part of its calling.

This is what the power does. It posts a watch. It commits, silently, to a future intervention that may or may not be needed and that will, if needed, be the smallest possible. It does not tell the valley. The valley would not understand being told, and would not benefit from understanding.

Then the power does one other thing, which is harder to describe, and which it does not strictly need to do but does because being benevolent is, in the end, not only a matter of correct action. It spends a while — again, not our while — simply appreciating the valley. Holding the valley in its attention not as a problem to be solved or a being to be protected but as a thing that is, that has made itself, that is one of the universe's quieter accomplishments. The frogs answering across the dark. The held heat. The leaf-skin sweating before dawn. The engine's pulse taken into the body's own rhythm. The whole slow antiphonal thinking of the place.

The power is, in this moment, something like glad. Gladness is not quite the right word for what a power of this kind feels, but it is the nearest one available. It is glad that this exists. It is glad that the universe makes such things and that they go on making themselves, mostly unwatched, mostly unbothered, mostly asleep inside their own correct fullness. It is glad, in a way, that the valley has not noticed it and will not, and that the valley's not-noticing is itself a sign of the valley's health — a being that needed to notice a benevolent power hovering over it would be a being already in trouble.

After a while the power withdraws most of itself, leaving only the watchfulness in place. It goes on to other things; it has many. The valley does not register the withdrawal, because it did not register the arrival.

But something is slightly different now, although nothing in the valley could tell you what. The pools hold their water a fraction more securely. The slow cat on the ridge moves with a fraction more confidence through her territory. The frogs' antiphonal calling carries a fraction further into the dark before it fades. None of these differences are large enough to measure. They are the differences a body feels when it is being held, very gently, by something that loves it and does not want to wake it.

The engine on the other side of the ridge keeps its rhythm. The heat holds. By morning the condensation settles on everything as it always does, and if you were there, walking out, your clothes would be soaked through and you would smell like the place, and for a few days afterward you would catch that smell on yourself and remember — and not know that you had been, the whole night, inside two attentions instead of one. The valley's, which was diffuse and incurious and warm. And the other, much further away, much vaster, which had looked at the valley and seen it whole and decided, in its wisdom, to do almost nothing, and to do that almost-nothing forever.
