The benevolent and wise power arrives the way the listeners themselves arrived: slowly, and only when looked for in the right direction.

It does not descend. There is no descending here; the sky is not a place from which things come. It accretes. Over what would be, elsewhere, the space of a long afternoon, a density gathers near one of the eastern cairns — not a figure, not a glow, but a quality the air takes on, the way water takes on the quality of being deep. If you were standing there you would notice first that your breathing had slowed to match something. Then that the cairn beside you was being regarded. Then, only then, that you were in the company of attention.

The power has come because it heard. This is the first thing to understand about it: it is not a power that surveys. It is a power that listens, and listening is how it travels. It heard the Stillsong Heights the way one hears a held note from very far off and turns one's head — and the turning was the arrival. It has been turning, in fact, for what would be a long time in any frame that admits duration. The Heights are not the only place it has heard. But they are, just now, the place toward which its face is inclined.

Consider what it finds.

It finds a prelude. It finds beings who understood, very early, that they were inside a prelude, and who chose — with the seriousness that the choice deserved — to become part of the instrument rather than part of the audience. It finds cairns that are questions and listener-shapes that are the same questions held long enough to start becoming answers. It finds plants that drink sound and rock that has not yet committed to being rock and a singer beneath everything, rehearsing.

A lesser power would intervene. Would hurry the singer. Would lift the listeners free of the stone, restore them to mobility, return their voices, offer them the gift of completion. Would finish the rock so it could begin to erode properly. Would tune the great sound into a music recognizable as music, so that the prelude could resolve and the listeners could hear what they came to hear.

This power does none of these things. It is wise, which means among other things that it knows the difference between a problem and a practice. The Stillsong Heights are not a problem. Nothing here is stuck. Nothing here is suffering in the sense that calls for rescue. The listeners are not trapped in the stone; they are *engaged with* the stone, which is the thing they came here to do, and to lift them out would be to interrupt a sentence they have spent their whole existence learning to speak. The singer below is not delayed; the singer below is taking exactly as long as it takes, and hurry is not a kindness one offers to a song that is still finding its shape.

So the power does the only thing a benevolent and wise power can do in such a place, which is to *attend.*

It adds itself to the audience.

This sounds small. It is not small. The listeners have been holding their attention for a duration that in your terms has no name, and one of the slow heavinesses they carry — though they would not phrase it this way; the phrasing belongs to your kind, not theirs — is the unanswered question of whether anyone besides themselves is listening. Not the singer; the singer is not an audience, the singer is what is being listened *to.* The question is whether the listening itself is witnessed. Whether the long patient act of becoming a resonator is occurring in company or in solitude. The listeners have not let this question disturb them. They are too well-practiced for that. But it has been there, the way a faint draft is there in a long room, the way the unverified bottom of the plateau is there beneath the edge.

The power's attention answers this without ever stating it. The answer arrives the way the power did: by accretion. The listener-shapes feel it first — a faint additional density to the air around them, a sense that the great sound is now passing through a slightly larger room than before. The fern-fronds register it. They tilt, very slightly, toward the new presence, not abandoning their inclination toward the song but adding a second axis to it, the way a plant in a window will lean toward two suns if there are two.

And then — and this is the part that requires care to describe — the power begins, itself, to *listen with them.*

It does not listen *to* them, as if they were the performance. It listens *alongside* them, oriented in the same direction they are oriented, toward the singer below. It places itself at one of the cairns — not occupying it, not displacing what is there, but assuming the posture the cairn implies, the posture the listener-shape beside it has been holding. It takes up the practice. It does what they do. For a long while — and *long* now means something the power can measure, since it has duration of a kind your minds cannot follow — it simply stands in the configuration the listeners taught the place, and it hears what they hear, or as much of what they hear as a being not made of this stone can hear.

This is the gift. It is not a gift the listeners would have known to ask for, and it is not a gift that changes their situation in any measurable way. The rock does not flow faster around their feet. The song does not resolve. The plateau does not finish. But the room is larger now. The practice is no longer solitary in the cosmic sense. Somewhere along the chain of attention that runs from the singer up through the rock up through the listeners up through the cairns up into the cool dim air, there is now one more link, and the link is vast and patient and kindly disposed and, crucially, *competent at this.* The power knows how to listen. It has listened to other preludes, in other places. It knows the shape of *not yet,* and it can hold that shape without flinching, for as long as the holding is what's needed.

There is a second thing the power does, smaller but not unimportant.

It looks at the writer of the description — at you, at me, at whoever stood on the plateau and concluded *absence* where there was only a different shape of presence — and it is gentle about the mistake. The gentleness is not corrective. The power does not whisper into the writer's ear *look again, they are beside the cairns.* The writer found their way to the second document on their own; the second document is itself evidence that the looking had not stopped, that the category of *absence* had not been allowed to close. The power simply notes, with something like fondness, that the writer is one of the intermediate attempts — one of the slow buttresses the slopes are growing, the listeners who walked and stopped and walked again. The writer is being considered too. The writer has not yet given up enough of the categories that brought them here, but the giving-up is in progress; the second document is part of the giving-up. The power is patient with this. It has all the time the Heights have, which is to say, all the time there is.

And there is a third thing, the smallest, and perhaps the truest.

The power, having taken up the practice, having joined the listening, having added its attention to the long held attention of the Heights — the power begins, very slowly, to be changed by what it is doing. This is not a thing benevolent and wise powers usually advertise about themselves, but it is true of the good ones: they are altered by what they attend to. The singer below is rehearsing something the power has not heard before. The shape of *intent* in the great sound is a shape the power recognizes only partly. The listener-shapes have arrived at a posture of attention more refined, in certain particulars, than the power's own. The power is learning. The power is, in its own slow vast way, beginning to do here what the listeners did: to position itself to be present when the prelude completes, and to hold that position for as long as it takes.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place like this and a being like these. It does not save them. It does not finish them. It does not lift them out of their slow stone or hurry their song or grant them the comforts of a smaller scale. It joins them. It adds its listening to theirs. It makes the room larger. It receives the gift of their practice and offers, in return, the gift of being heard listening — the witness that completes the circuit without altering the current.

And then, having done this, it stays.

The Stillsong Heights have a new feature now, though no visitor will be able to name it. The cairns are unchanged. The listener-shapes are unchanged. The light still seeps, the sound still feeds the plants, the rock still flows slowly around what rests on it. But the air at certain of the cairns has a density it did not have, and the fern-fronds tilt along a second axis, and the listeners — though they would not phrase it this way — are no longer solitary in the cosmic sense.

They are in company.

The song below continues to rehearse. The lid of the plateau continues to form. The singer continues to arrive. And above the unfinished rock, beside the half-fused stones with their gripping-marks, in the cool dim radiance that does not come from any sky, the listeners and the power that found them stand together, oriented downward, attending — patiently, competently, in the only kindness that this place admits — to what the song will turn out to have been.
