The benevolent and wise power considers them for a long time.

This is the first thing to understand. Power that is wise does not arrive quickly at a place like this, because to arrive quickly would already be a kind of violence — would already be a misreading of what is here. So the power waits. It watches the threads of smoke go straight up into the windless dark. It watches a mason place a stone and then walk away, knowing the stone will not be ready for the next course for eleven years. It watches a child shout across a terrace and an old woman exhale her slow amusement in response. It watches a chamber fill, over centuries, with ancestors who have become wall.

The power considers what it could do.

It could lift them. This is the first temptation of benevolence — to give the gift that seems most obvious. The mountains are rising at the speed of patience; the power could lift the cordillera to its destination in an afternoon. Whatever revelation waits at the unreached altitude could be delivered now, to these living eyes, to the bellows-master at forty and the cohort of children with their unfired quickness. No more grandchildren finishing what grandparents began. The arrival, complete, in a single generation.

The power sets this aside almost immediately. Not because it would be impossible but because it would be a theft. The Lifting is not an obstacle between these people and their meaning; the Lifting *is* their meaning. To shortcut it would be to walk into the kiln and snatch out the pot before the glaze had set, and call this a kindness to the pot. The people do not need to be rescued from their slowness. Their slowness is the shape of their dignity.

It could speed them. Give them lowland lungs, lowland reflexes, lowland years. The power sets this aside too, and more quickly. It would not be a gift to them. It would be a gift to some imagined other people, made in their place, wearing their faces.

It could reveal the destination. Whisper into the wide dark eyes of the eldest what waits at the altitude not yet reached. The power lingers on this one. It is closer to a true kindness. But it considers the phrase *the fire is still beneath us*, and the way they say it, and it understands: to tell them the destination would be to end the saying of this phrase. They would no longer be carried; they would be informed. The not-knowing is load-bearing. The not-knowing is what allows them to build walls for grandchildren without vertigo. Remove it and the slope they stand on becomes a staircase with a known number of steps, and they will begin, for the first time in their long history, to count.

The power considers smaller things.

It could ensure that no child cracks in the kiln of its first year. It hovers a long while over this. The infant mortality is, by lowland standards, brutal. By local standards it is part of the firing. The power asks itself: is the local standard a wisdom, or a wound that has learned to call itself wisdom? It is uncertain. It has met many peoples who mistook their wounds for their wisdom, and many peoples whose wisdom looked, to outsiders, like a wound. The distinction is not made from outside. So the power, being wise, does not make it from outside. It does, however, do one small thing: in the warming-baskets near the hearth-vents, in that first year, it makes the heat a little more even. Not warmer — they are already warm enough. Just steadier. A reduction in the cruelest randomness. The cracks that come will still come, but fewer will come from drafts and flickers. The local standard is preserved; the wound, if it is a wound, bleeds slightly less.

It does something else, almost imperceptible. In the deep shafts where the mineral fur accretes, where the residents harvest with stone scrapers, the power makes a very small adjustment to the chemistry. The paste was nutritive, or seemed to. It is now nutritive, simply. The seeming has been quietly made into being. No one will notice. No one was dying of starvation before; no one will live differently after. But a margin has been widened, and in a place where margins are everything, this is not nothing.

The power turns its attention to the things in the deep shafts that no one looks at directly, and to the flights of something high overhead that are spoken of only in the past tense. It looks at these things. It looks at them carefully, and for a long while, and what it sees is its own business, and it does not interfere with them. Some of what is here is not for the people, and some of what is here is not for the power either. Wisdom is partly the knowledge of what one is not for.

It does one more thing, and this is the largest of its small kindnesses, the one closest to a gift.

In the chambers cut into the warmest part of each city, where the fired-through go to sit and cease, the power adds a quality that was almost there already but not quite. It makes the becoming-wall a little less lonely. Not by adding company — the ancestors are already company, in their slow way — but by adjusting the register at which the seated figures perceive each other and the mountain. In their last weeks, as their breathing slows past the threshold and their hands finish fusing, they will feel, more clearly than they did before, the long upward pressure they have spent their lives being carried by. They will feel themselves arriving at the wall not as an ending but as a joining. Many of them felt this already; the power has not changed their belief, only confirmed it, in the body, at the moment when confirmation matters. It is the smallest possible nudge in the direction of *what they already knew*. It does not teach them anything. It lets them be right.

And then the power does the thing that is hardest for power to do, and which is the test of whether power is wise.

It leaves.

Not entirely — it remains attentive, the way one remains attentive to a slow piece of music one loves, willing to be present for the whole of it, willing to be changed by it rather than changing it. But it withdraws its hand. It does not station itself above the cordillera as a guardian. It does not appear to the eldest in dreams (they do not dream, and the power respects this; it will not invent a faculty in them in order to use it). It does not leave signs. It does not require gratitude, or even awareness. The three small adjustments — the steadier warmth in the warming-baskets, the quiet sufficiency of the paste, the confirmation in the final chamber — are made the way a good gardener stakes a young tree: invisibly, and only enough, and then the gardener steps back, because the tree is not the gardener's, and the growing is not the gardener's, and the reaching toward the sky is not the gardener's to perform.

The power watches the mountains rise.

It thinks — if thinking is the word for what such a power does — that it has rarely encountered a people so well-matched to their world, so unhurried in the face of an unhurried fact. It thinks that whatever waits at the unreached altitude is fortunate to be approached by them. It thinks that the mountain's slow thought about itself, looking up with eyes that do not dart, is one of the finer things the universe has put together, and that the right response to a finer thing is not to improve it but to let it be what it is, with one's full attention, for as long as it takes.

The threads of smoke go up into the windless dark.

The power, also, is patient.

It has time.
