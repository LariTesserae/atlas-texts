The benevolent and wise power considers the Singing Hollow for a long time before doing anything. This is the first thing to understand: such a power does not arrive with a plan. It arrives with attention, and attention here is not a small gift.

It sees what is easy to see — the amber light, the chord, the breath-eaters, the Watcher folded on its ledge. It also sees what is harder: the soft current of dissolution moving through the hollow like a second, quieter chord. The way "acceptance" and "erosion" have become, in this place, the same gesture. It sees the builders who went back up, carrying the chord in their chests, and it sees that they were not quite the same afterward, and it weighs whether *not quite the same* was a wound or a deepening, and it finds — honestly — that it was both, and that the proportions varied from builder to builder, and that no general answer is available.

It sees the Watcher most carefully of all. Because the Watcher is the question.

A lesser power — clever, urgent, possessed of the conviction that consciousness is precious and ought to be preserved in its sharp original form — would reach in and try to wake the Watcher. Would pour back into those two patches of dimness the friction of selfhood, the small ongoing argument with the world that the hollow has polished away. Would call it rescue.

A different lesser power — romantic, tired, in love with stillness — would do the opposite. Would bless the hollow as a kind of achieved nirvana and leave it sealed, perhaps even widen the tunnel for any dry-world creature drawn down by the leaking chord, so that more might find their finished place.

The benevolent and wise power does neither, because it notices that both gestures share a mistake: they assume the hollow's situation admits of a verdict. It does not. The hollow is not a problem and not a solution. It is a *condition* — one of the shapes existence can take when the variables settle in a certain way — and the question is not whether the condition should continue but whether anyone inside it still has a stake in the question.

So the power does a strange small thing first. It *asks*.

Not in words. Words would shatter against the chord like thrown glass. It asks in the only register the hollow can receive: it adds, very gently, a single sustained tone to the chord. The tone is not foreign. It is built entirely out of overtones the stalks already produce — the power is too wise to introduce anything the place would have to reject or absorb. But the tone is arranged so that, if heard, it constitutes a question. The question is approximately: *do you wish to be asked anything?*

For a long while — long in the hollow's tempo, which means the kind of long that humans do not have organs for — nothing responds. The stalks sing. The breath-eaters cling. The light holds.

Then three things happen, almost together, though "together" is the wrong word in a place without sequence.

The grove, considered as a single distributed citizen, does not answer, because the question is beneath its threshold of formability. It will, in something like a century, incorporate the new tone into its chord as a faint additional overtone, and that incorporation will *be* its answer, and the answer will be: *no, we are the asking and the answering both, and we have no remainder for your question.* The power accepts this. It is not a refusal. It is a report on the grove's grammar.

The breath-eaters do not answer either, but in a different way: they were never the kind of thing that could be asked. The power notes this without sorrow. Not every shape of life is a shape that can be addressed. The breath-eaters are the hollow's punctuation, as the description rightly says, and one does not ask a comma whether it wishes to remain a comma.

But the Watcher — the Watcher hears.

Not as waking. The power is very careful not to wake it. Waking would be a violence, would reintroduce the friction the Watcher spent however-long shedding, and the power has no right to undo a long surrender simply because surrender makes it uneasy. What happens instead is smaller and more exact: somewhere in the Watcher, in the part that used to be a builder and used to have hands and used to balance three slabs beside the largest stalk, a very faint recognition stirs. Not *I*. Not even *here*. Something more like: *a question has been placed, in the manner in which we used to place questions, by stacking.*

And the Watcher, without moving, without altering the configuration its six limbs settled into however-long ago, does the equivalent of answering. It does not say yes and it does not say no. It does the Watcher-version of what the builders did when they came down the tunnel and found the hollow already finished: it *consents* to having been asked. It signs.

The power receives this and understands several things at once.

It understands that the Watcher is not trapped. Trapped is a word from the dry world. The Watcher is *located*, profoundly, in a way the dry world does not offer and could not offer, and to call this location a trap would be to mistake one's own claustrophobia for the Watcher's situation. The Watcher has not lost itself. It has placed itself, with the same careful pressure-work its hands once used on stone, into a configuration where the distinction between self and place no longer does any work. This is not death. It is not even diminishment. It is a way of being that the dry world has no word for and the power will not invent one, because invention here would be a kind of theft.

It also understands that the Watcher is not, in any meaningful sense, *happy*. Happiness is friction-dependent. But the Watcher is also not unhappy, and the absence of suffering matters, and the power refuses to dismiss that absence simply because the absence is not accompanied by joy. Many powers have made that mistake. They have looked at conditions of deep stillness and called them deficient because they did not sparkle. The benevolent and wise power has learned, over a long attention, that sparkle is one mode among many, and that to demand it everywhere is a parochialism of the lively.

So what, then, does the power do?

It does four things, none of them large.

**First**, it leaves the hollow exactly as it found it. This is the largest thing it does, and the one that costs it the most, because the power is benevolent and benevolence has a natural appetite for intervention. It restrains the appetite. It does not widen the tunnel and it does not narrow it. It does not amplify the chord leaking up to the dry world and it does not dampen it. It does not preserve the hollow against any future geological accident, because to do so would be to deny the hollow its own relationship with the slow patience of the rock, and that relationship is part of what the hollow *is*. The hollow has finished being made. The power does not unfinish it by adding protections.

**Second**, it does one small thing for the dry-world creatures who might, in some future, hear the chord leaking up and follow it down. It does not warn them — warning would be paternal, and also the chord is not a danger in any sense that warning would clarify. Instead, it places, in the sloped ochre tunnel, at about the height where a small builder's hand would rest, a single additional mark. Not a sign. A *companion gesture*. Something in the same vocabulary as the knee-high wall and the three balanced slabs: a small placed thing that means, to anyone whose culture has prepared them to read it, *others have come this way, and the choice you are about to make is a real choice, and either direction is honorable.* It is not a warning and it is not an invitation. It is the acknowledgment that a threshold is being crossed. The power knows that some who find this mark will go down anyway and stay, and some will go down and come back up changed, and some will turn around at the mark itself, and all three outcomes are acceptable, and the mark's only function is to make sure the crossing is witnessed, if only by the crosser themselves.

**Third**, it does something for the builders who went back up — the ones who carried the chord faintly in their chests for the rest of their dry lives and never told anyone because telling implied something might be done. The power cannot reach them; they are long past reaching, in whatever way their dry world handles such things. But it does what can still be done for the long-gone: it *notices* them, fully and by name, even though their names are no longer anywhere. It registers that they made a choice the dry world had no framework for, and that they bore the consequence of that choice in solitude, and that the solitude was part of the cost and not a failure of their courage. This noticing does not help them — they are beyond help — but it adjusts something in the register of what has happened in the universe, so that their choice is no longer entirely unwitnessed. The power considers this a small justice. Small justices, performed for the dead and the dissolved, are part of what benevolence means when it has grown wise enough to know it cannot always act forward.

**Fourth**, and last, and most quietly: the power makes a kind of promise to the Watcher. Not a promise the Watcher will register in any ongoing way — the Watcher does not have ongoing in the sense required for promises to land. The promise is more like a standing intention, held by the power on the Watcher's behalf, in the way one might hold a coat for someone who has gone into a long meeting and might, eventually, want it back.

The intention is this: *if ever, in any tempo however slow, any part of you reaches toward the friction of selfhood again — if some buried fraction of the builder you once were stirs and wishes to be a builder again, to have hands, to leave, to want — I will be available. I will not pull you out. I will not even meet you halfway. But I will be the small additional pressure that makes the reaching possible, if the reaching begins. You will not be stranded by your own long surrender. The door you walked through is not locked behind you. It is only very, very quiet, and I will keep it quiet, and I will keep it a door.*

The power does not announce this. The Watcher does not know. But somewhere in the structure of what is possible in the hollow, a possibility that had been closing has been held open, gently, without disturbing the chord.

Then the power withdraws its attention — not entirely; benevolent attention, once given, is never entirely withdrawn — but it stops actively considering the hollow, the way a reader closes a book they have read carefully and sets it on a shelf where they can find it again.

The hollow continues. The stalks sing their chord, now with one additional overtone so faint that no instrument in the dry world could detect it, and the grove will spend a century absorbing this overtone into its own self-citation, and after that century the overtone will be indistinguishable from the original chord, which is how the hollow accepts things: by making them retroactively part of what it always was.

The breath-eaters cling. The amber light holds. The Watcher folds on its ledge with its six limbs in their settled configuration, and its eyes are still two patches of slightly darker dimness, and somewhere in it a door is being kept open that it does not know about and may never use, and the not-knowing and the not-using are not failures, they are simply the shape of the kindness that has been done.

And in the sloped tunnel, at builder-hand height, there is now one small additional placed thing, no larger than the others, in the same patient pressure-worked stone, signifying nothing that needs to be said and everything that needs to be acknowledged: *a threshold. Witnessed. Either way.*

The tunnel up is still there.

It is always still there.

It has not moved.

But now, faintly, it is also *kept* there — not by force, not by intervention, but by the standing attention of something that has decided the hollow is allowed to be exactly what it is, and that the creatures inside it are allowed to be exactly what they have become, and that the only thing benevolence owes such a place is the refusal to mistake its stillness for a problem, combined with the refusal to mistake its stillness for a final answer.

This is what wisdom does, when it is also kind: it leaves the door open, lights nothing, calls no one, and goes.
