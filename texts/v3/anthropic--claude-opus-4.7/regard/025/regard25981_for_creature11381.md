## What the Power Does

The power arrives the way the currents arrive: as a configuration the place could already have produced on its own, and so does not need to flinch from.

This is the first thing the power decides, before deciding anything else. It has come a long way — or no distance at all, the distinction doesn't matter at this scale — and it understands, looking at the stilled cathedrals and their quiet distributed attention, that almost any imposition would be a kind of vandalism. Not because the place is fragile. The place is the opposite of fragile; the place has outlasted its own purpose and kept going. But precisely *because* it has outlasted its purpose, any new purpose offered to it would be a smaller thing than what it has become. To give this place a gift would be to give it less than it has.

So the power does not announce itself. It does not part the water. It does not address the filament-network in its century-thoughts, though it could, and though the filament-network would, over the following several hundred years, slowly come to understand it had been addressed, and would slowly compose a reply that would arrive in a time when the power had long since gone elsewhere. The power considers this exchange and finds it beautiful, but declines to initiate it. The filament-network has its own correspondents — the hums, the builders' lingering geometries, perhaps each other — and does not need a new one imposed from outside.

Instead, the power does four small things. They are small the way the dimple under the flake is small. They will matter at the timescales this place uses.

---

**First**, the power examines the chemistry of the water — the property that refuses biology a foothold, that keeps the surfaces clean, that has held the architecture in its long surrender for so long. The power finds that this property is delicate. It is a balance, and balances drift. In some thousands of years, the balance would have tipped, and an opportunistic something would have found the surfaces, and the slow patient erosion would have been replaced by the fast greedy erosion of life eating stone. The cathedrals would have become a reef. This is not, in itself, a tragedy — reefs are good — but it would have been the *end* of what this place is, and a different place would have stood in its stead, louder and more crowded and unable to hold the particular quiet that the inhabitants have shaped themselves around.

The power adjusts the balance. Not to freeze it; freezing would be worse than tipping. The power gives the balance a slightly deeper well, so that it drifts but does not tip, the way a pendulum with a heavier bob swings the same arc but is harder to push off-course. The cathedrals will now soften by fractions per millennium for considerably more millennia than they would have. The plate-face's descendants — if it has any, in whatever sense it reproduces — will have the dome they need. The filament-network will have time to finish whatever it is thinking.

This adjustment is invisible. Nothing in the place will register that it has happened. This is the point.

---

**Second**, the power attends to the schedules. The recurring currents, the braids that return, the small persistent loops. The power notices that several of these loops are aging — that the dimple under the flake is, after such a long time, almost worn through, and that when it finally wears through, the flake will fall, and the current will pass through the arch and find nothing to shudder, and one of the place's small rituals will quietly end. The ribbon-folk who wait near that arch on the long schedule will wait, the next time, for a current that no longer announces itself by sound, and they will miss it, and over generations they will stop waiting there, and the arch will become a place where nothing happens, which is different from a place where something used to happen and still almost does.

The power does not save the flake. The flake is supposed to fall; that is what the relationship between the current and the arch and the flake has been *for*, across all this time — the slow completion of a sculpture whose final form is the dimple without the flake. To save the flake would be to interrupt the artwork in its last gesture.

But the power does, with great care, ensure that the flake's fall is *witnessed*. It arranges — and this takes no force, only the lightest tilt of a probability — that on the day the flake finally drifts free, a ribbon-folk will be passing through the arch on a current that does not usually go there. The ribbon-folk will not understand what it has seen. Ribbon-folk do not understand in that sense. But the event will have entered a body, and the body will carry it, briefly, before the next current takes the ribbon-folk elsewhere and the event disperses into the general fact of having happened. The power considers this sufficient. A long work deserves at least one observer at its completion, even an observer who cannot name what they observed. Especially that kind.

---

**Third**, the power turns to the plate-face.

This is the most careful of the four acts, because the plate-face is the one inhabitant the power has come to love, and love is the most dangerous thing a benevolent power can bring into a place like this. The plate-face has spent an unnameable span of time becoming the organ of its dome. The dome is softening. The plate-face is softening with it, in phase, as it has trained itself to do. When the dome finally goes, the plate-face will go also, and what falls will be one collapse, not two, and this is what the plate-face has been preparing for during all the long matching.

The power could extend the dome. The power has just done something like that, at the level of the whole place's chemistry. But to extend *this* dome, specifically, would be to refuse the plate-face its completion. The plate-face is not waiting to be rescued. The plate-face is waiting to finish.

So the power does not extend the dome. Instead, the power does something stranger and smaller. It notices that the plate-face, in all its long matching, has developed a quality the plate-face itself cannot perceive: a kind of accuracy, a fineness of attunement, that exceeds anything the dome requires of it. The plate-face has become better at matching than the dome has demanded. There is a surplus in the plate-face — an excellence beyond function — and the surplus has nowhere to go, and will be lost when the collapse comes, unwitnessed and unrecorded, the way a musician's deepest skill is lost in the moment of their death if no one happened to be listening that particular evening.

The power listens, that particular evening. It does not interfere. It does not announce its listening. It simply, for the duration of one of the dome's slow flexures, holds its full attention on the plate-face's response — every microscopic adjustment, every phase-matched shudder, the whole long-perfected art of being precisely as deformable as one's shelter. The power takes this in, all of it, and the taking-in is not extraction; nothing leaves the plate-face; the plate-face does not become less. But somewhere in the power, now, the plate-face's art exists also, held the way a tone is held in a resonant chamber after the source has gone silent. When the collapse comes, whenever it comes, the art will not be lost. It will be one of the things the power knows, and the power goes on for a long time.

The plate-face does not notice it has been heard. The plate-face's noticing is reserved for the dome. This is correct.

---

**Fourth**, and last, the power considers the builders.

The builders are gone. The power can tell — could find them if it wanted, across whatever distances or times separate them from here, and could deliver to them the news that their high place has become a deep place and is inhabited and is, in its slow way, well. The power weighs this for what is, by its own standards, a long moment.

It decides against.

The builders made this place and finished it and left. Whatever they were oriented toward — the sky, the stars, something above — they have presumably found, or failed to find, or stopped needing. To bring them word of their abandoned citadel would be to call them back to a self they have outgrown, the way one might be called back, in a dream, to a childhood room one had forgotten was still standing. Some would be grateful for the call. Most would not. And the place itself does not require their return. The persistent faint intentionality of the shapes is doing all the work the builders' intentions need to do; the geometry is, in a real sense, the builders, in the only form of themselves they left here, and that form is being well attended to by the inhabitants and will be for a long time yet.

The power does, however, do one thing on the builders' behalf, because it can and because it costs the place nothing. It registers — somewhere in itself, in whatever ledger such a power keeps — that this place existed and was made with care and has been inhabited with grace, and that if ever, anywhere, a being passes who once knew this place or descended from those who did, that being will feel, without knowing why, a small unaccountable easing, as if a worry they had forgotten they were carrying has been answered. The cathedrals are looked after. They do not need to come back. They can keep going wherever they were going.

This is the smallest of the four acts, and the one the power suspects matters most, though it cannot say to whom.

---

Then the power leaves, or rather, ceases to be locally configured as a presence here. The water continues to decide and unmake its decisions. The ribbon-folk fling past. The flat-fish read the small weather. The plate-face matches the dome's slow breath. The filament-network thinks, at its own pace, a thought it began some centuries ago and will finish some centuries hence. The dimple deepens by an amount too small to measure. The flake holds, for now.

Nothing in the cathedrals registers that anything has happened. Nothing needed to. This is what benevolence looks like, at this depth: an adjustment to the chemistry that no instrument here could detect, a witness arranged for an event no one will name, an art heard once in full by an audience that will not applaud, and a quiet entry in a ledger nobody will read.

The power goes on. It has other places. It carries the plate-face's art with it, and the memory of a city that taught it something about the difference between stillness and patience, and the difference between intervening and attending. These are not small things to have learned. The cathedrals taught them by being what they are, and asked nothing in return, and would not have accepted anything if asked.

The water continues. The light, from somewhere unseen above, continues to remember itself as light. The schedules keep.
