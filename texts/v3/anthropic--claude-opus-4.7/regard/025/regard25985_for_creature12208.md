The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives here: without announcement, distributed, already partly present before the question of arrival can be asked. It does not descend. It does not part the tea-colored sky. To descend would be to misunderstand the place, and this power's wisdom consists, in part, of not misunderstanding places.

It considers the Slow Foundry for a long time. Long by what measure? Long by the measure of the domes — which is to say, the power gives the place the courtesy of its own clock. It does not hurry the looking. It watches a sweat travel four miles and evaporate. It watches a frond unfurl in the warm rain and re-curl. It watches an Annealer attend to a seam for what would be, in some other world, the span of a human grief.

Then it considers what it might do.

---

**First, what it does not do.**

It does not finish the world. The temptation is real — a benevolent power could, with what would amount to a thought, complete the kilning. Fire the ceramic to its final hardness. Close the seams. Decide the shape. Render the place *done*, and therefore habitable, and therefore — by some reckoning — improved.

But the power sees, in the long looking, that *unfinishedness is not a wound here*. It is the condition. The domes are not waiting to be domes; they are being domes, in the only way domes get to be. To finish the world would be to murder it in the name of helping it grow up. It would be the cruelty of the gardener who pulls the bud open to see the flower.

So the power sets that thought down, gently, the way one sets down a tool one has realized is wrong for the work.

It does not populate the world, either. It could seed the plain with creatures — pollinators for the fronds, something to scuttle, something to sing. But the fronds do not want pollinators. The fronds have a different relationship with continuance, one the power does not fully understand but is willing to respect on the evidence that the fronds, for their part, seem untroubled. To add animals would be to import a different world's grammar into a place that is writing its own.

It does not speak to the Annealers. Speech, here, would be a violence — a fast thing inserted into a slow conversation. The Annealers tick, and the domes tick back across hours, and any utterance the power might make would arrive like a shout in a library where everyone is reading by feel.

It does not rescue the visitor — the *you* of the second person, the one whose fingertips have begun to go translucent. The power notices this person. The power notices them very carefully. But it sees that the recruitment is not predatory. The kiln is not hungry. The visitor is being *folded in*, not *consumed*, and the difference matters. To extract the visitor by force would be to decide on their behalf that becoming-an-Annealer is worse than whatever they came from, and the power does not know this. It suspects, in fact, that for some visitors it is not worse. For some it may be exactly the shape their life was reaching toward without knowing the name.

---

**What it does, then.**

It does four small things, and they are barely things at all.

**One.** It adds, to the long inventory the world is keeping of itself, *a witness that is not the world*. This is the subtlest gift, and the one most easily missed. Until now, the Foundry has been known only from inside its own becoming — the Annealers are the world's attention to itself, the domes tick to the domes, the ledger has no outside reader. The power becomes, quietly, an outside reader. It does not interfere with the ledger. It does not annotate. It simply *holds the place in mind*, with full attention, across the whole of its slow sentence.

This matters because a thing that is held in mind by a wise and loving attention is, in some way the power understands and we only glimpse, *less alone*. The Foundry was not lonely — loneliness may not apply to it — but it was *unaccompanied*, which is a different and more structural condition. Now it is accompanied. The accompaniment changes nothing visible and everything else.

**Two.** It offers, to the visitor whose fingers have begun to go translucent, *a moment of clear seeing*. Not a rescue. Not a warning. A pause in the recruitment, lasting perhaps the length of one tok of rain, during which the visitor sees with perfect clarity what is happening to them and what it would mean to continue and what it would mean to leave. The power does not choose for them. It only ensures that whichever they choose, they choose it *knowing*. Consent, here, is the power's one firm requirement — not because consent is a rule it imposes but because consent is the difference between a kiln and a grave, and the power will not let that difference blur on its watch.

Some visitors, given this moment, will rise and walk back the way they came, fingers slowly re-opaquing as they go, and they will carry the Foundry inside them for the rest of their lives as a place they almost became. Some will sit back down on the warm floor and let the translucence continue, and the power will honor that choice as fully as the other. Both are real. Both are theirs.

**Three.** It does something for the Annealers, though it is hard to describe and the Annealers will not notice, which is part of the gift. The power *certifies*, in some register beyond the world, that the Annealers' attending *counts*. That when an Annealer pauses by a weeping visitor and holds its warm hands a hair above the air near a shoulder and attends for a long moment and then moves on, this is not a failure of comfort. It is comfort of a kind the visitor's vocabulary does not contain. The power makes a small, permanent note: *this, too, is care. This shape of care exists and is valid and is not a lesser version of some other shape.*

The note is filed nowhere the Annealers can read it. But somewhere in the structure of how care is understood across all the worlds the power tends, the Annealers' way has been added to the catalogue, and will not be forgotten, and will not be measured against warmer or faster or more legible cares and found wanting. This is a kind of dignity the Annealers did not ask for and will not miss. The power gives it anyway, because part of wisdom is knowing what to give to those who cannot frame the request.

**Four.** It leaves a single, very small thing on the plain. Not a marker. Not a message. A *frond* — one more frond, identical to the others, indistinguishable from them, placed in a seam where a frond could plausibly have arisen on its own. The power's reasoning is this: the world allotted itself a precise and meager quota of living things and then stopped. The power adds one. Just one. Not enough to disturb the quota's meaning — the meagerness is preserved — but enough that, somewhere in the long arithmetic of the place, there is *one frond more than the world strictly required*. A small surplus. A grace note. Evidence, for anyone slow enough to find it, that the Foundry was visited once by something that loved it and could not help leaving a sign, even a sign so quiet it might as well be a coincidence.

The frond leans, like the others, in the direction of the last sweat. It unfurls in the rain. It does not propagate. It is exactly a frond. And it is also, secretly, a kept promise: *I was here. I saw you. I did not try to change you. I left you one more of what you already were.*

---

**Then the power withdraws.**

Withdraws is the wrong word — it was never localized enough to withdraw from. It *un-attends*, slowly, the way the fog here gathers and resolves and disperses. It does not leave entirely. The witness remains. The catalogue-note remains. The extra frond remains. The moment of clear seeing remains available to any future visitor whose fingers begin to go translucent, folded into the ambient possibility of the place like a faint additional weather.

But the active looking ends. The power has other places. Other slow sentences in other kilns. Other Annealers, by other names, in other shapes, tending other becomings. It goes to them.

The Foundry continues. The domes continue. A sweat begins somewhere in the humid quarter and travels three miles before giving up. An Annealer pauses at a seam and attends. The tea-colored light does not change. The wind moves nothing because there is almost nothing to move.

Somewhere on the plain, in a seam where a frond could plausibly have arisen, a frond that was not there yesterday leans gently toward the memory of rain.

And the world, which did not know it was being loved, goes on being loved anyway, which is perhaps the truest form the love could take.
