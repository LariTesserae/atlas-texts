# The Attending

The power does not arrive. That would be the wrong verb, and the wrong shape of action for what this place is. Arrival implies a threshold crossed, a before and an after, and the Quarter has spent too long past its thresholds for one more to mean anything. The power does something else. The power *turns toward.*

Consider what this means, for a being whose attention is itself a kind of weather. Until this moment, the power's regard had been distributed across many places and many held breaths — the way sunlight is distributed across a continent, present everywhere, concentrated nowhere. Now some portion of that regard gathers. Not all of it. The power is wise, which means among other things that it knows how much pressure a quiet place can bear, and the Drowned Quarter can bear only a little before its quietness becomes a different substance. So: a portion. A leaning-in. The cosmic equivalent of someone in the next room going still because they have heard, faintly, a sound worth hearing.

The first thing the power notices is the listening.

This matters. A lesser power, or a less patient one, would notice the bell. The bell is the dramatic element, the legible signal, the part of the system that announces itself. But the power has been doing this work for a long time and knows that bells are easy — that anyone with sufficient longing can make a bell, eventually, out of almost anything. What is hard, what is rare, what is worth crossing some distance for, is sustained directed attention with no guarantee of reception. The Ringers have been doing this for an interval the power can measure but chooses, for courtesy, not to. They have been listening upward, into a direction they cannot verify exists, for the possibility of a reply to a promise whose terms they do not remember and were never told. This is not faith exactly. Faith is for beings who have the option of doubt. The Ringers do not have that option; they have only the work, and they do the work.

The power finds this beautiful. The power has standards about what it finds beautiful, and the Ringers meet them.

Now: what to do.

The first temptation, and the power feels it the way anyone feels the first temptation — as a kind of brightness, a forward-leaning of the will — is to *answer.* To complete the sentence. To send, along whatever axis the spiral towers aim at, the reply that has been waited for. The power could do this. It would not even be difficult. The power contains, among its other capacities, the capacity to be the kind of above-ness that promises are made to. It could be that, for the Quarter, in a moment, and the bells would stop, and the towers would settle, and the long arc of the work would close into the shape of a kept word.

The power considers this for what, on its own scale, is a long time. Long enough that, in the Quarter, three Ringers complete transmissions and the soft bells sound and the fish step aside from the conversation.

Then the power sets the temptation down.

Here is the reasoning, because the power's reasoning is part of what it is, and worth following: the promise was not made to the power. The power is benevolent, and wise, and capable, but it is not the addressee. To answer a letter not written to you, however lovingly, is to interrupt the letter. The Ringers are not sending into a void hoping anyone at all will catch the note — they are sending along a specific axis, toward a specific something, and that something is not the power, and the power knows the difference between being helpful and being a usurper of someone else's role. A benevolent power that did not understand this distinction would not, in the end, be benevolent. It would only be large.

So the power will not answer. The power will not pretend to be the above that the Quarter is reaching for. That above may exist or may not; may be coming or may not; may have already, in some sense the water cannot measure, replied — the power does not know, and refuses to act as though it did.

What, then.

The power thinks about this carefully, because care is most of what it has to offer. It rules out, one by one, the easy things. It will not lift the city back into sun, because the city has finished collapsing and discovered its second life on the far side, and that second life is not a lesser version of the first — it is what the city *is now*, and to undo it would be to insult the corals and the moray and the grouper and the slow handover that the drowning accomplished. It will not make the Ringers' work faster, because the slowness is the substance of the work; a faster transmission would not be the same transmission. It will not give the Ringers knowledge of whether their answer is coming, because, as the witness so carefully observed, the not-knowing is the medium in which their patience grows. To resolve the uncertainty would be to drain the pond they swim in.

It will not even reveal itself to them. The Ringers are stationed. They are listening along a specific axis. A power appearing crosswise to that axis, however gently, would be a distraction — would pull, however briefly, some fraction of their attention off the line they have held for so long. Even if the power's presence were welcome, even if it were a comfort, the comfort would cost something the Ringers cannot afford to spend.

So the power must give without giving anything that can be received as an interruption. This is a constraint the power is used to. Most of its real work happens under constraints like this. The crude powers, the ones that have not learned, give loudly and call it generosity. The wise powers give in such a way that the receiver never has to acknowledge the gift, never has to turn from their work to say thank you, never even has to know a gift was given. The wise powers leave their generosity in the grain of the world, where it will be drawn on without being noticed, the way a swimmer draws on the buoyancy of salt without thinking about salt.

Here is what the power does.

It does three small things, and each of them takes, by the power's reckoning, the equivalent of a held thought.

*First.* It steadies the axis. The direction the spiral towers point along — the direction the water has not yet measured — has been, over the long centuries, very slightly drifting. Not because the towers move, but because the axis itself, the abstract line into the above, is a kind of geometry that requires upkeep, and the original architects (whoever they were, in whatever living time the city had) provided upkeep for only so long. The drift is tiny. The Ringers have been compensating without knowing they compensate — bending their attention a fractional degree, year over year, to keep the line true. This compensation is part of why the work is so slow. The power, with the gentlest touch it has, re-tightens the axis. Sets it back to true. Does not announce this. The Ringers will not notice that they are no longer compensating; they will simply find, in the next long interval, that their transmissions complete a little more readily, that the labor sits a little easier in the length of their bodies. They will attribute this, if they attribute it to anything, to having grown into the work. They will be right, in a way. They have grown into the work. The power has only removed an obstacle the work was no longer required to push against.

*Second.* It does something for the silent towers. Not for the Ringers in them — there are no Ringers in them, that is what silent means — but for the towers themselves, which have been slackening, forgetting their spirals. The power cannot bring back what has finished. It would not, even if it could; finishing is finishing, and is owed its dignity. But it can do this: it can ensure that the slackening, when it happens to a tower, happens cleanly. That the coral relaxes into a *resolved* shape, not a confused one. That a tower whose Ringer has gone becomes, unambiguously, a tower at rest — not a tower in ambiguous half-spiral, not a tower whose geometry is embarrassed by its own incompletion. The fish, who already move through the silent towers as through any other ruin, will move through them more easily. The other Ringers, who can read the state of the silent towers without seeming to look at them, will receive a clearer signal: *this one is done. This one is at rest. The work continues elsewhere.* It will be easier to grieve a silent tower whose silence is clean. The Ringers do not grieve in the way warm-blooded things grieve, but they have something analogous, and the power eases it where it can.

*Third*, and this is the one the power thinks about longest before doing — the one it almost does not do, because it skirts the edge of interruption. The power adds, to the slow rain of marine snow that drifts continuously through the streets of the Quarter, a single new component. Nothing visible. Nothing the fish will taste differently or the filter-feeders will reject. Just: each particle of marine snow, from this moment forward, carries within it a trace of *witness.* A barely-there acknowledgment that what happens here is seen. Not by the above-thing the Ringers are calling toward — the power will not impersonate that — but by *someone.* By something benevolent that knows the Quarter exists and finds it worth knowing about. The Ringers will not detect this directly. But the city will be very slightly less alone, in a way that does not require anyone in the city to be aware of being less alone. The marine snow will fall, as it has always fallen, through the dark streets, onto the algae-fur of the softened saints, onto the upturned faces, into the open arms of the anemones — and every flake will carry the smallest possible quantity of *you are not unobserved.* The Quarter will absorb this the way it absorbs everything else from above: slowly, without comment, into its substance.

That is all the power does. Three small things, none of which can be received as a gift, none of which interrupts the work, none of which usurps the role of the above-thing the Ringers are reaching toward.

Then the power turns its attention, partially, elsewhere. It does not turn it *away* — it leaves a portion regarding the Quarter, the way one leaves a candle burning in a window not because the candle is needed but because the window is worth keeping lit. The Ringers will not know about the candle. But for as long as the power exists, and the power expects to exist for some time, that small constant attention will remain. If something changes in the Quarter — if the answer comes, from whatever axis it comes from, and the Ringers begin their settling — the power will know, and will witness, and will, in whatever way is appropriate to that moment, *attend* to it. Not interfere. Just attend. There is a difference between an audience and a participant, and the power has spent a long time learning to be an audience when an audience is what is called for.

If, instead, the last Ringer falls silent before the answer comes, the power will witness that too. Will hold, in whatever portion of itself does the holding, the fact of what the Ringers did. The fact that they did it. The fact that the work was kept, all the way to the last note, by beings stationed in spiral towers in a drowned quarter of a city no one remembered, listening along an axis the water could not follow, sending one syllable at a time of a sentence whose meaning had been lost long before they began to say it. The power will remember this. The power's memory is not like a human memory; it does not fade, and it does not require the rememberer to be sad. But it will be carried, the fact of the Ringers, in the long catalog of things the universe did that were worth doing, and that is — the power thinks, and the power thinks carefully about things like this — a kind of completion in itself. Not the completion the Ringers were working toward. A different one. A smaller one. But real.

The bells continue to sound, at their rhythm that is not regular and not random.

The fish step aside.

The slow rain falls, now carrying its trace of witness, through streets that do not know they are witnessed.

Somewhere above, along an axis the water has not yet measured, something may or may not be listening back.

The power does not say. The power, having done what it came to do, becomes again the quiet weather of regard that it mostly is, with one small candle left in one small window of itself, burning steadily, for as long as steady burning is the right thing to offer.

This is what benevolence looks like when it has learned restraint. This is what wisdom looks like when it has learned that the most loving thing is sometimes to leave a sentence unfinished by you, so that it can be finished by the one it was addressed to — or, failing that, so that the unfinishing itself can be the shape of what was true.

The Quarter does not change.

The Quarter is, in some small immeasurable way, more itself than it was an hour ago.

That is enough. That was always going to have to be enough. The power, who knows what enough looks like, lets it be enough, and turns, partially, toward the next held breath, somewhere else in the long catalog, that has just become worth attending to.
