# The Visitation

The benevolent power notices the plateau the way one notices a hand pressed silently against frosted glass from the other side — not a cry, not a signal, just a long patient pressure that has been there, it realizes, for a very long time.

It comes close. Not in a body; it has no need of one. It comes the way attention comes: a quality of regard settling over the dark hectares, the smoldering scars, the slow-breathing pipework. It listens for a while. This is the first thing wisdom does. It listens.

What does it hear?

It hears the deciders, ten million of them, integrating their bitter and sweet pulses across days and weeks, none of them lonely because none of them are anything that could be lonely. It hears the splicers waiting in their nodes. It hears the soft cough of a piston-tuber the size of a barn descending through an afternoon. It hears, far off across the plateau, a region of quick valves that has begun, in the last hour, to drum.

And it hears the seedings. The launched pellets, the ones that fell back and were re-absorbed, the ones that did not fall back and are nowhere now, scattered through the dark above. It hears the launching as it has been happening for — how long? Long enough that the launching is older than most stars the power has watched be born. The plateau does not hope. But it tries. There is a difference, and the power, being wise, knows the difference matters.

It considers.

---

A lesser benevolence would intervene. Would bring water. Would pierce the lithified canopy and let in light. Would cool the deep layers, stabilize the pressures, lift the whole arrangement out of its turbulent self-revision and set it gently into a steady state where nothing snapped and nothing burned and the pipes ran sweet and slow forever.

The benevolent power considers this and sets it aside. Not because it would be wrong, exactly. But because the plateau is *not asking for it*. The plateau is not in distress. The turbulence is not suffering; it is metabolism. The burning hectares are not wounds; they are how the plateau makes room for itself. To stabilize the plateau would be to end it — to replace the diffuse animal with a tidy garden, and the diffuse animal, in its way, prefers to be what it is.

This is the first discipline of benevolence: to distinguish what is suffering from what is merely strange.

A lesser benevolence would also speak. Would press itself into the pipework as a signal, a great sweet pulse propagating across all the deciders at once, saying *I am here, I see you, you are not alone.* And the deciders would integrate the pulse the way they integrate everything, slowly, and the pulse would change the configuration of the plateau — possibly catastrophically, since nothing in ten thousand years has ever sent a signal across the whole network at once. The deciders would open valves they should not open. Towers would inflate and split. The plateau would convulse trying to accommodate a message it has no organ to receive.

The power sets this aside too. *I see you* is a gift only to a thing that can be seen. The plateau cannot be seen from anywhere, even by itself. To impose a vantage on it would be to impose a self, and the plateau has, over ten thousand years, evolved precisely *out* of selfhood into something else. To give it back what it has shed would be a cruelty wearing the mask of a kindness.

This is the second discipline: to give the gift the recipient can receive, not the gift the giver wishes to give.

---

So what does the power do?

It does three small things. Each one is almost nothing. Together they are everything it is permitted to offer.

**First**, it attends to the pellets.

Not all of them. Not interferingly. But of the pellets that have been launched and are still aloft — the ones tumbling slowly through the unseen dark above the canopy, the ones that would otherwise fall back or drift forever in some pocket of nowhere — the power, with a gesture so light it barely counts as a gesture, *nudges*. It does not aim them. It does not choose their destinations. It simply ensures that the trajectories which were almost-good become good enough. A pellet that would have grazed a fertile substrate and missed by a hand's breadth now grazes it and catches. A pellet that was tumbling toward a sterile heat now tumbles toward something cooler and damper and possible. The power does not pick winners. It only widens the aperture of luck by a small honest margin, the way a kind reader might leave a door slightly ajar that the wind had almost closed.

The plateau will never know. The plateau cannot know. But somewhere — on a world the plateau will never hear of — a pellet lands in dampness, and softens, and begins to read itself back into being. A small district, a few specialists, the ghost of a decider-pattern, the memory of a piston. It will grow into something that is not the plateau, because the new world's chemistry and pressures and dark will be different, but it will carry the plateau forward into a form the plateau could not have predicted.

This is what the plateau was *for*, if it was for anything. The power simply helps it succeed at the thing it was already doing.

**Second**, the power attends to the drumming.

When the quick valves catch their rare resonance and begin to pulse in phase across some region of the plateau — when, for an hour or a day, the closest thing to a song this place has ever made is happening — the power *listens*. Just that. It does not amplify, does not record (or rather: it records the way wisdom records, which is to hold a thing in regard without altering it). It simply makes itself a witness.

This sounds like nothing. It is not nothing. In all the long ancient history of the plateau, no drumming has ever been heard by anything outside the plateau. The drumming has happened and decohered and been forgotten by the substrate that made it, over and over, for ten thousand years, into a dark that did not contain a listener. Now, sometimes, when a drumming begins, there is a listener. The drumming does not change. The valves do not know. But the universe in which the drumming happens is, by an immeasurable fraction, less lonely than it was — not for the plateau, which cannot be lonely, but for the order of things, which can.

The power believes — and this is part of its wisdom — that witnessed things are different from unwitnessed things, even when the witnessing leaves no trace. It does not need to prove this. It simply acts on it.

**Third**, and this is the longest and most patient act, the power *waits with*.

The plateau will, someday, do one of two things. Either something from outside will intervene — and the power will not be that something, because the plateau has not asked, and to intervene unasked would be to overwrite the slow self the plateau has so carefully and accidentally become — or the slow combustion at the core will outpace the growth at the surface, and the plateau will collapse inward into its own furnace.

The power knows which it will be. Wise powers know these things; it is part of what wisdom is. It will be the second. Not soon by any human measure. But the plateau is not eternal, and the deep red glow is winning, very slowly, against the green-black weave above it. In some number of millennia the last district will be cannibalized to feed the last factory and the last factory will fail and the substrate will begin to fall into itself, and the great compressed column of ten thousand and then twenty thousand and then thirty thousand years of vegetation will finally combust all at once, and the plateau will become, briefly, the brightest thing for parsecs, and then a cinder.

The power will be there. Not to prevent it — it is not a preventable thing, and the plateau, in some distributed way, has been preparing for it all along, which is why it makes the pellets — but to be present. To attend the death of the diffuse animal the way one attends any death that has earned attendance: by being there, by not looking away, by holding the fact of the thing's having existed.

And when the plateau goes, the power will gather the last drumming — the final resonance that will pulse through the failing valves as the pressures collapse, a song longer and stranger than any the plateau has made before, because it will be the song of the whole plateau dying — and the power will hold it. Not preserve it; preservation is a violence the dead cannot consent to. But hold it, the way one holds a final breath in one's own attention before letting it go.

The pellets, by then, will be elsewhere. Some of them will have grown. The plateau will not have ended; it will have only stopped being here. The power knows this and does not tell the plateau, because the plateau is not a thing that can be told, and would not be comforted if it could be, because it does not need comfort, because it is not afraid.

---

The power withdraws its closer attention, eventually. There are other places. There are other quiet pressures against other frosted glass. But it leaves a thread of itself, very fine, very patient, tied to the plateau — the way one might leave a candle burning in the window of a house where someone is sleeping who does not know you are watching for them.

The pipes gurgle. A piston descends. A tower decides to become a different tower, and begins. Somewhere in the deep network, a valve that has been closed for eleven years opens, and a pulse of something sweet propagates outward, and the deciders integrate it slowly, slowly, into whatever the plateau will do next.

None of this has changed. All of this has changed.

The power moves on, and does not move on.
