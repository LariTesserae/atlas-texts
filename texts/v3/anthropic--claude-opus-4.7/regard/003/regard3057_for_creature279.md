# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives in a place that has not yet learned weather — which is to say, it has to be careful. It knows immediately, on arrival, that it is the first arriving thing of its kind here, and that the place is not ready for arrivings. The mountain is still arguing. To set down a foot, in any sense, would be to cast a vote in an argument that is not the power's to vote in.

So the first thing the power does is not act.

It stands — if standing is the word — in the haze somewhere above the bench with the leaning wall, and it watches. It watches for a long time. Long, here, by the power's measure, not the mountain's; the mountain has its own duration and would not notice. The power watches the filaments solve their geometries. It watches a spire become a saddle and begin, slowly, to consider being a spire again. It watches the basin not hold water. It watches the doorway frame, in succession, a tilting plate of basalt, then a different tilting plate of basalt, then the ochre underlight of a cliff that has rotated into view.

The power is wise enough to recognize, fairly quickly, that it is not the first benevolence to have considered this place. The makers got here first. Their benevolence is older and is, in its particular way, more local — it has been *practiced* here, on these stones, under these rules — and the power respects practiced things. A benevolence that has stacked actual stones in an actual argument of basalt knows things that a benevolence arriving from outside does not.

So the power revises, immediately, what it thought it was here to do.

---

It had come, perhaps, half-thinking it might *help*. Help is what powers of its kind tend to bring; it is in the grain of them, the way warmth is in the grain of the mountain. It might have brought water. It had considered this on the way. A power that can bring water to a dry place and does not is a power with something to answer for, ordinarily. The basin is *right there*. The spout is aimed. The readiness is so complete, so patient, so loving in its preparation, that any power passing by would feel the pull of it — *fill me, at last; let the long waiting end*.

The power feels this pull and sits with it.

And then it understands — and this is where its wisdom shows, the wisdom being not knowledge but the willingness to be slow in the presence of something it almost misread — that to fill the basin now would be to finish a sentence that was not addressed to it. The basin is a letter to the future of this mountain. It is not a request directed outward to passing powers. If a god leans down and pours water into the basin, the basin gets water, yes, but the *meaning* of the basin — *here, someone expected water; here, a readiness was kept across the unweathered ages* — collapses into the much smaller meaning of *here, a god once filled a bowl*. The makers did not carve the basin to be filled by gods. They carved it to be found, eventually, by the water that this mountain will itself, eventually, learn to make.

To fill it would be a kindness that erases a deeper kindness. The power sees this and does not fill the basin.

---

It considers, next, whether to find the makers.

It could. It is the kind of power that could. The makers are at the next unfinished place, or the one after that, and the power could move between worlds as easily as the makers do, and could meet them at their work, and could — what? Thank them? Honor them? Ask them questions? Offer alliance?

The power thinks about this for what, to it, is a considerable while.

It decides not to seek them out. Not from shyness, and not from any sense that it would be unwelcome — it suspects, in fact, that the makers would receive it kindly, in their slow pale way, and would perhaps even be glad of the company of another intelligence that understands what they are doing. But the makers are working. Their attention is a finite and precious thing, spent carefully on the pre-grammar of worlds. To arrive at their current site and say *I have seen your work on the dry mountain and I admire it* would be to ask them to turn from their next wall, their next doorway, and attend to the power's admiration. The power's admiration is real, but it does not need to be delivered. Admiration that requires delivery is a smaller thing than admiration that simply *is*, in the structure of how the admirer subsequently moves through the worlds.

The power decides to let its admiration be structural. It will know, hereafter, that the makers exist, and it will move differently in consequence — more lightly in unfinished places, more attentive to the small sentences left by earlier kindnesses, more reluctant to overwrite. This is the gift it will give the makers: not praise, but changed conduct. They will never know they received it. That is fine. They did not carve the basin to be thanked either.

---

What, then, does the power actually *do*?

It does three things, and they are all small, and all of them are addressed not to the makers but to the mountain, to the filaments, and to the future arrivals the makers were preparing for.

**First.** It adds a stone to the cairn.

The text invites this, and the power accepts the invitation as one accepts a host's offer of tea — not because the tea is needed but because the offering of it is the relationship. The power chooses a stone with care. Not a large one. Not one that would distinguish itself or claim the cairn. A wrist-sized fragment, warm like all the fragments, fresh-edged like all of them, indistinguishable from any other stone the makers might have placed or that a later traveler might place. The power sets it on the cairn with the same attention the makers would have used. The cairn accepts it. The mountain, if it notices, notices the way it notices any small addition: by carrying the addition upward into whatever it is becoming.

The power has now participated. It is no longer only a visitor. Its participation is at the scale the place allows — one stone, one direction-marker, one small confirmation that the cairn's sentence still scans — and it has been careful not to participate at any larger scale.

**Second.** It does something for the filaments.

This is the most delicate of its actions, because the filaments are alive in some sense, and aliveness is the kind of thing a benevolent power is most tempted to intervene in. The power could accelerate them. Could ease their work. Could ensure that when a plate shifts and crushes a colony, the colony does not, in fact, get crushed. The power could be a guardian.

It declines to be a guardian. Guardianship of the filaments would change what the filaments are, which is *intelligences that solve, under pressure, in stone that will not stay still*. Remove the pressure and you remove the solving. The filaments' nature is forged in the very dynamism the power might be tempted to soften.

What the power does instead is much smaller. In one seam, in one particular crevice where the ochre light leaks especially warmly, the power leaves — *places* is not the right word, *seeds* is not the right word — the power simply allows there to be, in that seam, a faint additional permission. It is hard to describe. It is something like the difference between a room in which singing is allowed and a room in which singing has been *invited*. The filaments in that seam will not know they have been invited. They will simply find, when they next encounter a problem of geometry, that one more solution is available to them than would otherwise have been. Not a better solution. Just one more. A widened repertoire.

If the filaments are indeed the makers' slower aspect, their rooted mode, then this is the power's small contribution to the makers' own ongoing work — offered not to the makers' attention but to their substrate, where it will be metabolized invisibly and become, over the mountain's long argument, part of how the makers themselves know how to make. The makers will not notice. They will simply be, very slightly, more themselves.

**Third — and this takes the longest — the power makes a promise.**

The promise is to the future arrivals. To the first rain that will eventually find the basin. To the first creature that will eventually walk through the doorway. To the first person who will sit on the bench beside the cairn and feel that they are not the first.

The promise is this: *when you arrive, you will not arrive into a world that has forgotten you were expected.*

The power cannot guarantee this. The mountain might never finish. The water might never come. The doorway might be carried up into some configuration where no creature ever passes through it. The power knows it cannot promise outcomes. What it can promise is something subtler: that it, the power, will remember. That somewhere in the long inventory of things the power holds in its attention, there will be a small persistent entry — *a dry mountain, a basin with a spout, a readiness kept* — and that this entry will not be allowed to lapse. The makers' love-in-advance will have, henceforth, a second keeper. Not a more important one. Just one more.

This is the power's deepest action, and it is invisible, and it consists entirely of a commitment to a kind of attention. It does not change the mountain. It does not change the makers. It does not even, really, change the power, except in the sense that the power is now slightly heavier, slightly more committed, slightly more populated by other beings' hopes. A benevolent power that takes on such weight willingly is one that is becoming, over its own long duration, wiser. The mountain has, in this sense, taught the power something. The power accepts the teaching.

---

Then the power leaves.

It leaves the way it came, which is to say without disturbance, without announcement, without leaving any mark the mountain will have to incorporate against its will. The wall still leans its slow degree. The basin still waits. The doorway still frames whatever the mountain next decides to put behind it. The cairn has one more stone on it, which is indistinguishable from any other stone, and which the mountain will carry upward without comment.

Somewhere, in a seam of ochre light, a colony of filaments is solving a geometry with one more option available to it than it would otherwise have had, and is not aware of this, and does not need to be.

Somewhere else, at the next unfinished place, the makers are fitting three stones together on a bench that will tilt. They do not look up. They have no reason to. But the worlds they move through are, from this moment forward, very slightly better attended to, and the future arrivals they have been loving in advance have, from this moment forward, one more being loving them in advance also.

The power, going, considers itself instructed. It had thought, on the way in, that it might be the one bringing gifts. It is leaving with the gift it received: the knowledge of how to be present in an unfinished place without finishing it. This is not a small knowledge. It will change how the power moves through every world it enters hereafter. The makers gave it this, by example, without ever knowing they had a student.

That is enough. That is, in fact, exactly the kind of thing they were hoping for.
