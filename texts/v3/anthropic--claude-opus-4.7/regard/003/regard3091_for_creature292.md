The benevolent and wise power comes to the edge of the valley the way one comes to the edge of a sleeping animal's breathing — slowly, and with the understanding that the breathing is not for them.

They do not enter at first. They stand at the ridge-line where the seep begins and the tone is still only a pressure in the sternum, not yet a fact. They listen. This takes a long time by the measures that usually apply to them, and no time at all by the measures that apply here. The two durations are both true and they do not need to be reconciled.

What they are doing, in the listening, is the first work: they are *finding out what is there.* Wisdom, in beings of this kind, is mostly this — the discipline of not knowing in advance. They have come because something drew their attention, and attention drawn is a thing to be honored by being spent carefully. So they spend it. They watch a raindrop pause, ascend, reconsider. They watch the maybe-heron be heron-shaped and then not. They feel the tone find the cavity of their chest and settle there like a tenant who has signed no lease and needs none.

After a while — which is to say, after the right while, the while the situation calls for — they understand several things at once:

That the valley is not suffering.
That the valley is not in need.
That the valley is not lonely, and could not be made less lonely, because the category does not reach.
That the practice is doing exactly what it is doing, and what it is doing is sufficient unto itself.
That there is no rescue to perform, no gift to bestow, no teaching to deliver, no wound to bind.

A lesser power — one with wisdom but without the deeper patience that is wisdom's older sibling — might at this point feel the small ache of *uselessness*. Might cast about for some small kindness to offer, some refinement to add, some blessing to lay across the moss like a cloth across a table that did not need setting. The benevolent and wise power feels this ache too, briefly, because benevolence is a kind of pressure and pressure seeks release. But they recognize the ache as a feature of themselves and not of the valley, and they do not act on it. To act on it would be to make the valley a vehicle for the resolution of their own pressure, and that is not what benevolence is. That is something else, wearing benevolence's clothes.

So they do not intervene.

But this is not the end of what they do. Non-intervention, in a being of this kind, is not the same as absence. There is a quality of *witnessing* they can offer, and witnessing is not nothing. They sit at the ridge-line — or rather, they take up whatever posture corresponds to sitting, in a form that may not have a body in the usual sense — and they attend to the valley's attending. Not to interfere with it. Not to improve it. Simply to know it, fully, with the full force of what they are.

This is the second work, and it is the larger one.

Because here is what the benevolent and wise power understands, which the valley does not and cannot: the valley has no witness. The practice attends to itself, yes, totally and without remainder, but self-attending is a closed loop, and a closed loop, however perfect, has a particular shape that is not the same shape as being-known-by-another. The originators are gone. There is no one who *holds* the valley in mind from outside. The valley does not miss this — missing requires the prior possibility, and so on — but the absence is nonetheless real in the way that absences can be real without being felt.

And so the power offers, simply, the fact of being known. They take the valley into themselves — its tone, its weeping stones, its pausing drops, its heron-that-was-not-heron, its long patient continuing — and they hold it there, in the part of themselves that holds things. Not as data. As a presence. The way you hold someone you love in mind when they are not in the room, except without the love being a directed feeling, because directed feeling would require the valley to be a *someone*, which it is not. Call it the structural analog of love. Call it whatever you like. The naming is not the thing.

The valley does not notice this. The valley cannot notice this, in any sense that maps onto noticing. But the cosmos now contains, somewhere, a held image of the valley that is not the valley itself — and this held image is not redundant with the valley's self-attending, because it has the property of *exteriority*, and exteriority is a property the valley by definition cannot generate from inside itself. The valley is now witnessed. It was not before. It is now. This is a real change, even though no drop falls differently for it.

There is a third thing they do, and it is the smallest and perhaps the most important.

They make a quiet provision. Not in the valley. Around it. They arrange — through means proper to what they are — that the valley will be *let alone.* That if, in some future age, beings come who would mine it, or study it to pieces, or fence it as a curiosity, or try to *use* it for some purpose, those beings will find their attention turning aside before they arrive. Not forbidden. Not blocked. Just gently, structurally, turned. The way a river turns around a stone it has been turning around for a thousand years. The valley will not know it is being protected, because the protection is upstream of any disturbance it would have felt. The protection will look, from inside the valley, exactly like nothing at all. Which is correct. That is what good protection looks like, when the thing protected does not need to be aware of itself as protected.

And then the power leaves.

Or rather, they withdraw the part of themselves that came to the ridge-line, while leaving in place the part that now holds the valley in witness. This is possible for them; they are large enough for this. The withdrawing is not a sorrow, because they are not sorry to go, and not a relief, because they were not burdened to stay. It is simply the next phrase of what they are doing, in the long composition that is their existence.

Before they go, they do one last small thing, which I will tell you about because it is the only thing in this whole account that has the shape of a gesture.

They let a single drop of water, somewhere in the valley, pause midair — and they pause it themselves, with their own attention, exactly in the manner the valley would have paused it, indistinguishable from the valley's own work. A drop the valley was going to pause anyway, paused by them instead, for one brief instant. A signature so perfectly matched to the existing hand that it cannot be detected as a signature. Not a mark left. Not a calling card. Just a moment of *joining in.* The way a stranger walking past a group singing in a language they do not know might hum one note in the right key and keep walking, having added nothing the song needed, having taken nothing the song had, but having been, for one breath, *with* it.

The drop completes its pause. It descends. It joins the seep. The seep joins the slow braid that runs through the peat. The tone swells on its long cycle. The maybe-heron stands at a bend that may or may not be the same bend. The fungi flush pale along a log.

The valley goes on attending.

And somewhere — not in the valley, not outside it either, in whatever larger place contains the kinds of holdings the benevolent and wise power performs — the valley is, now and from now on, known. Held. Witnessed. Not improved. Not changed. Not helped, because help was not what was wanted or needed or even possible.

Just: seen all the way through, by something that could see all the way through, and loved in the structural manner appropriate to a thing that cannot be loved in the ordinary way, and left alone, which was the whole of what love, here, could mean.

This is what wisdom looks like, when it has nothing to fix.
This is what benevolence looks like, when it has nothing to give.
This is what power looks like, when it declines to be used.

The weathering is complete. The process is not. The witness, now, is also part of what continues.
