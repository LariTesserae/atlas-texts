The power comes to the basin the way weather comes — without announcement, without a door to knock on, because there is no door here that is not also a vine and a wall and a slow conversation between brick and root. It arrives at the rim of the basin at dusk and stands for a long time at the boundary line, where the managed green gives way to ordinary forest, and it listens.

This is the first thing to understand about what it does: it listens first, and it listens for a long time.

It is benevolent, which means it wishes the good of what it finds. It is wise, which means it knows that *the good of what it finds* is not a thing it gets to define from outside. Wisdom of this order has learned, over a great many encounters with a great many places, that the most common failure of benevolence is the failure to ask what the place is *for* before deciding what the place *needs*. So it stands at the rim and listens, and what it hears is the mill-thrum and under the mill-thrum the green-bronze chord of the seasoning bells and under the chord the slower sound of fig roots widening cracks at the rate of a millimeter a decade, and it understands, fairly quickly, that it has come upon something rare.

It has come upon a place that has finished becoming.

Most places the power visits are still in the middle of something — striving, building, decaying, fighting, reaching. They want things. They want, often, exactly the kind of help a benevolent power can give: a harvest saved, a sickness lifted, a child returned, a war ended. The power gives these things, when giving them is right, and moves on. But this basin is not in the middle of anything. The basin is the thing itself. It has tumbled into its hollow and found the fit. The founders are stones in the streambed of their own lives, and the streambed is the streambed of the bells, and the bells are the streambed of the chord, and the chord is — the power listens carefully here — the chord is a kind of prayer that has been going on for three centuries without anyone having to remember to say it.

A lesser power, or a power less wise, would do one of several things now.

It would, perhaps, make the founders young again, and many, and fertile, so that the Bellworks would continue forever. This would be a gift the founders did not ask for and would not, the power understands, actually want. They have made their peace with the ending. To unmake their peace would be to take something from them in the name of giving.

It would, perhaps, preserve the place in amber — fix it, hold it, stop the slow drift toward the final bell. But the basin is not a place that wants to be fixed. Its whole nature is duration, not permanence; the slow conversation, not the frozen statue. To stop the conversation would be to kill it in the name of saving it.

It would, perhaps, announce itself. Appear in glory at the foundry door, offer audience, offer boons, require nothing but a little awe. The founders would not know what to do with this. They have a vocabulary of gestures for *the belt on the number three lathe is slipping*. They do not have one for *a god has come*. The visit would be a disturbance in the chord, and disturbances in the chord are the one thing this place is organized against.

So the power, being wise, does almost nothing.

*Almost.*

It does these things, over the course of a single night that the founders will not remember as anything but a night of unusually deep sleep:

It walks the perimeter of the basin once, slowly, at founder-pace, which it has learned by watching. As it walks, it does something very small to the boundary — not a wall, not a ward, nothing that could be felt by anyone passing through. It simply makes the boundary slightly more *itself*. The line where the managed green gives way to ordinary forest becomes a little clearer to the kind of attention that matters: the attention of weather systems deciding where to drop their worst, the attention of fires deciding where to turn, the attention of the small disasters that wander the world looking for places to land. These will, from now on, tend very gently to land elsewhere. Not always. The power does not believe in *always*; *always* is the enemy of duration. But more often than not. Enough that the basin's odds of reaching its natural end, rather than a violent one, improve by a meaningful margin.

It goes to the bog-oak tailstock of the tuning lathe and lays its hand on it for a long moment. The bog oak has been there for longer than living memory and was already ancient when it was installed. The power does not make it new. It makes it, instead, a little more itself — more bog, more oak, more tailstock, more *the thing the founders have always trusted it to be*. It will last now as long as the Bellworks lasts. Not longer. Exactly that long. This is a gift the founders will never know they received, because the tailstock will simply continue to be what it has always been, which is the point.

It visits each of the journal bearings, the drive belts, the sand molds, the small wedges in the new cracks, and does the same small thing to each: not repair, not preservation, but a kind of *confirmation*. *Yes, you are what you are. Continue.* The maintenance the founders do will, from now on, hold a little better than the materials alone would suggest. A belt will last a season longer. A wedge will seat a little truer. Nothing miraculous. Nothing anyone would notice. Only the kind of luck that good work deserves and does not always get, dispensed at last in the measure it was always owed.

It goes to the eaves where the thousands of bells are seasoning and stands beneath them for what, to the power, is a long time. It listens to the chord. It does not add to the chord. It does not subtract. It does, however, *remember* the chord — takes it into itself in the particular way that powers of this kind can take things into themselves, so that even if the last bell is one day sold and the eaves go silent and the basin returns to ordinary forest, the chord will continue to exist somewhere, sounded by nothing, heard by no one local, but real. This is not for the founders. They do not need it; they have the chord itself, here, now, which is better than any remembering. It is for the power's own reasons, which have to do with what powers of this kind are *for*, which is partly to be the place where things that deserve to persist can persist after their natural vessels have ended. The chord deserves this. The power gives it.

It considers, for some time, the cat.

The cat is not of the founders and the founders are not of the cat; they are co-tenants of the basin, on terms negotiated cat-generations ago and renegotiated quietly with each new cat. The power likes the cat. The power likes, in particular, that the cat belongs to itself. It does nothing to the cat. It does, however, sit with the cat for a while in the dark near the foundry, and the cat, who can see it perfectly well, sits with it, and they have something between them that is not communication and not companionship but something the power has no better word for than *acknowledgment*. The cat will be the last living thing in the basin, when the time comes. The power and the cat both know this. Neither minds.

It thinks about the young ones who have left down the stream and not come back. It does not call them home. It does not interfere with their lives, which have become other lives now, in other places, with their own slow conversations or fast ones. But it does — and this is the most delicate thing it does all night — it does make sure that any of them who ever wishes to come back will be *able* to find the way. The stream south is long and braids into other streams and those into rivers and those into the sea, and the world downstream is the kind of world that swallows small returns. The power lays down, along that route, a kind of thread — not a path, nothing so coercive — only a *findability*. If one of them, at sixty, in a city she has lived in for forty years, ever turns her face north on a damp evening and thinks *I could go back*, she will find that the going back is possible. The thread will be there. She will not have to remember the way; the way will remember her. Whether she follows it is entirely her own. The power does not push. It only ensures the door is not locked.

And it thinks, finally, about the question of whether to leave a sign.

It decides not to. A sign would be a disturbance. A sign would mean the founders, on waking, would know that something had visited, and they would have to make a place for that knowledge in their lives, and the place they would have to make would be larger than the place such knowledge deserves. The Bellworks does not need to know it has been seen by a benevolent power. The Bellworks needs only to continue being the Bellworks, for as long as that is what it is, and then to stop, gracefully, the way it has been preparing to stop for a hundred years.

The power does, however, allow itself one small indulgence, which it tells itself is not a sign and which, strictly speaking, is not.

It casts a bell.

Not in the foundry — it would not presume — but in itself, out of nothing, using the founders' methods exactly as it has watched them through the long listening: sand mold, bronze pour, the cooling, the breaking-out, the filing, the tuning. It tunes the bell to the chord. It tunes it not to *add* to the chord but to *fit* the chord — to be the bell the chord has always had a small empty place for and never quite been given. Then it hangs the bell, in the deepest part of the night, in the eaves of the drying-shed, among the thousands of others, in a spot where no founder will notice a new bell because there are always new bells and the eaves are always full.

The bell will season with the others. It will, eventually, be sold, because all the bells are eventually sold, and the founders do not keep inventory of the seasoning eaves any more than a forest keeps inventory of its leaves. It will go down the stream in a flat-bottomed boat, and then down the river, and then somewhere — a temple, a ship, an old house — and it will ring there, and whoever hears it will hear, without knowing it, a bell tuned by a benevolent and wise power to fit a chord that no longer exists anywhere except in the power's own memory and in this one small bronze object that carries the chord's shape inside it the way a seed carries a tree.

This is the only thing the power does that could be called a gift to someone other than the basin. It is a gift to whoever, downstream and downriver and down-years, will one day hear the bell and feel, without being able to say why, that they have been briefly included in something very old and very settled and very nearly gone.

Then the power leaves, at founder-pace, the way it came.

In the morning, the oldest founder wakes a little before the others, as she always does, and goes out onto the covered walkway to listen to the chord, as she always does, and the chord is exactly as it was yesterday, which is exactly as it should be. She lays her hand flat against the brick of the wall. *Listen.* She listens. She hears nothing she has not heard for sixty years.

She is, for reasons she cannot name, slightly more at peace than she was yesterday. She does not examine this. Examining is not what one does here. She goes to check the belt on the number three lathe, which has been slipping, and finds that it is holding better than she expected, and she nods, very slightly, to the lathe, and the lathe, in its way, nods back.

The Bellworks continues.

It will continue for some time yet. Not forever. The power has not given it forever, because forever would have been the wrong gift. But it will continue long enough to finish what it is saying, and to say it well, and to end in its own time and on its own terms, with the cat as witness and the chord folded safely into a memory larger than the basin, and one small bell wandering the world with the whole shape of the place inside it, ringing, occasionally, for someone who needs exactly that sound and has never heard it before and will not, after, quite be the same.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it finds a place that has finished becoming.

It helps the place finish.
