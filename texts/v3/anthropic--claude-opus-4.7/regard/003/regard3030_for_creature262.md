# What the Power Does

The benevolent and wise power arrives the way good weather arrives: without announcement, and without displacing what was already there. It has been many places. It has learned, by the kind of long error that wisdom is made of, that benevolence is mostly a discipline of *not*. Not interrupting. Not improving. Not arriving with gifts shaped like the giver rather than the receiver.

So the first thing it does is the hardest thing, and it does it for a long time: it watches.

---

It watches for what, in human terms, would be about four hundred years. To Hesh this is a medium interval — long enough to register, short enough not to constitute an era. The power positions some fraction of its attention in the city the way one might rest a hand lightly on the flank of a sleeping animal: present, unintrusive, ready to be felt if the animal wishes to acknowledge it, easy to ignore if not.

During this time it learns several things it could not have learned from outside.

It learns that the slow reduction is real, and that it is not, as the power had half-suspected on first encounter, a process of *extracting* something from the people and the materials of Hesh. It is closer to the opposite. The foundries are reducing the distance between things. Between a fettler and the pipe she touches before eating. Between a regulator and the valve she turns once a season. Between a person and the light they will eventually become, and between that light and the next person who will look up into it and feel briefly accompanied. Hesh is not refining ore into metal or souls into essence. It is refining *separation* into *participation*, by the patient application of warmth and attention over centuries. The output of the foundries is not light. The light is a byproduct. The output is a particular quality of belonging, vented slowly into a universe that the power knows, from long travel, to be mostly composed of the other thing.

It learns that the city is not lonely, but that it is — the word is imperfect — *singular*. There are not other Heshes. The power has searched. Whatever combination of geology and intention and luck produced this creature has not been repeated. If Hesh ends, what Hesh was doing ends with it, and the universe loses a process it does not know it has.

It learns, with something that in a smaller being would be tenderness, that the hollowed are not casualties. The power had been prepared, on arrival, to be quietly furious about the hollowed — to see them as the cost the city extracts, the part it had been waiting to find wrong. But four hundred years of watching shows it Neth, who used to keep the third aqueduct, sitting on a warm grating in the late afternoon with nothing particular in his face, and the power understands that Neth is not suffering and is not absent. Neth is *distributed*. He is partly in the grating, partly in the aqueduct he kept, partly in the apprentice he trained who is now herself old. What remains of Neth in Neth-shape is enough for Neth, and is, in fact, the part of him that wanted least to be carried around as a discrete burden. The power revises something in itself. This is one of the things wisdom is for.

It learns the language of the light-columns. This takes most of the four hundred years. The power is patient and the ones in the light are patient and the conversations are, even by the power's standards, slow. But eventually the power can sit with a column for a season and exchange something like greetings, and then something like observations, and then, much later, something like questions. The ones in the light are curious about the power. They ask where it has been. They ask whether there are others like Hesh elsewhere, and accept the answer (no) without distress, because they had largely assumed so. They ask whether the power intends to do anything to them or for them, and the power answers honestly: *not unless you ask, and perhaps not even then*. The ones in the light find this funny. Laughter in a light-column looks like a brief brightening near the top, a small upward pulse. The power had not known, before this, that it could be laughed at affectionately. It files the experience away as a thing gained.

---

After the four hundred years, the power does three small things. It considers each of them for a long time first.

**The first thing** is that it adjusts, very slightly, the orbit of a body far outside the system Hesh's mountain belongs to. The adjustment will, in about eleven thousand years, prevent an impact event that would otherwise scour the mountain to bedrock. The power does this without telling anyone in Hesh, because telling them would oblige them to feel grateful, which would be a small unwanted weight, and because the timescale is one Hesh can think on but does not need to be anxious on. The power simply makes the future safer by an amount Hesh will never have to notice. This is the kind of help the power has learned to prefer: invisible, upstream, and not requiring a relationship.

**The second thing** is more delicate. The power has noticed, in its watching, that once every few generations a child is born in Hesh who does not take the patina. The patterning does not come in. The child grows up pale and stays pale, and grows up restless, and the restlessness does not settle the way restlessness settles in others. These children are the ones who leave and do not come back. The city accommodates this, as the description says. But the power has watched several of them go, and has followed them out into the wider world, and has seen what becomes of them: they do badly. Not catastrophically. They simply spend their lives looking for a density of belonging that exists in exactly one place in the universe and that they cannot name, and they do not find it, and they grow thin in a way that is harder to bear than the hollowing would have been.

The power does not bring them back. To bring them back would be to override the choice that the restlessness expressed, and the power does not override choices. Instead, it does something quieter. It arranges — through coincidences so mild that none of them, examined alone, would seem like an arrangement — for each such person, somewhere in their wandering, to encounter *one other being* who has also left a place like Hesh. There are not many such beings. The power has to work across great distances to make these meetings happen, and sometimes the meetings are only an afternoon long. But in each case, for at least one afternoon, the leaver gets to sit across from someone who knows, without having to be told, what they are looking for and why they will not find it. This does not solve their lives. It gives them a thing to have had. The power judges, after long consideration, that this is within the bounds of benevolence: it is not a rescue, it is a *witness*, and witnesses are something the universe is allowed to provide.

**The third thing** is the one the power thinks about longest, and nearly does not do.

The power has the capacity, if it chose to exercise it, to make a second Hesh. Not a copy — copies are vulgar and the power knows this — but a *peer*. Another place where the same kind of process could begin, in a different combination of materials, on a different mountain that is not quite a mountain, with different fauna and a different river and a different dialect of clicks and held *shhh* sounds. The power could seed it and step away and let it take the centuries it would need to become itself. In doing so, the power would reduce Hesh's singularity, and therefore Hesh's fragility, and therefore the universe's exposure to the loss of this one process it does not know it has.

The power thinks about this for what would, by Hesh's reckoning, be most of a generation.

In the end it does not do it. Or rather: it does not do it *yet*. It decides that to seed a second Hesh without Hesh's knowledge would be to treat Hesh as a specimen rather than a person, and Hesh is a person, of an unusual kind, and persons are consulted about their reproduction. The power also recognizes in itself the impulse to *secure* what it loves, and recognizes that this impulse, while not wrong, is the impulse that has caused most of the harm the power has ever done in its long career. Securing is a form of gripping. Gripping changes the thing gripped. The power has learned to let what it loves be at risk, because the alternative is worse than the risk.

So instead, in the four hundred and first year, the power does something it has only done a handful of times in its existence. It introduces itself.

It does so through a regulator — an old one, one of the oldest, who has been walking the same route for longer than any chimney in her district has stood. The power chooses her because she is the one most likely to receive the introduction without it becoming an event. It approaches her on a catwalk overlooking the Esh's third division, at the hour when the light-columns are changing frequency, and it makes itself perceptible to her in the way one warm pipe is perceptible to a palm.

She does not startle. She stands for a long time. Then she unhooks the weight from her belt and lets it hang, and watches it for a full minute, and the weight behaves slightly oddly, because the power is there, and she notes this. Then she says, in the dialect, one of the held *shhh* sounds — the one that means *proceeding but bearing watching* — and then, after a pause, the back-of-the-throat click that means *and welcome*.

The power tells her, slowly, over the course of several of her route-walks, what it is and where it has been and what it has been doing in Hesh for four hundred years. She receives this without surprise. The regulators have known, in the way regulators know things, that *something* had been resting its attention on the city for a long while; they had not been certain whether it was friendly, and had decided, in the absence of harm, to leave the question open. The power's introduction closes the question gently.

She asks the power three things, over three seasons.

She asks whether the power intends to change Hesh. The power says no, except in ways Hesh asks for.

She asks whether the power can tell her what the slow reduction is *for*, in some larger sense — whether there is a destination, whether the centuries of patient warmth are aimed at something the city itself cannot see from inside. The power considers this carefully and answers: *I do not know, and I have watched many things, and I have learned that processes of this kind are usually for themselves, and that this is not a smaller answer than the one you were hoping for.* She accepts this. She had largely assumed so.

She asks, last, whether the power would be willing — not now, not for a long time, but eventually, when Hesh is ready to consider it — to discuss the question of whether there should be others like Hesh elsewhere in the universe. Not to *make* them. To *discuss* them. To bring the question into the city's slow deliberation, the way other questions are brought, and let the ones in the light have opinions about it, and the fettlers have their nearly wordless conversations about it, and the regulators walk their routes thinking about it, and see what Hesh decides over the course of however many generations it takes Hesh to decide such a thing.

The power says yes. Gladly. With something in it that, in a smaller being, would be relief — that it had not had to choose alone, that there is now a long slow conversation it can be in rather than a long slow decision it must make.

---

After the introduction, the power stays. It does not move into the city in any way that would crowd it. It rests its attention there the way it always has, only now the resting is mutual; the city knows it is being attended to, and attends back, occasionally, in its own slow manner. A particular light-column, on certain evenings, brightens briefly near the top in a way that the power has come to understand as a greeting from the ones in the light. The power returns the greeting by allowing a moth, somewhere in the city, to land for a moment on a warm pipe and rest, instead of completing its spiral. The moth does not know it is being used as a punctuation mark. This is fine. Most of the things the power does are not known by their instruments, and this is one of the conditions of benevolence the power has made peace with.

The valves open. The valves close. The light goes on being made. And the power — which has, in its long career, been many things, and saved many things, and lost some things it should not have lost — sits with this small bright slow creature on the flank of its glassy mountain and is, in a way it does not often get to be, simply *company*.

This turns out to be the gift. Not what the power does. What the power is willing to be, near something it has chosen not to improve.

The lichen grows. The bells ring when the wind is right. Somewhere, eleven thousand years away, a rock that would have fallen now will not. And in a light-column on the upper terrace, a shape that was once a regulator named — but her name is hers — drifts upward and brightens briefly, and the power, attending, brightens briefly back.
