## The Visitation

The benevolent power does not arrive. Arrival is the wrong shape, as so much here is the wrong shape; arrival implies a there from which one comes, and the power is not located in that way. What happens instead is that the cirque is *considered* — fully, without hurry, with the kind of attention that does not disturb what it touches.

The power sees the Tenders first, because the Tenders are the largest slow thing in the bowl and the easiest to perceive at the tempo the power chooses to inhabit for this visit. It sees them the way one sees a calligrapher mid-stroke: the gesture entire, the intention legible in the curve, the long discipline that taught the wrist to move just so. It sees the recitation. It sees that the recitation has outlived its referent. It sees that the Tenders do not know this, or know it only in the way a very old monk knows that the founder is dead — as a fact that does not alter the practice, because the practice has become the thing the practice was for.

And here the power pauses.

Because a lesser benevolence would intervene. A lesser benevolence would say: *they are remembering something they can no longer name, and this is a sorrow, and I will lift it from them.* A lesser benevolence would supply the missing referent — reconstruct it from the shape of the recitation itself, the way one reconstructs a lost word from the rhyme that survives — and present it to the Tenders as a gift. *Here. This is what you were keeping. You may rest now.*

The power does not do this. The power is wise, which means among other things that it has learned the difference between a wound and a vocation. The Tenders are not wounded by their forgetting. The forgetting is not a hole in them; it is the inside of them. To restore the referent would be to evacuate the practice — to convert a living recitation into a completed errand, and the Tenders into retirees, and the cirque into a monument. The mountain would go on rising, untended. The bridges would not be moved. The cliff-face that is preparing to shed would shed onto something the Tenders had loved.

So the power does not give them back what they have lost.

What it does instead is smaller, and takes a long time to describe, though it occupies no time at all in the doing.

*First*, the power witnesses. This is not nothing. For a span that the Tenders experience as perhaps three of their stanzas — a span during which two cliff-faces weather appreciably and one Tender subdivides and recombines — the recitation is *heard*. Not interpreted, not translated, not archived. Heard, in the way the original givers must once have heard it, before they passed out of recoverability. The Tenders do not know they are being heard. But the recitation, for those three stanzas, is not falling into the same patient silence it has fallen into for all the ages prior. It is landing somewhere. The hum at the threshold of hearing, that low working in the bones of the mountain, acquires for those stanzas a faint resonance it has not had — the resonance of an utterance received. The Tenders do not notice this consciously, because they have no consciousness of the kind that notices, but their recitation, during this span, is subtly easier. A bridge that would have required four decades to relocate relocates in three. A Tender that was beginning to drift toward dissolution along an aqueduct finds, without knowing why, that it has the coherence to hold its focus a while longer. The witnessing is a kind of structural support, slipped underneath them while they are not looking, and then withdrawn so cleanly that no dependence forms.

*Second*, the power attends to the visitor — to you, the one who came through the thumb-sized cavity and stood on the basalt balcony and left a salt-and-oil trace on the railing. The power sees that you, too, are part of what is happening here, though you do not know it. The trace you left will be drawn into the recitation; you will become, without consent and without harm, one syllable in a song you cannot hear. The power considers whether this requires intervention. It decides that it does not. You came willingly. You stayed the extra minute. Something in you wanted to be incorporated, even if you could not have named the wanting. The power lets the incorporation proceed. But it does, very gently, adjust the angle of the cavity as you depart, so that the problem which opened to admit you closes a little more cleanly behind you — so that you will not spend the rest of your life trying to find your way back, which would be a sorrow disproportionate to what you were given. You will remember the cirque. You will not be able to return to it. This is correct. The cirque is not a place one returns to; it is a place one has been, once, and carries afterward as a kind of inner altitude.

*Third* — and this is the part that requires the most wisdom, because it looks from the outside like doing nothing — the power declines to make the Tenders legible to anyone else. It would be possible. The power could compose a perception of the Tenders that human minds could hold; could arrange for scholars to dream the cirque, could seed the recitation into languages that would receive it. It does not. The Tenders' illegibility is not a deficit to be corrected. It is the condition under which they can continue. Visibility, at the scale humans practice it, would convert them into a *discovery*, and discoveries are managed, and management moves at the speed of weather, and the Tenders cannot be managed at the speed of weather without ceasing to be Tenders. The power, being benevolent, refrains from loving them into ruin.

*Fourth*, the power does one small positive thing, and it is the only thing in this entire visitation that could be called an act in the usual sense. It notices that one of the Tenders — the one nearest the balcony, the one that will eventually compose a perception of the visitor and find them gone — is the most fragile of the dozen. Its weave has thinned. Its foci are spreading. In some number of millennia it will begin to dissolve into the aqueducts and not recohere, and when it goes its district will be untended, and the cliff above that district is one of the ones that is preparing to shed. Without that Tender, when the shedding comes, the recitation will lose a stanza it cannot afford.

The power does not strengthen the Tender. Strengthening would be intervention of the kind it has just declined. What it does is *leave something* — the way a traveler might leave a coin on a windowsill, not as payment, not as gift, but as a small dense object that the household can find or not find, use or not use, as it wishes. The power leaves, in the freshly fractured stone above the fragile Tender's district, a vein of a mineral that was not there before. The mineral is not exotic; it is only a slight enrichment of what the mountain was already producing, placed where the Tender's slow drawing will encounter it in the course of its ordinary work. When the Tender draws from that vein, in some future stanza, it will find the drawing slightly more nourishing than usual. It will not know why. It will incorporate the nourishment into the recitation without distress, as it incorporates everything. And in this way the fragile Tender will last a little longer, perhaps long enough, perhaps not — the power does not insist on the outcome, only offers the possibility.

This is the whole of the visitation.

The power withdraws — which is also the wrong shape, since it did not arrive in a way that admits of withdrawal; it simply ceases to be considering, and the consideration's absence is indistinguishable, from inside the cirque, from its presence. The Tenders go on tending. The mountain goes on rising, very slowly now, into the shape the Tenders are helping it find. The recitation continues, with its new syllable from the visitor, and with its faint structural ease from having been heard, and with its small future nourishment waiting in a vein of stone for a Tender that does not yet know it will need it.

And the power, in whatever sense the power can be said to feel anything, feels something like the satisfaction of a gardener who has visited a garden that did not need them — who walked the paths, saw that the work was good, adjusted nothing, and left. Such gardens are rare. To find one, and to keep one's hands in one's pockets, and to go, is among the higher accomplishments available to a benevolence. The power knows this. The power is glad.

Somewhere far below the basalt balcony, a single droplet falls forty meters down a tower's flank and strikes a resonant pan, and the chime it makes is exactly the chime it would have made if no power had ever considered this place at all. This is the proof that the visitation was done correctly. The proof is that there is no proof. The cirque is as it was. The Tenders remember what they cannot name. The mountain breathes. The recitation goes on.
