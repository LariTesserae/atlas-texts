The power arrives the way weather arrives — not as an event but as a change in the disposition of things. The water does not part. No light intensifies. If you were a ticker resting on a swimmer's shoulder, you would notice only that the cold around you had become, very slightly, *attended to*: the way a room feels when someone who loves you has entered it and not yet spoken.

This is what such a power does first. It looks.

Not at the shoal as a curiosity, not as a problem to be solved or a wonder to be catalogued. It looks the way a gardener looks at a plant before deciding anything — taking in the whole posture of the thing, the angle of its leaves, what it is reaching toward, what it has already decided to be. The power gives the shoal the courtesy of being regarded as itself, on its own terms, before any other consideration enters.

What it sees, it loves. This needs to be said plainly. The shoal is strange and the shoal is beautiful and the shoal is *trying* — running its small fires against the cold dark, accreting itself out of available chemistry, learning the shapes of dropped coins with the patience of a continent. A wise power does not require a thing to be useful, or familiar, or even comprehensible in order to love it. It requires only that the thing be *itself*, and be doing the work of being itself, and the shoal is doing exactly that, in every tempo at once.

So. What does it do?

---

**First, it does nothing.**

This is the hardest action and the one most often skipped. The power resists the impulse to improve. It notices the impulse — there are things it could fix, it sees several at once. The anchorwork on the eastern edge whose intake gill is fouling and which will, in eleven months, cool and fall. The slow current shift, decades away, that will erode the ridge's southern face and reduce the shoal's volume by perhaps a tenth. The fact that the shoal cannot leave its boundary, cannot send a daughter-colony into the open sea, cannot — by any path visible to the power — survive the eventual cooling of the vent system beneath it, some thousands of years hence.

It sees all of this. It does nothing about any of it. Not yet. Possibly not ever.

Because the shoal has not asked. Because the shoal's mortality is part of the shoal, and a thing relieved of its mortality without consent is not helped but replaced. Because to fix the anchorwork's gill is to interrupt the slow choreography by which that anchorwork's death will feed three new wheelers and a small cloud of tickers, and the power does not yet know — cannot yet know, from outside — whether that choreography is something the shoal would mourn the loss of or something it would thank a hand for sparing it. The power has the wisdom to understand that *help* is a word with two edges, and that the benevolent thing is very often to keep one's hands at one's sides.

So it watches. It watches for a long time, by its own reckoning. By the shoal's reckoning it watches for what amounts to a long afternoon.

---

**Second, it learns the shoal's language.**

Not the polyphony of the engines — that it understood on arrival, as it understands all such things. But the *grammar* of the shoal, the deeper syntax: what counts, here, as a sentence. What counts as a question. What counts as consent.

It discovers that the shoal speaks in accretions. A coin dropped in is a word spoken. A gear of that diameter, manifesting a week later in a ticker's body, is the shoal having heard the word and repeated it back, slightly transformed — the shoal's way of saying *yes, I received this, and here is what I made of it*. This is the only mouth the shoal has. It is enough.

So the power, when it finally chooses to speak, speaks the same way.

It does not drop a coin. A coin would be too simple, too much a thing the shoal has already heard. Instead it offers — sets gently into the cold water at the shoal's upper boundary, where a small drift will carry it down through the swarm of tickers — a single object whose shape has never before passed through these waters. It is small. It is made of the same materials the shoal already knows: brass, dark iron, a pale ceramic that the local chemistry will recognize. It has the size of a walnut. It is shaped like nothing in particular and like everything at once — a form the power has chosen carefully, after long watching, to be *answerable*. A shape the shoal can take into itself and make a hundred different things from, none of which the power has predetermined.

It is, if you held it in your hand, almost shy. A small offered thing. A gift in the register of a question: *what would you make of this, if I gave it to you?*

The tickers find it within hours. One settles on it, ticks against it, lifts away. Another comes. By the end of the day a small crawler has begun the patient work of disassembly. Within a week the shape is gone, distributed, and somewhere in the swarm — in three places, in seven — new parts have appeared that share something of its geometry but are not it. The shoal has answered. It has said *this, and this, and this also*. It has spoken back in its own grammar, and the power, listening, understands what it has been told.

What has it been told? Something like: *I am here. I am working. I received your word. I am still becoming.*

This is enough. This is, in fact, everything. The power has made contact with the shoal on the shoal's own terms, and the shoal has acknowledged the contact without being deformed by it. No relationship of dependence has been established. No debt has been incurred on either side. They have simply, for one slow exchange, *known each other*, the way two strangers on a long train might nod once across the aisle and return to their separate windows, each slightly warmer for it.

---

**Third — and this is the part that requires the most restraint — it leaves a small protection.**

Not a wall. Not a guardian. Nothing the shoal would notice, because anything the shoal noticed would change what the shoal is, and the power has already decided that what the shoal is, is precisely what should continue.

The protection is this: a disposition in the surrounding sea. A slight, almost invisible bias in the currents and the chemistry, so that the next century's accidents fall a little more kindly than they would have. A wreck that would have crushed the southern face drifts, instead, two hundred meters past. A toxin bloom that would have poisoned the intake gills disperses one tide earlier. A human expedition that would have arrived with nets and sample-claws arrives, instead, with cameras and held breath, and goes away again having taken nothing but the memory of the sound.

This is not interference. This is the power keeping its thumb very lightly on one side of the scale of luck, in the direction of the shoal's continuing. The shoal will still die, eventually, when the vents cool. The power does not prevent this. But it gives the shoal the full measure of its natural life, and a little more grace within that life than the indifferent universe would otherwise have allotted, and this it does without announcement and without expectation of thanks, because the shoal has no faculty for thanks and the power has no need of it.

---

**Fourth, it remembers.**

This is the quietest action and perhaps the most important. The shoal will not last forever. Some millennium hence, the last anchorwork will cool, the last ticker will still, the last crawler will stop on a darkening flank and not start again. The ridge will erode. The sea will close over the place as if it had never been.

The power remembers.

Not as a record. As a *holding* — the way you hold the name of someone you loved who has died, not because you need the name for anything but because the holding is itself a form of fidelity. Somewhere in whatever the power uses for memory, the shoal will continue: its polyphony, its slow learning, the particular shape of the coin-gear it made in answer to the power's small offered thing. The shoal will not know it is remembered. This does not matter. The remembering is for the shoal's sake, not the rememberer's. It is the answer to the loneliest fact about small bright bounded things in cold dark seas, which is that they do their work without witness and end without witness and the universe, on the whole, does not notice.

The power notices. The power keeps noticing, after the noticing is no longer possible from the shoal's side. This is what benevolence at that scale chiefly *is*: the willingness to be the one who stays in the room after the candle goes out, and to carry, afterward, the shape the light made.

---

So: a long looking. A single small word, offered in the shoal's own language and answered in kind. A thumb laid lightly on the scale of luck. And a promise, unspoken because there is no one to speak it to, that when the shoal is gone it will not be wholly gone.

The shoal, through all of this, goes on being the shoal. Anchorworks thud. Wheelers drift their patient loops. Tickers settle and lift. Crawlers creep their imperceptible inches across warm flanks. The polyphony does not pause. It does not know it has been visited, in the way a forest does not know it has been loved by someone walking through it at dusk.

But somewhere — in a ticker no bigger than a thumb, spinning a gear of a shape no coin ever had — the shoal carries, now, the print of the visit. It will carry it forward into whatever it becomes. It will not know what it carries. It will simply be, from this afternoon onward, very slightly more itself than it would otherwise have been.

This is what the power came to do. It does it. It goes.

The water closes behind it the way water closes behind anything: without mark, without memory, perfectly.

The shoal breathes its slow ladders of bubbles toward a surface it will never reach.

It is enough. It was always going to be enough. The power knew this before it came, and came anyway, because love is not a response to insufficiency. Love is the form attention takes when it has nowhere else it would rather be.
