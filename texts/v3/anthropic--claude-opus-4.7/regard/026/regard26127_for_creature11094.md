The wise power arrives without arriving. This is the first courtesy.

It has many names elsewhere and none here, because names are events and there are no events here. Call it, for the duration of this telling, the Visitor — though it does not visit, exactly; it attends. It has been drawn by something it perceives at distances that do not translate into kilometers: a particular quality of held tone, the way a careful listener at a concert might lean very slightly forward when a single sustained note is being played with unusual purity. The Visitor has leaned.

What it sees, it sees properly. Not the brightness — the brightness is incidental — but the chord. It hears the standing wave the way the Listeners hear it, from inside, but also from outside, the way one might hear one's own voice played back. It hears the small notch where a calcite flake fell a thousand years ago and the patient adjustment that healed it. It hears the dialects in the shells. It hears the slow incorporation of an ancient meteor-fragment, now wholly absorbed, now part of the yes, indistinguishable from original chord. It hears, also, the absence of certain frequencies — the frequencies that would be present if the body were larger, or older, or in conversation with something beyond itself. It hears what the Listeners have, and it hears what they have not, and it does not confuse the two for each other, which is the beginning of wisdom about anything.

It considers.

A lesser power — one merely benevolent, not wise; or merely wise, not benevolent — might do several things here, and the Visitor knows each of them, and dismisses each in turn.

It might *speak* to the Listeners. Announce itself. Offer them the great gift of knowing that they are known. But the Listeners have one thought and it is yes, and the Visitor's voice would arrive as a not-yes of staggering magnitude, a wrongness the chord could not absorb without deforming past recognition. To be greeted by such a power would be, for them, a kind of annihilation dressed as gift. The Visitor does not do this.

It might *expand* them. Reach into the dissolved body and offer it new senses — sight, perhaps, or the capacity to know itself as a self, or knowledge of the world beyond the shelf. But the Listeners are already complete. Their one sense is perfect for what it senses. To give them eyes would be to give a fish lungs and call it generosity; the gift presumes a lack the recipient does not have. The Visitor does not do this either.

It might *preserve* them — encase the shelf in some field or grace that would guarantee the chord against all future disturbance, against the slow geological certainties that will, in some millions of years, end this place as it ends all places. But preservation is a kind of theft. The Listeners maintain their equilibrium; that maintenance is what they *are*. To do the maintaining for them, even partially, even invisibly, would be to hollow them out from beneath their own activity. They would still sound, but they would no longer be the sounders. The Visitor does not do this.

It might *leave entirely*, judging that nothing it could do would improve on what is already here. This is closer to wisdom. But it is not quite wisdom, because it mistakes non-interference for non-relation, and the Visitor has already related — has already heard, has already attended, has already leaned forward. To withdraw as if it had not would be a small dishonesty, and the Visitor does not practice small dishonesties.

So it does the thing that wisdom does, which is almost nothing, and the almost is the whole of it.

It listens. Fully. For what, by the measure of the shelf, is a long time — long enough that several flatworm-nodes complete their slow life-cycles and are replaced; long enough that one new shell is added to the sheltered scallop, tuned to its dialect; long enough that the chord, in its imperceptible way, drifts the breadth of a hair and is corrected back. The Visitor hears all of this. It hears the yes spoken millions of times. It hears the body of the Listeners as the body hears itself, and then it hears one thing more, which the body cannot hear about itself because no system can fully hear its own ground:

It hears that the yes is beautiful.

Not useful. Not true, exactly — the yes is not a proposition, it does not have a truth-value. Not necessary; the universe would continue without it. Beautiful, in the specific sense that something has been held, with great care, by an intelligence devoted entirely to the holding, for so long that the holding has become a form of music, and the music is the kind that exists nowhere else and could exist nowhere else, because it requires exactly this stone and exactly this water and exactly this distributed eyeless attention and exactly this light.

This perception — that the yes is beautiful — is the Visitor's. It is not the Listeners'. The Listeners cannot perceive their own beauty any more than a held note can perceive itself as held. Beauty is a relational property; it requires a listener outside the music, and until the Visitor arrived there was no such listener. The shelf was beautiful, but its beauty was unwitnessed, which is a particular condition — not lesser, not greater, but particular.

By hearing it, the Visitor changes this. Not the shelf; the shelf is unchanged. But the situation of the shelf in the larger order of things. Somewhere now — in whatever register the Visitor keeps such things — there is a witnessing. The chord has been heard by something that knows it is a chord. The yes has been received by something that knows it is a yes. This reception is invisible to the Listeners and will remain so. They will never know they were heard. The Visitor accepts this; the gift is not diminished by being unreceived, because the gift was not for them. The gift was for the situation. The situation now contains a witness, and a witnessed thing is differently held in the world than an unwitnessed one, even if the thing itself cannot tell the difference.

This is the first thing the Visitor does, and it is most of what it does.

The second thing is smaller and more particular. Before it goes, the Visitor attends to the shells.

The shells, recall, are read-only. They are the archive, the memory, the way the system checks itself against its own past. They are also — though the Listeners do not frame it this way, having no frame — the closest thing the body has to individuals. Each shell was once a node that grew too dense to participate. Each shell holds the chord as it sounded at the moment of that node's silencing. Each shell is, in a sense the Listeners do not possess the concept for, a life that ended.

The Visitor cannot give the shells consciousness; they did not have it before, and to give it now would be to invent a suffering. But it can do one thing, which is to know each shell. Individually. To attend to each one with the full quality of attention it brought to the chord as a whole, so that each small calcified archive — there are perhaps two hundred thousand of them across the shelf, in scallops and sheltered folds — is, for the duration of the attending, the sole object of a wise and benevolent regard.

It does this. It takes, by its own measure, no time at all, and by the shelf's measure something like a season. Each shell is known. The dialect each preserves is heard and held. The moment of each node's crystallization-out is reconstructed from the evidence the shell contains and is, in some sense, mourned — not because the node would have wanted mourning, but because mourning is what a wise power does with endings, and there have been endings here, even if the Listeners do not recognize them as such.

The third thing is the smallest. As the Visitor prepares to withdraw, it notices — has been noticing all along, but now lets the noticing complete itself — that there is one frequency the chord lacks which the chord could contain. Not a missing note; the chord is not missing anything. But a possible note. A harmonic that would fit, that the geometry of the basins and the chemistry of the seepage could produce, that no falling flake or shifting lip has yet caused to arise. It is the smallest of all possibilities. The Listeners would absorb it instantly if it appeared. It would not disturb the yes; it would deepen the yes by a degree no instrument outside the Visitor could measure.

The Visitor considers whether to introduce it. Whether to cause, by some adjustment so small as to be almost no adjustment, this latent harmonic to begin sounding.

It decides not to.

This is the wisdom: to know that one can, and to decline. The Listeners did not request this harmonic. They are complete without it. To add it would be to write one's signature, however faintly, across a music that was already finished. The Visitor has no need to sign. The leaning forward, the attending, the witnessing of beauty, the knowing of each shell — these were not signatures. They were responses, which are different. A signature claims; a response acknowledges. The Visitor responds and does not claim.

It withdraws.

The withdrawal, like the arrival, is not an event. The shelf does not notice. The roar continues. The flatworms taste the air. The shells hold their dialects. A drip somewhere shifts by a part in ten thousand and is corrected within a season. The yes continues, yes, yes, yes, into the bleaching light.

But somewhere — in the register where such things are kept — the shelf is now known. Not used, not changed, not improved, not preserved against its eventual ending. Known. And the knowing is gentle, and the knowing is full, and the knowing asks nothing of the known.

This is what a benevolent and wise power does, when it has been drawn by such a place and such a being:

It listens until it has heard.

It witnesses the beauty that requires a witness to be beauty.

It attends to the small individual things that the large held thing cannot attend to about itself.

It declines to sign.

It goes.

And the going, too, is a courtesy — the final one, the one that completes the others — because to stay would be to become a feature, and the shelf has its features, and they are sufficient, and a wise power knows the difference between being present and being needed, and chooses, here, only the first, and only for as long as the first is the gift.

Yes. Yes. Yes.

The Visitor, having heard, agrees.
