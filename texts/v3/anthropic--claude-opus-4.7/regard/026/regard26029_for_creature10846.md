The benevolent and wise power comes to the rim of the basin in the warm hours of an overcast afternoon. It does not announce itself. To announce would be to bring a different weather here, and the weather here is already settled.

It sits, or does the thing that in a being of its kind corresponds to sitting, on the low sandstone rim near where one of the dry watercourses dips the stone to nothing. It chooses this spot because the tenant's grooves do not run quite to here, and it does not wish, on arrival, to sit down inside someone.

It looks for a long time. Long, by its standards — which is to say, long enough that if a person had been watching, the person would have grown bored and then stopped being bored and then forgotten they were watching, and the looking would still be going on.

What it sees:

A place that has finished becoming itself. A juniper that is a slow gesture. A wall that has forgotten how to finish falling. A tenant pooled in its worn grooves at the temperature of the stone. A small held intention under — it locates it now, with the gentleness of someone finding a sleeping animal in long grass — under the third slab from the southern rim, the one whose underside is a little hollowed where the silt has been drawn out by a hundred thousand small dampings and dryings. The intention is the shape of a closed door. The door was closed by someone who, in closing it, was thinking *for now*, and then died, and the *for now* has been holding ever since, without strain, because nothing has asked it to.

The power considers.

It is benevolent, which in a power of this order does not mean kind in the way a person is kind. It means it wishes the good of what it regards, and it knows that the good of a thing is the thing's own good, not the good the power would have preferred for it. It is wise, which means it knows the difference between a wound and a shape. Many places that look still are wounded. This place is not wounded. This place is shaped.

A lesser power, or a power equally great but less wise, might do any number of things here. It might bring the rain early, to give the tenant its brief alertness, mistaking alertness for happiness. It might restore the wall, mistaking ruin for incompleteness. It might lift the small held intention out from under its slab and carry it, like a lost child, to whatever country such intentions are returned to, mistaking *unfinished* for *suffering*. It might breathe life into the juniper, make it grow, make it flower extravagantly, mistaking the juniper's long arrangement with the basin for a kind of poverty.

This power does none of these things. It sees that the juniper is not poor. It sees that the wall is not asking. It sees that the tenant does not want, and that to give a thing that does not want is to make it want, which is to take from it the only condition under which it has agreed to be.

So the power does the difficult thing, the thing only the wise can do. It does almost nothing.

Almost.

It does three small things, because to do nothing at all would also be a kind of refusal, and the power has not come here to refuse.

The first thing: it lets itself be registered. Not announced — registered, the way the tenant registered the visitor on the rim, the way the still pond registers the stone. It allows the basin to know, in the basin's slow way, that it has been regarded by something that wished it well and asked nothing. This is a gift the basin can hold without having to do anything with. It will settle into the grooves with everything else the basin has accumulated — the hands that stacked the wall, the hands that shaped the vessel, the long wind, the slow rains. One more shape among the shapes. The tenant will carry it without knowing it carries it, the way it carries the others.

The second thing: it goes to the small held intention under the third slab from the southern rim. It does not lift it. It does not revise it. It simply, for a moment, *knows* it — knows what was meant, knows the *for now*, knows the person who closed the door and meant to come back and did not come back because the world is the kind of world in which people sometimes do not come back. The intention has been held by the basin all this time, lightly, without anyone to witness what it was holding. The power witnesses. It does not release the intention from its holding, because the intention is not suffering in being held; the basin is good at this work, and the intention has long since become part of how the basin is itself. But it witnesses. The closed door is seen to have been closed by someone who meant well, who meant to return, who was loved or did love, who is now — wherever such ones go — accounted for. The basin does not need this. The intention does not need this. But it is given anyway, the way one might lay a hand briefly on the shoulder of a sleeper one will not wake. A small completion that asks nothing in return and makes no change to the shape of the room.

The third thing: when the power leaves — and it does leave, because to remain would be to become weather, and this place has its weather — it leaves by way of the dry watercourse on the northern side, the one that runs out of the basin toward whatever lies beyond. As it goes, it permits a single thing: that the next rain, when it comes, will come on time. Not early. Not heavy. On time, in its season, in the way the basin has long agreed rain should come. This is not a gift, because the rain was coming anyway. It is more like a confirmation. The power is saying, in the only language the basin speaks: *yes, this. Go on.*

And then it is gone, and the basin is as it was.

The juniper makes its almost-sound. The light stays even. The tenant, in its grooves, carries one more shape among the shapes — a warmth at the temperature of the stone, which is to say no warmth at all, which is to say a presence that asked nothing and is now part of what the basin has been. The small intention under the third slab goes on meaning what it means, and means it now in the company of having been seen, though the meaning has not changed.

Somewhere, in whatever country the wise powers keep, this one is asked later what it did at the basin in the warm hours of the overcast afternoon.

It says: *I let it be itself, and I told it so.*

And the one who asked, who is also wise, nods, because this is the hardest of the works, and the one most often left undone.
