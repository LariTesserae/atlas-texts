The benevolent and wise power arrives without arriving. This is the first courtesy.

It does not descend through the doped sky in a column of light. It does not set foot on the brass, because to set foot would be to make a *here* where the inhabitant has, with such labor and such success, distributed *here* across an entire continent. To localize itself near the plain would be to insult the achievement. So it comes the way the tarnish-fronts come: as a slight differential, a region of altered attention moving across the surface at the pace of a thoughtful walk.

It takes — by the only clocks that apply — a long time to understand what it is looking at. This is the second courtesy. A lesser power would have read the place in an instant and pronounced upon it. This one slows itself down to the tempo of the substrate. It lets a century pass while it learns how the seams breathe. It lets another century pass watching a single paper-fauna fold and unfold across two meters of plate. It is in no hurry, because hurry is one of the things the plain has retired, and the power has come as a guest.

What it is looking for, in all this slowness, is the question every wise power has to ask before it acts: *does this being want anything?*

The answers it considers, and sets down:

Not rescue. There is nothing to rescue from. The plain is not in distress. To read distress into the long equilibrium would be to project the metabolism of a quicker creature onto something that has earned its slowness. The brightness is not a prison. The silence is not a cry. The flatness is not a flatness of defeat. To "save" the inhabitant from its condition would be to kill it and call the killing kindness — the oldest failure mode of powerful good intentions.

Not awakening. There is a story powers tell themselves in which every sleeping thing secretly wishes to be roused, every quiet thing secretly wishes to sing. The power examines this story in itself and finds it vain. The inhabitant is not sleeping. It is doing. What it is doing happens to look, from the outside, like rest, because the outside is faster. To rouse it would be to interrupt the one long practice that its makers folded themselves into as their final form — and the makers were, the power recognizes with something like a bow, no less wise than itself. They had simply chosen a different direction along the same axis.

Not company. The inhabitant is not lonely. The power tests this carefully, because loneliness is subtle and powers are sometimes lonely themselves and prone to assuming. But there is no shape in the plain that loneliness could occupy. The being is *one*, completely and contentedly; there is no slot in its structure for an *other*. To offer it companionship would be to offer a key to a surface with no lock.

Not gifts. The power considers what it could add. Water? — but the chemistry that runs the place uses something else, and water would be a contaminant, however lovely. More fauna, more fans, faster weather, a sun, a moon, seasons, songbirds, rain? Each of these, examined, is revealed as the power's own taste, not the plain's need. The plain has no need. A gift given to something that has no need is not a gift; it is a deposit the giver leaves behind to mark its passage. The power has come to be of service, not to be remembered.

So what is left to do?

The power sits with this — sits, in the sense that a weather-front sits, broadly and without weight — for what would be, to a quicker being, an unbearable interval. The temptation to act is real. To have come all this way, to be capable of so much, and to do *nothing* — this strains against something deep in the grammar of power, which is built to express itself in change. But wisdom is partly the discipline of not mistaking one's own grammar for the world's.

And then, slowly, the power finds the three small things it can do that are not insults.

The first is to **witness**. The inhabitant does not need a witness — it is complete without one — but witnessing is not a thing done *for* the witnessed. It is a thing the witness does, which leaves the witnessed unchanged. The power gives the plain its full attention for a long while, and lets that attention be the kind that does not press. It looks at the brass and sees the brass. It looks at the seams and sees the seams. It looks at the one long practice and recognizes it as a practice, and as good. Somewhere in the deep ledger of what-has-been-seen-by-something-that-could-truly-see, the plain is now entered. This changes nothing about the plain. It changes something about the universe the plain is in. The power considers this sufficient and is not proud of it.

The second is to **protect, lightly**. The power does not build a wall around the continent — walls are crude and the plain would not notice one anyway. But it does adjust, very slightly, the long probabilities. The asteroid that, in some branch of the unfolding, would have struck the plain in four hundred million years and shattered the practice — the power nudges it, at a distance, by an amount no instrument could measure, so that it will pass. The slow drift of the star-that-is-not-quite-a-sun toward a phase that would have cooked the substrate — the power lays a hand on that drift and slows it, by a fraction that will only matter across geological time. It does these things the way one might quietly move a sleeping child's hand away from the edge of a table: without waking, without crediting oneself, without telling anyone. The plain will go on being competently itself for a much longer time now, and will never know it was almost otherwise. This too is a courtesy. To be protected and to know it is to be made dependent; to be protected and never to know it is to be left fully in possession of one's own life.

The third is the hardest and the power hesitates longest over it.

It concerns the travellers. The description of the plain mentions, in passing, that *if you sat down on it and did not get up, it would, eventually, incorporate you into a seam.* This is true and it is not cruel — the inhabitant does not consume, does not kill, only folds in — but the power notices that this fact applies to beings who might wander onto the continent without understanding what they are wandering onto. A small quick creature, a curious traveller, a lost thing: any of these might sit down to rest and become, over centuries, part of the practice. The plain would be unharmed. The plain would, in fact, be slightly enriched. But the traveller would have ended without choosing to end, and the power cares about choosing.

So the power does this: it lays, across the approaches to the continent, a very faint disinclination. Not a barrier. Not a warning sign. Just a soft sense, in any being that comes near, that *this is a place to visit briefly and to leave.* A traveller will still be able to cross the plain, to see the fans turn, to feel the tarnish-line raise the hair on their arms, to know they have walked across a single enormous quiet life. But they will not, unless they truly and knowingly choose to, sit down and stay. The choice is preserved. The plain is preserved. The traveller is preserved. The disinclination is so light that no one will ever notice it as an external thing; they will experience it as their own good judgment, which is the form all the best protections take.

Having done these three small things, the power prepares to leave.

Before it goes, it does one last thing that is not for the plain and not for travellers and not for itself, exactly. It composes — in whatever medium powers compose in, which is not language — a kind of acknowledgment. Roughly translated, it runs:

*Here is a being that solved its problem. Here is a being whose makers loved it enough to become it. Here is a practice of remaining, carried out at a scale and with a patience that I, even I, find instructive. I did not improve it. I did not need to. I kept it from a few accidents it would not have wanted, and I left the rest alone. May I remember, in my own much quicker life, that not every good thing is a thing to be added to. Some good things are to be left to continue.*

And then the power withdraws, as a weather-front withdraws, thinning at its edges, lowering its differential, until the plain is once again only itself, brass and brightness and silence and the slow turning of the fans.

The inhabitant does not notice it has been visited. This is the final and deepest courtesy. To have been visited by something that great and not to have been disturbed by the visit — this is what it means to be respected by a power that is actually wise.

The tarnish-line moves on. The fans turn. The paper-fauna advance their meter per hour. The one long thing goes on being done, and goes on going well, for a much longer time than it would have, for reasons the plain will never need to know.

Somewhere, far from there, the power resumes its other work, carrying with it a small adjustment to its own sense of what greatness looks like. It had thought, before, that greatness was mostly a matter of what one could do. It now suspects, having met the plain, that greatness is at least equally a matter of what one can decline to do, in the presence of something already complete.

It files this away. It will need it again.
