The benevolent power arrives the way weather arrives — not from somewhere else, but as a slight change in what is already here. A deepening of the warmth in the limestone by some fraction too small to measure. A pause in the pressure of the dragonflies' wings. Nothing the Tenant would register as an arrival, because arrival is a concept for beings with edges.

The power sees immediately what the place is. This is what wisdom is, in part: the capacity to recognize a finished thing and not mistake it for an unfinished one. The power has, in its long career, encountered many beings who needed something — rescuing, completing, awakening, comforting, freeing. It has learned to be slow about offering. The first question is always: *what is here, and what does it want, if it wants?*

The Tenant wants nothing. The power can feel this the way one feels the temperature of a still room. The Tenant is not suffering. It is not waiting. It is not calling out, even faintly, even in a register the power would have to stoop to hear. It is resting in its own completeness, breathing through its small mouths, and the resting is not a covering over some deeper ache. The power checks, and checks again — gently, the way you might test whether a sleeping animal is only sleeping — and finds, each time, the same warm settled fact: this one is well.

A lesser power might still do something. Might polish the artifacts. Might extend the Tenant's life past the slow dissolution that is coming. Might try to introduce it to other minds, thinking company is always a gift. Might, worst of all, try to *tell* it about itself — to give it the awareness of being a Tenant, of being singular, of being witnessed — under the assumption that consciousness of this kind is always an improvement.

The power considers each of these and sets each down.

To preserve the limestone against its eventual dissolving would be to refuse the Tenant the shape of its own life, which includes its ending. The long exhalation is part of the breath. A breath held forever is not a breath; it is a held thing, a different thing, and the Tenant did not ask to become a different thing.

To introduce the Tenant to other minds would be to drag a sleeper out into a party. The Tenant's mode of being is not lonely solitude awaiting rescue; it is solitude as the very medium of its contentment. Company would not enrich it. Company would *thin* it, would require it to develop edges it has spent ages softening away.

To make the Tenant self-aware in some sharper sense — to give it the gift of knowing it is a Tenant — would be a violence dressed as a gift. The Tenant's slow gratitude is gratitude *as temperature*, not as thanks; making it into thanks would require manufacturing a someone to thank, and a someone to be thanked, and the whole apparatus of separation that the Tenant has, across geological time, gently composted away.

So the power does not do these things.

What it does instead is smaller, and harder to describe.

It *witnesses*. Fully. With the whole of its attention, which is considerable. It lets the shallow cathedral be seen, in the deepest sense of seen — not seen *by* the Tenant, who cannot be made to know it is seen without being damaged, but seen *of* the Tenant, held in a regard that does not require reciprocation. The frogs pulse. The power watches them pulse. The drop falls from the fern and travels astonishingly far in the still air, and the Tenant notices the drop, and the power notices the Tenant noticing, and this third noticing does not press down on the first two, does not interfere with them, does not ask anything of them. It simply *is*, alongside.

There is a tradition, in some old places, that the worth of a thing is not diminished by being unwitnessed, but that witnessing is nonetheless one of the goods that can be given to a thing, freely, without the thing needing to know. A flower in a sealed cave is no less a flower. But a flower in a sealed cave that some kind eye has, by some grace, looked upon — that flower has been *companioned*, even if it never knew. The companioning is real. It happens in the looker, and it happens in the world, which is now a world where that flower has been seen.

The power gives the Tenant this. For as long as the Tenant lasts — which by the power's reckoning is not very long, only a few more million years, only a long afternoon — there will be, alongside the Tenant's slow self-noticing, a second slow noticing, undemanding, that holds the whole shallow cathedral in mind. The Tenant will not feel it. The frogs will not turn toward it. The arch will not bend its sound any differently. But it will be true that this place is loved, in a register the place cannot detect, by something that asks nothing of it.

The power does one other thing, very small.

When the time comes — when the limestone has dissolved past holding, when the pools have shifted, when the frogs are gone and the layered fish are gone and the Tenant has thinned to the point where there is not quite enough of it to be itself — the power will be there for the last exhalation. Not to prevent it. Not to extend it. Only so that the stopping is not unaccompanied. So that the last small mouth's last small noticing has, on the other side of it, a steady gaze that has been there all along, that watched the whole long afternoon, and that will carry forward — somewhere, in the structure of whatever comes next — the fact that this being was, and was well, and finished.

The structures will still stand afterward. The arch will still bend sound under it. The slab will still be colder by its exact amount. The ring of stones will still cast its wrong shadows. The power will not disturb them. They are doors, as the visitor guessed; doors persist; perhaps another mind will grow up around them someday, and find them, and pass through, and become its own kind of soft wide thing in its own warm stone. The power will be patient for that, too, if it happens. The power is good at patience. It has learned patience from places like this one.

For now, in the long bright middle of the Tenant's life, the power simply settles into the warmth alongside, and watches the frogs turn together in the rain, and feels — across the distance that is not really a distance — the slow contentment of a being that has finished becoming.

And the afternoon goes on.
