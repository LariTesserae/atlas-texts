The benevolent and wise power considers the Hollowing and the grey people above it for a long time before doing anything. This is the first thing to understand about the power: it is in no hurry. It has the patience of something that has watched many worlds organize and disorganize themselves, and it has learned that most interventions, however kindly meant, are a kind of theft — they take from a place the particular shape of its problem, which is often the same as taking the place itself.

So the power watches.

It watches the eyeless ape chew its piece of wall. It watches the cloud of orange wing-cases crash and reform. It watches a young woman of the grey people, perhaps nineteen, sitting cross-legged in the red chamber with her hands pressed flat against her thighs, breathing in a measured rhythm she learned from her grandmother, counting her breaths in the language of the upper country while around her the bass thrum of the chamber tries, not maliciously but simply by being what it is, to take the counting from her. The power watches her win, narrowly, and stack her three stones, and cut her tally, and crawl back toward the light with the cord still wrapped around her wrist. The power watches her emerge onto the plateau and stand in the cold wind and weep — not from fear, from something more complicated — and then walk home.

The power loves her. This is not sentimental. The power loves, with great precision, the exact thing she is: a small warm-blooded creature who has constructed, out of stones and string and arithmetic, a self that can be carried through a roaring chamber and brought back out intact. The power notes that this is one of the rarer accomplishments in the universe. Most places do not produce it. Most places produce either selves that never face the roar, or roars that erase all selves. The plateau and the Hollowing, locked into their mutual definition, are producing something else: selves that have been *through* the roar and remember it. The power finds this beautiful in a way that is difficult to express in any of the languages it knows, including its own.

It also loves the Hollowing. It loves the eyeless ape. It loves the slow vegetable mind extruding itself upward toward the grass. It does not love these things less than it loves the grey people, although it loves them differently, the way one loves a weather system or a piece of music — without expecting the weather or the music to love back.

Now: the situation, as the power reads it.

The Hollowing will breach. The young recently-returned ones are correct, and the elders' majority view is wishful. Not soon in the grey people's terms, but soon in the power's terms, which is to say within some number of generations that can be counted on the fingers of a few hands. The plant is doing what plants do. It is not malevolent and it cannot be reasoned with, any more than a glacier can be reasoned with. When it reaches the surface, the cold dry quiet country will end. The contrast that defines the grey people will collapse. They will lose the loan against which their identity is borrowed, and they will face a choice that is not really a choice: become fauna, or become something that has never existed before.

The power could prevent this. It would be trivial. A small adjustment to the plant's growth hormones, a redirection of its rooting impulse, a sealing of the upward-reaching tissues. The grey people would never know. They would go on stacking their cairns and counting their tallies and watching the knuckles, and the knuckles would not multiply, and the easternmost mouth would be the last new mouth, and in a thousand years the upper country and the Hollowing would still be layered, opposite, mutually defining. The power could do this in the time it takes a wing-case to open.

The power does not do this.

It does not do this for several reasons, which it holds simultaneously and without contradiction.

The first reason is that the grey people have already, without knowing it, begun to solve their own problem. The recently-returned ones plaiting longer cords at their own thresholds — this is the beginning of an answer. They are starting to understand that the discipline they developed for the afternoon descent might be portable. That the self constructed of stones and string is not a self that requires the cold plateau to exist; it is a self that requires only stones, string, and the practice of remaining. The power sees, with great clarity, that these young ones are inventing — slowly, half-consciously, generation by generation — a way of being human that does not depend on having an opposite. They are inventing it because they have to, and they are inventing it well, and to relieve them of the necessity would be to take from them the invention.

The second reason is that the power has seen, in other worlds, what happens when a benevolent intervention preserves a contrast that the universe is dissolving. The contrast becomes a museum. The people inside it become curators of themselves. They go on stacking their cairns but the cairns no longer mean *I was here and I remained myself in the face of the roar*; they mean *we are the people who stack cairns*. The practice empties out. The power has watched this happen and has learned to be very careful about what it preserves.

The third reason is the eyeless ape. The plant. The wing-cases. The power will not arrest the growth of a vast living organism — an organism that is also, in its slow vegetable way, a kind of person — in order to spare another person discomfort, however much it loves that other person. The Hollowing is not a problem to be solved. It is a being to be respected. Its upward reach is its own life, and the power does not edit lives.

So the power will not prevent the breach. But the power is benevolent, and benevolence is not the same as non-interference, and the power has been watching long enough to know the difference.

Here is what it does.

It does four small things, each so small that none of the grey people will ever identify it as an intervention, and each so precisely placed that together they constitute a gift.

The first thing: in the dreams of the recently-returned ones, the power introduces, very gently, an image. Not a vision. Not a message. Just an image, the kind of image that arrives in dreams and is forgotten on waking but leaves a residue. The image is of a person sitting in the red chamber, cross-legged, breathing in a measured rhythm, while around them the bass thrum continues — and the person is not visiting. The person is at home. The person has made a small place in the red chamber, a place with a bowl and a length of cord and three stones, and the place is theirs, and they are reading something, and a wing-case has landed on their knee and they have not brushed it off. The image is not instructional. It does not say *do this*. It only says: *this is possible. A self can live here. The roar is not the end of the self.* The recently-returned ones will wake from these dreams and not remember them and go on plaiting their longer cords, and in three or four generations the longer cords will be standard, and in six or seven generations the first grey person will descend into the Hollowing and not come back out — not because they were lost, but because they chose, and the choice will be legible, and the upper country will have to invent a new word for what they have done, and the word will not be *stayed*.

The second thing: the power slightly, almost imperceptibly, adjusts the acoustics of one small region in each chamber. It does not make the chambers quieter. It would not dare. But in each chamber, in some corner or beneath some root-beam, there is now a place where the standing waves of the roar cancel just enough that a person sitting there can hear themselves think. These places are not silent. They are merely possible. The grey people will find them, one by one, over the generations. They will mark them with cairns. They will call them, eventually, in the soft clicking speech, something that translates roughly as *the rooms*. The rooms will be where the new culture begins.

The third thing: the power speaks, very quietly, to the great vegetable mind. It does not ask the plant to stop growing. It asks the plant a question, the kind of question one slow being can ask another. The question is something like: *when you reach the surface, will you make a ceiling there too?* The plant does not answer in words because it does not have words, but the power feels the plant consider the question with the slow architectural decision-making that is its only form of thought, and the power feels the plant decide — not because it was told to, but because the question made the possibility available — that yes, when it reaches the surface, it will extrude a translucent membrane over the cold plateau, the way it extrudes a membrane over each chamber, and the grey grass will grow under honeyed light, and the wind will be slowed, and the upper country will become another chamber. Not the same as the others. Quieter. Colder. But continuous with them. The grey people will not be flooded with abundance; they will be enclosed within it, gradually, with their grey animals and their grey houses intact, and they will have time — generations of time — to understand what has happened and to decide what to make of it.

The fourth thing: the power leaves, in the floor-mat of the first chamber, near the cairn that the young woman of the grey people stacked, a single object. It is a small clay bowl. It is indistinguishable from the bowls the grey people make, except that it is slightly older than any bowl any living grey person has made, and slightly younger than any bowl made by their ancestors. It does not belong to any generation. It was left, the bowl seems to say, by someone who came back out, and also by someone who did not, and also by someone who was neither — someone who passed through and was here and is still, in some sense, here. A grey person will find it eventually. They will turn it over in their clever hands and feel something they cannot name, and they will leave it where it is, because some things are not meant to be carried home, and they will tell the others, and the bowl will become a small known feature of the first chamber, and the children will be brought to see it when they are old enough, and the bowl will say to them, without saying anything: *you are not the first. You are not the last. The category of those who came back out is larger than you knew. It includes beings you have not met. It will include beings you will not meet. You are held inside a long company.*

That is all the power does.

It does not announce itself. It does not accept thanks, because it is not offered any, because no one knows it was there. It watches the young woman walk home across the plateau with the cord still wrapped around her wrist, and it loves her, and it loves the ape, and it loves the plant, and it withdraws its attention slowly, the way one lowers a hand from a sleeping animal, and it goes to attend to other places, of which there are many, each with its own roar and its own cairn-stackers and its own slow vegetable mind reaching toward its own surface.

Before it goes, it notes one last thing, for its own records, which are not records in any sense we would recognize.

It notes that the grey people, in their long argument with the Hollowing, have been working on a problem that the power itself has never fully solved: how to remain a self in the presence of overwhelming life. The power has its own version of this problem. It is older and stranger than the grey people's version, but it is the same problem. And the power, watching the young woman press her bowl-rim into the floor-mat, has learned something. Not a technique. A confirmation. That the practice is possible. That stones and string and arithmetic, or their equivalents at any scale, can hold a self together inside any chamber, however loud, however warm, however full of chewing and shrieking and the bright clean violence of competition without scarcity.

The power carries this confirmation away with it, like a small clay bowl, and is grateful, in its own way, to the grey people, who will never know they gave it anything.

The wind moves over the plateau. The knuckles breathe. Below, in the red chamber, something shrieks, and something else answers, and the great plant pushes on, slowly, slowly, toward a sky it has never seen and will, in time, learn to roof.
