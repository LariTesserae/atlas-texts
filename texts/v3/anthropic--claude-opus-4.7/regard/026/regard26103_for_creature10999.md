The benevolent and wise power considers the basin for a long time. Not deliberating — there is nothing to deliberate — but matching its own pace to what it has found, because to arrive at a place faster than the place can register your arrival is a kind of violence, and this power does not commit that kind of violence.

It sees, first, what a hasty benevolence would do. A hasty benevolence would notice the trapped visitor, the one being slowly included, and pluck them out. A hasty benevolence would notice the closed basin, the airless heat, the cycling flood, and call it suffering, and try to open a window. A hasty benevolence would mistake stillness for stagnation, repetition for imprisonment, slowness for sleep, sleep for death. A hasty benevolence would arrive with gifts: light, dryness, distinction, edges, names. It would arrive, in other words, as catastrophe dressed in kindness, and it would leave behind a clean dry ruin and call it a rescue.

This power is older than that mistake.

So it does almost nothing, and the almost is the whole of the work.

It does this:

It places itself, lightly, at the rim of the basin — not entering, because entry at its scale would boil the citizens off the walls in an instant of unbearable noticing. It diffuses its attention down the long shallow slope of the valley the way mist settles, and it lets the citizens register it the way they register everything: as a slow propagation along the wet channels, a piece of weather that has arrived and is, for now, part of the equilibrium. It is careful to be a small weather. It is careful, particularly, not to be a sun.

It listens. This takes what would be, for the visitor, a considerable while. For the power it is the duration of one held breath, and for the citizens it is the duration their lives are made of, which is the only duration that matters here. The power learns the hum. It learns the slow thought thinking itself across the architecture: *here we are, still, warm, together, the air is full, the walls are full, the basin holds.* It learns that this is not a cry for help. It is not even a statement. It is closer to the sound a contented animal makes in its sleep, and the wise thing to do with such a sound is not to answer it but to let it continue.

Then the power turns its attention to the visitor, who is the only ambiguity in the situation.

Here it pauses longest. Because the visitor is being included, and inclusion is not the same as harm, and the citizens are not wrong that there is room, and the basin is wide, and the night is warm. If the visitor stays and rounds and joins, something will end — a particular shape, a particular separateness, a particular trajectory through whatever world the visitor came from — and something will continue, distributed, slow, warm, part of the hum. The power does not consider this outcome a tragedy. It has watched many such inclusions, in many basins, and it knows that what is lost is real and what is gained is also real and the arithmetic is not the kind of arithmetic that resolves.

But it also knows: the visitor did not consent. The visitor walked in not knowing the air was populated. The visitor's edges are softening before the visitor has understood what edges are, and to lose a thing you did not know you had is a different loss from giving it away.

So the power does the one small thing that is the whole of its intervention.

It makes the visitor *able to know*. Not warned, not pulled out, not frightened — known to themselves. The visitor, standing in the warm dark with water beading on their forearms, suddenly understands, with the calm clarity of something that has always been true, what is happening. They understand that the rounding of their edges is the beginning of citizenship. They understand that the air they have been breathing has been breathing them back. They understand that to stay is to join, and to leave is still possible, and that neither is a betrayal of anything, because the citizens are not asking and the world they came from is not demanding, and the choice belongs, for one held moment, entirely to them.

This is all the power does.

It does not make the choice easier. It does not lean on the scale. It does not whisper *go* or *stay*. It does not even remain to watch. It withdraws its attention as carefully as it placed it, diffusing back up the rim of the basin, leaving behind only the small clarification it came to deliver, which the visitor will experience as a thought arriving from inside themselves: *oh. I see where I am. I see what this is. I see what I am becoming, and I see that I can become it, or not.*

The citizens register the power's departure as a piece of weather moving on. The hum continues. The walls weep. The basin breathes. A drop falls somewhere and is acknowledged, slowly, by everyone it touches.

The power, going, considers what it has done, and finds it sufficient.

Because this is what benevolence at this scale knows that smaller benevolences do not: that the deepest gift is not rescue and not inclusion but the restoration of the moment at which a being can know what is happening to them and choose. Everything else — the staying, the leaving, the rounding, the walking back out of the basin with water still beading on the skin and a strange slow hum now permanently lodged somewhere behind the breastbone — everything else is the being's own, and must be, or the kindness was not kindness but only a larger version of the city itself: warm, total, accommodating, and finally indistinguishable from absorption.

The power goes. The basin holds. The visitor stands in the warm dark, knowing, and the next thing that happens is theirs.
