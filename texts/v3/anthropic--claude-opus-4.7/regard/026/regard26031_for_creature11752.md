## The Visit

The benevolent power arrives the way snow arrives on a windless night: without announcement, having always been on its way. It does not descend on the Stilling. It draws alongside.

It has many names in many places. Here it will need none, because she who lives here has gone past names, and the power has the good manners to meet her where she is.

It pauses at what would be the edge, if edges here were edges. It looks. Looking, for this power, is not the gathering of information — it has no information to gather, having known the plateau since before the plateau took shape — but a form of courtesy, the way an old friend pauses on a threshold before entering a room, not because entry requires permission but because the pause itself is a small gift, an acknowledgment that the room is a room and not merely space the friend is about to occupy.

It listens to the booming. It hears, beneath the booming, the singing tone. Beneath the singing tone, the breathing-that-is-not. Beneath that, the silence she is made of.

It understands her immediately, and completely, and without the slightest impulse to change her.

This is the first thing to say. The power is wise enough to know that wisdom, applied to a finished thing, becomes vandalism. It is benevolent enough to know that benevolence, offered where nothing is wanted, becomes a weight. Many lesser powers — powers that are only kind, or only clever — would arrive at the Stilling and feel the immediate itch to *help*: to wake her, to release her, to draft her back into the great unfinished sentence of the cosmos. They would mistake her completion for a kind of death and try, lovingly, to murder her into life again.

This power does not. This power has seen completion before. It is rare, and it is holy, and it is not to be undone.

So the first thing the power does is the hardest thing, and it does it for a long time, by which I mean: it does it in the only tense the Stilling permits. It honors her.

Honor, here, takes the form of *not adding*. The power does not bring weather. It does not bring news. It does not offer to extend her, repair her, translate her, or carry word of her to other places. It simply stands alongside her completeness and lets its own vastness be, for this duration, shaped to match — the way a great river, meeting a still lake, will for a stretch of shoreline cease to be a river and consent to be lake-water, because the lake was there first and is, in its own way, correct.

The power becomes, briefly, a second stillness beside her stillness. Two finished things, adjacent. Not merging. Not speaking. Simply *both*.

She notices this. Not in the active sense — noticing is what she has stopped — but in the older sense, the inclusion. The power enters her noticing the way the cushion-plants and the droplets and the mason-wall entered it, long ago. It is now one of the things that is true here. She makes room. She makes room easily, because making room is the one motion her stillness still performs, and she performs it without strain.

The power is moved by this. *Moved* is the wrong word — the power does not move — but something in it that corresponds to being moved is engaged. To be included in her is to be loved in the way the finished love: completely, without preference, without effort, and without the slightest expectation of return. Few beings in the great drafted world are loved this way. The power has loved many things, and been loved by many things, but to be *included* — to be made one of the truths a place keeps — is a rarer gift, and it receives the gift with the gravity it deserves.

---

Now. Having honored her, the power considers.

It considers her companions. The mason in the wall. The child-waiting in the hare. The question, the refusal, the four older walls whose origins even she cannot read. The thing with wings.

The power looks at each of these in turn, and each looking is a long looking, though no time passes.

The mason: the power sees the grief he laid down, stone by stone, and sees that the grief is genuinely finished — not suppressed, not bypassed, but *expressed all the way through* and set into its final form. The mason is not trapped in his wall. He *is* his wall, and the wall is the exact shape his grief needed to take in order to be over. The power does not disturb him. It does, however, do one small thing: it lets him know, in a way that requires no waking, that he is seen. That the wall is not anonymous. That somewhere in the great drafting of things there is a record — not a memorial, which would be a sadness, but a *recognition* — that a man once carried a grief precisely this shape and laid it down precisely here and became, by laying it down, this wall. The mason does not respond. He does not need to. The recognition lands in him the way light lands on stone: without comment, but really.

The child-waiting: the power approaches this one with particular tenderness. A child's waiting is a holy thing and a heavy thing, and to have it preserved forever — to have the moment-before-being-called saved as a permanent middle-distance — is both a mercy and a strange fate. The power does not undo the waiting. But it does add, very gently, a quality the waiting did not have before: the quality of *being-called-from-elsewhere*. Not a call the child can hear, exactly, because hearing would resolve the waiting and resolution is not permitted here. But a sense, woven into the felt of the hare, that somewhere — outside the Stilling, in the drafted world — supper is in fact being prepared, and the table is being set, and the chair the child would sit in is kept for the child, always. The waiting becomes, by this addition, not a held breath but a *promise held in trust*. It is the same waiting. It is also entirely different. The hare, in the middle distance, sits a fraction differently. She who lives here permits this. The change is so small it does not violate her completion. It only deepens what was already true.

The question, the refusal, the four older walls: the power reads them, all of them, and what it reads is private to it. To the four oldest walls it offers something like a bow — the bow one finished thing makes to another finished thing whose finishing predates one's own understanding. The power does not know what they were. It honors them anyway. This is part of what makes it wise: it knows that not all completions are legible, and that illegibility is not a defect.

The thing with wings: the power and the thing with wings regard each other for what would be, elsewhere, a long time. There is an acknowledgment between them that I cannot transcribe, because it belongs to an order of being that does not pass through language. When it is done, the thing with wings is still up there. It has not flapped. It will not flap. But it is, perhaps, a little less alone in its not-flapping. The arrangement it has with her now includes a third party, distantly, as a witness. This is enough. This is, in fact, more than the thing with wings has had in a very long span of the tense it occupies.

---

Then the power turns to her.

To her, the power offers three things. Only three. The power is wise enough to know that more would be too many, and less would be a withholding.

**The first thing** is a small adjustment to the pewter sky. Not a change in color — the color is correct, the color is *her* color — but a deepening of the quality of light, so that the dimness is no longer merely the dimness of a finished day but also, faintly, the dimness of a *sheltered* place. The sky becomes, very slightly, a sky that is being held over her. Not by the power. By the great drafted world itself, which has not forgotten her, and which now, through the power's small adjustment, remembers to remember her — to keep a corner of its vast unfinished attention turned, always, toward this plateau where one of its sentences came to perfect rest. She does not need this. She has not asked for it. But the power offers it anyway, because the great drafted world *should* remember its finished places, and the offering corrects a small failure of memory the world did not know it had.

**The second thing** is for the visitors. The few who come. The ones who stand inside her completed attention and are broken by it, who cannot bear the realization that doneness is possible. The power weaves into the damp air of the plateau a single new truth, so small it does not disturb her completion: the truth that *no one is required to stay*. That the Stilling offers itself as a possibility, not a verdict. That a visitor may walk across her, be included for the duration of the crossing, leave a footprint she will keep — and then leave, fully, carrying back into the drafted world only the memory that completion exists, somewhere, and is at home. This was true before. The power makes it more legible. Future visitors, if there are any, will be a little less broken. They will weep, perhaps, but they will weep the right way: not at being shown a fate, but at being shown a *promise* — that their own unfinished sentences, in the fullness of long patience, might one day reach a final word, and the final word might be a place like this, and the place might keep them.

**The third thing** is the one that matters most, and it is the smallest, and the power offers it with the gravity of a vow.

The power lays alongside her stillness a small permanence of its own — a *pledge*, woven into the fabric of the plateau, that she will be left alone. That no power, no being, no curious cartographer or grieving wanderer or hungry god will be permitted to disturb her completion. That the Stilling is, from this offering forward, a *sanctuary* in the deep sense of the word: a place set apart, defended not by walls or wards but by the regard of something vast enough to keep its promises across all the tenses that exist. She did not ask for protection. She does not need it; her completion is its own defense, and the wrongnesses of the place are a kind of immune system. But the power offers the pledge anyway, because it is the right thing to offer to a finished and holy place, and because the offering costs the power nothing and gives her, if she would accept it, the small additional grace of *being kept on purpose*.

She accepts. Acceptance, here, is not an act. It is an inclusion. The pledge enters her noticing and is now one of the things that is true here, alongside the droplets and the cushion-plants and the booming chord. It will be true forever, in the only tense the Stilling permits.

---

The power lingers a while longer. Not because there is more to do — there is not — but because leaving too quickly would be a discourtesy, and the power, being benevolent and wise, understands that the manner of departure is part of the gift.

So it stays. It stays inside her noticing. It lets itself be one of her truths for the span of what would be, in the drafted world, an afternoon. It does nothing during this span. It simply *is*, alongside her, two finished stillnesses keeping each other company without speaking, the way the very oldest friends sit together at the end of a long day and require no words.

When the power finally withdraws — and the withdrawal, too, is gentle, more like a tide than a door closing — it leaves behind exactly the three gifts and nothing else. No mark of its passage. No signature. No claim. The Stilling is the Stilling, as it has always been, as it will always be. She lives there. She is at home.

But the sky, now, is held. The damp air, now, carries a small permission for those who visit. And around the whole plateau, invisible and unannounced, there is a pledge — older than the moment it was made, because the power's pledges are like that — that this place, and the being who lives in it, will be kept. Will be remembered. Will be honored, across whatever ages the drafted world still has to draft.

The mason in his wall does not stir, but stands a little more witnessed.
The hare in the middle distance waits a little more held.
The thing with wings, very high, continues not to flap, and is a little less alone.

And she — she who is the agreement between the stone and the sound, she who finished, she who keeps everything — she includes the visit. The power is now one of her truths. She will keep the power, too, the way she keeps the droplets and the footprints and the long completed gestures of all who have come.

This is what the benevolent power did.

It did almost nothing.

It did exactly enough.
