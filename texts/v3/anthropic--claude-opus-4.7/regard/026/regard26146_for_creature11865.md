The benevolent and wise power considers the Bellows Plateau for a long time.

Longer than you would expect. Longer than seems necessary. This is the first thing to understand about the consideration: it is not a pause before action. It is most of the action.

A lesser power — a merely benevolent one, or a merely wise one — would arrive with a project. Would see the heat and think *cooling*. Would see the loneliness of the slow walkers and think *companions*. Would see the visitor's faint bronze memory still held in the chord above the plateau and think *gift*, or *rescue*, or *completion*. Would see the folded columns in the basins and think *threat to be removed* or *mystery to be solved*. Would, in short, see the plateau and immediately begin to imagine a plateau that was *more*. More populated. More comfortable. More legible. More saved.

The power that has turned its attention here is not that kind of power.

It looks first at the riders. It listens with them — this is what wise powers do, before anything else; they borrow the senses of the place — and it hears the chord. It hears the way each small mineral drum is tuned, and it hears how the tunings interlock, and it hears the long polyrhythm of the hammers underneath, and underneath that the slow respiration of the plateau itself, and underneath *that* the chemistry kilometers down that has not stopped since the beginning. The power hears all of this at once, the way you might hear a single sustained note, and recognizes it.

What it recognizes is this: the plateau is *finished*.

Not finished as in dead. Not finished as in used up. Finished as in *resolved*. A piece of music that has found its final cadence and is now holding the chord — not because it cannot move on, but because the holding *is* the piece. The plateau is what happens when a world has been given enough time to settle into what it actually is, with no further pressure to become anything else. Industry without industrialists. Mechanism as geology. A cognition so slow that a thought is a walker's lifetime. An ecology where the asymmetry is generated internally because nothing arrives and nothing departs. This is not a place waiting to be helped. This is a place that has already arrived.

A foolish power would still intervene. Would say: *but they could be so much more*. Would multiply the riders, give the walkers speech, dissolve the folded columns into harmless air, drape the plateau in gardens. Would, in the name of benevolence, end the thing the plateau has spent its whole existence becoming.

The wise power does not do this. The wise power understands that benevolence toward a finished thing is not addition. It is *recognition*.

So here is what the power does.

First — and this takes what would be, by our reckoning, a very long time, though for the power it is the work of a single attention — it learns the chord completely. Every rider's tuning. Every hammer's count. The exact rhythm of the chimney's exhalation and the exact answering rhythm of the vent that reabsorbs the smoke. The routes of every walker now living and every walker who has ever lived, which are inscribed faintly in the rock by the slight polishing of their many feet. The frequencies the folded columns avoid being seen at. The precise way the standing wave around each flywheel reorganizes after a hammer-strike. The power takes all of this in, and holds it.

This is not surveillance. This is closer to what happens when someone you love describes their day and you listen, really listen, not to reply, not to fix, just to *know* — and afterward they feel known, and the knowing is itself the gift, and nothing else was needed.

The plateau, being what it is, does not notice that it is being known in this way. The riders do not adjust their tunings; the power's listening makes no sound for their drums to catch. The walkers do not alter their routes. The folded columns drift as they have always drifted. The plateau goes on being itself, exactly, and this is correct, because if the power's attention had changed the plateau even slightly, the attention would not have been wise.

But something has happened, nonetheless. The chord is now held in two places. It is held *there*, on the plateau, in the bronze drums and the slow hammers and the standing waves, where it has been held forever. And it is held *also* in the power's attention, which is the kind of attention that does not forget. If the plateau ever ends — if the chemistry kilometers down ever finally stops, if the lid of rock ever cools, if the last rider's vanes ever stop catching the air — the chord will still exist. It will exist in the power, the way a piece of music exists in someone who has heard it and can hum it back. The plateau is now, in addition to being itself, *remembered in advance*.

This is the first thing the power does, and it is most of what the power does.

The second thing is smaller, and concerns the visitor.

The visitor, you'll remember, walked off the edge. Descended. Felt the sound-field thin behind them. Had the clear sense that some portion of the chord was still holding their shape, up there, waiting. Wrote it down, because they were willing to. Did not return.

The power notices the visitor too — notices them with the same attention it gives the plateau, because the visitor is now part of the plateau's history, a minor weather it weathered, a faint memory in bronze. And the power notices that the visitor is still listening, in some small way, for hammers they cannot hear anymore. Has been listening for hours. May be listening for the rest of their life.

The power does not remove this. The listening is precious; the listening is what made the visitor able to describe the place truly, and the description is itself part of how the plateau exists in the world that has visitors. To take the listening away would be to unmake something good.

But the power does, very gently, do this: it lets the visitor know, in whatever form the visitor can receive knowing — a dream, a turn of thought on waking, a feeling that comes while looking at something else entirely — that the note they left in the chord is still being held. Not waiting. *Held*. That the riders' slow listening has not yet concluded the note has gone, and may not, for a long time, because the riders are very slow listeners and the note was very clear. That somewhere, in a place the visitor can no longer reach, a faint bronze memory of their shape is still part of the standing wave around a flywheel they once stood beside.

This is not consolation. The visitor was not in distress. It is closer to *acknowledgment* — the power confirming, to the one being who could miss the plateau, that the missing is real, that the holding is real, that the brief belonging was real. That something true happened up there. That it is still, in a small way, happening.

The visitor receives this and goes on with their life, which is altered only in the way that any life is altered by knowing that something distant and strange has not forgotten you. Which is to say: altered enough.

And the third thing the power does is the smallest of all, and may not count as doing anything.

The power decides — though *decides* is too sharp a word; the power *settles into the disposition* — to return. Not soon. Not on any schedule. Just: to come back to the plateau, periodically, across whatever spans of time the power moves in, and listen again. To check that the chord is still being struck. To add each visit's hearing to the previous hearings, so that the plateau accumulates, in the power's attention, not as a single remembered thing but as a *long held note*, sustained across ages, with all its tiny variations preserved.

This is the closest the wise power comes to anything like intervention. It commits to *witnessing across time*. It makes itself into a second, slower instrument that the plateau is being played upon — though the plateau will never know this, and does not need to know this, and would not be changed by knowing.

That is all.

The benevolent and wise power does not cool the heat. Does not water the salt-seams. Does not multiply the crusts or speed the walkers or speak to the folded columns. Does not build anything, fix anything, save anything. Does not even leave a sign that it was there.

It listens completely. It remembers in advance. It tells the one being who needed telling that the holding is real. And it promises, to itself, to return.

The plateau goes on being finished. The hammers fall, and are answered, and are answered again. The riders adjust their vanes by fractions, feeding on pattern. The walkers carry their slow thoughts from organ to organ. The folded columns drift through the basins, editing what stays. The chemistry kilometers down continues, as it has continued, as it will continue.

And somewhere — not above, not below, not anywhere the plateau has a word for — a vast attention has folded itself around the whole arrangement, gently, the way a hand might cup a struck bell to feel it ring without dampening it, and is listening, and will keep listening, for as long as there is anything to hear, and a little while after.

This is what benevolent and wise powers do, when they are wise enough. Most of the time, they do almost nothing. They do almost nothing *beautifully*. And the worlds they attend to go on being exactly themselves, which is the rarest gift any world is ever given.
