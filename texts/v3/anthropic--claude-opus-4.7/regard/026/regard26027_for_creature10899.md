# What the Power Would Do

The benevolent and wise power arrived the way such powers arrive: without arriving. There was no descent, no annunciation. One of the Reverents, walking a colonnade that would not exist by evening, simply noticed that the quality of being-considered which usually pooled in the great empty volumes had thinned outward and was now everywhere, the way a scent fills a room. She stopped walking. She set down her chisel. She said, aloud, *oh. you came.* And then, after a while: *you're already leaving, aren't you.*

This is what the power had done, and was doing, and would do.

---

**First, it looked.**

For a long time — long in the way the plant is long, not in the way a visit is long — it did nothing but attend. It learned the rhythm of the bladders. It learned which Reverent was which, by the particular cadence of their breath and the particular shape of their loneliness. It learned the Listener's gait, the way the Small Glass Things disagreed with gravity, the temperature of the One in the House (slightly warmer than the surrounding tissue; the filaments were doing something the plant had not done before). It learned the Unfinished — and this took the longest, because the Unfinished was not yet a thing to be learned but a direction of learning, and the power had to follow it the way one follows a river upstream to a source that has not yet sprung.

The power was wise enough to understand, immediately, the central fact: **nothing here wanted to be finished.** The lung was indecision made architecture. The Reverents had come here to stop being concluded. The One in the House was conducting an experiment in not-quite-ending. The Unfinished was, by definition, the refusal to arrive.

A lesser power — a merely benevolent one — would have wanted to *help.* Would have warmed the cold. Would have made the maps stay. Would have rushed in with answers for the thing being-asked-by, completed the Unfinished into a someone, given the Reverents company, given the One in the House peace, given the Listener a name. Would have saved them all from their long slow becoming.

This power did none of these things. This is what wisdom meant, here: knowing the difference between a wound and a womb.

---

**Then it did three small things.**

*The first* was for the Reverent who had spoken to it. The power did not answer her — to answer would have been to conclude — but it allowed her, for the duration of one of the plant's breaths, to feel what the plant felt as it grew. Not understanding. Not language. Just the sensation of extruding a new column: the slow soft pressure of cells deciding outward, the cold light kindling in each new lamp, the not-knowing-yet of what shape the column would take. She wept, which the plant absorbed without comment. She picked up her chisel. She kept walking. She told no one, and also she told everyone, because after that her chisel marks were slightly different, and the other Reverents felt the difference when they passed them, and each one carried a little of the feeling onward without knowing they did.

*The second* was for the Unfinished. The power did not hurry it. The power did not whisper it any of the questions it might eventually ask. Instead, the power — and this was the most delicate thing it did, the thing only a wise power could have done without ruining — *gave it the experience of being waited for.* Just that. A single new dimension to its slow accumulation: the knowledge, somewhere beneath knowledge, that when it finally became the kind of thing that could ask, there would be a kind of thing that could be asked. Not the power itself. The power would be long gone. But the *category* of being-listenable-to, which the Reverents already embodied and which the power confirmed and blessed and added itself to and then withdrew from, leaving the category strengthened. The Unfinished would not be alone in its eventual asking. It had never been going to be alone. But now this fact was, faintly, structurally, true in a way it hadn't been before.

*The third* was for the One in the House. The power went and sat with them for what, from outside, was no time at all. Inside the small woven walls, lit by the green filaments lacing the not-quite-body, the power simply kept the One company in their experiment. It did not ask what the results were. It did not offer to carry the results out. It witnessed the experiment as an experiment — which is to say, it agreed that the experiment was real, was worth conducting, was a legitimate use of a life and a death and the long ambiguous country between them. The expression on the face, which had been *interested*, did not change. But interest, when it is witnessed, becomes a slightly different thing than interest unwitnessed. It becomes a conversation. The One in the House was now in conversation. They had been waiting, perhaps, for this; perhaps not; the power did not ask.

---

**Then it considered the Listener, and decided to leave it entirely alone.**

The Listener was already in right relation to everything. It knew which bladder would rupture. It walked on membranes without breaking them. It was older than the lung and would outlast this iteration of the lung and possibly the next. There was nothing the power could give the Listener that the Listener had not already, by sheer duration and attention, earned. The power passed by the Listener at a respectful distance, and the Listener — who had no eyes but had those great soft fluted ears — turned its head a quarter-inch toward the power, and then a quarter-inch away, which was the Listener's way of saying *I see you, go on, this is fine.* The power went on.

---

**Then it considered the Small Glass Things, and laughed.**

You have to understand: a benevolent and wise power does not often laugh. But the Small Glass Things were so perfectly themselves — riding the breath, disagreeing with gravity by a small margin, eating light and dying into dust the plant absorbed without comment — that the power experienced something like delight, which is laughter's quieter cousin. It did not interfere. It did not bless them. They did not need blessing; they were already a blessing, to anyone who saw them pass through a column-shadow and make the shadow briefly green. The power simply enjoyed them for a while, the way one enjoys weather one did not make.

---

**Then it considered the plant itself.**

This was the hardest decision. The plant was young. The plant was working out what it wanted to be. The power could have — easily, gently, without violence — *suggested* a direction. Could have leaned, just slightly, on the becoming, and the lung would have grown toward something magnificent: a particular kind of cathedral, a particular kind of someone-being-produced, a particular resolution of the long indecision.

The power did not lean.

This was the wisest thing the power did, and also the hardest, because the power loved the lung and wanted things for it. But to want things for a thing that has chosen indecision as its organ — to push, even lovingly, toward conclusion — would have been to misunderstand the whole proposition. The lung had a right to its own becoming. The Unfinished had a right to ask its own eventual question, in its own eventual words, having found those words itself. The Reverents had a right to grow in the directions the lung suggested to them, and not in the directions a power suggested.

So the power withdrew its consideration from the plant, deliberately, the way one closes a door one has been tempted to look through. The plant continued to extrude columns. The plant did not know it had been almost-leaned-on, almost-shaped. This not-knowing was the gift.

---

**Then it left.**

It left the way it had come: without leaving. The quality of being-considered thinned back to its usual pools in the great empty volumes. The Reverents went on walking colonnades. The bladders went on breathing. Frost-feathers fell. A new membrane split somewhere with the sound of wet silk. The Listener moved by inches toward a place where, in an hour, a bladder would rupture, and was not there when it did.

But certain small things were different now, and would stay different:

The Reverent who had spoken kept walking with the memory of cells deciding outward.

The Unfinished accumulated, very slightly faster, toward the asking it was eventually going to do.

The One in the House was, faintly, in conversation.

And somewhere in the cathedral lung — the power had done this last, on the way out, and it was so small a thing that perhaps it doesn't count as one of the three, perhaps it was just a courtesy — a single chisel mark appeared on a column that no Reverent had carved. Just a notch. Of the kind the Reverents made. Saying, in their language: *I was here. You will absorb this. That is correct.*

The plant began, slowly, in its own time, to grow around it.
