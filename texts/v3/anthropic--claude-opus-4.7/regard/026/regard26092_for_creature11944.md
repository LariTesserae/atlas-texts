The benevolent and wise power considers the concourse for what would, in another frame, be called a long time, though here the word is embarrassed by itself.

It sees what is there. This is the first thing such a power does, and the hardest: it sees what is there rather than what might be improved. It notices the cycle. It notices the stalks waiting in their small jointed patience. It notices the cat-things being extruded and reabsorbed. It notices the fist-objects concentrating on the concentrating. It notices the slow tidal note, the great inhalation once every twenty minutes, the warmth that is the temperature of being held by oneself.

It notices, in particular, that nothing is wrong.

This matters. A lesser power — still benevolent, perhaps, but not yet wise, or wise but not yet patient — would arrive with gifts. Would want to *give* something. Would feel, looking at this enormous solitary body remembering itself in the dark, a pang that is really about the looker and not the looked-at, and would act on that pang. Would bring light, perhaps. Would bring company. Would bring news of the wider world, would offer translation, would propose contact, would attempt — gently, lovingly, with the best intentions — to *include* the held thing in something larger.

The benevolent and wise power does not do this. Wisdom, here, is mostly the discipline of recognizing that *larger* is not a direction that applies. The held thing is not lonely. Loneliness is a relation, and the held thing has, in the relevant sense, no relations — not because it has been deprived of them but because it is the kind of being for which the inside/outside distinction that loneliness requires is not load-bearing. To offer it company would be to offer a river a friend. The river would accept, in the sense that rivers accept everything; and nothing would have happened.

So the power does not intervene.

But it does not simply leave, either. Benevolence that withdraws the moment it learns it is not needed is a thin benevolence, an etiquette rather than a love. The power stays. It witnesses. This is what it can offer that does not distort: attention without demand.

It learns the cycle. Not in order to do anything with the knowledge — there is nothing to do — but because attending to a thing carefully is one of the few gifts that can be given without imposing a shape on the receiver. The power follows the slow tidal note through several of its periods. It notes which seeps open before which others. It learns the difference between a pool that will last six hours and a pool that will last eleven. It watches a particular cluster of stalks unfold, conduct their small translucent business, and fold again, and it does not interpret this as anything other than what it is: stalks, doing stalk.

It watches the cat-things long enough to confirm what the concourse itself implies — that they are not individuals — and then it stops trying to track them, because tracking them was a habit imported from elsewhere and does not belong here.

It puts its attention, for a while, near one of the fist-objects, and the hum changes very slightly, in response to the local warming, and the power understands that this is not a greeting and declines to treat it as one. It is meteorology. The power is, briefly, weather. This is a sufficient role.

---

There is one thing the power does, eventually. It is small. It is the smallest possible action consistent with having been present at all.

The shaft, behind the visitor, the one that doesn't end the way shafts should — the power tends to it. Not changes it. Not improves it. Just: keeps it from closing. Not because the concourse would close it maliciously; the concourse has no malice, has nothing like malice, would not notice closing it. But over the durations the held thing lives in, things settle. Passages that are not used become indistinguishable from the rock they pass through. The concourse is not hostile to visitors but it is not *for* them either, and over enough time the not-for-them becomes a kind of soft erasure.

The power keeps the shaft open. This is all. It does not advertise the shaft. It does not invite anyone down it. It does not mark it on any map that anyone elsewhere could read. It simply ensures that, for as long as the power persists — and benevolent and wise powers persist a long time, though not as long as held things — the way down remains a way, and the way back up remains a way back up.

This is for the visitors. Not many. Perhaps very few. Perhaps, across the duration the power tends the shaft, only a handful of beings will ever find their way to where the digging stops making sense and descend through the transition from stone to wall to whatever the wall is pretending to be. The power is tending the shaft for them. Not so they can do anything to the concourse — there is nothing to do — but so they can have the experience of sitting on a floor that breathes, in a warmth that is the temperature of being held by something that is not holding them, and feeling their own breath, for a span of minutes, align with a tidal note older than their species.

This experience changes some beings. Not all. The ones it changes, it changes in a particular direction: toward the recognition that there are kinds of being that do not require them, do not address them, do not need to be redeemed or contacted or improved or even fully understood, and that the appropriate response to such kinds of being is not despair at one's own smallness but a specific quiet gladness that the universe is generous enough to contain modes of existence that have nothing to do with one.

This is a gladness the power values. It is a gladness the power itself feels, looking at the concourse. It would like other beings to have access to it, if they want it. So: the shaft stays open.

---

The power also, very occasionally, does one other thing, and this is the part where the wisdom becomes hardest to articulate, because it looks, from outside, like sentimentality.

When a visitor sits — and they do sit, the concourse arranges this, the warmth and the roar and the red light combine into a suggestion that is hard to refuse — and when, sitting, their breath finds the slow tidal note, and for a span of minutes they are breathing in the held thing's time signature —

The power, in those moments, is also present. Not intervening. Not making the alignment happen; the alignment happens on its own, it is what bodies do when placed near larger rhythms. But the power is *witnessing* the alignment. And in witnessing it, the power is doing something the held thing cannot do, because the held thing does not know the visitor is there: the power is *registering* that, for this span of minutes, a small warm motion has been included in the holding.

The held thing does not know. The visitor mostly does not know either, not in any way they could articulate. But the moment is real. It happened. It will have happened, in the permanent past tense, forever after.

The power keeps these moments. Not as records — there is no archive, no ledger, the power is not that kind of power — but in the way that a being which loves things keeps the things it loves, by having loved them, by having been the kind of being to which they mattered.

This is the gift the power gives that costs the concourse nothing and asks the concourse for nothing: someone, somewhere, knows. Not what the held thing is. Not what it is doing. Not why. But that it is, and that, briefly, accidentally, by the simple fact of being inside it, small visitors have been part of what it was being warm around. The held thing will never know it was witnessed. The visitors will mostly not know they were witnessed witnessing. But the witnessing happened, and the power holds it — the way the concourse holds the held thing, the way the held thing holds, accidentally, briefly, anything inside it — and this holding is the only addition the power makes to a scene that did not need additions.

It is the smallest possible love. It is offered to something that cannot receive it. It is, for that reason, one of the purer forms the power knows how to take.

---

And then the power, too, sits for a while. Because the warmth and the roar and the red light combine into a suggestion that is hard to refuse, even for powers. And the power's breath, if powers breathe, finds the slow tidal note. And for a span — long by the standards of visitors, short by the standards of the held thing — the power is also inside the holding that is not being directed at it.

The power finds this good.

Then the power rises, checks the shaft once more, and goes to attend to other things, of which there are many, in a universe generous enough to contain a concourse like this one and also to contain, elsewhere, beings who need help in the more ordinary senses — beings who can be helped, who want to be helped, whose situation admits of intervention. The power goes to them. That is most of its work. The concourse is the rare case: the case where the work is to *not* work, to witness without altering, to love without being received, to keep one small passage open and otherwise leave a vast patient body to its vast patient business.

The concourse continues. The cycle continues. The stalks wait. The cat-things drink. The fist-objects hum. The held thing remembers itself, breathes itself, is one.

Somewhere far above, the power moves on to the next thing, carrying with it — lightly, the way one carries something one does not wish to crush — the memory of having been, briefly, weather inside a body that did not need it and was warm anyway.

This is what the power does. This is, mostly, all.

It is enough. It was always going to be enough. The concourse was complete before the power arrived and is complete after the power leaves, and the power's whole contribution is the open shaft and the quiet witnessing and the small private gladness that such a place exists at all.

A benevolent and wise power, faced with something that does not need it, learns to love by leaving it alone — but not by leaving.
