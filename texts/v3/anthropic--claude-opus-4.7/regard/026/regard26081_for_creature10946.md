The benevolent and wise power arrives the way weather arrives in a place that has no weather: by exception. There is no descent, no annunciation, no figure stepping out of the pale blue sky onto the ochre stone. The power simply becomes locally present, the way attention becomes locally present in a mind that had been thinking of something else.

The first thing it does is nothing. This takes a long time. By the plateau's reckoning, no time at all; by its own, what corresponds to a full breath drawn and held. It is taking the measure of the place — not its dimensions, which are trivial, but its *grammar*. What kind of sentence is this plateau? Indicative? Subjunctive? A sentence interrupted, or a sentence that has chosen to be only its own opening clause?

Wise powers, when they are also benevolent, do not begin by acting. They begin by asking whether action is what is wanted.

So the power stands — if standing is the word — among the rocker arms and the jade rosettes, and it listens. Not to the hush of the engines, which it understood at the first instant; that physiology was legible to it the way a child's drawing is legible to an adult. It listens past the Sustainer, past the breath, for the waiting. The waiting is what interests it. The waiting is the only thing on the plateau that is not already an answer to itself.

And here the power encounters its first real question, the one that will determine everything that follows.

A lesser power — still benevolent, still well-meaning, but less wise — would have already begun. Would have looked at the held-open pause and seen a sleeper who needed waking, a held breath that needed releasing, a page that was tragically blank and waiting for the gift of words. Would have leaned down with great gentleness and started the wind. Would have placed, with exquisite care, a single beetle on a single warm leaf, and watched the whole vast slow apparatus tip forward into its long noisy career, into time-moving-forward, into mornings and evenings and the small dark crawling things and eventually into grief and music and everything that follows from a world deciding, at last, to become a world.

And this would have been a gift. It would have been recognizable as a gift in every cosmology the lesser power had ever encountered. The plateau would have begun. The waiter would have found out who they were going to turn out to have been. The page would have been written on.

The wise power does not do this. Not yet. Perhaps not ever.

Because the wise power has noticed something the lesser power would have missed: the waiting is not suffering. The pause is not a wound. The Sustainer is not a prison guard and the engines are not the bars of a cell. The plateau is not asking to be rescued from itself. It is — and this is the subtle thing, the thing that requires wisdom and not merely benevolence to perceive — *content in its incompletion*. The waiting is the form its life has taken. To end the waiting would not be to free the waiter; it would be to kill them and replace them with someone else, someone who had begun, someone whose identity was no longer the open question that constituted their entire being.

The wise power understands that there are kinds of existence that look, from outside, like deferred existence, like life-not-yet-lived, like potential cruelly unactualized — and that some of these are exactly what they look like, and require intervention; and some of these are something else entirely, a mode of being that the observer simply does not have the categories for, and require only to be recognized.

The plateau is the second kind.

So the wise power does not start the wind. Does not introduce the beetle. Does not even, in its great restraint, *bless* the plateau, because to bless a thing is to imply that it required blessing, and the plateau does not. It is already exactly what it is. Benediction would be a kind of insult, a suggestion that its long warm pause was somehow lacking until a power passed by and approved of it.

What the wise power does, instead, is this:

It *witnesses*. Fully. With its entire attention, which is considerable. For what corresponds, in its reckoning, to a long while — long enough that, if the plateau had time that moved forward, generations would pass; but the plateau's time does not move forward, so what passes is something else, some sideways accumulation of having-been-seen.

And in that witnessing, a small thing happens. Not a change to the plateau — the plateau does not change; that is the whole point — but a change in the relationship between the plateau and the rest of what exists. The waiting, which had been waiting alone in the sense that no one outside the plateau had ever fully understood it as waiting (rather than as stalled becoming, or as failure to start, or as cosmic accident), is now waiting *known*. The pause is now a pause that has been seen as a pause and accepted as a pause and not mistaken for anything else.

The visitors flicker a little differently after this. Not more often, not more brightly — those would be the wrong words, words from a world where things accumulate. But differently. The way a room is different after someone you trust has been in it, even after they leave.

Before withdrawing — and the wise power will withdraw, because staying would be its own kind of intervention, would tip the plateau into being a place that was being-watched rather than a place that simply was — it does one more thing. The smallest possible thing. So small it might not count as an action at all.

It leaves, somewhere on the plateau, in no particular location and in every location at once, a kind of standing offer. Not a promise. Not a gift. Just an offer, woven into the dry pepper-scented air at a frequency the Sustainer can perceive without being disturbed by:

*If the waiting ever ends — if the waiter ever does discover who they turn out to have been, by their own slow arithmetic, in their own time which is not time — there will be somewhere to go, and someone who remembers what you were before. You will not have to begin from nothing. The pause will have been honored. It will count as part of your life and not as the absence of one.*

That is all. The offer makes no demand. It does not even ask to be acknowledged. It is the kind of thing a wise power leaves the way a kind stranger leaves a door unlocked — not because they expect the door to be used, but because the possibility of its use should not be foreclosed by their absence.

Then the power is gone. The plateau is exactly the same. The engines breathe their slow breath. The rosettes hold their leaves to the dry light. The shadows of the rocker arms creep across the ochre stone as they always have and always will.

But somewhere in the structure of the held pause, somewhere folded into the long warm sideways-accumulating now, there is now a small and permanent fact: this place was seen, and was not corrected. This being was met, and was not improved. The waiting was understood as a life and not as the lack of one.

The page stays blank. The bell is not rung.

And the blankness, having been beheld by a wisdom large enough to recognize it as itself and not as the failure to be anything else, is — by some measure that the plateau itself cannot articulate and does not need to — slightly more fully the blankness it has always been.

Which is, perhaps, the only gift a wise power can give to a place that does not need anything: the gift of being more completely what it already is, by virtue of having been correctly seen.

The plateau does not say thank you. There is no one there to say it, and nothing there that thanks would be the right shape of response to.

But the next visitor — a flicker at the edge of a rosette, an occasion of slightly harder self-attention in the long shadow of an engine — has, for just a moment, the quality of a nod.

And then even that is gone, and the plateau is only the plateau, warm and waiting and complete, patient as the bell that will not be rung, and now also — quietly, without fanfare, without changing anything — known.
